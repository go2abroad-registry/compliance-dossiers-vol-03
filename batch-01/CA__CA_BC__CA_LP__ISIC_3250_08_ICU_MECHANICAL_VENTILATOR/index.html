Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and Income Tax registration via My Business Account portal; BC Ministry of Finance – Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration via eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership carrying on business in Canada must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account using Form RC1 or the CRA online Business Registration service; registration is mandatory if taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 small-supplier threshold or if the LP elects to register voluntarily; the partnership must also register for BC PST through eTaxBC when it makes taxable retail sales in BC; the statutory TIN for federal tax filings is the 9-digit CRA BN plus program account suffix (RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RC for corporate/income tax where applicable).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident Limited Partnership carrying on business in Canada must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account using Form RC1 or the CRA online Business Registration service; registration is mandatory if taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 small-supplier threshold or if the LP elects to register voluntarily; the partnership must also register for BC PST through eTaxBC when it makes taxable retail sales in BC; the statutory TIN for federal tax filings is the 9-digit CRA BN plus program account suffix (RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RC for corporate/income tax where applicable).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election under Canadian law; a Limited Partnership is mandatorily pass-through. Available statutory elections include: non-resident partner election under Income Tax Act s. 216 to file on net Canadian rental income, s. 45(2)/45(3) elective disposition/rollover, and partnership fiscal-period elections; there is no elective hybrid-mismatch entity classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small-supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter; BC PST registration generally applies to businesses making PST taxable sales regardless of a minimum threshold, subject to narrow exemptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of qualifying critical-care ICU mechanical ventilators exported by the non-resident Limited Partnership from Canada to a foreign customer is zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act when the seller has maintained proof of export and the goods are shipped or delivered outside Canada; qualifying export-related services are zero-rated when they relate to exported tangible property, are performed outside Canada, or are supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST; the exporter may claim input tax credits and file a GST/HST return to obtain refunds of attributable GST. BC PST does not apply to goods sold for export outside Canada where the seller obtains proper written evidence of export.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration or CBSA proof-of-report stamped by the Canada Border Services Agency
  • Commercial export invoice stating goods, value, buyer, and Canadian exporter
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to an overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank telegraphic transfer/letter-of-credit payment receipt evidencing foreign-currency repatriation
  • Packing list and transport documents linking the exported goods to the foreign buyer

No federal or BC statutory mandate for real-time fiscal e-invoicing, digital stamping, or government clearance of invoices; the CRA accepts electronic invoices issued by compliant accounting systems and administers a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoice framework aligned with PEPPOL; GST/HST returns are filed electronically via CRA My Business Account, with approved API/net-file services available to eligible filers.

The CRA may reassess and retrospectively disallow export zero-rating where export documentation is incomplete, non-contemporaneous, or false; penalties include gross-negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated), daily compound interest, and criminal prosecution for fraud; BC PST may also claw back the export exemption and impose separate penalties. Exporters must retain all export evidence for CRA records-retention periods to avoid reassessment, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction and the Canadian dollar is freely floating. Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility without exchange-control authorisation, conversion quotas, repatriation permits, or central bank pre-approval for profit remittances. A British Columbia limited partnership may lawfully receive foreign-denominated capital injections, hold USD/EUR and other foreign currency accounts, and remit partner distributions to non-residents. For tax purposes, the LP is treated as fiscally transparent, so ordinary business-income distributions are not subject to a compounding remittance tax; non-resident partners remain taxable on Canadian-source business income under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Part XIII withholding may arise only for separately characterised Canadian-source passive payments such as interest, rents, royalties or management fees, subject to treaty reduction, but such amounts require no Bank of Canada approval. Banks will perform sanctions/PEP screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act, which may delay transfers but does not represent a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime, no minimum conversion ratio, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no requirement that export receipts be sold to the central bank. A CAN/CA-BC LP may retain export proceeds in CAD or any foreign currency in a domestic or offshore account. Export compliance is limited to reporting requirements and declarations: the exporter must file a Canadian Export Declaration (B13A/CAED) through Statistics Canada or its customs broker for controlled goods or prescribed shipment values, maintain supporting commercial invoices and bills of lading, and ensure the goods are not on Canada's Export Control List or destined to a sanctioned end-user. These declarations are for trade statistics and customs administration, not foreign-exchange surrender or conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential banking supervision is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Auxiliary feasibility: Medium-High; direct-clearing feasibility: Low. Canada does not recognise the 'Electronic Money Institution' licence class. A UK FCA-authorised or EU/EMI-licensed digital bank may be used as an ancillary treasury visibility account, FX execution layer, or contingency payment rail, but it must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC if it obtains Canadian business, and it cannot provide cause in the Canadian Lynx / ACSS settlement system without using a Canadian Schedule I bank as sponsor. For an ICU mechanical ventilator manufacturer whose target payment rails are Government LC and SWIFT wires, an offshore EMI is legally viable for pre-settlement FX spot and smaller supplier payments, but it is not a permissible master clearing account because commercial letters of credit, confirmed export LCs, and government procurement disbursements require a full-service Canadian bank with direct SWIFT participation and OSFI-regulated trade operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident partners, shareholders, or intercompany treasury centres must be routed through a Canadian Schedule I bank direct participant in SWIFT. The sender should issue an MT103 with the beneficiary being the CA LP's operational or escrow account at the Canadian bank; include the Canadian beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2, BOFMCAM3, or NABCCAMM), the LP's name exactly as registered, the U.S. or CAD account number, and the bank transit/account combination. In the 77B / Remittance Information field, the sender should state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO BC LIMITED PARTNERSHIP' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN TO BC LP' and reference the B.C. registry number or financing agreement number. Although Canada does not prescribe a mandatory payment purpose code, the bank will require an anti-money-laundering source-of-funds narrative and a board/partner resolution; funds from sanctioned jurisdictions will be rejected. For loans, the instruction must also identify the loan agreement and use the correct interest/payment terms so that Part XIII withholding can be applied by the Canadian paying entity where relevant; the Canadian bank will not authorise or report on behalf of the LP, and all filings remain the LP's obligation.Trade settlements must use SWIFT MT103 for international wires and the Canadian Lynx / ACSS clearing systems for domestic CAD disbursements. For U.S. dollar supplier payments, the Canadian bank will transmit to its U.S. correspondent or use an internal USD clearing account; the LP should always include the invoice number and product HS 9019.20 classification. For high-value component procurement, the recommended rail is an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by the Canadian bank in favour of the overseas supplier, subject to UCP600, and confirmed by the supplier's bank if political or bank risk exists. If the LP is itself the exporter under ministry of health or public-hospital LC contracts, the outbound rail is typically a government LC confirmed by the Canadian bank, with shipping documents and the MDEL certificate as the settlement trigger. All trade financing should be structured through the bank's trade-services desk under an approved facility limit; settlement proceeds are received via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the LP's operating account and must be reconciled to the financed LC within the bank's trade-servicing workflow to avoid dormant escalation.Deliverable FX forwards in CAD/USD/EUR and any freely convertible settlement currency, executed under an ISDA master agreement or a short-form FX master agreement with an OSFI-supervised Canadian bank; permitted without regulatory pre-clearance because Canada has no exchange control on forward contracts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies that cannot be delivered offshore because of local convertibility restrictions, such as CNY, INR, KRW, or exotic destination-market currencies; permissible when the NDF matches a real ventilators or component-import receivable/payable and is booked with a regulated Canadian financial institution.Cross-currency swaps between CAD and USD/EUR, and between functional-currency CAD and the LC settlement currency, permitted for currency and interest-rate risk hedging on the LP's debt or long-term component supply agreements; subject to bank credit approval, partnership-authority opinion, and standard ISDA collateral terms.European-style FX options, zero-cost collars, and put spreads combined with deliverable forwards; these are permissible hedging instruments under Canadian banking law, but must be recorded as derivative transactions and reported to the bank's counterparty-credit desk as they are not available on the exchange-traded rails.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and has no separate federal or British Columbia corporate income tax liability. Income is allocated to partners. If income is allocated to a taxable Canadian corporation, the ordinary combined federal and British Columbia corporate rate is generally 27%: 15% federal and 12% British Columbia.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax generally applies to dividends paid or deemed paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident. A Canadian limited partnership does not pay dividends; distributions and allocations retain their character and may create partner-level Canadian tax, withholding, or filing obligations.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally within five months after the partnership fiscal period-end. T5013 slips and summaries generally must be provided to partners and filed by the last day of the third month after the partnership fiscal period-end. A partnership with a corporate partner, non-resident partner, or specified filing characteristics may be required to file even where it has no tax payable.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the end of the corporation's taxation year. Corporate tax instalments are generally monthly, or quarterly for eligible corporations. This form applies to a corporate general partner or separate operating corporation, not ordinarily to the limited partnership itself.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no formal statutory OECD-style Master File or Local File threshold. Contemporaneous documentation is required under Income Tax Act section 247(4) for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions. Form T106 generally applies where aggregate reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million, subject to statutory exclusions and filing instructions. Documentation should be prepared by the filing due date and provided to CRA within three months of request.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to the UAE or certain other jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the general anti-avoidance rule, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership principles, partnership characterization, treaty anti-abuse provisions, interest deductibility rules, and judicial doctrines. The partnership should maintain Canadian operational substance proportionate to the manufacturing activity, including genuine functions, personnel, facilities, equipment, risk control, decision-making, quality systems, inventory management, and commercially supportable financing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion can result in a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Civil gross-negligence penalties can generally equal 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit, in addition to reassessed tax and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face prosecution, fines, imprisonment, director assessment where applicable, and personal liability for certain unremitted source deductions. The corporate veil is not routinely pierced for ordinary corporate tax debts, but fraud, knowing participation, statutory director liability, sham arrangements, and improper dissolution can create personal exposure. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty but may arise through criminal process, bail conditions, judgment enforcement, or immigration consequences.
Failure to remit payroll, Part XIII, or other trust amounts

Statutory sanction: Late-remittance penalties can generally be 10% of the amount for a first failure and 20% for a repeat failure, subject to statutory rules, with prescribed interest and possible criminal prosecution. CRA may issue assessments against responsible directors under Income Tax Act section 227.1.

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions, subject to statutory due-diligence and limitation rules. General partners may also have direct liability under partnership law. Personal assets may be pursued after statutory collection conditions are met.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under section 247, impose a 10% transfer-pricing adjustment penalty where the statutory threshold is met, deny deductions, impute income, apply withholding tax, and charge interest. Secondary adjustments and treaty competent-authority consequences may arise.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically personally liable for a transfer-pricing reassessment, but intentional participation in fraud, false records, or abusive transactions can support civil and criminal exposure. A general partner may bear broader liability for partnership obligations.
Treaty abuse and beneficial-ownership failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty relief under beneficial-ownership, limitation-on-benefits, principal-purpose, or general anti-avoidance principles; reassess withholding at 25%; impose interest and penalties; and pursue anti-avoidance litigation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal exposure for knowing false certifications, dishonest assistance, tax-evasion conduct, or unremitted withholding. Treaty denial alone ordinarily does not pierce the corporate veil.
Shell company and sham partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize the arrangement, deny partnership status or deductions, apply the general anti-avoidance rule, assess tax and interest, and impose gross-negligence or criminal penalties where knowledge and intent are established.

Director personal liability: General partners may have unlimited personal liability for partnership obligations. Limited partners may lose limited-liability protection if they act outside statutory limitations or participate in management under applicable law. Fraudulent conduct can support personal restitution and criminal sanctions.
AML, proceeds of crime, and sanctions

Statutory sanction: Money laundering and proceeds-of-crime offences can result in forfeiture, substantial fines, and imprisonment, including imprisonment for life for the most serious laundering offences. FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties can apply for compliance failures, with amounts depending on the violation and entity or individual status. Canadian sanctions violations can result in criminal and administrative consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may face prosecution, asset restraint, forfeiture, fines, imprisonment, and prohibition orders where they authorize, facilitate, or knowingly ignore unlawful conduct. Travel bans are not automatic but may result from arrest warrants, bail conditions, or sanctions-related restrictions.
Corporate books, beneficial ownership, and recordkeeping failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may impose information-request penalties, deny deductions or credits, reassess using estimates, and refer persistent non-compliance for prosecution. Corporate and partnership registries may impose separate filing consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners can face statutory recordkeeping penalties and personal exposure where non-compliance is knowing, fraudulent, or connected with trust amounts. The absence of records materially weakens limited-liability and tax-defence positions.
Medical-device regulatory and tax-integrity failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory measures may include product seizure, recalls, stop-sale orders, licence suspension or cancellation, administrative monetary penalties, prosecution, and imprisonment under applicable federal health-product legislation. Customs misclassification can create duties, GST/HST assessments, penalties, and seizure.

Director personal liability: Corporate officers and directors may be prosecuted where they authorize, direct, or consent to regulatory offences. Civil product-liability claims, indemnity claims, recall costs, and reputational losses can be personally relevant notwithstanding ordinary corporate limited liability.
