Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A British Columbia limited partnership must maintain a valid registered office and records address capable of receiving legal notices and maintaining or accessing required records. A commercial virtual office may be acceptable if it provides a genuine physical address, reliable mail handling, records access, and a service provider willing to support statutory and financial-institution verification. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for a British Columbia limited partnership. Each limited partner must make an actual contribution as disclosed in the Declaration (money, property, or services); credit, promissory notes, or linked notes do not constitute valid contributions. No escrow account is mandated by statute. However, a limited partner may not withdraw capital if such withdrawal renders the partnership insolvent, and withdrawals within one year of dissolution or insolvency may be void against creditors under the Partnership Act.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and inspection under the BC Fire Code, including egress, occupancy limits, and sprinkler compliance for storage areas; municipal structural load clearance for racked storage; and WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety clearance, including OHS orientation, due diligence certification, and a documented safe handling protocol for electronics, chargers, and lithium-ion batteries.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Canada Revenue Agency Business Registration Portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-registration.html); GST/HST account administered via My Business Account / GST/HST NETFILE; BC provincial sales tax administered by BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA Business Registration Online service, and register for a GST/HST account if annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. The BN is obtained online using legal entity identification documents, partnership agreement, and a Canadian agent/representative contact; alternatively, complete Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). A simplified TIN is not separately issued for partnerships because the BN is the taxpayer identifier for GST/HST, payroll, and corporate income tax program accounts. Foreign general partner or designated representative must be identified for CRA correspondence; the LP should also register with the BC Corporate Registry as an extraprovincial limited partnership if it has a permanent establishment in BC, and separately register for BC PST if it makes taxable sales of consumer electronics in BC unless the supplier is considered to be the vendor and holds a BC PST number.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible—Canadian tax law treats limited partnerships as fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; the partnership is not a taxpayer and files only an information return (T5013), while each partner includes their allocable share of income/loss. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible—Canadian tax law treats limited partnerships as fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; the partnership is not a taxpayer and files only an information return (T5013), while each partner includes their allocable share of income/loss. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is treated as a separate person that must register, collect, and remit GST/HST on taxable supplies. The BC PST liability generally applies to the vendor of tangible personal property; an LP selling consumer electronics to BC consumers must be registered as a PST vendor when it has a physical presence, marketplace activity, or solicitation nexus in BC.

Available election forms

Check-the-box is not a Canadian federal mechanism; however, for Canadian tax purposes the LP may elect under subsection 96(3) to compute income at the partnership level in certain specified circumstances (not generally applicable). For US/Canada hybrid mismatch purposes, a foreign partnership classification election may be relevant under US 'check-the-box' rules, but Canadian CRA will rely on the legal form and the Canada-US tax treaty; Form T5013 Summary and Schedule 50 are required for foreign partners. A partnership may also elect under Income Tax Act s.10(1) to have the fiscal period end on a common calendar-year basis? No—partnership fiscal period is generally deemed to be the calendar year unless a business period election applies; for GST/HST, an election to use a fiscal year other than calendar year is available under s.238. If the LP is a 'selected listed financial institution' or has substantial Canadian GST/HST registrations, it may elect to file combined GST/HST returns with related registrants (s.225.2 election). No deemed-residency or substantive entity classification election is available to recharacterize the LP as a corporation for Canadian income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide annual gross revenues (excluding zero-rated supplies) from taxable supplies made in Canada triggers mandatory GST/HST registration; voluntary registration is permitted before threshold. For BC PST, registration is required before making taxable sales at retail in BC unless sales are below negligible volume and the person is not a 'vendor'; there is no general small-supplier PST registration exemption comparable to GST, and out-of-province online sellers who meet the 'permanent establishment' or specified nexus rules (including marketplace facilitators with BC sales) must register as BC PST vendors. For the provincial income tax component, an LP with a permanent establishment in BC is subject to BC corporate income tax on partnership income allocated to BC-resident or non-resident partners carrying on business through the BC permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under s.6 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies to a supply of goods exported by the vendor where the vendor receives satisfactory evidence of export within prescribed time limits and exports the goods directly, or delivers the goods to a common carrier/exports them as commercial cargo. For goods supplied to a consumer-electronics retailer that are exported from Canada, the supply is zero-rated only if the goods are exported as part of the supply chain and the documentation requirements in section 6 of Schedule VI and the Export of Goods Regulations are satisfied. Online sales of digital goods and remote services to non-residents may be zero-rated as an exported supply of intangible personal property or services where the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is not made in Canada. Sales of consumer electronics shipped from Canada to a foreign buyer are zero-rated for GST/HST, but BC PST zero-rating/export exemption requires proof that the goods are shipped out of BC by the vendor or common carrier to a destination outside BC. If the LP merely operates an e-commerce platform and does not physically export, the zero-rating may attach to the underlying transfer of goods by a fulfillment provider. A registrant exporting goods may also claim input tax credits (ITCs) for GST/HST paid on inputs; exporters must track export evidence and maintain a persistent audit trail to substantiate zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A Export Declaration or Canada Export Online / CAED), Bill of Lading / air waybill, Cargo Control Document (CCD), Commercial Invoice, Outward foreign remittance certificate / bank payment proof, Packing list, Delivery confirmation from common carrier, and if applicable, Form B3? (For exports from Canada a B13A is the export declaration; for third-country transit the originating export declaration from the prior country may be required).

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping at the federal level; however, every GST/HST registrant must issue an invoice containing prescribed information (vendor name, BN/GST/HST number, date, description, consideration, GST/HST charged) for taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30, and maintain electronic records in an accessible format for CRA audit. The CRA requires all GST/HST returns and payments to be filed electronically if annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1 million? (Mandatory electronic filing applies for GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1,000,000; all returns may be filed through GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account.) BC PST does not require e-invoicing; sellers must retain sales invoices, shipping documents, and exemption certificates for six years. There is no mandatory PEPPOL e-invoice network or clearance stamping regime in Canada; the federal government endorses the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and uses EDI-like electronic data interchange for procurement but not for tax fiscalization.

The CRA and CBSA may audit export documentation, and a failure to provide satisfactory evidence of export within two years after the supply can result in reassessment of GST/HST as payable, interest, and penalties under Excise Tax Act ss.280 and 285: penalties up to CAD 250 for failure to maintain books and records or up to greater amounts for false statements; a knowing misstatement constitutes tax evasion under s.327.1 and the Criminal Code; penalties range from 25% of the amount sought to 200% under s.285 for gross negligence, plus willful evasion criminal fines and imprisonment. BC PST reassessment and penalty for failure to collect or remit PST include 15% of the tax amount for repeated non-compliance (up to 100% for fraud) plus interest; furthermore, an exporter that claims an export exemption without shipping the goods outside Canada may be liable for uncollected PST, interest, penalties, and denial of the exemption. There is no administrative 'clawback' period shorter than the normal reassessment window: CRA can reassess within four years after the date of the return, or any time in the case of misrepresentation, fraud, or waiver; CBSA export compliance may also impose administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 4,000 per violation under the Customs Act for inaccurate export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating Canadian dollar with full capital and current account convertibility. There are no statutory exchange controls on the conversion of CAD, no prior approval for foreign direct investment remittances, and no limitation on dividend, interest, or partnership distribution repatriation to non-resident partners. The principal compliance hurdles are tax-related: Part XIII withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners (subject to treaty reduction under the Canada-US TIFA and other DTAAs), CRS/FATCA reporting, and documentation requirements for source-of-funds verification. CAD/USD and CAD/EUR liquidity are deep; conversion is executed at market rates with no surrender obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statuary timeline for the surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in onshore or offshore accounts; zero percent of export receipts must be converted to CAD. Statistical reporting to Statistics Canada is required for balance-of-payments purposes, but this creates no bank-level compliance obligation affecting the treasury profile.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML-related FX compliance administered by FINTRAC. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a complementary payment-processing rail for small-ticket e-commerce receipts, but Low as a primary banking substitute. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Platform, Mercury) do not hold Canadian bank charters, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot legally accept deposits of Canadian retail partner funds as a full-service credit institution. Any entity soliciting Canadian clients must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and maintain a Canadian compliance agent. A BC limited partnership should therefore use a Schedule I bank for treasury and held capital, while integrating Stripe/PayPal as payment acquisition rails rather than for settlement clearing.

Inbound capital injection from tax-haven or high-risk jurisdiction

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from tax-haven or high-risk jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Source-of-wealth evidence (contracts, invoices, bank statements preceding transfer), tax authority certificate of residency, evidence of legitimate commercial historical dealings, sanctions-screening reports (OFAC/UN/EU/DFATD), and any available Independent professional opinion confirming economic substance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound capital contributions via SWIFT MT103 to the CA_LP's operational account at a Schedule I bank, with beneficiary account in the legal name of the BC limited partnership and remittance information field reading 'CAPITAL_CONTRIBUTION_CA_LP'. No exchange-control permit is required. The bank will rely on the same KYC documentation used at onboarding to verify source of funds. For screening, include the BC Business Number (BN), corporate identification, and either 'Foreign Direct Investment' or 'Partner Capital Contribution' as the purpose code under the bank's internal BOP coding. If the capital injection qualifies as acquisition of control of a Canadian business, file a notice with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act. Ensure the wire is routed through the bank's New York or London correspondent only if the Canadian bank maintains a CAD/USD clearing arrangement; otherwise use a direct Canadian clearing path via LVTS.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a complementary payment-processing rail for small-ticket e-commerce receipts, but Low as a primary banking substitute. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Platform, Mercury) do not hold Canadian bank charters, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot legally accept deposits of Canadian retail partner funds as a full-service credit institution. Any entity soliciting Canadian clients must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and maintain a Canadian compliance agent. A BC limited partnership should therefore use a Schedule I bank for treasury and held capital, while integrating Stripe/PayPal as payment acquisition rails rather than for settlement clearing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the BC LP level (fiscally transparent pass-through entity; income is taxed in the hands of partners at their applicable rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation. A BC limited partnership does not legally pay dividends; distributions to non-resident partners representing allocated business income are not subject to this 25% rate but the non-resident partner must file a Part I tax return to report their Canadian-source income. If the LP distributes passive income (e.g., interest or royalties) to non-resident partners, the LP must withhold Part XIII tax at the applicable statutory/treaty rates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (annual aggregate value of cross-border non-arm's-length transactions; transactions below this threshold have a significantly reduced audit risk, but contemporaneous documentation should be prepared for any material related-party transaction to satisfy CRA section 247 records requirements).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the concept of 'active business carried on in Canada' to ensure an LP has real commercial substance. The partnership must maintain a physical presence, employees, inventory, and operational control in British Columbia. If the partnership is characterized as a 'specified investment flow-through' or as a mere conduit, income reallocation and GAAR penalties (25% of tax avoided) may apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Omission)

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239: fine of 50%–200% of the evaded tax and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. Section 162 penalties for failing to file information returns apply separately.

Director personal liability: The general partner (or its directors if a corporate general partner) may be assessed for the LP's unpaid tax under ITA section 227.1, including civil restitution, a CRA-recorded director's liability certificate, travel restrictions, and personal imprisonment for fraud convictions.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement under GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA section 245: denial of the tax benefit, reallocation of income to Canadian source, and a penalty of 25% of the avoided tax. The CRA may also reinstate withholding taxes on distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors/general partners who knowingly participated in the avoidance arrangement are jointly liable for the penalty amount and can be subject to asset freezing and a travel ban pending payment.
Transfer Pricing Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Section 247(2) adjustment to re-determine profits; section 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (minimum CAD 1,000) for insufficient documentation; daily accrual of interest and late-payment penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be liable under ITA section 227.1 for unremitted amounts, plus personal responsibility for wilful participation in TP adjustment amounts (in extreme cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion).
