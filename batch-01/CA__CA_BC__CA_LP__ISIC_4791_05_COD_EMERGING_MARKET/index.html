Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online portal at canada.ca, My Business Account for GST/HST and other program accounts

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) and open a GST/HST program account (type RT). Process: (1) assess mandatory registration status for non-resident carrying on business in Canada; (2) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and certain program accounts' through CRA Business Registration Online, by mail, or by fax; (3) provide partner identification and, if a representative is used, execute Form RC59; (4) supply non-resident entity registration details and foreign address; (5) receive BN and RT program account reference for GST/HST purposes. Additional accounts (payroll, import/export, excise) may be opened as needed.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign limited partnership must obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) and open a GST/HST program account (type RT). Process: (1) assess mandatory registration status for non-resident carrying on business in Canada; (2) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and certain program accounts' through CRA Business Registration Online, by mail, or by fax; (3) provide partner identification and, if a representative is used, execute Form RC59; (4) supply non-resident entity registration details and foreign address; (5) receive BN and RT program account reference for GST/HST purposes. Additional accounts (payroll, import/export, excise) may be opened as needed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election comparable to the U.S. regime. A Limited Partnership is automatically fiscally transparent for federal income tax unless it is a Specified Investment Flow-Through (SIFT) entity, in which case entity-level taxation applies on certain income. For GST/HST the partnership is treated as a distinct person and may register separately from its partners. Elections are limited to specific GST/HST streamlining provisions (e.g., closely related persons, qualifying branch) and partnership rollover provisions; no hybrid mismatch election exists, and the Income Tax Act anti-hybrid rules apply mandatorily to deny inconsistent cross-border tax outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); mandatory registration for non-residents carrying on business in Canada once this threshold is exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Item 1 of the Excise Tax Act when the supplier exports or delivers them outside Canada in the course of commercial activities and retains adequate proof of export. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, goods ordinarily situated in Canada, or personal services performed in Canada. Practical application requires documented export contracts, transport documents, customs lodgements, and payment evidence.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or authorized electronic export reporting
  • Bill of Lading, airway waybill, or other transport document showing delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales or e-commerce contract
  • Carrier's certificate or proof of physical export movement issued by the exporting carrier

No real-time fiscal clearance requirement, digital stamping mandate, or mandatory e-invoicing regime currently exists at the federal level. E-invoicing and EDI are voluntary. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 10,000,000 must file electronically via CRA NETFILE, My Business Account, or third-party XML APIs; no national real-time clearance API is imposed.

CRA audits export substantiation rigorously. If zero-rating is disallowed, GST at 5% (or HST where applicable) is reassessed retroactively with statutory interest. Civil penalties may be imposed under the Excise Tax Act, including the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax understated for false statements, and increased penalties for wilful misconduct. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion can result in substantial fines and imprisonment. Exporters bear the documentation burden for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange controls, no central-bank approval requirements for profit remittances, and no restrictions on partnership distributions to non-resident partners. Practical hurdles are limited to statutory withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on payments to non-resident partners, FATCA/CRS reporting, transfer-pricing documentation for cross-border intercompany transactions, and FINTRAC reporting for cross-border wires and cash remittances exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no obligation to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Export invoices may be denominated and retained in any foreign currency; however, all cash and wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); for foreign exchange surveillance, the Department of Finance, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) jointly provide the regulatory perimeter.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Payoneer, and Wise are permissible as ancillary payment and settlement rails, particularly for logistics remittance and cross-border disbursements, but they are not chartered banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and cannot serve as the primary liquidation account. They must register as Money Service Businesses with FINTRAC and, following the phased implementation of the Retail Payment Activities Act, become licensed payment service providers. A regulated Schedule I bank account in CAD remains mandatory for corporate tax remittances, statutory filings, and audit-grade documentation.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

High-velocity cross-border payment inflows other than card or wire remittances are received through a COD logistics model without transparent end-to-end transaction documentation.

Root cause: High-velocity cross-border payment inflows other than card or wire remittances are received through a COD logistics model without transparent end-to-end transaction documentation.

Defense evidence: Load 12 months of validated e-commerce sales records (transaction logs, refund ledger, fulfillment timestamps), logistics courier reports, warehouse delivery confirmation trails, merchant of record agreements, and the transaction routing diagram showing capture of funds at COD, custody by the logistics partner, and daily/weekly remittance to the LP's operational account.
Non-resident partners propose injecting large foreign-currency capital to a newly formed BC LP without demonstrable economic nexus or business substance.

Root cause: Non-resident partners propose injecting large foreign-currency capital to a newly formed BC LP without demonstrable economic nexus or business substance.

Defense evidence: Submit the executed LP agreement, partner contribution certificates, source-of-wealth statements with audited or certified financial references from each contributory partner, bank statements reflecting origin of funds, a detailed 3-year business plan, BC registration and corporate records, and draft tax filings evidencing the LP's anticipated income allocation.
Use of an offshore digital EMI (e.g., Airwallex or Wise account) as a concentration and settlement node for cross-border COD receipts before onshore clearing.

Root cause: Use of an offshore digital EMI (e.g., Airwallex or Wise account) as a concentration and settlement node for cross-border COD receipts before onshore clearing.

Defense evidence: Provide the EMI's money service business license or registration in its home jurisdiction, its AML/KYC program, a formal funds-flow schema including SWIFT traces from the EMI to the Canadian account, and contractual agreements confirming the EMI is used only as a payment utility and not as the primary custodian of operating capital.
Frequent outbound disbursements to logistics partners or trade creditors in high-risk emerging markets without written commercial contracts or commodity traces.

Root cause: Frequent outbound disbursements to logistics partners or trade creditors in high-risk emerging markets without written commercial contracts or commodity traces.

Defense evidence: Collate executed logistics services agreements, supplier purchase orders and invoices, customs/export declarations, delivery confirmation reports, and a board- (or GP-)approved disbursement policy setting limits, approval requirements, and reconciliation responsibilities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Deliver capital injections using SWIFT MT103 messaging from the contributing partner's bank to the BC LP's CAD account at a Schedule I bank. There is no statutorily prescribed purpose code; the payment narrative should read 'PARTNERSHIP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / EQUITY INJECTION' or use an internationally understood code such as 'THIRD PARTY / OPERATING FUNDS'. For each transfer exceeding CAD 10,000, FINTRAC reporting is mandatory. The receiving bank will complete a CPF (Client Payment Function) confirmation, run EDD/Source-of-Wealth verification, and assign an internal risk rating. Where any partner is a non-resident, the documentation package must include the partnership agreement, contribution certificate, and contractual explanation that no loan or disguised remuneration is being routed through the capital account.Trade settlements and operational disbursements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for global supplier payments, logistics remittances, and cross-border COD partner settlement, with all payment instructions carrying invoice reference and counterparty identification. Commercial letters of credit (typically irrevocable and confirmed/sight) are permissible for high-value or strategic procurement of goods, with the issuing Canadian bank acting as the confirming bank for emerging-market beneficiaries. Domestic, low-value payments may be made via EFT/ACH (e.g., payroll, HST remittance, utilities). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (combined federal 15% and British Columbia provincial 12% for general corporate income; note: the Limited Partnership is a pass-through entity and is not subject to entity-level corporate income tax—income is allocated to partners and taxed at their respective rates)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (domestic rate on dividends paid to non-residents; reduced under applicable tax treaties, e.g., 5% for substantial shareholdings, 15% for portfolio dividends under the Canada-US treaty).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending December 31, the T5013 must be filed by March 5 of the following year. For a non-calendar fiscal year, the return is due no later than the 5th day of the 3rd month after the fiscal year-end (i.e., 2 months and 5 days after year-end).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required if the total value of reportable transactions with non-resident related parties exceeds CAD 1 million in the fiscal year. Master File: required if the multinational enterprise group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 1 billion. These thresholds apply for taxation years beginning after 2022.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and the concept of 'carrying on business' require that the Limited Partnership have real economic substance (e.g., physical presence, employees, business activities) to be recognized as a valid partnership for tax purposes. A shell LP with no substance may be disregarded, and income may be reattributed to partners or the entity may be deemed a corporation under the 'association' rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Criminal Code. Penalties: fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the tax evaded plus interest.

Director personal liability: For a Limited Partnership, general partners have unlimited liability and may be personally liable for the partnership's tax debts. Limited partners may lose limited liability if they participate in management or engage in fraudulent conduct. Directors of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under the Income Tax Act (s. 227.1) if they fail to withhold and remit employee source deductions or GST/HST.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA may disregard the partnership structure under GAAR, reassess the income to the partners directly, and impose a 50% penalty on the tax benefit. Additionally, the partnership may be denied treaty benefits and subject to a 25% withholding tax on all distributions.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of the corporate general partner may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax liabilities and penalties. Travel bans may be imposed under the Income Tax Act for unpaid taxes exceeding CAD 50,000, and criminal charges may be pursued for tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities per violation. Criminal penalties for willful non-compliance include fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly or negligently fail to implement compliance programs. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) may issue compliance orders and impose personal fines.
Transfer Pricing Penalties

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net adjustment to the taxpayer's income (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 1 million) or 10% of the gross adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million). Additionally, interest and other penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be held personally liable for transfer pricing penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in the non-compliant transactions. The CRA may also impose a 50% gross negligence penalty on the partnership.
