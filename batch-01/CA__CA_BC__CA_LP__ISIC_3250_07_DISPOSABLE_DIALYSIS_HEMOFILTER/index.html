Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a British Columbia limited partnership; capital contributions may be deferred under the partnership agreement. No escrow account is mandatory for ordinary manufacturing partnerships, but escrow or statutory trust requirements will apply if the partnership is land-holding, a securities brokerage, or otherwise designated regulated activity.

Funding & unlock path: Fire marshal certificate of compliance issued under the BC Fire Code certifying fire separation, extinguishing systems, and storage of sodium hydroxide and isopropyl alcohol; Technical Safety BC electrical and ventilation inspections for radiation-free equipment; structural load inspection and anchoring certificate for extruders and potting machinery; and WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety clearance covering process noise, lock-out/tag-out, and solvent exposure controls.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; administered through the Pacific Region Tax Services Office (British Columbia); digital portal: My Business Account (MABA) and Business Registration Online (BRO) at www.canada.ca/taxes

E-government filing portal

A BC Limited Partnership obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a federal GST/HST registration account (RT program) by applying through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Accounts). Foreign/non-resident applicants also file Form RC402C (Non-Resident Business Registration) and Form RC4599 (GST/HST Application). The BC incorporation/registration certificate under the Partnership Act (BC) and the limited partnership agreement must accompany the application. CRA assigns the RT0001 identifier; the General Partner must provide its own SIN, BN, or foreign tax ID. No separate income tax TIN is issued to the partnership; partners report allocated income on their own returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; fiscally transparent for federal Income Tax Act purposes (the LP is not a taxpayer; income/losses flow to partners). The non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income through subsection 2(3). For GST/HST purposes under the Excise Tax Act, the LP itself is a 'person' capable of registering in its own BN; it is eligible for zero-rating of exported dialysis consumables and for the recovery of input tax credits (ITCs). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; fiscally transparent for federal Income Tax Act purposes (the LP is not a taxpayer; income/losses flow to partners). The non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income through subsection 2(3). For GST/HST purposes under the Excise Tax Act, the LP itself is a 'person' capable of registering in its own BN; it is eligible for zero-rating of exported dialysis consumables and for the recovery of input tax credits (ITCs). Provincial BC PST generally does not apply to exported goods.

Available election forms

There is no Canadian 'check-the-box' election; classification follows the CRA administrative view (a US LLC is generally treated as transparent if fiscally transparent in the US) and is subject to Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Part XXIV of the Income Tax Regulations). Relevant elections: (i) election under subsection 216 of the Income Tax Act for non-resident partners' Canadian rental/business income to be taxed on a net basis; (ii) joint GST/HST election under subsection 150 of the Excise Tax Act for related corporations; (iii) International election on Form FT2 (s. 216) and US Form 8832 entity classification election to fix the US federal treatment of the Limited Partnership; (iv) fiscal period election (ITA s. 249) to align the partnership's fiscal year with the partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) triggers mandatory GST/HST registration; suppliers below the threshold may voluntarily register to claim ITCs. Once registered, the exporter must remain registered for at least 12 months. PST registration (BC Municipal Affairs) is required only for taxable sales to BC consumers, not for exported goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 5 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act for supplies of tangible personal property (including PES hollow-fiber dialysis hemofilters) exported by the supplier in the ordinary course of business, provided the goods are delivered to a destination outside Canada. Services qualifying as 'services provided to a non-resident' under section 7 Schedule VI may also be zero-rated if not rendered in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property. The exporter must maintain contemporaneous documentary evidence that both legal title and physical possession transferred to the non-resident outside Canada. The 0% rate entitles the LP to claim ITCs on manufacturing inputs; BC PST is not payable on the export sale.

  • CBSA B13A Customs Export Declaration filed via eManifest Export (mandatory where declared value exceeds CAD 2,000)
  • Customs export acknowledgement / border clearance copy evidencing goods departed from Canada
  • Commercial invoice in foreign currency naming the non-resident purchaser and stating terms of sale (EXW/FOB/DAP)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or Canadian truck waybill showing destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Swift MT103/TT wire receipt evidencing settlement in foreign currency
  • Insurance and freight documents corroborating the transfer of title and risk outside Canada
  • All manufacturing quality records and lot-level traceability files for the exported hemofilter batches

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-approval of invoices. The CRA accepts standardized business-to-business e-invoices using the Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 framework and conventional PDF/EDI formats. Exports must be reported electronically through the CBSA eManifest/ACI system when the goods require customs export declarations (declared value generally exceeding CAD 2,000); B13A export declarations and all relevant invoice/transport records must be retained for six years to substantiate zero-rating.

The CRA will allow GST zero-rating and ITC refunds only upon contemporaneous export documentation. Failure to substantiate exports within the normal reassessment window (4 years, extended to 10 years for misrepresentation) triggers reassessment at 5% GST, interest, and late-filing penalties. Fraudulent or grossly negligent claims attract penalties under sections 280.1, 285, and 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act (fine up to CAD 25,000 and 50% of the understated tax for gross negligence), together with NCB (net-tolerance) adjustments and potential increase in duties under the Customs Act. The 180-day FX repatriation benchmark is enforced by financial institutions under AML/KYC policies and is treated by the CRA as documentary support for genuine export sale proceeds; failure to repatriate in that window may lead to recategorization of the transaction as an unsubstantiated supply and clawback of the zero-rating and ITCs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible current account regime under the Foreign Exchange Act with no capital controls on routine trade or profit remittances. Capital account transactions are liberalized subject to Investment Canada Act notification thresholds for direct investments above CAD 5 million and FINTRAC reporting for all FX flows exceeding CAD 10,000. Profit repatriation to foreign limited partners is unrestricted and requires only standard corporate tax filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or forced conversion requirements apply to export receipts. Canadian exporters may freely retain foreign currency proceeds and convert at market rates through authorized dealers. All transactions above CAD 10,000 trigger mandatory FINTRAC reporting for AML purposes with no conversion ratio mandates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore EMI cross-border services face strict PCMLTFA registration and FINTRAC oversight. Platforms such as Wise or Revolut may process inbound wires and outbound remittances for Canadian LPs but require integration with domestic banking rails for full corporate account functionality and cannot replace licensed Canadian bank accounts for trade settlements.
