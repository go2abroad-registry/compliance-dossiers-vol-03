E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Limited Partnership under the British Columbia Limited Partnership Act with the BC Corporate Registry to obtain the BC LP registration number. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and the GST/HST program account (RT), either online through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by paper Form RC348 where the LP is non-resident-controlled; the same BN is used to activate the CBSA import/export (RM) program account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada applies statutory legal-form classification rather than a US-style check-the-box regime. A BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96; each partner includes its share of partnership income or loss. Available statutory elections: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 96(3) to adopt a non-calendar year-end with partner consent; (ii) election under ITA s. 216 for a non-resident partner to be taxed on a net Canadian-source basis instead of gross Part XIII withholding; (iii) GST/HST closely-related-persons joint election under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 to treat internal supplies as non-taxable; and (iv) ETA input-tax-credit allocation elections. Hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.6) and limited-recourse at-risk rules (ITA s. 96(2.2)-(2.4)) apply mandatorily and cannot be overridden by any foreign entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies of goods and services (small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)); registration becomes mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, and voluntary registration is advisable for exporters to claim input tax credits. BC PST: no general minimum-sales threshold; PST registration is required before the first taxable retail sale in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA s. 9(1), a supply of goods is zero-rated (0% GST with full input tax credit entitlement) where the LP ships, transfers or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, or to a carrier or common carrier for direct export, provided the goods physically leave Canada within a commercially reasonable period (CRA administrative practice: approximately 60 days). Under ETA s. 7, supplies of services to non-residents are zero-rated where the service is for consumption wholly outside Canada and does not relate to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada. Self-heating exothermic meal units qualify as exported tangible personal property where the sale contract, commercial invoice, Incoterms and shipping evidence name a foreign consignee. BC PST does not apply where the goods are delivered to the purchaser outside British Columbia or outside Canada.

  • CBSA B13-A Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED)/eManifest portal, or evidence of the low-value exemption for United States-bound shipments below CAD 2,000
  • Through Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the foreign consignee and evidencing the date of shipment and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and sale contract setting out payment terms, Incoterms and non-resident purchaser identity
  • Inward payment evidence from the foreign buyer: SWIFT MT103 or bank wire/TT receipt, which serves as the functional equivalent of an inward remittance certificate since Canadian banks do not issue a statutory inward remittance certificate
  • Packing list, cargo manifest and freight-forwarder receipt evidencing physical exit from Canada
  • Foreign customs entry documentation (e.g., U.S. CBP entry summary) confirming arrival in the destination country

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, SAF-T filing, or API-based invoice validation. GST/HST invoices are acceptable in paper or electronic (PDF/EDI) form provided they contain the prescribed information under the Input Tax Credit Information (GST/HST) Regulations (SOR/91-45) and the records are retained for six years per ETA s. 286(1); simplified invoice rules apply to low-value supplies. No CRA pre-clearance or digital stamp is required before issuing an invoice.

CRA audits export zero-rating by verifying that the goods actually left Canada and that the LP retained the prescribed documentary evidence under ETA ss. 9(2) and 169(4). Where zero-rating is denied, the supply is re-characterized as taxable at 5% GST, the corresponding input tax credits are clawed back, and the LP becomes liable for net tax, arrears interest and penalties. Knowingly or grossly negligent false statements in GST/HST returns attract the ETA s. 285(1) penalty of the greater of CAD 5,000 and 25% of the net-tax understatement, and the ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of any resulting income-tax understatement at the partner level. Fraudulent export schemes may be prosecuted as criminal offences under the ETA and the Income Tax Act; where a foreign-affiliate or transfer-pricing element is present, ITA s. 247 permits the CRA to adjust the export consideration and impose consequential penalties. The normal four-year reassessment limitation period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, carelessness or wilful default is established. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-currency repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is adopted as the treasury benchmark for SWIFT/TT receipts and not as a legal requirement.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. CAD floats freely against all currencies; there are no exchange controls, no dual-rate systems, no convertibility licences, no capital-export limits, and no domestic-credit ring-fencing. A British Columbia limited partnership may hold capital contributions, revenues, and reserves in CAD or any foreign currency without BoC approval. Distributions, loan repayments, interest, royalties, and liquidation proceeds may be repatriated to non-resident partners without exchange-control authorization. The only frictions are fiscal: (i) allocations of Canadian-source income to non-resident partners are subject to Canadian Part I tax, and under s. 153(1)(p) of the Income Tax Act the partnership must generally withhold and remit provisional tax on distributions or amounts credited to non-resident partners (waivers and treaty-based reductions may apply, e.g., the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement lowers certain passive-income withholding to 10%); (ii) the partnership must file the T5013 Partnership Information Return (used for both federal and BC provincial reporting under the Tax Collection Agreement); (iii) inbound foreign direct investment by non-Canadians is reviewed under the Investment Canada Act, but a greenfield capital injection into a newly formed BC LP engaged in manufacturing does not constitute an 'acquisition of control' and will not be a notifiable transaction, provided no existing Canadian business is acquired and no SOE investor control is involved; and (iv) for CUSMA/WTO investors, notifiable acquisitions arise only above the CAD 1.34 billion enterprise-value threshold for direct investments in 2024, which is inapplicable to a greenfield self-heating food-manufacturing operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada; BoC), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), is the sovereign issuer of Canadian currency and the operator of the country's monetary policy framework. Canada abolished its Exchange Control Act in 1951 and maintains a free-floating CAD with no foreign-exchange control authority, no exchange-licensing regime, and no approval requirement for cross-border capital movements. Associated regulators: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises prudential and liquidity standards of deposit-taking institutions; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border EFT and AML/CFT reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Payments Canada operates the Lynx RTGS system; and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export declarations. No BoC, OSFI, or FINTRAC pre-clearance is required for a BC Limited Partnership to receive foreign capital or to convert CAD into foreign currency.
