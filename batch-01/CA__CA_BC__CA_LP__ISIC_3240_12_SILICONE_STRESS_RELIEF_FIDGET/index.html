Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); for British Columbia provincial sales tax, the Competent Authority is the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (eTaxBC portal).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or foreign entity aiming to carry on activities through a BC limited partnership must request a CRA Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST Program Account by filing Form RC1 (by mail/fax or through an authorized representative via My Business Account). The BN is the national tax identifier. Separate BC PST registration with the Consumer Taxation Branch/eTaxBC is required before making taxable sales in BC, unless a small-seller exemption applies. Foreign LPs without a Canadian permanent establishment may still register voluntarily to claim input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; a BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent under Canadian federal and BC tax law. The LP does not pay income tax; income/losses pass through to partners, including non-resident partners, who are taxable on Canadian-source income under Part I and are subject to Part XIII withholding on certain passive distributions or gross payments.

Available election forms

No CRA analogue to the US check-the-box election exists; a limited partnership cannot elect entity-level taxation. Limited partnership is inherently flow-through. Available elections include: a non-resident partner's section 216 election to file net-basis Canadian rental income, the joint venture election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST, and related-person elections to exempt supplies of financial services under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies connected with Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST; BC PST registration is required before making taxable sales in BC, subject to the BC small-seller exemption where taxable sales to BC customers do not exceed CAD 10,000 in the preceding 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate goods exported from Canada under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, including silicone sensory fidgets, when the goods are sold to a non-resident and shipped outside Canada as evidenced by acceptable commercial and customs documentation. Services performed on goods destined for export or supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Canada may also be zero-rated. Zero-rated supplies retain full input tax credits.

  • Commercial invoice
  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A/B13B)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank FX settlement evidence)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA does not require API-based invoice pre-clearance; electronic invoices are accepted if they contain the prescribed GST/HST invoice data. BC PST invoices must show seller registration data and transaction details, but there is no state-run real-time clearance requirement.

CRA may disallow zero-rating, reassess the taxpayer, and claw back any refunded GST/HST together with interest if export documentation is insufficient or if payments are not settled within the expected trade period. Gross-negligence penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act can reach 25% of the understated net tax; the Income Tax Act imposes penalties for false statements and tax evasion, including criminal prosecution. BC may separately reassess PST amounts within the statutory limi period and apply penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a full float currency regime with no general exchange controls under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act. Both current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible into Canadian dollars and foreign currencies. There are no restrictions on profit remittances by non-resident partners, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax on distributions, treaty relief eligibility, and FINTRAC transaction reporting for amounts equal to or above CAD $10,000. For inbound capital injections and outbound trade disbursements, CAD and USD clearing proceed through Payments Canada's Lynx/ACSS systems and correspondent banking networks without Central Bank approval. No material convertibility hurdles exist for silicone fidget toy manufacturing, importing food-grade silicone, or repatriating net cash flows to partnership investors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements exist for export proceeds in Canada. There is no timeline for converting foreign currency receipts into Canadian dollars, and no Central Bank conversion ratio is imposed. Exporters, including British Columbia limited partnerships, may retain export proceeds in foreign currency bank accounts, invoice in any currency, and freely decide whether to hold funds offshore or repatriate them, subject to FINTRAC cross-border reporting obligations and Canadian income tax reporting for foreign property interests (i.e., T1135 information return) where aggregate specified foreign property exceeds CAD $100,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada, acting jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions; the Department of Finance Canada sets national monetary and exchange rate policy; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) oversees AML/ATF surveillance of cross-border transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Limited / Medium for purely offshore digital payment facilitators. A non-resident fintech or electronic money institution (EMI) cannot open settlement accounts with Canadian banks unless it is registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and, if operating within British Columbia, licensed under BC's Money Services Business Act administered by Consumer Protection BC. Offshore digital EMIs that do not hold a Canadian MSB registration can only provide ancillary referral or technology services; they cannot serve as the final clearing institution for CAD-denominated transaction flows. The preferred structure for the fidget toy venture is to open primary banking accounts directly with a Schedule I Canadian bank and use platform-based payment processors (e.g., Shopify Payments, Stripe, PayPal Business) solely as collection channels that settle onward into those Canadian bank accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners should be executed by SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire into the LP's CAD or USD operating account at a Tier 1 Canadian bank. The remittance instruction must identify the beneficiary as the BC limited partnership, the account number, the account institution/transit number, and set the payment details as 'Capital Contribution' or 'Subscription Payment' referencing the specific partnership agreement clause. The originating bank must include sufficient correspondent routing details for Canadian clearing, typically via a US intermediary bank for USD wires and via SWIFT affiliate for CAD. No exchange control approval is required. For amounts above CAD $10,000, the receiving Canadian bank records the transaction for FINTRAC reporting. The LP should separately hold a Capital Escrow Account if funds are staged for capital commitments; such an account is supported by RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, and CIBC. All inbound transfers must be reconciled against capital contribution schedules and partner KYC files, and the transfer purpose code should be aligned with the foreign remitter's bank requirement, generally '6000 - Equity / Other Capital Contribution' for common global banking classifications.Outbound trade-related disbursements, including payments to overseas mold makers, raw silicone suppliers, logistics providers, and 3PL warehouses, should be made by SWIFT MT103 form for telegraphic transfers, or by commercial letters of credit issued at the instruction of the LP through its Canadian bank for higher-value and documentary trade. For CAD domestic payments, Payments Canada's ACSS/Lynx clearing rails apply; for USD cross-border payments, the Canadian bank's US clearing network routes via a US correspondent bank. For every trade disbursement, the LP must maintain an underlying contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading/waybill, and customs documentation. Payments to foreign related parties require transfer pricing support under the Income Tax Act s.247. Profit distributions to non-resident partners must be executed through SWIFT MT103 with a message narrative of 'Limited Partnership Distribution / Profit Share', and the LP must issue NR4-TA-NR slips and apply Part XIII withholding at 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined for corporate partners (federal general rate 15% + BC general rate 12%). A BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent; the LP itself pays no income tax, and each corporate partner is taxed on its allocable share.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (ss.212(1)(a) and 212(2)) for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. For the BC limited partnership, distribution of profits to limited partners is a partnership allocation, not a dividend; however, any deemed dividend from a Canadian corporate partner to its foreign parent is subject to the 25% statutory rate, reduced by an applicable DTA (US treaty: 5% on 10% corporate ownership, otherwise 15%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal periods ending December 31, the return is due March 31 of the following year; for non-calendar fiscal periods, due within five months after the fiscal period-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return due within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. For non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada, the same six-month rule applies, and the Part I balance-due date for non-CCPC corporations is two months after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate gross value of controlled transactions with non-resident non-arm's-length parties triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under s.247(4) of the ITA. In addition, CRA expects a Master File and Local File where the multinational group's annual Canadian revenue exceeds CAD 50 million; Form T650 must be completed and filed for those large MNE groups.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR); however, the doctrine is applied through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245), the 'sham' doctrine, and the OECD Multi-Lateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test. A BC limited partnership carrying on silicone manufacturing must demonstrate genuine commercial substance: physical production site, employees, equipment, independent contractual arrangements, and active business decisions in British Columbia. A shell LP with no operations and no real economic activity will be disregarded or restructured, and treaty benefits will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Statements

Statutory sanction: Under s.239 of the ITA, summary conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment: fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties under s.163(2) add 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, interest, and penalties if they sign false returns or direct culpable conduct; CRA may assess director liability under s.227.1, and a criminal conviction can result in travel bans, restitution orders, and incarceration.
Sham / Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA can apply GAAR to deny tax attributes, reassess all years on a net basis, and impose a 50% gross-negligence penalty; a 'sham' finding exposes the whole arrangement to unlimited assessment. Under BC's Partnership Act, a sham may be void as against the Crown, and relevant licences may be cancelled.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell general partner may be held personally liable for the partnership's unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under ITA s.227.1 and Excise Tax Act s.323; courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraudulent conveyance, issue travel bans, and seize personal assets.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 1,000,000 for corporations; Criminal Code s.462.31 imposes up to 10 years' imprisonment for laundering proceeds of crime. BC's Land Owner Transparency Act and the federal 'beneficial ownership' amendments (Bill C-42) impose per-day fines for contumacious non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability for knowingly failing to report suspicious transactions, civil restitution for laundering, travel bans pending prosecution, and forfeiture orders; personal exposure is not capped and can exceed the company's assets.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failures

Statutory sanction: A transfer-pricing adjustment is made to the arm's-length amount; a 10% gross-negligence penalty applies to the adjustment amount under s.247(3). Failure to file Form T106 triggers penalties under s.233.6, and interest and arrears are compounded. CRA may also deny the deduction for expenses paid to the related non-resident.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the T2 or T106 with false or unreasonable documentation can incur director liability for the corporation's penalties and may be subject to restitution claims; gross-negligence findings can lead to CRA criminal referral and travel restrictions.
Withholding Tax and Part XIII Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The payer remains liable for the full unwithheld amount under s.227(8); failure to remit attracts a 10% penalty under s.227(9) plus daily interest. Each missing NR4 slip carries a penalty of CAD 100 per day up to CAD 2,500. Repeat offenders face an increased 20% penalty and imprisonment for wilful non-remittance.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable for all unremitted Part XIII amounts and penalties under s.227.1; the CRA can issue a director's liability assessment before the partnership is wound up, pursue bank accounts, and obtain freezing orders.
