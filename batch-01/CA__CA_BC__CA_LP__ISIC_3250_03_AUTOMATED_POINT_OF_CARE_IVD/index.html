Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a BC limited partnership. Initial capital is established by the Limited Partnership Agreement; no escrow account is required by the Partnership Act unless the parties agree otherwise.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal tax administration via 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) - import/export program accounts; BC Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) - BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST); BC Registries - limited partnership registration under the BC Partnership Act. Filing portals: CRA My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE, Represent a Client, CBSA CARM.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Limited Partnership with BC Registries (extra-provincial registration if formed outside BC). (2) Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts via CRA BRO or Form RC1; the LP is a deemed 'person' and registers in its own name. (3) Open the GST/HST account (program code 'RC') - mandatory once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters; voluntary registration is commercially essential for exporters to claim input tax credits and issue zero-rated invoices. (4) Open the CBSA import/export account (program code 'RM') for customs declarations. (5) File the T5013 Partnership Information Return when the LP has 6 or more partners, a corporate partner, non-resident partners, or gross revenue above CAD 2,000,000. (6) Designate the general partner as CRA filer; non-resident LPs must appoint a Canadian resident representative.

(2) Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts via CRA BRO or Form RC1; the LP is a deemed 'person' and registers in its own name. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; classification follows legal attributes, with LPs fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96(1). Available elections: ITA s. 96(3) partnership salary/allowance allocation; ITA s. 97(2) tax-deferred property rollover into the LP; ETA s. 273(5) qualifying-partnership election (nil-consideration inter-partnership supplies); GST Quick Method election (ETA s. 225). Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) apply automatically with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148(1)) - aggregate value of taxable supplies made in Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, including supplies of associates; non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available and recommended from start-up for exporters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of immunoassay analyzers and microfluidic cartridges are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part I, s. 1 of the Excise Tax Act when sold to non-residents and exported by or on behalf of the supplier outside Canada within 60 days of ownership transfer; installation, training, and field services to non-residents qualify under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 9 where not performed for a resident of Canada or for use of property in Canada. Exported goods are exempt from BC PST. The LP must be a GST registrant to issue zero-rated invoices and claim input tax credits, generating a typical quarterly net-refund position for an export-dominant manufacturer.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Canadian G13/B13A Export Declaration filed electronically via the CBSA Electronic Commerce Client Requirements Document (ECCRD) portal or a licensed customs broker/forwarder, with HS classification (e.g., HS 3822 reagents, HS 9027 analyzers) and value declaration
  • Bill of Lading - through ocean or airway bill / Cargo Control Document naming a non-Canadian destination port and non-resident consignee; supplementary packing list and forwarder instructions
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - no statutory Canadian IRC exists; CRA accepts as the functional equivalent a bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice, foreign-exchange contract, or supplier ledger matched to remittance evidencing settlement in convertible currency from the non-resident buyer within normal trade terms

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, government digital stamping, or mandatory API clearance at federal or BC level. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework (adopted 2023); CRA operates the GST/HST Registry API for counterparty verification. Ordinary PDF/EDIFACT invoices are compliant; the LP must retain full invoice metadata and supporting export documentation for CRA audit purposes.

CRA applies GST/HST Memorandum 4-4-1 evidentiary standards and risk-scores zero-rating claims by medical-device exporters; insufficient documentation triggers reassessment at 5% GST plus daily-compounded prescribed interest (ETA s. 280) and failure-to-file penalties (ETA s. 280.1). False or negligent claims attract the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the overstated claim (ETA s. 285(1)); gross negligence on income attribution attracts a 50% penalty (ITA s. 163(2)); deliberate fraud is prosecutable under ETA s. 327(1) and ITA s. 239(1) with fines of 50%-200% of tax evaded and imprisonment. The four-year reassessment limitation is suspended indefinitely for misrepresentation (ETA s. 298(2); ITA s. 152(4)). Export receivables outstanding beyond 180 days without documented trade terms trigger transfer-pricing and imputed-income scrutiny (ITA s. 247), and circular export structures may be recharacterized as domestic taxable supplies if goods never physically leave Canada.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, management fees, capital distributions and trade settlements may be conducted freely in CAD or any convertible currency. The only relevant hurdles are statutory tax withholding on certain cross-border payments and FINTRAC reporting thresholds for electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in foreign-currency bank accounts and convert them at commercial rates. Large cross-border electronic transfers remitted to or from Canada must be reported to FINTRAC when the amount is C$10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary and financial-system authority; foreign exchange administration and cross-border transaction reporting are governed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, with prudential oversight of banks by OSFI.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-moderate. Offshore fintech EMIs can provide auxiliary low-value settlement, multi-currency treasury visibility and expedited supplier payments, but they cannot act as the primary liquidity-clearing institution because they are not LVTS members and cannot clear CAD directly. A foreign-exchange service provider that deals in FX or remits funds from Canada must be registered as a money services business with FINTRAC. The EMI channel is legally viable only as a parallel rail; the mandatory treasury backbone must reside at a tier-1 Canadian Schedule I bank.
