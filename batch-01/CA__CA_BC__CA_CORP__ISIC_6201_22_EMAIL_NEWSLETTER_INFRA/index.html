Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Registration and filing portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance via eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

The BC corporation obtains a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with CRA via BRO, by mail, or by fax. The application may simultaneously create program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll) as applicable; CRA then issues a BN confirmation letter containing the 9-digit BN and program-account identifiers (e.g., 123456789 RT0001). GST/HST registration is mandatory once annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 and may be backdated to the month the threshold was exceeded; voluntary registration below the threshold is recommended to claim input tax credits. A non-resident corporation without a Canadian presence may use the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration process (no threshold) and must appoint a Canadian representative or provide a Canadian mailing address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Canadian federal tax law. Relevant elections include: (i) the closely related corporation nil-consideration election under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156; (ii) the Income Tax Act s. 85(1) rollover election for transferring property into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; and (iii) the Income Tax Act s. 216 election for non-residents with Canadian rental or timber income. Hybrid mismatch rules applicable to cross-border arrangements (implementing BEPS Action 2) operate by automatic deduction denial without taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (measured on a quarterly trailing four-quarter basis) triggers mandatory GST/HST registration for resident suppliers; voluntary registration is permitted below this threshold. Non-resident digital-economy suppliers using the simplified regime must register without any threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported email-infrastructure/SaaS services may be zero-rated at 0% GST under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: s. 7 (services to non-residents) and s. 10 (intangible personal property). Conditions: the recipient is a non-resident and is not registered for GST/HST; the supply is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; and the service is not rendered to a person resident in Canada. Where the foreign customer is GST/HST-registered, zero-rating remains available if the supply forms part of the customer's commercial activity. Comprehensive non-residency and delivery evidence must be retained to substantiate the zero-rated treatment and the resulting input tax credit refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or EDI export report) where physical goods or media are exported; not applicable to pure digital SaaS delivery
  • Bill of Lading or shipping/transport documentation for goods, or the signed Master Subscription Agreement / service order evidencing the export service contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, FX credit advice, or bank statements documenting receipt of foreign currency consideration
  • Non-resident status evidence: registered business address, IP geolocation, foreign tax identification number/VAT ID, and written non-residency representation
  • Service delivery substantiation: API usage logs, SMTP delivery reports, bandwidth/usage analytics, and support tickets matching invoiced SaaS fees

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based digital stamping, and no continuous transaction control mandate. GST/HST invoices over CAD 30 must contain prescribed particulars (supplier BN, date, description, tax rate and amount); simplified invoices are permitted where total consideration is CAD 150 or less (CAD 100 in HST provinces). Peppol-based e-invoicing and the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework are voluntary procurement standards, not tax clearance obligations.

CRA scrutiny of zero-rating claims tests whether the recipient was a non-resident at the time of supply. Insufficient documentation triggers reassessment at 5% GST, disallowance of input tax credits, compounded daily arrears interest under ETA s. 280, and penalties under ETA s. 286 (false statements or omissions: the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the amount sought to be evaded). Misrepresentation extends the four-year reassessment limitation period and may attract gross-negligence penalties under Income Tax Act s. 163.2, director/officer liability under ETA s. 323, and criminal prosecution under ETA s. 285 and the Criminal Code.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under Canada's IMF Article VIII obligations, with a freely floating exchange rate and no exchange-control licensing, conversion quotas, or capital-registration approvals for either current or capital account transactions. Inbound equity capital, shareholder loans, and cross-border service revenues may remit into Canada freely. Profit remittance is constrained only by taxation: non-resident dividend recipients are subject to Part XIII withholding of 25% under the Income Tax Act, typically treaty-reduced to 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders holding 10% or more in a resident corporation (e.g., Canada-United States Tax Convention). No practical FX hurdles apply to the repatriation of export proceeds for ISIC 6201 software subscription services.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency in onshore or offshore accounts. Software and SaaS export invoices may be settled in USD, EUR, CAD, or other freely tradable currencies at commercial exchange rates. The only compliance obligations are: (i) FINTRAC reporting by regulated financial intermediaries of cash transactions at or above CAD 10,000 and of suspicious transactions; (ii) taxpayer disclosure of specified foreign property via form T1135 when aggregate value exceeds CAD 100,000; and (iii) customs-statistical export data reporting under the Canadian International Trade Single Window, which is not administered as a foreign-exchange control.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank, with foreign exchange policy coordination through the Department of Finance Canada; prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (Canada); anti-money laundering and terrorist financing surveillance by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); domestic clearing and settlement infrastructure under Payments Canada (Lynx and ACSS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as an auxiliary collection rail; Low as the primary treasury account. Canada does not recognise a foreign e-money institution licence as a passport. An offshore digital EMI providing money-transfer or retail payment services to Canadian clients must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) supervision takes operational effect, registered with the Bank of Canada as a retail payment service provider. An unregistered overseas wallet (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, or similar) may be used to receive foreign-currency balances, but it cannot legally substitute for a Canadian Schedule I bank account for capital escrow, GST/HST compliance, vendor payroll, or dual-signatory treasury control. Recommended blueprint: maintain the anchor operating account at a Tier 1 Canadian bank and deploy a FINTRAC-registered EMI or a Canadian MSB-sponsored arrangement only for FX velocity and last-mile cross-currency settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection must be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder, parent treasury, or single-purpose funding vehicle directly into the beneficiary's account at the Canadian Schedule I bank. The wire must carry the beneficiary's legal name, the Canadian bank's SWIFT BIC (RBC: ROYCCAT2; TD: TDOMCATT; BMO: BOFMCAM2; Scotiabank: NOSCCATT; CIBC: CIBCCATT), and the full Canadian routing set comprising the 3-digit institution number, 5-digit transit number, and account number. Purpose-code mapping: classify subscriptions under the BPM6 direct-investment category as 'equity injection into a Canadian business' where funds are paid against issued share capital; classify shareholder advances as 'intercompany loans / other investment' backed by a demand promissory note. The treasury KYC file must hold the board resolution approving the capital structure, the share subscription agreement or loan agreement, the remitting entity's identification and bank statement, the issuing bank's MT103 execution confirmation, and a source-of-funds memorandum. For injections at or above CAD 1M, proactively answer the bank's enhanced due-diligence questionnaire and Statistics Canada's direct-investment survey obligations. Wires from sanctioned jurisdictions are non-settiable and must be intercepted by pre-funding screening.Operating disbursements clear through four primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for all cross-border payments to software vendors, infrastructure providers, agencies, and licensors in USD, EUR, or GBP; (ii) Payments Canada Lynx for same-day CAD high-value payments and CAD payroll distributions; (iii) EFT/ACH networks for routine Canadian and US-dollar disbursements, including Stripe payout settlement which enters the operating account in CAD or USD via Stripe's sponsoring bank or Canadian clearing partner; and (iv) commercial standby or documentary letters of credit (UCP600) reserved for material technology acquisitions or enterprise contract performance guarantees where bank-intermediated settlement assurance is commercially required. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as an auxiliary collection rail; Low as the primary treasury account. Canada does not recognise a foreign e-money institution licence as a passport. An offshore digital EMI providing money-transfer or retail payment services to Canadian clients must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) supervision takes operational effect, registered with the Bank of Canada as a retail payment service provider. An unregistered overseas wallet (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, or similar) may be used to receive foreign-currency balances, but it cannot legally substitute for a Canadian Schedule I bank account for capital escrow, GST/HST compliance, vendor payroll, or dual-signatory treasury control. Recommended blueprint: maintain the anchor operating account at a Tier 1 Canadian bank and deploy a FINTRAC-registered EMI or a Canadian MSB-sponsored arrangement only for FX velocity and last-mile cross-currency settlement.
