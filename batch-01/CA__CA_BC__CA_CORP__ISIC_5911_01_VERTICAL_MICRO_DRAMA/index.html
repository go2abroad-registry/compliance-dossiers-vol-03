Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC) — national tax administration for corporate income tax, GST/HST, and payroll; Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca and CRA My Business Account are the operational portals. BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch administers the BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through the eTaxBC vendor portal; BC Registry Services and the OneStop Business Registry handle BC extra-provincial registration for federally incorporated entities.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate a Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or BC Business Corporations Act; a 9-digit Business Number (BN) is automatically issued by CRA upon filing with Corporations Canada. (2) If federally incorporated, file BC extra-provincial registration via BC OneStop before carrying on business in British Columbia. (3) Activate CRA program accounts through My Business Account: 'RC' for corporate income tax, 'RT' for GST/HST, and 'RP' for payroll where applicable. (4) Register as a BC PST vendor through eTaxBC before making taxable supplies of digital content, streaming subscriptions, or entertainment services to BC consumers. (5) A foreign non-resident entity entering Canada without Canadian incorporation must apply for a BN via BRO or Form RC1, provide certified foreign incorporation documents (with English/French translation), nominate a Canadian address for service, and register for non-resident GST/HST under ETA s. 240 unless the small-supplier threshold applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under ITA Part I taxed on worldwide income (ITA ss. 2(1), 3, 250); no pass-through election exists. The relevant sub-status is Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) under ITA s. 125(7), which permits the small business deduction on active-business income up to CAD 500,000 (combined federal/BC rate approximately 11%) versus the general combined rate of approximately 27% (15% federal plus 12% BC). Territorial scope is worldwide taxation for Canadian-resident corporations and Canada-source taxation for non-residents with a permanent establishment; Part XIII withholding applies to certain cross-border payments. No tax-exempt zone status is available to a BC-based corporation.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists for Canadian corporations; CRA classifies entities by legal form and applies OECD hybrid mismatch neutralizing rules (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3) that deny or defer deductions on hybrid and reverse-hybrid arrangements without an affirmative election. Elections available to the micro-drama group include: (i) GST/HST closely-related corporation election (ETA s. 156) to remove GST/HST on intercompany administrative charges; (ii) GST/HST joint venture election (ETA s. 273) for co-production ventures; (iii) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s. 237), available to small suppliers; (iv) ITA ss. 85 and 86 rollover elections on incorporation and restructuring; and (v) CCPC status under ITA s. 256, which is mechanically determined rather than elective. For US counterparties, W-8BEN-E or W-8IMY documentation and treaty-based reduced Part XIII withholding elections apply to dividend, royalty, and licensing flows.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in any four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148); registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, with voluntary registration available to claim input tax credits. Non-resident digital suppliers of subscription services (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing) must register under the CRA simplified non-resident GST/HST framework when taxable supplies to Canadian subscribers exceed the same CAD 30,000 threshold. BC PST: no general small-supplier registration threshold exists — a vendor making taxable sales of digital goods, streaming subscriptions, or enumerated entertainment services to BC consumers must register with eTaxBC before the first taxable supply into BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For tangible goods: zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V, ETA where goods are exported as part of the supply and leave Canada within the statutory benchmark of 60 days after delivery, with documentary evidence retained. For the core digital micro-drama business: foreign-distribution revenue settled through Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, or Stripe offshore is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7(1) where the supply of streaming, copyright licensing, or other intangible services is made to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and the recipient is outside Canada at the time of supply; place-of-supply rules for digital goods and services attribute supply location based on subscriber billing address, IP geolocation, and payment BIN data. Revenue from Canadian end-users remains taxable at 5% GST, with BC PST applying separately where the subscriber is in British Columbia. The registered supplier claims input tax credits on the export supply chain and recovers net GST refunds through the GST/HST return; non-resident suppliers may claim the unregistered foreign supplier GST rebate where applicable.

  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill and carrier ACI e-manifest cargo control number for any physical export leg

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API tax-authority stamping, or digital stamping mandate applies to GST/HST or BC PST. Mandatory electronic reporting applies through CRA and eTaxBC: (i) GST/HST registrants with annual taxable revenues above CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically via CRA's GST/HST Internet File Transfer; (ii) corporations with gross revenue above CAD 1 million must file T2 returns electronically through NETFILE or an approved service provider; (iii) BC PST returns must be filed via eTaxBC; and (iv) all tax records, platform settlement reports, and export documentation must be retained in Canada in an electronically readable format for at least six years (ETA s. 286; ITA s. 230). CRA may exercise e-audit powers to remotely inspect cloud-hosted financial and platform records.

CRA audits zero-rating claims against platform settlement data, subscriber geolocation, and transfer-pricing documentation. If export substantiation fails, the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Canada and the net tax is reassessed with arrears interest from the original filing date. Penalties escalate by severity: (i) civil penalty for false statements or omissions in GST/HST returns under ETA s. 285; (ii) gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax sought to be avoided, applying the ITA s. 163(2) standard, with retrospective clawback of input tax credits and denial of refunds across the six-year reassessment window (extended indefinitely on misrepresentation or fraud); (iii) BC PST interest and penalties reassessed under the Provincial Sales Tax Act for unreported BC subscriber sales; and (iv) criminal prosecution for willful evasion under ITA s. 239 and corresponding ETA provisions, carrying fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility. Since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act (1951), Canada has imposed no exchange controls, no prior-approval regime, and no restrictions on inbound equity, shareholder loans, app-store settlement inflows, royalty payments, or dividend repatriation. Canada is fully compliant with the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Current Invisible Operations. The only structural hurdles are tax-based: outbound dividends and royalties attract Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%, reduced under Canada's ~90 tax treaties) and must satisfy transfer-pricing and thin-capitalization documentation under sections 247 and 18(4) of the Income Tax Act. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely; there is no dual exchange rate, no surrender obligation, and no official settlement approval for treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, no minimum domestic deposit period, and no holding restriction on export receipts, foreign-currency app-store settlement, or distribution royalties. Practical compliance constraints instead arise from: (1) FINTRAC's requirement to report every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, and any sequence of transfers totaling CAD 10,000+ within 24 hours; (2) CRA Form T1135 disclosure when specified foreign property has an aggregate cost exceeding CAD 100,000; (3) retention of documentary evidence of the commercial purpose of all cross-border flows to support CRS/FATCA classifications and the entity's corporate-banking AML profile.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange administration; regulatory competence is shared by the Department of Finance Canada (Currency Act, exchange-rate policy), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank conduct, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border reporting and AML/CFT supervision.

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium (supplementary rails only). No Canadian statutory approval is required, but the compliant protocol is: (1) adopt a board resolution and subscription agreement naming CA_CORP; (2) identify the remitting shareholder and its UBO in SWIFT field 50K; (3) state 'PAID-IN CAPITAL' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' in field 72; (4) route the credit through a Schedule I correspondent (RBC, TD, BMO, CIBC, or Scotiabank) in CAD or USD; (5) remember that FINTRAC reports international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more; (6) file the Investment Canada Act notification if any shareholder is a non-Canadian establishing a new Canadian cultural business (motion-picture production and distribution are cultural businesses that may attract a net-benefit review); (7) issue the corresponding shares within 30 days and update the BC corporate registry; (8) retain the complete source-of-funds dossier for seven years.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 wire transfers in CAD and USD for talent, post-production, casting agencies, international distributors, and marketing vendors, with Part XIII withholding certificates attached for non-Canadian beneficiaries. For procurement above CAD 250,000 with non-investment-grade counterparties, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit governed by ICC UCP600 issued by the corporate bank. Apple and Google settle inbound as merchant-of-record payouts into the designated USD sub-account; cross-border USD sweeps to U.S. group entities flow via Fedwire/ACH through the bank's U.S. affiliate (e.g., RBC Bank Georgia N.A., TD Bank N.A., BMO Harris N.A.). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium (supplementary rails only). Canada does not issue EU-style electronic-money institution licences; non-bank cross-border payment firms must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Deposit-taking in Canada is restricted to institutions authorized under the Bank Act (s. 545) and provincially regulated credit unions. Foreign EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer are legally available for vendor payouts and international disbursements, but they cannot serve as the entity's principal operating account, payroll bank, or FX hedging counterparty. A U.S.-domiciled fintech banking partner may be used to receive Apple/Google USD settlement flows, provided the Canadian treasury ledger consolidates the balance and all FINTRAC, CRS, and T1135 reporting obligations are discharged from Canada.
