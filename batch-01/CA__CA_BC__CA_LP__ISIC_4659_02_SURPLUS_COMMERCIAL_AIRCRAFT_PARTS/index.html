Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration; federal entity registration and tax account procurement are effected through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html). Provincial BC counterpart: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — eTaxBC portal (etax.gov.bc.ca) for Provincial Sales Tax (PST); and BC Registry Services, Corporate Online (corporateonline.gov.bc.ca) for registration of the underlying limited partnership.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the BC limited partnership by filing a Statement of Registration through BC Corporate Online to obtain the provincial LP registry number; Canadian limited partnership registration is exclusively provincial — no federal entity registration exists. (2) Apply to CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) for the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN), electing the RT (GST/HST) program account — mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 (ETA small-supplier threshold); add the RP payroll account and open the partnership's T5013 information-return account as applicable. (3) Where the LP is owned by non-residents, file Form RC1 with the 'non-resident' indicator and register for GST/HST under ETA s. 241(1); the LP itself is the GST/HST registrant even though income is fiscally transparent to partners. (4) Register separately for BC PST through eTaxBC and obtain a PST account number before making taxable retail sales in BC; the CRA BN is not used for PST purposes. (5) Adopt a fiscal year-end; a partnership must generally use a calendar year-end (ITA s. 249.1) unless an election to use a qualifying non-calendar period is available. CRA issues a BN confirmation (Form RC52) within approximately 15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC limited partnership is mandatorily fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96: the LP is not a taxable entity and each partner includes its allocable share of income or loss. Territorial scope: Canada taxes non-resident partners only on Canadian-source income, including income from a business carried on through a permanent establishment in Canada, which the BC-based aviation spares distribution business will constitute; resident partners are taxable on worldwide income. The LP nonetheless acts as the GST/HST registrant and BC PST collector, and files the annual T5013 information return.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity-classification election; partnership transparency is statutory and irrevocable. Available elections relevant to this LP: (1) ITA s. 216(1) election — non-resident persons deriving Canadian-source rental or leasing income from tangible property (e.g., rotable or avionics leasing to Canadian carriers) may elect to file a net-basis Part I return in lieu of the 25% gross Part XIII withholding (treaty-reduced where applicable); (2) ETA s. 241(3) election — a non-resident registrant may appoint a resident GST/HST agent and file returns using an inventory-based system; (3) ETA s. 178 joint election for inter-company supplies between qualifying related registrants — unavailable where the partnership's 'specified member' passive rules apply; (4) ITA s. 249.1(2) partnership fiscal-period election — permitted only where the LP meets the 'qualifying business activity' test; (5) anti-hybrid rules (ITA ss. 94A–94C) override any foreign classification mismatch, so a foreign check-the-box transparent entity feeding the Canadian LP receives no Canadian recognition.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (ETA s. 148(1) small-supplier threshold); the LP's aviation-parts trading volumes will exceed the ceiling, making mandatory registration effective on the transaction that crosses the threshold. BC PST: no monetary threshold for a vendor with a place of business in BC; remote sellers without a BC physical presence must register once taxable sales into BC exceed CAD 10,000 per calendar year. Aircraft parts distribution through a BC LP triggers both registrations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating (0%) applies under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA: (a) s. 1 — supply of tangible personal property to a non-resident recipient who exports the goods 'as soon as is reasonably possible' without further processing in Canada, with title passing to the non-resident; (b) s. 5 — supply of goods delivered by the vendor to a common carrier for direct delivery outside Canada; (c) s. 8 — supply of a service to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada (e.g., aviation spares brokerage, logistics coordination, and offshore MRO administrative services). For surplus certified commercial aircraft parts, the export sale additionally qualifies only if the LP holds contemporaneous written evidence of export (ETA s. 180; CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.1, Proof of Export). Domestic BC sales to Canadian air carriers are taxable at 5% GST plus 7% PST. For PST, the direct-export exemption applies when the vendor ships the goods to the non-resident's foreign destination, supported by a valid exemption certificate; PST is a retail sales tax with no input-credit mechanism, so the LP cannot recover PST embedded in its costs unless it holds a BC PST Vendor/Transfer permit.

  • Canada Export Declaration — CBSA Form B13A / CAED / ACE export reporting for shipments exceeding the CBSA reporting threshold
  • Commercial invoice in Canada Customs format (HS code 8803, Incoterms, legal name and foreign address of the non-resident buyer)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the common carrier evidencing physical departure of the parts from Canada to the foreign consignee
  • Swift Wire Transfer Confirmation and bank remittance records evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer (functional equivalent of an inward remittance certificate)
  • Certified proof of export prepared by a licensed Canadian customs broker or the exporter's own signed Certificate of Export
  • Packing list, insurance certificate, and airworthiness certification records (FAA Form 8130, EASA Form 1, or Canadian AMO release) evidencing the commercial nature and value of the surplus aircraft parts

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API tax-authority authorization exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-Canadian B2B E-Invoice Standard (PEPPOL framework; pan-canada-einvoice.ca). Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns through CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE, due within one month of the end of the reporting period (monthly filing applies to large registrants with taxable supplies exceeding CAD 10 million); PST returns are filed through eTaxBC. Invoices must be issued no later than 15 days after the supply (ETA s. 223(1)), and all export substantiation records must be retained for six years.

CRA treats export zero-rating as high-risk. If the substantiation bundle is incomplete or the export timeline is not commercially reasonable, CRA retroactively denies zero-rating and assesses the LP as a registered supplier for the uncollected 5% GST under ETA s. 222, plus interest and penalties: ETA ss. 285/285.1 penalties of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the unreported tax; ETA s. 327 criminal prosecution on summary conviction (fines up to CAD 25,000 and/or 12 months imprisonment) or indictment (fines up to CAD 200,000 and/or 5 years imprisonment). The normal GST reassessment window is four years from the filing date (ETA s. 298), extended to eight years for misrepresentation or fraud. On the provincial side, the BC Consumer Taxation Branch may audit and assess uncollected PST within four years, plus a 10% late penalty and prescribed interest, whether or not the federal assessment succeeds. From an income-tax perspective, a failed zero-rating claim recharacterizes the revenue as Canadian-source business income allocable to partners; non-resident partners become liable for Part I tax on the reassessed amount, gross-negligence penalties under ITA s. 163(2) of up to 50% of the understated tax, and Part XIII 25% withholding on any rent, royalty, interest, or management fees paid in the structure (treaty-reduced where eligible). No statutory Canadian FX repatriation deadline exists; the 180-day benchmark is adopted as the contractual treasury standard for evidence of receipt of proceeds and aligns with the ETA requirement that goods be exported as soon as reasonably possible.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they participated in or acquiesced to the evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may apply.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they directed or authorized the sham arrangement; possible civil restitution and prohibition from serving as directors.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for individuals up to CAD 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly failed to comply; travel bans and seizure of assets may be imposed.
