Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses.html) and British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, via eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca/etaxbc/).

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership transacting in Canada must first obtain a 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) as its statutory tax ID. Use Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or CRA's online Business Registration service, designating the required program accounts: RP for GST/HST, RC for income tax, RT for payroll, and RM for import/export. The BN with RP suffix constitutes the GST/HST registration number. For British Columbia, the limited partnership must separately register for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through eTaxBC if it carries on business in BC or makes taxable sales in BC; the BC PST account may be linked to the CRA BN or issued as a separate consumer-tax number. A non-resident limited partnership must submit proof of existence and registration in its home jurisdiction, appoint a Canadian agent or address for service, and, if applicable, provide the names and addresses of all partners. CRA generally issues the BN and GST/HST account within four to six weeks by mail; eTaxBC PST registration is typically immediate online.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - A BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes and is not itself a taxable entity; each partner is taxed on its allocable share. However, for GST/HST purposes, the limited partnership is a distinct 'person' and must maintain its own BN/GST/HST registration. For BC PST, the limited partnership is the vendor responsible for collection and remittance.

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' entity-classification election. For a limited partnership, the principal statutory elections are: (1) ITA s. 97(2) tax-deferred rollover election on transfers of property to or by the partnership; (2) fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(1) where all partners consent, subject to statutory restrictions; and (3) GST/HST closely-related joint election under ETA s. 273 for certain reorganizations. None of these allow a limited partnership to elect corporate taxation. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2 to 18.6 are mandatory and do not provide elective entity classification; cross-border entity treatment is determined by CRA administrative interpretation of foreign law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: $30,000 CAD of worldwide taxable supplies in the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold). BC PST: for a BC-registered limited partnership with physical presence in BC, there is no monetary threshold; registration is mandatory when the vendor carries on business in BC. For remote out-of-province sellers and marketplace facilitators, BC applies an economic-nexus threshold of C$10,000 of taxable B.C. sales for specified e-commerce and taxable supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may claim 0% GST/HST on goods sold by way of sale and physically exported from Canada as part of the sale. Eligibility is founded principally on ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1: the supplier must sell and ship the goods outside Canada directly or through a common carrier, and retain documentary proof of export obtained within a reasonable time after shipment (CRA commonly expects supporting evidence within 60 days and record retention for six years). A supply of associated services, if any, may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 where the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered under GST/HST and the service is performed wholly or substantially outside Canada. Canada does not statutorily prescribe an FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure used in this dossier reflects the banking/export-credit benchmark and audit clean-documentation standard. BC PST also exempts goods delivered by the vendor to a destination outside BC, provided the same export documentation substantiates the exemption.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic export report where applicable)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading / air waybill / highway eManifest evidence
  • Carrier-certified proof of delivery to a destination outside Canada with destination and export timestamp
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-currency inward remittance advice documenting receipt of export proceeds
  • Purchase order and supporting evidence of non-resident buyer status

Not applicable. Canada and British Columbia currently impose no real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or statutory electronic invoicing mandate. GST/HST registrants must issue valid tax invoices for taxable supplies containing the BN, transaction date, vendor name, description, and tax charged; paper and electronic formats are equally accepted. BC PST retailers must issue sales invoices or receipts and file returns through eTaxBC, but no pre-clearance or digital stamping is required. For customs purposes, exporters must file electronic export reports through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) eManifest/export reporting system, which is not a tax clearance mechanism.

CRA rigorously audits export zero-rating claims. If contemporaneous proof of export is absent or insufficient, the supply is reclassified as taxable in Canada, and the applicable GST/HST (5% federal plus BC PST where relevant) is clawed back from the original reporting period, with daily compounded interest and late-filing or failure-to-register penalties. Civil penalties for false statements or gross negligence in a GST/HST return include ETA s. 285.1 penalties of the greater of $100 and 25% of the net tax misstated; at the partner level, ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties may apply at 50% of tax understated. CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period for carelessness, neglect, or wilful default, and there is no limitation period where fraud or misrepresentation is established. Criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 289 or ITA s. 239 can result in fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years, in addition to BC Sales Tax Act penalties and interest for improperly claimed PST export exemptions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under IMF Article VIII; Canada has no exchange control legislation and does not restrict current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance by a Limited Partnership is not FX-gated, but non-resident partner distributions may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada); treaty relief is available under the Canada-US Tax Convention. FATCA and CRS reporting declarations are mandatory at onboarding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies under Canadian law. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings without restriction. Reporting obligations are limited to Statistics Canada Balance of Payments surveys and FINTRAC Large Transaction Reports (CAD 10,000 or more) under the PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-to-High only as a supplemental payments rail (e.g., Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer) and not as a primary treasury account.
