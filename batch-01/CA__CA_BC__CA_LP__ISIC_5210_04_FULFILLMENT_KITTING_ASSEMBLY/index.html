Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada for GST/HST and federal income tax, via the CRA My Business Account portal; the British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, for British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST), via the BC OneStop and BC eTax portal.

E-government filing portal

Register the foreign-owned limited partnership under the British Columbia Partnership Act through BC Corporate Online to obtain the BC limited partnership registration number; then apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1A or CRA Business Registration Online. The CRA BN is the Canadian statutory TIN/registration number and supports separate program accounts for GST/HST (RT) and income tax. Separately register with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST through BC OneStop or BC eTax. Non-resident partners must provide their foreign legal identifiers and complete any required Canadian partner withholding information declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Register the foreign-owned limited partnership under the British Columbia Partnership Act through BC Corporate Online to obtain the BC limited partnership registration number; then apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1A or CRA Business Registration Online. The CRA BN is the Canadian statutory TIN/registration number and supports separate program accounts for GST/HST (RT) and income tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid entity election is available under Canadian law. The BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and BC income tax purposes, with partners computing their proportional shares, while the partnership itself is a separate registrant for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act. Partners may jointly make partnership income elections under the Income Tax Act, including section 96 partnership return and income allocation elections, and the partnership may make administrative GST/HST registration elections. Hybrid mismatch and treaty allocation rules operate as a matter of law and are not achieved through elective entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: threshold is CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, whichever triggers first (small supplier rule). BC PST: no small-supplier threshold; registration with the BC Ministry of Finance is mandatory before making taxable retail sales, leases, or taxable service supplies in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies to tangible goods sold by the supplier and delivered to a non-resident outside Canada or physically exported by the supplier with documentary proof of removal. A kitting or assembly service physically performed on tangible personal property located in British Columbia at the time the service is supplied is generally not zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI section 9(2)(b), even if the kit is later exported. The subsequent sale of the kitted goods to a non-resident with delivery outside Canada may be zero-rated where the exporter retains complete export evidence. BC PST does not apply to goods sold for delivery outside BC when shipped by common carrier or otherwise removed as an export and documented. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure stated in this dossier is the commercial and AML due-diligence benchmark used to substantiate export settlement evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A/CAED or CBSA electronic export declaration in Canada)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail, or highway carrier document evidencing physical export from Canada)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or MT103 proof of receipt of export settlement from the non-resident customer)

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate exists. CRA accepts electronic invoices and accounting records where legible, complete and accessible; Pan-Canadian public-sector e-invoicing and PEPPOL standards are procurement-specific and not a general registration condition. BC PST is self-assessed and reported through periodic tax returns without an e-invoice clearance or approval process.

CRA may retroactively disallow zero-rating and deny GST/HST refunds or input tax credits where the exporter cannot substantiate physical removal of goods from Canada, reassessing tax, interest and penalties within statutory periods generally of three to four years, extended for misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties may include Excise Tax Act false-statement penalties, gross-negligence penalties under the Income Tax Act, prosecution under ETA section 239 (with fines up to double the evaded tax and imprisonment), and BC PST reassessment with late-payment penalties and compound interest. Clawback liability may also attach to third-party logistics operators whose incomplete or inaccurate export documentation causes the export zero-rating claim to fail.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. A British Columbia limited partnership may generally receive foreign investment, make partner capital contributions, borrow cross-border, pay suppliers, receive customer proceeds, distribute partnership cash, and remit profits without prior foreign-exchange approval. Principal practical hurdles are bank KYC and beneficial-ownership verification, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence, sanctions screening, tax characterization and withholding analysis, transfer-pricing support, partnership allocation documentation, non-resident information reporting, and contractual or lender restrictions. Canada does not generally require a central-bank approval for profit repatriation or impose a statutory dividend-remittance limit. A partnership distribution remains subject to partnership-law documentation, solvency and creditor considerations, the partnership agreement, tax compliance, and any applicable withholding or treaty analysis. Canadian-dollar conversion is normally available through authorized financial institutions, subject to market liquidity, dealer onboarding, and transaction monitoring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement for exporters to surrender export proceeds to an authorized dealer, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the entity's discretion. Banks may require transaction purpose, invoice, bill-of-lading, customer, and beneficial-owner information and may apply internal settlement or documentation deadlines. Tax, customs, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, accounting, and contractual reporting obligations remain applicable. If goods are exported, customs and trade reporting may be required independently of foreign-exchange conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration that imposes exchange controls, mandatory conversion, or routine approval requirements for ordinary commercial remittances. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Canada Border Services Agency, the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, and applicable sanctions authorities may impose separate AML, sanctions, prudential, tax, or reporting requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium to High for payments and foreign-exchange execution, but not a substitute for a Canadian primary operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution may provide collection, multicurrency wallets, virtual accounts, FX conversion, and cross-border payouts only within the scope of its home-jurisdiction authorization and the applicable Canadian regulatory model. The provider must not represent itself as a Canadian deposit-taking institution unless federally authorized. Canadian payment-service activities may trigger registration and operational obligations under the Retail Payment Activities Act, while money-transfer and foreign-exchange activities may trigger FINTRAC money-services-business registration and AML-program requirements. Customer funds may be safeguarded or held in trust under the provider's structure, but balances may not have Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation protection. Availability of Canadian local rails, including EFT or Interac-related services, depends on the provider's Canadian regulatory and sponsor-bank arrangements. Use Airwallex or a comparable provider only after confirming Canadian entity eligibility, funds safeguarding, settlement account ownership, data access, sanctions controls, chargeback procedures, insolvency treatment, and whether the account is in the partnership's legal name.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner capital, shareholder funding, or affiliate funding from an account held in the named funder's legal name through a regulated bank or compliant payment institution into the Canadian partnership's account. Use an accurate payment narrative such as limited-partnership capital contribution, partner loan, or intercompany loan; do not describe capital as sales revenue or trade proceeds. Canada generally has no mandatory FX purpose-code system for ordinary inbound wires, but the receiving bank may require internal purpose selection, sender relationship, agreement references, and source-of-funds evidence. Pre-notify the bank for material or unusual amounts, provide the executed partnership or loan documents, reconcile the wire to the capital ledger, and retain MT103 or equivalent confirmation. Verify sanctions, AML, tax, transfer-pricing, thin-capitalization, interest-withholding, beneficial-ownership, and partnership allocation consequences before funding. While struck off, the limited partnership cannot carry on business. Restoration requires court approval, rectification of filings, and payment of restoration fees in the CAD 150–260 range. The general partner may also be subject to summary conviction penalties for non-compliance.

Turnover: No statutory independent audit is required for a British Columbia limited partnership under the Limited Partnership Act or the Partnership Act. A certified audit becomes mandatory only when (i) the partnership agreement expressly requires it, (ii) a lender, customer, or investor covenant imposes it, or (iii) CRA, in an examination under sections 230-231.1 of the Income Tax Act, determines the books and records are inadequate and requires a certified report. Revenue, asset, or partner-count thresholds alone do not trigger an audit for an unlisted BC limited partnership.
