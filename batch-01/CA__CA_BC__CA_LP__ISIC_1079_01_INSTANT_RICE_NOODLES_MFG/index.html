Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) for GST/HST and federal income tax administration via the My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online (BRO); BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administered through the eTaxBC portal; BC Corporate Registry (Corporate Online) for extra-provincial registration of the Limited Partnership.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership extra-provincially with BC Corporate Registry (Corporate Online) to obtain a provincial registration/compliance number. Step 2: Apply to CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and open the required program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll, if employees), and RC (corporate/scientific research — noted for information purposes; partnerships use T5013 program account). Step 3: Register for BC PST with the BC Ministry of Finance via eTaxBC — no registration threshold. Step 4: Obtain GST/HST registration automatically when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary immediate registration is available. No Canadian residency or citizenship requirement; a foreign-managed LP may register using its Canadian registered office. Timeline: BN issued instantly once the BRO application is submitted; PST account typically issued within 5–10 business days. The BN is the composite tax identifier used for all federal tax filings, including GST/HST returns, payroll remittances, and the T5013 Partnership Information Return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A British Columbia limited partnership is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (ITA). The partnership is not a taxpayer; each partner is assessed on their share of partnership income or loss. The LP files only the T5013 Partnership Information Return. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada on their share of Canadian-source partnership business income and may be subject to Part XIII withholding or, where applicable, treaty relief under the Canada-US Tax Convention. For GST/HST purposes the LP itself is the registrant and must collect and remit GST/HST on taxable supplies.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style IRC Section 7701 check-the-box election; pass-through treatment is the statutory default for a limited partnership. Relevant elections include: (i) ITA s. 96(1)(f) joint designation of a Canadian partnership for income allocation; (ii) ITA s. 216 election by a non-resident partner to report Canadian-source rental income on net basis (not applicable to manufacturing income); (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election between closely related corporations and partnerships to treat intra-group supplies as made for nil consideration; (iv) GST/HST Quick Method election for eligible small businesses; (v) optional election to have a partnership fiscal period end on December 31. A foreign limited partnership may be recharacterized as a corporation under the anti-deferral rules in ITA ss. 94.1/94.2 only if it holds foreign investment properties, a fact pattern absent for an operating instant-noodle manufacturing LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in gross worldwide taxable supplies during a single calendar quarter or over the four preceding calendar quarters (small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148). BC PST has no small-supplier exemption; PST registration is mandatory from the first taxable retail sale in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods by a GST/HST registrant are zero-rated under section 6 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier exports the goods by shipment to a destination outside Canada, and the goods are exported before the earlier of the day the consideration becomes due and the day on which the supplier issues the invoice for the supply. For the instant rice noodle manufacturer, packaged retort noodles shipped to US distributors and buyers in other non-Canadian destinations qualify for 0% GST provided documentary evidence is retained. Domestic sales of basic groceries to Canadian wholesalers may also be zero-rated as food for human consumption under Schedule VI Part III; however, single-serving snack-style noodle products may be recaptured as 'snack food' and become taxable at 5% GST plus 7% PST. Services performed by the LP for non-residents are zero-rated under section 7 of Schedule VI unless they are rendered in respect of real property situated in Canada.

  • B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) system, CAED++ or approved EDI channel
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of the goods within 60 days after the consideration is due or paid
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order stating Incoterms 2020 and the foreign destination
  • Proof of consideration received — SWIFT/TT inward remittance advice, letter of credit, bank statement, or payment confirmation from the non-resident buyer
  • GST/HST zero-rated export record prepared under ETA s. 169(2) documenting supply number, value, date of export, and purchaser identity
  • T5013 Partnership Information Return with supporting schedules confirming the allocation of export income to partners

Canada does not mandate real-time electronic fiscal clearance for invoices (no CFDI-equivalent digital stamping regime). The CRA has adopted the voluntary Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing standard based on PEPPOL/UBL effective April 2024, initially for business-to-government transactions; commercial paper or PDF invoices remain lawful. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST NETFILE) applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million; smaller registrants may file paper returns. BC PST returns and remittances are filed through the eTaxBC portal; no digital stamping or API-side fiscal clearance is imposed on packaged food manufacturers.

Canada imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day pipeline figure is a conservative internal control aligning with the CRA's normal reassessment cycle and the ITA s. 15(2) shareholder-loan repayment rule. The GST/HST zero-rating claim requires that the goods be exported within 60 days; otherwise GST/HST must be charged and remitted. Failure to do so triggers CRA reassessment within the standard 4-year limitation period (indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud). Penalties include: (i) ETA s. 280 late-filing penalty of 5% of unpaid net tax plus 1% per full month, capped at 10% (doubled to 20% if a prior assessment was made within the preceding 3 years); (ii) ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated income/tax; (iii) criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239 and ETA s. 327, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; (iv) under the Customs Act, seizure and forfeiture of goods valued at up to 50% of their value for false or misleading export declarations. Retrospective clawbacks will apply full prescribed interest, compounded daily, from the original due date of the GST/HST amount.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility exists on both capital and current accounts. There are no Canadian exchange-control restrictions on capital injections, profit distributions, or liquidation proceeds payable to non-resident limited partners. Repatriation is unrestricted, subject only to fiscal formalities: (i) Part XIII withholding tax at 25% statutory rate applies to the non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source allocable profit, treaty-reduced (e.g., 15% under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement at 10% participation or above); (ii) returns of contributed capital are excluded from withholding where the LP's capital account accurately documents contributions and distributions; (iii) the LP must file Form NR301/NR4 reporting for non-resident partners. Under the Investment Canada Act, direct foreign acquisition of control of a food-manufacturing business above the annual WTO-investor threshold (approximately C$1.3B-C$1.4B, indexed annually) triggers a net-benefit review; routine limited-partner capital injections fall far below that threshold. British Columbia imposes no provincial currency controls or remittance restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and manufacturers may freely retain export earnings in foreign-currency accounts. Compliance obligations are record-keeping and reporting only: (i) a Canadian-resident partner holding foreign property (including a foreign bank account or partnership interest) with an aggregate cost exceeding C$100,000 must file Form T1135 with the Canada Revenue Agency; (ii) any cross-border electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more received or sent by the Canadian bank triggers a FINTRAC EFT report; (iii) physical goods exports require CBSA B13 export declarations and, where applicable, Global Affairs Canada permits under the Export and Import Permits Act; (iv) no Bank of Canada, OSFI, FINTRAC, or CFIA rule imposes a surrender deadline or conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) holds monetary authority as the sole note-issuing institution for the Canadian dollar. Residual foreign-exchange and payment-system powers rest with the Minister of Finance (Department of Finance Canada) under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Prudential regulation and licensing of banks are administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing supervision is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no separate Foreign Exchange Administration bureau and imposes no exchange-control licensing regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM as a supplementary disbursement channel; NOT viable as the sole treasury, escrow, or letter-of-credit rail. Legal constraints: (i) the term 'EMI' is not a recognized licence class under the Bank Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); only incorporated banks and credit unions may take deposits in Canada; (ii) a foreign digital EMI must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC before initiating payment activity in Canada; (iii) since November 2024, retail payment functions also require registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); (iv) cross-border payment platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex type) can be engaged for low-value payroll or supplier disbursements provided the operator holds valid FINTRAC registration, but Schedule 1 banks will not rely on an EMI as a clearing counterparty for documentary credits or high-value settlements. Preferred architecture: primary Canadian resident bank account for capital escrow and LC issuance, with an EMI/BPaaS corridor used only for ancillary offshore supplier wires.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (combined federal 15% + British Columbia general corporate rate 12%) for a taxable corporate partner; the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by a taxable Canadian corporation to a non-resident; for a Limited Partnership, profit distributions to partners are not dividends and are taxed under Part I at the partner level, with no Part XIII withholding on active business income allocations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 must be filed when the aggregate value of reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required for all reportable transactions regardless of size if selected for audit.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245 of the Income Tax Act disregards transactions lacking economic substance, and the BC Partnership Act requires a real registered office and commercial presence. Treaty benefit may be denied to an LP that is a conduit without substantive business activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under s.239 of the Income Tax Act: up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to 200% of evaded tax; civil penalty under s.163(2) equals 50% of the understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partner officers are jointly and severally liable for source deductions and GST/HST under s.227.1; personal restitution, criminal prosecution, and CBSA restrictions on departing Canada may follow.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Cancellation or strike-off under the BC Partnership Act; CRA may apply GAAR to deny treaty benefits and reassess with up to 50% gross negligence penalties; non-resident partners may lose limited liability.

Director personal liability: Limited partners who participate in control lose limited liability protection; directors of the general partner may be assessed personally for unpaid corporate and payroll liabilities and face penalties for false filings.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative fines up to CAD 500,000 and criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code carrying up to 14 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors of the general partner may be personally prosecuted; personal travel bans and extradition can be pursued for foreign directors involved in laundering.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust to the arm's-length range under s.247(2), assess 10% transfer pricing penalty on the adjustment amount (minimum thresholds), interest, and potentially 50% false-statement penalties.

Director personal liability: The 10% transfer pricing penalty is assessed on the entity, but directors of the general partner who sign or authorize inaccurate returns may be liable under s.163.2 for corporate misrepresentations; criminal tax evasion liability may attach in egregious cases.
