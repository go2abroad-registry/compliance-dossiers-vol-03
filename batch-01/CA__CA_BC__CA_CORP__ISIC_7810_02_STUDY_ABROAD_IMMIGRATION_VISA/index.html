Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – Business Registration Online portal (canada.ca/business-registration-online)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA’s Business Registration Online, or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), and may concurrently register for GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, and other program accounts. Non-resident corporations without a permanent establishment may still require a BN for withholding, excise, or GST/HST purposes if Canadian-source activities exist. For British Columbia, a separate PST registration is required with the BC Ministry of Finance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime. The entity's legal form determines tax classification. Certain elections exist under the Income Tax Act, including the subsection 216(1) election for non-residents on Canadian rental income, the subsection 216(4) election, deemed-disposition elections under subsection 128(1), and hybrid mismatch elections under sections 18.2 and 18.3 to align Canadian and foreign tax treatment of structured arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small-supplier threshold is CAD 30,000. A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register regardless of threshold if carrying on business in Canada or soliciting orders through an agent. BC PST registration is required for sellers making taxable retail sales in British Columbia; BC EHT registration threshold is annual BC payroll of CAD 500,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 of the Excise Tax Act applies to supplies of goods exported by a registrant to a non-resident, and services supplied to a non-resident where the service is not consumed in Canada. Study-abroad immigration and visa advisory services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient, provided the recipient is not resident in Canada, the service is not an exempt financial service, and the service is not made available for consumption in Canada.

  • CBSA Cargo Export Declaration via Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS)
  • Export permit or licence under the Export and Import Permits Act (if applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing export
  • Commercial invoice with zero-rating notation and GST/HST registration number
  • Proof of settlement (wire transfer, SWIFT confirmation, bank statement or inward remittance certificate)
  • Contract and supporting correspondence showing non-resident recipient status
  • Detailed evidence that the service was performed outside Canada for non-resident end consumption

No universal real-time fiscal clearance mandate. Canada supports voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing (Pan-Canadian e-invoice framework, PEPPOL BIS 3.0). CRA does not require pre-clearance API submission or digital fiscal stamping. BC PST requires displayed PST registration number and prescribed invoice data, but no provincial real-time clearance platform.

CRA may reassess within four years, or ten years if misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default is established. Denial of zero-rating or input tax credits triggers repayment of tax, interest under the Excise Tax Act, and a 25% penalty for false or fraudulent claims under section 285. Criminal prosecution under section 327 for evasion may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Provincial PST and EHT authorities impose separate interest, penalties, and director liability.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Exchange Control Act administered by the Bank of Canada. Current account transactions, including profit remittances, consulting fees, and service payments, are freely convertible without prior approval. Capital account transactions require notification or approval from the Bank of Canada or OSFI only in limited cases involving significant foreign direct investment or portfolio flows exceeding CAD 100 million annually. Profit remittance for Canadian corporations is unrestricted subject to corporate tax filing, beneficial ownership disclosure, and anti-money laundering verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian exporters are not subject to mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Banks may request supporting documentation for Income Tax Act compliance and anti-money laundering purposes. No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies; voluntary surrender through spot or forward contracts is permitted at the exporter's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions such as Wise and Revolut maintain limited cross-border viability in Canada under the Payment Services Act and FINTRAC regulations. Offshore digital EMIs are constrained by licensing requirements and must partner with domestically regulated Canadian payment service providers. Full remote issuance without local presence is generally prohibited for corporate clients handling volumes above CAD 50,000 monthly.
