Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administrator; online registration and filing portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-registration.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). Provincial counterpart for British Columbia: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — PST vendor registration via BC eTax (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/taxes-on-purchases/provincial-sales-tax).

E-government filing portal

A federal or BC-incorporated Business Corporation receives its statutory tax identifier as follows: (1) incorporate with Corporations Canada or the BC Corporate Registry (OneStop business registry); (2) CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation; where no automatic BN is issued, apply via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC151; (3) add program accounts under the BN — RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RC (corporate income tax), RM (customs import/export); (4) file corporate income tax using the BN through CRA My Business Account; (5) register separately with BC Consumer Taxation Branch for PST through the BC eTax portal, disclosing the CRA BN. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must apply for a BN before GST/HST registration and may use the foreign-entity registration procedures in CRA information circulars.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the Business Corporation (Inc. / Corp. / Ltd.) is taxed as a separate person under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) at federal corporate rates and under the British Columbia Income Tax Act at the provincial corporate rate; shareholders are taxed only on distributions, producing entity-level liability with no pass-through to owners; commercial model (3D art outsourcing) does not qualify for a Tax_Exempt_Zone and the entity does not elect pass-through status because Canadian corporations are not eligible for flow-through classification. — A federal or BC-incorporated Business Corporation receives its statutory tax identifier as follows: (1) incorporate with Corporations Canada or the BC Corporate Registry (OneStop business registry); (2) CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation; where no automatic BN is issued, apply via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC151; (3) add program accounts under the BN — RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RC (corporate income tax), RM (customs import/export); (4) file corporate income tax using the BN through CRA My Business Account; (5) register separately with BC Consumer Taxation Branch for PST through the BC eTax portal, disclosing the CRA BN. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must apply for a BN before GST/HST registration and may use the foreign-entity registration procedures in CRA information circulars.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Business Corporation (Inc. / Corp. / Ltd.) is taxed as a separate person under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) at federal corporate rates and under the British Columbia Income Tax Act at the provincial corporate rate; shareholders are taxed only on distributions, producing entity-level liability with no pass-through to owners; commercial model (3D art outsourcing) does not qualify for a Tax_Exempt_Zone and the entity does not elect pass-through status because Canadian corporations are not eligible for flow-through classification.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election; entity status is fixed by incorporation. Available elections and overrides: (1) voluntary GST/HST registration election for non-residents making or intending to make taxable supplies in Canada (Excise Tax Act s. 240(2)); (2) GST/HST fiscal-year election to adopt a non-calendar reporting period (ETA s. 259); (3) election under Income Tax Act s. 115(2), allowing a non-resident corporation carrying on a business in Canada to file on treaty-protected Canadian-source business income; (4) allocation election among associated corporations for the small business deduction (ITA s. 125(7)); (5) hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA s. 18.2 and 18.3 apply mandatorily to deny or defer deductions on arrangements exploiting double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes (no elective check-the-box relief is available for such outcomes).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: small-supplier threshold is C$30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in the last four consecutive calendar quarters or in the current calendar quarter; registration becomes mandatory as soon as the threshold is exceeded and is also mandatory for non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada unless the small-supplier test is met; voluntary registration is available below the threshold to recover ITCs. BC PST: every vendor making taxable sales in BC must register through the BC eTax portal — there is no general monetary exemption threshold; non-resident vendors selling taxable goods or specified digital services into BC must register upon first taxable sale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, a supply of a service made to a non-resident person is zero-rated where the service is not rendered to the non-resident while the non-resident is present in Canada, does not relate to real property situated in Canada, and does not relate to tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not a prescribed service (legal, accounting, consultancy or property-related services). AAA game 3D sculpting, character rigging, and digital art asset services delivered electronically to foreign game studios qualify as zero-rated if the supplier is a GST/HST registrant, the recipient is a non-resident of Canada, the contract and invoice evidence the non-resident supply for consumption or use outside Canada, and the supply is not in respect of Canadian real or tangible property. The zero-rated exporter must file the GST/HST return using the net tax calculation and may claim full input tax credits on GST paid for inputs (subcontractor fees, software, hardware, studio overhead) attributable to the zero-rated export stream; any provincial PST paid on taxable purchases in BC may be available as an input tax credit under the BC PST Act.

  • Service agreement / work order with the non-resident game studio or publisher identifying the non-resident recipient by foreign jurisdiction and business registration or tax identification number
  • GST/HST zero-rated invoice compliant with ETA s. 221, bearing the supplier BN, date, description of 3D art / rigging services, recipient name and address, and explicit zero-rating reference
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / wire-transfer and payment rail evidence (foreign-bank SWIFT or PayPal settlement records) evidencing cross-border payment and CAD conversion
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the recipient — foreign business registry extract, foreign VAT or corporate tax number, or statutory declaration
  • CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) export declaration and transport document (bill of lading / airway bill) — required only if tangible physical media, prototype files, or hardware crosses the border; services delivered purely electronically do not generate a customs export declaration

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-clearance, or digital-stamping regime for GST/HST or BC PST; there is no requirement for pre-approval of invoices by the CRA. Electronic invoices are valid if they contain the prescribed information under ETA s. 221 and the GST/HST Information Regulations (supplier BN, date, recipient, description, consideration, GST/HST paid or zero-rating status). The federal Pan-Canadian PEPPOL B2B e-invoicing framework is being piloted (2024 onward) on a voluntary basis. Zero-rated exporters must retain the supporting documentation and produce it on CRA demand; BC PST compliance remains record-based rather than invoice-clearance-based.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted free convertibility for both current and capital accounts under the Foreign Exchange Act. Corporate profit remittances, including dividends and intercompany distributions, face no regulatory hurdles beyond standard corporate tax filings and FINTRAC reporting. Capital account transactions such as equity injections and loans are fully permitted for Canadian corporations and non-resident owners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or repatriation of export proceeds applies to Canadian service exporters. 3D game asset outsourcing receipts may be retained offshore and converted freely at market rates. All FX transactions require FINTRAC reporting if thresholds are met and must reference appropriate purpose codes in banking rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No monetary threshold for purely domestic transactions; international related-party transactions require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where gross revenue thresholds are met (typically CAD 500,000)

Economic substance scope: No formal Economic Substance Regulations; however, transfer pricing rules mandate arm's length pricing supported by economic substance, with the General Anti-Avoidance Rule applicable to artificial arrangements

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file corporate tax return

Statutory sanction: Penalty of $50 per day up to a maximum of $5,000, plus interest on any unpaid tax from the due date

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties and interest where the failure is attributable to their neglect or willful default
Understatement of income or false statements

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 50% of the understated amount, plus interest and potential criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal liability for the tax owing and penalties; in cases of gross negligence or willful omission, criminal charges may result in fines and imprisonment
Non-compliance with transfer pricing documentation requirements

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to $250,000 per transaction or 10% of the transaction value, plus adjustments to transfer prices by the CRA

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the penalties and any resulting tax adjustments
Artificial or sham transactions

Statutory sanction: Application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to recharacterize transactions, denial of deductions, and imposition of penalties

Director personal liability: Directors can be subject to personal liability for tax liabilities arising from recharacterization; potential civil or criminal sanctions
