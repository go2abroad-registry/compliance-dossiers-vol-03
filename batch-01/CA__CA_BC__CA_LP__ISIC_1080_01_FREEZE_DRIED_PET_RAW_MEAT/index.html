Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal income tax, Goods and Services Tax (GST) and customs duty administration; portal: CRA My Business Account (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch - BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST); portal: eTaxBC (etax.gov.bc.ca). Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) - export reporting, valuation and border administration liaison.

E-government filing portal

The statutory tax identifier in Canada is the CRA Business Number (BN), a 9-digit number that also functions as the VAT/GST ID; no separate VAT ID exists. Procedure: (1) register the BC limited partnership through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) using the BC registered office or authorized agent address, or, for non-resident-controlled structures, file paper Form RC1 with the local CRA tax centre, attaching the BC registration extract and notarized general partner resolution (processing 4-6 weeks); (2) open the GST/HST Program Account by adding the 'RT' program code to the BN (format 123456789 RT 0001) in BRO; (3) register separately for BC PST via eTaxBC and obtain the PST vendor account (9-digit PST number plus '-PST-0001' suffix); (4) file the annual T5013 partnership information return within 5 months of the partnership year-end and issue T5013 slips; non-resident partners must provide their own Canadian TIN/BN and are subject to Part I filings for deemed Canadian business income (ITA s.96(2.4)) and to Part XIII / Regulation 105 withholding on specified payments such as interest, royalties and fees for services rendered in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - A British Columbia limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and BC income tax purposes (ITA s.96): no entity-level corporate income tax or BC capital tax applies, and each partner is taxed on its allocated share of Canadian-source business income. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is a separate 'person' and registrant under ETA s.272.1 and must collect/remit GST on its taxable supplies and claim input tax credits (ITCs) on inputs. Territorial scope: the LP carries on business in Canada; exports of freeze-dried pet raw meat are zero-rated for GST and exempt from BC PST. Tax-exempt-zone regime: not applicable.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian law; classification flows from the BC Partnerships Act and ITA s.96. Available elections: (a) ITA s.249.1(1)(b) non-calendar fiscal-period election for a partnership all of whose partners are non-residents; (b) ITA s.97(2) tax-deferred rollover on property contributed to the LP by partners; (c) ETA s.156 election between the LP and a closely-related registrant (e.g., the corporate general partner) to treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration, eliminating GST leakage; (d) hybrid-mismatch ordering and elective adjustments under ITA ss.18.4-18.6 (in force July 1, 2022) that neutralize deduction-without-inclusion outcomes for hybrid entities, including reverse-hybrid limited partnerships; a foreign partner's Form 8832 check-the-box classification is not recognized by the CRA for Canadian tax characterization of the LP.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has a freely convertible Canadian dollar and no general exchange-control regime. Current-account payments, including payments for imported ingredients, packaging, equipment, freight, insurance, services, and ordinary business expenses, may generally be made without prior foreign-exchange approval. Capital-account transactions, including partner contributions, partner loans, distributions, acquisitions, and intercompany financing, are generally permitted without exchange-control approval, but must be commercially documented and may trigger Canadian tax, transfer-pricing, partnership-allocation, withholding, beneficial-ownership, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, customs, or foreign-investment review requirements. A British Columbia limited partnership should maintain a partnership agreement, general-partner authority evidence, contribution and loan records, ownership and control information, tax residency certificates where relevant, and board or partner resolutions supporting material remittances. Non-resident partners may generally receive distributions and loan repayments in foreign currency, subject to available cash, the partnership agreement, solvency considerations, Canadian withholding tax where applicable, and bank verification of source, purpose, and beneficiary. Canadian-dollar conversion is normally available through the account bank or an authorized foreign-exchange dealer. There is no general statutory requirement to repatriate foreign currency receipts or convert them into Canadian dollars. Payments involving high-risk jurisdictions, sanctioned parties, unusual payment chains, virtual assets, or unexplained third-party funding may be delayed, rejected, reported, or subject to enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed deadline for converting export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may generally remain in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency accounts, including accounts with Canadian financial institutions, subject to the account agreement and applicable tax and financial-crime controls. The partnership should retain export contracts, commercial invoices, shipping and customs records, bills of lading or air waybills, certificates of origin where relevant, customer payment records, and reconciliation schedules. Large cash transactions and certain international electronic funds transfers are subject to Canadian anti-money-laundering reporting and recordkeeping rules; the bank or reporting entity may file a Large Cash Transaction Report or Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC where statutory thresholds and conditions are met. Exporters should also comply with Canada Revenue Agency books-and-records rules, GST/HST requirements, export documentation requirements, and sanctions or export-control restrictions. No mandatory foreign-exchange registration or routine central-bank approval is required for ordinary commercial export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Foreign-exchange transactions are also subject to federal financial-crime, sanctions, customs, tax, and reporting requirements administered or enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Canada Border Services Agency, the Canada Revenue Agency, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, and Global Affairs Canada, as applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplemental payment operations and Low to Medium as the primary treasury bank. A foreign electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, virtual IBANs, collection services, foreign-exchange conversion, and cross-border transfers, but it does not automatically provide a Canadian deposit account, Canadian deposit insurance, Canadian cheque or domestic-payment functionality, or unrestricted access to Canadian clearing. The partnership must verify the provider's home-jurisdiction authorization, Canadian licensing or exemption position, safeguarding arrangements, settlement-bank structure, sanctions controls, insolvency treatment, funds availability, and permitted customer jurisdiction. A foreign EMI cannot be used to bypass Canadian AML, sanctions, tax, customs, payment-transparency, or beneficial-ownership requirements. Canadian banks and counterparties may reject funds routed through an EMI where the payer, beneficial owner, source of funds, or payment purpose is unclear. Use a regulated Canadian commercial bank as the primary account, maintain an approved secondary provider only after legal and operational due diligence, and avoid personal accounts, pooled accounts lacking transaction-level transparency, unlicensed payment intermediaries, and nominee payment structures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner capital through a Canadian commercial-bank account in the exact legal name of the British Columbia limited partnership, preferably from an account held by the named contributing partner or its fully documented treasury entity. Use SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank wire instructions containing the partnership's legal name, account number, transit and institution identifiers, SWIFT/BIC, currency, and a clear payment narrative such as 'capital contribution under partnership agreement' or 'partner loan under agreement dated [date]'. Canada generally does not prescribe universal foreign-exchange purpose codes for ordinary commercial wires; use the sending bank's accurate purpose classification and do not substitute a false trade or service code. Before receipt, provide the bank with the partnership agreement, contribution or loan agreement, partner resolution, source-of-funds evidence, ownership chart, expected amount and currency, and tax treatment memorandum. The receiving bank may request confirmation of the ultimate originator, beneficial owner, purpose, source of wealth, and relationship between sender and partnership. Do not split payments to evade reporting or screening thresholds. Reconcile each receipt to the partner ledger, issue an internal capital or loan record, and retain wire confirmations and bank statements. For substantial related-party loans or cross-border funding, assess transfer-pricing, thin-capitalization, interest-withholding, Part XIII withholding, imputed-interest, foreign-exchange gain or loss, and partnership allocation consequences before funding.Use Canadian-bank domestic electronic funds transfers, wires, SWIFT MT103, and documentary commercial letters of credit where appropriate. For supplier payments, match the payment to an approved purchase order, commercial invoice, customs classification, shipping evidence, and supplier master record. For higher-risk or new suppliers, use an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit, preferably confirmed where country or bank risk justifies confirmation, with documentary conditions limited to commercially necessary transport, invoice, origin, insurance, and regulatory documents. For routine international wires, include accurate originator, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, invoice, contract, and goods-description information and avoid vague narratives such as 'services' where the payment is for goods. Canadian banks may apply sanctions, AML, fraud, customs, and trade-control screening before release. Maintain three-way matching among purchase order, goods-receipt or shipping evidence, and invoice; require dual approval; prohibit payments to personal accounts unless independently justified and approved; and screen suppliers, carriers, banks, end users, and destinations. For exports, retain customer contracts, invoices, bills of lading, export declarations, and receipt reconciliation. Consider documentary collections or letters of credit for customer credit risk. A foreign electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, virtual IBANs, collection services, foreign-exchange conversion, and cross-border transfers, but it does not automatically provide a Canadian deposit account, Canadian deposit insurance, Canadian cheque or domestic-payment functionality, or unrestricted access to Canadian clearing.
