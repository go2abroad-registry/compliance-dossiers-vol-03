Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Business Registration Online portal at canada.ca and My Business Account; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, including BC Registries and Online Services (BC Corporate Registry) and eTaxBC for provincial tax accounts.

E-government filing portal

A captive reinsurance SPV that is a non-resident or Canadian-incorporated corporation must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN), which is the statutory TIN for federal tax purposes. For a foreign entity, the first step is extra-provincial registration in BC under the Business Corporations Act (if it carries on business in BC) or incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act. The entity then applies for a CRA BN using Form RC1 or online through Business Registration Online, requesting the applicable accounts, especially the GST/HST account (RT) and corporate income tax account. For BC PST, registration must be made through eTaxBC, and the provincial tax ID will be the PST vendor number. Supporting information includes the articles of incorporation, registered office address, Canadian service-of-process address, directors/officers, business activity declaration (NAICS 524130 / ISIC 6512), and ownership structure of the captive.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian tax law; a captive reinsurance corporation is always a separate entity-level taxpayer. Available elections and mechanisms include election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related corporations to treat certain taxable supplies as non-supplies for GST/HST purposes, corporate group loss-allocation arrangements under section 256 of the Income Tax Act, and various elective provisions for financial institutions relating to mark-to-market accounting. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.2 and 18.4 of the Income Tax Act are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions; no election is available to opt out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration is mandatory once the entity and associated entities exceed the small supplier threshold of CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four calendar quarters; below this threshold registration is voluntary. BC PST registration has no general turnover threshold and is required as soon as the entity sells or provides services subject to PST, although captive reinsurance and financial services are generally exempt or zero-rated. Combined headline rate is 12%, composed of 5% GST plus 7% BC PST on taxable retail supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A captive reinsurance SPV may zero-rate exported financial services, including reinsurance risk-pooling arrangements, under Part VII of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property located in Canada at the time the service is performed. The premium or fee must be for reinsurance of risks situated outside Canada and must not be supplied to a GST/HST registrant carrying on a business in Canada. Documentary evidence must demonstrate the non-resident cedant, payment from outside Canada, and foreign risk situs; otherwise the supply remains exempt rather than zero-rated and no refund mechanism applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration where physical assets or documentation are exported; otherwise service-export evidence is used
  • Reinsurance treaty, binder, or placement slip identifying the non-resident cedant and the ceded block of policies
  • Certificate of non-resident status, foreign tax identification document, or equivalent Cedant declaration
  • Premium invoice marked with the Canadian supplier GST/HST registration number and zero-rating citation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/telegraphic transfer confirmation establishing payment from outside Canada
  • Risk-situs schedule and policy-level ceding statement demonstrating that no insured risk is located in Canada
  • Captive SPV transaction register, underwriting reserve ledger, and BC corporate registry records for audit trail

Canada does not have a federal real-time e-invoicing clearance, fiscal stamping, or API-based CRA approval requirement for invoices. However, GST/HST registrants must maintain prescribed records and issue valid invoices for taxable supplies; many registrants are required to file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account. BC PST registrants must file and remit through eTaxBC, and large businesses are subject to CRA electronic record and data-format requirements. There is no universal BC or federal continuous transaction control regime.

CRA may reclassify a claimed zero-rated export as exempt or taxable on audit if the documentation does not prove non-resident status, foreign risk situs, or foreign payment. Reclassification triggers assessment of applicable GST, interest, and may disallow associated input tax credits. Gross negligence penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act may reach 50% of the net tax amount, and willful evasion charges under section 329 can result in criminal fines and imprisonment. BC PST exposure remains available for any non-exempt local component, with a 10% penalty plus interest under the Provincial Sales Tax Act if PST is not collected and remitted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian Dollar (CAD) floats freely and qualifies for full IMF Article VIII convertibility, with no licensing, prior-approval, repatriation, or conversion-ratio restrictions on either current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance from a British Columbia captive is subject only to corporate-law solvency tests (dividends declared out of available retained earnings) and Canadian tax compliance: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (default 25% on dividends; treaty-reduced, e.g., 5% for a US parent owning at least 10% of voting shares) and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. No FX clearance from OSFI, the Bank of Canada, or the Department of Finance is required for capital outflows or dividend remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation duty on export proceeds, reinsurance premiums, or claim recoveries. The captive may keep CAD, USD, or third-currency balances on deposit with any Canadian or foreign financial institution, subject to FINTRAC electronic-transfer reporting above CAD 10,000 (originator/beneficiary travel-rule data) and to BCFSA admitted-asset and credit-concentration rules governing unsecured foreign-currency placements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the Minister of Finance retains formal authority over the exchange-rate regime, but neither the Minister nor the Bank of Canada imposes exchange controls. The Bank acts as settlement agent for Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and supervises financial market infrastructures under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for regulatory escrow, capital, and premium-trust functions; Medium only for ancillary non-trust disbursement utilities. BC's Captive Insurance Companies Act and BCFSA reserve/financial-adequacy guidance require statutory capital, premium trust balances, and admitted assets to be held with a federally regulated Schedule I bank or a Canadian trust company. Offshore digital EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and are outside OSFI prudential supervision and CDIC protection; therefore they cannot lawfully host the captive's capital escrow, premium trust, or claims settlement accounts. A FINTRAC-registered money-services business or a payment service provider registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act may be used only for minor FX conversion or intercompany recoveries, with explicit treasury-policy limits.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Board resolutions approving share subscription, premium funding, and treasury policy; audited financial statements of the captive and ultimate parent; independent actuarial opinion on gross and net reserves; certified true copy of the BCFSA captive insurance licence; actuarial certificate; transaction-level ledger tracing each funding inflow; bank reference letter from the remitting institution; description of the enterprise-group risk pool.
Sanctions and high-risk-jurisdiction exposure embedded in the reinsurance/cedent portfolio

Non-resident directors, shareholders, or signatories outside Canada triggering enhanced due diligence and PEP screening

Opaque outbound claims disbursements to cedents or third-party claimants flagged as money-movement risk

Root cause: Opaque outbound claims disbursements to cedents or third-party claimants flagged as money-movement risk

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent or group treasury wires the captive's statutory capital by SWIFT MT103 (with MT202 cover where applicable) in CAD, USD, or EUR to the Capital Escrow Account at the captive's Schedule I bank, using the remittance narrative 'Capital Injection — Share Subscription — [Captive Legal Name]' and a bank-specific equity-investment purpose code; CAD legs clear domestically via the LVTS, while USD legs clear via Fedwire/CHIPS through the bank's US correspondent. The receiving compliance team pre-screens the beneficiary name and tax identifier, verifies the BCFSA captive licence, requires the source-of-funds bundle (board resolutions, parent audited financials, ownership schedules, and the group organizational chart), and files any mandatory FINTRAC EFT report for transfers above CAD 10,000. No exchange-control approval, central-bank registration, or repatriation certificate is required before the capital is booked to stated capital and admitted assets under BC law.Claims settlements, loss-adjustment expenses, and reinsurance recoveries are executed through three rails: (i) domestic CAD via the LVTS as a same-day irrevocable payment; (ii) cross-border single payments via SWIFT MT103 and batched fan-out payments via MT102, using remittance narratives 'Premium' or 'Claims' with full originator and beneficiary identifiers to satisfy the PCMLTFA travel rule; and (iii) commercial standby or documentary letters of credit issued by the captive's bank under SWIFT MT700 wherever the cedent's reinsurance agreement requires collateral in the form of cash, LOC, or bank guarantee. Disbursements are pre-screened against SEMA, UNSC, and OFAC sanctions, and any blocked payment is reported to FINTRAC. A multicurrency account sweep and same-day CAD/USD execution with the bank's FX desk minimize conversion latency on each settlement cycle.Deliverable outright forwards in CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP, and CAD/JPY with tenors up to 10 years under the ISDA 1998 FX Master Agreement and a bilateral credit support annex; no regulatory pre-clearance is required because Canada has no exchange controls.FX swaps and rollover swaps for temporary liquidity management, collateral funding, and margining of letter-of-credit obligations.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) to align long-dated reinsurance reserve liabilities with premium and capital currency cash flows.OTC FX options, participating forwards, and zero-cost collars for reserve protection against adverse CAD movements while retaining favourable scenario participation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not applicable to CAD: the Canadian Dollar is fully convertible with deep offshore deliverable markets and no administered reference fixing, so Canadian Schedule I banks will not offer CAD NDFs, and treasury policy must reject any proposed CAD NDF as a non-permissible instrument.
