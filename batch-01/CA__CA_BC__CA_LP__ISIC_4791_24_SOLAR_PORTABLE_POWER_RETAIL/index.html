Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch - eTaxBC portal: https://www.etaxbc.gov.bc.ca

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated limited partnership that carries on business in BC must obtain a 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (account type RT). Registration can be completed remotely using CRA Business Registration Online or, for non-residents, CRA Form RC350 Non-Resident Registration Application. The BN is the statutory TIN for GST/HST; Canada does not issue a separate VAT number. After obtaining the BN, the same legal entity registers with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC to receive a BC PST number, which is separate from the federal BN. The BC PST registration application requires partnership agreement details, general and limited partner information, and physical or remote sales locations. No local director or capital deposit is required for an LP; a mailing address and authorized representative are sufficient for federal and provincial program accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: a BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96; it is not a taxable entity and its income or loss is allocated to general and limited partners. For GST/HST and PST, the partnership may register as a registrant or vendor in its own name.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime. A BC LP is transparent by statute. Examples of available elections relevant to a limited partnership include an ITA s.96(3) election for certain deemed realizations, an ITA s.85(2) partnership rollover election when transferring eligible property to a corporation, and administrative elections for GST/HST division or branch registration. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss.18.2 and 18.3 apply to cross-border financing and partnership arrangements, and no elective opt-out is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Federal GST/HST: the small-supplier exemption applies while worldwide taxable revenue in the preceding calendar quarter and all prior quarters in the current calendar year is CAD 30,000 or less; registration becomes mandatory when the threshold is exceeded. For BC PST: a remote seller must register if it makes taxable sales in BC and is not a small seller, defined generally as gross taxable sales in BC of CAD 10,000 or less in a calendar year; marketplace provider and fulfillment centre rules also impose deemed registration requirements for qualifying e-commerce sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies to a registrant supply of tangible personal property that is exported by the supplier as part of the sale, provided the property is delivered to a destination outside Canada and the supplier retains satisfactory evidence of export meeting CRA guidelines. Solar portable power equipment shipped from a Canadian warehouse to a non-resident consumer qualifies for zero-rating when export documentation is complete. Input tax credits on goods and services used to make zero-rated exports remain fully recoverable and any excess is refundable on the GST/HST return. BC PST is not charged when the seller ships goods outside Canada for use outside Canada, subject to export evidence being maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A export declaration or approved electronic equivalent where required)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail, or truck carrier document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of receipt of offshore payment
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Carrier tracking and delivery confirmation (courier scan or proof of delivery)
  • Purchase order or sales contract
  • Evidence of export within the prescribed GST/HST time period
  • CRA form GST11 or equivalent export evidence worksheet

Canada has no national real-time e-invoicing clearance mandate, no digital stamping regime, and no mandatory transaction-level fiscal API. GST/HST registrants must issue ordinary commercial invoices containing prescribed information and retain electronic records in accessible format. CRA accepts EDI, PDF, and XML filings; GST/HST returns are filed through CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE, and BC PST returns are filed and remitted through eTaxBC. No pre-clearance approval or fiscal stamping is required before invoices are issued.

Canada has no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is documentary best practice for zero-rating audit readiness. CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years from the date of the return, or ten years where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. If export zero-rating is disallowed, the supplier becomes liable for uncollected GST/PST, arrears interest, and statutory penalties under ETA ss.280 and 285.1: a 5% failure-to-remit penalty plus 1% per full month to a maximum of 10%, and a 25% civil penalty for knowingly or negligently making a false statement. BC PST penalties include 10% of the unremitted tax or a minimum fine, plus interest, and can be assessed retrospectively on denial of an export exemption. Deliberate fraudulent export schemes may also trigger gross-negligence penalties, director or general partner personal liability, and criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 25 March 1952 and maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. No Bank of Canada or FINTRAC authorization is required to inject capital into a British Columbia Limited Partnership, to repatriate profits, or to convert CAD into USD, EUR, CNH, or any other currency. The only practical friction for a non-resident investor is fiscal and documentary: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at 25% statutory on dividend/royalty/interest-type payments, reduced under the Canada-China and Canada-US tax treaties (typically 10-15% on dividends, 0% on certain interest, 10% on royalties); (ii) the LP's obligation to file T5013 partnership information returns and to remit any Part XIII withholding to the CRA by the 15th day of the following month; and (iii) for the acquisition of control of a Canadian business, the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review applies only above the indexed multi-billion-CAD enterprise-value threshold for WTO investors (re-indexed each January), which will not be triggered by a passive capital contribution to an e-commerce limited partnership. Profit remittances may be paid in any freely convertible currency and may be structured as US-dollar wires without any FX surrender or allocation approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. The now-quiescent Exchange Control Act and the Currency Act do not oblige a Canadian exporter to repatriate or convert foreign currency earned by online retail sales (ISIC 4791) into CAD. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or CNH operating accounts held at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The compliance layer is purely transactional: (i) FINTRAC real-time reporting by the financial institution for any international electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more; (ii) Form E311 cross-border currency reporting for physical currency movements of C$10,000 or more at the border; (iii) record keeping under the PCMLTFA for any large cash transaction exceeding C$10,000; and (iv) standard Canadian GST/PST and income-tax reporting for the e-commerce revenue.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Department of Finance Canada in respect of the Currency Act and monetary policy. Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange control administration comparable to an Asian capital-control regulator; the functional authorities are (i) OSFI for prudential supervision of Schedule I banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46), (ii) FINTRAC for the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) registration and reporting regime, and (iii) the Canada Revenue Agency for cross-border tax and withholding administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection or payout rail; LOW as a substitute for tier-1 commercial banking. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK electronic-money-institution (EMI) passport. An offshore EMI or a non-Canadian digital wallet cannot accept CAD deposits as a deposit-taking business without an OSFI authorized-foreign-bank or Canadian-branch license. Any non-resident money-services business (MSB) clearing CAD cross-border must register with FINTRAC as a foreign money-services business, appoint a designated person in Canada, maintain a written compliance program, and file electronic-funds-transfer reports (EFTR) for all international EFTs of C$10,000 or more. In practice, Stripe, Payoneer, and Wise operate through FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB entities with correspondent banking via an OSFI-approved Schedule I bank. Recommended usage is strictly limited to (i) e-commerce receivable collection (Stripe, Affirm BNPL payouts) and (ii) sub-C$10,000 vendor disbursements. All partner capital contributions and all trade settlements above C$10,000 must be cleared through the Schedule I bank account to preserve auditability and avoid TBML red flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All partner capital contributions shall be routed as SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 equivalent pacs.008) to the LP's operational account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. Mandatory protocol: (i) Field 50F must fully identify the ordering partner with name and account number to satisfy the FATF Recommendation 16 travel rule; (ii) Field 59 must name the LP exactly as registered in British Columbia (e.g., '[Name] Limited Partnership / LP-#####'); (iii) Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT DATED [DATE] - PARTNER: [UBO NAME] - PURPOSE: WORKING CAPITAL FOR SOLAR PORTABLE POWER E-COMMERCE'; (iv) the bank will conduct OFAC/UN sanctions screening and PEP screening on the ordering institution; (v) no FX authorization is required, but the bank will report any international EFT of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC; (vi) the LP must credit the partner's capital account in the partnership ledger and retain the MT103 confirmation as tax evidence for T5013 allocation and subsequent return-of-capital computations. Capital contributions are not Part XIII distributions; do not issue an NR4 for the injection. If the contribution is funded in USD or CNH, the general partner may direct a spot conversion to CAD or retain the foreign currency in the LP's multi-currency account at the bank's prevailing treasury spot rate.For disbursements to PRC suppliers of portable power stations, solar panels, and accessories: (i) Open-account trade payments via SWIFT MT103 using the LP's Canadian bank correspondent network, with the remittance narrative citing the underlying purchase order, invoice number, and HS classification (HS 8504.40 for portable battery generators; HS 8541.43 for photovoltaic cells/solar panels); any payment above C$50,000 should be accompanied by the supplier's commercial invoice, packing list, and transport document to avoid TBML escalation. (ii) Commercial letters of credit: issue an irrevocable documentary LC (SWIFT MT700) at-sight through Scotiabank or RBC trade finance, advised and possibly confirmed by a PRC advising bank (e.g., Bank of China), with shipping documents comprising full set of clean on-board bills of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin; this rail is preferred for pre-payments or first-time supplier relationships exceeding C$100,000. (iii) For e-commerce refunds and chargebacks, Stripe and Affirm settle net proceeds directly to the LP's Canadian operational account in CAD through bank card networks. (iv) CBSA duties and GST/PST remittances are discharged through the bank's tax-payment portal, not via FX wires. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection or payout rail; LOW as a substitute for tier-1 commercial banking. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK electronic-money-institution (EMI) passport. An offshore EMI or a non-Canadian digital wallet cannot accept CAD deposits as a deposit-taking business without an OSFI authorized-foreign-bank or Canadian-branch license. Any non-resident money-services business (MSB) clearing CAD cross-border must register with FINTRAC as a foreign money-services business, appoint a designated person in Canada, maintain a written compliance program, and file electronic-funds-transfer reports (EFTR) for all international EFTs of C$10,000 or more. In practice, Stripe, Payoneer, and Wise operate through FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB entities with correspondent banking via an OSFI-approved Schedule I bank. Recommended usage is strictly limited to (i) e-commerce receivable collection (Stripe, Affirm BNPL payouts) and (ii) sub-C$10,000 vendor disbursements. All partner capital contributions and all trade settlements above C$10,000 must be cleared through the Schedule I bank account to preserve auditability and avoid TBML red flags.
