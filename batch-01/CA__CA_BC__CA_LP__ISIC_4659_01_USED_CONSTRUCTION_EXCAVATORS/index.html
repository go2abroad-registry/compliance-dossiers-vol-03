E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Limited Partnership under the BC Partnership Act (RSBC 1996, c.348) through BC Corporate Online and obtain the BC registration number. (2) Apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1; the BN anchors program accounts including GST/HST ('RT') and the T5013 Partnership Information Return. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act s.96, the Canadian LP is fiscally transparent: income, losses and tax credits flow through pro rata to partners and the LP pays no federal income tax. For GST/HST purposes the LP is nevertheless a separate 'person' and registrant under Excise Tax Act s.221(1). Territorial scope: Canadian-source business income allocations are taxable to both resident and non-resident partners, with additional reporting and withholding obligations in respect of non-resident partners' allocations. No tax-exempt zone treatment applies in British Columbia.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or elective entity reclassification regime; the LP's transparent status is statutory. Available elections include: (i) the functional-currency election under ITA s.261 (USD tax measurement where at least 90% of the LP's gross revenue and expenses are in USD); (ii) the GST/HST joint election for closely related corporations under ETA s.273; (iii) the GST Quick Method for registrants whose annual taxable revenue does not exceed CAD 400,000; and (iv) a non-calendar fiscal-period election under ITA s.249.1 where business reasons are established. OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act (deduction/non-inclusion and deduction-denial provisions) apply non-electively and may restrict benefits where a foreign jurisdiction treats the LP or its partners asymmetrically.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible, freely floating currency (CAD) with no general exchange-control legislation; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act impose no restrictions on current-account or capital-account convertibility. A British Columbia Limited Partnership may receive and remit CAD, USD, or any freely convertible currency without Bank of Canada approval. Profit distributions and remittances to non-resident partners are freely transferable but remain subject to the Income Tax Act (Canada) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at a statutory rate of 25%, potentially reduced by an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Article XI of the Canada-US Convention to 15% on dividends and 0% on certain capital gains); no convertibility hurdle exists, but the partnership must register for, collect, and remit withholding taxes and file NR4 information returns. Practical impediments are limited to enhanced Know-Your-Customer documentation, FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Reporting (CAD 10,000 or more), sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act, and bank-level compliance for large FX wires; these are procedural frictions, not currency-control denials.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose mandatory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation quotas, or forced conversion ratios. Export receipts arising from wholesale remarketing of used hydraulic earthmovers and rollers may be retained in CAD, USD, or other convertible currency in a foreign-currency account at a Schedule I bank without any statutory conversion obligation. Exporters must nonetheless observe CBSA customs entry and declaration requirements under the Customs Act, the Export and Import Permits Act (for controlled or listed goods), retain full commercial documentation (commercial invoices, bills of lading, customs entries, inspection certificates) for at least six years under the Income Tax Act and Customs Act, and ensure that settlement proceeds comply with FINTRAC large-transaction reporting and Canadian economic sanctions screening. No surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank regulation and supervision; Department of Finance Canada as the competent foreign exchange administration policy body; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF supervisory and intelligence unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (legally restricted). Non-bank financial technology providers and electronic money institutions operating in Canada are regulated as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and must register with FINTRAC; British Columbia additionally requires licensing under the Money Services Business Act (British Columbia). Digital EMIs such as Wise, Ebury, Corpay, and OFX cannot take deposits, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide custody, and they are not authorized under the Bank Act (Canada) to operate as banks. For a wholesale used-excavator trading Limited Partnership, EMIs are viable only as supplementary rails for low-value foreign supplier payments, small FX conversions, and certain cross-border expense settlements; the primary treasury anchor must be a Schedule I bank account in the name of the Limited Partnership, with the General Partner as authorized signatory. EMIs may be disallowed for high-value escrow, LC-backed settlements, and large capital injections due to their absence of LC issuance capability and lower institutional AML thresholds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for non-resident partner capital contributions and equity injections: route via SWIFT MT103 with Field 50K (ordering institution details), Field 59 (beneficiary: BC Limited Partnership legal name and account number), and narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO BRITISH COLUMBIA LIMITED PARTNERSHIP [registration number] DATED [date] PER LIMITED PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT'. Canada does not maintain a statutory transaction purpose-code registry, but the receiving Canadian Schedule I bank will map the credit under FINTRAC large-value reporting (threshold CAD 10,000) and, where applicable, OSFI enhanced due diligence protocols. USD-denominated injections clear via the Canadian bank's US correspondent network (commonly JPMorgan Chase, Bank of New York Mellon, or Citibank New York) using ABA routing and the LP's USD sub-account; CAD injections clear via Lynx (Payments Canada high-value payment system, successor to LVTS) and are settled same-day. Verification steps: (a) obtain SWIFT MT199 or bank confirmation of value date; (b) reconcile funds to the LP's capital ledger; (c) ensure the contribution is documented as contributed capital, not a loan, to avoid deemed-interest attribution under the Income Tax Act; (d) if proceeds will ever be directed to real property acquisition in BC, pre-clear registration under the Land Owner Transparency Act (LOTA); (e) segregate any funds that could be construed as client trust monies.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate income tax rate for business carried on in British Columbia (federal 15% + provincial 12%); the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and does not pay income tax itself — each partner is subject to tax on its distributive share (corporate partners at corporate rates, individual partners at personal rates).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid or credited to non-residents. For a Limited Partnership, periodic profit allocations to non-resident partners are generally treated as Canadian-source business income rather than dividends, and are taxed on a net basis through the partner's own tax return unless a DTA reduces or eliminates Canadian taxing rights.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 5 months after the partnership's fiscal year-end. For a December 31 year-end, the T5013 and BC Schedule 50 are due no later than May 31 of the following calendar year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The corporate partner's T2 is due within 6 months after its own taxation year-end; the balance-due date is 2 months after year-end (3 months where the corporation is a CCPC and claims the small business deduction).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 — aggregate cross-border non-arm's length transactions in a taxation year triggers the mandatory T106 Information Return for Transactions with Non-Residents. CRA's transfer-pricing documentation policy recommends a Local File and Master File where controlled cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000; Country-by-Country Report applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of €750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a labelled 'Economic Substance Regulation' statute, but the GAAR (Income Tax Act s. 245) denies tax benefits for transactions lacking genuine economic substance, a reasonable non-tax purpose, or that create artificial structuring through shell partnerships. A BC limited partnership will be respected only where it has substantive business activity, onshore management, and commercial operations in Canada. The anti-avoidance rules, hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.2, 18.3), and case law on 'sham transactions' may recharacterise the LP as a taxable corporation or attribute its income directly to a controlling non-resident principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act s. 163.2 / 239)

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion: fine of CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for an indictable offence; on summary conviction, a fine of up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 6 months. Civil penalties: gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax and arrears interest at prescribed rates.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling partners are jointly and severally liable for unremitted or evaded Canadian-source corporate and Part XIII tax amounts under s. 227.1, including penalties and interest, if they caused or acquiesced to the evasion; personal travel bans may be issued under tax enforcement, CRA may seize personal assets, and director disqualification applies in serious cases.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke GAAR to disallow the loss structure, reallocate income to the beneficial owner, and impose a 50% gross-negligence penalty. BC's Registrar under the Partnership Act may strike or dissolve the LP for non-compliance; intentional misrepresentation may trigger criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced — partners are personally taxed on the partnership income as carried on directly; directors may be held civilly liable for CRA arrears, face asset freezes, and be banned from holding directorships in Canada under the Canada Business Corporations Act s. 248.
Money Laundering / AML (FINTRAC, Criminal Code)

Statutory sanction: Under PCMLTFA, administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for non-compliance; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s. 462.31 carries imprisonment up to 10 years. FINTRAC penalties apply for missing CDD records, unregistered money services, and unreported cross-border currency movements.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and compliance officers who knowingly facilitate money laundering face criminal prosecution, seizure of personal assets, travel restrictions, and permanent director disqualification; liability extends to every intermediary in the wire/LC chain with knowledge.
Transfer Pricing Penalties (Income Tax Act s. 247)

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5 million or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue. The penalty applies without a deduction; interest accrues from the tax-filing due date; CRA may also impose gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the tax shift.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve mispricing and fail to file T106 documentation can be jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for the penalty, and may face personal civil claims from CRA; in egregious cases, criminal liability attaches for wilful participation in a false document.
