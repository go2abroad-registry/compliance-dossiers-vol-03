Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal income tax and GST/HST; portal: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online. BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — BC PST administration (no single-window federal/provincial fiscal portal exists).

E-government filing portal

1. Register the BC limited partnership with BC Registries under the British Columbia Limited Partnership Act to obtain the BC LP registration number. 2. Apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with GST/HST (RT) and/or income tax program accounts using Form RC1 or through an authorized representative in CRA's Represent a Client system. 3. Add a BC PST account through the BC provincial tax portal once taxable retail activities in BC commence. 4. For export goods, obtain/register a CBSA exporter identifier and file export declarations through the Export Reporting System. The BN serves as the Canadian TIN; CRA will issue separate program accounts for GST/HST, payroll, and income tax; a GST/HST registrant must quote the BN-RT on commercial invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election and no discretionary entity classification regime. A BC LP is per se transparent for Canadian income tax: profits and losses flow to partners and are taxed in the partners' hands; no entity-level federal income tax is imposed on the LP. For GST/HST, the partnership itself is a 'person' and must hold its own registration; no election can change that liability. Certain routine elections (partnership taxation-year alignment and GST/HST related-party filing elections) are available, but no hybrid mismatch election can reclassify the LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST small-supplier threshold over any four consecutive calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies; BC PST registration has no comparable general dollar threshold for BC vendors making taxable retail sales).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods (RPG dice) are zero-rated under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier sells tangible personal property and exports them from Canada, or where the non-resident recipient exports them directly from Canada after purchase. The BC LP must not know or be indifferent that the goods will be diverted for Canadian consumption; delivery to a foreign destination must be substantiated. Services, if any, are zero-rated only where supplied to a non-resident and not performed in Canada. For BC PST, no PST applies when goods are shipped outside British Columbia for delivery outside the province, provided the sale completes in export and is appropriately documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (Export Reporting System reference or approved self-classified export document, where filing thresholds apply) identifying the goods, destination, value, and HS classification (likely under HS 95.04/95.06 for games/gaming equipment)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill and commercial invoice with packing list showing physical export movement from Canada to the non-resident purchaser
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank or payment-rail settlement report (Stripe/Kickstarter/PayPal payout statement, wire confirmation, or FX inward remittance) proving gross export consideration was received in the seller's account

Not applicable. Canada and BC do not mandate real-time electronic invoice clearance, fiscal stamping, or API-based government server reporting. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants with taxable annual revenues exceeding C$1,500,000; all accounting and export evidence must be kept in English or French for six years and be available to CRA/CBSA on demand.

Zero-rating is accepted only if documentary evidence is retained and traceable to the specific export sale. If CRA or BC's tax auditor cannot link the invoice to actual export and FX receipt, the sale is recharacterized as a taxable domestic supply; GST (5%) and BC PST (7%) are clawed back with statutory interest. False or grossly negligent statements attract a civil penalty of 25% of the understated net tax (minimum C$100) under s. 285.1 ETA, while deliberate schemes may be prosecuted under s. 327 ETA; refunds claimed on fraudulent export documentation are denied and can be recaptured within four years, or without time limit for misrepresentation or fraud. For BC PST, the provincial uncollected-tax liability is reassessed and penalties/interest apply from the original due date.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a floating exchange rate regime with unrestricted capital and current account convertibility. Profit remittances to non-resident limited partners are permitted without prior approval or capital controls, subject solely to standard beneficial ownership verification, tax reporting obligations under the Income Tax Act, and FINTRAC compliance. No exchange controls apply to routine corporate treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirements exist for export receipts. Canadian exporters retain full discretion to utilize foreign currency proceeds. No statutory timelines, conversion ratios, or surrender mandates apply; voluntary surrender to authorized dealers is available but optional.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined for ordinary corporations (15% federal + 12% British Columbia); the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent, so income is taxed at the partner level at applicable personal or corporate marginal rates

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding rate (s. 212(2) ITA) applies to dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents; for a Limited Partnership, profit distributions are not dividends — non-resident partners are taxed on their allocated share of Canadian-source business income under Part I, and actual cash distributions representing previously taxed income or return of capital are generally not subject to further withholding

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 — aggregate fair-market value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with any single non-resident in a taxation year triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (s. 247(4)); Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue above CAD 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA) disallows tax benefits from transactions lacking genuine economic substance where the main purpose is tax avoidance; the Supreme Court of Canada's Copthorne test requires a real economic result. Under s. 65 of the BC Partnership Act, a limited partner who takes part in the control or management of the partnership loses limited liability and is treated as a general partner, so the LP's actual commercial activity must align with the filed partnership agreement and tax filings

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: s. 239 ITA: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil reassessment with arrears interest under s. 161 and a 50% gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2)

Director personal liability: General partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's tax debts; directors of a corporate general partner are personally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST; the CRA may seek civil travel bans, bank account freezes, and enforcement against personal assets
Failure to File / Non-Remittance

Statutory sanction: T5013 late filing: $25 per day up to $2,500; GST/HST late filing: 1% to 6% of net tax plus daily interest; prosecution under s. 238 ITA carries fines from $1,000 to $25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 ITA and s. 323 ETA for unremitted amounts; the CRA can issue requirement payments to Kickstarter, Stripe, and PayPal and seize the partnership's bank accounts
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: s. 247(3) ITA re-determination of the arm's-length price; 10% penalty on the net transfer-pricing adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000) for failure to provide documentation; unresolved double taxation subject only to Mutual Agreement Procedure relief

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the mispricing may be personally assessed where gross negligence is established; personal assets of directors of the corporate general partner may be pursued in civil collections
Shell Company / AML / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 2,000,000 for FINTRAC non-compliance; Criminal Code s. 462.31 money-laundering offence with imprisonment up to 10 years; GAAR under s. 245 cancels tax benefits; the BC Registrar may strike the LP from the registry

Director personal liability: A limited partner who participates in control becomes personally liable for all partnership debts under s. 65 of the BC Partnership Act; directors of the corporate general partner face unlimited liability, asset seizure, and deregistration consequences
