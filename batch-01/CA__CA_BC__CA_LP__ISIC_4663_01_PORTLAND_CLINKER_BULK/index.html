Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Ottawa — federal income tax, GST/HST and other federal program administration; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administration. Portals: CRA My Business Account / GST/HST NETFILE and BC Tax Information and Self-Service (TISS / eTaxBC).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada obtains a statutory Business Number (BN) from the CRA as its federal tax identification number, generally by registration through CRA My Business Account or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The BN is used for the GST/HST program account and is the required taxpayer identifier for Canadian indirect tax filings. For BC PST, the partnership must register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance before making taxable sales or leases in BC; a separate BC PST account number is issued. GST/HST registration is mandatory if the partnership is not a small supplier, i.e., its total worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters exceed C$30,000, or where it makes taxable supplies in Canada outside the small-supplier exemption; voluntary registration is available to persons making zero-rated exports.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A British Columbia limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; the partnership computes its income and losses, but each partner is taxed on its allocated share. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is treated as a separate person and any registered status is held by the partnership itself. Non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source amounts allocated to them through the partnership, subject to applicable tax treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box or elective entity classification regime. The pass-through status of a limited partnership is statutory and generally mandatory. Certain deeming provisions under section 96 of the Income Tax Act govern partnership status and Part XIII withholding in respect of non-resident partners. Hybrid mismatch rules in Income Tax Act sections 18.2 to 18.4 are mandatory and do not operate as elective mechanisms; taxpayers may only restructure their commercial and payment terms to avoid disallowed deduction mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); BC PST: no general monetary registration threshold — PST registration is required before carrying on taxable sales or leasing activities in BC unless an explicitly exempt occasional-seller rule applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, a supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated where the supply is made to a non-resident person and the property is exported from Canada in accordance with the applicable regulations, or is delivered in Canada to a carrier for direct shipment outside Canada. Bulk Portland clinker loaded from a Canadian port onto an ocean vessel consigned to a foreign destination therefore qualifies for zero-rated GST when the supplier retains sufficient documentary proof of export and actual export occurs within a reasonable period. The associated ocean freight and vessel transportation services provided for exported goods are also zero-rated. BC PST does not apply to goods exported for use outside British Columbia, and no PST should be charged on a genuine export sale. Input tax credits for GST embedded in Canadian purchase costs may be reclaimed through the GST/HST net-filing or refund mechanism once the zero-rated export is substantiated.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration or ACI electronic export report for the ocean shipment
  • Bill of Lading (original ocean bill naming the foreign consignee, with vessel name, Canadian port of loading, and foreign port of discharge)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or foreign-exchange bank credit advice evidencing payment received from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice with full seller/buyer identification, HS classification 2523.10 (Portland cement), INCOTERMs, quantity, and unit price
  • Packing list and cargo manifest for the bulk clinker shipment
  • Export sales contract and any charter party or freight agreement demonstrating the movement of goods outside Canada
  • Weight certificate or loading survey for bulk clinker issued by an independent surveyor, where required by the vessel or customs authorities

Canada and British Columbia do not impose a real-time e-invoicing clearance mandate, digital stamping, or invoice-level tax-authority API validation. GST/HST registrants must file periodic GST/HST returns electronically in certain cases, including where a corporate registrant exceeds the prescribed C$1 million annual gross revenue threshold. BC PST returns are filed electronically through TISS/eTaxBC. Invoices must contain prescribed GST/HST information, including vendor name, Business Number, registration number, date, description, and tax charged; export invoices should be retained with complete transport, customs, and payment evidence. No pre-clearance of individual invoices is required.

CRA may disallow an export zero-rating claim if the documentary bundle is incomplete or inconsistent. The supply is then recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST, and CRA can deny the corresponding input tax credits, claw back any refund paid, and assess interest compounded daily. The normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years, but it is unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. A knowingly or grossly negligent false zero-rating claim triggers a penalty under Excise Tax Act section 285.1, which is the greater of C$100 and 25% of the overstated refund; willful evasion is prosecuted as an offence under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, with fines and imprisonment available. Although Canada has no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline, the 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is retained as an export compliance substantiation standard; failure to evidence payment receipt within 180 days without adequate explanation will materially increase audit risk and may lead to retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, and no prior regulatory approval is required for inbound capital contributions, outbound trade disbursements, or profit remittance. The only effective remittance hurdle is fiscal rather than monetary: non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under subsection 212(1) of the Income Tax Act on allocated Canadian-source income, generally at 25% unless reduced by treaty, and the general partner must withhold, remit, and file accordingly. Returns of contributed capital are not subject to withholding, but the capital-account trace must be documented to the bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation quota on export receipts; 100% of export proceeds may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency in any authorized institution. Monitoring obligations arise exclusively under the PCMLTFA: FINTRAC reporting applies to international electronic funds transfers and large cash transactions at or above the CAD 10,000 threshold, alongside five-year record-keeping duties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration; the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act govern legal tender and monetary policy, while exchange-rate and reserve-management functions are shared with the Department of Finance. Prudential supervision of banks is vested in OSFI, and AML/CFT supervision in FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for secondary collection and disbursement rails, Low as the primary treasury rail. Canada has no EU-style electronic-money institution licence; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Corpay) providing payment services to Canadian clients must register as money services businesses with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act where conducting retail payment activity. Such entities may efficiently clear low-value TT wires and supplier micropayments in USD, but they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, hold capital escrow, or provide trade finance; therefore the core operating account must remain with a Schedule I bank.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general (Federal Part I 15% + British Columbia 12%); the LP itself is fiscally transparent — the C$27 per C$100 of taxable income is borne by partners at their applicable rates; non-resident corporate partners are liable at the 27% combined rate on their Canadian-source business income allocation (no separate BC 'LLP' rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% — statutory Part XIII rate (ITA s.212) on dividends paid by a Canadian company to a non-resident. For the LP structure: no Part XIII withholding is imposed on a cash distribution to a non-resident partner; the non-resident is taxed under Part I on its allocated Canadian-source business income (net basis at the 27% combined rate). If, however, the LP holds shares of a Canadian operating subsidiary, the subsidiary's dividend to the LP (a deemed distribution to non-resident partners via the flow-through) is fully Part XIII-taxable at 25% to the non-resident beneficial owners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The LP must adopt a calendar-year fiscal period (ITA s.249.1). If all partners report on a calendar year: T5013 return due March 31 of the following year; otherwise due within five months after fiscal year-end. T5013SUM partner slips are due February 28 (paper) or March 31 (electronic) following the calendar year. BC Annual Report (Partnership Act registry) is due within 30 days of the LP's anniversary date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed with the LP's T5013 by the T5013 due date (the later of March 31 and five months after fiscal-year end).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (C$1 million) — cumulative fair-market value of all transactions with each non-resident non-arm's-length person (T106 trigger); CAD 5,000,000 (C$5 million) for a single loan agreement. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory when consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds CAD 1,000,000,000 (ITA s.233.8); within the C$1M–C$1B band, the CRA expects a contemporaneous Master File/Local File package on request.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone ESR code, but the substance doctrine operates through: (i) GAAR (ITA s.245) denial of a tax benefit from an avoidance transaction where there is no bona fide purpose other than tax; (ii) the sham doctrine, where a veneer LP with no BC employees, premises, or records is set aside; (iii) ITA s.96(2.1)/(2.4) at-risk rules: a limited partner's deductible loss is capped at its contributed capital plus undrawn income/share of partnership income, and unused limited-partnership losses expire after 10 years; (iv) BC Partnership Act requires a genuine registered office and annual registry returns. The 'economic substance' quality of the LP is measured by real receipt, inventory risk, credit risk and decision-making in BC.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion — false filings (ITA s.239)

Statutory sanction: Criminal: indictment — fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; summary — 50%–200% fine plus imprisonment up to 2 years; civil s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax, plus interest at the prescribed rate (accrued daily).

Director personal liability: Each director, officer or agent who 'directed, authorized, assented to, acquiesced in or participated in' the offence is individually liable (s.242); CRA garnishes personal and trust accounts; directors of the GP are jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted Part XIII amounts (s.227.1); criminal warrants become INTERPOL Red Notices, freezing international travel.
Transfer pricing misstatement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at arm's length under s.247(2); penalty under s.247(3): 10% of the net upward adjustment (when the adjustment exceeds C$5,000); if documentation is deemed grossly negligent, the 50% gross-negligence penalty is stacked; T106 late filing: C$24/day (max C$2,400).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false T5013/T106 schedules face personal gross-negligence assessments; a s.160 assessment transfers liability to non-resident limited partners who received distributions within three years while understatements were outstanding; the CRA may examine directors under oath and seize bank accounts.
Shell LP and GAAR abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization with denial of the loss/deduction and reassessment of the full margin (s.245); penalty: 50% of the tax deliberately misstated; BC Registrar strikes the LP off the register and forfeits the LP's name and assets to the Crown where annual reports are two years in arrears; the court pierces the veil under the 'shield or cloak' test.

Director personal liability: A limited partner who 'takes part in control or management' of the LP loses limited-liability protection and becomes jointly and severally liable for the LP's debts (BC Partnership Act); the GP's directors are exposed to claims in fraud and misrepresentation; courts can order restitution out of the directors' personal assets; bail conditions prevent travel abroad pending trial.
AML / sanctions — PCMLTFA and SEN

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties: up to C$500,000 per contravention for a first occurrence, C$1,000,000 for a repeat; criminal conviction under PCMLTFA: up to C$500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment for knowingly directing an offence; under the Special Economic Measures Act: fines up to C$25,000,000 and 5 years imprisonment, asset seizure and freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who knew or acquiesced are personally prosecuted; the BC Civil Forfeiture Office may forfeit the LP's partnership interests and the personal assets of officers traceable to the offence; federal border agencies and INTERPOL Red Notices restrain travel and cross-border wealth movement.
Failure to file and unremitted withholding

Statutory sanction: s.227(1)/(2) penalties: 10% of the unremitted amount (a further 20% applies to the second default within three years); arrears interest accrues daily at the prescribed rate plus 4%; late filing penalties (C$30/day, cap C$2,500); failure to file the T5013 for a year in which a non-resident partner exists is a strict-liability offence.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its directors are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's remittance debts (s.227.1); the CRA can register a federal lien (s.223) against the GP's personal property, garnish receivables, require the BC Registry to flag the LP as non-compliant, and — where a criminal warrant issues — the person cannot board international flights.
