Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A business corporation is a 'taxable Canadian corporation' under subsection 89(1) of the Income Tax Act. Relevant elections include: (a) section 216 election for non-resident rental income; (b) joint election under subsection 124(4) for income tax allocation; (c) hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.4 and 18.5; and (d) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status designations for tax rate and refundable tax purposes. No electoral impact exists on GST/HST status, which follows the entity's registration threshold.

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act, a supply of a service is zero-rated if made to a non-resident and the service is not delivered to any person present in Canada, is not performed in respect of real property in Canada, is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and is not for the benefit of a GST/HST-registered non-resident carrying on a commercial activity in Canada. Remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software subscriptions (remote desktop access and unattended machine telemetry) are eligible for zero-rating when supplied electronically to non-resident customers who are not registered for GST/HST and the service is provided from Canada without any in-Canadian situs nexus.

  • Executed software subscription or SaaS agreement naming the non-resident purchaser, describing the remote screen-share/IoT telemetry service, and specifying the delivery architecture
  • Commercial invoice stating 'zero-rated supply to non-resident', showing the CRA Business Number and no GST/HST charged
  • Foreign exchange remittance or bank advice evidencing payment from the non-resident's bank account to the Canadian corporation's British Columbia bank account
  • Technical delivery and network logs demonstrating that the service was supplied remotely to non-resident users and did not access or utilize tangible personal property in Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or government devolution clearance mandate exists in Canada. GST/HST invoices must contain the prescribed particulars set out in the GST/HST Information Regulations and the Business Number. Electronic invoices are permitted if the content is complete and legible. Mandatory electronic filing and electronic remittance apply to registrants above certain size thresholds, but there is no continuous transaction control or mandatory digital stamping.

CRA may reverse a zero-rating claim upon audit if the documentation fails to establish the non-resident recipient, the Canadian situs exclusion, or the actual performance of the service. Reassessment will include the 5% GST/HST on the supply amount, and arrears interest will run from the due date of the original return. Penalties for false statements or omissions include a 10% substantial misstatement penalty and a 50% gross negligence penalty for knowingly or repeatedly providing false documentation. Criminal tax evasion charges under the Excise Tax Act may result in fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to two years. The normal four-year statutory reassessment period is extended to ten years where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect of books and records is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility with no exchange controls. Profit remittances are permitted without FX approval, but are subject to Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) tax compliance: non-resident shareholders face Part XIII withholding tax (default 25%, reduced by applicable tax treaties, e.g., 15% on dividends under the Canada-US treaty) and must file an NR4 annual information return. A Canadian corporation must pass the solvency test under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) to pay dividends, and all dividends must be declared in Canadian dollars unless the entity explicitly maintains capital in foreign currency. No statutory approval is required for foreign direct investment or profit repatriation, except FINTRAC reporting of any incoming/outgoing international electronic transfer of CAD $10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any residency-based conversion mandate. Exporters may retain foreign currency (typically USD) in designated CAD/USD corporate accounts and choose convert timing freely. However, all cross-border wire transfers exceeding CAD $10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting, and the corporation must maintain auditable records of trade invoices and telecommunication/software licence export contracts to support the non-residence status of income. For remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software (ISIC 6201), export proceeds derived from subscription licences are treated as business income and must be reported on Canadian corporate tax returns (T2).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary policy and systemic stability); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking regulation; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF compliance; no single dedicated foreign exchange administration – FX matters are governed by the Currency Act, OSFI guidelines, and FINTRAC transaction reporting rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (as supplementary payment rails, not as primary treasury account). Legal constraints: Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, PayPal Business) that are not registered under Canadian legislation cannot hold customer funds as deposit liabilities, are not CDIC-insured, and must themselves be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC to receive cross-border transfers. A British Columbia corporation may open an EMI account solely to route low-value subscription payments from Stripe/Paddle, but the settlement funds must be swept daily to a tier-1 chartered bank account to avoid concentration risk, comply with OSFI outsourcing guidance, and ensure legal recourse for treasury operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal-provincial general corporate income tax rate of 27% (federal 15% + British Columbia general corporate rate 12%); BC small business rate 2% on active business income up to CA$500,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation (CA_CORP) to non-resident shareholders; treaty-reduced to 5% for a corporate shareholder that beneficially owns at least 10% of the voting stock (e.g., Canada–US Treaty Article X), or 15% for portfolio investors; other Canadian tax treaties commonly provide 5%–15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due 6 months after fiscal year-end (e.g., December 31 year-end due June 30 of the following year). Tax balance-due day: 2 months after year-end, extended to 3 months for a qualifying CCPC claiming the small business deduction; instalments required for corporations with annual tax payable exceeding CA$3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes no explicit Local File / Master File statutory thresholds at the CA_CORP level; OECD-aligned contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is administratively required (CRA IC 87-2R) for all significant non-arm's length cross-border transactions, and penalty protection depends on reasonable efforts with contemporaneous documentation. Mandatory filing triggers: Form T106 (Request for Information and Reporting of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) where aggregate value of reportable transactions exceeds CA$50,000; Country-by-Country Report (Form RC4649) where the MNE group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds CA$750 million. CRA audit focus is triggered for recurring intercompany license and service fees exceeding CA$1 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not adopt US-style or UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations; instead, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) denies the tax benefit of an avoidance transaction lacking a bona fide purpose or economic substance, and CRA's 'central management and control' jurisprudence treats a nominal Canadian corporation as a non-resident if board direction and decision-making occur offshore. For a remote-screen-share/IoT carrier, commercial substance is established through: local employees, active management, cloud infrastructure contracts, and arm's length contractual allocation of intellectual property and risk; a shell holding company that merely licenses Canadian SaaS revenue to a low-tax affiliate is a fatal GAAR risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Willful Omission of Revenue)

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239 and Excise Tax Act s.327: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus mandatory interest; default penalty under ITA s.162(2) and gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; CRA may freeze bank accounts and obtain court orders to seize assets.

Director personal liability: Director and officer liability under ITA s.227.1 and ETA s.323 for unremitted source deductions, GST/PST, and Part XIII withholdings: CRA may assess directors personally for the unremitted amounts if the corporation defaults; the due-diligence defense requires demonstration of supervisory efforts; criminal tax evasion can expose directors to personal fines, restitution orders, and imprisonment, with extradition arrest warrants effectively restricting international travel.
Aggressive Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(3) transfer-pricing penalty: 10% of the net adjustment (the excess of the adjusted amount over the arm's length amount), with a minimum penalty of CA$5,000; the penalty is waived only where the taxpayer makes reasonable efforts and documents its allocation methodology contemporaneously; CRA may also reassess previously closed years (waiver of normal assessment period for misrepresentation).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false T106 returns or who direct the pricing structure can be named in CRA gross-negligence penalty assessments; under the 'knowing participation' doctrine in tax matters, a director may be jointly and severally liable for civil penalties; criminal charges under s.239 can follow where the transfer pricing scheme is designed to conceal taxable income.
Shell Company / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s.245: the avoidance benefit is entirely disallowed, foreign tax deductions denied, and the amount is re-characterized as Canadian-source income subject to Part I tax plus a 25% GAAR penalty on the benefit; CRA can disregard the shell's legal persona for Canadian tax liability; judicial precedence exposes the ultimate Canadian shareholder to assessments on the deemed dividend.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil for 'sham' or 'artificial transaction' findings: courts may hold controlling shareholders and directors personally liable for the corporation's unpaid tax and penalties where the corporation is a mere facade; directors face civil restitution, BC Business Corporations Act s.118 liability for shareholder/creditor losses, and possible travel bans or restricted exit from Canada applied through court-ordered asset freezes and passport surrender for executives charged with tax fraud.
Cross-Border Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ITA s.215 liability: the payer is absolutely liable for the unremitted withholding tax, regardless of good faith; penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount plus daily interest; CRA may deny the expense deduction for the underlying royalty under s.247(2) or s.67 where withholding compliance is absent; late-filing penalties for NR4 information returns apply (CA$25 per day per return, up to CA$2,500).

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for any unremitted Part XIII withholding if the corporation is unable to pay; CRA routinely pursues director assessments, and failure to exercise due diligence (e.g., delegating to a bookkeeper without supervision) exposes personal bankruptcy risk; fraudulent diversion of the withholding funds elevates the act to criminal tax evasion, carrying imprisonment up to 5 years and a travel restriction through an arrest warrant issued for the director's apprehension.
