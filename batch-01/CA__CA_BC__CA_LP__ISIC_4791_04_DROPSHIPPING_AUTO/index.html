Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal income tax and GST/HST; portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); customs: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Single Window; British Columbia Ministry of Finance — PST via eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership under the BC Partnership Act (RSBC 1996, c. 348, Part 3) through the BC Registrar of Companies (OneStop Business Registry). Step 2: Obtain a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or, for partnerships with all non-resident partners, Form RC4607, online via CRA My Business Account. Step 3: Add the RP (GST/HST) program account and, if required, the RM (import/export) program account for CBSA border transactions; the T5013 tax year is assigned automatically. Step 4: Register for BC PST through eTaxBC using the BN plus PST account number (Application for PST Registration, Form PST-121) if the LP sells taxable goods to BC consumers above the remote-seller threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and provincial income tax: under ITA s. 96 the partnership is not a taxable entity; income, gains, losses and credits flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. It is not eligible for entity-level taxation; limited partners have loss-restriction and at-risk rules (ITA ss. 96(2.1)–(2.4)). Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment, subject to treaty relief, and Part XIII withholding may apply to certain partnership allocations under ITA s. 212(13.1). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and provincial income tax: under ITA s. 96 the partnership is not a taxable entity; income, gains, losses and credits flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. It is not eligible for entity-level taxation; limited partners have loss-restriction and at-risk rules (ITA ss. 96(2.1)–(2.4)). Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment, subject to treaty relief, and Part XIII withholding may apply to certain partnership allocations under ITA s. 212(13.1). No Canadian tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; flow-through treatment is mandatory. Elections available to the LP: (1) ITA s. 216 election by a non-resident partner to file a special Part I return on rental/timber income rather than Part XIII withholding; (2) ITA s. 249.1 partnership fiscal-period election to adopt a non-calendar year only with Ministerial approval (calendar-year default); (3) hybrid-mismatch integrity rules (ITA ss. 94.2–94.4) apply where the LP is treated as an opaque corporation in a foreign jurisdiction and require prescribed disclosure of hybrid financial instrument/entity positions; (4) GST/HST elections are limited — ETA s. 156 corporate close-connection election is not available to LPs; instead the LP claims input tax credits and may apply for non-resident GST rebate (ETA ss. 252–253) if funds are not credited.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD $30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including services and zero-rated supplies) triggers mandatory registration (ETA s. 148); below the threshold registration is voluntary. BC PST: remote sellers and marketplace facilitators must register for PST once PST-taxable sales to BC purchasers exceed CAD $10,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters; sellers with a physical or economic presence in BC must register regardless of a threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: s. 5 zero-rates tangible personal property exported from Canada and delivered to the recipient outside Canada in a continuous journey by the supplier or an agent; s. 7 zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident person where the service is not performed in Canada, or is performed in Canada but relates to real property or tangible personal property situated outside Canada. For automated dropshipping: if the LP is the supplier of record and goods ship from a Canadian fulfilment centre to a foreign buyer, the supply is zero-rated and the LP retains full ITC entitlement for attributable inputs. If goods are sourced from a foreign supplier and shipped directly to a Canadian buyer (import dropshipping), no export occurs: the LP is the importer of record, GST is payable on importation (ETA s. 212) and GST/PST applies to the domestic sale. If goods are shipped between foreign suppliers and foreign customers without touching Canada, the Canadian LP has no Canadian indirect tax nexus for those flows.

  • Electronic B13A Export Declaration filed via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System for goods valued over CAD $2,000 (or controlled/goods subject to permit); low-value courier shipments rely on the carrier cargo manifest
  • Transport document: commercial invoice, Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or courier waybill showing the foreign destination, dispatch date, and consignee (continuous-journey evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or banking/settlement proof: SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or Stripe/PayPal settlement reports evidencing payment from the foreign buyer; Canadian law imposes no statutory 180-day repatriation deadline, and the schema default of 180 days is an AML/correspondent-banking benchmark rather than an ITA/ETA requirement
  • CBSA electronic export report and cargo-control number; customs broker records where applicable
  • Commercial contract file: purchase orders, automated dropshipping agreements, warehouse dispatch notes and delivery tracking events (ETA s. 286 books and records)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital-stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing mandate at federal or provincial level. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD $1.5 million and for all non-individual registrants carrying on business in Canada; paper filing is otherwise permitted. B2B/B2C e-invoices must contain the prescribed ETA s. 3 information (vendor name, BN, date, description, tax amount), but no government pre-clearance or API tax authority clearance is required. For customs, the CBSA mandates electronic reporting of imports and exports through the Canadian Single Window (Integrated Import Declaration and Canadian Export Reporting System), and the CRA requires API/EDI exchange only for high-volume customs brokers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains no exchange-control regime. The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account (trade in goods and services) and capital-account (capital subscriptions, loan repayments, profit distributions, and equity repatriation) transactions. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was terminated in 1951, and no prior-approval, quantitative restriction, or repatriation-licence mechanism applies to profit remittance from British Columbia. Operational frictions are tax- and reporting-based only: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at 25% on treaty-protected passive payments (treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% dividends and 0% certain interest under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention), Part I Canadian-source business income compliance for non-resident partners of the LP, and FINTRAC's CAD 10,000 reporting threshold for cross-border electronic funds transfers. For a BC Limited Partnership with pass-through classification, business profits allocated to non-resident partners are not exchange-controlled but are subject to Canadian income-tax filing and payment obligations; no profit-remittance approval is required by the Bank of Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for collection, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export or dropshipping sales proceeds. Cross-border receivables may be retained freely in foreign currency (including USD settlement balances in the LP's bank accounts). Compliance triggers are limited to FINTRAC EFT reporting for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, the banks' large-transaction record-keeping rules, and invoice- and contract-retention duties under the Income Tax Act supporting the LP's revenue recognition.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential supervision of domestic banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), anti-money-laundering oversight by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and payment-system oversight under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act administered jointly with Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Canada does not recognize the EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence; non-bank payment service providers must register as Retail Payment Activity Providers (RPAPs) with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which came into force in November 2024 with a transitional registration period for existing providers and mandatory registration for new providers before commencing Canadian retail payment activities. A BC LP may maintain settlement or payout accounts with authorized RPAPs such as Wise Canada or Airwallex, but an unregistered foreign EMI cannot lawfully provide payment services to Canadian end-users or hold Canadian settlement balances, and RPAPs must safeguard client funds in a regulated Canadian financial institution. For an automated dropshipping model, the recommended architecture is a Tier-1 bank operating account for primary treasury liquidity with a registered RPAP used only for multi-currency disbursement acceleration; prime responsibility for settlement integrity remains with the Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital contributions are remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting partner's bank to the LP's CAD operating account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank, with field 70 containing the explicit narrative 'Capital contribution to [LP name] per Limited Partnership Agreement dated [date]' and the LP's CRA Business Number. Transfers from the United States may be routed via correspondent CHIPS/Fedwire using the receiving Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent ABA and the LP's account number in a USD concentration sub-ledger. All inbound EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting by the receiving institution, and the LP must attach the capital-call notice and source-of-funds statement to the onboarding file. For large injections exceeding CAD 1 million, settlement should be effected through Lynx, Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system, where the remitting institution is a Lynx participant. If a Capital Escrow Account is established, it must carry a restricted-usage instruction releasing funds to the operating account only upon satisfaction of documented operational milestones set out in the partnership agreement.Supplier payments for dropshipping inventory are executed by SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) for routine open-account settlements, referencing the commercial invoice and the supplier's SWIFT BIC, with the LP's operating account as the ordering account. For volume pre-purchase programmes, purchase-order financing, and larger inventory commitments, a commercial letter of credit (sight or usance) is opened by the Tier-1 bank on behalf of the LP, with documents against payment and compliance with UCP 600. Domestic Canadian supplier disbursements clear through Lynx or the Automated Clearing Settlement System; USD-denominated disbursements clear via CHIPS/Fedwire through the bank's U.S. correspondent network and may be consolidated in the LP's USD sub-account to minimize conversion spreads. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Not directly applicable — British Columbia Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent. Income is allocated to partners; a corporate partner is subject to combined federal (15% general / 9% small business) and BC provincial (8% general / 2% small business) rates, yielding approximately 23% general and 11% small business effective combined headline rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act on deemed dividends and distributions to non-resident limited partners where the distribution is characterized as a dividend. Profit distributions to partners that constitute partnership income allocations are not Part XIII dividends but may be subject to Part I tax if the partner carries on business in Canada through the LP.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) where the aggregate fair market value of controlled transactions with non-resident related parties exceeds CAD 1,000,000 for goods and CAD 1,000,000 for services. Mandatory Form T106 – Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents is required above this threshold on an annual basis. Below the threshold, reasonable documentation must nonetheless be kept to support arm's length pricing under section 247.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in UAE-style jurisdictions. However, the CRA applies the economic substance doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), the sham and business purpose doctrines, and the non-recognition of artificial or stepping-stone arrangements. Ontario and BC provincial courts and the Tax Court of Canada can disregard transactions lacking real economic substance and commercial purpose. The BC LP should maintain office, bank accounts, sales infrastructure, decision-making records, and IT systems in Canada; otherwise a non-resident principal or platform owner may be deemed to be carrying on business in Canada, creating permanent establishment exposure and potential application of the 'carrying on business in Canada' rules under section 253.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Systems

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated taxes under subsection 163(2). Willful evasion is a strict-liability criminal offence under subsection 239(1): a fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA may also audit 3 years (or 4 years where a T5013 is considered to contain a misrepresentation attributable to neglect).

Director personal liability: General or limited partners with management authority and directors of a corporate general partner may be personally assessed under section 160 for unreported income and penalties. Criminal prosecution may carry personal fines, restitution orders, and travel bans for non-resident partners. The BC Partnership Act provides that a limited partner is not personally liable beyond contributed capital, but veil-piercing occurs upon participation in control. A limited partner who influences the daily operations of the automated dropshipping business becomes 'participating in control' and assumes joint and several liability for partnership obligations and taxes.
Shell Company and Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: The CRA may disregard the LP under the sham and business-purpose doctrines, reassess the non-resident as directly carrying on business in Canada, impose Part I tax and Part XIII withholding, and apply a 50% gross-negligence penalty. Where the arrangement is marketed as 'foreign confidentiality through LP', the CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to deny tax benefits and impose interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: The promoter and the corporate general partner's directors may be accountable for third-party penalties of CAD 100,000 or more. A travel ban under the Income Tax Act may issue against non-resident directors who are assessed for unreported taxes after failed CRA collection attempts within Canada. Criminal charges of counselling tax evasion carry liability up to 5 years imprisonment.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties range from CAD 1,000 to CAD 100,000 per contravention for entities; wilful non-compliance yields fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. FINTRAC may compel production and publicize non-compliant operations.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner and any limited partner acting in control are liable for AML penalties if the LP fails to establish a compliance program and internal control framework. Repeat violations trigger prohibition orders, suspension of payment processor access, and personal assessment for penalties where the entity is insolvent.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 247(2), the CRA may adjust the amounts to arm's length; penalties under subsection 247(3) of 10% of the net underassessment (reduced by the portion attributable to a full and truthful disclosure to be penalized) if adequate documentation was not made or if the net adjustment exceeds CAD 5,000,000. Interest accrues on all reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the inflated transfer pricing arrangement may be assessed under section 160 for amounts that the LP cannot pay, where they participated in the transfer-pricing scheme. Non-resident directors may be personally served by CRA and prohibited from re-entering Canada until the assessment, interest, and penalties are satisfied.
Regulatory and Industry Vexation

Statutory sanction: The CRA may audit, reassess, impose a 50% penalty, and require disclosure under Criminal Code s.380 for fraud exceeding CAD 5,000. The BC Ministry of Finance may simultaneously levy penalty and interest for unremitted provincial sales tax if the LP fails to register under the PST Act for taxable supplies sold to BC consumers.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST and PST under excise and BC provisions; travel bans and asset freezes may be used to collect. Prosecution for fraud affecting public revenue carries a maximum 14-year sentence.
