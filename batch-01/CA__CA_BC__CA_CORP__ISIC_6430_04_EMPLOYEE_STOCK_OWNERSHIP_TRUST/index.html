Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or flow-through election for Business Corporations; every corporation is an entity-level taxpayer under Part I ITA (residents taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits; non-residents taxable only on Canadian-source income). Principal elections available: (1) s. 85(1) ITA rollover on property transfers to/from the corporation; (2) s. 83(2) ITA capital dividend account election; (3) s. 89/90 paid-up capital elections; (4) s. 261 ITA functional currency election; (5) s. 7 ITA stock option plan elections for employee share purchase/ESOP plans; (6) s. 144.2 ITA Employee Ownership Trust (EOT) regime (effective 2024) with 10-year capital gains deferral on qualifying share sales; (7) s. 111(4)/256.8 loss restriction and control acquisition elections. Hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.2, 18.3, 20.9, 212.4 ITA, BEPS Action 2) apply mechanically from July 1, 2022 without elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 148): mandatory GST/HST registration once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the current or any preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (zero-rated exports excluded). Export 0% zero-rating conditions

V, Part VII); (3) qualifying ESOP/trust administration services to a foreign non-registered plan sponsor or employer qualify for s. 7 zero-rating; documentary evidence of non-resident status, service contract and Canadian-dollar remittance must be maintained contemporaneously.

  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Canada to the non-resident destination

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice pre-authorization, digital stamping or CTC (continuous transaction control) regime in Canada. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) with payment through CRA My Payment; books and records (electronic invoices, contracts, bank records) must be retained for six years. Voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Standard (CRA 2023) adopts Peppol BIS 3.0 / EN 16931 on an opt-in basis. Physical exports require electronic filing of the B13A export declaration through CBSA CERS; no stamping process.

CRA Export Verification audits: exporter must produce, generally within 90 days of written demand, full export substantiation package (B13A, bills of lading, remittance records); non-compliance reverses zero-rating and reassesses GST at 5% on gross consideration with interest. Reassessment windows: standard 4-year limit; unlimited where misrepresentation arises from neglect, wilful default or fraud (ETA s. 298(4); ITA s. 152(4)). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with full current and capital account convertibility; the Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating and fully convertible. There are no statutory controls on inward or outward capital movements, dividend remittance, or capital repatriation. Profit remittance from a British Columbia (BC) corporation is unrestricted at the exchange-control level, subject only to income tax withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (typically 25% statutory, reduced to 5-15% under applicable tax treaties) and to corporate solvency tests under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia). Capital account injections (share subscriptions, shareholder loans, and ESOP trust contributions) may enter and leave Canada without regulatory approval, but amounts exceeding CAD 10,000 have mandatory reporting obligations to FINTRAC and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for cross-border currency or monetary instruments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements for export proceeds or foreign currency receipts. Exporters and treasury entities may retain, convert, or repatriate foreign currency freely. All FX transactions are executed at market rates through authorized financial institutions, and no timeline or conversion ratio is mandated for ESOP trust distributions, share settlement proceeds, or dividend repatriations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in conjunction with the Department of Finance Canada (Minister of Finance) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no standalone Foreign Exchange Administration; foreign exchange policy is implemented through the floating-rate framework under the Currency Act, while cross-border fund transfer monitoring is performed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for ancillary treasury functions (FX conversion, cross-border expense payments) but Low for escrow and equity-settlement functions. Legal constraints: Foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not banks in Canada and cannot hold deposits in the legal sense; they must be registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, if offering payment services, must obtain a Retail Payment Activity (RPA) license from the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (in force November 1, 2024). An unlicensed foreign EMI cannot lawfully open a Canadian settlement account or clear through Lynx/ACSS. For Employee Stock Ownership Trust administration — where participant funds, option exercise proceeds, and statutory payroll distributions are held — a Schedule I Canadian bank or a regulated trust company is required to maintain statutory trust protections and CDIC insurability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections (equity subscription, shareholder loans, ESOP trust contribution) must be routed through the Canadian banking system via SWIFT MT103 with mandatory purpose code. Canada accepts the OECD Balance of Payments purpose codes: use code '4000' for investment fund shares/equity capital or code '4100' for intercompany debt; for BC corporate filings, the reference field must state 'BC Corporation Number [XXXXXXXX] - ESOP Trust Capital Injection'. For inbound wire amounts exceeding CAD 100,000, the receiving bank will require pre-advice and a completed FINTRAC 'Large Cash Transaction / EFT Report' (ECR) declaration; beneficial ownership and source-of-funds attestations (within the last 30 days) are non-negotiable. Verification protocols: the bank's compliance team will match the wire's originator line against the KYC dossier, validate the SWIFT BIC of the sender, and confirm the CAD/USD FX conversion rate on the value date; if settled in USD, the bank credits a USD clearing account (US Federal Reserve / CHIPS correspondent) and performs an internal FX conversion to CAD at the daily corporate rate. For high-value injections exceeding CAD 10 million, use Lynx (Payments Canada's real-time gross settlement system) with a wire-release cut-off of 14:30 ET; same-day settlement is guaranteed under Lynx. (2) Domestic payroll disbursements — standard pre-authorized debit (PAD) via ACSS batch clearing with a two-business-day settlement cycle, or same-day Lynx for time-critical full payment. (3) Trade-related disbursements (payments to vendors of share administration platforms, auditors, or regulatory fees) — SWIFT MT103 direct transfer, or a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued under UCP600 rules for larger procurements, with the BC corporate treasury as applicant and the bank as issuing institution; sight drafts are standard, with a 5-business-day examination period under UCP600 Article 14. (4) Equity transfer settlement — the final leg of an option exercise or share repurchase is cleared via the designated securities custodian (e.g., CDS Clearing and Depository Services through a Canada-registered broker) rather than the bank wire system, with cash settlement routed through the operational account. All outbound SWIFT MT103 instructions require the beneficiary bank's BIC/SWIFT code, IBAN or account number, and any applicable intermediary bank routing details; sanctions screening is applied at the transaction level under OSFI Guideline 13 (FINTRAC interpretations), and rejected transactions must be repurposed with a corrected compliance narrative within 48 hours.Deliverable FX outright forwards (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR up to 12-month tenors) with a Canadian Schedule I bank, transacted under a standard ISDA FX Master Agreement (2018) or bank's internal Treasury Master Agreement; documentation requires a CAD 500,000 minimum line and collateral posting under a Credit Support Annex (CSA).FX swaps (roll/swap of value-date) permitted for liquidity management of the ESOP trust's payroll cycle, executed only with OSFI-regulated counterparties.Cross-currency swaps (CAD vs. USD/EUR) for long-term hedging of the trust's share redemption liabilities, permitted under ISDA 2002 with a bank-approved legal opinion; cleared bilaterally or through CDCC (Canadian Derivatives Clearing Corporation) for standardized tenors.Vanilla OTC FX call/put options (European-style) for premium-based caps on conversion costs of participant distributions; physical delivery settled on exercise; no barrier or exotic options permitted for treasury hedging of a 'Low_Risk_Standard' entity.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — technically permissible for hedging exposures to non-convertible emerging-market currencies (e.g., CNY, INR) incurred by non-resident employees' settlement legs, but only with the booking bank's risk approval and an underlying real exposure; CAD/USD NDFs are not standard because the Canadian dollar is fully deliverable and clearing occurs in Lynx/CHIPS.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium for ancillary treasury functions (FX conversion, cross-border expense payments) but Low for escrow and equity-settlement functions. Legal constraints: Foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not banks in Canada and cannot hold deposits in the legal sense; they must be registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, if offering payment services, must obtain a Retail Payment Activity (RPA) license from the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (in force November 1, 2024). An unlicensed foreign EMI cannot lawfully open a Canadian settlement account or clear through Lynx/ACSS. For Employee Stock Ownership Trust administration — where participant funds, option exercise proceeds, and statutory payroll distributions are held — a Schedule I Canadian bank or a regulated trust company is required to maintain statutory trust protections and CDIC insurability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T1066 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed with the T2 when aggregate non-arm's length cross-border transactions with a non-resident related party exceed CAD 1,000,000 during the taxation year, or when a non-arm's length loan or advance exceeds CAD 100,000 in principal. CRA Information Circular 87-2R and administrative practice require contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation once these thresholds are exceeded; documentation must be prepared no later than the T2 filing deadline to sustain a reasonable-efforts defence against the s.247(3) penalty. Multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000 are subject to country-by-country reporting (CbCR) obligations administered by the CRA via its electronic portal; master file and local file information must be produced within 30 days of an audit request.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute equivalent to offshore ESR regimes; however, the substance doctrine is enforced through (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA), which imposes a 50% penalty on tax-benefit planning arrangements lacking a bona fide business purpose; (ii) the common-law sham doctrine, which recharacterizes contractual arrangements if the parties' true intent differs from the written documents; (iii) the risk-allocation and contractual-substance requirements embedded in s.247 transfer pricing rules, requiring that contractual risk allocations be consistent with the parties' actual conduct, personnel, capital, and risk-bearing capacity; and (iv) the anti-bare-trust and nominee rules in Part XIII, which attribute Canadian-source dividend and interest income to the beneficial owner rather than the intermediary. An ESOP trust administrator operating in BC must demonstrate real staff, office premises, contractual agreements, board minutes, bank accounts, fee billing records, and compliance systems to satisfy both CRA audit standards and any treaty limitation-on-benefits or principal-purpose-test provisions.
