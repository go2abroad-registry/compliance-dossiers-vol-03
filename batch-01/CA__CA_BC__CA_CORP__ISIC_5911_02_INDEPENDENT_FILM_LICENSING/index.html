Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporate Registry, Ministry of Finance, Province of British Columbia; Registrar of Companies, Victoria, BC (portals: BC Corporate Online at www.corporateonline.gov.bc.ca and BC OneStop Business Registry)

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c. 62 (BCA) as the primary formation statute: ss. 10–16 (incorporation procedure and name requirements), s. 17 (Certificate of Incorporation), s. 29 and Schedule 1 (standard articles), ss. 108 and 110 (director consent and qualifications), ss. 190–193 (share subscription and issuance), Part 3 Division 1 (registered office and records office), s. 229 (Notice of Articles); federal alternative if CBCA incorporation is preferred: Canada Business Corporations Act, RSC 1985, c. C-44, ss. 8–20.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notice of Incorporation (Form 1) filed under s. 16 of the BCA, accompanied by the Incorporation Agreement, Notice of Registered Office, Notice of Directors, and Articles (if the incorporators elect articles other than the standard Schedule 1 articles); the Registrar issues the Certificate of Incorporation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement for an ordinary private business corporation under the BCA; shares may be issued for no par value and for such consideration as the directors determine. Mandatory escrow rules apply only to prescribed public-company or Securities Act triggered distributions, which are not engaged by a privately held independent film licensing corporation.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a municipal occupancy permit and fire safety inspection under the BC Fire Code (Office Occupancy Class E), including means-of-egress certification and automatic sprinkler verification where required; complete a structural load inspection for cinematic archive shelving and storage vaults; register with WorkSafeBC and maintain occupational health and safety compliance, including first-aid certification, in accordance with the Workers Compensation Act, RSBC 1996, c. 492, Part 3.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement for an ordinary private business corporation under the BCA; shares may be issued for no par value and for such consideration as the directors determine. Mandatory escrow rules apply only to prescribed public-company or Securities Act triggered distributions, which are not engaged by a privately held independent film licensing corporation.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no sectoral industrial licensing board governs ISIC 5911 film copyright distribution in British Columbia. A general municipal licence is issued by the City of Vancouver, Business Licence Division. No CRTC broadcasting distribution undertaking licence is triggered because the corporation is engaged only in private distribution and licensing of motion picture copyrights.

Category: No statutory operating or manufacturing permit is required; however, the corporation must display a City of Vancouver Business Licence (General Commercial Division, category: Motion Picture and Video Distribution) at its physical premises as a condition of lawful operation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/taxes plus CRA My Business Account (MyBA); GST/HST returns filed via GST/HST NETFILE or compliant accounting software; BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance and is not within CRA jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

For a federal or provincial business corporation owned by non-residents: (1) incorporate federally under the CBCA or provincially under the BC Business Corporations Act, or file an extra-provincial registration in BC; (2) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation, or for non-resident branches apply through Form RC1 / Business Registration Online; (3) open CRA program accounts via the RC1 schedule: Corporate Income Tax (RC account), GST/HST (RT account), Payroll (RP account if employees); (4) register for GST/HST once taxable supplies exceed the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold or earlier where zero-rated exports justify input tax credit recovery; (5) obtain BC PST vendor licence from the BC Ministry of Finance only if selling taxable tangible goods or specified software/IP in BC; film distribution licensing requires no statutory industry licence, but TIN/BN evidence is mandatory on all invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian corporations are taxed as separate entities: a resident corporation pays federal Part I income tax on worldwide income (net 15% federal base rate, plus 12% BC provincial corporate income tax, subject to general rate reductions and surtaxes); a non-resident corporation earning Canadian-source film-licensing royalties is subject to Part XIII 25% gross withholding (or Part I net basis where a permanent establishment exists or a s.216 election is filed); the independent film licensor is not a pass-through entity; no check-the-box equivalent exists in Canada. — For a federal or provincial business corporation owned by non-residents: (1) incorporate federally under the CBCA or provincially under the BC Business Corporations Act, or file an extra-provincial registration in BC; (2) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation, or for non-resident branches apply through Form RC1 / Business Registration Online; (3) open CRA program accounts via the RC1 schedule: Corporate Income Tax (RC account), GST/HST (RT account), Payroll (RP account if employees); (4) register for GST/HST once taxable supplies exceed the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold or earlier where zero-rated exports justify input tax credit recovery; (5) obtain BC PST vendor licence from the BC Ministry of Finance only if selling taxable tangible goods or specified software/IP in BC; film distribution licensing requires no statutory industry licence, but TIN/BN evidence is mandatory on all invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian corporations are taxed as separate entities: a resident corporation pays federal Part I income tax on worldwide income (net 15% federal base rate, plus 12% BC provincial corporate income tax, subject to general rate reductions and surtaxes); a non-resident corporation earning Canadian-source film-licensing royalties is subject to Part XIII 25% gross withholding (or Part I net basis where a permanent establishment exists or a s.216 election is filed); the independent film licensor is not a pass-through entity; no check-the-box equivalent exists in Canada.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; Canada determines entity classification by legal incorporation and central management and control. Available statutory elections: (i) ITA s.216 election for non-resident film-rights owner to report Canadian-source rent/royalty income on a net Part I basis instead of Part XIII gross basis; (ii) ETA s.156 closely related corporation GST/HST election to disregard inter-affiliate supplies; (iii) GST/HST filing-frequency elections (annual, quarterly, monthly) under ETA ss.248-250; (iv) Canadian-controlled private corporation elections to waive the small-business deduction are available but typically not relevant to foreign-owned film licensors; (v) hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss.18.2-18.4 apply automatically with no elective relief mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 CAD of annual worldwide taxable supplies excluding zero-rated exports; a person becomes a GST/HST registrant once total taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold; mandatory registration is required when a non-resident film licensor supplies a copyright licence for use in Canada to a Canadian broadcaster or distributor; voluntary registration is available to zero-rated exporters to claim input tax credits on Canadian production/acquisition costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies at 0% GST where a Canadian film licensor supplies a copyrighted motion-picture licence (intangible personal property) to a non-resident licensee and the copyright is not for use or exploitation in Canada, pursuant to Schedule VI Part V section 10 of the Excise Tax Act; where physical film prints, master media, or marketing materials are transported out of Canada, zero-rating applies to qualifying exports of tangible personal property under Schedule VI Part V; the licensor must hold written proof of foreign territory rights, foreign licensee residence, and payment flows; supplies to Canadian resident distributors for Canadian theatrical or home entertainment exploitation remain taxable at 5% GST.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration or Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) electronic filing — mandatory for physical film prints, masters, and promotional media leaving Canada; for purely digital IP licensing, a written no-physical-export declaration and CERS exemption basis are maintained
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — evidencing export transport from Canada to the foreign consignee; for digital delivery, a system-generated digital delivery receipt detailing territory restrictions and recipient jurisdiction substitutes for transport documents
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank statement, SWIFT MT103, letter of credit settlement advice, or correspondent bank credit evidence confirming that the non-resident licensee remitted foreign-currency proceeds into the Canadian licensor's bank account within the contractual payment window
  • Executed international film distribution and licensing agreement — setting out the licensed territories outside Canada, the reservation of Canadian rights, royalty/sublicensing terms, and the requirement that the licensee exploit the film only outside Canada

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system; there is no government API clearance or digital stamping regime for e-invoices; all GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically where annual gross revenue exceeds C$1.5 million (GST/HST NETFILE or approved commercial software); invoices must contain the registrant BN, date, vendor/purchaser details, consideration, and tax amount per ETA s.223 and GST/HST Memorandum chapters; electronic payment of net tax is remitted through CRA My Payment; Peppol/XML e-invoicing is permitted voluntarily as commercial electronic records but is not nationally mandated.

CRA scrutiny targets fictitious or related-party foreign licensees, retroactive amendments to territory clauses, and missing GST/HST records; the normal four-year reassessment window is extended to eight years where a registrant has misrepresented facts and is unlimited where fraud or wilful default is established; zero-rating claims will be clawed back with refund interest computed daily from the date the refund was paid; civil false-statement penalties under ETA s.285 are the greater of C$100 and 25% of the tax sought to be avoided; gross negligence triggers a 50% additional-income-tax penalty under ITA s.163(2), and criminal evasion under ETA s.327 or ITA s.239 may result in fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment; documentary records must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII. Film licensing royalties, profit remittances, dividends, intercompany loan repayments, and equity injections are freely transferable without exchange control approval. No foreign exchange allocation or quota exists. The only structural non-tariff barrier is Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on outbound royalties and dividends (typically 25% statutory, reduced under an applicable treaty such as the Canada-US Treaty to 0% for certain copyright royalties and 5%/15% for dividends). A payer must obtain a valid treaty-based exemption or reduced-rate certificate (e.g., country-specific Form NR301 / NR302 in Canada) before remitting without full withholding. For corporations, any remittance of funds, whether inbound or outbound, is subject to mandatory FINTRAC reporting for transactions greater than CAD 10,000; this is a reporting obligation, not a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no fixed repartriation timeline for export receipts. Film licensing revenue, being an intangible/royalty flow, is not subject to the B13A goods export declaration. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in domestic foreign-currency bank accounts or offshore accounts and convert at commercial rates when advantageous. Statutory obligations are limited to maintaining books and records for CRA income tax purposes, reporting intra-group cross-border royalties under the International Transaction Assessment (ITSS) surveys when requested, and ensuring wire transfers above CAD 10,000 are captured by the bank's FINTRAC reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no standalone foreign exchange control administration; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises deposit-taking institutions, and FINTRAC performs cross-border wire transaction monitoring.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (15% federal general corporate rate + 12% British Columbia provincial general corporate rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder, subject to any applicable DTA reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 due no later than six months after the fiscal year-end. Final balance of tax payable due in full two months after year-end for most corporations (three months for CCPCs under certain conditions); arrears rates apply from the filing-due date.

Failure to report income subject to gross negligence penalty of 50% of misreported amount. Criminal prosecution for deliberate evasion may include fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not set a formal Local File/Master File filing threshold; a taxpayer must prepare contemporaneous TP documentation for all non-arm's-length cross-border transactions (CRA Information Circular 87-2R). Country-by-country reporting is mandatory for Canadian-headquartered groups with consolidated group revenue above CAD 750,000,000. Penalties under s.247(3) are triggered where a transfer-pricing adjustment is made and the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts; no monetary minimum is prescribed.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no Economic Substance Regulations. Nonetheless, GAAR (s.245) and the Supreme Court's substance-over-form principles require a real business purpose and actual activity in BC for treaty benefits. Absent staff, premises, risk assumption and decision-making, licensing income may be reallocated to the beneficial non-resident principal, with Part XIII withholding and treaty-application denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: fine 50-200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of misreported amount plus arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and tax preparers who knowingly participate in evasion are personally liable for fiscal penalties, tax arrears when the corporation is insolvent (for unremitted source deductions), and may face criminal restitutions, travel bans and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: s.247 transfer-pricing adjustment plus penalty of 10% of the net adjustment plus interest; secondary audit may trigger gross-negligence penalties of 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP certificates or direct fraudulent pricing may be assessed personally for penalties and subject to director's liability for withheld amounts; in egregious cases, criminal tax prosecution and asset freezing orders.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterisation, denial of treaty benefits under MLI/principal purpose test, Part XIII reassessment at 25% withholding, and penalties under s.163.2 for third-party misrepresentations.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for unpaid Part XIII tax, may be 'pierced' as a sham vehicle, receive travel bans, and face prosecution for false statutory declarations.
AML/Regulatory Violations

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 per administrative violation; criminal sanctions up to 10 years imprisonment for intentional money laundering; asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and compliance officers who fail to discharge AML duty can be subjected to personal fines, prohibition orders, and prosecution; personal assets exposed if corporate veil used as a shield.
