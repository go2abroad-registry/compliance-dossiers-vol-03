Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administrator for Business Numbers, GST/HST, and customs accounts; portal: CRA 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online. BC PST registration is administered by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through the BC Corporate Online / OneStop portal.

E-government filing portal

Register the BC limited partnership with the BC Registrar of Companies through Corporate Online (or extra-provincially if formed outside BC), then obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online or Form RC1. Add the GST/HST (RT) program account for the partnership as a separate GST/HST person, and, if exporting goods, the Import/Export (RM) program account. Open the BC PST account with the BC Ministry of Finance; the PST account is keyed to the CRA BN. Non-resident partnerships without a Canadian address may submit Form RC1 with a non-resident mailing address and a Canadian representative or mailing address.

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists; the LP is statutorily fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (each partner is taxed on allocable income). VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or cumulatively over the last four calendar quarters (small supplier rule); voluntary registration is available. BC PST: registration is required before carrying on taxable PST activity in BC; no small-supplier monetary exemption applies for a retail operation with a BC establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part I: a supply of tangible personal property (e.g. desktop 3D printers and resin filaments) is zero-rated where the registrant exports the goods as part of a continuous journey outside Canada, goods are delivered to a common carrier for export, or possession of the goods is transferred to a non-resident person for export, supported by adequate documentary evidence. Related services performed wholly outside Canada, or exported goods-related services supplied to a non-resident, can also be zero-rated. The supplier must keep evidence satisfactory to CRA proving foreign destination or delivery outside Canada; if the goods are diverted to Canadian use, tax applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA electronic export report / Form B13A)
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank remittance/payment evidence)

Canada does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. Registrants must issue and keep GST/HST invoices for taxable supplies (B2B) in an electronically readable form with prescribed particulars; returns are filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE/online, but no API-linked transaction-level clearance is required. Exports require electronic reporting to CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System / eManifest, not a fiscal invoice stamp.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, or without limitation where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud is established. If export zero-rating is not substantiated, the supplies are reclassified as taxable and the LP must remit 5% GST (and PST if applicable) on the full value, with interest; related input tax credits may also be clawed back or apportioned. Civil penalties include false-statement penalties and failure-to-file penalties under the Excise Tax Act; criminal prosecution for deliberate evasion can result in fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment. Retrospective clawback may also reach prior refunds and input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime with no exchange control regulations. Current and capital account transactions are generally fully convertible, with no statutory restrictions on converting Canadian dollars (CAD) into foreign currency or remitting profits abroad. There is no requirement for Central Bank approval for cross-border capital movements. Profit remittances from a British Columbia limited partnership to non-resident limited partners are subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory rate 25%, subject to tax treaty reductions), but there is no FX convertibility hurdle. The Bank of Canada does not administer routine cross-border transfer approval; reporting is limited to aggregate statistical disclosure by financial institutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline within which export receipts must be repatriated or converted into CAD. Exporters may retain foreign-currency balances in domestic or foreign bank accounts. Reporting obligations are limited to customs export declarations under the Customs Act, financial information reporting to Statistics Canada for balance-of-payments purposes, and, where applicable, FATF-driven anti-money laundering verification by the reporting financial institution.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as an ancillary treasury route, but Low as a sole or primary banking facility. Offshore digital EMI accounts (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are legally usable for cross-border collections and disbursements, provided the Canadian entity of the EMI registers as a money services business under FINTRAC if it conducts payment services in Canada, and the EMI's operations comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act as applicable. However, these are not chartered banks, are not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC), and may carry higher compliance friction with Canadian Tier-1 clearing partners. For a low-A/ML-risk e-commerce limited partnership, such EMIs are viable for fast onboarding and multi-currency collection, but the primary corporate liquidity account should remain at a regulated Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting partner's bank to the BC limited partnership's designated Tier-1 Canadian operational account. The wire instruction should clearly reference the LP legal name and account number, and include a payment narrative such as 'Capital Contribution to CA LP' or 'Limited Partnership Capital Account Injection' to distinguish equity subscription from trade receivables. All inward transfers are subject to the receiving bank's source-of-funds verification; the partnership should maintain a signed contribution schedule, LP agreement, and bank application record.
