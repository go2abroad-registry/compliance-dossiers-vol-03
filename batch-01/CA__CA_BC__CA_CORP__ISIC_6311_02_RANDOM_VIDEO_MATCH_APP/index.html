Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum authorized or paid-up capital is prescribed under the BCBCA for incorporation; the incorporator may subscribe for one or more shares at any nominal issue price (e.g., CAD 0.01 per share). All issued shares must be fully paid under BCBCA s. 30 before a share certificate is delivered. If shares are issued for non-cash consideration, a written contract satisfying BCBCA s. 33 must be adopted and retained. No escrow account is required at the formation stage; escrow of promoter shares arises only if the corporation later becomes a reporting issuer under Canadian securities law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and occupancy permit under the BC Fire Code and BC Building Code issued by the local fire authority; structural load inspection for the leased office; building emergency and evacuation plan under the Emergency Program Act (RSBC 1996, c. 111); WorkSafeBC workplace registration and compliance with occupational health and safety standards under the Workers Compensation Act (RSBC 1996, c. 492); electrical and fire alarm system certification by Technical Safety BC where installed

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal income tax, GST/HST, and Business Number (BN) administration. Portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); Business Registration Online (BRO) (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register-business-online.html). Provincial: BC Ministry of Finance for Provincial Sales Tax via eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate as a BC Business Corporation (BC Registry/Companies Online) or federal CBCA corporation extra-provincially registered in BC; obtain corporate registry ID. Step 2 — Apply to CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with corporate income tax (RC) program account via BRO, normally within 90 days of commencing Canadian business; the BN is the statutory TIN (no separate VAT ID exists). Step 3 — Register for GST/HST (RT account) online or by Form RC1; non-residents use the simplified non-resident registration process. Step 4 — Register for BC PST via eTaxBC if taxable goods/services are supplied in BC (including digital products effective July 1, 2025). Step 5 — Maintain a Canadian records address and, where lacking Canadian physical presence, appoint an authorised CRA representative under 'Represent a Client'.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation is resident under paragraph 250(4)(a) of the Income Tax Act and taxed on worldwide income; no pass-through is available. CCPC status (federal 9% + BC 2% small business deduction on first CAD 500,000 active business income) requires Canadian-resident control; with non-resident ownership permitted, the general rates (federal 15% + BC 12% ≈ 27%) apply. Territorial_Scope: worldwide taxation of the Canadian-resident corporation with foreign tax credits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation is resident under paragraph 250(4)(a) of the Income Tax Act and taxed on worldwide income; no pass-through is available. CCPC status (federal 9% + BC 2% small business deduction on first CAD 500,000 active business income) requires Canadian-resident control; with non-resident ownership permitted, the general rates (federal 15% + BC 12% ≈ 27%) apply. Territorial_Scope: worldwide taxation of the Canadian-resident corporation with foreign tax credits. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none in Canada/BC.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. Available elections: (i) GST/HST subsection 156(2) election among closely related corporations for nil-consideration cross-supplies; (ii) GST/HST Quick Method election for small suppliers; (iii) Income Tax Act section 216 election for non-resident recipients to report net Canadian rental income; (iv) section 217 election for non-resident pension income; (v) subsection 256(9) joint election to remove associated-corporation designation where control is by a related person. Hybrid mismatch: mandatory deduction-denial rules (ITA ss. 18.2–18.4) apply non-electively; foreign 'check-the-box' classifications are not recognised — CRA uses functional equivalence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (annual worldwide taxable supplies; small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148). For non-resident suppliers of specified digital services, the threshold is measured only against taxable supplies to Canadian recipients; simplified non-resident registration requires no Canadian physical presence. BC PST: registration is required upon the first taxable sale; from July 1, 2025, PST expands to digital products, including apps and online services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST applies to video-matching and social telephony services supplied to a non-resident recipient not registered for GST/HST, provided the service is not performed in respect of real property in Canada or tangible personal property in Canada (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 9). The supplier must retain evidence of non-resident status: executed agreement, billing address, IP/location data, and payment routing through Apple IAP/Paymentwall. Input tax credits on related Canadian inputs remain recoverable; app-store intermediation does not defeat zero-rating where the underlying supply is made to the non-resident end-user.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Export Reporting (only for tangible goods; not required for pure digital service exports)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent delivery log (Apple IAP / Paymentwall transaction records evidencing supply and delivery)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank or PSP settlement statement evidencing non-resident payment inflow
  • Non-resident self-declaration and executed SaaS/end-user agreement confirming recipient status

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping regime in Canada/BC. The CRA accepts electronic invoices (PDF, XML, EDI) without format pre-approval. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing Framework voluntarily adopts Peppol BIS; Quebec's B2B e-invoicing mandate (2026) does not apply to BC. Supporting records, including PSP settlement data from Apple IAP and Paymentwall, must be retained for six years under ETA s. 286.

CRA may reassess within four years of the original assessment (unlimited for misrepresentation or fraud). Unsubstantiated zero-rating is reassessed at 5% GST on consideration plus compound daily interest (ETA s. 280); penalties include 25% for false statements (ETA s. 285) and criminal liability for evasion under ETA s. 327 (fines up to 250% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years). Previously refunded GST/HST may be clawed back. Records must be kept six years (ETA s. 286). Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline for service exports; the figure is a commercial benchmark — materially delayed or absent settlement evidence may cause CRA to challenge non-resident status. BC PST may be separately assessed with interest and penalties for unregistered digital-product sales in BC.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both capital and current accounts under the Currency Act and Canada's OECD obligations. There are no exchange-control approvals for foreign direct investment, inbound capital injection, or dividend/profit remittance, subject to customary sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and Canadian withholding tax on distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, holding-period, or mandatory conversion obligation on export receipts; proceeds may be freely retained, converted, or disbursed in any currency. Standard tax and cross-border reporting obligations still apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange-control agency; monetary authority is the Bank of Canada, while financial institutions are prudentially regulated by OSFI and AML/CFT supervised by FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMI/e-money institutions are not permitted to operate as depository institutions in Canada without authorization under the Bank Act. Such entities may engage a Canadian bank sponsor or use a registered money-services-business framework, but they must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act, FINTRAC registration as a money services business, and provincial consumer-protection rules. A purely offshore EMI cannot legally issue bank-level guarantees or hold guaranteed client funds in Canada.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal + 12% British Columbia provincial = 27% combined general corporate income tax rate (as of 2024/2025; subject to provincial budget changes)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII) on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced by treaty (e.g., Canada–US treaty reduces to 5% on substantial holdings or 15% otherwise; Canada–China treaty 10% on 10%+ holding; UK/Ireland treaties 15%/5%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. For a fiscal year ending December 31, deadline is June 30. Payments of federal and provincial tax instalments are due quarterly for corporations exceeding thresholds; balance due 2 months after year-end for CCPCs, 3 months for non-CCPCs (certain loss offsets may extend).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation (Master File and Local File) must be prepared if the aggregate gross value of specified transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 for a fiscal year (Schedule 88 threshold). No statutory service fee threshold for documentation; only threshold is CAD 1 million. A Master File is required if the multinational group's Canadian revenue or assets exceed CAD 50M, or if total group revenue exceeds CAD 750M and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1M. Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750M / CAD 1.1B.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a general ESR; however, treaty benefit provisions (PPT, LOB), GAAR (General Anti-Abuse Rule under s.245 of the Income Tax Act), and CRA's anti-avoidance doctrine scrutinize entities lacking substance. A foreign parent company claiming treaty relief must demonstrate real substantive business presence, decision-making, and risk management in its residence state. Dividend stripping rules (s.55) deny capital dividend treatment where a transaction is essentially to avoid tax. Canada's Place of Effective Management (POEM) doctrine and the Authorized OECD Approach (AOA) may recharacterize profits if a Canadian corporation is managed from abroad.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239 of the Income Tax Act: fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded or up to CAD 100,000, imprisonment up to 5 years (federal). Civil penalties: 50% gross negligence penalty, 100% penalty for false statements or omission (s.163), plus accumulated interest. BC Provincial: similar penalties under BC Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable under s.227.1 for unpaid corporate withholdings (e.g., employee source deductions, GST/HST). Personal liability can also attach for prosecutions if the director was willfully blind or actively participated. Prison sentences of up to 5 years; travel bans can be imposed under criminal justice procedures; civil restitution to CRA may include seizure of personal assets and bank accounts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under GAAR ss.245(2) and treaty PPT/LOB clauses. CRA may recharacterize dividends as deemed taxable dividends or royalties under s.212, apply 25% Part XIII withholding, charge interest and penalties of 50% for reckless omission, and impose double taxation risk.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or cause the corporation to misrepresent its structure may be subject to civil assessments under joint and several liability (s.221.1), and under the Criminal Code for fraudulent manipulation of stock exchange or tax evasion (s.380). Personal assets may be frozen; travel bans where criminal prosecution is initiated.
AML / Money Laundering (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for non-compliant MSBs (per violation); failure to report suspicious transactions up to CAD 1,000,000. Criminal penalties for knowingly laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code s.462.31: imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally prosecuted if they knew or ought to have known of the laundering activity; civil penalties and orders to disgorge profits; travel bans may be imposed by court as a bail condition. Personal criminal liability includes imprisonment up to 5 years (money laundering) or 10 years (organized crime).
Withholding Tax / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Payer is personally liable for unwithheld tax under s.215(4); penalty of 10% if not withheld, and director liability under s.227.1. GAAR recharacterization results in deemed dividends with Part XIII tax, plus interest and penalties. Gross negligence penalties (50%) if intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed authorization for payment without proper withholding become jointly and severally liable for the unremitted tax and can be pursued for personal assets; criminal negligence can lead to imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Penalty

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under s.247(3): 10% of the gross understated income (or overstated loss) for each adjustment, in addition to any other Canadian tax assessed. No cap under Canadian law. For repeated or egregious cases, CRA may apply negligence penalties (50%).

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length transactions without independent functional analysis may face civil liability for resulting corporate tax shortfalls if they acted in bad faith; sexual? No; but personal liability for unreported withholdings remains. In severe cases, criminal prosecution for making false statements (s.380) can lead to imprisonment up to 5 years.
