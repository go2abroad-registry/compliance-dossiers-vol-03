Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — national competent authority for GST/HST, federal income tax, payroll, and entity tax accounts, with the primary digital portal being My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) is the competent authority for customs export declarations, and the BC Registrar of Companies (BC Registry Online) administers the underlying statutory LP registration under the BC Limited Partnership Act, RSBC 1996, c. 341.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the BC limited partnership with the BC Registrar of Companies via BC Registry Online, filing the Limited Partnership Statement of Information and identifying each general partner and limited partner, including non-resident beneficial owners. Step 2: Establish the Canadian permanent establishment footprint and request the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Form RC1 or the Business Registration Online (BRO) service, activating the GST/HST account (RT code), payroll account (RP), and import/export program accounts (RM/RS) as applicable; an income tax account is not required for the partnership itself because the LP is fiscally transparent. Step 3: Register as a BC PST vendor through eTaxBC if the LP makes taxable retail sales in BC or has a place of business in BC; there is no small-supplier PST exemption. Step 4: If any equity interest is held by non-residents, the LP must file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually, appoint a CRA representative (Form RC59), and ensure that the non-resident partners' Canadian-source allocation is documented under the partnership agreement. The CRA issues the BN and GST/HST registration immediately upon electronic filing; the effective GST/HST registration date is the earlier of the date the LP first makes a taxable supply in Canada and the date of registration, subject to the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a British Columbia limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and provincial corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act; the partnership itself is not a taxpayer and each partner, including a non-resident limited partner, is taxed on its allocated share of Canadian-source income, gains, losses, and credits. For BC PST, the LP is the vendor and collection agent for the 7% provincial retail sales tax. The LP may qualify as a Canadian partnership under ITA s. 102 only if every partner is resident in Canada; if one or more limited partners are non-resident, the LP is not a Canadian partnership but retains its pass-through status, subject to enhanced T5013 reporting and transfer-pricing documentation. The appropriate classification for this dossier is therefore Pass_Through_Eligible with a Canadian permanent establishment in British Columbia.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. The relevant statutory elections and mechanisms for this cross-border LP are: (1) ITA s. 216.1 election — the non-resident limited partners, through the partnership, may elect to file a full Part I net-basis Canadian income tax return rather than being subject to gross-basis Part XIII non-resident withholding on partnership distributions; (2) ITA s. 17.1 functional-currency election, subject to CRA approval, allowing the LP to compute Canadian tax results in US dollars where the plant keeps its books in USD; (3) ETA s. 156 election to allow the LP and an associated GST registrant to file one combined GST/HST return, which may be used if the oat milk plant operates under an integrated group; (4) US federal Form 8832 election by the US partner to treat the Canadian LP as a partnership or as an association taxable as a corporation for US federal tax purposes, which must be carefully reconciled against Canada's OECD hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 12.3 and 18.4 to prevent inappropriate double-non-taxation or duplicate deduction outcomes; and (5) a BC PST manufacturing and production machinery exemption application, filed through eTaxBC, to exempt the aseptic UHT plant equipment and qualifying packaging-line machinery from the 7% PST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in cumulative taxable supplies made in Canada during the current calendar quarter or the preceding four calendar quarters, with mandatory registration effective before the earlier of the date taxable supplies are first made after losing small-supplier status and 30 days after the threshold is exceeded. BC PST: no small-supplier registration threshold exists; the LP must register as a BC PST vendor through eTaxBC if it maintains a place of business in BC or makes taxable retail sales in BC. Export sales delivered outside BC are exempt from PST, but the vendor registration obligation remains for any BC retail activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST: Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part I, a supply of goods by a registered supplier is zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods in the course of its commercial activity; the packaged UHT oat milk cartons sold to non-resident distributors qualify for 0% GST where the sale contract, Incoterms 2020 delivery term, freight engagement, and CBSA export documentation collectively establish that the goods left Canada as part of and in connection with the supply. Services, including tolling and co-packaging services performed at the plant for a non-resident principal, are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V when the benefit of the service is consumed outside Canada. Additionally, domestic supplies of basic groceries, a category that includes most unflavoured plant-based beverage formats such as oat milk sold as a food for human consumption, may already be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part III, so the LP can claim full 5% input tax credits on raw materials, packaging inputs, and production overhead while remitting no GST on qualifying grocery-format cartons. BC PST: export sales are exempt from PST where the vendor delivers the goods to the buyer outside BC/Canada, and the UHT aseptic plant's production and packaging machinery is eligible for the BC PST manufacturing and production machinery exemption.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government invoice stamping, or continuous transaction control regime exists in Canada as of the current tax compliance pipeline. The CRA accepts electronic invoices and retained records where they are accurate, legible, and reproducible for the statutory six-year retention period; GST/HST returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account, or certified EDI, but invoices themselves are not transmitted to the CRA for clearance. The CBSA, however, operates the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), which requires electronic pre-export declaration data through a certified service provider API for all commercial export shipments exceeding CAD 2,000, generating an acknowledgement message that serves as the fiscal export clearance record. The Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-Invoicing framework is voluntary for B2B and B2G transactions; the aseptic UHT oat milk plant should nevertheless design its ERP to archive CERS acknowledgements, batch-level production records, and electronic invoices in a machine-readable format to support future audits.

The CRA has a normal four-year reassessment window for GST/HST, extended indefinitely where a person has made a misrepresentation or committed fraud. If export zero-rating is disallowed, the LP will be assessed 5% GST on the export proceeds, lose the corresponding input tax credits, and owe compound daily interest from the original filing due date. Statutory penalties include the ETA s. 285 false-statement penalty equal to 25% of the understated net tax and the ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of understated income where income tax is also understated; criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239 or ETA s. 327 for deliberate evasion may lead to fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to two years per count. The CBSA can independently impose administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 25,000 per violation for failure to file accurate export declarations or for late, incomplete, or non-compliant CERS transmissions. The LP must retain export books and records for six years under ETA s. 286, and the CRA may issue a requirement under ITA s. 231(1) to produce records held outside Canada. Particular audit scrutiny applies to related-party export sales, offshore tolling arrangements, and export invoices not matched by inbound SWIFT settlement; the general partner may be personally assessed if it directed, assented to, or acquiesced in improper zero-rating. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter in this dossier is adopted as the group's treasury and transfer-pricing documentation benchmark for confirming that export receivables are actually settled and traceable.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general corporate income tax rate 15% plus British Columbia general corporate income tax rate 12%, producing a combined headline rate of 27% for a taxable corporate partner carrying on business in BC; the BC limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and has no entity-level corporate income tax liability.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident recipient. For a BC limited partnership, a distribution of profit is not a dividend; instead, the non-resident limited partner is taxed under Part I on its allocated share of Canadian-source partnership income. If a Canadian corporate partner distributes such after-tax income as an actual dividend to its foreign shareholder, the 25% statutory dividend withholding rate applies subject to treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Five months after the partnership's fiscal period-end if the partnership has any Canadian-resident partner or a Canadian permanent establishment; eighteen months after fiscal period-end if the partnership has no Canadian-resident partners and no permanent establishment in Canada. The BC filing obligation follows the same deadline because CRA administers the BC Income Tax Act for partnerships.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the corporation's taxation year-end; quarterly corporate income tax instalments are due, and a corporate limited partner must remit instalments based on its estimated taxable income allocated from the partnership.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate fair market value of controlled transactions with non-residents in a taxation year, which triggers the mandatory Form T106 information return and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. A Local File should also be maintained for the BC UHT plant when the aggregate value of controlled transactions with a non-resident related party exceeds this threshold. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report may be required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000, and CRA may request a Master File for any cross-border related-party transaction even below that threshold when audit risk exists.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the Income Tax Act's general anti-avoidance rule under s.245 and the sham doctrine require commercial substance and a genuine business purpose. The BC limited partnership must have real fixed-place-of-business attributes—an operating UHT plant, physical inventory, employees, plant assets, management decisions, and commercial risk—so that CRA cannot treat it as a conduit, agency, or artificial entity. Treaty benefits can also be denied under the OECD Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test if obtaining tax treaty advantages was one of the principal purposes of the limited partnership structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Gross Transfer-Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess income under s.247 and impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the net adjustment. Gross negligence penalties under s.163(2) add 50% of the tax avoided. Criminal conviction under s.239 of the Income Tax Act is punishable by a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years, plus forfeiture and interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner who knowingly participate in the misstatement may be jointly and severally assessed for penalties; courts may pierce the corporate veil if the general partner is a mere facade; travel bans, asset freezes, extradition, and imprisonment can be imposed in a criminal tax proceeding, and civil restitution remains a non-dischargeable debt in bankruptcy.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: The payer is personally liable for the full amount of unwithheld tax under s.215, plus interest and penalty. Negligent non-compliance can lead to a s.227.1 director's liability assessment and a 10% late-withholding penalty plus 20% per year interest; criminal prosecution may result in a fine up to CAD 25,000 per offence and imprisonment for up to 12 months under s.238, or up to five years for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable for withheld taxes not remitted to the CRA, including interest and penalties; the CRA can issue a director's liability assessment without first exhausting the partnership's assets; civil suits and seizure orders may extend to personal bank accounts, residences, and travel restrictions.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CRA can recharacterize the arrangement under s.245(2), deny treaty benefits under the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test, and assess tax, interest, and the 25% GAAR penalty equal to the benefit obtained. The BC limited partner may also lose the limited-liability shield if it participates in control of the business, exposing it to general partner liability for the LP's debts and obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors and signatories of the corporate general partner may be held personally liable for the partnership's tax debts and civil liabilities if the court finds the entity is an agent or sham; an order can be made to lift the corporate veil, and travel bans can be imposed where evidence of flight risk exists.
AML / Financial Crime Red Flags in Trade Settlement

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC may issue administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, which can reach CAD 1,500,000 for institutional violations and CAD 500,000 for individuals; criminal money-laundering convictions under the Criminal Code can result in imprisonment up to 10 years, forfeiture of the UHT plant and bank accounts, and debarment from federal procurement.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly fail to implement AML/ATF controls, or who direct employees to bypass CDD/beneficial-owner checks, can be charged personally; the court may impose travel bans, seizure of passports, and restraints on disposing of personal and corporate assets, and the Crown may seek civil restitution and punitive damages.
