Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); GST/HST and corporate income tax via My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); customs processing through Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or participating in a British Columbia limited partnership must first obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Registration is completed online through Business Registration Online (BRO), by remitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or via a registered tax professional using CRA Represent a Client. The non-resident partnership or its general partner supplies entity identity documents, partnership agreement, and proof of Canadian place of business. The CRA assigns a 9-digit BN and may activate separate program accounts such as GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM). A non-resident limited liability partner that is not carrying on business in Canada through the partnership is generally not required to obtain a Canadian BN for income tax, but the partnership itself must register for GST/HST if it makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a full check-the-box regime comparable to the U.S. Treasury Regulations. For Canadian income tax purposes, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent and allocates income, gains, losses, and tax credits to partners under the mandatory partnership rules of the Income Tax Act (Canada) (s. 96). A partnership or partners may elect different fiscal year-ends under s. 249.1(4) and may file Form T5013 Summary with the CRA. For GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate person from its members and may elect under s. 273 of the Excise Tax Act for nil consideration; foreign partners may make a s. 216.1 election in respect of Canadian-source rental income. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act (ss. 94, 94.1-94.4 and related provisions) address non-arm's length offshore partnership interests and can override partnership treatment in specified cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in gross revenue from taxable supplies worldwide made by the person or partnership over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); non-resident partnerships without a Canadian establishment that make taxable supplies in Canada are generally registrants by mandate rather than by threshold if they solicit sales in Canada and do not qualify as small suppliers, and may be required to register even when only recovering GST paid on imports

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property (e.g., grade A refined copper cathodes) is zero-rated under s. 158(1) of the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports the goods, maintains documentary proof that the goods were shipped from Canada to a destination outside Canada, and supports the export in a commercially reasonable manner. The seller must have possession and transfer of goods to a carrier in Canada and must receive in hand, before filing, evidence of export such as the Canadian export declaration, bill of lading, customs control entry at destination, freight or courier documentation, or a combination of commercial invoices and bank payment records. The zero-rated treatment also applies to a supply of services performed on exported goods and certain ancillary services if the owner is a non-resident at the time the services are performed, provided the supply is not made to a registered resident and the services relate to property held outside Canada at the time of performance.

  • CERS/ATS Export Declaration (formerly B13A form) filed with the CBSA
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical shipment from Canada to a non-resident purchaser
  • Commercial Invoice identifying the goods, HS classification, quantity, and export destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/letter of credit documentation proving the consideration was received from the non-resident buyer into a Canadian or authorized foreign bank account
  • Cargo control document and, where applicable, customs seal records, packing lists, and insurance certificates

Canada does not currently impose any mandatory national real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping regime (no CTC/CIUS equivalent). Registered GST/HST filers must transmit returns and net-tax schedules electronically through CRA NETFILE or approved third-party software, with annual filing applicable in limited circumstances. CRA has published an API-enabled business connectivity framework allowing taxpayers to submit GST/HST returns and view accounts programmatically through My Business Account. The CBSA mandates electronic export declarations for goods exported from Canada pursuant to the Customs Act, and, for customs bonded or duty-deferral transactions, digital records must be retained for six years, but there is no real-time fiscal stamping or tax clearance requirement prior to invoice.

The CRA may reassess a registrant to reverse a zero-rated claim if the exporter fails to maintain adequate documentation of export or the goods are subsequently repurposed. On reassessment, unremitted GST becomes due with statutory interest calculated daily at prescribed rates, plus a 5% participation penalty plus 1% per month up to 12 months on any net-tax deficiency. If a false statement or omission is made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence in connection with export zero-rating, the penalty for each false statement is 25% of the amount the registrant sought to avoid remitting under ss. 280 and 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act. Knowingly making a false or deceptive export declaration may also trigger criminal liability under the Customs Act or s. 380 of the Criminal Code, with fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment. Where the clawback is based on misrepresentation, CRA may collect the GST/HST plus interest and penalties from the partnership and, for income tax purposes, the excluded recovery is denied as a deductible expense.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Canada maintains a pure floating exchange-rate regime under the Currency Act and broadly adheres to IMF Article VIII obligations; there is no exchange-control approval, licensing, or compulsory hedging requirement for CAD conversion, trade flows, or capital movements. Non-resident limited partners of a British Columbia Limited Partnership may remit profit shares freely; the only substantive remittance hurdle is fiscal, not exchange-control-related, namely Part XIII withholding on distributions allocable to non-resident partners, subject to treaty relief and FATCA/CRS transparency obligations. No surrender or conversion mandate applies to foreign-currency receipts held or repatriated by exporters.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Formal export-proceeds surrender requirements were abolished in the early 1950s and have never been reinstated; a BC export trading limited partnership may hold, retain, net, or repatriate export proceeds in any currency at its commercial discretion. The bank-level obligation is documentary and regulatory in nature, not convertibility-based: cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC, and balance-of-payments statistics must be coded so that proceeds from copper-cathode physical arbitrage are classified as goods-export receipts rather than financial flows.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for the prudential regulation of Schedule I and II banks and with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision of banks and money service businesses

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury, documentary credit, and LME settlement operations; Moderate only as a secondary or auxiliary payment rail. Canada does not maintain a general-purpose EMI deposit-taking licence; digital money-service businesses must register as MSBs with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and remain non-deposit-taking. Such platforms may execute limited-volume FX conversions and cross-border wires, but they cannot issue documentary letters of credit or standby undertakings, cannot hold or transfer LME-linked warrants, and their use by a mid-risk metals-trading LP should be restricted to minor disbursements, with all principal treasury activity concentrated in Schedule I chartered banks.

The beneficiary narrative must state: 'Capital contribution to CA_LP — ISIC 4662 copper-cathode wholesale trading', and the transfer must match the amount, currency, and value date recorded in the LP capital schedule. No exchange-control approval is required for CAD inflows; the bank must nonetheless complete FINTRAC large electronic-fund-transfer reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, obtain pre-advice for large sweeps, and retain the incoming-wire advice together with the LP's subscription records as part of the audit trail.Trade settlements use: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier payments, including refinery gap-fill purchases, with an explicit goods-export purpose narrative; (ii) SWIFT MT700 documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 for contracted copper-cathode purchases, supported by MT720 amendments and ISBP 745-compliant presentation of bills of lading, certificates of origin, and inspection certificates; (iii) SWIFT MT760/MT799 bank undertakings where secured pre-payment in the physical chain is required; and (iv) LME electronic warrant transfer and delivery-versus-payment settlement through an LME member broker for title transfer of physical copper held in accredited warehouses. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate rate (Federal 15% + British Columbia 12%). The Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and is not a corporate taxpayer; income is allocated and taxed at the partner level. Canadian-resident corporate partners pay tax at the combined 27% rate on the LP's Canadian-source business income allocated to them.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents, subject to DTA reduction. A BC limited partnership is not a corporation and generally does not pay dividends. Cash distributions to a non-resident limited partner are treated as return of capital, partnership profit drawings, or partnership income allocations, and are not automatically subject to Part XIII withholding. However, if the LP is commercially operated as a corporate surrogate, pays management fees, or distributes rental, royalty, interest, or other 'amounts paid or credited' of a prescribed Part XIII nature, CRA can recharacterize the payment and impose 25% withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than the last day of the third calendar month after the end of the partnership fiscal period. A BC LP formed after 1999 must generally have a December 31 fiscal year-end, making the standard deadline March 31.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return is due six months after the corporation's taxation year-end. The balance-due day is two months after taxation year-end for a non-resident-owned corporation not claiming the Canadian-controlled private corporation small business deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no statutory Canadian import of the OECD Local File / Master File label. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is mandatory for all cross-border non-arm's-length transactions. The statutory penalty threshold is: aggregate value of non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceeding CAD 5,000,000 in the taxation year and a mispricing adjustment exceeding the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue. Penalty is 10% of the mispricing adjustment. Country-by-country reporting is mandatory for a Canadian ultimate parent of an MNE group with consolidated revenue equal to or greater than CAD 750,000,000, using CRA Form RC4649.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations. The functional equivalent is the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Income Tax Act section 245, the MLA/PPE treaty abuse rules, the sham doctrine, and CRA's substance-over-form audit practice. A BC LP engaged in copper cathode trading must maintain substantive Canadian business activity: a real general partner, a physical or virtual office with management decision-making, personnel responsible for commodity risk, bank accounts and LME/clearing arrangements in the name of the partnership, and commercial documents evidencing negotiation, risk assumption, and contract performance. A shell LP with no employees, no books, and no business purpose interposed solely to access a DTA or to defer tax will be disregarded, recharacterized, or denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239: on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded; on summary conviction, imprisonment up to 2 years and similar fines. Gross negligence civil penalty of 50% of misstatement, plus non-deductible interest and full reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners, and senior officers who direct, authorize, or knowingly participate in fraudulent filing can be prosecuted personally. Directors of a corporate general partner are liable for unremitted source deductions and corporate tax amounts under section 227.1 ITA. Criminal proceedings can include bail conditions, travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition risk.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may re-price the transaction to arm's length under subsection 247(2), assess a 10% transfer pricing penalty under subsection 247(3) where the CAD 5,000,000 aggregate transaction and mispricing threshold are met, and assess gross negligence penalties. Related-party adjustments can also trigger non-resident withholding tax at 25% on deemed dividends, interest, or royalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false documentation can face tax evasion charges. If the partnership or corporate general partner is insolvent, CRA may assess directors personally for the full amount of unremitted tax, penalties, and interest. The general partner of the BC LP is fully liable to CRA for the LP's compliance obligations, and limited partners who participate in day-to-day price-setting or trading control can lose their limited liability protection.
Shell Company, Treaty Shopping and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR can deny the tax benefit in full under section 245 ITA; treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test in the Multilateral Instrument; CRA may impose a 50% gross negligence penalty if the avoidance transaction was made knowingly or in circumstances amounting to gross negligence; interest accrues on reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: General partners and corporate directors who structure abusive avoidance may be penalized as tax-avoidance promoters under section 237.1 ITA; associated penalties can reach CAD 100,000 plus personal liability for the taxpayer's reassessment. Travel bans and criminal fraud charges may follow if unlawful shifting of assets is proven.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, reporting entities may face FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for entities. The Criminal Code provides fraud and money-laundering offences with imprisonment up to 14 years. Violations of Canadian sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act can lead to prosecution and penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per offence and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the general partner can be personally named in FINTRAC compliance findings and prosecutions. Corporate veil protection does not shield a partner who physically directs wire transfers, signs false shipping documents, or instructs banks to conceal the overseas beneficiary. Sanctions breaches can also trigger personal asset freezes and travel restrictions.
Non-Resident Withholding and Remittance Failures

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act subsection 227.1, unremitted amounts held in trust are recoverable directly from directors and partners. CRA can assess 10% late-remittance penalties, daily interest, and gross negligence penalties. Directors are jointly and severally liable even if the corporation or partnership is dissolved or bankrupt.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the BC LP is an unlimited-liability person. The directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable for every amount withheld or required to be withheld from non-resident payments. Conviction for intentional non-remittance can lead to imprisonment under section 239, travel bans, and personal civil restitution orders in favor of the Crown.
