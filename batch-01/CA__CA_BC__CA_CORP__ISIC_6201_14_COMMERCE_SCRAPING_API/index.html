E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian business corporation is a resident taxpayer subject to federal corporate income tax (15%) and BC provincial corporate income tax (12%) on worldwide income; no pass-through eligibility, no territorial election, and no tax-exempt zone. The BC small business deduction reduces the combined rate to approximately 11% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income where eligibility conditions are met.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election; corporate tax classification is fixed by incorporation law. Hybrid mismatch rules have been proposed/implemented through Income Tax Act sections 18.1-18.3 (draft legislation released November 2022), effective for taxation years beginning on or after October 1, 2023. Relevant statutory elections include: subsection 85(1) tax-deferred incorporation rollover; subsection 256 corporate attribution/amalgamation elections; GST/HST voluntary registration under subsection 240(3) of the Excise Tax Act; the quick method of accounting election; and section 156 associated-corporation loss-transfer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies, over any four consecutive calendar quarters; registration is required within 30 days of ceasing to be a small supplier, and voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Schedule VI, Part V, section 9 of the Excise Tax Act zero-rates a service supplied to a non-resident recipient unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada, relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada, is rendered to a non-resident individual while present in Canada, or is a prescribed service under section 9.1 supplied for consumption in Canada. For the ISIC 6201 web-scraping and proxy API gateway, zero-rating applies where the recipient is a non-resident, is not registered under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act, and consumes the API/data output outside Canada; the supplier must document the non-resident status and overseas place of consumption. Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, conditional on physical export in accordance with the Customs Act and prescribed evidence requirements. BC PST does not apply where the digital service is not acquired for use in BC.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, or the digital equivalent (API access logs, server timestamps, IP geolocation records) evidencing delivery/performance outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent foreign-currency receipt evidence (SWIFT MT103, Stripe payout ledger, or USDC on-chain transaction record) demonstrating receipt and repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days of supply

None at the federal level. Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transactional invoice certification for GST/HST purposes; the CRA accepts paper, PDF, and EDI/XML filings. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoice standard based on Peppol BIS 3.0 is progressively being adopted for B2B and federal B2G procurement. CRA digital filing channels (GST/HST NETFILE and My Business Account APIs) relate to return submission, not per-invoice clearance.

The CRA may reassess within four years (unlimited where misrepresentation, fraud, or failure to file exists), deny the zero-rating, assess 5% GST on the disputed supplies, and claw back related input tax credits. False statements made knowingly or through gross negligence attract a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax under Excise Tax Act section 285.1; tax evasion prosecutions under section 341 can result in fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment. Unremitted amounts bear compound interest, and directors may incur personal liability under section 323. Although Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, exceeding the 180-day benchmark, without credible commercial justification, materially increases audit scrutiny of the underlying export documentation and may lead to retrospective assessment of GST plus penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible G10 currency with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. The exchange rate is market-determined and floating; no surrender, repatriation, advance-deposit, or exchange-control approval obligations exist. For the target entity (a BC business corporation operating an ISIC 6201 public commercial web-scraping and proxy-API gateway), profit-remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than monetary: (1) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%–15% for dividends, interest, and royalties) applies to distributions to foreign shareholders; (2) exported data and API services are zero-rated for GST/HST under ETA section 7, eliminating domestic indirect-tax friction; (3) the company must maintain Canadian economic substance to avoid CRA and OSFI-conduct audit challenge, but no central-bank permission is ever required. Non-resident ownership of 100% of the BC corporation is lawful; the Investment Canada Act applies only to reviewable acquisitions of control of existing Canadian businesses, not to de novo incorporation and initial capitalization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation requirement for export receipts or any foreign-currency earnings. A BC exporter of API-metadata, scraping data, or gateway services may retain 100% of USD or any third-currency proceeds indefinitely, either in Canadian accounts or abroad. The only compliance obligations are: (i) FINTRAC large-EFT and large-virtual-currency transaction reporting, which attaches to financial institutions and VASPs rather than to the exporter; (ii) CRS/FATCA due-diligence documentation; (iii) CRA recognition of foreign-exchange gains or losses under ITA section 39(1); and (iv) use of accurate wire purpose codes to allow the bank's own correspondent-bank compliance. No Bank of Canada export-proceeds declaration exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Cross-border banking supervision is administered jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and Payments Canada (operator of the Lynx RTGS and ACSS clearing systems). In British Columbia, the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) licenses money-services businesses and virtual-currency dealers under the Money Services Business Act. Canada maintains no exchange-control law: the Bank of Canada does not approve, license, or obtain declarations for individual cross-border payments, export proceeds, or profit remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium, permissible only in a sponsored-bank model. Offshore fintech EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) cannot hold a direct settlement account at Payments Canada and must operate through a Canadian schedule I bank sponsor. In British Columbia, the Money Services Business Act and FINTRAC MSB rules require registration/licensing where the entity carries on foreign-exchange, remittance, or virtual-currency payment business in the province. For the ISIC 6201 scraping-API enterprise, an EMI is acceptable for low-value operational disbursements and agent micro-payouts, but not for the statutory capital escrow, tax-relevant dividend remittance, or major trade settlements, which should remain on tier-1 Canadian bank rails. The dual-rail strategy adopted here is: tier-1 schedule I bank for CAD/USD fiat treasury, plus a FINTRAC-registered licensed VASP solely for USDC programmatic payouts, with documented treasury controls bridging the rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium, permissible only in a sponsored-bank model. Offshore fintech EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) cannot hold a direct settlement account at Payments Canada and must operate through a Canadian schedule I bank sponsor. In British Columbia, the Money Services Business Act and FINTRAC MSB rules require registration/licensing where the entity carries on foreign-exchange, remittance, or virtual-currency payment business in the province. For the ISIC 6201 scraping-API enterprise, an EMI is acceptable for low-value operational disbursements and agent micro-payouts, but not for the statutory capital escrow, tax-relevant dividend remittance, or major trade settlements, which should remain on tier-1 Canadian bank rails. The dual-rail strategy adopted here is: tier-1 schedule I bank for CAD/USD fiat treasury, plus a FINTRAC-registered licensed VASP solely for USDC programmatic payouts, with documented treasury controls bridging the rails.
