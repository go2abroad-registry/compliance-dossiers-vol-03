Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a British Columbia limited partnership. The Declaration of Limited Partnership must state whether contributions are to be made and, if so, the dollar amount and timing; no escrow account is mandated by the Partnership Act. Practical capital contributions are established by the partners' agreement and credited to capital accounts.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and life-safety clearance under the BC Fire Code (BC Reg 318/2021) issued by the local Assistant Fire Commissioner; post-disaster structural load certification for the crew recruitment office; WorkSafeBC occupational clearance evidenced by a Certificate of Recognition (COR) or letter of good standing for the workplace safety program; additional emergency response plan review required for marine terminal, gangway, and crew-transfer operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal registration and administration portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). Provincial counterpart: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Provincial Sales Tax (PST) Division, administered via eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Limited Partnership with the BC Corporate Registry under the BC Partnership Act (name search, Form LP-1, and registry issuance of a BC incorporation/registration number). 2) Apply to CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), selecting 'Partnership' as the entity type; CRA will issue a mandatory 9-digit BN and programme accounts including 'RP' (T5013 partnership income return) and 'RT' (GST/HST); add 'RZ' payroll account only if the LP employs Canadian crew. 3) Designate a resident Canadian partner or manager as the partnership representative for GST/HST compliance; for an LP with non-resident partners, file RC1 with the legal name, Canadian address, and the names and addresses of all general partners, and where no Canadian resident partner exists, the CRA may require a Canadian-resident agent to be appointed under ETA s.272. 4) The LP must register for GST/HST within 30 days of exceeding the CAD $30,000 small-supplier threshold; for crew recruitment services with immediate international revenue, registration is mandatory from the first supply. 5) Register with BC eTaxBC for PST only if the LP supplies PST-taxable goods or enumerated services within BC; crew recruitment and manning management services are generally not PST taxable, so no BC PST account is normally required. 6) Upon registration, issue tax invoices showing the BN plus RT0001 suffix and file GST/HST returns electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style Section 7701 check-the-box regime and no hybrid-dragnet entity election; a limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for income tax under ITA s.96 (income allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level), while for GST/HST the partnership is a distinct 'person' under the Excise Tax Act. Available elections include: (a) non-calendar fiscal-period election under ITA s.249.1(4) to align the partnership's year-end with foreign partner jurisdictions (subject to CRA approval and the (c) limitations in s.249.1); (b) election of a GST/HST fiscal year matching the partnership's taxation year, enabling annual GST/HST filing under the ETA; (c) voluntary GST/HST registration to claim input tax credits after waiving the small-supplier threshold; (d) designation of a partner/representative to administer GST/HST obligations; and (e) voluntary BC PST registration if taxable goods or enumerated services are introduced, though not required for pure crewing services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - under ETA s.148 (small supplier rules), a person (including a partnership) must register within 30 days after worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in either the current calendar quarter or the preceding four calendar quarters; the ship crew recruitment agency will normally exceed this threshold immediately upon signing its first crewing contracts, making GST/HST registration mandatory at commencement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The crew recruitment and vessel crew management agency may claim a 0% GST/HST rating on crewing, payroll administration, and manning services under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 (supply of a service to a non-resident person) and Schedule VI, Part V, s.6 (services rendered in respect of vessels used primarily in international commercial transportation), provided: (i) the recipient is a non-resident shipowner not registered for GST/HST under the normal registration regime; (ii) the supply is not an excluded service (not in respect of real property situated in Canada, not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, not agency services performed in Canada, and not a service rendered to an individual present in Canada at the time of supply); (iii) the vessel is flagged in a foreign jurisdiction and engaged in international commercial voyages; and (iv) the LP holds contemporaneous documentary evidence of the non-resident status, the export service contract, and the cross-border settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13 or Canada Border Services Agency GCA electronic export reporting) - required only where goods or crew equipment are shipped outside Canada; for pure service exports, no customs declaration is required and the executed export service contract is the operative equivalent.
  • Bill of Lading / Vessel Documentation Set - IMO vessel number, flag-state registry extract, current charter party or ship management agreement evidencing the vessel's engagement in international commercial transportation; where crew personal effects or equipment accompany the vessel, retain the corresponding bill of lading and cargo manifest.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank SWIFT MT103, Canadian bank credit advice, or foreign-exchange conversion record naming the non-resident shipowner as remitter and evidencing settlement of the zero-rated invoices within the repatriation window.
  • Executed Crewing and Manning Agreement - contract with the non-resident shipowner specifying the scope of crew recruitment, crew payroll processing, vessel assignment, zero-rating intent, and payment currency.
  • GST/HST Tax Invoices (BN-RT0001) - CRA-compliant invoices expressly worded 'Zero-Rated - Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act' or 'GST/HST 0%'.
  • Non-Resident Status Evidence - foreign incorporation certificate, foreign tax registration number of the shipowner, sworn declaration of non-residence, and confirmation that the shipowner is not registered under Division V of Part IX of the ETA.

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital-stamping, or continuous-transaction-control (CTC) regime. Since 2023, CRA has adopted the voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0 delivered through the E-Invoicing Portal; paper and PDF invoices remain legally valid, but CRA requires tax invoices to disclose the BN plus RT programme account and, for zero-rated export supplies, an express statement that GST/HST is charged at 0%. BC imposes no separate e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate for professional services; eTaxBC is used solely for returns and remittances.

CRA may reassess zero-rated supplies within the normal four-year reassessment window (unlimited in the case of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud), deny zero-rating where export substantiation is deficient, and assess unremitted GST/HST at 5% on the full invoice value plus daily compounding interest at the CRA prescribed rate (ETA s.280). Gross-negligence penalties apply under ETA s.285(1): the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the related net tax. Fraudulent claims may lead to criminal prosecution under ETA s.239, with significant fines and imprisonment up to five years upon indictment. The clawback extends to the disallowance of related input tax credits recovered against the zero-rated income stream. In parallel, BC PST audits can recharacterise ancillary supplies and assess 7% PST plus penalties where enumerated taxable services are involved, and the LP's foreign partners may face income tax reassessment with penalties under ITA ss.163.2 and 162 for filing misleading export-related filings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations on 25 March 1952 and maintains no exchange controls, no capital-account authorization regime, no forced conversion, and no foreign-exchange licensing requirement for trade or treasury operations. Sectoral review under the Investment Canada Act applies only to acquisition-of-control transactions and is not a foreign-exchange control. A British Columbia limited partnership may hold CAD and permitted foreign-currency sub-accounts (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, HKD) at domestic banks. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is not restricted by exchange regulation: the active business income of the partnership retains its character as business income under the Income Tax Act (ITA), is taxable to non-resident partners under Part I on their allocated share, and is generally not subject to Part XIII withholding. Embedded interest, rent, royalty, or dividend components in any distribution remain Part XIII taxable at the 25% statutory rate, subject to treaty reduction; the LP should therefore maintain contemporaneous income-allocation documentation and file Form T5013.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation or export-receipt verification obligation. The only mandatory obligations are: (i) reporting to the Canada Border Services Agency of any physical cross-border movement of currency or bearer monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more under sections 12 and 14 of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); (ii) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting by financial entities for all EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more to or from Canada; and (iii) continuous compliance with sanctions lists under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, the Criminal Code, and Canada's export-control framework. USD, EUR, and other currency receipts may be retained, converted freely, or held in permitted onshore foreign-currency accounts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising currency issuance and retail-payments oversight under the Retail Payment Activities Act, with foreign-exchange and trade-administration functions shared among the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), FINTRAC, Global Affairs Canada, and the Canada Border Services Agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. A purely offshore digital EMI (EU payment institution, UK electronic-money institution, or Singapore major payment institution) cannot lawfully provide retail payment services to a BC corporate client without registration as a Payment Service Provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and registration as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC. RPAA user-fund safeguarding rules require provider-held balances to sit in a Canadian trust account at an eligible financial institution, which effectively precludes an offshore EMI from custodian float positions. Canadian chartered banks will ordinarily decline treasury structures in which the LP's receipts or disbursements transit an unregistered offshore EMI. A workable alternative is the use of a Canadian-registered PSP/payroll aggregator that receives the LP's SWIFT funding and delivers local-currency credits to individual seafarers, while the principal employer funding remains in the LP's domestic bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal small business rate (taxable income ≤ CAD 500,000), 38% general rate; British Columbia provincial small business rate 11%, general rate 12%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends from Canadian corporations to non-residents; no dividend withholding tax on LP profit distributions

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory threshold; documentation required upon CRA audit for significant related-party transactions

Economic substance scope: No specific Economic Substance Regulations; General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and economic substance tests apply to prevent artificial structures

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal charges under Income Tax Act; fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; civil penalties 50% of evaded tax plus interest

Director personal liability: General partners face personal liability for partnership tax obligations; limited partners protected
Shell Company or Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization; denial of deductions and penalties up to 50% of tax benefit

Director personal liability: General partners personally liable for tax liabilities and compliance failures
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 500,000 or imprisonment under Proceeds of Crime Act; CRA-FINTRAC information sharing

Director personal liability: General partners personally liable for regulatory and tax compliance breaches
