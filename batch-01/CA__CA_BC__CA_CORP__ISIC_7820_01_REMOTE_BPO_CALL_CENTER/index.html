Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - National tax administration portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); provincial BC corporate registry (Corporate Online, BC Registries) handles incorporation filings, while CRA issues the federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign corporation establishing a Canadian Business Corporation must first incorporate federally or in British Columbia, then obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN). Procedure: (1) Complete Federal/BC incorporation and obtain certificate of incorporation; (2) Register via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) and check the GST/HST account (BN-RT) election; (3) Submit certified corporate governing documents, director/shareholder identification, registered office address in Canada, and authorization of a CRA filer; (4) CRA verifies identity and issues BN with program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll); (5) For foreign-incorporated entities carrying on business in Canada, file Form RC1414 (Registration) or apply for a deemed BN under the non-resident GST/HST registration rules. Non-residents may appoint an agent or representative for service of process. No separate TIN exists: the BN is the single federal tax identifier.

Procedure: (1) Complete Federal/BC incorporation and obtain certificate of incorporation; (2) Register via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) and check the GST/HST account (BN-RT) election; (3) Submit certified corporate governing documents, director/shareholder identification, registered office address in Canada, and authorization of a CRA filer; (4) CRA verifies identity and issues BN with program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll); (5) For foreign-incorporated entities carrying on business in Canada, file Form RC1414 (Registration) or apply for a deemed BN under the non-resident GST/HST registration rules. Non-residents may appoint an agent or representative for service of process. No separate TIN exists: the BN is the single federal tax identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime; corporate classification is statutory under the Income Tax Act. Relevant elections include: (i) Section 216 ITA election allowing non-residents to file a net-basis Canadian income tax return for rental income; (ii) Section 219 ITA deemed-disposition election for treaty-exempt non-resident corporations winding up Canadian branch operations; (iii) Hybrid mismatch arrangement elections and informed filings under ITA sections 12.2, 18.2, 18.3, 18.4, and 18.8 addressing deductible/non-deductible mismatches; (iv) associated corporation status allocation elections for the small business deduction (CCPC); (v) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA section 240 for below-threshold suppliers; and (vi) ETA section 273 election for closely related corporations to treat intercompany taxable supplies as non-supplies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies per calendar quarter triggers mandatory GST/HST registration for non-resident suppliers carrying on business in Canada (ETA section 240). Remote BPO/call-center suppliers with non-resident customers must assess whether their activities constitute carrying on business in Canada; if the threshold is exceeded, registration is mandatory, while voluntary registration below CAD 30,000 is available to claim input tax credits. A GST/HST registrant must collect 5% GST on taxable supplies made in Canada unless the supply is zero-rated or exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Offshore BPO, multilingual customer inbound helpdesk, and outbound tele-sales services supplied by a Canadian registrant to a non-resident recipient qualify for zero-rating at 0% GST under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 23, provided all of the following conditions are met: (i) the recipient is a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant at the time the supply is made; (ii) the service is supplied for consumption or use primarily outside Canada, with the 'primary beneficiary' being the non-resident; (iii) the service is not in connection with real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or a service performed on tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of performance; and (iv) adequate records demonstrate the non-resident status and offshore delivery of the service. Call-center and BPO services physically performed in Canadian premises exclusively for foreign clients, where no direct intermediary supplies are made in Canada, will generally satisfy the zero-rating test. The supplier must retain contemporaneous third-party evidence and must not claim input tax credits on expenses related to exempt supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System transaction or carrier-generated export declaration) evidencing outward movement of any associated goods
  • Commercial Invoice or detailed Statement of Account identifying the non-resident client and the exact BPO/helpdesk service description
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or electronic export tracking record proving physical export where tangible deliverables accompany the service
  • Signed Master Services Agreement (MSA) or service contract with the non-resident counterparty demonstrating offshore delivery and primary beneficiary
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign currency payment evidence (SWIFT MT103, Wise B2B transaction record, wire transfer confirmation) showing consideration received from the non-resident
  • GST/HST registration certificate (BN-RT) and zero-rating reconciliation worksheet mapping each supply to Schedule VI Part V section 23

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-authority clearance, or digital stamping mandate (distinct from Latin American or certain European regimes). CRA requirements: GST/HST registrants must issue and retain tax invoices showing the vendor's BN (RT account), invoice date, description of supply, and GST charged; invoices for taxable supplies over CAD 30 and invoices exceeding CAD 150 require complete content and recipient details. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies through CRA NETFILE/My Business Account where annual gross revenue exceeds CAD 1.5 million (ETA section 273.1). E-invoicing (Peppol/Pan-Canadian Digital Interchange Standard) is voluntary only. Books and records must be kept in Canada or made available to CRA on demand.

CRA may reverse zero-rated treatment on audit where the exporter fails to substantiate the non-resident status of the recipient, the offshore primary-beneficiary test, or the absence of a Canadian nexus. CRA assessment and clawback powers: normal reassessment window is four years, extended indefinitely for misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect. ETA penalties include: (i) failure to file - 1% of net tax per month, minimum CAD 100 (section 280); (ii) failure to report - 5% of unreported amount, increased to 10% for large unreported amounts (section 285); (iii) false statements or omissions - 50% of the gross-negligence amount (section 285.1); (iv) general wilful evasion penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (subsection 286(1)); and (v) interest accrued at CRA prescribed quarterly rates on reassessed net tax. Criminal prosecution under section 289 of the ETA or section 380 of the Criminal Code for tax fraud can result in fines without upper limit and imprisonment up to five years. Retrospective clawback also denies input tax credits and strips prior refund claims under ETA sections 261 and 262 where export documentation is deficient, including claims made within the four-year period that relied on non-resident status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada fully liberalized cross-border capital and current account transactions; the Canadian dollar floats freely. There are no surrender, conversion, or central bank approval obligations for capital inflows, profit remittances, loan repayments, or dividend distributions. Profit repatriation is subject only to standard withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% treaty-qualified lower rate) and corporate income tax withholdings. Inward capital injections via share subscriptions or shareholder loans are unrestricted. The only material compliance requirement is reporting direct investment transactions and property acquisitions through Statistics Canada (via report forms such as FS 101/101A) and resident/non-resident reporting to banks under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements exist in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in domestic or overseas bank accounts without conversion timelines or prescribed ratios. However, funds received by a Canadian bank account are reported as cross-border currency transaction reports when exceeding CAD 10,000, and beneficiary accounts must satisfy OSFI/FINTRAC record-keeping and beneficial ownership attestations

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank), with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential banking regulator; foreign exchange oversight is minimal and no separate exchange control administration exists

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary payment clearance, Low for primary operating bank account. Canadian law does not recognize a licensed 'electronic money institution' akin to the EU EMI regime. Digital cross-border payment platforms such as Wise B2B or merchant FX providers must be registered as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and are permitted to transmit funds of clients, but they cannot provide deposit-taking banking services, CDIC insurance, or settlement at Bank of Canada level. Such platforms may be used for remittance or disbursement rail acceleration, but a Tier 1 domestic bank account remains requisite for treasury operations, capital escrow, and trade finance. Legal constraint: funds held with unregulated foreign EMIs may violate OSFI expectations regarding Canadian deposit-taking and expose the corporation to currency control/repatriation friction

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-Canadian parent entity funding the Canadian subsidiary via shareholder loans

Root cause: Non-Canadian parent entity funding the Canadian subsidiary via shareholder loans

Defense evidence: Financial statements of parent, source-of-funds declaration, executed shareholder loan agreement with commercial terms, board resolution authorizing account opening, corporate registry certificates from the parent's jurisdiction, and a detailed corporate structure chart showing the BPO entity's ultimate beneficial owners
Remote BPO/call-center revenue model generating large volumes of low-ticket wire transactions to pay domestic staff and offshore vendors

Root cause: Remote BPO/call-center revenue model generating large volumes of low-ticket wire transactions to pay domestic staff and offshore vendors

Defense evidence: Fully executed BPO service contracts with structured fee schedules, payroll register and remittance evidence, list of expected Canadian counterparties (landlord, telecom, utilities), transaction-volume forecast under CAD 100,000 per automated clearing call, and an anti-money laundering operational manual demonstrating end-to-end transaction monitoring
Directors or beneficial owners with only foreign passports and no Canadian residency

Root cause: Directors or beneficial owners with only foreign passports and no Canadian residency

Defense evidence: Certified UBO passports, ownership attestations, background clearance letters or police certificates from their jurisdiction, two independent references from regulated financial institutions, and a sworn affidavit explaining the legitimate commercial provenance of the call-center business and anticipated funding flows
Business activity in a jurisdiction perceived as offshoring or outsourcing of customer support with cross-border data transmission

Root cause: Business activity in a jurisdiction perceived as offshoring or outsourcing of customer support with cross-border data transmission

Defense evidence: Compliance proof under the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) or BC PIPA, data-processing agreements, BCPRA (if financial-sector clients) attestations, cybersecurity certification such as SOC 2 or ISO/IEC 27001, and a contractual undertaking restricting collection of client data to non-high-risk geographies
Ambiguous economic substance and no physical presence beyond registered office

Root cause: Ambiguous economic substance and no physical presence beyond registered office

Defense evidence: Lease agreement for physical office facilities in British Columbia, proof of issuance of Canadian payroll (source deductions including CRA payroll account), utility bills, employee onboarding records, and a written explanation that the BPO platform's operational headquarters are in BC even where agents work remotely

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the non-resident parent must be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers using RTGS/LVTS for same-day CAD settlement. The beneficiary account should be the Capital Escrow Account held at the Canadian Tier 1 bank. The remittance instruction must specify the ISO 20022 purpose code such as 'INTR' (intra-group financial operations) or 'CORP' (non-convertible currency settlement) as recognized by the receiving bank, and should include the corporate name, provincial incorporation number, and beneficial ownership attestation in the beneficiary field. For share subscriptions, an executed subscription agreement should be uploaded via the bank's treasury portal; for shareholder loans, a properly documented intercompany loan agreement bearing commercial interest rates is required. Amounts exceeding CAD 10,000 are automatically reported to FINTRAC as international EFTs; amounts exceeding CAD 25,000 may trigger definitive AML scrutiny and require provenance evidence.Outbound trade payments (vendor salaries, software licensing, contractor disbursements, and telecommunications fees) should clear via the Operational Account using SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wires, Canadian LVTS for domestic high-value wires, or EFT/ACH via the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for Canadian vendor payments. Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are permissible but typically unnecessary for low-risk BPO services. For service contracts, TCH or Fedwire equivalents are not used in Canada; instead SWIFT interconnectivity is via Canadian banks' correspondent networks. All outbound wires require the beneficiary's BIC, full account number, and purpose code (e.g., 'SERV' for service payments or 'TECH' for software licences). To mitigate FX spread, establish a USD operating sweep account and use the bank's online FX dealing portal; settle invoice amounts on exact due dates to avoid unintended FX exposure.Spot Foreign Exchange Contracts: Settlement within two business days; used for immediate Canadian-dollar conversion of USD receipts.Outright Forward Contracts: Up to 12-month tenors, allowing the BPO entity to fix CAD/USD rates for forecast payroll and vendor costs; available from Tier 1 banks without regulated status.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): Permissible for hedging distressed-currency exposures in ancillary markets if currency convertibility constraints arise; for CAD/USD these are unnecessary and rarely offered onshore.Cross-Currency Swaps: Available for longer-term intercompany loan hedging to convert floating USD interest exposure into fixed CAD obligations; subject to OSFI prudent risk-management review and ISDA documentation.Zero-Cost Collars / Options: Bank-provided option structures may be acquired for BPO revenue hedging, provided derivatives documentation and prudent risk policies are followed; derivatives positions must be reported internally to the Canadian bank's treasury desk and comply with OSFI capital adequacy guidelines.
