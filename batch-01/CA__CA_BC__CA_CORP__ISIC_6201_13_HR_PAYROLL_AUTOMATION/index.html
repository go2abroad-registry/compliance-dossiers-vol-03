Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the BCBCA; the Company may incorporate with zero issued capital and no monetary consideration; shares may be issued at nil par value with nominal consideration, and no paid-up capital escrow account is required. Non-resident ownership is permitted without special capital escrow conditions.

Funding & unlock path: WorkSafeBC employer registration and occupational health and safety orientation under the Workers Compensation Act; fire safety inspection certification under the BC Fire Code; structural occupancy and egress clearance for Group D (business) occupancy under the BC Building Code; electrical safety, first-aid, and emergency preparedness verification for the registered office.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the BCBCA; the Company may incorporate with zero issued capital and no monetary consideration; shares may be issued at nil par value with nominal consideration, and no paid-up capital escrow account is required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; portal: CRA My Business Account at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html); BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (provincial sales tax and Employer Health Tax administration; portal: eTaxBC at etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

For a BC-incorporated Business Corporation: (1) incorporate under the BC Business Corporations Act via BC Corporate Online, or federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act followed by extra-provincial registration in BC; (2) obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically through the Integrated Business Registration process at incorporation; (3) register for the GST/HST program account (BN-RC1) through CRA My Business Account or Form RC1 once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, or voluntarily; (4) register for the corporate income tax program account (BN-RC5) and payroll deductions account (BN-RC2) as applicable; (5) register with eTaxBC for PST and Employer Health Tax; (6) for a non-resident branch, file Form RC1 with the CRA International and Non-Resident Tax Office; non-resident suppliers of digital services may use the simplified GST/HST registration regime effective 1 July 2021.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident Business Corporation is taxed on worldwide net income under the Income Tax Act at the federal rate (15% general after provincial abatement) plus the BC general corporate rate (currently 12%), with no consolidated filing and no pass-through treatment; a BC branch of a non-resident corporation is taxed only on Canadian-source business income at the same combined rate plus the federal branch tax. — For a BC-incorporated Business Corporation: (1) incorporate under the BC Business Corporations Act via BC Corporate Online, or federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act followed by extra-provincial registration in BC; (2) obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically through the Integrated Business Registration process at incorporation; (3) register for the GST/HST program account (BN-RC1) through CRA My Business Account or Form RC1 once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, or voluntarily; (4) register for the corporate income tax program account (BN-RC5) and payroll deductions account (BN-RC2) as applicable; (5) register with eTaxBC for PST and Employer Health Tax; (6) for a non-resident branch, file Form RC1 with the CRA International and Non-Resident Tax Office; non-resident suppliers of digital services may use the simplified GST/HST registration regime effective 1 July 2021.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident Business Corporation is taxed on worldwide net income under the Income Tax Act at the federal rate (15% general after provincial abatement) plus the BC general corporate rate (currently 12%), with no consolidated filing and no pass-through treatment; a BC branch of a non-resident corporation is taxed only on Canadian-source business income at the same combined rate plus the federal branch tax.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election; classification is statutory. Available elections relevant to this structure: (a) voluntary GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240; (b) Quick Method of accounting for small suppliers (ETA ss. 236.01-236.03); (c) simplified GST/HST accounting for non-resident registrants; (d) ETA s. 156 election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration; (e) income tax fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1; (f) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 12.6, 18.4-18.6 and ETA ss. 44.1 and 123(1), with reduced withholding for partnerships and financial instruments; none of these alter the entity-level corporate classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000; GST/HST small-supplier threshold based on worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of digital services under the simplified registration regime; BC PST registration is required when the vendor makes taxable sales of software/services in BC while carrying on business in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating for exported automated payroll SaaS: the supply qualifies as a zero-rated supply of a service to a non-resident under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the non-resident is not in Canada at the time the service is performed and the service is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; for intangible property (software/IP licences), zero-rating applies under s. 10 Part V where the supply is made to a non-resident and does not include a right to use the IP in Canada in connection with real property or goods in Canada. BC PST does not apply where the software is sold to a non-resident for use outside BC. The exporter must obtain contemporaneous documentary evidence of non-resident status and absence of Canadian consumption, and file applicable returns with CRA.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CERS/CAED) - required for physical goods exceeding CAD 2,500; not applicable to pure SaaS digital delivery
  • Signed commercial contract with the non-resident customer identifying the licensed software, jurisdiction, and absence of Canadian use rights
  • Commercial invoice annotated 'GST Zero-rated Export' showing the supplier's Business Number and the customer's foreign address
  • Inward remittance or SWIFT wire transfer evidence from a foreign banking institution or equivalent foreign exchange record
  • Non-resident status evidence, such as foreign corporate registration certificate, tax residence certificate, or W-8BEN-E equivalent

None; Canada and British Columbia do not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government API pre-clearance, or digital stamping for e-invoices. Electronic invoicing is permitted in XML, EDI, PDF, or any retrievable format. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or XML/EFT; no continuous transaction control (CTC) model applies.

CRA may reassess disputed zero-rated exports and claw back uncollected GST/HST plus compound daily interest at the prescribed rate under ETA s. 280; failure to register or remit attracts a penalty of 6% of unremitted tax, increasing to 10% for a repeat failure; false statements or omissions attract a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the gross tax attributable under ETA s. 285.1; knowing or intentional evasion is prosecuted under ETA s. 327 with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. BC PST reassessments by the BC Ministry of Finance carry a four-year limitation period (unlimited in fraud cases), interest under the BC Tax Administration Act, and penalties of up to 25% for repeated non-compliance; taxpayer relief under ETA s. 281.1 is rarely granted for zero-rating documentation failures.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. There is no foreign-exchange licensing, approval, allocation, or repatriation permit requirement for dividends, shareholder loan repayments, or repatriated capital. Practical profit-remittance hurdles are exclusively tax-related: Part XIII dividend withholding tax (25% statutory rate, typically treaty-reduced to 5%–15% under bilateral income tax conventions), Part XIV branch tax on unincorporated permanent establishments, and the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review only for large non-resident acquisitions above statutory threshold values. A greenfield BC payroll-software subsidiary does not trigger foreign-exchange review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no central-bank surrender obligation for export or services proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign-currency proceeds offshore or convert through any chartered bank. The only administrative duty is statistical reporting under Statistics Canada's International Transactions in Services and the Canada Customs Invoice regime, none of which restricts treasury discretion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no dedicated foreign exchange administration body exists — the statutory exchange control powers under the Currency Act are dormant; day-to-day oversight rests with the Canada Revenue Agency and Statistics Canada for balance-of-payments and cross-border reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for standalone EMI usage, Medium for partner-assisted architecture. Offshore digital EMIs and e-money institution licences from the UK/EU do not confer deposit-taking authority in Canada and cannot access Payments Canada clearing rails (ACSS/Lynx/Real-Time Rail) without either a bank charter under the Bank Act or an authorization as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), the registration windows of which began phasing in 2024–2025. An offshore EMI may legally act only as a technology or FX-layer initiating SWIFT payments through a sponsoring Canadian chartered bank; it cannot hold CAD settlement balances in its own name or serve as the depository for Canadian client payroll funds. Therefore, any treasury design must maintain primary bank accounts at a Tier-1 CDIC-insured institution and use the EMI as a supplementary execution-only channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from a foreign parent must be executed as SWIFT MT103 credit transfers (or MT202 on the banking side), with beneficiary account in the Canadian entity's name, precise IBAN-style account details, and remitter parent name in field 50K. Field 23B should be CRED and field 70 should carry a clear narrative such as 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION / EQUITY CONTRIBUTION – BC CORPORATION'. Banks will record the flow as an inter-company capital contribution for source-of-funds and may request a Capital Injection Statement, a notarized directors' resolution authorizing issuance of shares, and a certified share subscription agreement. There is no mandatory Canadian government purpose-code; however, for internal compliance, use Statistics Canada Balance-of-Payments classifications such as 'Direct Investment – Inward Equity'. If the aggregate investment crosses Investment Canada Act notification thresholds, a one-time filing is required; greenfield SaaS payroll subsidiaries at ordinary capitalisation are generally below thresholds and require no pre-clearance.Domestic payroll disbursements should clear through Payments Canada's ACSS using bulk EFT files or ISO 20022 pain.001/pain.002; real-time requirements may use Lynx or, once fully operational, Real-Time Rail. Cross-border vendor and international PEO payments should be executed as SWIFT MT103/pacs.008, settled in CAD through Lynx correspondent accounts or in USD through CHIPS/Fedwire for dollar-drawn invoices. For service agreements exceeding counterparty credit thresholds, a commercial letter of credit (documentary credit) issued by the Canadian bank and confirmed by the beneficiary's bank is permissible, though payroll SaaS recurring service fees will ordinarily be paid by MT103 straight-through processing rather than letter of credit.
