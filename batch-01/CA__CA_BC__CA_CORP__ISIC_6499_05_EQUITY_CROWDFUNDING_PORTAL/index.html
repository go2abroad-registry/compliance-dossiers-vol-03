Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal portal: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online (BRO); BC Ministry of Finance (PST) administered separately via eTaxBC portal; securities licensure by BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) is complementary, not a tax registration.

E-government filing portal

Obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online, Form RC1, or at the time of provincial/federal incorporation (B.C. Corporate Online files carry-forward to CRA). Add program accounts: RC (Corporation Income Tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (Payroll) where applicable. Foreign non-resident corporations carrying on business in BC must simultaneously register extra-provincially under the Business Corporations Act (BC) and apply for a BN; GST/HST registration is mandatory when the portal ceases to be a small supplier or supplies financial services for consideration in Canada; BC PST registration with eTaxBC is required before collecting PST on non-exempt supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to federal (15%) and BC provincial (12%) general corporate income tax for a combined headline rate of 27%; no check-the-box pass-through is available; resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income (Income Tax Act, ITA s. 2(1), 250); the equity crowdfunding portal's commission income is generally a GST/HST-exempt financial service (Excise Tax Act, ETA Schedule V, Part VII), with no tax-free-zone status applicable in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. Available elections: (1) ETA s. 150 — joint election for closely related corporations to disregard certain intercompany taxable supplies; (2) ETA s. 156 — declaration for financial institutions to use an alternate net-tax calculation; (3) ITA anti-hybrid rules (ss. 18.4(3), 18.6, 20(1)(bbb), 94.2) disallow deductions on hybrid mismatch arrangements; (4) no statutory pass-through election exists at the federal level for public corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (tracked on a calendar-quarter and prior-four-quarter basis) defines the GST/HST 'small supplier' threshold (ETA ss. 148-149); a non-resident portal supplying services to Canadian recipients must register regardless of threshold if it is engaged in a commercial activity in Canada; BC PST has no analogous low threshold and registration is due before making taxable sales in BC; inputs for the exempt commission stream do not generate input tax credits (ITCs).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1; services (other than financial services) supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under Part V, s. 7 where the service is not performed in Canada for a Canadian resident and is not in relation to Canadian real property or goods. For the equity crowdfunding portal, core commission revenue constitutes a financial service: exports to non-residents may be zero-rated only under Schedule VI, Part IX, s. 1 where the service does not relate to a Canadian financial instrument, Canadian-resident party, or Canadian permanent establishment; otherwise the supply is exempt without ITC recovery. Any separate taxable online platform fees can qualify for 0% only with documented evidence of the non-resident recipient's status and non-Canadian place of use.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) filing where goods exceed CAD 2,000; Commercial Invoice and Packing List; through Bill of Lading (ocean/air/highway) evidencing shipment from Canada; foreign customs entry or arrival certificate; SWIFT MT103 or other inward-remittance record evidencing foreign-currency settlement; signed non-resident declaration and contract showing place of supply.
  • For the portal's ITC claims on any taxable inputs: CRA-compliant supplier invoices bearing the supplier's BN/RT, proof of payment, and Form GST 507 if a non-registrant refund path is used; CRA's Input Tax Credit Information Requirements under ETA s. 169(4) remain mandatory.

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or QR-code mandate. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or E-file) where annual global taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million, or where the registrant claims certain refunds (ETA s. 273.1). CRA requires invoice and books/records retention under ETA s. 286; the federal government promotes voluntary PEPPOL e-invoicing for public procurement but it is not mandatory for private crowdfunding platforms; the portal must nonetheless issue tax-compliant invoices showing the BN/RT number for any taxable auxiliary services (e.g., data, marketing, non-financial support) and retain full audit trails.

Canada has no general statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the billed 180-day benchmark is a contractual/commercial convention and does not override ETA or ITA rules. Exporters must convert foreign currency receipts to CAD using Bank of Canada exchange rates for income tax reporting (ITA s. 261). CRA reassessment windows are 4 years normally, extended to 10 years for misrepresentation or negligence (ITA s. 152(4)); GST/HST penalties include 50% gross-negligence penalties (ETA s. 285(1)), false-statement penalties (ETA s. 296), director liability (ETA s. 323), and interest on clawed-back refunds/ITCs; BC PST penalties apply under BC PST Act s. 116 for uncollected PST. Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239 and ETA s. 327 can result in fines of up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 5 years for export zero-rating fraud or fabricated offshore service claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible under both current and capital accounts. No exchange controls, prior approval mechanisms, or capital account restrictions apply to inbound investment, profit remittance, dividends, interest, management fees, or capital repatriation for a British Columbia equity crowdfunding portal. Payments may be settled in CAD or any freely convertible currency, subject only to statutory withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaties. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply to cross-border currency and monetary instruments above CAD 10,000, but no central bank conversion or surrender is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines. For an equity crowdfunding portal, the concept is inapplicable; however, investor funds received from non-residents in foreign currency may be converted, held offshore, or remitted to Canada freely without central bank approval or forced conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; OSFI supervises chartered banks for prudential soundness. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; exchange rate policy is implemented by the Department of Finance in coordination with the Bank of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. A Canadian equity crowdfunding portal must hold investor funds in escrow with a regulated Canadian chartered bank, bank-financed trust, or eligible Canadian trust company. Foreign digital EMI/payment institutions may not act as the primary settlement rail without registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada and applicable provincial money-services-business licensing. Offshore EMIs such as Wise Business or Revolut may be used only for auxiliary foreign-currency disbursements and must never hold client crowdfunding escrow balances without Canadian regulatory protection.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident directors and diverse international investor base

Root cause: Non-resident directors and diverse international investor base

Defense evidence: Validated passports, two independent utility bills, home-jurisdiction bank reference letter, certified corporate registry extract, clear source-of-funds and source-of-wealth statements, and a documented explanation of the platform's regulatory status in Canada and the home jurisdiction.
Equity crowdfunding platform classified as a high-risk securities intermediary

Root cause: Equity crowdfunding platform classified as a high-risk securities intermediary

Defense evidence: Current registration or recognition by the British Columbia Securities Commission (BCSC) and/or securities dealer registration under applicable provincial exemptions; corporate securities compliance manual aligned with National Instrument 45-110 and BC crowdfunding rules; AML/ATF policy approved by board; FINTRAC registration where applicable; and an independent third-party audit opinion or no-action letter.
Expected large volume of ACH, EFT, and wire inflows from unaffiliated retail investors

Root cause: Expected large volume of ACH, EFT, and wire inflows from unaffiliated retail investors

Defense evidence: Segregated sub-account bookkeeping; investor-level KYC register; payment-processor agreement with a regulated Canadian PSP; transaction monitoring and sanctions screening system; automated large-value and suspicious-transaction reporting protocol; and a well-defined money flow diagram from investor to escrow to issuer.
Complex shareholding, trust structures, or holding-company ownership

Root cause: Complex shareholding, trust structures, or holding-company ownership

Defense evidence: Notarized trust deeds or on-chain shareholder register; CDD on the full chain of ultimate beneficial owners; source-of-wealth evidence for all material contributors; legal opinion on the ownership structure; and an organizational chart with percentages and jurisdictional distribution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity crowdfunding subscriptions must be routed to the designated Canadian escrow account through compliant wire transfers. Use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COV with explicit purpose codes, for example 'GR' (investment/equity subscription), 'SP' (securities purchases), or 'TS' (third-party settlement). The portal must record the remitter's beneficial owner name, remitter account number, country of origin, and the underlying subscription reference. Cross-border disability: no Bank of Canada authorization is required, but all transfers over CAD 10,000, and any transaction meeting large-volume thresholds, must be reported to FINTRAC. Sanctions screening and OFAC/UN/EU denial-list verification must occur pre-settlement; clear narrative between source account and escrow account is mandatory to avoid hold or rejection.Outbound settlement to issuers, portfolio companies, or refunding retail investors is executed through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for domestic CAD payments or SWIFT MT103 for international wires. For cross-border trade disbursements, the equity crowdfunding portal may use irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by a Canadian chartered bank, documentary collections, or direct MT103 wires. Each MT103 must include the final beneficiary's bank BIC, account number, beneficiary legal name, and an unambiguous purpose code referencing the underlying disbursement, refund, or trade settlement. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Canada federal general corporate rate: 15%. British Columbia general corporate rate: 12%. Combined general rate: 27%. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation may generally access a combined small-business rate of 11% on eligible active business income within the applicable CAD 500,000 business limit, subject to associated-corporation, passive-income, and business-qualification rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident, subject to a reduced treaty rate. Under many Canadian treaties, the rate is 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% where the beneficial owner is a qualifying corporate shareholder meeting the applicable ownership threshold. The Canada-United States treaty commonly provides 15% and 5% rates, subject to its conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return is due within six months after the end of each taxation year. Any balance is generally due two months after year-end, or three months after year-end for qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations meeting the statutory conditions. Instalment dates apply during the year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally due with the corporation's T2 return for the relevant taxation year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

NR4 information returns and slips are generally due by the last day of March following the calendar year. Part XIII amounts are generally due by the 15th day of the month following the month of payment or credit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose a universal transaction-value threshold before contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is required under Income Tax Act section 247(4). Form T106 generally applies where reportable non-arm's-length non-resident transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate or another statutory filing condition is met. Country-by-Country reporting generally applies to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated annual revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year. A formal local file and master file should be maintained where applicable under Canadian rules and OECD-aligned practice.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the Income Tax Act, the general anti-avoidance rule, treaty anti-abuse principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, transfer pricing, thin capitalization, interest limitation, foreign-affiliate rules, and corporate-residence principles. The portal should maintain Canadian personnel, decision-making, risk control, technology or operational functions, regulated-dealer oversight, books and records, and commercially rational intercompany arrangements. A company incorporated in Canada may be treated as resident in Canada, but treaty residence and effective management issues require separate analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act penalties may include a penalty of up to 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit for gross negligence, reassessment, interest, and prosecution. Tax evasion may result in fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal prosecution, personal fines and imprisonment, civil recovery for statutory liabilities, and director liability for certain corporate tax debts where the statutory due-diligence defence is unavailable. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty but may arise from criminal process, bail conditions, or enforcement orders.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: The corporation may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax, arrears interest, remittance penalties generally ranging from 3% to 20%, information-return penalties, and gross-negligence penalties in appropriate cases.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed under Income Tax Act section 227.1 for unremitted source deductions if the corporation failed to remit and the statutory due-diligence defence is unavailable. Criminal exposure may arise for deliberate evasion.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may make a transfer-pricing adjustment, deny or reduce deductions, impose interest, and apply a penalty generally equal to 10% of the adjustment where the statutory conditions are met. Failure to provide required records can result in further penalties and extended assessment exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for resulting source deductions or corporate tax debts and may face prosecution where records were intentionally falsified or tax evasion was involved.
Unregistered Securities Dealer or Illegal Crowdfunding Activity

Statutory sanction: British Columbia securities-law sanctions may include cease-trade orders, registration suspension or revocation, disgorgement, administrative penalties, investor compensation orders, director and officer bans, and prosecution. Criminal or quasi-criminal offences can carry substantial fines and imprisonment under applicable securities legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal orders, bans, civil liability for misrepresentations or illegal distributions, restitution, and criminal prosecution. Travel bans or imprisonment may arise only through applicable enforcement or criminal proceedings.
Prohibited Financing and Client-Money Misuse

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include securities enforcement, breach-of-trust claims, restitution, disgorgement, regulatory penalties, licence loss, insolvency claims, and criminal fraud or theft charges.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face fiduciary-duty claims, constructive-trust remedies, personal restitution, civil damages, and imprisonment for dishonest conversion or fraud.
AML, Sanctions, and Proceeds-of-Crime Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act can reach approximately CAD 1,000,000 for a single serious violation by an entity, depending on the violation and classification. Criminal offences may result in substantial fines and imprisonment, including imprisonment for up to five years or longer under applicable offences.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face prosecution, fines, imprisonment, asset restraint or forfeiture, and personal liability for knowing participation, authorization, or wilful blindness. Regulatory travel restrictions may follow criminal proceedings or sanctions enforcement.
Beneficial-Ownership and Corporate-Transparency Failure

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include corporate-record penalties, securities and AML sanctions, tax reassessments, denial of treaty benefits, interest, administrative penalties, and criminal charges for fraud, laundering, or false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal fines, civil damages, restitution, disqualification, and imprisonment where they knowingly participate in concealment or false reporting.
Artificial Residence, Treaty Shopping, or Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits under beneficial-ownership, limitation-on-benefits, principal-purpose, or general anti-avoidance principles; reassess tax, interest, and penalties; and apply transfer-pricing or withholding-tax adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for source deductions and corporate tax debts, civil claims for dishonest conduct, and criminal prosecution if the structure involved deliberate deception. Imprisonment is fact-dependent and not automatic.
Payroll, GST/HST, and Provincial Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Assessments may include unremitted tax, interest, late-filing and late-remittance penalties, gross-negligence penalties, denial of input tax credits, and prosecution for deliberate non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unremitted payroll source deductions and may face personal assessments for certain GST/HST liabilities where statutory conditions are met.
Corporate Insolvency and Wrongful Trading

Statutory sanction: Remedies may include transactions-at-undervalue recovery, preference recovery, oppression remedies, director compensation orders, bankruptcy proceedings, and prosecution for fraud or fraudulent preference.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, contribution orders, disqualification, personal damages, and imprisonment for fraud or other criminal insolvency offences.
