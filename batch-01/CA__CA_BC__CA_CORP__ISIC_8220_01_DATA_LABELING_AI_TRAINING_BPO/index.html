No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a British Columbia company. Shares may be issued for consideration determined by the directors, subject to the Business Corporations Act and the Articles. Any subscription consideration should be received into the company bank account and recorded in the share register and accounting records.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal Business Number (BN), GST/HST, payroll, and federal/provincial corporate income tax, accessible through My Business Account at canada.ca; British Columbia Ministry of Finance for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and related provincial tax accounts, accessible through the BCeTax portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian business corporation carrying on business in British Columbia: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under British Columbia's Business Corporations Act and obtain the certificate/incorporation number; (2) register for the CRA Business Number (BN), which is the statutory federal tax identifier, using Business Registration Online or, for non-resident corporations, Form RC460 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts); (3) within the same CRA registration, open the GST/HST program account (RC) and, if payroll will be incurred, the payroll program account (RP); (4) separately register for the BC PST with the BC Ministry of Finance through BCeTax to obtain the PST vendor/registration number; (5) where applicable, register for the BC Employer Health Tax account through BCeTax; and (6) maintain corporate records, directors' information, a business address in BC, and timely program account filings in English or French.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election and does not maintain a general hybrid-mismatch election; a Canadian business corporation is mandatorily taxed at the entity level. Available statutory elections relevant to indirect tax include the GST/HST group registration election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act and the closely related corporation election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act to treat certain taxable supplies as made for nominal consideration. Federal and BC corporate income tax do not provide a partnership-style pass-through election for a business corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, based on total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding capital property) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; registration is compulsory once the threshold is crossed. BC PST: there is no general small-supplier monetary exemption; any person making taxable sales of goods or taxable services in BC in the course of a business must register, subject to narrow occasional-seller and marketplace exemptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For GST/HST purposes, exported goods are zero-rated when the goods are supplied by or on behalf of the supplier to a non-resident purchaser, are exported in a timely manner, and are not consumed or used in Canada. Services, including data-labeling and AI-training BPO services, are generally zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient and the service is not related to real property situated in Canada, is not performed in connection with tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not otherwise a Canadian domestic benefit. A data-labeling service delivered electronically to a non-resident client will normally qualify for the zero-rating if the recipient is non-resident and the service is not supplied to a non-resident individual while he or she is in Canada. BC PST does not apply to exported goods or to services delivered, used, and consumed wholly outside British Columbia; the supplier should retain evidence of the export nature of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for goods: Form B13A export report or CBSA ACI eManifest; for pure service BPO, the underlying service contract and delivery evidence constitute the substantive export record)
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill/courier receipt for physical goods showing shipper, consignee, and foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (in Canada: foreign exchange dealing record, SWIFT or bank credit confirmation, or foreign currency deposit documentation evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds from the non-resident client)
  • Executed service agreement, statement of work, and delivery logs for data-labeling BPO services demonstrating the AI client is non-resident and the service benefit is offshore
  • Commercial invoice and the non-resident client's confirmation of non-resident status, together with a written statement that the supply is zero-rated for GST/HST and not subject to BC PST

Canada does not currently mandate government-authorized electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based preclearance of invoices. The CRA accepts correctly issued electronic invoices provided the records are complete, legible, and retained for at least six years. A GST/HST zero-rated invoice must show the supplier's name and Business Number, the date of invoice, a description of the supply, the consideration, and a clear indication that the supply is zero-rated. BC PST invoices must separately show PST charged where applicable, and the PST registration number must appear on sales invoices or delivery documents.

CRA auditors may disallow export zero-rating retroactively if the documentary evidence does not contemporaneously prove actual export, non-resident receipt, and foreign economic benefit, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within a commercially reasonable period; a 180-day inward remittance benchmark is commonly used and longer delays increase audit risk. Reassessment of uncollected GST/HST, denial of input tax credits, refund clawbacks, and arrears interest may follow. Civil penalties for gross negligence or false statements under the Excise Tax Act generally equal up to 50% of the net tax avoided, with unlimited retrospective reassessment where there is misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default, and criminal prosecution remains possible for deliberate tax offences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely convertible currency on both current and capital accounts. There are no statutory exchange controls requiring central bank approval for cross-border trades, services payments, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily tax-based: dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), generally 25% absent treaty, reduced to 5%–15% under most comprehensive income tax treaties. No foreign exchange license is required for the conversion of CAD to USD or other major currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio on export proceeds. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts and to convert at commercially negotiated rates. However, all export transactions equal to or exceeding CAD 30,000 must be reported to Statistics Canada under the Canadian International Merchandise Trade (CIMT) survey, and all international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable by financial institutions to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. There is no distinct timeline for repatriation of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority, together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money laundering and cross-border transaction reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility as supplemental treasury rails, but not as primary accounts. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise and Payoneer may operate only as money services businesses (MSBs) in Canada after FINTRAC registration and do not qualify as Canadian banks for deposit taking or settlement under the Bank Act. They can provide efficient cross-border payout processing for low-value AI training and data-labeling disbursements, but cannot provide CAD clearing infrastructure, payroll direct deposit, or Lynx settlement membership. Primary corporate liquidity must remain domiciled in a Canadian Tier 1 bank account, with EMIs used only for global gig-worker and overseas contractor settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are routed through SWIFT MT103 messages from the foreign shareholder's bank to the Canadian corporate operating account. The wire must carry the beneficiary account details using the Canadian institution number and branch/transit number (e.g., Royal Bank: 003, branch number, account number). For SAC/SIB coding, the remittance description should clearly state 'Equity Injection / Share Capital Contribution' and the OECD purpose code corresponding to 'Direct Investment Equity Capital' (e.g., purpose under the Bank of Canada Balance of Payments classification). Verification is performed by the bank against the corporate account's designated signatory list; FINTRAC reporting is triggered for amounts at or above CAD 10,000. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in total cross-border transactions between the Canadian reporting corporation and each non-resident related party or class of transaction in a taxation year triggers mandatory Form T106 and comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation. CRA expects a Local File and contemporaneous benchmarking; a Master File is required where the MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia and Canada do not have standalone ESR legislation; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and CRA transfer-pricing audit practice mandate economic substance for intercompany arrangements. A data-labeling BPO company must demonstrate that its Canadian entity performs significant people functions, owns or contracts for valuable intangibles, bears commensurate risks, and justifies profit allocation under arm's length principles. State-only shell holding vehicles without Canadian staff or operational substance are at high risk of recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act section 239(1) with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil penalties of 50% of gross negligence under section 163.2

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, interest, and penalties under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act; criminal conviction can extend to personal fines, travel bans, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to void tax benefits, reassess all years, apply 25% penalty on the denied avoidance portion under subsection 245(7), and invoke treaty abuse limitation-of-benefits provisions, denying Part XIII reductions

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed for gross negligence penalties and held personally liable for the provincial and federal tax amounts if they directed or assented to the arrangement; travel bans and property seizures can be imposed during collection or criminal proceedings.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: BC corporate penalties up to CAD 25,000 for register violations and personal fines up to CAD 20,000 for directors under Business Corporations Act; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for Money-Service-Business failures; criminal money-laundering sanctions up to 5 years imprisonment under Criminal Code section 462.31

Director personal liability: Responsible directors face personal monetary penalties, debarment from board service, travel restrictions pending extradition, and imprisonment for knowingly failing to report material beneficial-ownership changes or laundering funds.
