E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-resident BC Business Corporation is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; no pass-through or check-the-box eligibility; no territorial exemption; no tax-exempt zone status; foreign tax credits mitigate double taxation.

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch election exists for Canadian incorporated entities; hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 17.1-17.7) apply automatically. Available elections: (i) ITA s. 85(1) tax-deferred rollover election for qualifying property/IP transfers; (ii) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related corporations to value intercorporate supplies at nil for GST; (iii) ITA s. 83(2) capital dividend account election; (iv) treaty-based Part XIII withholding relief for non-resident shareholders (investor-level, not entity-level).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The Canadian dollar is freely floating with no residual exchange-control legislation. Profit remittance is unrestricted, but non-resident dividends, management fees, royalties, and interest paid by a British Columbia corporation are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) at 25% statutory, treaty-reduced as applicable (e.g., 15% dividends and 10% royalties under the Canada-China DTA). Repatriation of capital, repayment of shareholder loans, and liquidation proceeds may be transmitted without prior regulatory approval; the only frictions are standard FINTRAC anti-money-laundering reporting (large cash transactions over CAD 10,000, cross-border EFT reporting over CAD 10,000) and income-tax arm's-length/section 15 benefit rules where advances to shareholders are involved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts or any foreign-denominated revenue. A British Columbia corporation may lawfully maintain USD, EUR, GBP, and CNY bank accounts in Canada or abroad and may convert export or service proceeds at its treasury's discretion. FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting obligations do not restrict conversion freedom.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting as monetary authority and fiscal agent for the Minister of Finance; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act; foreign exchange administration is governed by the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Exchange Fund Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. E-13), with operational FX intervention executed by the Bank of Canada; no separate central-planning exchange-control administration exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary payment rail, Low as a primary banking solution. Wise (TransferWise Canada Limited) and Payoneer are registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, not banks under the Bank Act; they cannot legally accept deposits or originate Canadian-dollar debits through Payments Canada rails. Their Canadian trust balances must be held at Schedule I chartered banks. The patent prosecution agency may use Wise for low-value international disbursements to foreign patent offices and associates, but the principal operating and client trust accounts must be maintained at a Canadian chartered bank to satisfy College of Patent Agents and Traders (CPAT) trust-account rules and Canadian counterparty expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share-subscription proceeds are routed via SWIFT MT103 annotated 'Share Subscription - BCBCA s.190 - CRA Business Number [xxxx]' to the bank operating account in CAD; the initiating corporate treasury must attach the executed directors' resolution authorizing the issuance, the share-subscription agreement, and a funds-verification statement. No prior approval is required under the Investment Canada Act for a de novo incorporation of a patent prosecution agency where enterprise value remains below the WTO threshold (CAD 1.298 billion for 2025 direct acquisitions). The receiving Schedule I bank files FINTRAC cross-border EFT reports within five business days for wires over CAD 10,000. Additional CAD liquidity may be injected as shareholder loans evidenced by a written promissory note carrying prescribed interest under Income Tax Act s.80.4 to avoid a deemed shareholder benefit; the corporate accountant must update the BC issued-share register and issue share certificates.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wire transfers and Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail, Large Value Transfer System, or Automated Clearing Settlement System for domestic CAD payments.
