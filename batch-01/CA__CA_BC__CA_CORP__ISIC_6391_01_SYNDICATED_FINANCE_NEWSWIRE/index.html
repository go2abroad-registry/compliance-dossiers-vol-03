Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal via canada.ca/business-registration; GST/HST filing via CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first establish its Canadian legal presence (e.g., federal or British Columbia extra-provincial registration), then apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Accounts), or through Business Registration Online where eligible. The BN must be maintained as the GST/HST registration identifier, displayed on invoices, and used in filings. If the foreign entity lacks a Canadian physical address, a registered representative or tax agent may be required for service of notices and electronic portal access.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not apply a check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the legal form of incorporation and residence under the Income Tax Act. Relevant elections for a foreign-owned Canadian business corporation include the extension/waiver of limitations agreements, the s. 93 election for eligible return of capital from a foreign affiliate, treaty-based relief elections, and hybrid-mismatch limitation rules under s. 18.4–18.6 applicable to entities or instruments that receive inconsistent classification between Canada and a foreign jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) per calendar quarter and over the four preceding calendar quarters for supplies made in Canada; a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada above this threshold must register for GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier exports the goods in the normal course of business, or transfers possession to a non-resident who then exports within 60 days, and the supplier retains documentary proof of export. Services (including financial newswire data feeds) qualify for zero-rating where supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under GST/HST, the service is not an excluded financial service, and the service is performed entirely outside Canada or is otherwise determined to be consumed offshore.

  • Form B13A or Canadian Export Declaration filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CES), or approved exporter report confirmation
  • Commercial invoice identifying the non-resident purchaser and the zero-rated supply designation
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier waybill with export exit evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the Canadian financial institution evidencing receipt of offshore consideration
  • Packing list and cargo control document for physical exports
  • Written contract or subscription/API agreement establishing non-resident use and offshore delivery for services

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or a universal e-invoice platform. GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically where required by CRA thresholds and matrix reporting rules. Tax invoices must contain the business number, date, vendor/purchaser identifiers, description, and tax charged. PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is voluntary and used mainly in federal procurement and large buyer mandates.

CRA may reverse zero-rating where export documentation is incomplete, internally inconsistent, or created after the fact. A clawback results in reassessment of the supply at 5% GST (plus any applicable provincial component where the supply is deemed made in Canada), interest, and penalties. False statements may attract a penalty of the greater of $250 and 5%–10% of net tax misstatement, gross negligence penalties may reach 50% of the additional tax payable, and intentional evasion can trigger criminal fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment. The normal reassessment limitation is four years, extended to seven years where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts; the Canadian dollar is free-floating with no surrender, quota, licensing, or repatriation-approval requirements. Profit remittance via dividends, interest, royalties, or branch profits requires no Bank of Canada consent; the only frictions are tax compliance, namely Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%–15% on dividends under the Canada–United States tax treaty), branch remittance tax of 25% on deemed remitted branch profits, and the filing of a permanent-establishment tax return. For the ISIC 6391 financial-news aggregation and syndication feed, the activity is a non-cultural business service under the Investment Canada Act; a non-resident establishing this new Canadian business must file the Form B New Business Notification, but is not subject to net-benefit review or to any currency-related investment conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished mandatory export-proceeds surrender and exchange controls in 1952; exporters may retain receipts in any foreign currency in Canada or abroad with no statutory conversion ratio or timeline. The only operational obligations are FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting by financial institutions for any wire equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, CBSA statistical B13A export declarations for goods (not services), and CRA tax-translation of foreign-currency income into CAD equivalents. The BC newswire API delivery of subscription and licence data is an export of services and therefore triggers no customs surrender rule, no B13A filing, and no provincial repatriation requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); monetary authority and operator of the Lynx high-value settlement system, but Canada maintains no exchange-control administration. Prudential licensing of banks is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); deposit insurance is provided by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC); AML/CFT supervision is performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); foreign-investment screening under the Investment Canada Act is administered by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a supplementary clearing rail, but not viable as a primary treasury bank. Canadian banking law does not recognize the European-style 'EMI' as a deposit-taking licence; foreign digital banks and payment facilitators (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) service Canadian clients only by registering as Money Service Businesses with FINTRAC, maintaining a Canadian agent, and holding client float in CDIC-insured Schedule I chartered banks. Such entities have no direct access to the Lynx settlement system, no CDIC deposit insurance, and cannot issue Canadian clearing instruments in their own name. For the low-risk ISIC 6391 news-feed API model they are acceptable for US and GBP collection sweeps, but long-term treasury balances must remain at a Tier-1 domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity funding is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the external investor's bank to the BC Tier-1 operational account; the remittance information (Field 70) must clearly state 'Paid-up capital subscription; BC Corporate Registry Number; Investment Canada Act Form B Notification date'. The bank may map the transfer to ISO purpose code INVS (investment and securities) or CASS (cash management) in the electronic payment header; the beneficiary bank must file a FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer report for any amount equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000. Onboarding teams verify the corporate registry status, the shareholder resolution, the UBO declaration, and destination-account ownership before crediting contributed surplus or share capital. No Bank of Canada exchange-control approval or investment-permit procedure applies.Domestic CAD settlement uses Lynx, Payments Canada's real-time gross-settlement system, for finality on high-value payments; cross-border vendor, royalty, and media-partner disbursements use SWIFT MT103 messages through established correspondent corridors, with purpose codes such as TRAD (trade payment) or LICF (licence-fee royalty), depending on the bank's mapping. Documentary trade settlements for platform infrastructure and data-centre purchases are supported by commercial letters of credit issued under SWIFT MT700 and amended under MT707. USD-denominated disbursements are optimized through the Canadian bank's US affiliate (for example, RBC's US banking subsidiary or BMO Harris Bank and TD Bank, US) to eliminate double conversion spreads. Canadian banking law does not recognize the European-style 'EMI' as a deposit-taking licence; foreign digital banks and payment facilitators (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) service Canadian clients only by registering as Money Service Businesses with FINTRAC, maintaining a Canadian agent, and holding client float in CDIC-insured Schedule I chartered banks. Such entities have no direct access to the Lynx settlement system, no CDIC deposit insurance, and cannot issue Canadian clearing instruments in their own name. For the low-risk ISIC 6391 news-feed API model they are acceptable for US and GBP collection sweeps, but long-term treasury balances must remain at a Tier-1 domestic bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents triggers Form T708 reporting. CRA contemporaneous-documentation expectations apply to all such transactions; larger MNE groups are expected to prepare OECD-aligned Master File and Local File.

Economic substance scope: No dedicated Economic Substance Regulations in Canada. The general anti-avoidance rule (ITA section 245) recharacterizes transactions lacking genuine economic substance and business purpose; applicable penalties and interest are assessed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under ITA section 239: fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalty for gross negligence (s.163(2)) equals 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and withholdings under ITA; criminal convictions can trigger travel bans, asset freezes, restitution orders, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under subsection 245(2); CRA can assess additional tax, interest, and a 50% penalty; BC Registrar may dissolve the company for failure to maintain a registered office under the Business Corporations Act.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the corporation's unpaid tax under section 160 (asset transfer rules); courts may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere sham; criminal enforcement can impose personal fines and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ITA subsection 247(3) candor penalty: 5% to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (not offset by taxes), plus applicable interest. Adjustments may also trigger double taxation relief issues.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly endorse or cause inaccurate transfer-pricing records may be assessed under director-liability provisions; if fraud is proven, criminal liability and restitution orders apply.
AML/CRS/T1134 Information Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of CAD 2,500 per each unfiled T1134/T1135 return under ITA s.162(10.2); CRS non-compliance penalties up to CAD 2,500 per return; Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act fines up to CAD 500,000 for entities.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for repeated non-compliance penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in the failure; willful AML violations carry imprisonment of up to 5 years and personal asset freezes.
