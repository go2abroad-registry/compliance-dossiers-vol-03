Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada – Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/account-numbers/register-business.html); British Columbia Ministry of Finance – Consumer Taxation Branch / eTaxBC portal (https://www.etaxbc.gov.bc.ca) for PST.

E-government filing portal

The BC limited partnership must obtain its own CRA Business Number (BN) before carrying on commercial activity in Canada. A foreign-owned LP should register through Business Registration Online or Form RC1 and submit the partnership agreement, identification of all general and limited partners, their residency status, addresses and profit-sharing interests. CRA will issue a 15-character BN, and the LP must activate a GST/HST program account (RT) if it makes taxable supplies in Canada. Registration for British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is obtained separately through eTaxBC once the LP begins making PST-taxable retail sales in British Columbia. A Canadian authorized representative or agent may be appointed for CRA and BC tax administration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election applies; an LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act section 96, with each partner taxed on its allocated share. Available elections include voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier under ETA section 148 and partnership fiscal-year elections under ITA subsection 96(9); Canada’s hybrid mismatch rules do not provide an elective reclassification of a domestic limited partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; BC PST: no general monetary small-supplier threshold — vendor registration is required before taxable sales are made in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may zero-rate supplies of canned boba tapioca pearls under ETA Schedule VI, Part V only where the goods are exported from Canada as part of a commercial supply, delivered to the foreign buyer or out of Canada before use or consumption in Canada, and the LP retains contemporaneous export evidence. For PST purposes, British Columbia does not impose PST on goods delivered by the vendor to a destination outside British Columbia; the export exemption must be documented on the vendor’s records.

  • Canada Export Declaration (B13A) or electronic export report/CERS transaction number where export value is CAD 2,000 or above
  • Commercial invoice describing the tapioca pearls, quantity, export sale price, Incoterms and foreign buyer
  • Original bill of lading, air waybill, or CERS-linked truck manifest proving delivery outside Canada
  • Packing list, weight certificate, or container seal record evidencing physical movement from Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank credit advice confirming foreign-currency settlement within 180 days of export

No mandatory national or British Columbia real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists for GST/HST or PST. CRA requires prescribed invoice elements including supplier name, BN/RT account, transaction date, supply description and GST amount; BC PST requires a valid invoice or receipt for taxable sales. E-invoicing through B2B APIs or Peppol is voluntary, and no government authorization, API stamping or fiscal clearance is required.

CRA may reject zero-rating and reassess net GST/HST at 5%, with statutory interest, if the export documentation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent; the normal reassessment period is four years and may extend indefinitely for fraud or misrepresentation. Gross negligence or false export claims can trigger civil penalties of up to 25% of the understated tax and, in serious cases, criminal prosecution for evasion. BC PST may likewise be restored at 7%, with late payment penalties and compound interest, if the export exemption cannot be substantiated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full and free convertibility on both the current and capital accounts. No exchange-control statute exists; the Currency Act contains only dormant emergency powers that have never been activated. Profit remittance is unrestricted and requires no regulatory approval. The sole fiscal frictions are Part XIII withholding tax on outbound interest, royalties, dividends, and rental payments, and, for non-resident partners of a Canadian Limited Partnership carrying on business in Canada, the obligation to file Canadian Part I income tax returns on the Canadian-source business income allocated to them. There are no capital import/export licenses, no foreign-investment exchange permits, no repatriation quotas, and no restriction on holding foreign-currency bank balances in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Canned boba/tapioca exporters may retain proceeds in any currency and any jurisdiction. Two reporting duties apply: FINTRAC receives a report on every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, and CBSA Form E311 is mandatory for physical cross-border currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000. Export invoices must be retained under the Customs Act and Excise Tax Act, with zero-rated GST/HST treatment available for qualifying exports.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority. Foreign exchange oversight is shared with the Department of Finance Canada (policy and emergency powers under the Currency Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of banks, FINTRAC for AML/CTF surveillance, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for physical currency movement reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognize 'electronic money institution' as a standalone licensed category. A foreign fintech EMI must either (a) register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and obtain a licence under the BC Money Services Act, or (b) integrate through a regulated Canadian bank via banking-as-a-service. Unregistered offshore EMIs cannot obtain a Canadian financial institution number, cannot access the Lynx or Settlement Optimization clearing systems, and OSFI-regulated Canadian banks will not sponsor unlicensed EMIs for CAD settlement. For a canned-boba manufacturing LP, reliance on an offshore EMI as the primary treasury rail is not recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident partner's bank to the LP's Canadian Capital Escrow Account or Operational Account, with a remittance narrative referencing 'capital contribution' and the BC registration number. Canadian banks do not apply Chinese-style FX purpose codes; instead the remitter must provide a source-of-funds narrative and the beneficiary must lodge a funds-receipt letter citing the LPA clause authorising the contribution. Every international wire of CAD 10,000 or more is reported to FINTRAC. Escrow-held funds are released only upon a solicitor's written direction and a signed funds-release authorisation under the escrow agreement.Export settlements for canned boba/tapioca pearls should be executed from the operational account via: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct transfer for prepaid or small consignments; (ii) irrevocable sight or usance documentary Letters of Credit issued by foreign banks and advised or confirmed through a Canadian bank under UCP600 for first-time or high-value buyers; and (iii) documentary collections under URC 522 for established relationships.
