Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online, GST/HST accounts, and income tax administration; British Columbia Ministry of Finance — PST registration via BC eTaxBC (https://www.etax.gov.bc.ca/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership registers for a Canadian Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account through CRA's Business Registration Online or by submitting Form RC1 / RC151. The partnership must provide its partnership agreement, proof of foreign registration, Canadian contact/agent information, and details of anticipated Canadian sales. After obtaining the BN and GST/HST registration, the LP must separately register with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST through the provincial online portal.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), a limited partnership is fiscally transparent; income, losses, and credits flow through to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the limited partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and may register as a GST/HST registrant. Territorial scope: Canadian-source income is allocated to resident and non-resident partners; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income, subject to treaty relief. — A foreign limited partnership registers for a Canadian Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account through CRA's Business Registration Online or by submitting Form RC1 / RC151. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), a limited partnership is fiscally transparent; income, losses, and credits flow through to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the limited partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and may register as a GST/HST registrant. Territorial scope: Canadian-source income is allocated to resident and non-resident partners; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income, subject to treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide check-the-box entity elections; partnerships are transparent by default. Available statutory elections include an elected fiscal period for partnerships under ITA s. 249.1(4), a section 216 election for non-residents with Canadian rental income, and GST/HST joint elections under ETA s. 156 for closely related corporations. Hybrid mismatch deductibility is restricted by ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 where a payment is deductible in Canada but treated as non-includible by the payee jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in the current and preceding three consecutive calendar quarters, or across a single calendar quarter for small supplier status; BC PST: CAD 10,000 in gross taxable sales in the immediately preceding 12 months (small seller exemption).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods (including ultra-HD 4K minimally invasive endoscopy towers classified under ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339112) is zero-rated to 0% GST/HST if the supplier exports the goods, or ships them at the direction of the recipient, to a destination outside Canada. Services and intangibles may be zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is not using or consuming them in Canada. PST does not apply to goods delivered outside Canada under a contract of sale where the seller ships to an export destination.

None. Canada and British Columbia do not mandate real-time electronic invoicing clearance, API-based fiscal stamping, or a central clearance platform for GST/HST or PST. Suppliers may issue paper or electronic invoices; CRA accepts electronic records if they remain readable and auditable. No digital stamping or continuous transaction control framework applies.

CRA may reassess an export zero-rating claim within the normal four-year reassessment period; where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established, reassessment is not time-limited. A zero-rating claim that fails substantiation exposes the taxpayer to reassessment at 5% GST plus applicable BC PST, interest, and a 25% false-statement or omission penalty under ETA s. 285(1). BC PST officials may impose a 75% penalty for wilful evasion or attempt to evade tax. Criminal prosecution can result in fines and imprisonment. Retrospective clawback of exported refunds and input tax credits is available where the 180-day foreign exchange repatriation or documentary requirement is not met.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency regime under the Bank of Canada Act. Current account transactions are freely convertible without prior approval. Capital account transactions, including profit remittances, dividends, and interest, are generally permitted but require prior notification or approval from the Bank of Canada for amounts exceeding prescribed thresholds; profit remittance is unrestricted for Canadian residents subject to tax reporting and beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign exchange proceeds or convert them freely at market rates. All foreign exchange transactions must be reported to the Bank of Canada (via authorized dealers) and to the Canada Revenue Agency for tax compliance; recommended repatriation timeline is 30-90 days for operational accounting purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods; CAD 500,000 for services, interest, royalties, and other transactions with each non-resident

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the OECD economic substance requirements; the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) can recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance. For a limited partnership, the CRA may scrutinize the allocation of income and the existence of real business activity, especially in manufacturing operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239); fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors (general partners) may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may apply.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR may deny tax benefits; penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit; CRA may reassess and impose interest.

Director personal liability: General partners may be held personally liable for the partnership's tax liabilities if the partnership is deemed a sham; limited partners may lose limited liability if they participate in management.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties for individuals; potential seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly failed to comply; travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years for criminal offences.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Adjustments to income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete), and interest on reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they signed false statements or knowingly participated in the mispricing; civil restitution and potential criminal charges.
Failure to File Returns

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of $25 per day per partner (max $2,500); additional penalties for failure to provide information; interest on any unpaid amounts.

Director personal liability: General partners may be personally liable for penalties if they were responsible for filing and failed to do so; CRA may issue a director's liability assessment.
