Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration; official digital portal: CRA My Business Account via Canada.ca; British Columbia Ministry of Finance through BC Registry / BC OneStop for provincial PST and Employer Health Tax registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN), which is the Canadian TIN for corporations, by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or by registering online through CRA My Business Account. If the foreign corporation begins making taxable supplies in Canada, a registered GST/HST account is part of the same BN application. The BN is composed of a 9-digit account identifier plus a program account suffix (for example, RT for GST/HST, RC for corporate income tax). For foreign entities without Canadian incorporation, CRA may issue a non-resident BN for GST/HST where the entity has no permanent establishment in Canada but is required to collect GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act. Before carrying on business in British Columbia, an extraprovincial corporation must also file an extraprovincial registration under the BC Business Corporations Act and obtain BC PST registration through BC OneStop if selling taxable goods or services in BC. No minimum capital is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or entity classification election for corporations; a Business Corporation is a distinct taxpayer under the Income Tax Act. For eligible Canadian corporations, available elections include: (i) functional currency reporting election under ITA s. 261, (ii) non-calendar taxation-year election under ITA s. 249.1, and (iii) loss-utilization elections under ITA ss. 110 and 111. For British Columbia provincial tax, an election may be required for allocation of taxable income between provinces under the federal/provincial tax collection agreement; hybrid mismatch rules are automatic and do not provide an elective check-the-box mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, determined under section 148 of the Excise Tax Act (small-supplier threshold); once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. BC PST: remote sellers and marketplace facilitators must register if taxable sales to BC individuals exceed CAD 10,000 in the previous 12 months; no general threshold applies to BC-based sellers with an ordinary business presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating may be claimed for (i) goods exported in accordance with the Excise Tax Act as part of a sale and accompanied by proof of export, and (ii) services or intangible personal property (including EdTech subscription access and automated tutoring software) supplied to a non-resident person when the supply is not in respect of real property in Canada and not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada. For online language-learning subscriptions delivered through Apple App Store and Google Play, the place-of-supply rule generally treats the supply as made outside Canada if the consumer is outside Canada; the supplier must retain reasonable evidence of the non-resident status of the customer, delivery to a non-Canadian account or storefront, and payment settlement without a Canadian billing address. The supply is zero-rated for GST/HST purposes, and the registrant may claim input tax credits attributable to the zero-rated supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or authorized reporting) for physical export shipments; not applicable to online-delivered EdTech subscriptions.
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / courier export manifest for tangible goods; not applicable to zero-rated digital services.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank wire confirmation, or payment processor settlement report evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer.
  • For digital services and subscriptions: executed service agreement, non-resident customer self-declaration, IP address/geolocation logs, device/storefront country evidence, Apple App Store and Google Play sales/tax reports, and internal delivery logs showing no supply made to a Canadian recipient.

Canada does not require real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, digital stamping, or a government-designed e-invoicing network; CRA accepts electronic books and records if they are accurate and legible. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices or simplified receipts containing prescribed information (BN, date, description, tax rate and amount) for taxable supplies. Electronically filed GST/HST returns through CRA My Business Account are mandatory for certain registrants; PST invoices in BC must separately disclose the PST collected, but there is no pre-clearance or continuous transaction control regime.

CRA and Canada Border Services Agency may reassess and reverse zero-rating, disallow input tax credits, deny refunds, and recover GST/HST with interest. Penalties for misstatements or fraudulent export claims include gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the net tax sought to be evaded, minimum penalties for false returns, fines and imprisonment for tax evasion under sections 280 and 239 of the Excise Tax Act, and imposition of director/participant liability where a corporate claimant knowingly participated in a sham. BC PST authorities may likewise assess uncollected PST plus interest and penalties if the export exemption is not substantiated.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, and the repealed Exchange Control Act framework abolished in 1958. There are no balance-of-payment exchange controls, no prior-approval requirements for foreign investment, and no restrictions on profit repatriation. Dividends and branch remittances are freely payable abroad, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (base 25%, typically reduced to 5-15% under an applicable income tax treaty). The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating, fully convertible currency with an actively traded offshore market. For an EdTech subscription company incorporated in British Columbia, paid-up capital injections, license royalty payments, and intercompany service invoices are all remittable without exchange-control consent or surrender requirements. Practical hurdles are limited to banking AML/KYC due diligence and FINTRAC reporting obligations for Canadian-dollar and foreign-currency transactions at or above CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters, including EdTech software and subscription-service providers, may hold foreign currency in USD or multi-currency accounts indefinitely without any surrender obligation to the Bank of Canada or Export Development Canada. Although no surrender rules apply, transactions of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting duties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; financial institutions must also comply with Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) guidance on correspondent banking and sanctions screening. Corporates are, however, required to report certain cross-border movements of physical currency and monetary instruments in excess of CAD 10,000 via the Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting (CBSA/Finance) regime.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), payment-system oversight by Payments Canada and the Bank of Canada; no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration exists in Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium viability with mandatory Canadian registration. Canada functionally does not recognize the European/UK EMI licence or any equivalent 'Electronic Money Institution' passporting. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and multi-currency platforms must either obtain an 'authorized payment service provider' registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), administered by the Bank of Canada with transitional registration closing deadlines, or partner with a Schedule I chartered bank as the clearing sponsor. An offshore EMI cannot independently provide a Canadian bank account number or hold client CAD funds without RPAA compliance; the bank-facing treasury architecture should therefore treat EMIs as supplementary collection/settlement rails, not as the primary corporate banking account holder, to avoid OFSI and CDIC protection gaps. For British Columbia, a Canadian Schedule I bank account remains the fully viable custody and clearing base, with EMI rails used only for low-value consumer collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages from the parent or overseas investor to the corporation's Schedule I Canadian bank account. Canada does not operate a foreign-exchange purpose-code approval regime; the remitter's narrative field generally states 'Share Capital Subscription', 'Paid-up Capital', or 'Intercompany Loan'. The Canadian receiving bank will require the corporation to complete a standard account-opening questionnaire and the bank to file an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC for any single inbound credit or same-day aggregate of CAD 10,000 or more (including outbound transactions). No Bank of Canada or OSFI prior notification is required for the capital injection itself, although banks must satisfy Customer Due Diligence on the investor and source of funds. Recommended routing: parent bank SWIFT payment in USD is accepted directly into its USD-transit account, with a same-day WesBank/CLS or correspondent FX conversion to CAD; alternatively, injection in CAD to avoid conversion friction. All in-bound investment documents, board resolutions, and subscription agreements must be archived in the corporate treasury folder for immediate production to the onboarding compliance team.For trade settlements and vendor payables, Canadian corporates use SWIFT MT103 for single cross-border payments and MT202 for interbank cover. High-value CAD clearing leverages Lynx (the Canadian RTGS system, successor to the Large Value Transfer System); batch low-value retail payments run through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). For USD-denominated disbursements, the preferred structure is a USD correspondent account through the Canadian bank's US clearing bank, or a concentration account at a US correspondent, enabling cross-border payment in USD without double FX conversion. Where specific performance or repayment security is needed for content-licensing or infrastructure-provider agreements, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 and advised/confirmed by a Schedule I Canadian bank, or a standby letter of credit (ISP98), is considered permissible. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for cross-border services; CAD 5 million for other controlled transactions

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits and foreign affiliate status. Transactions lacking commercial rationale may be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of tax evaded; criminal fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years (Income Tax Act s. 239)

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold or remit (s. 227.1); travel bans and seizure of personal assets possible
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization; denial of treaty benefits; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in sham arrangements; civil restitution and potential imprisonment
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for willful non-compliance; travel bans and asset freezes possible
