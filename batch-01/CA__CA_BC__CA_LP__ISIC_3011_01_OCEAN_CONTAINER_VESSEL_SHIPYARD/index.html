Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary portals include My Business Account (MyBA), Business Registration Online (BRO), and CRA Represent a Client; provincial registry counterpart for BC limited partnerships is BC Registry Services / BC Corporate Online.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Limited Partnership in British Columbia under the Limited Partnership Act by filing a declaration with BC Registry Services. (2) Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) with the GST/HST program account code RT via Business Registration Online, Form RC1, or the CRA Business Window; a non-resident partnership without a Canadian branch address must register through the CRA International Tax Services Office by mail or fax, attaching the partnership agreement and foreign tax identification evidence. (3) Enrol an RC program account for payroll deductions if Canadian employees are engaged and an RP account for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) vendor registration with the BC Ministry of Finance. (4) For non-resident limited partners, file Form NR3010 for a treaty-based exemption or arrange section 216 / Part XIII withholding under the Income Tax Act before any income distribution or instalment remittance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (partnership is flow-through for Canadian income tax; each limited partner is taxed on its allocable share and the partnership is not an income-tax taxpayer). For GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate person and must hold its own BN/RT registration to claim input tax credits on shipyard inputs. Territorial scope: Canadian-source income of non-resident partners is taxable in Canada, while Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income. No federal tax-exempt-zone regime applies to BC shipyards.

Available election forms

Available elections: (i) partnership fiscal period election under subsection 249.1(6) ITA to adopt a non-calendar tax year; (ii) section 216 ITA election for a non-resident partner to file a net-basis return on Canadian-source shipping or rental income instead of gross-basis Part XIII tax; (iii) joint subsection 273(1) ITA election permitting a non-resident to be assessed on net Canadian tax; (iv) deemed year-end election under subsection 150(2) ITA; (v) section 150 of the Excise Tax Act election for closely related entities to simplify GST/HST reporting; (vi) voluntary GST/HST registration election under subsection 240(1.1) ETA. Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the legal form of the partnership, subject to treaty tie-breaker rules and CRA administrative guidance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters (subsection 240(1) ETA). A non-resident carrying on commercial activity in Canada must register regardless of the threshold where it solicits orders in Canada, has a permanent establishment, or supplies through a Canadian agent; maritime manufacturers typically register voluntarily to reclaim input tax credits on production inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A BC shipyard may zero-rate a completed ocean container vessel or shipbuilding components where (i) the goods are supplied to a non-resident recipient and exported by the supplier or its agent as soon as reasonably possible and generally before the last day of the calendar month following transfer of possession (Schedule VI, Part I, section 6, Excise Tax Act); (ii) services rendered to a non-resident in respect of the vessel construction, assembly, or repair qualify under Schedule VI, Part I, section 7 as zero-rated supplies to non-residents; or (iii) the supply is an ocean-going vessel for use in international commercial transportation under Schedule VI, Part I, section 5. Evidence must establish the foreign destination, actual export date, and the non-resident status of the purchaser; any sale for domestic use or installation in Canada remains taxable at 5% GST plus 7% BC PST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) required for exports valued above CAD 2,500 or for controlled goods
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or multimodal port-specific shipment document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing foreign-currency receipt and payment repatriation
  • Commercial invoice and sale/purchase contract with INCOTERMS
  • Packing list and cargo control document
  • Export permit or foreign-trade-zone approval for controlled or regulated vessel components
  • Proof of carrier receipt and vessel export manifest

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, e-stamping, or continuous-control stamp regime exists in Canada. Electronic invoicing is voluntary through the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network administered by the CANADA PEPPOL Authority and through EDI formats accepted by the CRA. Registered shipyards whose taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million annually must file GST/HST returns electronically, but per-invoice clearance APIs and fiscal digital stamps are not mandated.

The CRA Export Verification Unit audits zero-rated shipyard exports using cross-referenced customs receipts, bank statements, and third-party trade data. Where zero-rating is denied or substantiation is deficient, CRA reassesses GST at 5%, retrospectively claws back input tax credits, and charges interest from the original filing due date. Penalties include the 5% late-remittance penalty plus 1% per full month under subsection 280(1) ETA; a false-statement penalty under subsection 285(1) ETA equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the overstated net tax; a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of any unreported or over-claimed amount; and criminal prosecution under section 327 ETA with a fine of up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment of up to two years. The normal four-year reassessment period is extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or wilful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally require approval for foreign investment, capital contributions, intercompany loans, dividend distributions, royalty payments, or repatriation of sale proceeds. Banks must nevertheless complete anti-money-laundering, sanctions, tax-reporting, beneficial-ownership, source-of-funds, and transaction-purpose reviews. Payments involving designated persons, sanctioned jurisdictions, restricted goods, defense-related activity, or unusual maritime counterparties may be delayed, rejected, blocked, or reported. A British Columbia limited partnership should maintain partnership agreements, partner registers, contribution records, audited or management accounts, tax filings, invoices, contracts, and resolutions supporting all cross-border movements. Canadian withholding tax, transfer-pricing, thin-capitalization, treaty-entitlement, taxable-presence, and partnership-allocation rules may affect the net amount available for remittance even though the foreign-exchange movement itself is not generally restricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, convert export receipts into Canadian dollars, or convert a prescribed percentage within a prescribed period. Export receipts may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the partnership's discretion. The exporter must retain commercially reasonable evidence of the underlying export, including shipbuilding contracts, invoices, bills of lading or delivery certificates, customs records where applicable, letters of credit, and bank advices. Financial institutions may request transaction information under anti-money-laundering and sanctions rules. Customs, tax, export-control, controlled-goods, and sectoral licensing obligations remain separate from foreign-exchange convertibility.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general exchange controls. Prudential and payment-system oversight also involves the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Department of Finance Canada, and the applicable provincial financial-services and securities regulators.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplemental collections, foreign-currency holding, and payment administration; Low as the sole treasury bank for a Canadian shipyard LP. An offshore electronic-money institution does not automatically provide a Canadian deposit account, Canadian payment-system access, commercial-letter-of-credit issuance, escrow services, or regulated trust protection. Availability depends on the provider's licensing perimeter, Canadian solicitation restrictions, correspondent-bank arrangements, sanctions controls, safeguarding model, and acceptance of a non-corporate or partnership customer. Offshore balances may create additional tax-residency, information-reporting, permanent-establishment, source-of-funds, and insolvency-risk issues. Use a Canadian chartered bank for core operating, payroll, tax, project, escrow, and trade-finance functions, with any EMI restricted to documented ancillary flows after legal and counterparty due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner contributions, equity-like funding, and partner loans through the LP's Canadian chartered-bank account from an account held in the contributor's legal name. Use SWIFT cross-border payments with complete originator, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, address, account, and payment-purpose information. Canada generally does not impose a mandatory foreign-exchange purpose-code system for ordinary commercial receipts; the bank's internal purpose classification and transaction narrative should state, for example, "limited partnership capital contribution," "partner loan," or "shipyard project funding." Before release, provide the partnership agreement, contribution or loan instrument, partner resolution, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, originating-bank details, and expected-flow schedule. Reconcile the received amount to the approved funding instrument and record foreign-exchange rates, fees, value date, and intercompany balances. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated group entities, cash, or unapproved payment intermediaries. Review thin-capitalization, withholding, transfer-pricing, partnership-allocation, sanctions, and beneficial-ownership consequences before classifying the funds.Use Canadian chartered-bank accounts and approved correspondent banks for supplier, subcontractor, freight, insurance, classification-society, port, and equipment payments. SWIFT MT103 or the applicable successor payment message is suitable for ordinary wire settlement; include contract or invoice reference, vessel or hull number, milestone, Incoterms, and complete beneficiary information. Use commercial letters of credit, standby letters of credit, documentary collections, bank guarantees, or controlled escrow for high-value maritime installments. The issuing bank should conduct sanctions, export-control, dual-use, vessel, vessel-owner, port, and counterparty screening and verify documentary conditions before payment. For letters of credit, specify UCP 600 or another agreed ICC rule set, presentation requirements, inspection certificates, delivery evidence, partial-shipment and transshipment terms, discrepancy handling, governing law, and reimbursement bank. Retain customs, shipping, invoice, acceptance, tax, and payment records. Apply withholding tax and non-resident reporting where required, and separately assess import GST, duties, customs valuation, controlled-goods, and export-licensing requirements.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with a Canadian bank for documented commercial exposures, supported by an exposure schedule, settlement currency, maturity, notional amount, counterparty limit, and hedge-accounting or treasury approval.Non-deliverable forwards where the relevant currency or jurisdiction is not freely deliverable, subject to bank eligibility, Canadian derivatives documentation, cash-settlement terms, sanctions screening, and evidence of an underlying commercial exposure.Currency swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps for recurring foreign-currency funding, long-dated shipbuilding contracts, or partner-loan exposures, subject to master trading documentation, collateral terms, valuation, close-out, accounting, and counterparty-credit approval.Foreign-exchange options and collars for protected minimum or maximum conversion rates, subject to suitability, premium approval, disclosure of contingent obligations, and prohibition on speculative or uncovered structures unless expressly authorized by the treasury policy.Spot foreign-exchange conversions for settlement and operational liquidity, with segregation of hedging and speculative activity, documented hedge ratios, counterparty limits, independent valuation, confirmation matching, and periodic effectiveness testing.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for supplemental collections, foreign-currency holding, and payment administration; Low as the sole treasury bank for a Canadian shipyard LP. An offshore electronic-money institution does not automatically provide a Canadian deposit account, Canadian payment-system access, commercial-letter-of-credit issuance, escrow services, or regulated trust protection. Availability depends on the provider's licensing perimeter, Canadian solicitation restrictions, correspondent-bank arrangements, sanctions controls, safeguarding model, and acceptance of a non-corporate or partnership customer. Offshore balances may create additional tax-residency, information-reporting, permanent-establishment, source-of-funds, and insolvency-risk issues. Use a Canadian chartered bank for core operating, payroll, tax, project, escrow, and trade-finance functions, with any EMI restricted to documented ancillary flows after legal and counterparty due diligence.
