Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a BC limited partnership; capital contributions are determined by the partnership agreement and may be cash or property. No escrow or mandatory deposit account is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the BC Fire Code, after inspection of the die-casting molten metal area; structural load inspection of the building foundation and mezzanines for melting furnaces and presses; occupancy permit from the municipal building inspector confirming egress, fire separation, and ventilation; WorkSafeBC clearance for equipment guarding, personal protective equipment (PPE), and molten metal handling procedures.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a BC limited partnership; capital contributions are determined by the partnership agreement and may be cash or property. No escrow or mandatory deposit account is required.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online portal and CRA My Business Account. British Columbia provincial sales tax is administered separately by the Consumer Taxation Programs Branch of the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

Obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online, by telephone, or by the applicable CRA registration form. A Canadian limited partnership generally requires a BN when it has Canadian tax filing, payroll, import-export, GST/HST, or information-reporting obligations. Register the GST/HST program account using the BN where taxable supplies require registration. Non-resident entities should provide legal formation documents, partnership agreement, ownership and authorized-representative information, Canadian business activities, physical and mailing addresses, and supporting authorization. A non-resident entity carrying on business in British Columbia must separately assess provincial registration obligations, including British Columbia PST registration through eTaxBC where it is a PST collector or vendor. The BN and GST/HST account are not the same as a provincial corporation or partnership registration number. The CRA does not issue a general VAT ID because Canada operates a GST/HST system rather than a European-style VAT identification system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes: income, losses, and other tax attributes are allocated to partners under the partnership agreement and applicable tax rules. The partnership itself generally does not make a general corporate check-the-box election to become a taxable corporation. Partners may be subject to Canadian filing, withholding, treaty, and information-reporting rules based on residence, source, and allocated income. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules can deny or adjust deductions, inclusions, or tax attributes where hybrid arrangements create deduction or inclusion mismatches; these rules are not an elective entity-classification regime. A US check-the-box election may be available under US law for US tax purposes, but it does not change the Canadian legal or Canadian income-tax classification of the limited partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration is generally required when a person exceeds the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under the applicable calculation rules, including the relevant taxable supplies over the prescribed quarterly or consecutive-four-quarter period. Certain public service bodies and special categories have different thresholds. A non-resident may be required to register despite not being resident in Canada where it makes taxable supplies in Canada and does not qualify as a small supplier or where a special registration rule applies. British Columbia PST has separate registration and collection rules; there is no single universal PST threshold equivalent to the federal CAD 30,000 rule, and registration may be required based on the nature of taxable goods, software, services, business presence, solicitation, inventory, or other specified nexus factors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are generally zero-rated for GST/HST where the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including delivery or making the goods available to a recipient outside Canada in the prescribed circumstances, or shipment from Canada by the supplier or an agent to a destination outside Canada. The supplier must retain evidence that the goods were exported and must apply the applicable place-of-supply and export rules. Services may be zero-rated where they are supplied to a non-resident and qualify under the exported-services rules, subject to exclusions for services connected with Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, litigation or dispute proceedings in Canada, and other statutory exceptions. Goods supplied for export may also qualify for zero-rating where the purchaser exports them under the prescribed conditions and the supplier obtains required documentary evidence. Zero-rating does not automatically apply merely because the customer is non-resident, payment is received in foreign currency, or the contract describes the transaction as offshore. British Columbia PST generally does not apply to goods that are properly exported from British Columbia for use outside the province, but the supplier must substantiate the exemption under provincial rules.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, purchaser, description, quantity, value, currency, and destination
  • Canada Border Services Agency export declaration or electronic export reporting record, including the applicable Canadian export declaration requirements and filing reference
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier document showing shipment from Canada to a destination outside Canada
  • Packing list and transport documentation linking the exported goods to the invoice
  • Proof of delivery or importation in the destination jurisdiction, where available
  • Sales contract, purchase order, and customer correspondence establishing the foreign destination and export terms
  • Accounting records linking the zero-rated invoice to inventory records, shipment records, and customs records
  • Proof of payment or bank records supporting the commercial transaction; an inward-remittance certificate is not a general Canadian GST/HST statutory requirement
  • For exported services, evidence of customer non-residence, contract scope, place of performance, deliverables, customer-use location, and payment trail

Canada has no general nationwide real-time GST/HST invoice-clearance or electronic fiscal-stamping mandate for ordinary commercial transactions. GST/HST registrants must issue compliant invoices or receipts when required, retain books and records, maintain audit trails, and support input tax credit claims with prescribed documentary information. Electronic invoices are generally acceptable if they preserve reliability, readability, accessibility, and record-retention requirements. CRA electronic filing requirements apply to specified returns and registrants, but they do not constitute real-time invoice clearance. British Columbia PST likewise has no general real-time invoice-clearance mandate for ordinary sales. Importers and exporters may have to use the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) electronic systems, including the applicable CARM processes, for customs accounting, declarations, and payment.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement to obtain an inward-remittance certificate to support GST/HST zero-rating. CRA and provincial auditors may deny zero-rating, assess the applicable GST/HST or PST, deny related input tax credits or rebates, and impose interest where export conditions or documentary substantiation are not met. A supplier that incorrectly treats a taxable domestic supply as zero-rated may be assessed the uncollected tax, applicable arrears interest, and gross-negligence or other penalties where the statutory conditions are satisfied. False statements, omissions, fabricated records, or knowingly misleading claims may attract civil penalties and, in serious cases, criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act, Income Tax Act, Customs Act, or applicable provincial legislation. The normal reassessment period may be extended for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful blindness, or fraud; fraud or a false statement attributable to fraud can remove ordinary limitation protection. Export GST/HST rebates and input tax credits may be reassessed and recovered, with interest and penalties, where the claimant cannot establish eligibility. GST/HST registrants must retain supporting records generally for six years after the end of the relevant year, subject to specific statutory exceptions and extensions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under a liberalized capital account regime with full convertibility for current account transactions; non-resident partners in a Canadian LP may remit profits subject to 25% withholding tax and FINTRAC/CRA reporting; capital account investments require FIRB notification where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds; free convertibility upon receipt. Banks require commercial invoice, bill of lading, and CBSA export declaration for FX transactions. Statutory reporting to CBSA mandatory within 30 days for exports exceeding CAD 2,500.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
