Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a BC business corporation may incorporate with a single no-par-value share. No escrow account, capital deposit, or bank confirmation rule applies to non-distributing private corporations under the BCA.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal for federal GST/HST, and British Columbia Ministry of Finance – eTaxBC portal for provincial PST and employer health tax

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Business Corporation in British Columbia must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by submitting Form RC1 Request for Business Number. For GST/HST and payroll accounts, the application must include the entity's certified articles of incorporation, registered office address in BC, the legal entity name, and the foreign parent/beneficial owner's foreign tax identification number. The CRA grants a single 9-digit BN; each program account (GST/HST, corporate income tax) carries a two-letter program code suffix (e.g., RT for GST/HST). For British Columbia-based sales, the entity must separately register with the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through the eTaxBC portal for PST under the Social Service Tax Act. Foreign non-resident corporations carrying on business in BC can complete CRA Form RC4135V for non-resident corporate income tax registration or apply directly through BRO for GST/HST if taxable turnover exceeds CAD 30,000. After CRA issuance, the BN is used for all federal filings and tax remittances, including Excise Tax Act returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. A BC business corporation is treated as a separate taxpayer liable to Part I federal corporate tax and BC corporate income tax. Available statutory elections include: (i) GST/HST election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to have related corporations form a GST/HST group; (ii) section 156 of the Income Tax Act election for a non-resident corporation to file deemed taxable Canadian profits and claim treaty relief in respect of business carried on in Canada; (iii) capital dividend account election under subsection 83(2); and (iv) Healthy Comparison. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada applies the OECD hybrid mismatch arrangements rules in sections 12.2 to 12.91 of the Income Tax Act, but no functional currency or flow-through election is available for a BC business corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: mandatory registration for non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada with taxable worldwide revenues exceeding CAD 30,000 in a single quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; British Columbia PST: mandatory registration if the entity makes taxable retail sales, leases, or sales of software/cloud services to BC consumers exceeding nil threshold (PST registration is required before carrying on business in BC except limited promotions), though small business exemptions do not apply to cloud software taxable services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A BC business corporation may claim a zero-rated (0% GST/HST) supply for (i) goods exported from Canada directly by the supplier, the purchaser, or a related agent, provided the goods are exported as soon as they are delivered and documentary proof of export exists; (ii) services provided to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and where the service is 'zero-rated' under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, including software and cloud services supplied solely to a non-resident but with substantive use and enjoyment outside Canada; and (iii) digital or cloud services, including vertical CRM software offerings, where intangible property or IP is delivered electronically to a non-resident outside Canada. PST generally does not apply to exported goods or services sold to non-residents where the buyer takes delivery if that buyer has no location in BC. For software-as-a-service subscription to a non-resident customer, zero-rating eligibility requires evidence that the non-resident does not use the software within BC.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency – CUSMA/CAED Export Report)
  • Bill of Lading or Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Proof of Settlement from a Canadian bank or foreign counterpart
  • Signed contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident purchaser
  • Commercial invoice referencing the exemption category under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act

Canada has no national fiscal-real-time electronic invoice clearance regime. Mandatory electronic GST/HST filing applies where filing volume, ASIC, and corporate income tax thresholds are met. BC PST does not require government pre-approval of invoices. Canadian e-invoicing is voluntary and uses the Pan-Canadian Peppol standard; there is no digital stamping mandate. The CRA requires all GST/HST registrants filing with annual taxable supplies over CAD 6 million to file electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or the GST/HST EDI system. B2B and B2C sellers in BC must issue PST-compliant sales invoices showing separate PST, GST, transaction date, and seller's registration number; cloud API-based invoicing integrated with the BC PST system is expected if the ERP CRM platform generates tax determinations, but no immediate CRA clearance API is required.

Under the Excise Tax Act, a person claiming a zero-rated export who knowingly or under circumstances giving rise to gross negligence makes a false statement may be liable to a penalty of 25% (or 50% if made knowingly) of the net tax sought by means of the false statement; additional interest under section 28 applies. For GST/HST, any refund or rebate based on an invalid zero-rating claim may be reassessed retrospectively without time limit if misrepresentation or fraud is proven, with a normal four-year reassessment window ending on the later of three years from the filing date or three years from the notice of assessment. For PST on exports, BC under the Social Service Tax Act may impose civil penalties of 10% of tax evaded plus 2% per month for late remittance, and criminal fines of up to CAD 200,000 for corporations for fraudulent export claims. The CRA and BC Ministry of Finance routinely cross-check customs declarations and FX inflow records through the CUSMA export verification portal; a clawback including principal tax, interest, and penalties will be assessed for any transaction where the 0% rate is not supported by the mandatory documentation bundle or the foreign exchange consideration is not repatriated within the reported timeline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under the IMF Article VIII obligation. Profit remittances, including dividends, interest, and royalties, are freely transferable through authorized banks without central bank approval. Non-resident repatriation is subject solely to Part XIII withholding tax rules under the Income Tax Act, with treaty-reduced rates available. Cross-border electronic payments require standard AML/ATF reporting under FINTRAC but do not require exchange control permits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts may be retained in domestic or foreign currency accounts. Exporters are required to file customs declarations under the Export Reporting requirements of the Canada Border Services Agency and to report balance-of-payments data to Statistics Canada; no FX turnover or delivery obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises deposit-taking institutions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for offshore digital EMIs. Canada regulates money services businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act through FINTRAC, and payment service providers holding client funds must be authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act. An offshore EMI lacking local FINTRAC registration, RPAA authorization, or a Canadian banking partner is not a viable primary clearing rail for treasury operations. Regulated Canadian fintech banks or payment platforms with a full-service or MSB license may serve as valid supplemental rails if operational through a Tier 1 bank sponsor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections into the BC software subsidiary should be transmitted through the parent bank using SWIFT MT103 with purpose code '100 - Direct Investment in Canada (Equity)' or, for intercompany loans, '150 - Direct Investment in Canada (Loan to Resident)'. The receiving Canadian bank must receive the funds to a dedicated capital-escrow or treasury account and may require the submitting institution to complete the bank's 'Cross-Border Payments' form and provide underlying documentation covering the investment agreement, board resolution, and ultimate beneficial ownership. Canadian banks accept US dollars or Canadian dollars; conversion is executed at the corporate treasury rate applicable at settlement.For operational trade settlements, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 corporate wire transfers (with GPI tracking) for invoice payments, including licensed software fees, hosting fees, and subcontractor disbursements. Commercial documentary letters of credit issued by the Canadian bank are used for high-value equipment procurement or multi-jurisdictional software distribution contracts requiring title documentation. For domestic recurring client refunds and partner settlements, the Canadian real-time payment system (Real-Time Rail, where available), wire-based Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), and Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) support batch direct debit transactions in CAD.
