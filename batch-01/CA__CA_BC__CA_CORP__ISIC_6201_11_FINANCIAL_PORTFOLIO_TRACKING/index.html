Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

British Columbia prescribes no minimum paid-up share capital for a business corporation; s. 227 BCA permits directors to issue shares for any consideration they determine. No escrow account, capital deposit, or bank block account is required at formation, and the company may commence operations with nominal issued capital.

Reg. 296/97, including ergonomic workstation, electrical safety, and emergency preparedness requirements for all personnel.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box/pass-through election for Canadian corporations. Hybrid mismatch rules (sections 18.2/18.21 ITA) govern dual-consistency and deduction/non-inclusion outcomes; treaty relief and subsection 85 rollover elections remain separately available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or a single calendar quarter) triggers mandatory GST/HST registration; BC PST registration is required from the first taxable sale in BC (no threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when exported by the supplier in a commercial transaction or shipped to a foreign addressee under a bill of lading. Services and intangible supplies (including subscription cloud analytics) are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and where the supply is not performed on Canadian real or tangible personal property. Evidence must show the non-resident's status and the destination/use outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Cargo Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment proof of foreign currency proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping at federal level. CRA accepts electronic records; GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed statutory particulars. Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing is voluntary; BC PST returns are filed via eTaxBC, without API-mandated fiscal clearance.

CRA can reassess beyond the normal 4-year limitation period for misrepresentation, claw back refunds/input tax credits, impose penalties (5% of misreported tax under ETA s. 280; up to 50% for gross negligence) and institute criminal proceedings for intentional evasion; directors and controlling officers may be held personally liable.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible. Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no capital or current account restrictions, no FX licensing, and no general profit-remittance approval. Dividends to non-residents may be repatriated freely but are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory rate, reduced under applicable tax treaties) and require NR4 reporting to the CRA. SaaS export revenue, royalty and share-redemption proceeds face no currency rationing or conversion hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian exporters and SaaS vendors are not required to surrender export proceeds, convert receipts within a statutory timeline, or maintain a minimum conversion ratio. Foreign-currency receipts may be held offshore; however, for tax purposes all revenue must be measured in CAD unless the corporation validly elects a functional currency under Income Tax Act section 261, and the Bank of Canada exchange rate must be documented for each transaction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and cross-border funds-flow monitoring by FINTRAC; Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange administration, foreign-exchange policy being administered by the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act and the Financial Administration Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low–Medium. The European-style electronic-money institution (EMI) has no direct Canadian statutory analogue; an offshore EMI cannot lawfully take deposits, issue electronic money, or hold client balances in the Canadian market without becoming a bank or a licensed MSB. Given the entity is a pure SaaS analytics platform (ISIC 6201, no required statutory license), an offshore EMI is not advisable commercially and should be replaced by a Canadian tier-1 bank account coupled with regulated PSP payment acceptance (Stripe, Apple IAP). Any holding of third-party funds or unlicensed money transmission would trigger the Bank Act banking privilege and FINTRAC MSB registration requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliance protocol: route inbound funds exclusively as SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit transfers to the designated CAD operating account, with a purpose code mapping to equity subscription (OECD transaction code 5120 is standard, while Canadian banks accept domestic code 009). FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer (EFT) reporting is triggered on all cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in relevant scenarios; the company must retain the share subscription agreement, board resolution, source-of-funds statements, and the originating bank's reference to demonstrate a lawful non-money-laundering purpose. Funds must be booked as contributed surplus or share capital in the CRA tax filings and do not require Bank of Canada approval.Outbound disbursements settle over: (i) SWIFT MT103 for non-urgent international vendor payments; (ii) Payments Canada Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for institutional CAD wholesale settlements; (iii) Real-Time Rail (RTR) once fully launched for CAD instant payment requests; and (iv) UCP-600 documentary credits via SWIFT MT700/MT707 for high-value hardware/cloud-infrastructure procurement. Standby letters of credit (SWIFT MT760) are available from the tier-1 bank to guarantee SaaS delivery obligations. Stripe and Apple IAP aggregations settle via the PSP's payout cycles into the CAD operating account, with merchant integration governed by those PSPs' commercial terms.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards under an ISDA/CSA master agreement with the tier-1 bank at maturities up to 24 monthsNon-deliverable forwards for restricted or illiquid currencies in intercompany settlement scenariosCross-currency swaps and cross-currency basis swaps for multi-currency treasury liabilities
