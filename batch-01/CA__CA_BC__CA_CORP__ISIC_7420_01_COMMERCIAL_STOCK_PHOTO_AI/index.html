E-government filing portal

Maintain a registered agent in Canada and issue T4, T5, and NR4 information returns as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; a Canadian business corporation is intrinsically a separate entity taxed at the corporate level under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Available elections include: functional-currency election under s. 261 ITA; capital cost allowance elections; the closely-related corporation joint election under s. 156 ETA to render intercompany supplies nil for GST/HST; hybrid-mismatch alignment elections under ss. 12.2, 18.2, and 18.3 ITA covering structured finance arrangements; and CCPC/small business deduction association elections under ss. 125 and 256 ITA. Foreign tax credit and non-resident withholding elections apply at the shareholder level.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
0.05%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
GST/HST: mandatory registration when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four calendar quarters (ETA s. 240), including zero-rated export supplies. BC PST: registration required for out-of-province vendors of specified digital goods and services when BC sales exceed CAD 10,000 in the preceding 12 months (BC PST Act s. 10.4).

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Supplies of intangible property (stock photo licences, AI-generated visual assets) and digital services are zero-rated under ETA s. 23 where the recipient is outside Canada at the time of supply and is not a GST/HST registrant; s. 23.1 zero-rates services performed in Canada whose benefit is enjoyed entirely outside Canada. Tangible exports must leave Canada within 60 days (Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1(d)). BC PST does not apply to goods or services exported for consumption outside BC.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax filing system or digital stamping applies in Canada. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed information under s. 223 of the Excise Tax Act and the GST/HST Information Returns Regulations, including seller name and BN, buyer identification, description of supply, tax charged, and invoice date. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million; T2 corporate income tax returns must be filed electronically once gross revenue exceeds CAD 1 million; BC PST returns must be transmitted via eTaxBC. The federal Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing protocol is voluntary at present and applies predominantly to supplier-government procurement.

      
CRA disallows zero-rating and related input tax credits on any export where the prescribed documentation bundle is incomplete; the normal reassessment window is four years, unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Penalties include: s. 285.1 ETA gross-negligence penalty of 25% of net tax understated; s. 291 ETA civil penalty for false statements up to 200% of tax evaded; s. 327 ETA criminal liability (on indictment) up to CAD 250,000 fine or twice the tax evaded and up to five years imprisonment; s. 163(2) ITA civil penalty of 50% of understated income tax. The general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245 ITA / s. 274 ETA) applies to sham export schemes lacking a genuine business purpose, and all assessments carry daily prescribed-rate interest plus 4%.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts, consistent with IMF Article VIII. No statutory restriction, licensing, or prior central-bank authorization applies to royalty payments, licensing revenue, service-export proceeds, dividends, or equity inflows. Profit-repatriation friction arises only from Part XIII of the Income Tax Act non-resident withholding taxes (25% statutory; treaty-reduced typically to 5% for dividends and 0%-10% for royalties) and, for non-Canadian acquirers of control, from Investment Canada Act notification and review thresholds (C$1.283 billion for WTO investors in 2024). Separate legal confirmation is required as to whether stock-photo and generative-content licensing constitutes a 'cultural business' under the Investment Canada Act schedules, which would lower the review threshold. Capital may be held in CAD or any foreign currency without conversion or surrender obligations.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export proceeds or cross-border service receipts. Export and royalty proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any currency. The compliance framework instead centres on: (i) FINTRAC large-transaction reporting above C$10,000; (ii) Form T1135 foreign-income verification for foreign property exceeding C$100,000; (iii) recognition of foreign-exchange gains and losses under section 39 of the Income Tax Act; and (iv) deemed interest under section 17 on outstanding intercompany foreign-currency advances.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the central monetary authority under the Bank of Canada Act; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces AML/CTF obligations. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange administration in Canada; the country maintains a freely floating exchange-rate regime and does not operate exchange controls.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium as an auxiliary payout mechanism; Low as the primary treasury provider. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Stripe, PayPal) are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and operate as payment service providers (PSPs). Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), administered by the Bank of Canada, PSPs offering funds transfer, clearing, or settlement services to Canadian end-users must be registered and comply with safeguarding, incident-reporting, and operational-risk rules; foreign EMIs may not commingle client funds with their own balance sheet. The recommended architecture is a primary operating account at a Schedule I bank layered with EMI rails solely for mass micropayment distribution to global stock-photo contributors, with all EMI balances swept daily to the regulated Canadian account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Equity capital from non-resident shareholders must move through transparent, non-conduit banking channels. The compliant protocol is: (i) transmit by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own regulated bank account to the corporation's CAD/USD concentration account at a Schedule I bank, with Field 70 Remittance Information stating 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION', including the board-resolution date and shareholder name; (ii) prohibit routing through unrelated third-party accounts, shell treasury vehicles, or unmarked crypto conversions; (iii) supply to the bank a documentary dossier containing the board resolution authorizing share issuance, the amended Notice of Articles, the share-subscription agreement, and the updated register of members for source-of-funds clearance; (iv) assess Investment Canada Act notification where a non-Canadian shareholder acquires control above the applicable WTO threshold, and separately confirm cultural-business classification for content-licensing activities; (v) obtain W-8BEN-E or IRS Form 8833 from U.S. shareholders to evidence treaty entitlement where applicable; and (vi) complete CRS and FATCA self-certification identical to the beneficial-ownership schedule. No Canadian central-bank pre-approval or CAD conversion mandate applies to equity injections.Outbound trade and royalty disbursements to global contributors should use the following clearing hierarchy. (i) Cross-border bank-to-bank settlements via SWIFT MT103 from the Canadian concentration account to contributor banks in USD, EUR, GBP, and major Asian corridors, with Field 70 purpose descriptors such as 'ROYALTY PAYMENT', 'LICENSE FEE', or 'CONTRIBUTOR PAYOUT'. (ii) Canadian domestic disbursements via Payments Canada ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) for standard electronic credits, and Lynx (the high-value real-time gross settlement system) for same-day large-value wires. (iii) Mass micropayment distribution to a fragmented global contributor base via fintech payout automation (Stripe Connect, PayPal Mass Pay, Wise Batch), funded from the Schedule I primary account, with every payout leg traceable to the underlying licensing event and captured in tax, CRS, and record-keeping remits. (iv) Commercial letters of credit are not applicable to service or royalty flows and should be reserved exclusively for physical-media distribution or content-supply contracts where documentary credit is contractually required. All outbound wires must be screened against OFAC, UN, and Canadian Sanctions Act lists, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds must be monitored (C$10,000 for large currency or virtual-currency transactions, including attempted transactions of C$30,000 or more).Spot FX contracts with the Schedule I dealing bank or an OSFI-regulated counterparty for same-day settlement of CAD/USD/EUR/GBP receivables and contributor payables.Outright plain-vanilla currency forwards with a maximum 12-month tenor covering forecast royalty revenue and contributor-payout exposure, with notional limited to the underlying business flow (no speculative leverage).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or thinly traded currencies (e.g., INR, CNY, PHP, IDR, BRL) where onshore forward convertibility is legally unavailable to the counterparty, settled by CAD/USD difference payment and documented as a business-purpose hedge for contributor corridors.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) for matching intercompany funding into foreign-currency settlement accounts, supported by prudent hedging documentation and income-tax elections under section 39 of the Income Tax Act.Vanilla FX options (call and put) for time-bounded protection on major licensing revenues where the contributor share is denominated in a volatile currency, with premiums treated as operating expense and no resale or speculative derivative overlay.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as an auxiliary payout mechanism; Low as the primary treasury provider. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Stripe, PayPal) are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and operate as payment service providers (PSPs). Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), administered by the Bank of Canada, PSPs offering funds transfer, clearing, or settlement services to Canadian end-users must be registered and comply with safeguarding, incident-reporting, and operational-risk rules; foreign EMIs may not commingle client funds with their own balance sheet. The recommended architecture is a primary operating account at a Schedule I bank layered with EMI rails solely for mass micropayment distribution to global stock-photo contributors, with all EMI balances swept daily to the regulated Canadian account.
