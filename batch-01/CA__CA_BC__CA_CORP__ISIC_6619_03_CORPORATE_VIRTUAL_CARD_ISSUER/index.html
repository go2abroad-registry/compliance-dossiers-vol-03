Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada. Principal portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). British Columbia's 7% provincial sales tax (PST) is separately administered by the BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC portal); BC corporate income tax is collected by CRA on a federal-provincial tax collection agreement basis.

E-government filing portal

File incorporation under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia, SBC 2002, c. 57) or the Canada Business Corporations Act using the provincial/federal registry; the CRA will automatically issue a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and corporation income tax account (RC). If the account is not auto-created, register via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or deliver Form RC1 (Request for Business Number Registration). The GST/HST account (RT) must be opened once annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, at which point the issuer ceases to be a small supplier, or earlier where the issuer voluntarily registers to claim input tax credits. Open a payroll account (RP) before hiring Canadian employees and obtain an import/export account (RM) only if the issuer physically crosses goods; a pure fintech supply of services generally requires no RM account. Non-resident directors or shareholders authorizing tax representatives must file Form RC59 (Business Consent Form). The BN must appear on all invoices and CRA returns (T2, GST/HST return, T4/T4A summaries).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the BC-incorporated entity is automatically a taxable Canadian corporation resident in Canada (Income Tax Act, s. 89(1) definition) and cannot be treated as pass-through for federal or provincial income tax. The issuer is subject to Part I federal corporate income tax at the 15% general rate plus BC corporate income tax at the 12% general rate; for a Canadian-controlled private corporation with under CAD 500,000 of active business income, the combined small-business rate is 11% (9% federal plus 2% BC), subject to association rules. Canada taxes worldwide income of resident corporations; there is no territorial exemption and no check-the-box classification.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Canadian law. Statutory elections relevant to the virtual card issuer include: the s. 85 rollover on transfer of property and intangibles to the corporation on incorporation; the s. 256 association and control determinations affecting the CCPC small-business deduction; the GST/HST elections in Excise Tax Act s. 273 (closely related corporations and partnerships), s. 156 (nil-consideration supplies among registrants of a closely related group) and s. 167 (supply of a business as a going concern); and the hybrid mismatch deduction and non-inclusion neutralizing adjustments under Income Tax Act ss. 18.2 and 20.2, effective for amounts becoming payable or obligations incurred after September 30, 2022, with annual schedule filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of annual worldwide taxable supplies under the ETA s. 152 small supplier rule; a person making only exempt financial supplies has no mandatory GST/HST registration, but may voluntarily register under ETA s. 240 and, if a listed financial institution, may use the Special Attribution Method to claim input tax credits on the exempt-side inputs. BC PST requires separate registration with the BC Ministry of Finance for persons making taxable retail sales in BC despite the absence of a stated monetary PST threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% GST/HST where the supplier proves that the goods are exported from Canada within a reasonable time and retains the ETA Part IX s. 152 proof of export records. Services are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V where supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under the ETA and the service is performed in Canada for the non-resident, except services realistically connected to Canadian real property or goods; intangible personal property is zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada. For this B2B virtual card issuer, the majority of revenue streams (interchange, card program management, funding and interest fees, and settlement fees) will constitute financial services under ETA s. 123(1) and Schedule V Part VII; financial services supplied to non-residents are exempt rather than zero-rated, and exemption blocks general input tax credit claims unless the issuer is a listed financial institution applying the Special Attribution Method. Isolated zero-rated sales (for example, exported software-as-a-service functionality of the spend-management platform) must be documented separately by platform contract and user licence evidence.

  • CBSA export declaration / Canada Border Services Agency Form A8A (CAED) where goods are exported, not applicable to pure financial services
  • Commercial invoice or platform service agreement evidencing the non-resident recipient, consideration, and place of performance
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or courier proof of export for goods zero-rated under ETA s. 152
  • Evidence of the recipient's non-resident and non-registered status, such as foreign address corroboration and foreign PST or VAT registration certificate
  • Foreign inward remittance or FX settlement confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds within a reasonable period

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, no statutory API-based invoice validation and no digital stamping requirement. CRA requires electronic filing of all GST/HST returns for registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1,000,000 and for returns prepared by third-party tax preparers. Invoices must be issued for every taxable supply and retained in readable paper or electronic form for at least 6 years under ETA s. 286.1. The federal government supports Peppol-compatible e-invoicing in public procurement via CanadaBuys but imposes no private B2B e-invoicing obligation; any API or cloud invoicing solution must preserve an unalterable audit trail to sustain input tax credit claims.

The CRA, jointly with CBSA on customs proofs, audits zero-rating within the applicable reassessment windows set by ETA s. 298 and ITA s. 152(4), extended for neglect or fraud. Failure to substantiate the 0% rate causes a retroactive reassessment at 5% GST/HST, reinstatement of the net tax liability, clawback of input tax credits and refunds, and compounded arrears interest under ETA s. 279. Penalties include: ETA ss. 280 and 280.1 for failure to file and remit; ETA s. 285(1) false statement penalty of 25% of the net tax deficiency with a CAD 100 minimum; criminal prosecution under ETA s. 286 with fines up to CAD 25,000 on indictment and possible imprisonment; director and officer liability for unremitted GST under ETA s. 323; and income tax gross negligence penalties of 50% of avoided federal and BC tax under ITA s. 163(2), plus GAAR recharacterization under ITA s. 245. Absence of foreign currency repatriation evidence draws separate review under the foreign reporting and T1135 penalty regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account, with no general exchange-control regulations, no foreign-ownership screening for virtual card issuer capital, and no central bank pre-approval for cross-border capital flows. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders triggers Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (default 25% subject to treaty relief); to ensure that a distribution is treated as a dividend and not as a return of capital, the corporation must maintain accurate PUC (paid-up capital) schedules, file prescribed forms, and satisfy shareholder-level residency documentation. Foreign direct capital injections are routed through an authorized Canadian financial institution, with the receiving bank applying CDD and FINTRAC record-keeping thresholds for cross-border wires.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Canada. There is no statutory obligation to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds into Canadian dollars. Export receivables may be retained in foreign currency or repatriated as the corporation chooses. The only prudential obligations are FINTRAC cross-border transaction reporting for international electronic wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 and record-keeping requirements, together with Income Tax Act quasi-in-position reporting arising from inter-company foreign-currency receivables.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (under the Bank of Canada Act); foreign exchange administration is otherwise distributed among the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and, at the provincial level, the British Columbia Financial Services Authority for provicially-licensed and registration matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with strict constraints. A purely offshore e-money institution (EMI) cannot directly open a Canadian payment card programme without being registered as a money services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and without entering a BIN sponsorship arrangement with an OSFI-regulated Canadian bank that is a Mastercard or Visa network principal. Non-resident EMIs are generally treated as high-risk; they may be accepted only for funded-but-not-issued programmes. A separate BC Money Services Business registration, or an exemption order under the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), is required when the EMI seeks to establish a local physical presence or maintain custody of BC-client funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant cross-border routing uses SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV messages for CAD-denominated and foreign-currency injections respectively. A Canadian receiving bank applies a prescribed purpose code of 'S301' for capital investment (equity injection) or 'S302' for inter-company operating capital, and must obtain a completed purpose-of-payment confirmation, US/Canada tax self-certification (W-8/W-9 equivalents), and evidence of the ultimate funding entity identity. Injections above CAD 10,000 are automatically reported to FINTRAC, and those above CAD 100,000 are subject to enhanced due-diligence reviews. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined effective headline: 15% federal general corporate rate (38% statutory base less 10% provincial abatement less 13% General Rate Reduction) plus 12% British Columbia provincial general corporate rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (s. 212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

T2 filing due 6 months after the fiscal year-end; final tax balance due 2 months after the fiscal year-end (extended to 3 months for CCPCs with SBD entitlement); corporations with prior-year federal plus provincial tax exceeding C$3,000 must pay quarterly instalments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000: gross revenue per year is the threshold below which the s. 247(3) transfer pricing documentation penalty is not applied; above this threshold, contemporaneous complete and accurate documentation (Local File and Master File harmonized with CRA Transfer Pricing Memorandum TPM-09 and the six statutory categories of s. 247(4)) is mandatory. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of C$750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) codification for BC corporations, but substance is rigorously enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s. 245), foreign affiliate rules and Foreign Accrual Property Income (FAPI) provisions (s. 91-95), the beneficial ownership doctrine, the Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test, and CRA factual audit practice. A virtual card issuer must maintain real commercial substance in British Columbia: an operating office, resident employees, active board meetings, substantive risk management, treasury and AML compliance functions, and decision-making data indicating that card-issuing and spend-management contracts are genuinely managed in Canada. Substance alone cannot justify artificial re-routing of income; the CRA may disregard a shell arrangement, reallocate income to the economic owner, deny treaty relief, and impose gross negligence penalties where the company exists predominantly to exploit tax asymmetries.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code s. 380(1) fraud: imprisonment up to 14 years; Income Tax Act s. 239(1): fine of 100% to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment, or 50% to 200% plus up to 2 years on summary conviction; independent civil gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) equal to 50% of understated tax, minimum C$100.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized, participated in, or wilfully closed their eyes to the evasion are jointly exposed to criminal prosecution, restitution orders, asset freezes, travel restrictions pending trial, and disqualification under the Canada Business Corporations Act and the BC Business Corporations Act; the corporate veil will be pierced where the company is found to be a sham.
Gross Negligence and Misrepresentations

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 163(2): penalty equal to the greater of C$100 and 50% of the understated tax or overstated credits; compounded by daily compound arrears interest at the prescribed rate and a 10% repeated-failure penalty for persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unremitted source deductions under s. 227.1 and, where the gross negligence is egregious, criminally charged for counselling or procuring the misstatement.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalization Misstatements

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(2) adjustment reallocating income and disallowing excessive payments; s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (minimum C$5,000); debt push-down restrictions under the EIFEL rule in s. 18.2 limiting net interest expense to 30% of tax EBITDA; disallowance of deductions for withheld foreign reporting on Form T106.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length pricing may be assessed personally for the penalty where the corporation is insolvent, and may face criminal liability if the mispricing is part of a scheme to evade Canadian tax.
Shell Company, Abusive Treaty Shopping and FAPI

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s. 245 denying the tax benefit; application of the Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test to deny treaty relief; recharacterization of foreign passive income under FAPI (s. 91-95); Part XIII reassessments of deemed dividends; regulatory revocation of the payment services licence by the BC regulator or the federal Retail Payment Activities Act registration.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham or nominee-board shell can be held personally liable as de facto principals for unpaid corporate taxes, prosecuted for conspiracy to commit tax fraud, and made subject to director bans and travel controls pending resolution of criminal proceedings.
AML and FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative Monetary Penalties up to C$2,000,000 per contravention for entities and C$500,000 for individuals under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; criminal liability for laundering proceeds of crime under s. 462.31 of the Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 10 years; FINTRAC compliance agreements and licence revocation; BC-specific AML directives following the Cullen Commission reforms.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who directed, authorized, acquiesced in, or failed to correct the compliance failure are personally liable for the penalties, and can be criminally prosecuted for laundering, subjected to court-ordered travel bans, and barred from acting as directors of any federally or provincially regulated entity.
Director Liability for Unremitted Source Deductions and GST/HST

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227.1, Excise Tax Act s. 323 and corresponding CPP/EI provisions make each director jointly and severally or solidarily liable for up to 100% of the unremitted amounts; CRA may issue a director's liability assessment without first requiring a fraud finding; enforcement includes garnishment, liens and collection proceedings.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced as a matter of statute: directors must demonstrate they exercised reasonable care, diligence and skill to prevent the failure; criminal charges may additionally expose directors to imprisonment for up to 5 years and travel restrictions while out on bail.
