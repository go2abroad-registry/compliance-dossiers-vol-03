Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal tax administration and GST/HST registration via CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). British Columbia PST registration is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account before making taxable supplies in Canada. The standard procedure is to file Form RC1 with the CRA or electronically through My Business Account, providing the partnership legal name, registered home jurisdiction, Canadian or agent address, principal business activity (NAICS 459140), names and addresses of general partners, expected quarterly taxable supplies, and, where applicable, Form RC71 appointing a Canadian representative. A foreign LP that fails to register when required may be assessed uncollected net tax plus interest and penalties. Voluntary GST/HST registration is available even below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold when the LP carries on a commercial activity in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime. For federal income tax, a limited partnership is treated as fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act section 96 and is not an entity-level taxpayer; income and losses are allocated to partners. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is itself a registrant person if its taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold. No hybrid mismatch election exists; hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically where treaty or foreign classification differences are exploited.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; BC PST registration is generally required for a seller with physical or economic presence making taxable sales in BC and has no equivalent small-supplier exemption for retail activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate sales of musical instruments and audio equipment where the goods are exported as part of a sale to a non-resident address outside Canada. The essential conditions are: (a) the registrant makes a taxable sale of tangible personal property; (b) delivery is made by the registrant by mail, common carrier, or courier to an address outside Canada, or the goods are delivered to a non-resident person who immediately exports them in substantially the same state in continuous transboundary shipment; and (c) the registrant retains contemporaneous documentary proof of export. BC PST does not apply when physical delivery occurs outside British Columbia. For Stripe or PayPal online sales, the registrant should obtain shipping destination, courier airway bill, and card-processor settlement evidence to support the zero-rated treatment.

  • Commercial invoice showing item description, HS classification, quantities, unit prices, and sale terms
  • Customs export declaration or Electronic Export Report where required under CBSA export reporting obligations
  • Bill of lading or courier airway bill with foreign destination and shipper/receiver data
  • Delivery confirmation and carrier tracking record demonstrating departure from Canada and foreign delivery
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent bank settlement evidence, including Stripe/PayPal payout history

Canada does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal digital stamping, or government clearance of invoices. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE and payments are made through CRA My Payment. E-invoices and electronic records are acceptable for substantiation if they meet CRA record-retention requirements. BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC, but there is no transaction-level API clearance or digital stamping regime.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess uncollected GST on the full selling price, plus arrears interest and penalties, where the registrant cannot produce prescribed evidence of export or where the goods remain in Canada for use or consumption. False or grossly negligent export claims give rise to civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including percentage-based misstatement penalties, and serious cases may be prosecuted for tax evasion with fines and imprisonment. An unsupported export zero-rating claim is treated as an unreported tax liability, allowing CRA to claw back input tax credits and apply gross-negligence penalties. Although Canadian law imposes no express outward-remittance deadline, CRA examines whether export sales were actually settled, and a 180-day settlement deadline is conservatively applied as an audit control for the online-retail model.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully free-floating currency with no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Canada permits unrestricted conversion of CAD into foreign currency, unrestricted repatriation of capital, profits, dividends, interest, and limited-partner distributions to non-residents. Non-resident partners of a Canadian-resident limited partnership are subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (default 25%, typically reduced to 5%-15% under applicable tax treaties, including the Canada-U.S. Treaty) on distributions and on the share of Canadian-source income allocated to non-resident partners. The LP's passive retail e-commerce activity also engages the Investment Canada Act only at a notification level: establishing a new Canadian business requires a simple post-closing notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under s.12 of the ICA, because the enterprise value falls far below the current WTO-investor review threshold (CAD 2.082 billion in 2024). No exchange-control approval, quota, or administrative license is required for inbound or outbound transfers, and no limitations on foreign ownership of an LP or of the underlying musical-instrument retail business apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Sales proceeds received in USD or other foreign currencies may be retained indefinitely in interest-bearing foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks, or converted to CAD at the bank's prevailing rate without CRA or Bank of Canada interference. For CRA income-tax measurement, the LP must report Canadian-dollar equivalent amounts using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or a consistent published rate under s.2611 of the Income Tax Regulations; functional-currency reporting is available but requires a CRA election. Reporting obligations are limited to (a) FINTRAC Currency Transaction Reports for cash disbursements/receipts over CAD 10,000, and (b) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for any EFT (wire or payment-card settlement) exceeding CAD 10,000 in a 24-hour sequence, regardless of whether the funds are retained in USD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority, responsible for issuing the Canadian dollar and for payments-system oversight of Lynx and the Retail Payment Activities Act registration, while the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises all Schedule I banks. The Department of Finance Canada administers the Currency Act and the Investment Canada Act, and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, including cross-border electronic fund transfer reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary cross-border payouts, but legally constrained as a primary treasury rail. Non-Canadian EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury U.S., Payoneer) cannot act as a Canadian clearing bank for the LP because (a) the Bank Act prohibits carrying on retail deposit-taking business in Canada without a federal bank license; (b) the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021, c.23) and Retail Payment Activities Regulations require any payment service provider (PSP) operating CAD-denominated payment functions for a Canadian entity to be registered with the Bank of Canada, with a registration deadline of September 8, 2025 (and commercial-compliance obligations including safeguarding of funds, operational risk, and incident reporting); (c) such EMIs settle CAD obligations through an underlying Canadian partner bank, meaning the LP cannot obtain a legally independent CAD account with a direct Payments Canada clearing route; and (d) EMIs do not provide commercial letters of credit or trade finance. Accordingly, the LP should use EMIs solely for small-value foreign-supplier payouts (< CAD 1,000 per payment), while relying on a Schedule I bank for CAD-denominated merchant settlement, FX, and trade finance. Stripe and PayPal remain the designated merchant collection rails for retail e-commerce sales, but their CAD settlement must be directed to the LP's Schedule I operational account, not to an offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol for equity capital injection from non-resident partners: (1) Instruct the remitting bank to execute a SWIFT MT103 (wire transfer) from the foreign partner's account to the LP's CAD-denominated operational account at the Vancouver branch of the chosen Schedule I bank, with the 70 field remittance narrative reading 'Capital contribution to a British Columbia limited partnership for working capital and inventory acquisition', referencing the BC Registry LP filing number and CRA Business Number. (2) Canada does not require an inward remittance permit, purpose-code, or foreign-ownership license; however, the remitting jurisdiction's exchange controls (e.g., SAFE or RBI) apply at the sending end, and the Canadian bank will verify compliance through the UBO and source-of-funds check. (3) For amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, the receiving bank is required to file a FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report, attaching the remitter's legal name, account number, and address from the MT103; the LP should pre-notify the relationship manager of the incoming wire to avoid a 24-hour hold. (4) If the capital is routed via a law firm's trust account, the lawyer's undertaking letter and an executed trust-transfer instruction are required, and the bank may open a temporary 'Capital Escrow Account' to hold funds until the completion of verification, after which funds are released same-day to the operating account. (5) All CAD conversions of foreign-currency capital must be booked using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate; no CAD conversion is mandatory. (6) Capital contributions from U.S. partners to a U.S.-dollar operating sub-account may be cleared through Canada-U.S. cross-border banking facilities (TD's or RBC's cross-border banking) without any FINTRAC export report, since the funds remain in the same beneficial-owner account chain and are reportable only when settled into the Canadian clearing system.Clearing rails for trade disbursements: (1) Domestic CAD supplier payments: route via Payments Canada's Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for EFT under CAD 10 million, or via Lynx (the Bank of Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system) for larger payments; a simple bank transfer referencing the supplier invoice number and LP purchase order satisfies record-keeping. (2) Foreign supplier payments under USD 50,000-equivalent: issue an SWIFT MT103 serial or cover payment with full 72A/72B remittance instructions (invoice number, order number, supplier name and account); settlement typically completes in 2-3 business days via the Schedule I bank's correspondent network. (3) Foreign supplier payments above USD 50,000-equivalent or with documentary-risk triggers: issue a commercial letter of credit via an MT700 from the Vancouver branch, available by sight payment or deferred payment at the counters of the advising bank (commonly the supplier's bank in China, Korea, or the U.S.); require the beneficiary to present the Commercial Invoice, 3/3 Clean on Board Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, Packing List, Certificate of Origin, and Insurance Certificate; payment is released against document compliance, not against goods inspection. (4) E-commerce merchant settlement rails: Stripe and PayPal clear customer card/NFC payments to the LP's Schedule I CAD operational account; Stripe Canada settles in CAD via ACSS EFT (T+2 business days), PayPal Canada settles via EFT to the linked bank account (T+3), and both platforms maintain a 'currency balance' mechanism permitting USD retention or conversion at the platform's own rate; for accuracy, the LP must record platform settlement conversions separately from bank FX conversions. (5) Small-value supplier payouts (< CAD 1,000): permissible via Wise Business or other registered PSP, but the PSP account must be fully disclosed to the Schedule I bank, and the periodic top-up from the CAD operating account must carry a transfer narrative of 'trade payable disbursement' to satisfy FINTRAC's PCP and EFT reporting requirements.Deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) for firm quarterly sales conversion, tenor up to 12 months, entered into with the Schedule I bank's treasury desk under a bank Master Forward Agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) on non-deliverable currencies (principally CNY and KRW) only for hedging USD-based indirect exposure to Chinese and Korean supplier costs, executed under ISDA 2002 with a Schedule I or Schedule II bank counterparty.FX options and zero-cost collars (CAD/USD) covering forecasted online sales revenue for up to 12 months, with premium paid in CAD, where the underlying exposure is the projected USD-denominated payout stream from Stripe and PayPal.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CAD vs USD, notional up to USD 2,000,000) to economically convert USD-denominated inventory financing or intercompany payables into CAD-hedged cost, subject to OSFI's non-cleared derivatives margin rules (exempt for this end-user LP given total aggregate notional well below the CAD 12-billion margin exemption threshold).Rolling forward (window-forward) contracts offered through the bank's e-commerce cash-management facility enabling daily USD conversion of platform settlements at a fixed forward rate over a one-month or two-month window period.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary cross-border payouts, but legally constrained as a primary treasury rail. Non-Canadian EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury U.S., Payoneer) cannot act as a Canadian clearing bank for the LP because (a) the Bank Act prohibits carrying on retail deposit-taking business in Canada without a federal bank license; (b) the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021, c.23) and Retail Payment Activities Regulations require any payment service provider (PSP) operating CAD-denominated payment functions for a Canadian entity to be registered with the Bank of Canada, with a registration deadline of September 8, 2025 (and commercial-compliance obligations including safeguarding of funds, operational risk, and incident reporting); (c) such EMIs settle CAD obligations through an underlying Canadian partner bank, meaning the LP cannot obtain a legally independent CAD account with a direct Payments Canada clearing route; and (d) EMIs do not provide commercial letters of credit or trade finance. Accordingly, the LP should use EMIs solely for small-value foreign-supplier payouts (< CAD 1,000 per payment), while relying on a Schedule I bank for CAD-denominated merchant settlement, FX, and trade finance. Stripe and PayPal remain the designated merchant collection rails for retail e-commerce sales, but their CAD settlement must be directed to the LP's Schedule I operational account, not to an offshore EMI.
