Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration via Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html); British Columbia Ministry of Finance — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administration via BC eTax (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/consumption-taxes/pst)

E-government filing portal

The foreign limited partnership must obtain a Business Number (BN) from CRA by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the applicable program accounts for GST/HST, payroll, and corporate income tax where required. The LP must appoint a Canadian representative or authorized agent with a Canadian address; if no physical presence exists, the non-resident partnership and each partner must provide supporting information to establish Canadian nexus. For BC PST, the partnership must register online through the BC eTax portal using the same BN plus a PST-specific program account; registration must occur before the first taxable sale is made in British Columbia and before a GST/HST return is due. Direct banking, payment-rail processors (Stripe or PayPal) do not satisfy statutory registration requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a bipartite 'check-the-box' entity classification system. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act: income, losses, and GST/HST net tax balances are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners. The partnership may execute a fiscal-period election under ITA subsection 96(10) where all partners have non-calendar taxation years, and may choose to have the partnership's GST/HST net tax remittance performed by a designated representative. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA subsections 18.4 and 18.8 restrict deduction-double-non-taxation outcomes and may be mitigated by statutory elections available to Canadian-resident partners in cross-border structures; no proprietary CRA election exists to treat the LP as a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: national small supplier threshold of CAD 30,000 in cumulative worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) over four consecutive calendar quarters before mandatory registration; BC PST: registration is required for every vendor carrying on business in British Columbia, including non-resident remote sellers who solicit sales in BC, with no definitive statutory de minimis threshold under the Provincial Sales Tax Act; PST registration typically applies from the first taxable sale into the province unless a specific marketplace-facilitator exception applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of GST/HST is available for supplies of goods made to a non-resident purchaser only where the supplier satisfies CRA that the goods were actually delivered outside Canada. The POD custom apparel must be exported as commercial goods, with title passing outside Canada. The supplier must retain evidence showing the physical export and the foreign destination; maritime, air, or highway shipments are covered if a commercial export declaration was filed and the carrier's documentation shows delivery to a foreign port. Services performed on the goods before export are not zero-rated merely because the goods are destined abroad. Where the goods are sold to a Canadian customer and subsequently exported, the supply is taxable unless an express zero-rated export supply arises.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency export regime, including the Commercial Export Reporting System or successor portal)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the foreign consignee and non-Canadian destination
  • Commercial Invoice with export terms and complete description of the custom apparel
  • Carrier's certificate or third-party logistics confirmation of crossing at a Canadian port of exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, foreign exchange conversion record, or other banking proof demonstrating receipt of export proceeds from a foreign counterparty
  • Sales order and shipping manifest evidencing the POD customization and the direct shipment abroad

Canada does not mandate real-time electronic fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or pre-clearance invoicing through a central government API. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically in most cases using CRA's NETFILE-mode GST/HST electronic filing or an approved software interface; BC PST returns are filed through BC eTax. B2B e-invoicing is permitted on a voluntary basis under the PEPPOL framework, but no fiscal authority clearance is required before issuing an invoice or documenting a sale.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims by comparing customs declarations, payment flow, bank remittance documentation, and reported revenue. If the export evidence is deficient or the goods are later re-imported to Canada without an approved temporary-export regime, CRA will reassess the supply as taxable GST/HST, require the partnership to remit the uncollected tax, and charge interest from the original return due date. Gross negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act (ETA section 285) can reach 50% of the net tax understated, and false-statement penalties may be imposed under ETA section 281.1 up to CAD 25,000, plus criminal prosecution and a fine of up to CAD 200,000 for a first offence involving fraudulent zero-rating under the Competition? In practice, BC PST audit adjustments for improperly zero-rated sales or unreported interprovincial removals attract interest and penalty under the Provincial Sales Sales Tax Act; retrospective clawbacks can cover a multi-year period and bar mitigation after a voluntary-disclosure application becomes stale. For any clawback of the GST/HST input tax credits originally claimed on export inputs, the LP is jointly and severally liable for the tax, and payments routed through Stripe/PayPal must be traceable to the foreign buyer within the 180-day repatriation window to withstand audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility with a free-floating Canadian dollar. Canada imposes no exchange controls, no central-bank prior-approval requirement for inbound or outbound capital, and no restriction on profit remittance, LP income allocations, or disposition proceeds payable to non-resident partners. Remittance hurdles are tax-driven, not FX-driven: (1) Part XIII withholding at the statutory 25% rate (treaty-reduced for US-resident partners under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention) applies to passive amounts paid or credited to non-residents, including interest, royalties, and management fees; (2) non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP remain taxable under Part I on allocated Canadian-source business income, subject to treaty permanent-establishment relief; (3) CRA reporting on Form T106 (transactions with non-residents) and Form T5013 (partnership allocations) is mandatory; and (4) a standard notification filing under the Investment Canada Act is required for the new Canadian business, though no pre-merger review applies below the WTO review threshold (approximately CAD 1.3 billion for 2024).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation exists in Canadian law: there is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts onshore or offshore indefinitely and may convert at market rates through any Canadian Schedule I bank. Residual compliance anchors: FINTRAC automatic reporting of cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more under the PCMLTFA; CBSA export declarations filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods leaving Canada; Canadian-resident partners' Form T1135 filing where aggregate specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000; and books maintained in CAD unless an ITA s. 261 functional-currency election is made.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); the Department of Finance manages the Exchange Fund Account under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52); OSFI supervises federally chartered banks; Payments Canada operates the retail (ACSS) and RTGS (Lynx) clearing rails; FINTRAC enforces AML/CFT obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: High as an ancillary payment rail; Medium as a primary treasury vehicle. Canada does not license offshore EMIs; fintech providers (Wise, Payoneer, Stripe, PayPal) operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or Foreign MSBs and must register with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. The Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23) superimposes a Bank of Canada registration and supervision regime for payment service providers (regulations in force 2024). These PSPs cannot accept deposits or create deposit liabilities, which remain exclusive to federally chartered banks under the Bank Act, so CAD settlement must ultimately terminate in a Schedule I bank account. Recommended architecture: Schedule I bank as treasury hub; one registered PSP for card acquiring and marketplace settlement; and an FMSB for low-cost cross-currency sweeps.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions are executed by SWIFT MT103 over the beneficiary bank's Lynx (RTGS) or Fedwire rails; reference the LP's 3-digit institution number, 5-digit transit number, 7-to-12-digit account number, and a purpose narration such as 'Capital contribution - limited partner to BC Limited Partnership (ISIC 4791 retail)'.
