Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and CRA My Business Account. British Columbia provincial indirect taxes are administered by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Programs Branch, through its eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A British Columbia limited partnership carrying on business in Canada should obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and register the applicable CRA program accounts. The partnership or its authorized representative generally submits the registration through CRA Business Registration Online, by telephone where permitted, or by the prescribed paper application. The CRA may require the partnership agreement, British Columbia registration or certificate of limited partnership, legal name, business address, fiscal period, responsible partners, ownership information, and authorization evidence. A GST/HST program account is opened as RT0001; a payroll account, if applicable, is opened as RP0001; and a T5013 partnership information-return account may be assigned as applicable. A non-resident partner or foreign person does not receive a Canadian individual or corporate TIN merely by investing, but may require a CRA account or Individual Tax Number where required for Canadian tax filings, withholding, treaty claims, or reporting. The partnership must register for GST/HST when required or may register voluntarily while carrying on commercial activities. Separate registration with the British Columbia Ministry of Finance is required for provincial PST where the partnership sells or leases taxable goods, software, or taxable services in British Columbia, unless an exemption applies. Importers may also require a Canada Border Services Agency import-export RM account and a valid BN9 for customs purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a British Columbia limited partnership. A partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes: income or loss is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners under the partnership agreement and applicable Canadian tax rules, while partners are generally taxed on their allocated shares. A Canadian limited partnership does not ordinarily pay Canadian income tax as an entity, although withholding, information-reporting, payroll, GST/HST, customs, and other liabilities may apply at the partnership level. Partnership classification is determined under Canadian law and relevant foreign-law characterization principles rather than by an elective federal entity-classification form. Canadian anti-hybrid mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences for certain foreign hybrid arrangements, but they do not provide a general elective hybrid classification regime. A partnership can make specific statutory elections where available, including certain GST/HST elections, tax-deferred rollover elections, accounting-method elections, and foreign-affiliate or transfer-pricing elections where their statutory conditions are met; these are transaction-specific and do not convert the partnership into a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Federal GST registration generally becomes mandatory when taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. The threshold generally applies to the person and associated persons, subject to the Excise Tax Act rules. Voluntary GST registration is permitted for eligible persons carrying on commercial activities. British Columbia PST has separate registration rules and no universal GST-style threshold for all vendors; a small-seller exemption may be available where the prescribed conditions are satisfied, including generally gross revenue from retail sales of taxable goods, software, and taxable services of CAD 10,000 or less in the relevant circumstances. Businesses selling taxable goods in the ordinary course, leasing taxable goods, or providing taxable software or services in British Columbia must independently test the PST registration requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada to a destination outside Canada are generally zero-rated for GST purposes where the supplier delivers or ships the goods outside Canada and satisfies the documentary and delivery conditions under the Excise Tax Act and its regulations. Goods supplied in Canada and subsequently exported by the purchaser may qualify for zero-rating only where the specific statutory conditions are met, including prescribed purchaser status, export timing, destination, and documentary evidence. Goods sold for export to a non-resident are not automatically zero-rated merely because the purchaser is non-resident. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated where the service is supplied to a person who is not resident in Canada and the statutory conditions are satisfied; important exclusions apply, including services in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, certain personal services performed in Canada, and other specifically excluded services. Services performed in Canada for non-residents must therefore be analyzed separately. British Columbia PST generally does not apply to qualifying goods shipped directly out of British Columbia for use outside the province, but the vendor must retain evidence of delivery, destination, purchaser status, and the applicable exemption. Exporters generally recover GST through input tax credits rather than a broad standalone export VAT refund. Non-resident businesses may seek GST/HST registration and claim eligible input tax credits if they meet the statutory registration and commercial-activity requirements.

  • Canada Border Services Agency export declaration or applicable Canadian export reporting record, including any required Canadian Export Reporting System filing or exemption evidence
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, purchaser, goods, value, currency, terms of sale, and destination
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or carrier delivery record
  • Evidence that the goods were delivered or made available outside Canada, including carrier proof of delivery, tracking record, or foreign import/customs documentation where available
  • Purchase order, sales contract, customer correspondence, and payment records linking the exported goods or services to the zero-rated supply
  • GST/HST invoice and the supplier's CRA Business Number and GST/HST registration number where applicable
  • For services, contract, statement of work, customer residency evidence, service-delivery records, and evidence supporting the statutory zero-rating category
  • Foreign import entry, destination-country customs record, or equivalent third-party evidence where reasonably available; an inward remittance certificate is not a standard Canadian statutory requirement
  • For British Columbia PST treatment, exemption certificate or out-of-province delivery evidence where required by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance

Canada and British Columbia do not currently impose a general real-time electronic invoicing, continuous transaction controls, or mandatory pre-clearance regime for ordinary domestic B2B sales by a British Columbia limited partnership. Invoices must contain sufficient information to substantiate GST/HST charging and input tax credits, including supplier identity, BN or GST/HST registration number where required, date, description, consideration, tax rate, and tax amount. Electronic invoices are generally acceptable if they preserve integrity, reliability, accessibility, and auditability. CRA electronic-filing obligations apply to specified returns and information filings, including GST/HST returns and applicable T5013 partnership returns. Customs declarations and commercial import/export data are filed electronically through the Canada Border Services Agency systems where required. British Columbia PST records and returns must be maintained and filed in accordance with Ministry of Finance requirements, commonly through eTaxBC. Digital invoicing does not replace customs evidence, export records, or tax-record retention obligations.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and does not generally require an inward remittance certificate to substantiate GST zero-rating. CRA and the British Columbia Ministry of Finance may audit whether the supply actually met the export or out-of-province conditions and whether records establish delivery, destination, purchaser status, and the nature of the supply. If zero-rating is unsupported, GST or PST may be assessed retrospectively, together with applicable interest, penalties, and denial or recovery of related input tax credits. A person that knew or ought to have known that a zero-rating claim was false may face gross-negligence penalties, generally up to 50% of the understated tax or overstated refund, in addition to tax and interest. False statements, omissions, obstruction, or fraudulent conduct may trigger civil penalties and, in serious cases, prosecution under the Excise Tax Act, Income Tax Act, or applicable provincial legislation, including fines and possible imprisonment. Incorrect customs export reporting can result in administrative monetary penalties, reassessment, seizure or forfeiture consequences, and other enforcement measures under the Customs Act and the applicable export-control framework. Records supporting GST/HST and PST treatment should generally be retained for at least six years after the end of the relevant year, subject to statutory extensions, audit notices, or longer periods required for unresolved matters.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free currency regime with unrestricted current account convertibility. Capital account transactions require FINTRAC declaration for values exceeding CAD 10,000. Profit remittances to non-resident partners are permitted following tax withholding and partnership tax filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender requirement or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Receipts may be retained or freely converted. Transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC with supporting documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
