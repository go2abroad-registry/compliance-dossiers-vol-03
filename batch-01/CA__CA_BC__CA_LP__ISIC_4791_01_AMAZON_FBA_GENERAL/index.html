Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a British Columbia limited partnership. Capital contributions are established in the limited partnership agreement, and no escrow or deposit account is mandated at registration.

Funding & unlock path: If commercial premises are leased, obtain an occupancy permit or fire safety inspection under the BC Fire Code and maintain fire suppression, emergency egress, and panic hardware. Register with WorkSafeBC before engaging workers. Virtual and third-party-logistics FBA operations require no additional physical clearance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a British Columbia limited partnership. Capital contributions are established in the limited partnership agreement, and no escrow or deposit account is mandated at registration.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED by the General Partner of the Limited Partnership: (1) the Statement of Registration filed under the Partnership Act (British Columbia) is approved, and the General Partner is authorized to complete and submit the same; (2) the British Columbia registered office identified in the Statement of Registration is adopted as the continuing office for service of process; (3) the General Partner is authorized to execute the limited partnership agreement and to accept capital subscriptions from limited partners; (4) such officers, managers, or agents as the General Partner designates are appointed to open banking and payment accounts and to conduct Amazon FBA and e-commerce operations; and (5) these resolutions take effect upon issuance of the Certificate of Registration.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board is applicable to pure e-commerce marketplace resale in British Columbia. Occupational safety oversight is vested in WorkSafeBC and local fire authorities where premises are occupied.

Category: No manufacturing or physical retail operating permit is required for ISIC 4791 retail sale via internet and Amazon FBA general operations. A municipal business licence may be required by the local government where the partnership maintains a physical office, warehouse, or inventory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal income tax, GST/HST and payroll administration; national portals: My Business Account (MyBA), Business Registration Online (BRO), and GST/HST NETFILE for mandatory electronic filing; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — export reporting via the Canadian Export Reporting System (ERS) and the CARM single-window platform; British Columbia Ministry of Finance — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and Employer Health Tax administered through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Limited Partnership extraprovincially with the BC Registry (Corporate Online) to establish legal existence in the jurisdiction. Step 2: Apply to CRA for a 15-character Business Number (BN) with program accounts using Form RC1 or BRO; the LP itself (not the individual partners) must open an RT (GST/HST) program account because the Excise Tax Act treats a partnership as a distinct person for GST/HST, even though the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes. Step 3: Open an RP payroll account if BC employees are engaged, and register for BC Employer Health Tax (triggered where annual BC remuneration exceeds CAD 500,000) and WorkSafeBC coverage. Step 4: Open an RM import/export account with CBSA if the LP will self-clear cross-border shipments. Non-resident partners do not obtain Canadian TINs; they report their allocated Canadian-source income under their home-country TINs and file, where required, T5013/T1/T2 returns with CRA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Canadian Limited Partnership is a full flow-through conduit under ITA s. 96; no entity-level income tax; each partner is taxable on its agreed share of Canadian-source income. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxed in Canada only on Canadian-source income allocated by the LP, subject to applicable tax treaty protection. The LP remains a separate registrable person for GST/HST under ETA s. 2(1). FBA inventory stored in Canadian fulfilment centres may create a fixed-place-of-business permanent establishment risk for non-resident partners under certain tax treaties (OECD 2017 commentary); PE mitigation requires analysis of the Amazon FBA Seller Agreement and the partner's broader Canadian activity.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; entity classification is statutory. Available elections: (i) ITA s. 96(3) joint partnership/partner election for alternative income or loss allocation; (ii) ITA s. 249.1(4) non-calendar fiscal-period election; (iii) ETA s. 273 election between a registrant and a closely related corporation or partner to treat intercompany taxable supplies as made for nil consideration, eliminating GST/HST on internal recharges; (iv) ETA s. 211.2 fulfilment-seller exception under which a non-resident seller that is itself a GST/HST registrant may displace the online platform's deemed-supplier status, subject to the platform operator's due diligence; (v) simplified non-resident GST/HST registration regime for remote e-commerce suppliers with no Canadian residency or permanent establishment requirement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small-supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in Canada in a single calendar quarter or across the four preceding calendar quarters. Non-resident remote sellers operating through their own channels must register once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded; goods stored in Canadian fulfilment centres and sold through a deemed-supplier platform are taxed at the platform level with no registration threshold for the underlying seller. BC PST: out-of-province sellers engaged in business in BC must register with the BC Ministry of Finance, with a de minimis remote-seller relief where BC taxable sales are CAD 10,000 or less in the preceding 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Sch. VI Part V s. 1 to the supply of goods exported by the supplier with proof of export, and under Part V s. 7 to services supplied to non-residents and performed for use exclusively outside Canada. For Amazon FBA, sales delivered to Canadian addresses attract 5% GST (plus 7% BC PST or the applicable provincial HST); sales of goods actually exported from Canada to foreign delivery addresses can be zero-rated only where the LP (or Amazon acting on its behalf) holds contemporaneous documentary proof of export. Transfers of the LP's inventory into Amazon's Canadian fulfilment centres are non-supply inventory movements and are not zero-rated.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A/CAED) filed with CBSA through the Export Reporting System where shipment value exceeds CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled or regulated
  • Commercial invoice (Amazon Seller Central output) showing FOB origin, HS tariff codes, unit values in CAD and purchaser details
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) in the shipper's name, or courier delivery manifest for DHL/FedEx/UPS parcel movements
  • Customs broker export and import release records (PARS/ERS acknowledgements) for goods transiting Canadian ports and border crossings
  • Foreign exchange receipt evidence: SWIFT MT103 credit advices, Airwallex/Stripe settlement statements, or bank inward remittance certificates evidencing repatriation of export proceeds in CAD
  • Marketplace order-log extracts demonstrating the non-resident status and foreign delivery address of each end consumer

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or API-based e-invoicing mandate currently exists in Canada or BC. Registrants must issue a proper GST/HST tax invoice containing prescribed particulars within 15 days after the end of the month of the supply; electronic records are accepted under ETA s. 286 provided they are legible, complete and retained in a CRA-approved format. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via NETFILE once annual gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Canada is voluntarily adopting the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service (PEPPOL network), but there is no legislative clearance API. Amazon Seller Central and the Amazon Marketplace Tax Report function as the transactional e-invoice and sales-data backbone for audit substantiation.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims using Amazon marketplace order data, customs transit records and banking remittance documentation; missing export proof triggers reassessment of uncollected 5% GST/HST and BC PST, plus daily compound interest at the prescribed rate and a 5% default penalty escalating by 1% per month (maximum 12 months). False statements and gross negligence attract penalties up to 50% of the tax avoided, with criminal prosecution available under ITA s. 239 and ETA s. 328 carrying fines up to 200% of the tax and imprisonment. The normal four-year reassessment window extends to seven years by waiver and up to ten years for misrepresentation or fraud. All export substantiation records must be retained for at least six years from the date of the supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible with no exchange controls. Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement since 1951, guaranteeing unrestricted current account and capital account convertibility. The Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act impose no conversion limitations. Profit remittance (dividends, partnership distributions) is free of foreign exchange restriction; however, non-resident distributions may attract Part XIII withholding tax (15% under Canada-US tax treaty reduced rate; otherwise 25%) and require Form NR4 filing by the partnership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio exists in Canada. Under the repealed Foreign Exchange Control Act (1951), all surrender requirements were eliminated. Exporters may freely hold foreign currency in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency accounts. Cross-border electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with legal constraints. Canada has no EU-style EMI licensing regime. Airwallex, Wise, and Payoneer operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, not as banks. They may hold funds but cannot create deposit liabilities or issue credit cards domestically without OSFI licensing under the Bank Act. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% federal combined general corporate income tax rate, plus British Columbia provincial rate of 12% for a combined top statutory rate of 39% (2024). For active business income eligible for the federal small business deduction, the combined federal-provincial rate is approximately 11% (federal 9% plus BC 2%) up to the BC small business limit of C$500,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Not directly applicable at the LP level because Canadian limited partnerships are tax transparent; distributions are generally treated as return of capital or income allocations. Part XIII withholding tax of 25% applies to amounts paid or credited to non-resident partners as income from Canadian-source partnership business or services, unless treaty reduced. For a Canadian corporation receiving dividends from a taxable Canadian corporation, the statutory rate is 25%, but that is outside the LP structure.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The partnership information return (T5013) must be filed no later than the day that is five months after the partnership's fiscal year-end. A nil or late return may extend assessment; installment obligations may apply to partners directly.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian transfer pricing documentation is required when the aggregate amount of transactions with non-resident non-arm's-length persons exceeds C$1 million for the taxation year. Since the LP is a partnership, the threshold applies at the partnership level. Contemporaneous documentation must be prepared by the partnership's filing due date. No separate Country-by-Country reporting obligation exists at the LP level unless consolidated group revenue exceeds C$750 million; such obligation rests with an ultimate parent that is a Canadian reporting entity.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), statutory anti-avoidance rules for partnerships, and the economic substance / business purpose doctrine under Canadian common law can deny tax benefits from transactions lacking genuine commercial substance. The LP must demonstrate real business activity, decision-making in Canada or through treaty-compliant management, and arm's length substance to avoid recharacterization by the CRA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Canadian Income Tax Act, tax evasion is an indictable offense subject to fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Civil penalties include gross negligence penalties of 50% of understated tax plus interest and reinstated assessments without limitation periods.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of the LP may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and withholding taxes; directors of a corporate general partner can face personal liability for unpaid corporate taxes under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act. Criminal culpability can result in personal fines, restitution orders, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to five years.
Shell Company / Sham

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny tax benefits, recharacterize revenues, and impose reassessments with interest and penalties. A sham transaction can result in full reassessment of income to the beneficial owner and negligence penalties. The LP may also be struck for non-compliance under BC Partnership Act if it fails to maintain a registered office and filings.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally liable for all LP debts and obligations if the LP is found to be a sham or lacking substance. Directors of corporate general partners may be liable for taxes, penalties, and interest; courts may impose personal civil restitution and travel bans pending criminal proceedings.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with AML registration (if the partnership is a money services business or engaged in MSB activities) can result in administrative monetary penalties up to C$1 million per violation for corporations, and criminal fines or imprisonment for individuals. CRA and FINTRAC enforcement can freeze accounts and assess penalties on unreported taxable benefits.

Director personal liability: Senior officers and directors who knowingly participate in or fail to prevent AML breaches face imprisonment up to five years and/or fines. Beneficial owners of the LP are subject to transparency reporting; concealment of true ownership is a criminal offense and may lead to personal liability, freezing orders, and travel bans.
Withholding Tax / Non-Resident Compliance

Statutory sanction: A 10% penalty on the amount of withholding tax required but not remitted (up to C$100 per slip per day for failure to file information returns); penalties may reach 25% of the unremitted amount for repeat failures. Interest accrues daily on unpaid liabilities. Directors of the general partner are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for the full amount of unremitted Part XIII tax, plus interest and penalties. Personal liability can be enforced through civil proceedings, asset garnishment, and travel bans if criminal tax evasion charges are laid.
