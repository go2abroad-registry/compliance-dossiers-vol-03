Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal GST/HST, income tax, payroll and customs program accounts (Business Registration Online at canada.ca; My Business Account portal). Provincial Sales Tax (PST) in British Columbia is separately administered by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch through eTaxBC (etax.gov.bc.ca), with PST registration via Form FIN 419. Business registry: BC Registry Services (Corporate Online) under the Partnership Act, RSBC 1996, c. 348.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) for the BC limited partnership by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) with the Sudbury Tax Centre or online via Business Registration Online (BRO); non-resident applicants must appoint a Canadian representative/agent authorized under Form RC59 and provide a Canadian address for service. Step 2: Add the GST/HST program account (RT suffix) once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident below the threshold may voluntarily register to claim Input Tax Credits and must designate a resident agent for CRA notices. Step 3: Register the LP for BC PST at the Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC once gross revenue from BC taxable sales exceeds CAD 10,000 in any month or CAD 50,000 in any 12 consecutive months, obtaining a 9-digit BC PST vendor number. Step 4: Register the limited partnership with BC Registry Services by filing the Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form LP-1) and file the annual report; an extra-provincial or foreign LP carrying on business in BC must also extra-provincially register. Step 5: Add the CRA Import/Export program account (RM suffix) for customs purposes; for exports, report through CBSA using the Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) or carrier-submitted ACI eManifest data. The general partner manages all fiscal registrations; partners are not separately registered for the partnership's GST/PST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax: the LP computes income under Part I of the Income Tax Act but does not pay income tax itself; each partner (individual, corporation, or non-resident) is allocated a share of partnership income/loss and taxed at partner level. The LP has territorial Canadian-source scope through its Canadian permanent establishment; non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII gross-base withholding (25% or treaty-reduced) on allocated Canadian-source business income unless protected by treaty or a net-basis election. Distinct from income tax, the LP is a separate deemed person for GST/HST under ETA subsection 272.1 and a mandatory 'vendor' for BC PST; the LP's tax-exempt-zone status does not apply (no free-zone benefits for toys manufactured in BC).

Available election forms

Canada has no general check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. Relevant mechanisms: (i) Income Tax Act subsection 216(1) election — a non-resident partner with Canadian-source rental or real-property income from the partnership may file a net-basis Part I return instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (ii) ITA subsection 102(1)/102(2) — the partnership is a 'Canadian partnership' only if all partners are Canadian-resident; the presence of foreign partners makes it a non-Canadian partnership, triggering Part XIII compliance and s. 216 eligibility; (iii) ETA subsection 211(1) voluntary registration election — a small supplier may elect to register for GST/HST early to recover ITCs on pre-registration inputs; (iv) hybrid mismatch timing election under ITA subsection 18.2(13) and ETA section 272.1 — where a US partner classifies the Canadian LP as an association taxable as a corporation under US Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3 (check-the-box), the LP is a reverse hybrid; Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2/18.3, effective for later taxation years) automatically deny deductions, and the election may only align timing where the mismatch is eliminated; (v) ETA subsection 273(1) joint election to make zero-rated exports taxable — available but commercially inadvisable for an export manufacturer and should not be executed; and (vi) for BC PST, no elective regimes exist; vendor registration and PST collection are mandatory once thresholds are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide supplies in a single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); registration is mandatory above the threshold for a person carrying on business in Canada, and voluntary below it. BC PST: CAD 10,000 in gross revenue from taxable sales in BC in a single month, or CAD 50,000 over the immediately preceding 12 consecutive months. Both thresholds apply to the BC LP in its own capacity as GST registrant and PST vendor; the LP must also file annual BC registry reports, but no separate BC income-tax registration is required for the partnership entity itself.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Supplies of designer blind-box toys are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (sections 1-5) where the supplier exports the goods and delivers them to a destination outside Canada; export must occur 'as soon after' the supply as is reasonable (CRA administrative guidance: generally within 60 days), and the supplier must retain prescribed export evidence per GST/HST Memorandum 4-3. Goods shipped to a Canadian address (e.g., a third-party logistics warehouse in Richmond, BC for domestic fulfilment) are not zero-rated. Services and IP: design, prototyping, rotocasting/moulding, marketing, and licensing/royalty services supplied to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, sections 7 and 10, provided the supply is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; the BC LP should confirm the GST/HST status of each foreign licensee in its style-guide and character-IP licensing agreements. Export sales generate input tax credit refunds through the normal GST/HST net-tax calculation; no separate VAT refund mechanism is required for a registered exporter.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) or, for low-value courier shipments under CAD 2,000, the Courier Low-Value Shipment (CLVS) commercial-invoice data, showing the CRA RM/BN number, export date, HS heading 9503 (toys), destination country, and declared value.
  • Transport document evidencing departure from Canada and foreign delivery: ocean Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill, Air Waybill (AWB), cross-border truck manifest/CMR, or courier waybill bearing the foreign consignee's name and address.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent payment evidence: SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation, PayPal/Stripe settlement report, or buyer's purchase order matched with bank receipt, evidencing foreign-currency settlement and the CAD equivalent used on the export invoice.

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-validation, e-invoice digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 through the CRA-accredited Canadian e-invoicing Service Provider network). Mandatory compliance is limited to: (i) invoice content under ETA subsection 221(1) and section 223 — seller's name/trading name, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, taxable amount, tax rate and tax amount, and buyer name/address for supplies over CAD 1,500; (ii) separate disclosure of the 5% GST and 7% BC PST on every invoice/receipt under BC PSTA record-keeping rules; (iii) electronic filing of GST/HST returns (Form GST34) via GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account on monthly, quarterly, or annual cycles; and (iv) electronic filing of BC PST returns via eTaxBC on the assigned filing frequency; Shopify Payments and Stripe must be configured to apply the correct BC PST code for retail tangible personal property and the 5% GST at the point of sale, with no API-based fiscal clearance required.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims under GST/HST Memorandum 4-3 by demanding the export declaration, transport document, and payment/remittance evidence within 30 days of a written request; failure to substantiate reverses the zero-rating, reassesses GST, claws back any ITC refunds with interest from the original refund date, and may trigger penalties. Legal consequences include: (i) gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded or refund improperly obtained (ETA section 285); (ii) failure-to-file and failure-to-remit penalties (ETA section 280) of 6% plus 2% per complete month of arrears, with compound daily interest at CRA's prescribed rate; (iii) criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 and Income Tax Act section 239 with fines up to CAD 200,000 and/or up to two years imprisonment; (iv) deregistration and public disclosure for wilful misrepresentation; and (v) joint and several liability of the general partner — and, per CRA administrative position, limited partners — for the partnership's GST/PST debts under ETA subsection 272.1(3) and the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is a contractual and bank-compliance benchmark only, while the ETA requires export within a 'reasonable time' (60-day CRA administrative guide) and non-resident partner distributions remain separately subject to Part XIII withholding and treaty reporting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime. Canada imposes no material exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions; profit remittance by way of non-resident dividends, partnership distributions, or head-office charges is not exchange-controlled. Non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) may apply and be reduced by an applicable income tax treaty, but this is a tax compliance matter rather than a currency restriction. There is no requirement to obtain a central-bank permit for capital contributions, trade settlements, or hedging payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no mandatory surrender rule for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in a Canadian bank account or converted at a Canadian bank at commercial rates; there is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no conversion ratio. FINTRAC requires a cross-border currency and monetary instrument report for physical cash movements exceeding CAD 10,000 or its foreign-exchange equivalent. For Canadian tax and accounting purposes, export revenue must be converted to Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada noon exchange rate or a consistent acceptable rate under IFRS, but this is a tax recognition requirement, not a surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the monetary authority, but Canada maintains no dedicated centralized exchange-control administration. Foreign exchange prudential oversight is embedded in the Bank Act, the Canadian Payments Act, and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) guidance for authorised Schedule I banks. FINTRAC administers cross-border currency reporting and suspicious transaction monitoring, while the Investment Canada Act addresses non-resident investment review, not exchange controls.
