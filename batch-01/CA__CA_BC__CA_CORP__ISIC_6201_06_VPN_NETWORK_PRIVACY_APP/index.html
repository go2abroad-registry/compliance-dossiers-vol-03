Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Ottawa, ON, Canada; online portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html). Provincial component: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch and eTaxBC portal (https://etaxbc.gov.bc.ca). Corporate registries: Corporations Canada for federal incorporation and BC Corporate Online / BC Registry Services for provincial incorporation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Canadian business corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, Articles, registered office address, and directors' information. Step 2: Apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for a federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online, by mailing or faxing Form RC1, or through the Canada/BC one-stop business registration service. Select the required CRA program accounts: RC0001 for corporate income tax, RT0001 for GST/HST, and RP0001 for payroll where applicable. Step 3: Register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) before making any taxable sales in British Columbia. Step 4: A foreign non-resident corporation may obtain the CRA BN and GST/HST registration directly using Form RC1 and filing with CRA's International Tax Services Office; the entity must provide authenticated incorporation documents, foreign registered address, director/officer details, and the name and contact information of any Canadian representative or statutory agent. The CRA Business Number is the national TIN and takes the format: nine-digit BN plus a two-letter program identifier plus a four-digit reference, e.g., 123456789 RT0001 for GST/HST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and is not a pass-through entity for Canadian income tax purposes. A resident Canadian corporation is subject to federal income tax on worldwide income, plus BC provincial corporate income tax administered by CRA under the Provincial Tax Collection Agreement. A non-resident corporation is taxed in Canada only on Canadian-source business and specified investment income, subject to treaty relief where applicable.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a United States-style check-the-box election; corporation classification follows the statute under which the entity is incorporated. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s. 85 rollover election for qualifying transfers of property to a corporation; (ii) Income Tax Act s. 216 non-resident corporation election to report Canadian rental, royalty, and investment income on a return rather than suffering gross Part XIII withholding; (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election between closely related corporations to treat certain supplies as non-taxable or zero-rated for GST/HST purposes; and (iv) simplified accounting elections available under GST/HST, including the Quick Method and streamlined net-tax calculation for smaller suppliers. Hybrid mismatch elections are not separately available; Canada addresses hybrid mismatch concerns through foreign tax credit, foreign affiliate, thin-capitalization, and transfer-pricing rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide gross taxable supplies made by the entity during the immediately preceding four calendar quarters, which is the CRA small-supplier threshold for mandatory GST/HST registration. British Columbia PST has no small-supplier monetary threshold; a vendor must become registered and collect PST once it makes taxable sales in BC. Non-resident suppliers of digital products and services to Canadian consumers may be required to register under CRA's simplified non-resident GST/HST registration regime at the same CAD 30,000 Canadian-sourced revenue threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VPN/privacy software supplied as SaaS is classified as intangible personal property for GST/HST purposes. The supply qualifies for zero-rating at 0% GST/HST where it is made to a non-resident recipient, provided the recipient does not acquire it for consumption, use, or supply in Canada, and where the supply is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For B2B exports, zero-rating is available if the non-resident recipient is not a GST/HST registrant using the service in Canada. For B2C exports, the service is zero-rated when delivered to the foreign individual outside Canada, evidenced by foreign billing address, foreign payment method, IP-address geolocation, foreign device activation records, and subscription terms that place delivery offshore. BC PST is not chargeable where the service is not made available to, or received by, a customer in British Columbia. Supporting documents must prove the non-resident status of the customer and the offshore place of supply or use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or carrier Bill of Lading / Airway Bill where tangible media or encrypted hardware is exported; generally not applicable to dematerialized VPN SaaS deliveries
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-currency conversion/remittance statement showing proceeds were received in Canada from the non-resident customer within the prescribed repatriation window
  • Export sales contract or SaaS subscription agreement naming the non-resident recipient, specifying the foreign place of use, and stating that services are delivered and consumed outside Canada
  • Technical delivery and usage evidence: IP-address geolocation logs, server access logs, VPN device activation records, payment gateway settlement reports from Stripe/Paymentwall, and any BC/CRA records establishing no Canadian place of use

Canada does not currently impose a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate. A GST/HST registrant must issue adequate tax invoices showing the vendor's name and GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description of the supply, taxable amount, and tax payable. Electronic invoices are valid if legible and retained in an accessible CRA-compliant format. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically where required through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account; BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC. There is no mandatory national e-invoice clearance API, but records must be sufficient to support the tax status, zero-rating, and the ultimate foreign destination of exported digital services.

CRA may deny the zero-rated treatment, reassess at 5% GST or the applicable 12% combined GST/PST rate, claw back claimed input tax credits and refunds, and impose interest on any shortfall. The normal reassessment period is four years, extended to ten years where the filing contains a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or fraud. Civil penalties include fixed minimum fines plus a percentage of the tax sought to be evaded for false statements or omissions. Wilful evasion is an offence under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and, on conviction, may attract fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment. The BC Ministry of Finance may separately assess unremitted PST, apply collection remedies, and impose provincial penalties, including penalties for deliberate avoidance where export evidence is fabricated or false.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible G10 fiat currency. Canada imposes no exchange control restrictions under the Currency Act, the Bank Act, or the Income Tax Act; both current account (subscriptions, licensing fees, intercompany services) and capital account (equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends) transactions may be effected freely in CAD or any foreign currency. Profit remittance friction is fiscal, not regulatory: non-resident dividends, royalties, and management fees attract Part XIII withholding tax at 25% statutory, typically reduced to 5%-15% under an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada-US Treaty), payable by remittance to the Canada Revenue Agency with a Form NR601 accompanying the NR4. Greenfield incorporation of a British Columbia business corporation is not subject to Investment Canada Act review (which applies only to acquisitions of control of a Canadian business above indexed WTO thresholds, currently CAD 1.125 billion for non-state-owned acquirers as of 2024); a VPN/privacy SaaS business requires no statutory license, though export of encryption software is dual-use in nature and must not be supplied to sanctioned jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. VPN subscription and licensing receipts may be settled, retained, and disbursed in CAD, USD, or other freely convertible currencies. Statutory obligations are AML/CFT-oriented and proportionate: (i) EFT reporting is discharged by the bank or payment processor as the FINTRAC reporting entity, but the corporation must supply the purpose code and counterparty detail for all wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) the corporation must file a Large Cash Transaction Report if it accepts CDN 10,000 or more in cash in a single transaction in the course of business (not applicable to Stripe/Paymentwall card flows); (iii) anyone transporting currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more across the border must file FINTRAC Form E677; (iv) all books, records, and the register of individuals with significant control must be accessible in Canada under the Income Tax Act and the BC Business Corporations Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) chartered under the Bank of Canada Act; prudential supervision of authorized banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision and reporting administration by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); sanctions administration by Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act and the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act; payment clearing standards administered by Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary collection and disbursement rail; Low as the primary treasury authority. Foreign EMIs operating in Canada (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) are registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC rather than as chartered banks; they are not members of Payments Canada for direct ACSS settlement, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot issue SWIFT-WIFT-direct LCs, banker's acceptances, or deliverable FX forwards. Legal constraints: (i) client funds held by an EMI are not segregated from the EMI's own settlement pool from the perspective of the BC corporation and therefore must not be used for capital escrow or intercompany loan warehousing; (ii) EMI-issued CAD account numbers are typically 'pooled settlement' identifiers rather than dedicated transit/account numbers, which creates auditability gaps for the ISC register and CRA books-and-records rules; (iii) onboarding an offshore EMI as a long-term receivable collection node is viable only where the EMI holds a Canadian MSB registration in good standing and the BC corporation remains the legal and beneficial owner of the account. Recommended treasury role: USD concentration and vendor payouts below CAD 50,000 per transaction, with the primary CAD operating account held at a Schedule I bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined statutory headline rate: 15% federal Part I corporate income tax plus 12% British Columbia provincial corporate income tax. Canadian-controlled private corporations may access a reduced combined rate of 11% on the first CAD 500,000 of eligible active business income through the federal small business deduction and the BC small business deduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to non-residents of Canada, pursuant to ITA 212(2). The rate may be reduced under an applicable tax treaty, commonly to 5% for corporate beneficial owners holding at least 10% of the voting shares and to 15% for portfolio dividend recipients in many Canadian DTAs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is required where the aggregate value of reportable transactions between a Canadian corporation and non-arm's-length non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. A multinational entity with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 850 million must also prepare a CbC Report and maintain global Master File documentation aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to offshore financial centres. However, the CRA applies substance-over-form and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA 245, and treaty benefits can be denied where a corporate structure is artificial or lacks real economic activity. The Canadian VPN company must demonstrate actual substance in BC: employee payroll, engineering and technical operations, server configuration/ownership responsibility, management decisions, bank accounts, billing records, and technical support. A subsidiary that merely holds billing contracts while foreign affiliates perform all value-creating functions is vulnerable to reallocation of profits and denial of treaty relief under recent OECD Principal Purpose Test / Multilateral Instrument positions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Omission of Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA 239(1): fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years, in addition to civil reassessment of omitted income, gross-negligence penalties under ITA 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax, and prescribed-rate interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct or participate in wilful tax evasion may be personally prosecuted. Corporate-veil protection is unavailable for fraud or sham transactions, and courts may impose civil restitution, director-disqualification orders, asset seizure, and travel restrictions through criminal proceedings. Directors of a Canadian corporation also face statutory joint and several liability for unremitted source deductions under ITA 227.1, but payroll/withholding must be diligently administered.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Migration to Foreign IP Shell

Statutory sanction: The CRA may adjust consideration to arm's-length levels and reallocate profits. ITA 247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue for the year. Unsupported deductions are disallowed, and penalties may be stacked with gross-negligence penalties if documentation is knowingly false.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for transfer-pricing penalties, but they face personal criminal liability if they sign or procure false T2 or T106 filings. Gross-negligence findings can also lead to director-level litigation in oppressed or fraudulent corporate structures, and cross-border information exchanges via CRS and CbCR can expose group-wide profit reallocation and joint corrective assessments.
Shell Company Abuse and Anti-Money-Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Canada's Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act imposes administrative monetary penalties for failing to implement compliance programs and for making false or misleading statements, with fines reaching CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for individuals in serious cases. BC corporate registry and beneficial-ownership disclosure violations carry separate administrative penalties under provincial corporate law. Payment processors including Stripe and Paymentwall routinely require bank verification, director identification, and UBO disclosure; false information submitted to financial institutions is independently actionable as fraud.

Director personal liability: Where a shell company is used to conceal revenue, evade tax, or launder customer payments, Canadian courts will pierce the corporate veil and hold controlling directors and shareholders personally liable for civil fraud, unjust enrichment, and equitable compensation. Individuals may be barred from leaving Canada if criminal charges are laid, and directors may be disqualified from serving as directors of any Canadian corporation following conviction for an indictable offence under BC law.
