Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via My Business Account (cra-arc.gc.ca) for federal GST/HST and income tax; British Columbia Ministry of Finance via eTaxBC (etax.gov.bc.ca) for Provincial Sales Tax (PST).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by applying through CRA's Business Registration Online or filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The GST/HST account is opened on the same application where taxable activity in Canada is identified. Non-resident corporations may register using Form RC1 and should appoint a Canadian representative or provide a mailing address acceptable to CRA. PST registration with BC Ministry of Finance is separately required (or obtained through BC OneStop) when the corporation begins making taxable retail sales in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not recognize U.S.-style check-the-box elections. A Business Corporation is taxed on its income at the entity level and may make Canadian income tax elections such as qualifying as a Canadian-controlled private corporation for the small business deduction, choosing a non-calendar taxation year, or electing under section 216 for non-resident employment/services where applicable. For GST/HST, registrants can elect simplified accounting methods (e.g., Quick Method, Special Quick Method for publishers) and closely related corporation elections, provided eligibility requirements are met. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s.18.2, s.18.3) constrain arrangements exploiting differences in tax characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small supplier threshold: $30,000 CAD annual gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada (entire calendar year). BC PST: no small-supplier threshold; registration required before making taxable sales of goods or services in BC unless a specific exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

UI/UX design subscription services provided to a non-resident client are eligible for 0% GST/HST under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act if the supply is made to a non-resident and not connected to Canadian real property, tangible personal property located in Canada, or certain preserved services. The supplier must retain documentation showing the recipient's non-resident status, the place of performance/delivery, and the B2B nature of the transaction. Export of physical design deliverables may also qualify under export provisions when shipped outside Canada.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (for physical goods exported with the design subscription deliverables, where applicable)
  • Commercial invoice and signed design subscription agreement identifying the non-resident client
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international shipment (if tangible deliverables exported)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 confirming settlement in foreign currency from the non-resident counterparty
  • Digital delivery logs and handover confirmation records showing performance offshore
  • GST/HST zero-rating substantiation worksheet and internal CRA record-keeping checklist

No real-time electronic invoicing or tax authority clearance mandate exists at the federal or BC provincial level. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information (supplier name, BN/GST registry number, transaction date, tax rate and amount) and may use electronic invoicing provided records satisfy CRA's record-keeping requirements. BC PST invoicing does not require digital fiscal clearance, but sellers must disclose PST charged on invoices for retail sales.

CRA can reassess GST/HST returns within four years (or beyond for willful misrepresentation). A zero-rating claim that is not substantiated results in additional GST/GST remittance, interest, and penalties under Excise Tax Act s.280 and s.285. Knowingly false export documentation carries civil penalties up to 50% of the tax avoided, and criminal prosecution is possible for fraud. BC PST audits may separately reassess uncollected PST plus penalties for invoices issued without PST on taxable sales.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada provides full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely floating, with no exchange-control authorizations, prior approvals, or repatriation restrictions. Profit remittance via dividends to non-residents is permitted without exchange-control hurdles, though Canadian withholding tax and treaty relief apply. Cross-border subscription revenue is freely convertible and remittable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios apply to export receipts in Canada. Exporters of digital services may retain, convert, or repatriate proceeds in any permitted currency at their discretion without central-bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) within the policy framework of the Department of Finance Canada; no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility for regulated Canadian fintech payment platforms (e.g., Stripe, Wise) to manage cross-border subscription inflows and supplier outflows, provided such platforms are registered as money-services businesses with FINTRAC and, where applicable, hold provincial licences. Offshore digital EMIs cannot operate as unlicensed payment institutions in Canada; they may be engaged only as licensed processing rails that settle into a Canadian bank account.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate income tax rate in British Columbia (federal 15% + British Columbia 12%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to dividends paid or credited by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner. Non-resident corporations carrying on business through a Canadian permanent establishment are instead subject to 25% Part XIV branch tax on after-tax Canadian business income, subject to treaty reduction and reasonable reinvestment allowances.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the end of the taxation year. For a December 31 fiscal period, the due date is June 30 of the following year. The balance of Part I tax is generally due two months after year-end, or three months after year-end for qualified Canadian-controlled private corporations, with instalments throughout the year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada applies no fixed CA$ revenue threshold for ordinary transfer pricing documentation; every Canadian taxpayer engaging in a non-arm's length cross-border transaction must prepare contemporaneous defence documents. Form T106 is mandatory and is filed with the T2. For country-by-country reporting, the consolidated group revenue threshold is CA$750,000,000. The statutory penalty threshold is a net transfer pricing adjustment exceeding the lesser of CA$5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, Canadian case law and CRA practice treat central management and control as the decisive test of corporate residence, and transactions must have economic substance to avoid General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) recharacterization and treaty abuse challenges. A British Columbia corporation that is managed by Canadian directors, carries on real UI/UX design activities, maintains its books in Canada, and receives subscription revenue for substantive services will normally be respected as a Canadian tax resident and able to claim treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Return

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all underpaid federal and BC income tax, interest, gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax, and criminal prosecution under ITA s.239 with fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to five years on indictment.

Director personal liability: A director or officer who directed, acquiesced in, or knowingly signed a false T2 return can be criminally charged as a party to the offence. The corporate veil does not protect fraud; courts may impose restitution, asset restraint orders, travel bans pending criminal proceedings, and up to five years imprisonment.
Unremitted Withholding and Source Deduction Liabilities

Statutory sanction: CRA assesses the unremitted amounts, interest, penalties and collection enforcement. Directors are personally liable under ITA s.227.1 for up to 100% of unremitted source deductions and Part XIII amounts, regardless of corporate dissolution or bankruptcy.

Director personal liability: Statutory director liability operates as a piercing of the corporate veil: CRA may pursue household assets, bank accounts and director remuneration; serious cases may also attract criminal sanctions, travel bans and imprisonment where the withholding failure is part of a deliberate fraud.
Transfer Pricing / Undocumented Intercompany Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under ITA s.247(2), assess a 10% transfer pricing penalty under s.247(3) where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CA$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue, impose late T106 penalties, and deny treaty benefits if the arrangement is a sham or lacks substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement an artificial transfer pricing structure can be assessed for gross negligence penalties if they signed false returns; managing directors may be personally exposed where intercompany distributions are recharacterized as taxable benefits or unpaid dividends, and criminal fraud charges can lead to imprisonment and travel restrictions.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s.245 can deny the tax benefit and recharacterize the full arrangement; CRA can apply Part XIII withholding tax on deemed dividends or royalties, disallow foreign tax credits, impose gross negligence penalties, and deny reduced treaty withholding rates under anti-treaty-shopping rules such as the principal purpose test in the OECD MLI.

Director personal liability: Civil courts may disregard the corporate entity as a mere agent or conduit, exposing the parent and directors to reassessment, recovery from personal assets, and injunctions. In criminal tax investigations, courts may impose asset freezes, travel bans and imprisonment for the controlling minds of the shell structure.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Record Integrity

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and related BC corporate law, false records and unreported cash or structured transactions can trigger administrative monetary penalties, criminal fines, and prosecution. Corporate income tax reassessment and gross negligence penalties may follow from the same factual findings.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the company to be used as a conduit for undisclosed funds may be held personally liable for money laundering penalties and ordered to make restitution; serious cases support extradition-adjacent court orders, travel bans, and imprisonment for the principals.
