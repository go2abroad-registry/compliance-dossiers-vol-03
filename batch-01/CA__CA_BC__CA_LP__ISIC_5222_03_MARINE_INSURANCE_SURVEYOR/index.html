Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Federal and Provincial Tax Authority, official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities and non-resident partners in Canadian Limited Partnerships must register with the CRA to obtain a Business Number (BN) and GST/HST identification number. The standard procedure involves online registration through the My Business Account portal or submission of Form RC59 accompanied by supporting documentation including proof of foreign entity status, articles of incorporation or equivalent, business address, and activity description. Canadian Limited Partnerships register directly via CRA's partnership filing requirements under the Income Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian Limited Partnerships are statutorily classified as pass-through entities under the Income Tax Act with no entity-level taxation at the partnership level. No standard check-the-box election exists; however, hybrid mismatch rules under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and transfer pricing provisions may apply to certain cross-border arrangements. Limited partnerships may elect corporate taxation treatment in specific circumstances such as real estate investment vehicles under subsection 12(11) of the Income Tax Act.

Standard VAT / GST rate

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% GST rate applies to exports of goods and to services performed outside Canada or where the recipient is located outside Canada for applicable categories. Marine insurance surveying and vessel hull/cargo damage inspection services qualify for zero-rating when the surveying activity occurs outside Canadian territory or the client is a non-resident. Proof of export or performance outside Canada must be substantiated through contracts, travel records, and client attestations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (if goods involved)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent shipping documentation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of foreign client payment

All taxable supplies require issuance of a tax invoice containing prescribed information including GST/HST registration number, description of supply, amount, and tax rate. Large suppliers exceeding $2 million in annual taxable supplies are subject to mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns via the CRA's My Account portal. Real-time digital reporting is not mandated under current Canadian law, though CRA enforces compliance through post-filing audits and data analytics.

False or fraudulent claims of zero-rating on exports trigger retrospective adjustments under the Excise Tax Act. Penalties include administrative monetary penalties up to 200% of the evaded GST, interest charges from the due date, and potential criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code for wilful evasion. CRA conducts targeted audits on export claims with high scrutiny on marine and offshore service documentation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full current account convertibility under the Exchange Control Regulations administered by the Bank of Canada. Capital account transactions, including profit remittances to non-resident partners, require prior declaration and may necessitate approval from the Department of Finance or Bank of Canada if classified as investment-related. For a British Columbia Limited Partnership, repatriation of earnings is facilitated through authorized foreign exchange dealers; however, delays or hurdles may arise when remittances are deemed capital in nature rather than current account service fees, subject to Income Tax Act withholding requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Although the core activity involves marine insurance surveying rather than direct exports, any export-related proceeds would require mandatory surrender to a licensed authorized foreign exchange dealer within statutory timelines, typically 30 days, at the prevailing market rate. Conversion ratios align with market-driven mechanisms under the Currency Act; controlled goods under the Export and Import Permits Act trigger additional reporting, but surveying services predominantly constitute current account transactions without surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions such as Wise or Revolut encounter substantial legal constraints for Canadian entities under OSFI and FINTRAC oversight. Cross-border services are accessible via licensed Canadian fintech providers, but full EMI licensing for offshore operations remains restricted; entities must rely on traditional correspondent banking with enhanced due diligence, AML reporting, and compliance attestations to mitigate regulatory risks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23% combined federal/BC general corporate rate (15% federal + 8% BC). The Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent; income is not taxed at the entity level but is allocated to partners and taxed at partner-level rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII general withholding rate on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation. Direct distributions of partnership profits by the LP are not treated as dividends; they are allocated income, and the non-resident partner is taxed under Part I. A non-resident corporate partner's after-tax earnings that are repatriated may attract 25% Part XIV branch tax, subject to treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a December 31 fiscal period end, the T5013 must be filed by March 31 of the immediately following calendar year. For other fiscal period ends, the return must be filed by the first day of the third calendar month after the fiscal period end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within six months after the end of the corporation's taxation year. The corporate partner's federal/BC tax balance is generally due two months after the year-end, or three months for a CCPC eligible for the Small Business Deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 in aggregate non-arm's length cross-border transactions with non-residents in a fiscal period. This triggers mandatory Form T106 disclosure and the expectation that contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File / Master File equivalents) be prepared and retained. Canadian transfer pricing rules under section 247 of the Income Tax Act do not impose a separate statutory dollar threshold for documentation, but CRA expects documentation where the threshold is met or exceeded.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain offshore Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those in some common law offshore jurisdictions. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and CRA administrative doctrine require genuine economic substance, real business purpose, and commercial rationality. A limited partnership resident in British Columbia must have actual physical presence in BC, credible marine survey operations, revenue-generating activity, and risk management exercised by partners or employees in Canada. Treaty claims are denied if the recipient lacks beneficial ownership or is a mere conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion or False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239: on summary conviction, a fine of at least 50% and up to 200% of the tax evaded, and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Gross negligence penalty under section 163(2) is 50% of the understated tax. Interest on unpaid tax is compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and general partners who authorized, consented to, or acquiesced in the evasion are personally liable under section 227.1 for unremitted withholding, corporate tax, source deductions, and related penalties and interest. Criminal offenders may face travel bans, extradition, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under section 247(2). The transfer pricing penalty is 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment when the taxpayer fails to provide adequate documentation or acts without reasonable effort to determine the arm's length price. GAAR can fully re-characterize or disallow the transaction, and the associated tax benefits are reassessed with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be assessed for the corporate penalty if they directed or accepted the mispriced terms, especially where the arrangement has no commercial substance. Personal liability extends to unpaid corporate taxes, penalties, and interest; in egregious cases, directors can be charged with complicity in tax evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can invalidate the structure under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, deny treaty benefits, impose a 50% gross negligence penalty, and reassess all years open for assessment. BC financial services authorities may suspend or revoke the statutory licence required for vessel hull and cargo damage marine claims inspection. Criminal sanctions apply if the shell is used to misrepresent taxable Canadian income.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners who cause the LP to operate as a sham may be jointly and severally liable for the full amount of taxes, penalties, and interest, and may be subject to BC cease-and-desist orders, personal asset seizure, and criminal proceedings for fraud or misrepresentation.
Anti-Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administrative penalties can reach up to C$2,000,000 for non-compliance by a reporting entity. Criminal money laundering under Criminal Code section 462.31 is punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years. Seized proceeds of crime are subject to forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners, officers, or employees who knowingly participate in laundering, or who fail to report suspicious transactions when required, may be prosecuted as parties to the offence and face imprisonment, seizure of personal assets, and travel restrictions. In BC, the Civil Forfeiture Act also permits the recovery of property acquired through unlawful activity.
