Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal tax portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax-businesses.html). British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst). Corporate registry services for a federal or BC extra-provincial business corporation are provided by Corporations Canada and BC Registry Services / Corporate Online respectively.

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian federal or BC business corporation, the statutory tax identifier is the CRA Business Number (BN), a 9-digit number obtained upon incorporation; the incorporating corporate registry transmits the entity data to CRA, after which the corporation opens program accounts through Business Registration Online (BRO), the material accounts being the corporate income tax account (T2) and the GST/HST account (BN plus the RT0001 program identifier). A non-resident-owned corporation without a Canadian permanent establishment appoints a resident Canadian agent using Form RC59, and may file Form RC273 to support the registration; CRA then issues the BN confirmation. Before carrying on business in BC, a federal company must complete extra-provincial registration under the BC Business Corporations Act; a BC PST vendor account is then registered through eTaxBC. Statutory accounting records, GST/HST books and records, and supporting export documentation must be maintained in Canada for at least six years.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime and a business corporation cannot elect any form of pass-through transparency. Applicable statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s.256(25) election to allocate the Canadian-controlled private corporation small business deduction among associated corporations; (ii) ITA s.216 election allowing a non-resident transport operator to file a separate Canadian return; (iii) Excise Tax Act s.156 election among closely related corporations to treat inter-corporate supplies of services (including management, platform, or data services) as made for nil consideration; (iv) ETA s.148 voluntary registration election before the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded; and (v) ITA s.18.5 MLI hybrid-mismatch substitution election for arrangements otherwise re-characterized by the hybrid rules. The federal and BC corporate tax systems otherwise treat the corporation as an entity-level taxpayer with no classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — under ETA s.148 a small supplier is a person whose total worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated supplies, did not exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 in the aggregate of the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once the threshold is exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory from the first day of the following month, and voluntary registration is available to a market research platform seeking input tax credit recovery on Canadian costs. The BC PST has no analogous small-supplier exemption threshold; a PST vendor account with the BC Ministry of Finance is required before taxable retail supplies of software or goods are made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for the consumer panel survey platform is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7: a supply of a service made to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated unless the service is rendered in respect of real property situated in Canada, in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is supplied to a person registered under Division V (a GST/HST registrant). Market research data services, survey dashboard access, and analytics services delivered to a foreign corporate client that is not registered for GST/HST and has no Canadian permanent establishment connected to the purchase qualify for the 0% rate. Physical exports of panel incentive goods or research samples are zero-rated under sections 20 to 24 of Part V where the exporter retains contemporaneous proof of export; service elements must be separately valued from any goods included in the same invoice.

  • Customs export declaration: CBSA B13A Export Declaration or CBSA Electronic Export Reporting (ENS) record for physical goods exported from Canada; for pure cross-border service exports the CRA-accepted export file is the executed service agreement and a CRA-compliant GST/HST tax invoice evidencing the zero-rated supply to the foreign recipient.
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill or courier proof of delivery evidencing removal of panel-incentive goods from Canada, together with freight forwarding instructions, carrier invoices, and CBSA export authorization where applicable.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: commercial bank credit advice, MT103 wire transfer record, or foreign-exchange dealer conversion statement evidencing receipt of the export invoice consideration in foreign currency into the Canadian corporate bank account within the 180-day repatriation benchmark.

Canada does not impose real-time electronic fiscal clearance, digital stamping, QR code authorization, or government API invoice clearance for GST/HST or BC PST; e-invoicing (PEPPOL/EDI) is voluntary commercial practice rather than a legal mandate. GST/HST registrants must issue tax invoices under ETA s.221(1) containing the supplier's Business Number, invoice date, supplier and recipient identification, description and quantity of goods or services, total consideration, and the GST/HST amount or an indicated rate; simplified invoices are permitted for supplies below CAD 150. BC PST vendors must display their PST registration number, issue invoices showing PST charged, and file returns electronically through eTaxBC; all records must be retained for six years.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims through technical reviews and field examinations. Where the exporter cannot substantiate the non-resident status of the recipient, the place of supply outside Canada, or the receipt of consideration, the zero-rating is reversed, GST is assessed at 5%, and interest accrues compounded daily at the prescribed rate (90-day Treasury bill rate plus 4%). Penalties include: ETA s.281.1 false-statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the misstatement; ETA s.285 penalty of 5% of the refund or input tax credit claimed plus 5% of any amount assessed attributable to the misstatement, with an additional CAD 250 if made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence; ETA s.280 failure-to-register penalty of CAD 250 plus CAD 50 per day up to CAD 2,500, or 0.05% of gross revenue for the registration period; and criminal prosecution under ETA s.327.1 for evasion, carrying fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years upon indictment. The ordinary four-year reassessment period does not apply to fraud, wilful default, or misrepresentation; erroneous or fictitious export zero-rating is therefore subject to indefinite retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, with no exchange controls, surrender requirements, or prior licensing of inward or outward fund transfers. The Canadian dollar (CAD) trades freely in global FX markets, and the financial system permits unhedged foreign-currency accounts for residents. The only material hurdles to profit repatriation are (i) Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and similar payments to non-residents (statutory 25%, typically treaty-reduced, e.g., to 5% to 15% for a treaty-resident parent corporation), (ii) the corporate solvency test under the Canada Business Corporations Act and the British Columbia Business Corporations Act requiring that the corporation have no reasonable grounds to believe it would become insolvent after payment, and (iii) GST/HST and transfer-pricing documentation obligations where the platform charges foreign arms-length survey buyers. No official approval is required to remit shareholder loans, dividends, or consultant fees outside Canada, provided the payer is tax-compliant, the payee identity terms are met, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds are observed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no prescribed conversion ratio. A British Columbia market-research corporation may freely retain export receipts in CAD or foreign currencies in domestic or offshore bank accounts. The relevant compliance anchors are (i) FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting for any single transaction of CAD 10,000 or more, including cross-border wires and settlement aggregations, (ii) Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) foreign-reporting regime (Form T1135) requiring disclosure of specified foreign property with a cost amount over CAD 100,000, which would capture meaningful Wise, PayPal, or USD bank balances held offshore or in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks if such balances constitute specified foreign property, and (iii) transfer-pricing rules under the Income Tax Act requiring arm's-length pricing of intercompany export receipts invoiced through the platform.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the central monetary authority and guardian of Canadian-dollar clearing and liquidity; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates and supervises Schedule I banks, trust companies, and loan companies; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers Canada's AML/CTF and cross-border wire reporting regime. Canada operates without a dedicated foreign-exchange administration; currency and FX policy is managed by the Bank of Canada and the Department of Finance through open-market operations and a free-floating exchange rate framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for purpose-limited use, LOW as a primary treasury bank. Wise (registered in Canada as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC) and PayPal (licensed payment intermediary via its Canadian MSB structure) are legal and viable for a low-risk Market-Research-SaaS platform to support mass panel payouts, cross-border settlement, and low-value incentives. However, neither is a Schedule I bank under the Bank Act: balances are not CDIC-insured, the provider does not extend operating credit, and regulatory treatment subjects the fintech to MSB-level AML/KYC obligations. OSFI-regulated institutions require that the corporate operating account be held at a Canadian chartered bank; EMIs must therefore be configured as sub-accounts, payout rails, or treasury concentration adjuncts rather than the sole banking relationship. Legal constraints include FINTRAC compliance on the part of the EMI, the corporation's obligation to provide beneficial ownership and identity attestation, and the practical need for dual approvals for batch files exceeding CAD 10,000.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Routing protocol: all initial and any subsequent parent/shareholder capital injections into the BC operating account must be executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 (for bank-to-bank value transfer) from the parent's treasury bank into the corporation's Schedule I CAD account. Purpose codes must be explicit: use SWIFT transaction-type code 'CAPL' or client credit narrative 'Inbound capital contribution / shareholder subscription' to avoid erroneous treatment as revenue or a taxable dividend; for debt-equity structures, the narrative must specify 'Shareholder loan / intercompany facility under the Income Tax Act section 15.2 thin-capitalization rules' and the related loan agreement must be lodged with the relationship manager. The funds-verification packet includes the corporate resolution authorizing the account, the KYC files of the remitting entity, and confirmation of source of funds supported by the parent's audited financial statements. The corporation must also verify whether the foreign parent's establishment of the Canadian business triggers a Notification or filing obligation under the Investment Canada Act (new Canadian business controlled by non-residents), and must preserve actual proof of banking chain to ensure clean provenance. Inbound wires in excess of CAD 10,000 will be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; banks will also apply sanctions-screening and beneficial-ownership verification to the remitter.Clearing-rail strategy divides into four lanes. First, tier-1 SWIFT MT103 wires for high-value cross-border disbursements (third-party data licensing, US-based contractor invoices, and Canadian vendor settlements) executed from the Schedule I account, each with industry-level purpose codes for the underlying service. Second, for physical-goods or documentary transactions (not typical for SaaS) a Commercial Letter of Credit can be issued against bank-approved 30-60 day drafts, but for market-research services the standard instrument is a Standby Letter of Credit or demand guarantee securing client milestone obligations. Third, mass micro-payouts to consumer panelists must use PayPal Mass Payout API and Wise Business batch payments, both of which settle via local ACH, SEPA, and domestic rails, avoiding per-transaction SWIFT costs and automatically tagging the payee's reference number; these rails are permissible because the business registers as a money transmitter's client and maintains sub-account balances only to the extent of the immediate settlement float. Fourth, domestic Canadian disbursements of panel incentives should be cleared via Interac e-Transfer or direct ACH/EFT through the bank to achieve complete auditability. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for purpose-limited use, LOW as a primary treasury bank. Wise (registered in Canada as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC) and PayPal (licensed payment intermediary via its Canadian MSB structure) are legal and viable for a low-risk Market-Research-SaaS platform to support mass panel payouts, cross-border settlement, and low-value incentives. However, neither is a Schedule I bank under the Bank Act: balances are not CDIC-insured, the provider does not extend operating credit, and regulatory treatment subjects the fintech to MSB-level AML/KYC obligations. OSFI-regulated institutions require that the corporate operating account be held at a Canadian chartered bank; EMIs must therefore be configured as sub-accounts, payout rails, or treasury concentration adjuncts rather than the sole banking relationship. Legal constraints include FINTRAC compliance on the part of the EMI, the corporation's obligation to provide beneficial ownership and identity attestation, and the practical need for dual approvals for batch files exceeding CAD 10,000.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate income tax rate (15% federal Part I rate + 12% British Columbia provincial rate); 11% combined active business rate for Canadian-controlled private corporations on the first CAD 500,000 of qualifying active business income (9% federal small business rate + 2% BC small business rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII tax on dividends paid or credited to non-resident shareholders by a resident Canadian corporation, absent treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with a specific non-resident person triggers mandatory filing of T106 Information Return and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File); a Master File is required for MNE groups whose consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000 and that file CbC reports (Form RC4646).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime similar to the UAE/offshore financial centres. Instead, economic substance is assessed through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA), the sham doctrine, and the permanent establishment concept. A BC-incorporated business corporation is deemed resident in Canada and taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation carrying on a consumer poll/data platform is taxable only if it creates a Canadian permanent establishment. Ottawa may re-characterize artificial transactions lacking commercial substance under GAAR, disallow treaty benefits under the Limitation on Benefits provisions, and impose penalties if arrangements are designed primarily to avoid tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Deceptive Statements

Statutory sanction: Upon conviction on summary indictment, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years; upon indictment, a fine up to 5 years imprisonment and a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 500% of the tax sought to be evaded. The CRA also imposes a civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax plus daily arrears interest; criminal forfeiture and seizure of property may follow.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil for fraud; directors and officers who knowingly participate, consent, or acquiesce in the false filings become jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax and penalty assessments, and may face travel restrictions, surrender of passports as bail conditions, and imprisonment after conviction.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement / GAAR

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply GAAR to disregard the tax benefit and reassess the taxpayer on a basis that reflects the economic reality, adding interest and penalizing gross negligence under s.163.2; the re-characterized transaction may also result in deemed withholding on non-resident payments under Part XIII and denial of treaty relief.

Director personal liability: If the corporation is a sham or bare trustee, directors are exposed to assessments for unremitted source deductions and 10% director liability (s.227.1); a court may further order civil restitution for misrepresented corporate ownership, and directors who obstruct or misrepresent substance may be criminally charged and subject to travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: A 10% transfer-pricing penalty applies to the net transfer-pricing adjustment that exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue, unless the taxpayer can demonstrate reasonable efforts; the CRA also levies late-filing penalties on missing T106 (CAD 100/day, up to CAD 2,500, plus CAD 350 gross negligence) and re-characterizes the transactions under ss. 247(2) and (3).

Director personal liability: Directors who endorse or direct false TP documentation may be prosecuted for abetting evasion under s.239; knowingly failing to disclose a foreign transferee arrangement triggers director exposure for civil fraud and the company's tax liabilities, including personal seizure of personal assets under court judgments.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering / Unreported International Transfers

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, an administrative monetary penalty may be imposed on a corporation (up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation, with higher amounts in persistent/egregious cases); criminal money-laundering is an indictable offence under the Criminal Code carrying penalties up to 10 years' imprisonment, and failure to register with FINTRAC exposes the business and its principals to mandated court-ordered seizure and forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who know of or negligently permit the AML breaches are personally liable for fines and restitution; they may be charged as parties to money laundering, be subjected to travel bans pending trial, and in serious cases receive custodial sentences and a finding of personal criminal participation.
