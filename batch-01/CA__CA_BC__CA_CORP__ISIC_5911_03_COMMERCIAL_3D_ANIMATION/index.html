Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement under the BCA; the corporation may be formed with shares without par value, and issued shares need not be fully paid until the board calls the balance.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the BC Fire Code for office occupancy, including portable extinguishers, emergency lighting, and a written fire safety plan where required; building occupancy permit and structural load clearance from the municipal building official for render-farm server racks and electrical installations; WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety registration with an approved injury-prevention program before employees commence work.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement under the BCA; the corporation may be formed with shares without par value, and issued shares need not be fully paid until the board calls the balance.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: (1) That the incorporation application and Notice of Articles are confirmed. (2) That the incorporator's share subscription is accepted and 100 Class A common shares without par value are allotted and issued upon payment of the subscription price. (3) That the registered office and records office are established at the Notice of Articles address in British Columbia. (4) That the directors are appointed President, Secretary, and Treasurer, each serving until the next annual general meeting. (5) That the officers are authorized to open bank accounts, execute leases and contracts, engage advisors, and complete all registrations with the Canada Revenue Agency, WorkSafeBC, and municipal authorities.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for office-based CGI, visual effects and 3D animation production; municipal licensing authority for the studio premises (e.g., City of Vancouver Business Licence Office under the Vancouver Charter or the relevant municipal office under the Community Charter).

Category: Business Licence (municipal) authorizing commercial office/creative studio use; no provincial industry-specific operating or manufacturing permit applies to ISIC 5911 activities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada. Registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA). BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC) administers the separate British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or BC Business Corporations Act via Corporations Canada or BC Corporate Online; obtain the incorporation number/BC Company Number. Step 2: Apply for the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) using BRO or Form RC1; the BN is created automatically upon registering the T2 corporate income tax program account (RC suffix). Step 3: Open program accounts on the same BN: GST/HST (RT suffix) for indirect tax, Payroll (RP suffix) if employees are engaged, and Import/Export (RM suffix) only if tangible deliverables are shipped. Step 4: For BC PST, register separately through eTaxBC / BC OneStop; no statutory minimum capital applies, non-resident shareholders are permitted, and a BC registered office and records address are mandatory. Step 5: Confirm the GST/HST registration effective date and quote the BN/RT on all invoices; a non-resident corporation with no Canadian establishment may use the CRA simplified non-resident registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope_Worldwide_Residence_Based / Foreign_Controlled_Non_CCPC / No_Pass_Through_Election_Available / BC_PST_Separate_Provincial_Regime

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA s. 156(2) to treat intercompany supplies for nil consideration; (ii) Quick Method accounting under ETA s. 227 for qualifying small suppliers; (iii) input tax credit allocation choices for mixed-use property; (iv) hybrid mismatch arrangement adjustments and elections under ITA ss. 18.2-18.6 (BEPS Action 2); (v) contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation election under ITA s. 247(4) to extend the filing deadline; (vi) treaty-based relief from Part XIII non-resident withholding via Form NR301/NR302 where cross-border dividends, interest, or royalties are paid. For a foreign-controlled corporation, the federal general corporate rate (15%) and BC general corporate rate (12%) give a combined headline rate of approximately 27%.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters (small supplier threshold, ETA s. 148); once exceeded, mandatory registration is retroactive to the first supply. BC PST: registration is required for any vendor making taxable sales in BC, with a specific CAD 10,000 annual threshold for non-resident and internet sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Commercial 3D animation/CGI services supplied by the Canadian corporation to a non-resident client qualify for zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 (services supplied to a non-resident) provided: (i) the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST; (ii) the service is not rendered to an individual present in Canada at the time of performance; (iii) the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada; and (iv) the service does not relate to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. Digital delivery of animation/VFX to offshore studios, and licensing of the associated copyright and intellectual property to a non-resident, are zero-rated export supplies under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 10, with full input tax credit entitlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) - required only where physical masters, hard drives, or tangible deliverables are shipped; for purely digital services, no customs declaration is generated and the zero-rated commercial invoice constitutes export evidence.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - retained for any tangible deliverable shipments to substantiate export and support the 0% rate.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or Wise Business statement evidencing FX repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days.
  • Signed service contract and statement of work identifying the non-resident recipient, place of supply, delivery milestones, and intellectual property transfer terms.
  • Non-resident status evidence of the recipient (e.g., foreign tax residence certificate, W-8BEN-E equivalent, or CRA non-resident confirmation).
  • GST/HST zero-rated invoice quoting the BN/RT account number, issued within 30 days of the supply.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax clearance, or digital stamping applies in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary: the CRA supports the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol BIS 3.0) as well as EDI, XML, and PDF formats. Invoices must quote the 9-digit Business Number and GST/HST program account (RT). Records must be retained for six years. BC PST must be separately indicated when applicable.

CRA audit window: four years for corporations under ITA s. 152(4), with unlimited reassessment where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default exists. Civil penalties: ETA s. 285 imposes the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax assessed for false statements or omissions; ETA ss. 280-284 add arrears interest and daily penalties for failure to register, collect, or remit. Zero-rated export refunds are clawed back with prescribed-rate interest where the non-resident supply and payment substantiation fail audit. Criminal exposure: ETA s. 327 - on conviction by indictment, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to five years. Transfer pricing overlay: ITA s. 247(2) may reallocate export revenue and s. 247(3) imposes a 10% penalty where the adjustment exceeds the threshold and arm's-length contemporaneous documentation is absent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian Dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada has maintained no general exchange-control regime since 1951. Profit remittance (dividends, management fees, royalties) is unrestricted and requires no central-bank approval; however, remittances to non-resident shareholders attract Part XIII withholding tax under Canada's Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, usually reduced to 5-15% under applicable tax treaties) and must satisfy corporate solvency and financial-condition tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or British Columbia Business Corporations Act. Capital inflows (equity injections and shareholder loans) are free of FX conversion mandates; the only non-FX hurdle is the Investment Canada Act review for cultural businesses such as 3D animation, where direct acquisitions by WTO investors above CAD 5 million in asset value require net-benefit review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A British Columbia commercial 3D-animation studio may retain USD, EUR, or other foreign-currency proceeds in Canadian Foreign Currency Accounts indefinitely and select its own commercial settlement rate. Where a bank provides trade finance or receivables discounting, the lender may contractually require assignment or collection of export proceeds as collateral, but such an obligation is purely contractual and not a central-bank regulatory mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); functional prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT transaction monitoring and registration by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); trade and export controls administered by Global Affairs Canada under the Export and Import Permits Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Conditional for non-bank cross-border platforms. Canada does not recognize a standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence comparable to the European E-Money Directive. Foreign fintechs such as Wise Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex operate as extra-provincially registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA, registered with FINTRAC, and hold client float funds in trust at a Schedule I Canadian bank. They may be used for low-to-mid-value B2B disbursements, international payroll, and USD invoice collection, but are legally constrained from direct participation in the Payments Canada clearing system, are not CDIC-insured as deposit institutions, and cannot provide Canadian credit or escrow-grade treasury facilities. Therefore, an MSB should be deployed only as a supplementary settlement channel; the primary, tax-relevant treasury rail must remain a domestic Schedule I bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder loans must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary Schedule I bank using the institution number and branch transit code, with the wire narrative referencing 'share capital contribution' or 'non-resident shareholder loan' and an investment purpose code consistent with IMF BPM6 direct-investment classification (D.3 equity / D.4 loan). To protect FX economics, instruct the payee bank to hold the incoming USD/EUR in a dual-currency Foreign Currency Account without auto-conversion; conversion occurs only when the treasury team executes a spot or forward trade. The beneficiary account legal name must exactly match the registered BC entity name to avoid FINTRAC suspicion reports and return-fee disputes. Accompanying documentation: board-approved capital-resolution, executed shareholders' agreement, source-of-funds bank reference, and, where the cultural-sector threshold is exceeded, the Investment Canada Act notification or net-benefit confirmation.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments to subcontractors, VFX vendors, and licence holders in USD/EUR/GBP; (2) Payments Canada Lynx (large-value real-time gross settlement) for high-value domestic CAD disbursements; (3) commercial documentary letters of credit issued via MT700/MT707 and standby letters of credit via MT760 for milestone-based production contracts and capital-equipment purchases from overseas vendors; (4) bank-originated FX conversion at interbank-plus-spread pricing executed under a master forward/spot agreement. For repetitive low-value artist and boutique-vendor payments, a FINTRAC-registered MSB (e.g., Wise Business) may serve as a supplementary rail, but all tax-relevant treasury balances must be reconciled daily to the CDIC-insured Schedule I operating account.Deliverable outright FX forwards up to 24 months (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) documented under an ISDA Master Agreement or Canadian Bankers Association master FX agreementCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for converting longer-term USD-denominated production funding into CAD liability exposureVanilla FX options, zero-premium collars, and participating forward structures for production-budget protection on known USD revenue streamsNon-deliverable forwards (NDF) limited strictly to restricted emerging-market currencies with no deliverable onshore market; G10 currencies must be settled through deliverable outright forwards or swaps

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Conditional for non-bank cross-border platforms. Canada does not recognize a standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence comparable to the European E-Money Directive. Foreign fintechs such as Wise Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex operate as extra-provincially registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA, registered with FINTRAC, and hold client float funds in trust at a Schedule I Canadian bank. They may be used for low-to-mid-value B2B disbursements, international payroll, and USD invoice collection, but are legally constrained from direct participation in the Payments Canada clearing system, are not CDIC-insured as deposit institutions, and cannot provide Canadian credit or escrow-grade treasury facilities. Therefore, an MSB should be deployed only as a supplementary settlement channel; the primary, tax-relevant treasury rail must remain a domestic Schedule I bank account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined (15% federal general corporate rate + 12% British Columbia general corporate rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, unless a tax treaty reduces the rate. Under the Canada–US tax treaty, the rate may be 5% for a corporate owner of at least 10% voting shares and 15% for portfolio dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 — the aggregate annual value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a non-resident that mandates filing Form T106 and maintaining contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. For transactions with a lesser value, documentation must still be prepared on a reasonable-efforts basis for the CRA's audit review; taxpayers liable to penalties under ITA section 247 must provide documentation within three months of a CRA request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the Income Tax Act applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA section 245 to disregard transactions that are primarily tax-motivated and lack genuine economic substance. A BC corporation engaged in 3D animation/VFX production must demonstrate real production activities, employees, premises, equipment, and business risk in Canada; otherwise the CRA can recharacterize transactions under the economic substance and substance-over-form doctrines, and treaty benefits may be denied under limitation-on-benefits clauses or the principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Wilful Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Offences under ITA sections 238 and 239 and Criminal Code section 239 (tax evasion); on indictment, fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. On summary conviction, fines up to 50% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to two years. Civil gross negligence penalties under ITA section 163(2) may add 50% of the understated tax, plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA section 227.1 for unremitted or evaded withholding taxes, and can be personally proceeded against for civil restitution. A director who signs false returns or participates in the evasion may face personal fines, travel bans, seizure of assets, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under ITA sections 247(2) and 247(3); a 10% penalty is automatically applied to the net transfer-pricing adjustment where contemporaneous documentation is absent or deficient. Part XIII withholding may also be assessed on deemed royalties. Interest accrues on all reassessed amounts from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally removed profit from the Canadian legal entity without a valid corporate purpose may be assessed personally if they procured transfers of assets under ITA section 160(1); they can be made liable for the value of assets transferred, and may face injunctions, travel bans, and consequences under the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act.
Negligent Failure to File T106 and Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Late and failure-to-file penalties under ITA section 162(2) and section 162(7) accrue at CAD 100 per day up to CAD 2,000 per return. A separate transfer-pricing documentation penalty under ITA section 247(3) imposes 10% of the net adjustment where documentation is incomplete, regardless of intention.

Director personal liability: Although the direct corporate penalty is payable by the corporation, directors who directed or acquiesced in non-compliance can be liable for unpaid withholding-related portions of the assessment. CRA may also raise director liability for failure to exercise care where the corporation is insufficient to satisfy penalties.
Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation and CAD 500,000 for an individual; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code section 462.31 carries imprisonment up to ten years, confiscation of proceeds, and a criminal fine.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly authorised, participated in, or failed to implement a FINTRAC compliance program can be personally prosecuted. Liability includes monetary penalties, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment; travel bans may be imposed in money-laundering investigations.
Failure to Withhold Part XIII Tax on Repatriated Profits

Statutory sanction: CRA can collect the full unwithheld Part XIII tax from the Canadian payer as if it were tax remitted by the corporation, together with interest and penalties. Trust-like liability arises under ITA subsection 227(4); directors are liable under section 227.1 for failure to withhold and remit.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the dividend resolution or authorised the foreign transfer without implementing withholding become personally liable for the full unremitted amount, plus interest and penalties. Civil enforcement may include freezing of personal assets, travel bans, and restoration orders in fraud proceedings.
