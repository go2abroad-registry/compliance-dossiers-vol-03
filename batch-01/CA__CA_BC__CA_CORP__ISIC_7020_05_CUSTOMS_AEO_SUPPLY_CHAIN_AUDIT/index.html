Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. The BCBCA imposes no minimum authorized capital or escrow deposit obligation; shares may be issued for the consideration fixed by the directors, and the Notice of Articles sets out the authorized share structure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services.html); customs counterpart: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA); BC provincial consumption taxes: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (eTaxBC portal).

E-government filing portal

(1) Determine whether the foreign corporation carries on business in Canada or makes taxable supplies in Canada (GST/HST registration duty) and whether it maintains a permanent establishment in BC. (2) Appoint a Canadian representative/agent for CRA correspondence and service of process. (3) Submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or file online via Business Registration Online; CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) ending in program-account codes: RT for GST/HST, RC for corporation income tax, RM for import/export, RP for payroll. (4) Non-resident applicants must provide a certified copy of foreign incorporation documents, registered address, director/officer identification, and BC address of record. (5) If a permanent establishment exists in BC, separately register for BC PST with the Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC. (6) For customs/AEO (PIP/C-TPAT), register for an importer/exporter account with the CBSA (BN-RM program account) and obtain a PIP approval where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; corporations are discrete taxpayers under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada). Residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed on Canadian-source business income and certain capital gains, with an additional 25% Part XIV branch tax on after-tax profits (reducible by treaty). No pass-through characterization is available for a business corporation.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. Available elections: (1) ITA s. 216(1) election for non-residents to report net rental/investment income under Part I in lieu of Part XIII withholding; (2) ITA s. 219(1) branch-tax remittance election; (3) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) for qualifying small suppliers; (4) GST/HST annual filing election (Form GST22) for net revenue not exceeding CAD 400,000; (5) GST/HST instalment-filing election; (6) hybrid-mismatch compliance is governed by ITA s. 18.4 (OECD BEPS Action 2) — deduction disallowance applies without an elective opt-out, but taxpayers may file prescribed forms to evidence statutory exceptions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada, excluding zero-rated exports, over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory on the first day of the calendar month following the month a supplier ceases to be a small supplier; foreign suppliers may also be required to register when carrying on business in Canada even below the threshold if they are selected vendors or have a permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Excise Tax Act Sch. VI Part V s. 10 when the supplier has documentary evidence that the goods were exported in accordance with CRA Policy P-208 and B-085, normally within 180 days of the sale. Services (AEO supply-chain auditing/consultancy): a supply made to a non-resident is zero-rated under Sch. VI Part V s. 23 unless the service is in respect of Canadian-situ real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or is acquired for consumption/use in Canada and actually so consumed/used; the supplier must secure a signed non-resident certificate (Form GST464 or equivalent), a copy of the service contract, and proof the consideration was received from outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or G7 eManifest EDI transmission for export shipments above CAD 2,000)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (carrier-issued transport document naming the foreign consignee and destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank/SWIFT MT103 evidence that the non-resident purchaser settled the consideration)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (itemized export description, HS classification, value, and destination)
  • Signed Non-Resident Certificate and Service Agreement (for zero-rated consultancy/audit services, certifying the recipient is not resident in Canada and the supply is consumed outside Canada)

No mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance or digital stamping exists in Canada. CRA mandates electronic filing (GST/HST NETFILE) for registrants with gross annual revenue exceeding CAD 1 million; all other registrants may file electronically. E-invoicing is voluntary and must satisfy CRA record-retention standards (legible, chronological, complete and audit-ready six-year retention). CBSA requires electronic export declarations for goods above CAD 2,000 through the Canadian Export Reporting System (G7 eManifest/CESR); importers must transmit customs data by EDI (CUSANSWER) or portal. No CRA-approved API invoice clearance or fiscal stamp requirement applies.

CRA audits export zero-rating strictly: failure to produce the documentary bundle within the prescribed period leads to reassessment of 5% GST/HST on the gross supply plus interest; willful or gross-negligent claims attract ETA s. 285 (25% penalty), ETA s. 281 (summary conviction fine up to CAD 25,000 and up to 200% of the evaded tax), or Customs Act s. 127.1 penalties for false export declarations (up to CAD 25,000 or 5% of goods value), with retrospective clawback covering up to 6 years for ordinary negligence and no statutory limitation for misrepresentation. Canada imposes no legal FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is a commercial/audit standard, but unexplained foreign receivables trigger transfer-pricing and GAAR reassessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unencumbered current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. The Canadian dollar floats freely; no exchange-control approvals, registration gates, or repatriation licenses apply to corporate treasury flows. Non-resident profit remittance is lawful and immediate, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at the 25% statutory rate, typically reduced by applicable tax treaty. For a BC AEO/supply-chain audit firm, there are no structural FX convertibility hurdles; the operative compliance layer is anti-money-laundering record-keeping and the Income Tax Act foreign-exchange gain recognition, not exchange-control permissioning.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation quota exists in Canada. Exporters and cross-border service providers may retain export and fee receipts in CAD or in foreign-currency accounts at any eligible deposit-taking institution. The only mandatory reporting is financial-institution-led: FINTRAC large transaction reports and EFT reports apply to wire transfers at or above CAD 10,000, and CBSA cross-border currency declarations apply to physical movement of CAD 10,000 or more. These are surveillance requirements, not conversion or surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-9), is the monetary, settlement, and central clearing authority; the Department of Finance Canada owns and directs foreign exchange reserve policy; OSFI supervises federally regulated banking institutions; FINTRAC administers AML/CFT transaction reporting. No separate Foreign Exchange Administration exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-to-High only as a supplementary disbursement channel for low-value recurring payments, e.g., Wise Business operating as a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business and a Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) payment service provider supervised by the Bank of Canada. Legal viability is adequate, but it is Low as a standalone treasury platform: no CDIC deposit insurance, no direct LYNX settlement membership, and new business accounts may carry prolonged hold periods or account-verification friction. A BC AEO audit firm should retain a Tier-1 domestic DDA for capital, statutory reserves, and high-value clearing; Wise or an equivalent EMI may be linked as an operational recurring-payment rail only after six months of clean Tier-1 banking history and a documented treasury risk assessment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Execute via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the CAD operating account at a Tier-1 BC branch of RBC, TD, BMO, or Scotiabank; field 59 must contain the exact BC corporation legal name and CAD account number, and field 70 should read 'Share subscription / paid-in capital under BC Business Corporations Act'. Domestic settlement occurs over LYNX/ACSS, with US-sourced funds typically arriving through Fedwire and a Canadian correspondent before final LYNX settlement. No Bank of Canada or FINTRAC pre-approval is required; the receiving bank performs standard corporate-identity verification, files a large transaction report at the CAD 10,000 threshold, and will request the share subscription agreement, board resolution, and directors' identification as the verification package. For USD-denominated injections, route through the bank's USD nostro or correspondent (e.g., BNY Mellon/JPMorgan for RBC) and either maintain the USD segment or convert at the agreed treasury rate; for restricted-convertibility currencies, convert first into USD or EUR in the offshore corridor and then wire clean CAD to the beneficiary account.Base rail is the SWIFT MT103 cross-border wire for professional-fee settlements to foreign subcontractors and AEO-audit consultants; high-value domestic payables settle through LYNX/ACSS in CAD, while USD payables should clear via the bank's US correspondent network to avoid double conversion. For recurring payments under CAD 25,000 per transaction, Wise's safeguarded e-money rail is legally permissible and cost-effective under its RPAA/FINTRAC status; engagement confidentiality and audit-trail retention remain the client's duty. Commercial letters of credit are applicable only to physical procurement supporting AEO platform security hardware, where an irrevocable sight LC may be opened at the RBC/CIBC trade desk; LCs are not appropriate for professional service fees. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal (15%) and British Columbia provincial (12%) general corporate rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, absent treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the 6th month immediately following the fiscal year-end. For a December 31 year-end, the return is due by June 30 of the following calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Canadian taxpayer must prepare and maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File/Master File) for all transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents if: (i) aggregate fair market value of cross-border transactions in a taxation year exceeds CAD 1 million (for goods, services, intangibles, leases), or (ii) outstanding loans/debts from non-residents exceed CAD 1 million (requiring Form T106). The CRA sets a separate penalty threshold: gross negligence penalty applies where transfer pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue of the taxpayer and CAD 4 million, or CAD 1 million in documented mispricing. Country-by-country reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of CAD 800 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. The CRA applies the domestic general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), and treaty benefit eligibility requires a 'resident person' with substantive business activity and beneficial ownership. For a BC-based AEO consultant engaged in cross-border supply-chain audit services, the entity must demonstrate presence, active management, and actual commercial functions in Canada; a shell company with no business substance may be denied treaty benefits and face reassessment under section 245 GAAR or the 'sham' transaction doctrine. Federal and provincial rules also require a registered Canadian address, audited financial statements, and active directors for corporate maintenance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Concealment of Income

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act, s.238(1) summary conviction: fine up to CAD 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months. Indictable offence s.239(1): fine up to 200% of tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross negligence penalty under s.163.2: 50% of the understated tax. Interest is continuously compounded.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax remittances under ITA s.227.1(1) if the corporation fails to deduct or withhold amounts, or fails to report with intent. Courts will 'pierce the corporate veil' where the director personally participated in fraudulent misrepresentation, diverted trust funds, or knowingly transferred assets out of the corporation. Directors may face civil restitution, travel bans (under Canada's fugitive mechanism abroad via Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties), and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may vacate treaty residence and recharacterize the entity as a conduit; GAAR reassessment applies a 20% gross-error penalty under s.163.2, and the company may be struck from the British Columbia corporate registry under the Business Corporations Act for failure to comply with annual filings (Annual Report and registered office requirements).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allowed the shell to make fraudulent dividend claims or misrepresent its substance to the AEO customs authority are liable for the full amount of dividends paid plus interest. In extreme cases, the BC Supreme Court may order a director's assets to be traced and garnished; the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) can recommend criminal prosecution under the Customs Act for AEO accreditation misuse.
AML / Money Laundering (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Under PCMLTFA, administrative monetary penalties: up to CAD 1 million for an individual and CAD 6 million for a corporation. Criminal offence for 'instructing the commission of a money laundering offence' can lead to imprisonment up to 10 years and fine in the court's discretion. Wash-trading in real estate/invoice pass-through schemes is treated as predicate offence under the Criminal Code s.462.31.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failing to implement a compliant AML program only if they directed or participated in laundering transactions. Under the Criminal Code, an officer/ director with supervisory authority who knew or was willfully blind of the transaction can be indicted, subject to asset freeze, travel restrictions, and extradition. FINTRAC compliance officers are also personally subject to penalties.
Transfer Pricing Misdocumentation

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to revise income to the arm's-length amount; gross negligence penalty under s.163.2 is 10% of the net upward adjustment (minimum CAD 1,000) and applies to each taxation year. The T106 penalty is CAD 100 per month per transaction, subject to CAD 2,500 maximum; multiple transactions can reach CAD 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false T106 fits within the definition of 'culpable conduct' and may be assessed personally under s.163.2 as 'participants.' For a director to be personally liable for the corporate penalty, the CRA must establish the director directed or approved a transaction knowing it was mispriced. Disqualification as a customs AEO operator is likely.
Unremitted Withholding Taxes

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.227.8, the amount required to be withheld is immediately a debt due; failure to remit triggers a civil penalty equal to 20% of the unremitted amount if unpaid by the 15th of the following month. Daily interest at prescribed rates; the penalty is increased to 50% where the failure is made knowingly. Criminal prosecution under s.242 applies for willful contravention of withholding obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors are absolutely liable for the unremitted withholding amounts under ITA s.227.1(1) if they were directors at the time the tax was required to be remitted and the corporation cannot pay. The CRA may issue a director's liability assessment and pursue civil recovery; a director who moves assets outside the reach of Canadian courts may be subject to a Canadian judgment recognized in foreign jurisdictions, including travel bans where criminal charges are laid. The time limitation is 3 years from the assessment.
