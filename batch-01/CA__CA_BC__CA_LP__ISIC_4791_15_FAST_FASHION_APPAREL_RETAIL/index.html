Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal tax registration and GST/HST portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered by the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

A BC limited partnership (CA_LP) with non-resident or foreign partners must: (1) confirm whether it is carrying on business in Canada and whether it has a GST/HST obligation; (2) apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, providing the BC LP registration certificate, partnership agreement, and each partner's identifying and tax identification details; (3) obtain the BN program accounts (e.g., RT for GST/HST, RC for income tax, RP for payroll, RM/RD for customs) as needed; (4) register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST through eTaxBC on Form BC-BAS if taxable goods are sold to BC consumers; (5) appoint a Canadian resident representative for service and tax administration if the partnership does not maintain a physical Canadian presence; (6) comply with periodic filing requirements and record retention (typically six years); and (7) collect and report each non-resident partner's TIN for treaty, FATCA, and CRS purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a BC limited partnership is treated by statute under section 96 of the Income Tax Act as a fiscally transparent pass-through arrangement, not an entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections relevant to cross-border retail include: (i) the section 216 election for non-resident partners with Canadian rental or timber royalty income; (ii) the section 217 optional return election for non-residents receiving certain Canadian-source amounts; (iii) branch income reporting under section 115 for non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada; (iv) partnership continuity and deemed-disposition elections on admissions, withdrawals, and winding-up; and (v) compliance with hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.15 to 18.18 of the Income Tax Act, which disallow deductions for hybrid mismatch payments without any elective entity reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST small-supplier threshold); for non-resident e-commerce and platform suppliers selling to Canadian consumers, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once Canadian-source taxable revenue exceeds CAD 30,000. BC PST has no general registration threshold: any person making taxable retail sales or leases of goods in BC must register for PST before commencing such sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier exports the property or delivers it to a common carrier, customs broker, or freight forwarder for export, and the goods leave Canada within the applicable timeframe. Services supplied to non-residents are generally zero-rated where the non-resident is not a GST/HST registrant and the service is not performed in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property physically situated in Canada at the time of performance. Fast-fashion e-commerce export sales qualify for zero-rating where title and risk of loss pass to the foreign buyer, shipment is evidenced by a bill of lading or waybill to a foreign destination, and export proceeds are repatriated to the Canadian partnership.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA form B13A/B13B or electronically transmitted export entry)
  • Bill of Lading or waybill/airwaybill showing foreign destination and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank credit/forex advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer

There is no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoice mandate in Canada. GST/HST registrants must retain invoices, contracts, and supporting export documents; returns must be filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account where annual gross revenue exceeds CAD 1.5 million. PST returns must be filed through eTaxBC. E-commerce platform operators may be jointly liable for GST/HST collection under the place-of-supply rules and must maintain Canadian tax registration evidence.

The Canada Revenue Agency may reassess GST/HST returns within four years of the filing due date, extended to seven years where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation, failed to report, or committed fraud. Where zero-rating is denied, CRA may claw back the zero-rated treatment, assess unremitted GST/HST, deny corresponding input tax credits, and impose interest plus a 50% gross-negligence penalty under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act. Deliberate fraudulent export claims are indictable offences under subsection 239(1), carrying substantial fines, imprisonment, deregistration, and joint and several liability for partners of the limited partnership.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains no exchange-control law. The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible, freely floating currency; current-account flows (online fast-fashion retail receipts and apparel import settlements) and capital-account flows (capital contributions to a British Columbia limited partnership, partner distributions, loan principal repayments) are unrestricted and require no central-bank licence or conversion permit. Profit remittances to non-resident partners are freely transferable in CAD or foreign currency; however, Canada Revenue Agency compliance applies, including potential Part XIII withholding tax on partnership distributions or deemed remittance amounts unless reduced by treaty, with valid Canadian-residence and treaty-entitlement documentation on file.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, no forced repatriation timeline, and no central-bank approval for retaining export receipts offshore. Export proceeds may remain in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency. Reporting obligations are limited to customs/statistical entry filing and FINTRAC large-value electronic funds transfer reporting for transactions at or above C$10,000; such reports do not restrict the treasury's liquidity mobility or clearing choices.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-control policy resides with the Department of Finance Canada; prudential supervision of banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing transactional surveillance is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Regulated cross-border fintech and electronic money institution providers (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are feasible as secondary disbursement rails; feasibility rating is Medium. They are not Canadian chartered banks, cannot hold the master settlement account for Shopify Payments, and have no direct access to Lynx high-value clearing; therefore the limited partnership must maintain a Schedule I bank account as its treasury anchor. Offshore or unlicensed EMI use for Canadian-facing CAD settlement is legally non-compliant without FINTRAC money-services-business registration and will also impede Klarna BNPL onboarding viability.
