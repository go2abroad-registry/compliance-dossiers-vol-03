Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portfolio; BC Ministry of Finance administers the Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through eTaxBC, separate from the federal GST/HST

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA's Business Registration Online service or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with a schedule listing each partner. The partnership uses the BN to open a GST/HST account (RC account) and, if required, a separate BC PST account via eTaxBC. For non-resident partnerships, evidence of a Canadian agent/member and completed Form RC372 (Non-Resident Requirement) may be necessary; identification documents for all partners, the partnership agreement, and the province of registration in BC (through BC Corporate Online / BC Registry) are typically required.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign limited partnership must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA's Business Registration Online service or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with a schedule listing each partner. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) a partnership may elect to be separate GST/HST registrant (section 21). Partners may file elections under the Income Tax Act respecting fiscal period alignment and the allocation of income or loss among partners. There is no US-style check-the-box election; Canadian hybrid mismatch rules in section 18.4 of the Income Tax Act may apply to deny a deduction for certain hybrid payments, and the rules require a 'designated party' determination. Limited partnership agreements may contain income tax allocation elections and liability apportionment clauses.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual taxable supplies in Canada (small supplier threshold); non-residents meeting or exceeding CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada during a calendar year are required to register for GST/HST

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods such as drummed bitumen and asphalt are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act where the supplier ships or delivers the goods to the purchaser or the purchaser's agent for export and the goods are exported as soon as practicable. The supply of freight, insurance, and handling services directly related to the exported goods may also be zero-rated. For BC PST, a sale for export outside BC or outside Canada is generally exempt from PST when the goods are delivered by a common carrier, delivered to an export carrier, or the seller ships directly to a non-resident for delivery outside Canada. For exports carried out by the foreign LP from BC, evidence must include a customs export declaration, bills of lading, and proof of storage or shipment without use or consumption in Canada.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form A64/CAED)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or compulsory e-invoicing mandate exists under CRA/BC rules; GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically (except limited exemptions) and retain invoices in paper or electronic format; BC PST returns are submitted through eTaxBC. Large filers may use EDI/API-based filing with CRA; there is no pre-clearance of individual invoices.

CRA may deny zero-rating if the goods were not actually exported or if documentation fails to evidence shipment within a reasonable time; supplies are then reclassified as taxable at 5% GST plus applicable BC PST. Reassessments can be issued up to four years after the original return for ordinary errors, and indefinitely for fraud, wilful misrepresentation, or omission. Penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act include 25% of the tax amount for false statements and a 50% gross-negligence penalty for reckless conduct; fraudulent GST/HST claims may also trigger criminal prosecution under section 327, with fines and imprisonment. The absence of FX repatriation within 180 days may lead to a deeming that the transaction was not an actual export and that the zero-rating should be revoked, resulting in clawback of any ITCs claimed, plus interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Canadian dollar. There are no exchange controls, surrender requirements, or licensing rules for cross-border trade payments. Profit remittances by a British Columbia Limited Partnership to non-resident partners are generally free from exchange-control hurdles; however, such distributions may be subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (typically 25% absent treaty relief) if characterized as dividends or investment income, and must pass through Canada’s anti-money laundering screening and FATF-compliant reporting. Trade-related payments are unrestricted, and only high-risk transactions require additional due diligence documentation. The Bank of Canada intervenes in foreign exchange markets solely to preserve orderly conditions, not to restrict convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists under the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, or any Canadian foreign-exchange regulation. Exporters of bitumen and asphalt products may lawfully retain all export proceeds in foreign-currency-denominated bank accounts held in Canada or abroad, convert them freely at market rates, or use them to settle import obligations. Nonetheless, Canadian customs and tax law require accurate reporting of export transactions on Form B13A and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declarations, and all incoming or outgoing remittances exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC under PCMLTFA obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the designated cross-border financial intelligence unit; Department of Finance Canada for exchange rate policy and regulatory oversight

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with material legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) can provide fast multi-currency payment routing but generally do not issue documentary credits or structured trade finance instruments commonly required for containerized bitumen wholesale. Any entity offering cross-border money services, foreign exchange, payment processing, or digital wallet services in Canada must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and comply with provincial consumer protection requirements in British Columbia. For a BC Limited Partnership engaged in wholesale bitumen trading, traditional Schedule I Canadian banks are strongly preferred to maintain LC issuance, cash management, and correspondent banking relationships. An offshore EMI may be used only as a secondary ancillary rail, provided the EMI holds an MSB registration in Canada or operates through a licensed Canadian bank partner; otherwise, remittance blockers may arise for large commercial wires.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a BC Limited Partnership being capitalized by a foreign parent or limited partner, all inbound wires must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with clear payment purpose, ideally using a major Canadian bank's Toronto, Montreal, or Vancouver clear centre. The beneficiary account should be designated either as a Capital Escrow Account (to hold equity/debt contributions strictly for approved uses such as asphalt inventory purchases) or an Operational Account. Compliance protocols include: (i) remittance must reference the LP's commercial name and the specific terms of the Limited Partnership Agreement; (ii) the purpose code should identify 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'LONG-TERM LOAN' with accompanying loan agreement if applicable; (iii) for payments exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank will file a Large Cash Transaction/Electronic Funds Transfer report to FINTRAC; (iv) the sending bank must complete SWIFT MT103 fields such as /INV/ references, bank-to-bank instructions, and full legal names to avoid sanitisation; (v) currency conversion should be executed at the receiving bank's spot rate unless a benefit forward is pre-arranged; (vi) supporting documentation must include the LP registration certificate, capital call notice, board resolution of the General Partner, and source-of-funds statements from the investing entity. If the injection is for a capital escrow arrangement held by a third-party escrow trustee, a tri-party escrow agreement and instruction matrix must be pre-cleared with the Canadian bank's treasury department.Outbound settlement for bitumen and asphalt product purchases through British Columbia will primarily use two clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for advance deposits, freight payments, and smaller spot purchases where the counterparty accepts TT; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits) issued by a Schedule I Canadian bank for large bulk imports, usually payable at sight or with a deferred payment tenor. Each disbursement must include: HS Code and ISIC classification in the invoice reference (e.g., ISIC 4661 / NAICS 424720 for bitumen wholesale); Incoterms 2020 rule (CIF, FOB, CFR) matching the underlying transport document; authorized signatory approval evidenced by dual-signing for amounts above CAD 50,000; bank-specific payment templates for recurring commodity suppliers; and full FATF-compliant counterparty due diligence. For outbound trade disbursements routed through a correspondent bank, the Canadian bank will issue an MT103 with proper bank identifier, purpose code (e.g., 'TRADE PAYMENT'), and a supporting SWIFT advisory (MT199) where the payment is tied to a commercial LC.
