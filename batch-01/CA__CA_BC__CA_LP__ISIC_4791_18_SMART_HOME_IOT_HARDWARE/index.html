Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online / My Business Account portal (GST/HST Program Account 'RT' and Income Tax Program Account for partnerships); BC Ministry of Finance — Consumer Taxation Branch, eTaxBC portal for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a non-resident Limited Partnership) doing taxable business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before filing GST/HST and income tax. Application is made through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), by mail, or by telephone. The partnership uses the BN plus a program account code (e.g., 'RT' for GST/HST) and separately registers with BC's eTaxBC for a PST number if taxable retail sales are made in British Columbia and no exemption applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity (including a non-resident Limited Partnership) doing taxable business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before filing GST/HST and income tax. Application is made through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), by mail, or by telephone. The partnership uses the BN plus a program account code (e.g., 'RT' for GST/HST) and separately registers with BC's eTaxBC for a PST number if taxable retail sales are made in British Columbia and no exemption applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes, with members taxed directly on allocated shares under the Income Tax Act and BC Income Tax Act. Certain administrative elections exist for partnership fiscal year-end and for GST/HST purposes, where a partnership is treated as a separate registrant and may, under arrangements with CRA, elect to file GST/HST returns as one registrant with joint responsibility. Hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules in the Income Tax Act (sections 18.2 and 18.3) may apply to deny inappropriate mismatching benefits; no elective check-the-box regime applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies imposed by GST/HST ETA; BC PST registration normally required for non-small sellers with PST taxable sales exceeding CAD 10,000 in a calendar year (special marketplace rules apply).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST may be zero-rated under ETA s. 22 for supplies of goods that the vendor sells for export, exports as part of the sale, or has delivered outside Canada to a non-resident where documentary evidence establishes exportation and the goods are not subsequently supplied in Canada before export. BC PST does not apply if the goods are delivered outside BC in the seller's ordinary course of business or the contract requires delivery outside BC. Services ancillary to exported goods supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated if eligibility criteria are met.

  • B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration (electronic via CBSA)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Packing List
  • Transport/Logistics evidence ending with foreign delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of payment from non-resident purchaser where used to substantiate cross-border consideration

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping exists in Canada or British Columbia. CRA and BC Ministry accept electronic invoices provided they are legible, complete, and retained for 6 years. GST/HST returns must be filed through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or approved web service, and BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC. There is no government pre-approval of invoice formats or API-based fiscal invoice clearance.

CRA and BC Ministry can deny zero-rating and reassess tax, interest, and penalties if export evidence is inadequate, documents are false, or transactions lack commercial substance. ETA penalties include penalties for false/omitted statements under sections 280-285, which can be up to 6% of the net tax or a minimum of CAD 250, plus interest. Tax evasion prosecution can result in fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment under Income Tax Act s. 239. Retrospective clawbacks apply to fraudulent export claims, and directors or partners may be jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency and currently does not impose exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for trade, capital injections, distributions, dividend remittances and liquidation proceeds, with no prior approval requirement from the Bank of Canada or the Minister of Finance. Practical hurdles in cross-border treasury flows are AML/KYC-driven and tax-driven: FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for foreign wires of CAD 10,000 or more, source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership verification, sanctions screening, and Canadian withholding-tax documentation for non-resident limited partners receiving income or partnership distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements, mandatory repatriation windows, or fixed conversion ratios under Canadian federal law or British Columbia provincial law. An ISIC 4791 smart-IoT retail entity is free to retain foreign currency receipts in an approved foreign-currency bank account and may convert them at commercial market rates. Exports from Canada must still be reported through CBSA export declarations and trade records where applicable, and banks independently report cross-border electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the central bank, while federal prudential supervision over banks and foreign-bank branches in Canada is performed by OSFI. Cross-border wire transfers and money services businesses are monitored and regulated by FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. The Canadian high-value payment and settlement system (Lynx) is operated by Payments Canada. British Columbia has no provincial central bank or separate exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium viability as the sole treasury infrastructure. An offshore digital EMI cannot directly provide Canadian-dollar clearing, Lynx RTGS participation, SWIFT BIC-based CAD settlement, cheques, or a domestic bank account with CDIC deposit coverage. If the EMI performs payment services connected with Canadian e-commerce or money transfers, it will be subject to registration supervision under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada and must also register with FINTRAC as a money services business. Therefore the preferred structure is to use an offshore EMI only as a collection or international payout gateway, while maintaining a Schedule I Canadian chartered-bank account as the primary capital, settlement and clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners should be executed through a high-value SWIFT MT103 credit transfer to the BC limited partnership's designated capital-escrow or operational account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The SWIFT field 70 remittance information must clearly state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - BC LIMITED PARTNERSHIP' and identify the entity registration number. The receiving bank will classify the transfer for FINTRAC large-electronic-funds-transfer reporting, for OSFI monitoring and for Canadian balance-of-payments statistics under direct investment in Canada. No Bank of Canada approval is required, but the LP should have ready a capital-call notice, a subscription ledger, a general-partner resolution, source-of-funds evidence and a BC corporate-registry search before the bank releases funds into an operating account.
