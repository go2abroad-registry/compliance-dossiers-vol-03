Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a British Columbia limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No government escrow account or capital import certificate is required, including for non-resident limited partners.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the BC Fire Code, including annual fire inspection, alarm and suppression system testing; structural load clearance under the BC Building Code for cleanroom and needle-assembly equipment; WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety clearance confirming workplace hazard assessment, machine guarding, and chemical safety protocols; and a municipal building occupancy permit issued before commencement of production.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a British Columbia limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No government escrow account or capital import certificate is required, including for non-resident limited partners.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; federal tax portal: CRA My Business Account; provincial sales tax authority: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, BCeTax portal (www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership (LP) must first obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or through the CRA online registration service. The LP must maintain a valid BC limited partnership registration with the BC Registry and provide its partnership declaration/foreign registration evidence. If the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada that exceed CAD 30,000 per rolling four consecutive calendar quarters (or in a single quarter), it must register for a GST/HST Program Account under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. For British Columbia provincial sales tax, the LP must separately register with the BC Ministry of Finance through BCeTax before selling taxable goods or services at retail in BC, unless it qualifies as an out-of-province seller with annual taxable sales into BC of CAD 10,000 or less, in which case mandatory registration may be deferred; voluntary PST registration is available.

The LP is not an income taxpayer; its income or loss is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners annually on the T5013 Partnership Information Return. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A British Columbia Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Canadian Income Tax Act (ITA), specifically ITA s.96 and s.102. The LP is not an income taxpayer; its income or loss is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners annually on the T5013 Partnership Information Return. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are subject to Part I income tax on their share of the partnership income attributable to the Canadian business, and certain Canadian-source payments and distributions may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax or treaty-related reduction. The LP is also an eligible limited liability vehicle for non-resident ownership; capital contributions are not subject to statutory minimum capital requirements.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election or a free-standing entity classification election for federal tax purposes. A limited partnership's pass-through status arises from domestic partnership law and ITA s.96, not by election. Available elections relevant to an LP include: an election under ITA s.216 for certain non-resident partners to report Canadian rental income on a net basis rather than by Part XIII withholding; a partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s.249.1(7); GST/HST net-tax election for closely related entities or joint venture elections; and hybrid mismatch arrangement reporting/notification requirements under ITA ss.18.4 and 18.5, which contain no partnership reclassification election. No election exists to treat the LP as a corporation or as a disregarded entity for Canadian income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 (taxable supplies in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters or a single quarter); BC PST: no general threshold for a person with a place of business in BC; non-resident/out-of-province sellers whose annual taxable sales into BC are CAD 10,000 or less are not required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.1, a supply of tangible personal property (surgical suture needle assemblies) is zero-rated where the supplier: (1) sells the goods under a verbal or written agreement of sale; (2) causes the goods to be delivered or shipped directly to a non-resident purchaser or to a carrier for export to a non-Canadian destination; and (3) obtains and retains documentary evidence showing the goods left Canada in a continuous journey and were permanently absent from Canada. For services performed in Canada for a non-resident customer, zero-rating is available under Schedule VII if the services are directly tied to the exported goods or are of a type supplied to the non-resident in connection with businesses carried on outside Canada. The LP must also ensure that BC PST is not charged on export sales; BC PST generally does not impose tax on tangible personal property sold for delivery outside BC to a foreign destination, provided the seller maintains proof of export. A claimed zero-rating is conditional on timely documentary substantiation; otherwise CRA will reassess the supply at the applicable domestic rate (12% combined, but GST at 5% and PST separately) plus interest.

  • CBSA export declaration or eManifest confirmation (Canadian Automated Export Declaration or carrier/agent report) showing the goods are leaving Canada
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air/rail) or multimodal waybill issued to the foreign purchaser or its agent, with a stated foreign port/place of discharge
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Canadian LP to the non-resident customer showing full sale details and terms
  • Foreign purchase order / contract and any amendment confirming the export sale
  • Proof of payment in foreign currency (SWIFT MT103, inward wire advice, letter of credit settlement, or banking credit advice) although no statutory FX repatriation certificate is prescribed under Canadian law
  • Cargo control record and carrier certificate of export from the shipping line/customs broker

Canada and British Columbia do not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice authorization. Vendors may issue paper or electronic invoices, but must retain invoices and records in a machine-readable format acceptable to CRA for six years (or longer if objection/appeal pending). BC PST requires invoices to disclose the PST amount separately or to show that the sale is exempt/comprised of zero-rated exports. B2B EDI and third-party electronic exchange is permitted without any prior government clearance.

CRA may conduct an export verification audit and disallow zero-rating if the required evidence is incomplete or contradictions in dates or quantities are found. Reassessment may impose GST/HST at the applicable 5% or 12% rate, plus section 33(2) interest (generally 6% in BC according to the prescribed rate) and late penalties. Under ETA s.285.1, knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence making a false or fraudulent claim of exports may trigger a penalty equal to 50% of the amount of the refund or credit that would have resulted; failure to collect or remit tax attracts a 10% penalty under s.280; conviction on indictment for tax evasion can result in a fine up to CAD 400,000 and imprisonment up to 24 months. In BC, the Ministry of Finance can reassess PST for up to two years after the end of the taxation year, or four years in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect, and may assess an additional penalty not exceeding the tax due. The LP should retain the export proof bundle for at least six years from the end of the calendar year in which the supply is made.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for both the current account and the capital account. Canada does not operate an exchange-control system or maintain surrender requirements for exporter proceeds. Non-resident profit remittances from a British Columbia limited partnership are generally subject to Part XIII withholding tax on distributions treated as income or deemed interest/dividends to non-residents, with treaty relief available. No formal central-bank approval is required for capital repatriation, but banks enforce PCMLTFA/FINTRAC reporting thresholds on transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not mandate conversion, repatriation or surrender of export earnings, nor does it impose a statutory timeline for receipt of export proceeds. Exporters may retain and manage foreign currency freely. Financial institutions are obligated to record and may report electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Department of Finance Canada; no separate Foreign Exchange Control Administration exists. Foreign-exchange policy and payment-system oversight are exercised by the Bank of Canada, while deposit-taking institutions are supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and money-services businesses by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Non-bank fintechs and electronic money institutions cannot take deposits or issue bank accounts unless authorized as deposit-taking institutions. Offshore EMIs may register as money-services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC for e-money transmission and payment facilitation, but their corporate treasury services are narrower and settlement finality is weaker than chartered-bank rails. A BC limited partnership should use a tier-1 bank as primary treasury and an MSB/EMI only as an ancillary collection channel.
