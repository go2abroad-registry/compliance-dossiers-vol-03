Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement. Limited partners must contribute cash, property, or agreed value as recorded in the Limited Partnership Agreement; the general partner manages the contributions in trust and records each limited partner's capital account for pass-through tax allocation.

Funding & unlock path: Occupational health and safety clearance under the Workers Compensation Act and WorkSafeBC regulations; fire safety certification under the BC Fire Code for warehouse/storage occupancy; structural load inspections for pallet racking under the BC Building Code; municipal building occupancy certificate for the premises. Civil-defense-specific clearances are not required.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted; inward equity contributions to and profit remittances from a British Columbia limited partnership require no central-bank approval or convertibility license. Non-resident partner distributions may be subject to Canadian tax, withholding, and reporting obligations, but not exchange-control barriers. Payments to sanctioned jurisdictions/parties are restricted by Canadian economic sanctions law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory deduction, and no conversion timeline for export proceeds. A BC exporter may invoice in CAD, USD, or other freely convertible currencies and may retain foreign currency abroad or in domestic foreign-currency bank accounts. FINTRAC reporting applies to electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more, and commercial transaction records should evidence the underlying garment purchase and resale to prevent trade-based money laundering.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential supervision of Schedule I banks); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML/CFT transaction reporting); Global Affairs Canada (economic sanctions administration). Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control bureaucracy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary regulated banking corridor. Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not chartered banks in Canada and cannot provide OSFI-regulated deposit accounts. A non-resident EMI may be used only as a secondary payment aggregator/wallet in its own jurisdiction, subject to its licensing, and should not hold Canadian client funds or present itself as a Canadian bank. Correspondent concentration with a tier-1 Canadian bank remains the compliant route.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque beneficial ownership in the limited partnership structure when no individual holds a clear 25%+ interest or control is split among non-resident limited partners.

Root cause: Opaque beneficial ownership in the limited partnership structure when no individual holds a clear 25%+ interest or control is split among non-resident limited partners.

Defense evidence: Executed limited partnership agreement; current register of individuals with significant control; organizational chart from ultimate beneficial owners through each intermediate entity; proof of identity and residential address for all 25%+ partners; general partner declaration of control; certificate of existence/good standing from BC Registry.
Sanctions, PEP status, or adverse-country exposure arising from supplier/payment jurisdictions used for overstock apparel sourcing.

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP status, or adverse-country exposure arising from supplier/payment jurisdictions used for overstock apparel sourcing.

Defense evidence: Global Affairs Canada sanctions screening results for all parties; independent open-source adverse-media report; PEP declarations for every beneficial owner; fund-sourcing explanation supported by bank statements and audited/compiled financial statements; jurisdiction-specific legal entity due diligence for each supplier jurisdiction.
High-volume cross-border trade payments without visible nexus to physical goods, triggering trade-based money-laundering suspicion.

Root cause: High-volume cross-border trade payments without visible nexus to physical goods, triggering trade-based money-laundering suspicion.

Defense evidence: Signed purchase orders and sales contracts; commercial invoices; packing lists; bills of lading/waybills; customs entry forms (Canada CBSA), inspection certificates; payment reconciliation schedule matching invoices to MT103 remittances; supplier onboarding file confirming vendor existence.
Use of third-party payments or encrypted/fast-payment channels inconsistent with the stated wholesale liquidation business model.

Root cause: Use of third-party payments or encrypted/fast-payment channels inconsistent with the stated wholesale liquidation business model.

Defense evidence: Written ACH/SWIFT-only payment policy; third-party payment register identifying purpose, contractual basis, and ultimate counterparty; prohibition of cash and virtual-currency settlement for trade disbursements; annual compliance attestation by the general partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity/capital injections should be routed as SWIFT MT103 payments from the foreign parent/limited partner to the BC LP's tier-1 Canadian bank account. The wire must reference the LP's legal name, the designated account number, and a clear purpose description such as 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION / LP CAPITAL CALL'. Supporting documentation for the bank file should include the capital call notice, LP agreement, source of funds in the remitting account, and a Canadian filing/record confirming the LP registration. FINTRAC EFT reporting obligations are generally discharged by the bank, but the LP must maintain its own records of all inbound transfers of C$10,000 or more. There is no Canadian FX approval requirement; USD funds may settle through Fedwire/CHIPS and be converted to CAD at market rate.Outbound supplier payments for finished garments should be made by SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) or by commercial letter of credit (UCP600) for documentary shipments. For domestic Canadian suppliers, Payments Canada ACH/credit-push and EFT rails are accepted. USD vendor payments may be cleared through the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent network (Fedwire/ACH). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (15% federal + 12% British Columbia provincial) for general corporations; limited partnership is pass-through, partners taxed at individual or corporate rates

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents; for limited partnership distributions to non-resident partners, the withholding tax applies to the partner's share of Canadian-source income at a rate of 25% (Part XIII of the Income Tax Act)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transactions with non-resident related parties exceeding CAD 1 million (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services, interest, royalties, etc.) trigger mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions (e.g., under BEPS MLI) require genuine economic substance. Partnerships must demonstrate real business activities, employees, and premises to avoid recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act: fine up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties: 50% of tax evaded plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to withhold and remit; travel bans and seizure of personal assets may be imposed
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment: disallowance of treaty benefits, additional tax at 25% withholding, and penalties up to 10% of the tax avoided; CRA may also impose a 5% penalty for failure to file T5013

Director personal liability: General partners may be held personally liable for all taxes, interest, and penalties if the partnership is deemed a sham; travel bans and asset freezes under the Tax Collection Act
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment for willful non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years; personal assets may be subject to forfeiture if involved in money laundering
