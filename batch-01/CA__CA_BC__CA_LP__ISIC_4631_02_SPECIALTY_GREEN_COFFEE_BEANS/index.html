Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office is permissible for the limited partnership's registered office, provided that: (i) the registered office address is a physical street address within British Columbia, and not solely a post office box; (ii) the address is accessible during normal business hours for service of process; (iii) if using a registered office services provider or a lawyer's office, the provider must agree in writing to accept service and maintain custody of corporate/partnership records; (iv) virtual office arrangements where the provider merely forwards mail and does not maintain a staffed physical presence are generally accepted by the BC Registry for a limited partnership as long as the address satisfies the Partnership Act requirement for a 'registered office' in BC; (v) however, for banking and trade finance purposes (documentary letters of credit), the financial institution will likely require a physical operating address, a commercial substance declaration, and may request proof of a warehouse, import-export facility, or consolidation point in BC, or a contractual arrangement with a British Columbia-based logistics provider if actual office space is not occupied. For cannabis, tobacco, or regulated goods not applicable to green coffee, a virtual office would be insufficient; for ISIC 4631 coffee distribution, a virtual office is acceptable for statutory purposes but constitutes a heightened substance indicator that could be questioned by anti-money laundering (AML) reviewers if the general partner or UBO is non-resident. The BC Registrar may request evidence that the partnership is not a 'shell' if triggered by the suspicious transaction reporting; a virtual office should therefore be paired with a registered agent who is a licensed BC lawyer or a corporate services company holding a valid license under the Business Corporations Act, and the partnership should maintain an operating bank account in BC and records reflecting the actual physical inventory at a third-party logistics warehouse. Financial institutions may apply a 'virtual office risk rating' of 30% higher than a physical office for account opening; documentary LC transactions will be more easily supported if shipping documentation shows a BC-bonded warehouse or transshipment hub as destination. If the partnership is used as an import and distribution pass-through entity without any employees in BC, CRA may deem that the partnership has no permanent establishment for Canadian federal purposes if it lacks a present employee or inventory in BC; but if inventory is stored at a BC warehouse owned by a third party, the partnership may have a permanent establishment due to the dependent agent test under Article V of the Canada-USA tax treaty or OECD model. The partnership should maintain a minute book and accounting records in BC (or at its registered office) containing all invoices, bills of lading, contracts of carriage, and LC documents. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Primary portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://bro-pen.cra-arc.gc.ca, CRA My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html, CBSA CARM (Assessment and Revenue Management) portal at https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/carm, and BC eTaxBC at https://etax.gov.bc.ca.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership (Form 6) with the BC Corporate Registry under the Partnership Act (BC) to obtain a 7-digit BC incorporation/registration number. Step 2: Apply to CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); the BN issued to the partnership serves as the account identifier for GST/HST, payroll, and Import/Export (RM) accounts. Step 3: Mandatorily open a GST/HST account under s. 240 ETA before claiming input tax credits on coffee imports; the GST paid to CBSA on the commercial entry (Form B3-3/CARM) is recovered as an ITC on the GST/HST return. Step 4: Open an RM (Import/Export) account for HS 0901.11/0901.12 customs reporting, including use of a customs broker for CARM electronic filing if no in-house broker is engaged. Step 5: Register as a vendor with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC; because unroasted green coffee beans constitute PST-exempt food for human consumption, the LP may file a nil or exempt-status registration, but a BC vendor registration is legally required when a physical place of business exists in BC. Step 6: For non-resident limited partners, the LP must file CRA Form NR101 (or the partner files Form NR6) where income allocation attracts Part XIII withholding or treaty relief; the LP itself is not the taxpayer for income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent under ss. 96-98 Income Tax Act (Canada); the LP does not pay federal income tax on partnership income, and each limited/general partner includes its distributive share in its own return. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a distinct 'person' and a registrant under s. 122 ETA, so it must collect GST on domestic taxable supplies and recover ITCs on imports. Territorial scope: the LP's commercial substance is in British Columbia; income from the BC wholesale distribution of green coffee is allocated to BC for provincial tax purposes, and export sales to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V ETA. No Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment applies.

Available election forms

(i) GST/HST representative partner election under s. 273 ETA, designating one member to apply for registration, file returns, and receive assessments on behalf of the LP; (ii) partnership fiscal period election under s. 249.1 ITA to adopt a non-calendar year-end matching the partners' commercial cycle; (iii) no Canadian 'check-the-box' mechanism exists, but the LP should document its fiscal transparency to Canadian and foreign counterparties to prevent characterization mismatches under s. 94.1 ITA and the hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.4-18.6 ITA), especially where a US partner's check-the-box election treats a Canadian LP as a corporation; (iv) optional election under s. 225.1 ETA to have the partnership itself compute ITCs without allocating them among partners; (v) election under s. 96(3) ITA to permit a limited partner's share to be computed at the partnership level for certain deemed dispositions; (vi) BC PST exemption election for qualifying food wholesalers to file on an exempt-activity basis instead of monthly PST remittance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold for GST/HST registration under s. 148(1) ETA, measured on total worldwide taxable supplies in a calendar quarter and over the preceding four calendar quarters); BC PST has no general monetary threshold for a vendor with a BC place of business, but qualifying sales of unroasted green coffee beans are exempt from PST as food for human consumption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of specialty green coffee beans actually exported by the LP are zero-rated under item 1, Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act (supplies of goods delivered outside Canada by the supplier or a bailee/shipper). The LP must (a) be a GST/HST registrant, (b) ship the coffee to a non-resident purchaser at a destination outside Canada, and (c) obtain and retain documentary evidence of export, generally within 60 days of the date of supply. Input tax credits on the GST paid to CBSA at importation are fully recoverable to the extent the imported beans are exported zero-rated or sold to domestic taxable customers. If a shipment is mischaracterized as an export but the goods are diverted to a Canadian location, the zero-rating is void and GST at 5% applied to the full transaction value. No offshore services are in scope under ISIC 4631.

  • CBSA Import Entry (Form B3-3 or CARM electronic entry) showing GST paid at importation, serving as the ITC evidence
  • Canada Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments of goods valued over CAD 2,000, with the LP's RM account number
  • Commercial invoice in English and/or French stating HS 0901.11 (not decaffeinated) or 0901.12 (decaffeinated), commodity description, country of origin, and declared value
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (original or electronic, e.g., Bolt) or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of the shipment out of Canada to the foreign consignee
  • Freight forwarder certificate of export or international carrier's proof of destination as corroborating evidence of actual exportation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Swift MT103 payment confirmation evidencing settlement in foreign currency and repatriation of proceeds to the LP's Canadian bank account
  • CRA Form GST189 (General Application for Refund of GST/HST) used where the ITC is claimed outside a regular GST/HST return or where a prompt refund is required
  • Free Trade Agreement certificate of origin (e.g., CPTPP certificate or Canada-Colombia FTA certificate) supporting tariff preference claims at importation and traceability of green coffee lot origin
  • Packing list and container-level documentation (container seal number, port of loading, port of discharge) reconciling the containerized coffee volumes with the commercial invoice and export declaration

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no certified tax-software requirement, and no digital stamping obligation. CRA requires source records to be retained in a legible and retrievable electronic format for six years. Voluntary e-invoicing via the Canadian PEPPOL Access Point network is recognized by CRA as supporting an efficient audit trail. For customs, all import and export entries must be transmitted electronically through the CBSA CARM portal or a licensed customs broker using the Single Window Initiative (SWI); the CBSA provides a commercial accounting declaration (CAD) and electronic data interchange (EDI) for line-item tariff classification, valuation, and GST remittance at the border.

CRA and CBSA jointly audit export zero-rating claims using risk-based transaction verification. If the LP fails to substantiate export with the complete documentation bundle, CRA assesses 5% GST on the full invoice value under s. 165 ETA, plus compound interest from the date the tax should have been remitted. Specific penalty exposure: (i) failure to collect or remit GST — 6% penalty and 6% daily interval penalties under s. 280 ETA; (ii) false statements or gross negligence — penalty of CAD 100 or 50% of the understated tax, whichever is greater, under s. 285.1 ETA; (iii) Customs Act s. 109.1 administrative monetary penalties for failure to report an export or for miss-statements, ranging from CAD 100 to CAD 25,000 per shipment; (iv) criminal tax evasion under s. 327(1) ETA — on summary conviction, a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; (v) retrospective clawback normally limited to a 4-year reassessment period, but extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default under s. 298 ETA. Although Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, remittance periods exceeding 180 days increase CRA scrutiny and transfer-pricing recharacterization risk under s. 247(2) ITA, and documentary letter-of-credit or open-account terms exceeding that window are closely examined for economic substance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Bank of Canada Act, Currency Act, and Canada Business Corporations Act regime. There are no restrictions, permits, or approval requirements for overseas profit remittances, partner distributions, loan repayments, or capital repatriation by a British Columbia limited partnership. The Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency with no surrender or retention obligations; the Investment Canada Act applies only to acquisitions of control of Canadian businesses by non-Canadians and does not apply to a BC import LP's routine treasury flows. Cross-border payments are subject solely to FINTRAC reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 or more) and to bank-level sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada. Export proceeds, including any incidental re-export of green coffee samples or re-sale of containerized inventory, may be settled in any convertible currency, retained offshore, used for supplier payments, or converted to CAD at a commercial bank without regulatory approval. The only administrative obligation is CBSA export documentation where applicable and FINTRAC reporting for cross-border cash or EFTs exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and sole issuer of the Canadian dollar; banking regulation is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); international trade statistics and customs controls are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and Statistics Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating for offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, MultiPass, Intergiro) is Medium for ancillary settlement only. Legal constraints: non-bank EMIs are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act of Canada and cannot hold notional Canadian deposit accounts or provide CDIC-insured balances; they must be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC in Canada or their foreign equivalent and must comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. EMIs are not viable for documentary letter of credit issuance or for large USD/CAD clearing against Canadian counterparties, but they are permissible for low-value advance payments to overseas cooperatives where a Schedule I bank rail is impractical.
