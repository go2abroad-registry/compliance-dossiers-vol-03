E-government filing portal

Foreign entities incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act, then obtain a 9-digit Business Number (TIN) by a single-step registration with the CRA using Form RC1 or the CRA online New Business Registration service; GST/HST registration is applied for in the same filing. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian-resident or deemed-resident corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act. The effective federal general corporate rate is 15%, plus British Columbia provincial corporate income tax of 12%, for a combined general rate of 27%. Territorial scope is worldwide taxation for resident corporations with credit relief for foreign source income; no federal tax-exempt zone applies in British Columbia.

Available election forms

Canadian federal income tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election permitting pass-through treatment for corporations; all corporations are statutory separate taxpayers. Relevant statutory elections include the joint election under subsection 184(3) for deemed dividends, the subsection 216(1) election for non-resident rental income, the section 90(9) deemed-dividend election for foreign affiliate surplus, the 2022 hybrid mismatch rules under Part XVI.3, and under the GST/HST regime the voluntary registration election, the quick method election, and the joint election under section 23 of the GST/HST Rules to support zero-rating of exported services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD of worldwide gross annual taxable supplies from all jurisdictions, being the small supplier threshold; non-resident suppliers carrying on business in Canada must also assess the GST/HST registration nexus rules under ETA section 143, and BC PST registration may apply where a permanent establishment in British Columbia exists; voluntary registration is available immediately from the first supply to enable input tax credit recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of overseas public relations or communication services qualifies for 0% GST under section 23 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and is not resident in Canada, and the service is not performed in whole or in part for or in connection with real or tangible personal property situated in Canada and is not a service to an individual present in Canada. Pure offshore media relations, crisis communication, and foreign-market content creation services supplied to non-resident principals ordinarily satisfy this zero-rating test when the contemporaneous documentation demonstrates the foreign nexus.

  • Signed service agreement or statement of work with the non-resident client
  • Commercial invoice identifying the zero-rated foreign supply and the applicable GST/HST relief classification
  • Non-resident status evidence (certificate of residence or CRA registration check confirming the client has no GST/HST registration)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange/bank transfer receipt evidencing payment in foreign currency
  • Deliverables log and contemporaneous time-and-expense records showing where PR activities and client communications were performed
  • Customs export declaration (B13A/A1) where tangible promotional materials or fixtures are physically exported

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance obligation, or digital stamping mandate. GST/HST is self-assessed and filed monthly, quarterly, or annually through CRA NETFILE/GST/HST MEMFILE; mandatory electronic filing applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies in excess of CAD 1.5 million. The CRA encourages voluntary Peppol-aligned e-invoicing standards, but the absence of real-time clearance means foreign B2B invoice receipt is not a precondition for input tax credit recovery.

CRA audit teams verify zero-rating claims against the full documentation bundle; the normal reassessment window is four years, extended to ten years for misrepresentation or fraud at common law and for GST/HST purposes under subsection 298(1) of the Excise Tax Act. Fraudulent or negligent export zero-rating claims invoke a 50% gross negligence penalty under subsection 285(1) of the ETA, interest on understated tax, denial or clawback of input tax credits, and in criminal fraud cases prosecution under section 327 of the ETA with unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under a fully floating exchange rate regime with no material restrictions on current or capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible, and portfolio or direct investment flows do not require central bank approval. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is freely permitted, subject to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) at the statutory rate of 25%, which is commonly reduced to between 5% and 15% under applicable tax treaties (e.g., Canada-United States Tax Convention). The entity must maintain normal corporate tax filings (T2) and comply with Canadian resident director requirements under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) and federal Bank Act guidelines. There is no exchange control tribunal, and no surrender ratio or timeline is mandated for export or service receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines. Export and professional service proceeds in foreign currency may be held freely in domestic or offshore foreign currency accounts. For statistical balance-of-payments purposes, large cross-border receipts are tracked, but filing thresholds relate only to tax reporting (e.g., CRA Form T1134 for foreign affiliates, T106 for transfer pricing) and the FINTRAC reporting threshold of CAD 10,000 for structured cash or suspicious transactions. The entity is therefore at liberty to retain USD and EUR receivables in correspondent clearing accounts solely for operational liquidity management.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Canada does not recognize a distinct 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licensing category as found in the EU or UK. Offshore EMIs (such as Mercury, Wise Business, or Airwallex) are permitted to operate only as supplementary payment facilitation rails; they may not serve as the primary corporate depository for statutory payroll, GST/PST remittance, or audit-grade cash management. To open and maintain a high-volume operational treasury account in British Columbia, the entity must maintain a domestic Schedule I chartered bank account. Fintech EMIs are viable for USD wire disbursement and conversion to CAD, but they carry vendor concentration and sanctions-screening risk. Any EMI holding or moving funds in or out of Canada is subject to provincial Money Services Business (MSB) registration via FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), introducing an additional compliance burden for a low-risk PR agency.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.0% combined statutory headline rate for a Canadian Business Corporation in British Columbia (federal Part I rate 15.0% + BC provincial rate 12.0%). An active qualifying CCPC pays 11.0% combined on the first CA$500,000 of active business income (federal small business deduction 9.0% + BC small business rate 2.0%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Canada Part XIII, Income Tax Act s.212(2): 25% of the gross amount of dividends paid or credited to a non-resident shareholder. The rate may be reduced to 5% or 15% under a comprehensive income tax treaty for a shareholder owning at least 10% of the voting stock (5%) or all other dividend recipients (15%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed within 6 months after the end of each taxation year. The balance of tax due is payable on or before the same filing deadline; a December 31 year-end therefore requires a filing by June 30 of the following calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000 - CRA administrative expectation for contemporaneous Local File-style transfer pricing documentation where the aggregate value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents exceeds CA$1,000,000 in a taxation year. The T106 information return is mandatory whenever specified reportable transactions exceed CA$50,000 in a year. A Master File is expected for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding CA$500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) register comparable to offshore financial centres. The substance doctrine is instead implemented through the Income Tax Act General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245), the arm's-length requirement of s.247, the beneficial-owner test embedded in Canada's taxation treaties, and the CRA's substance-over-form analysis of conduit arrangements. A BC PR firm receiving overseas retainers must demonstrate real employees, active management, local premises and risk assumption to uphold treaty withholding reductions and to avoid GAAR recharacterization.
