Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC). Portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Business registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at bro.service.canada.ca. For British Columbia, the Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, through the eTaxBC portal (tax.gov.bc.ca). The BC Employer Health Tax is collected by the CRA through the federal payroll accounts.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the legal vehicle under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) via Corporations Canada or under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act (BCBCA) via BC Registry Online to obtain a provincial/national incorporation number (e.g., BC1234567). Non-resident ownership is unrestricted for CBCA and BCBCA corporations. Step 2 - Obtain the CRA Business Number (BN): register through Business Registration Online, or file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) by mail; CRA issues a 9-digit BN to which program accounts are attached: RC (Corporate Income Tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (Payroll) as applicable. A foreign corporation that already exists must first extra-provincially register in BC (Part 12 of the BCBCA) and may then obtain a BN though a Canadian permanent establishment. Step 3 - GST/HST registration: mandatory once total worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, exceed C$30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available to recover input tax credits, with non-resident registrants needing a Canadian resident agent or security under Excise Tax Act s. 240. Step 4 - BC PST registration: register through eTaxBC only for taxable sales of tangible personal property or enumerated PST-taxable services in BC; tax-structuring and management-consulting services are generally not PST-taxable. Step 5 - Register the CRA payroll RP account and BC Employer Health Tax account when employing staff whose total remuneration exceeds the C$500,000 annual exemption. Step 6 - File T2 corporate income tax returns annually with the CRA; BC income tax is filing-aligned through the same T2 and allocation forms under Part IV of the federal Income Tax Regulations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act and the BC Income Tax Act. It does not qualify for pass-through treatment; taxable income, losses and capital gains are computed and taxed at the corporate level, with distributions subject to separate dividend taxation. A resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is taxed on Canadian-source income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment. Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status permits the federal/BC small business deduction (combined 11% up to the federally allocated C$500,000 active-business-income limit among associated corporations; general combined BC rate is 27% based on federal 15% plus BC 12%). Foreign affiliate dividends are included in corporate income and may be deducted under ITA s. 113, operating as an effective participation exemption; cross-border interest deductibility is capped by the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) rules under ITA s. 18.2.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by domestic law, tax treaties and the CRA's general anti-avoidance and hybrid-mismatch framework. Statutory elections relevant to a cross-border tax structuring entity include: (i) ITA s. 85(1) rollover election allowing tax-deferred transfers of property to a taxable Canadian corporation; (ii) ITA s. 216(4) election by a non-resident with Canadian rental income to file a net-income return; (iii) ITA s. 156(2) joint election by associated CCPCs to allocate the small business deduction limit; (iv) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related GST/HST registrants to treat supplies as made for nil consideration, simplifying input tax credit tracking; (v) nominee or agency reporting elections where the entity acts as a disclosed agent for a foreign principal, affecting permanent-establishment risk; and (vi) EIFEL-related relief mechanisms and elections to exclude qualifying group entities from the interest cap where a hybrid mismatch would otherwise arise. Hybrid-mismatch outcomes (deduction/non-inclusion or double deduction) are statutorily constrained in Canada rather than elected around.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter. Once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. BC PST: no monetary registration threshold applies to a vendor carrying on business in BC that makes taxable sales of tangible personal property or enumerated taxable services; however, PST is generally not exigible on pure management-consulting or tax-advisory services. BC Employer Health Tax registration applies only when annual remuneration exceeds C$500,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border international tax structuring and transfer-pricing advisory services are zero-rated under paragraphs 7(a) and 23(a) of Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (ETA). Eligibility requires: (i) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada, not registered for GST/HST; (ii) the service is supplied to that non-resident and not to a Canadian permanent establishment of the non-resident; (iii) for advisory, consulting or professional services under s. 23(a), the service is not acquired for use in Canada in the course of the non-resident's commercial activity and does not relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada; (iv) the supplier maintains contemporaneous books and records showing the offshore nature of the supply and the non-resident status of the client; and (v) the consideration is paid from outside Canada. Input tax credits attributable to zero-rated exported services remain fully recoverable, and net refunds are claimed through the regular GST/HST return (Form GST34) or through Form GST106 where the CRA requests a schedule of zero-rated export transactions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency / Canadian Export Reporting System B13A) — required only for physical goods subject to export reporting; pure cross-border advisory services do not require a CBSA customs declaration.
  • Executed service agreement and statement of work identifying the non-resident client, the offshore commercial activity, and the absence of Canadian permanent-establishment consumption.
  • Zero-rated invoice explicitly stating: Supply zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI, ETA, ss. 7 and 23, together with the supplier's Business Number and GST/HST registration number.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent: bank wire records, SWIFT MT103 messages, foreign exchange contracts, and foreign bank credit advisories demonstrating that the consideration was received from outside Canada.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / postal consignment record — applicable only where ancillary physical deliverables (e.g., bound transfer pricing reports, CDs or software media) are shipped from Canada; supported by the B13A export report where the value exceeds the C$2,000 reporting threshold.
  • Proof of non-resident status: foreign business registry extract, certificate of incorporation of the client, or CRA non-resident withholding tax exemption evidence, to be kept with the GST/HST books and records.

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamp, or third-party invoice validation mandate. The CRA does not require e-invoices to be cleared against an official platform before use; e-invoicing through the Pan-European Public Procurement OnLine (Peppol) network is accepted on a voluntary pilot basis. Mandatory electronic transmission applies to T2 corporation income tax returns for corporations with gross revenues over C$1 million in any prior taxation year and to GST/HST returns for registrants with taxable supplies over C$1.5 million (via GST/HST NETFILE or EFILE). Suppliers must issue invoices within 30 days of a request under Excise Tax Act s. 223(1), and invoices must contain the supplier's name and Business Number, the date, description of the supply, total consideration, the GST/HST rate and amount, or a statement that the supply is zero-rated.

The CRA's GST/HST refund and verification programs, including the International and Large Business Audit and Offshore Compliance divisions, actively audit zero-rating claims where non-resident status, export evidence, or foreign-source payment is missing. If zero-rating is reversed, the supply is treated as taxable in Canada: GST/HST becomes payable, corresponding input tax credits are disallowed or re-apportioned, refunds are clawed back, and statutory interest accrues from the due date. Reassessment windows: GST/HST generally four years from the due date of the return (ETA s. 298); corporate income tax generally three years from the original assessment (ITA s. 152(4)), with no time limit where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default. Gross negligence penalties under ETA s. 285 and ITA s. 163(2) equal the greater of C$100 and 50% of the net tax or refund understated. Criminal evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 can result in fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years. At the provincial level, the BC Ministry of Finance may assess uncollected PST with penalties within four years of the reporting period, including director-liability measures for knowing participation in PST evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under a free-floating foreign exchange regime. There are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no central bank approval for profit remittance. Non-resident shareholders may receive dividends, interest, royalties, and service fees through standard banking channels, subject only to Part XIII withholding taxes (typically reduced by tax treaty) and Canadian corporate solvency and dividend law requirements. The Canadian dollar is freely tradable in spot and derivative markets.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio into Canadian dollars, and no requirement to deposit receipts with a designated domestic bank. Foreign-currency receipts from cross-border consulting services may be retained in CAD or foreign-currency operating accounts. FINTRAC anti-money-laundering reporting applies (any single electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, or 24-hour aggregate, must be reported by the reporting entity).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) with oversight of the financial system and currency issuance; prudential regulation of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange transaction monitoring under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) administered by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary settlement rail; Low as a primary banking solution. Non-bank fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not licensed Canadian banks under the Bank Act and do not provide CDIC deposit insurance; they may be used for low-value cross-border disbursements and inbound collection, but must not replace a Schedule I bank account held in Canada. If the EMI maintains a Canadian presence or solicits Canadian clients, it must be registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC. Funds held with unregulated EMIs raise counterparty, operational, and audit-risk concerns for a tax advisory practice.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity or subordinated shareholder loans should be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the BC corporation's CAD account at a Schedule I bank. Remittance information must clearly state purpose as 'Share Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan' and include the Canadian corporate name and account number. Canada does not require a government-specific FX purpose code, but the receiving bank will classify the inflow for FINTRAC EFT reporting if above CAD 10,000; for capital injections, supporting documents (board resolution, subscription agreement, share registry, financing statement) must be held on file. Foreign-owned Canadian corporations must preserve proof of the transfer to support treaty residence and tax-basis analysis, and file Form T1135 if foreign property holdings exceed CAD 100,000.For a cross-border tax structuring consultancy, standardized settlement rails are SWIFT MT103 (now ISO 20022 pacs.008 for participating banks) for wire-based vendor payments, subcontractor fees, intercompany tax planning fees, and dividend distributions; domestic CAD payments can clear through Lynx/ACSS settled via Payments Canada. Commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT710) are not standard for intangible consulting services and are reserved for goods-based trade financing; where used, an irrevocable, confirmed documentary LC may support large equipment or service subcontracts. All outbound EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC reporting; wire instructions must contain full beneficiary name, account number, and compliance-compliant purpose description; funds should remain traceable through SWIFT GPI to satisfy audit and transfer pricing documentation.CAD/USD deliverable FX forwards executed with an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and supporting Credit Support AnnexCross-currency swaps (FX swaps and long-dated cross-currency interest rate swaps) between CAD and USD for balance-sheet hedging of cross-border intercompany loansVanilla deliverable FX options and zero-cost collars/risk reversals with a licensed Canadian bank or authorized Treasury counterpartyNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only where the underlying currency is non-convertible or restricted within its home jurisdiction (e.g., CNY/CNH, INR); NDFs must be documented under ISDA and backed by a real economic exposure statement; NDFs are not required or standard for the freely floating CADDirect Canadian-dollar offsetting of matching foreign-currency receivables and payables (natural hedging) as a reported treasury policy, accompanied by monthly board-level FX exposure and mark-to-market reporting

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as a supplementary settlement rail; Low as a primary banking solution. Non-bank fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not licensed Canadian banks under the Bank Act and do not provide CDIC deposit insurance; they may be used for low-value cross-border disbursements and inbound collection, but must not replace a Schedule I bank account held in Canada. If the EMI maintains a Canadian presence or solicits Canadian clients, it must be registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC. Funds held with unregulated EMIs raise counterparty, operational, and audit-risk concerns for a tax advisory practice.
