Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST and Business Number portal; combined federal-provincial registration through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), with British Columbia administered under the provincial Ministry of Finance for PST and the federal CRA for GST/HST.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or registering as a British Columbia limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA's Business Registration Online portal or by completing Form RC1. The partnership must register for GST/HST if it carries on business in Canada and has taxable supplies exceeding the $30,000 annual threshold; non-resident partners may also require a BN for program accounts including GST/HST, payroll, and import/export. Registration is completed online using the partnership’s legal name, Canadian agent or representative authority details, and a BC limited partnership filing number issued by BC Registry; the LP may also register for BC PST separately through the BC Provincial Sales Tax registration system.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity establishing or registering as a British Columbia limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA's Business Registration Online portal or by completing Form RC1. The partnership must register for GST/HST if it carries on business in Canada and has taxable supplies exceeding the $30,000 annual threshold; non-resident partners may also require a BN for program accounts including GST/HST, payroll, and import/export. Registration is completed online using the partnership’s legal name, Canadian agent or representative authority details, and a BC limited partnership filing number issued by BC Registry; the LP may also register for BC PST separately through the BC Provincial Sales Tax registration system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The Canada Revenue Agency generally treats a limited partnership as a pass-through entity and not a taxpayer; under the Income Tax Act, any provincial twist (including the British Columbia limited partnership statute) does not create a separate federal taxpayer. Partners may elect, where eligible, to compute income in accordance with the partnership agreement under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; there is no check-the-box election for federal tax classification analogous to the U.S. form 8832. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada applies the hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act, and a foreign entity may be fiscally transparent in its residence jurisdiction; treaty-based tie-breaker and domestic anti-hybrid rules apply to deny inappropriate deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada (including cross-border trucking services); non-resident carriers transporting goods into or through Canada are generally required to register even if below threshold when making taxable supplies in Canada, with special rules for international transportation and zero-rated transport of goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST is available for exports of goods supplied to a non-resident, provided the goods are exported as soon as practicable and documentary evidence is retained. Cross-border trucking services between Canada and the United States or other foreign points are zero-rated when supplied by a carrier with a place of business in Canada and the destination is outside Canada. International transportation of goods by a common carrier, and related freight services that are part of an international continuous movement, are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part VII of the Excise Tax Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form A6 or Canada Customs Export Declaration for goods exported by truck, when required)
  • Bill of Lading (through bill issued by the cross-border trucking carrier)
  • TIR Carnet (safeTIR electronic notification) or truck manifest approved by CBSA
  • Commercial invoice / packing slip
  • Proof of export (border crossing stamps, CBSA exit report)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment evidence evidencing transfer of funds from the non-resident purchaser

Canada does not mandate a centralized real-time e-invoicing clearance system. However, GST/HST registrants must issue valid tax invoices (sales invoices) containing the supplier’s BN, invoice date, and prescribed particulars; electronic exchange via EDI or API is permitted. British Columbia requires copy invoices only for PST-exempt sales. For customs brokerage and cross-border bonded trucking, the CBSA (Canada Border Services Agency) uses the Integrated Import Declaration (IID) / Canadian Customs Self Assessment (CSA) and the TIR carnet electronic safeTIR system; no fiscal digital stamp is required.

CRA may reassess, deny zero-rating, and impose penalty equal to the unreported GST/HST plus interest if the required export documentation is not provided; penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a false statement penalty of 50% of the net tax amount where the taxpayer knowingly, or under circumstances amounting to culpable conduct, makes a false statement or omission in relation to a refund or credit. For exporters, the failure to maintain sufficient evidence of export may result in a clawback of input tax credits and an assessment for unremitted GST/HST. Under the Income Tax Act, a false statement made in a GST/HST return may also trigger a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax and criminal prosecution under section 239, subject to fines of up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally impose prior approval, quantitative capital controls, or mandatory repatriation for ordinary foreign investment, intercompany funding, trade settlement, dividend payments, or loan servicing. A British Columbia limited partnership may receive and remit foreign currency through authorized Canadian financial institutions, subject to bank AML/KYC, sanctions screening, source-of-funds verification, beneficial-ownership identification, partnership-agreement review, and applicable tax documentation. Profit and cash distributions must comply with the partnership agreement, solvency requirements applicable to the relevant entity and transaction, lender covenants, withholding-tax rules, and Canadian and foreign tax reporting obligations. Non-resident partners may face Canadian withholding or information-reporting requirements depending on the nature and source of the income, including income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment or Canadian business activities. Banks may require evidence of commercial purpose, invoices, customs records, transport contracts, and tax residency information before processing unusual or high-value remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, convert export receipts into Canadian dollars, or repatriate proceeds within a prescribed period. Export proceeds may generally remain in Canadian or foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual, tax, sanctions, AML, customs, and accounting requirements. Cross-border trucking receipts should be supported by freight contracts, invoices, bills of lading, TIR Carnet or other transit documentation where applicable, customs records, and proof of delivery. Export-control or permit requirements can apply to controlled goods, technology, or sanctioned destinations, but ordinary freight-service receipts are not subject to a general mandatory conversion ratio. Foreign-currency balances should be recorded under applicable Canadian accounting and tax rules, with foreign-exchange gains and losses appropriately reported.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate foreign exchange administration imposing general exchange controls. Relevant supplementary authorities include the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), and Global Affairs Canada for controlled goods and trade permits.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary payment functions and Low to Medium as the primary treasury bank. Offshore electronic-money institutions are not substitutes for a Canadian deposit-taking institution and may not provide Canadian deposit protection, domestic clearing access, CAD account functionality, or reliable support for a Canadian limited partnership. Use is subject to the provider's licensing perimeter, Canadian payment-services rules, sanctions controls, source-of-funds checks, foreign-exchange restrictions in the provider's jurisdiction, account terms, and tax-record requirements. A foreign provider operating payment functions in Canada may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and related Bank of Canada supervision, depending on its activities and implementation status. Maintain a Canadian bank as the principal account for payroll, fuel-card settlement, tax payments, customs-related disbursements, and customer collections.
