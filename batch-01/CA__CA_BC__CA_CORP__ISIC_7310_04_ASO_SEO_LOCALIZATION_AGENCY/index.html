Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under British Columbia law. A BC business corporation may be incorporated without initial capital; shares may be issued at any time for any lawful consideration fixed by the directors under s. 51 of the Business Corporations Act. The authorized share structure is set out in the Notice of Articles and Articles.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy and fire safety approval for the leased office issued by the local fire authority under the BC Fire Code; structural load and Building Code Part 3 compliance confirmed by the municipal building inspector for the commercial premises; WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety registration and clearance for the employer; municipal business licence confirming zoning and use compliance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal corporate income tax and GST/HST; British Columbia Ministry of Finance for BC provincial income tax and Provincial Sales Tax (PST). Primary portals: CRA My Business Account and BC eTaxBC / BC Corporate Online.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned BC corporation must first be incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or registered as an extra-provincial company under the BC Business Corporations Act. It must then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) through CRA’s Business Registration Online service or Form RC1, and activate the GST/HST and corporate income tax program accounts. If the corporation will make PST-taxable sales in British Columbia, a separate BC PST registration is required through eTaxBC. Non-resident ownership is permitted; no minimum capitalization or Canadian director residency is required for this entity structure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A Canadian-incorporated BC corporation is a resident corporation subject to federal and BC corporate income tax on worldwide income. No entity-classification election is available at the federal or provincial level. Hybrid mismatch and excessive interest-financing rules in the Income Tax Act apply automatically, including restrictions designed to neutralize reverse-hybrid structures and double-dip deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada that makes taxable supplies must register for GST/HST when worldwide GST/HST taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000. BC PST registration has no general turnover threshold and becomes mandatory as soon as the business makes PST-taxable sales in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

SEO/ASO localization services supplied to a non-resident client may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The agency must show that the recipient is a non-resident, the recipient is not GST/HST-registered in respect of the supply, and the service is not rooted in Canadian real property or tangible personal property. Services performed in Canada for a client located abroad generally qualify when the client does not receive the benefit through a Canadian business. If the recipient is a registered GST/HST person, the supply generally becomes taxable at 5% GST unless another specific zero-rating provision applies.

  • No customs export declaration is required for intangible digital services; CRA substantiation relies on the executed client agreement, itemized invoices, and contemporaneous proof that the recipient is a non-resident such as a foreign corporate registry extract, exchange of information certificate, or other statutory residency declaration.
  • Bank statements, SWIFT confirmations, or Wise/Payoneer settlement records evidencing receipt of foreign currency into the Canadian corporate bank account and traceability to the exported service.
  • For any ancillary physical goods shipped from Canada, the standard export package would include Form B3-3 Canada Customs Coding Form or equivalent electronic export declaration, commercial invoice, packing slip, bill of lading or airway bill, and payment evidence; this component is inapplicable to pure digital ASO and SEO services.

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API invoice clearance. GST/HST invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form provided they satisfy the disclosure requirements in the Excise Tax Act. CRA is implementing voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing and EDI channels for public procurement, but there is no statutory requirement for a private SEO/ASO agency to obtain pre-approval or digital stamping on invoices.

Insufficient export zero-rating substantiation permits CRA to reassess the supply as taxable at 5% GST/HST and to collect unpaid tax, daily statutory interest, and penalties. A negligent or fraudulent zero-rating claim can attract penalties of up to 25% of the tax assessed, and the normal four-year reassessment period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is shown. CRA may also coordinate with the Canada Border Services Agency and foreign tax treaty partners to verify export proceeds, non-resident status, and banking flows; wilful evasion can result in prosecution and court-imposed fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both the current and capital account. There is no statutory requirement for central bank approval on capital inflows, dividend remittances, loan servicing, or profit repatriation. Funds may be freely converted to CAD or foreign currency at market rates; the only material fiscal hurdles are withholding taxes on distributions and corporate reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts; standard CRA tax filing, transfer-pricing documentation, and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting requirements still apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no separate foreign exchange administration for FX control purposes. Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises AML/ATF compliance, and Global Affairs Canada administers economic sanctions and export controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility is High for using Wise Business, Payoneer, and comparable regulated non-bank payment service providers as secondary disbursement and collection rails. These entities are not authorized Canadian banks and do not offer CDIC deposit insurance; their Canadian activity is permitted only through FINTRAC-registered money services business arrangements or equivalent exemption. EMIs domiciled in the EU/UK can lawfully assist cross-border treasury operations but cannot be relied upon as the primary corporate bank account for statutory corporate purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions or shareholder loans must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 from the investing shareholder's licensed banking institution to the British Columbia corporation's Schedule I operating account. The remittance message should include in field 70 a precise purpose narrative such as 'Shareholder equity injection' or 'Shareholder loan per loan agreement dated [date]' and reference the applicable corporate resolution. Bank compliance expects a board resolution, updated share register or loan agreement, source-of-wealth documents, and a beneficial-ownership declaration.
