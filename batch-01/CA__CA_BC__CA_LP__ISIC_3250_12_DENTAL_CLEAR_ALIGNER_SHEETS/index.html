Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO); British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered by the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC; exports are declared through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System.

E-government filing portal

The BC limited partnership obtains a statutory 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) in the partnership name. The general partner, or an authorized Canadian representative, files Form RC1 or registers online through BRO and selects the GST/HST program account. The filing must be accompanied by the BC certificate of limited partnership, the executed partnership agreement, and the identity and address details of all partners, including non-resident foreign corporate partners. There is no minimum capital requirement, and non-resident ownership is permitted. A non-resident partnership without a Canadian establishment may still register for GST/HST where it carries on business in Canada or makes registrable taxable supplies; in such case CRA may require a Canadian agent for service and a mailing address in Canada. BC PST requires a separate registration through eTaxBC before the partnership makes taxable retail sales or leases in BC.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; income is computed at the partnership level under the Income Tax Act and allocated to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is itself a separate registrable person (the Excise Tax Act definition of person includes a partnership), so the same LP may hold both a partnerships BN/GST account and remain remitted at the partner level for income tax. There is no separate entity-level income tax at the LP level and no territorial or tax-exempt zone classification applies to the BC activities. — The BC limited partnership obtains a statutory 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) in the partnership name. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; income is computed at the partnership level under the Income Tax Act and allocated to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is itself a separate registrable person (the Excise Tax Act definition of person includes a partnership), so the same LP may hold both a partnerships BN/GST account and remain remitted at the partner level for income tax. There is no separate entity-level income tax at the LP level and no territorial or tax-exempt zone classification applies to the BC activities.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box elective classification regime; classification is statutory. Available statutory elections and mechanisms for a cross-border BC LP: (i) an election under the Income Tax Act to treat a foreign entity that is opaque under foreign corporate law as a partnership for Canadian partners; (ii) designation of a nominated or elected partnership member for GST/HST registration, administration, and input tax credit claims; (iii) an election to adopt a non-calendar partnership fiscal period for Canadian income tax; (iv) the Excise Tax Act mechanism permitting a partner to act as agent for the partnership in GST/HST matters; and (v) automatic application of Canada's hybrid mismatch arrangement rules, which deny deductions or recharacterize passthrough payments when an entity is treated differently in another jurisdiction; no elective carve-out is provided for hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: total consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada, including zero-rated exports, in a single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters triggers mandatory GST/HST registration. Registration is required once the threshold is crossed, with liability applying from the first supply that caused the threshold to be exceeded. BC PST registration is required before making any taxable retail sales or leases in BC without regard to a revenue threshold; zero-rated export sales of dental aligner sheets delivered outside BC are excluded from PST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Section 6 zero-rates tangible personal property, such as clear aligner thermoform sheets, supplied to a non-resident purchaser and exported by or on behalf of the supplier from Canada to a destination outside Canada in the ordinary course of commercial activity within the prescribed export time frame. Section 7 zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident recipient when the services are not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The BC limited partnership must be a GST/HST registrant at the time of supply to claim the 0% rate and must retain evidence of export as prescribed by the Excise Tax Act and the Exporters of Goods Regulations. Exports physically delivered outside British Columbia are also outside the scope of BC PST.

  • Customs export declaration (B13A or electronic export declaration EED) filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Carrier-issued bill of lading or air waybill evidencing the foreign destination and the actual export date
  • Commercial invoice including the declared HS classification (e.g., 9018.49 or 3920.9) and the packing list
  • Purchase order or executed supply agreement with the non-resident customer
  • PST exemption or billing documentation confirming direct delivery outside BC where required for BC PST purposes
  • Bank remittance evidence, comprising inward credit advice or an FX conversion record confirming payment received in CAD within the repatriation window

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or government API invoice pre-clearance regime applies in Canada. CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework built on the Peppol network for B2B and B2G transactions. Electronic GST/HST return filing via NETFILE or EDI is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5M, and electronic remittance through CRA My Business Account is required for such registrants. BC PST returns must be filed electronically through eTaxBC. No statutory IT or API clearance requirement applies to the export of thermoformed dental aligner sheets.

CRA and CBSA audit export substantiation against physical exit of the goods from Canada. Incomplete, inconsistent, or fraudulent export documentation causes retrospective disallowance of zero-rating and reassessment of GST at 5% with interest and late-remittance penalties; falsified export declarations trigger the Excise Tax Act gross-negligence penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated, a seven-year reassessment window for misrepresentation, and criminal liability under ETA section 327 with, on indictment, a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. BC PST audits may additionally claw back uncollected PST where goods are found to have been consumed in BC, with penalties under the Provincial Sales Tax Act. The complete evidence bundle, including the commercial invoice, export declaration, bill of lading, and inward remittance evidence, is therefore a statutory, audit-critical condition of the export zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely, and there are no exchange-control restrictions on dividend, interest, or royalty remittances by a British Columbia limited partnership. Cross-border payments are subject only to FINTRAC large-transfer reporting and Part XIII non-resident withholding tax where applicable; no ex-ante approval from the Bank of Canada is required for either inbound capital contributions or outbound profit distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None — Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion obligation on export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in CAD or foreign currency at the entity's discretion; however, transactions over C$10,000 crossing the border must be reported under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and trade documentation (commercial invoices, bills of lading) must substantiate exports declared to Statistics Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) / Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer may operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) only if registered with FINTRAC; their Canadian footprint and Bank of Canada settlement access are indirect, and they do not qualify as primary licensed deposit-taking institutions. Feasibility is Medium (viable as supporting cross-border payout and FX remittance rails, but not for escrowed capital, regulatory license deposits, or Letters of Credit, which require a Schedule I bank relationship).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections flow via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Canadian bank account using purpose code (e.g., ISO 20022 purpose 'CAPT' for capital payment / 'INVC' for intercompany invoice) and referencing the LP's Legal Entity Identifier (LEI). For transfers above C$10,000, FINTRAC reporting applies; the beneficiary bank will verify KYC before credit. CAD funding may alternatively be routed via Payments Canada Lynx for domestic large-value settlement.
