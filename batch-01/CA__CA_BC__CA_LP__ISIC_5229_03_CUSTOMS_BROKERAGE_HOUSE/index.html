Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — My Business Account portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance (BC Taxpayer Account and BC Corporate Online) for BC PST registration and extraprovincial limited partnership registration

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity first applies to CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through the My Business Account online portal. If carrying on business in BC, the partnership must also register as an extraprovincial limited partnership under the BC Partnership Act through BC Corporate Online. Once taxable marketplace activity in Canada exceeds the CRA small-supplier threshold, the partnership must open a CRA GST/HST program account (RT). A separate BC PST account with the BC Ministry of Finance is required only for lease or sale transactions taxable under the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent by default under the Income Tax Act; partnership income is allocated through T5013 schedules and taxed at the partner level. Non-resident partners may make an Income Tax Act section 216 election for net-basis taxation of Canadian real-property rental income, and treaty-protection/hybrid-relevance elections may be available under section 94.2 in certain offshore structure cases. For GST/HST, eligible registrants may elect annual filing under Excise Tax Act section 239(1) and the Quick Method where statutory thresholds permit.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide taxable supply revenue from Canada in any calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters triggers mandatory CRA GST/HST registration for non-small suppliers (Excise Tax Act, Part IX, sections 240–241); BC PST registration threshold differs for PST collection on taxable vendor sales

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Canada and services supplied to a non-resident person may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the service is supplied in respect of exported goods and the non-resident supply is not consumed in Canada. A licensed customs broker acting for a non-resident consignee or foreign principal can zero-rate its export-related services if the billing contract clearly identifies the foreign recipient, ancillary supply relation to a specific export of tangible property, and the service is not performed for the direct benefit of a Canadian importer or resident user.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency on export date
  • Ocean / Air / Highway Bill of Lading or cargo-control waybill evidencing physical export and overseas destination of the goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer receipt proving the non-resident foreign-currency consideration was received in Canada in the exporter/broker's Canadian bank account

Canada does not operate a real-time national electronic-invoicing clearance or digital-stamping system. CRA mandates electronic filing and remittance for large registrants, and GST/HST invoices must satisfy the prescribed information requirements in Excise Tax Act section 169(3) and section 169.4. Customs brokerage and export declarations are processed electronically through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal, including automated broker interface (ABI) EDI channels and importer/broker accounts.

CRA audit teams apply heightened scrutiny to zero-rated export claims and require contemporaneous documentation. If export substantiation is absent or incomplete, CRA will reassess the supply at 5% GST and disallow corresponding input tax credits/refunds with arrears interest computed daily. Gross-negligence penalties may reach 50% of the understated net tax, and knowingly or wilfully making false statements can result in criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act, monetary penalties, and prohibition claims. Retrospective clawbacks are also triggered for goods re-imported into Canada or services later shown to have benefited a Canadian resident.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada has not maintained an exchange control regime since the termination of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951; no approval is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or partnership capital repatriation to non-resident partners. A British Columbia limited partnership engaged in customs brokerage may freely receive foreign capital contributions and remit distributable profits abroad, subject only to Canadian withholding tax under Income Tax Act section 212, FINTRAC cross-border transaction reporting, and standard bank attestations. No capital account restrictions, no exchange control licenses, and no advance deposit requirements apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist in Canada. Export proceeds and customs brokerage receivables may be retained in any freely convertible currency without a mandated conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. The only compliance obligations are (i) FINTRAC reporting for every cross-border wire transfer of C$10,000 or more under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and (ii) record-keeping under the Customs Act and the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations. The LP may maintain offshore and onshore collections accounts, but treasury policy should align foreign-currency balances with CAD liquidity needs to avoid adverse FX exposure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; prudential supervision of banks is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision and cross-border wire reporting are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); domestic payment and clearing oversight is conducted by Payments Canada (Canadian Payments Association) under the Canadian Payments Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with defined legal limits. Canada does not recognize the European 'electronic money institution' license; foreign EMIs must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, if providing retail payment functions, register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. EMIs cannot accept Canadian deposits or issue a deposit-taking account because deposit-taking is reserved to banks under the Bank Act. Cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, Corpay) are legally viable for pay-in/pay-out and vendor settlement but cannot replace a scheduled Canadian bank for CBSA duty payments, pre-authorized debit arrangements, or LVTS/Lynx clearing. Recommended structure: maintain an operational account at a Tier 1 bank for statutory customs payments and use a FINTRAC-registered MSB/EMI channel for operational FX and bulk remittances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: route capital via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Canadian bank account with remittance information identified as 'Limited Partner Capital Contribution – [Entity Name]'. The sender must select the bank-designated Canadian wire purpose code applicable to direct investment/business capital (major banks employ a 4-digit Business/Purpose Code matrix; for capital injections, codes such as 'Direct Investment – Equity' or the internal equivalent are used). The receiving bank reports the transaction to FINTRAC if the amount is C$10,000 or above; the LP must retain the subscription agreement, incoming wire confirmation, and partner register entries for audit. Banks may additionally request a Foreign Currency Questionnaire or Non-Resident Transaction Declaration when the originator is an offshore partnership account.Primary rails: (1) CBSA duty and tax payments via the Customs Automated Clearing System through Pre-Authorized Debit (PAD) from the LP's Canadian-dollar clearing account; (2) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor, freight, and foreign-broker settlements; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) and standby LCs for high-value or documentary trade transactions; (4) Payments Canada Automated Funds Transfer (ACH) for domestic payment runs; and (5) Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Lynx same-day euro-wire equivalent for same-day CAD clearing above C$25 million. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million in transactions with non-residents (aggregate for partnership) or CAD 50 million gross revenue; documentation required if either threshold exceeded.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific ESR, but General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies; partnerships must demonstrate real economic substance to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of understated tax, plus interest; criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset seizure possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, penalties under GAAR

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for gross negligence if they participated in the scheme.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals, CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable if they knowingly failed to comply.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment, penalty of 10% of net adjustment, plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable if they signed false statements or knowingly participated.
