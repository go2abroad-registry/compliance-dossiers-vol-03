Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – official administration body for federal tax, GST/HST, and customs duties coordination; taxpayer portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/access-business-account-online). For British Columbia, the provincial sales tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation not resident in Canada that commences carrying on business in Canada and makes taxable supplies must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA and register for GST/HST program account (RT) using Form RC1 or the online GST/HST registration service. The corporation must identify its non-resident status, provide foreign incorporation details, and may be required to report an agent or representative in Canada. Provincial registration for BC PST (a provincial sales tax number) is mandatory if the corporation makes sales of tangible personal property or certain services within British Columbia; registration can be completed online via the BC Provincial Sales Tax Registration (eTaxBC).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign corporation not resident in Canada that commences carrying on business in Canada and makes taxable supplies must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA and register for GST/HST program account (RT) using Form RC1 or the online GST/HST registration service. The corporation must identify its non-resident status, provide foreign incorporation details, and may be required to report an agent or representative in Canada. Provincial registration for BC PST (a provincial sales tax number) is mandatory if the corporation makes sales of tangible personal property or certain services within British Columbia; registration can be completed online via the BC Provincial Sales Tax Registration (eTaxBC).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A corporation is by law a separate taxpayer taxable at entity level on worldwide income if resident in Canada, or on Canadian-source business and property income if non-resident. Available statutory elections include: (i) consolidated GST/HST group registration under Section 240 of the Excise Tax Act; (ii) quick method election for GST/HST accounting; (iii) annual GST/HST filing election; (iv) Section 216 election for a non-resident to file a Canadian income tax return on rental income; and (v) election to recharacterize a branch or subsidiary for tax treaty purposes only where expressly permitted under the Canada-US Treaty (limited hybrid mismatch and dual-resident rules apply under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) made in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters — exceeding this 'small supplier' threshold triggers mandatory GST/HST registration; a non-resident may voluntarily register immediately, but must apply as a non-resident registrant and may be required to provide security.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: a supply is zero-rated where tangible personal property is exported by the supplier or on behalf of the supplier, and the supplier retains and provides Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) proof of exportation, including an export declaration and bill of lading showing delivery outside Canada within a reasonable time. For services: a supply made to a non-resident is generally zero-rated where the service is performed wholly outside Canada or is in relation to real property situated outside Canada, or in respect of intangible personal property where the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is unrelated to use in Canada. Offshore digital gaming services sold via Apple IAP/Google Play Billing to foreign end-users qualify if the service supply contract and actual provision point consistently support the foreign destination and no Canadian economic activity is performed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency – Form B13A or equivalent Electronic Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and purchase orders linking the supply to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/foreign exchange documentation proving payment and repatriation of proceeds from exported goods/services

Canada does not currently mandate a government-based real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance system. GST/HST reporting is self-assessed through periodic returns remitted via CRA's GST/HST NETFILE and prescribed electronic payment systems. B2B e-invoicing remains format-agnostic (EDI, PDF, XML), though the Canadian federal government adopts the Pan-European Public Procurement (PEPPOL) standard for supplier invoices to government entities. British Columbia similarly requires no real-time digital stamping; a BC PST vendor must issue prescribed sales invoices only. Emerging CRA digital verification initiatives (e-audit, payroll API) require registrants to retain machine-readable records, but there is no immediate continuous-transaction-control obligation.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims with strict documentary substantiation under Section 169 of the Excise Tax Act and the GST/HST New Export Declaration requirements. Inadequate or false export documentation will result in denial of the 0% rate, reassessment to the 5% GST plus BC PST where applicable, interest on the unremitted amount, and the imposition of a 25% gross negligence penalty under Section 285 of the Excise Tax Act; knowingly making false statements or withholding information can trigger criminal penalties and a conviction offence under Section 327, including fines and imprisonment. BC's Penalty and Interest provisions under the Provincial Sales Tax Act apply on under-collected PST for exported goods not substantiated by exemption certificates.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible and Canada meets IMF Article VIII obligations with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Capitalization of a CA_BC Business Corporation by non-resident shareholders requires no government approval, exchange control filing, or convertibility certificate. Profit and dividend remittance is unrestricted; the only fiscal friction is non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends, being a statutory 25% generally reduced under applicable tax treaties (e.g., US/Canada Treaty to 5%-15%), assessed at the source and remitted by the withholding agent rather than through any currency convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, retention timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Revenue derived from cross-border digital publishing, Apple IAP settlements, Google Play Billing, and licensing royalties may be retained in CAD, USD, or any freely convertible foreign currency in a Schedule I bank account or abroad. The Income Tax Act only requires recognition of foreign exchange gains or losses under sections 39 and 142.3. FINTRAC may receive transaction reports from financial institutions where cross-border transfers exceed CAD 10,000, but no exporter-level filing, conversion requirement, or repatriation deadline applies to the CA_CORP entity.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange policy competence shared with the Department of Finance Canada and prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada operates no separate foreign exchange administration or licensing agency; the Minister of Finance may, under the Bank of Canada Act, direct intervention in foreign exchange markets to maintain orderly conditions, but no prior approval is required for inward or outward remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a standalone treasury account; MEDIUM as an auxiliary disbursement rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer do not hold Canadian banking licenses and cannot accept CAD client deposits as principal in the manner of a Schedule I bank; they operate in Canada as Money Services Businesses registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and must obtain registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) once fully in force. Legal constraints: foreign EMI accounts are unacceptable for the capital escrow or liquidity-stabilization function, and routing more than a minor share of operating revenue through an unregistered EMI creates an enhanced due diligence red flag for the primary Schedule I banker. A compliant hybrid structure uses a Schedule I master treasury account supplemented by one FINTRAC-registered EMI limited to small-value disbursements such as contractor payments and marketing vendor fees, with the EMI swept to zero daily from the master account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$5,000,000 (net transfer-pricing adjustment threshold for the 10% penalty); C$750,000,000 (consolidated group revenue threshold triggering Country-by-Country reporting). Canada imposes no autonomous filing threshold for Local/Master File, but contemporaneous documentation must be available at the CRA's request.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filings, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) and treaty-shopping case law require genuine business purpose and substantive activities. A social casino operator must ensure that core intangible development (virtual slot mechanical design, platform code, user algorithms) is performed in Canada with Canadian staff; an IP-holding shell without local substance will be denied treaty benefits and subject to GAAR recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to 200% of the tax evaded (or C$10,000,000 per count, whichever is greater); civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; CRA may reassess beyond the normal limitation period.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in the scheme may be jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts and civil restitution; criminal conviction for tax evasion carries imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Misstatements

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to arm's-length royalty, application of a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the net adjustment above the C$5,000,000 threshold, plus interest accruals.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unremitted corporate taxes and penalties under director-liability provisions if they directed the sham intercompany payments; they risk suit for breach of fiduciary duty.
Treaty Shopping / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: Application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to deny treaty benefits, recharacterization as fully taxable Canadian-source income, reassessment to the full 25% withholding tax, and a 50% gross-negligence penalty for aggressive filing positions.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil will be pierced against directors who operate the entity as a mere façade, exposing them to civil recovery and, in fraud scenarios, criminal liability.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC monetary penalties up to C$2,000,000 per violation; criminal prosecution for laundering proceeds of crime may result in fines up to C$2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly permit AML breaches are personally exposed to criminal charges; the corporate veil does not shield willful non-compliance with anti-money-laundering statutes.
