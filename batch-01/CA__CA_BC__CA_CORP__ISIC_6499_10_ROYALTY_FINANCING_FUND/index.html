Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and income tax registration portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/cra-login-business.html); British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, through the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account by completing Form RC1 or by using the CRA's 'Register for a GST/HST, payroll, or import/export account' feature in My Business Account. Non-resident registrants may file Form RC460 (Application for GST/HST Registration for Non-Residents). For BC PST, a separate registration is required through eTaxBC using the Provincial Online Services application. A Canadian representative must be authorized by power of attorney or equivalent mandate; proof of non-resident status and director/officer identification is required. The BN is issued immediately for online applications or within several weeks for paper filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation (federal or provincial) is a separate taxpayer from its shareholders. Resident Canadian corporations are subject to federal Part I income tax under the Income Tax Act and BC income tax under the Income Tax Act (British Columbia). There is no pass-through election available for a corporation.

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification exists under Canadian tax law. A corporation may qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) if it is incorporated in Canada and not controlled by non-residents or public shareholders. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are limited by anti-avoidance rules, including section 18.5 of the Income Tax Act, which denies interest deductions where the interest is not subject to normal taxation in the recipient jurisdiction. No general hybrid-election mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide gross taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) in a calendar quarter or over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters, unless voluntary registration is desired. For BC PST, there is no small-supplier exemption for non-exempt retail vendors; registration is required once the vendor makes taxable sales in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports the goods as soon as reasonably possible after delivery and the purchaser is a non-resident at the time of purchase. Selected services are zero-rated under section 9 of the Excise Tax Act when provided to a non-resident and performed wholly outside Canada, or when all consideration for the service is attributable to services performed outside Canada. For an ISIC 6499 revenue-based financing fund, most financial services are GST/HST-exempt rather than zero-rated; any separately charged financing fees that do not qualify as an exempt financial service may be zero-rated if the conditions in section 9 are met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Canada Export Declaration filed via CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign-exchange confirmation of payment received from the non-resident purchaser

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for GST/HST. Electronic invoices are acceptable if they are legible, complete, and traceable under CRA books-and-records requirements. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI by monthly, quarterly, or annual filiers. BC PST returns are filed separately through eTaxBC with payment obligations based on the PST collected.

If the CRA determines that export zero-rating was claimed without a qualifying export, the supply will be reassessed at the regular GST rate plus applicable PST, with compound interest from the filing date. Penalties include late-filing penalties, false statements or omissions under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the net tax, and criminal prosecution under section 331 for tax evasion, including fines and imprisonment. Any refund claimed under a zero-rated export may be clawed back in full if the required evidence is not produced within CRA's statutory review period, and a deemed non-resident supply can arise if the funds are not repatriated within a reasonable period, resulting in additional penalties and retrospective reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current and capital account convertibility for ISIC 6499 royalty financing funds. Dividends, interest, royalties, and redemption proceeds may be remitted offshore without exchange control approval, subject only to Canadian withholding tax (Part XIII) and FATCA/CRS reporting. No profits repatriation license or central bank authorization is required. Chartered banks may nevertheless apply enhanced due diligence for medium-risk non-dilutive financing vehicles, requiring source-of-funds and commercial substance documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion obligation exists under Canadian law. Exporters and financing entities are free to hold and deploy foreign currency proceeds. However, receipts above CAD 10,000 in a single transaction or related transactions are reportable to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); Ministry of Finance (exchange policy); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border currency transaction reporting. No statutory foreign exchange control licensing authority exists because Canada maintains free convertibility under IMF Article VIII.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Canada does not recognize EU-style Electronic Money Institution licensing. Payment service providers are regulated as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and must register with FINTRAC, while banking-like payment activities are generally accessible only through a chartered bank or provincial payment institution licence. Offshore digital EMIs may support Canadian entities as secondary rails, but primary treasury accounts remain with tier-1 chartered banks due to CAD clearing, deposit insurance, and regulatory certainty. Feasibility for the royalty financing fund is low for primary operational use and medium for ancillary payment routing, requiring the EMI to be FINTRAC-registered if it holds Canadian funds or clears CAD.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined Canadian federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate of 27% (federal net Part I rate 15% and BC provincial rate 12%) on taxable income of a non-CCPC business corporation; the basic federal statutory rate is 38%, reduced by a 10% provincial abatement and a 13% general rate reduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII Canadian non-resident withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

File no later than six months after the fiscal-period end; balance of tax payable ordinarily due within two months after fiscal-period end, extended to three months only for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations that claim the small business deduction.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Schedule 1 / Line 180 – Taxable Income Reconciles financial net income/loss to federal taxable income after reserves, tax deductions and prior-period loss carryforwards; includes total business income, property income, taxable capital gains and eligible dividends from Canadian corporations. A non-resident corporation computes Canadian taxable income only to the extent of Part I Canadian-source active business carried on through a permanent establishment and Canadian taxable capital gains; treaty PE income must be isolated and supporting T2 schedules and a treaty business declaration retained. Late T2 filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax, plus 1% per full month of delinquency, capped at 12 months (17% maximum); repeat failures within three years attract a 10% base plus 2% per month up to 24 months. Gross-negligence misrepresentations add a 50% civil penalty on the understated tax; unpaid balances accrue daily compound prescribed interest, generally CRA prime plus 4%.
Line 200 / Schedule 1 – Federal Part I Tax Computes federal tax on taxable income at 38%, then subtracts the 10% provincial abatement for income earned in Canada and the 13% federal general rate reduction, producing the 15% federal headline rate; non-refundable tax credits, including SR&ED, are entered here. Non-residents are generally entitled to the general rate reduction but remain subject to Part XIV branch tax on Canadian PE after-tax earnings; the provincial abatement is available only in respect of Canadian provincial income. Late T2 filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax, plus 1% per full month of delinquency, capped at 12 months (17% maximum); repeat failures within three years attract a 10% base plus 2% per month up to 24 months. Gross-negligence misrepresentations add a 50% civil penalty on the understated tax; unpaid balances accrue daily compound prescribed interest, generally CRA prime plus 4%.

Non-residents may apply non-capital losses only against income that is subject to Canadian Part I tax, particularly Canadian-source PE business/taxable property income; foreign losses must not be netted. Late T2 filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax, plus 1% per full month of delinquency, capped at 12 months (17% maximum); repeat failures within three years attract a 10% base plus 2% per month up to 24 months. Schedule 13 / BC Provincial Tax Calculates BC income tax at 12% of taxable income allocated to BC; provincial tax credits reduce the tax; the BC return is filed on the same T2 form. For a non-resident corporation carrying on business through a BC permanent establishment, allocate BC income using the uniform permanent-establishment formula; if no BC PE, no provincial tax is due. Line 44 / Summary of Total Tax Payable Tie-out of Part I tax, additional BC provincial tax and special taxes; includes total payable before withholdings and instalments. Non-resident corporations must also include Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source rents, royalties, dividends, interest and certain other payments in the NR4/Part XIII remittances rather than in the T2 total. Late T2 filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax, plus 1% per full month of delinquency, capped at 12 months (17% maximum); repeat failures within three years attract a 10% base plus 2% per month up to 24 months. Gross-negligence misrepresentations add a 50% civil penalty on the understated tax; unpaid balances accrue daily compound prescribed interest, generally CRA prime plus 4%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and T106 Information Return triggers apply to all non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents whose aggregate value is above C$1,000,000 in a fiscal period; Canadian members of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above C$750,000,000 must maintain OECD-compliant Master File/Local File documentation and file a Country-by-Country report.

Economic substance scope: Canada's equivalent of economic-substance review is the GAAR under ITA s.245, the sham doctrine applied by courts, and treaty limitation-of-benefits rules. A royalty-focus financing fund must have a substantive BC office, employees, active board/credit decisions, real cash flow, audited records, and arm's-length intercompany agreements with proper risk allocation. If CRA recharacterizes royalty payments or capital structure as artificial, it may disallow deductions, reject loss claims, deny treaty benefits, reassess under s.247(2), and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1) felony: imprisonment up to 5 years; fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax if misrepresentation was knowing or grossly negligent, plus reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed, participated in, or signed false tax filings are jointly and severally liable for resulting penalties where their act was the effective cause; criminal convictions attach personally; flight risk can trigger travel bans and Ministry of Justice extradition/warrant measures.
