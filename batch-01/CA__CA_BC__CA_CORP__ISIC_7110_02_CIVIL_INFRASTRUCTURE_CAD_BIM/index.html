Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full and unrestricted convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions under a free-floating CAD regime. Profit remittance is unrestricted: dividends may be paid and transferred outward after BCBCA solvency tests are met, subject only to federal Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, reduced by applicable income tax treaty) and bank/FINTRAC reporting of cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. No Central Bank permit, genuine-transaction test, or exchange-control approval is required for dividend, interest, or service-fee repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, forced conversion, or repatriation timeline for export or service-export proceeds under the Currency Act. Invoices may be denominated and settled in USD or any freely convertible currency; a CAD-denominated operating account and a separate USD sub-account may be maintained simultaneously. Cross-border electronic transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 are reported to FINTRAC by the financial institution as large cross-border EFT reports. Retained foreign currency may be held indefinitely and converted at prevailing market rates without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange policy is administered under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act. Canada has no dedicated exchange-control agency; FINTRAC supervises cross-border financial transaction reporting for AML purposes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High as an auxiliary rail; low as a primary treasury account. Wise (registered as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC and compliant with the BC Money Services Businesses Act) may be used for low-value cross-border disbursements and multi-currency settlement. Offshore electronic money institutions without FINTRAC registration or without compliance with the federal Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), supervised by the Bank of Canada, cannot lawfully hold or settle CAD for Canadian resident corporations. Primary corporate banking should remain with a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank; EMI balances are uninsured and unsuitable for capital escrow or large wire aggregation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined (15% federal general Part I rate + 12% British Columbia provincial general rate); 11% combined (9% federal + 2% BC) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income for a Canada-controlled private corporation (CCPC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders (s. 212(2)); BC imposes no additional provincial dividend tax; treaty-reduced rates: 5% for a US corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% of voting shares, else 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Filing-due date is 6 months after fiscal year-end (e.g., 31 December year-end due 30 June); balance-due date is 2 months after year-end for non-CCPCs and 3 months after year-end for CCPCs.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 reporting threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate arm's-length value of goods and/or CAD 50,000 of services, interest, rents, royalties and other transactions with any one non-resident in a taxation year; CRA requires full contemporaneous documentation under s. 247(4) whenever transactions are priced between related parties, regardless of dollar value, and treats the taxpayer as bearing the burden of proof for penalties.

Economic substance scope: Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations are not legislated in Canada; instead the CRA applies (i) the OECD place-of-effective-management (POEM) residency test, (ii) GAAR (s. 245) to artificial transactions, (iii) s. 247(2)(b) re-characterization of commercially irrational cross-border structures, and (iv) bare-bones 'mailbox' entities are disregarded for treaty entitlement under the Multilateral Convention (BEPS MLI Article 7/9, beneficial-ownership rules). A BC corporation performing civil-infrastructure CAD/BIM services must maintain employees, decision-making, risk-taking and bank-account control in BC for each invoiced project.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Indictable offence: a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: 50%-200% fine and imprisonment up to 2 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; CRA criminal referral to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada and publication of convicted taxpayers.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in or consented to the false return face personal criminal charges, personal restitution orders, seizure of personal assets, prohibition on leaving Canada upon criminal indictment, and, after conviction, a travel ban; the corporate veil is pierced under the doctrine of fraud.
