Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c. 57 (B.C. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. A B.C. company may be incorporated with nominal capital and no escrow or paid-up capital confirmation. Directors may issue shares for such consideration as they determine; a single subscriber share at CAD 1.00 (approx. USD 0.73) is sufficient. No escrow bank account is required at formation.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a registered-office-only SPV. If a physical office is established, obtain a municipal occupancy or building permit and fire safety inspection clearance from the local fire authority; if employees are engaged, register with WorkSafeBC for occupational injury insurance and comply with the Workers Compensation Act and OHS Regulation, including structural load and electrical safety certificates for the premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. A B.C. company may be incorporated with nominal capital and no escrow or paid-up capital confirmation. Directors may issue shares for such consideration as they determine; a single subscriber share at CAD 1.00 (approx. USD 0.73) is sufficient. No escrow bank account is required at formation.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable at formation for a passively held PE buyout SPV. Category: None required — the SPV is classified as a financial holding and administrative vehicle; no manufacturing, mining or hazardous activity operating permit is required at formation. If the SPV becomes the direct operator of an acquired business, it must obtain the applicable municipal business licence and any sectoral operating permit.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — legal tax administration for Canadian federal corporate income tax and GST/HST; online portal: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). British Columbia Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC) administers Provincial Sales Tax (PST) separately.

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian Business Corporation (federal or BC provincial) formed under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Business Corporations Act (British Columbia): obtain incorporation or registration, then apply to CRA via Business Registration Online to acquire a 9-digit Business Number (BN); activate a GST/HST account only when taxable supplies equal or exceed C$30,000 unless voluntary registration is chosen; open customs/import-export account for export transactions; obtain BC PST account with BC Ministry of Finance if PST-collectable sales in BC are made. Non-resident corporations without Canadian presence apply to CRA through Form RC1 and may be required to provide security in a form and amount acceptable to the Minister under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act. BC PST registration follows a direct application via eTaxBC.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a Canadian Business Corporation (federal or BC provincial) formed under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Business Corporations Act (British Columbia): obtain incorporation or registration, then apply to CRA via Business Registration Online to acquire a 9-digit Business Number (BN); activate a GST/HST account only when taxable supplies equal or exceed C$30,000 unless voluntary registration is chosen; open customs/import-export account for export transactions; obtain BC PST account with BC Ministry of Finance if PST-collectable sales in BC are made. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box system; tax classification is imposed by the Income Tax Act (ITA) and Excise Tax Act (ETA). A Business Corporation is a separate taxable person at the entity level. Elections relevant to a PE buyout SPV include: (i) ITA s.93(2) election on distributions from a foreign affiliate to adjust proceeds or avoid deemed dividend treatment; (ii) ITA s.216(1) election for non-resident controlled rental income; (iii) ETA s.156 election between closely related corporations to zero-rate intercorporate supplies; (iv) ETA s.273 joint venture election; (v) the absence of any OECD-style hybrid entity election means a Canadian SPV cannot be classified as a branch or disregarded entity; foreign hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.94B-94D) operate mechanically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies of goods and services, being the mandatory registration threshold; voluntary registration is available before the threshold. BC PST: no general registration threshold in the same sense; any seller making taxable sales of tangible personal property, taxable services, or admissions in BC must register for and collect PST, subject to specific exemptions and exemptions for non-resident sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated for GST/HST if the registrant sells tangible personal property by way of sale and either delivers the goods outside Canada, exports them under CRA-approved conditions, or supplies them to a non-resident for removal from Canada; documentary evidence must be retained. Services provided to non-residents are zero-rated only where the service is supplied to a non-resident person and is not performed in Canada or does not relate to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada at the time of performance. Financial services supplied by a PE SPV are generally exempt, not zero-rated, unless a specifically exported service exception applies. For BC PST, goods exported by the seller in continuous transit to an out-of-province destination may be exempt under the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act rules.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System CERS summary declaration)
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or equivalent contract of carriage naming the exporter and destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the exported goods and buyer
  • Bank foreign-remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103 or credit advice confirming receipt of export proceeds, accepted in lieu of an Inward Remittance Certificate)

No real-time tax authority clearance, fiscal API, or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices and records containing the prescribed Excise Tax Act information and retain electronic records in a machine-readable format. Registrants with annual gross taxable revenue above C$1.5M must file GST/HST returns electronically using CRA GST/HST NETFILE; all registrants generally report through their GST/HST accounts online. BC PST returns are filed electronically via eTaxBC; no per-invoice pre-approval is required, but sellers must maintain complete sales records, PST remittance records, and zero-rated or exemption substantiation documentation.

CRA may reassess zero-rated exports within the normal four-year reassessment period, with no time limit if misrepresentation or fraud is established; denial of zero-rating results in clawback of input tax credits and assessment of GST/HST at the applicable rate, plus BC PST if in-province use is found. Penalties include excise tax late or inaccurate penalties, false statements penalties under ITA s.163(2), and criminal prosecution under Excise Tax Act s.280(1) or Income Tax Act s.239 for fraudulent export zero-rating schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible. Canada has maintained no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions since the abolition of the Foreign Exchange Control Board in 1951. Inbound equity, shareholder loans, intercompany funding, dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds require no central-bank approval or exchange-control permit. Profit and capital remittance to non-resident investors is unrestricted; the sole material friction is Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), at a statutory 25% generally reduced to 5–15% under tax treaties including the Canada–US Convention. British Columbia imposes no sub-federal currency or capital-movement restrictions, although securities-law filings and the B.C. Business Corporations Act transparency register apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, conversion ratio, or remittance timeline on export receipts. Exporters and SPVs may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts without repatriation deadlines. For a PE buyout SPV, no direct export proceeds are anticipated; if a portfolio target generates export revenues, such receipts may be held in USD or other freely convertible currencies without mandatory conversion. Only statistical reporting thresholds under the Statistics Act (International Transactions Reporting System, ITRS) and FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting may apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision and foreign-bank entry licensing delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering administration by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) regulates non-bank financial services within the province.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Canada does not recognise the EU 'Electronic Money Institution' licence class; a foreign EMI wishing to service a Canadian client is instead treated as a money services business (MSB) requiring FINTRAC registration and, within British Columbia, an MSB/remittance licence from BCFSA. Offshore fintech platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are viable only for low-value ancillary disbursements such as legal fees, notary costs, and research subscriptions. They are unsuitable as the primary capital-escrow or syndicated-loan clearing bank for a PE buyout SPV because the sponsor and senior lenders will require a Schedule I Canadian bank to hold escrow balances, provide Lynx/Fedwire settlement finality, and execute an account control agreement perfecting security over deposit accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan injections are routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 over correspondent banking networks. CAD-denominated payments settle irrevocably on Lynx, the Bank of Canada's high-value real-time gross settlement system (the successor to LVTS); USD payments clear via Fedwire, CHIPS, or the Canadian USD Settlement Service. The recipient Canadian bank must file a FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report for any transaction at or above CAD 10,000 or an intentionally structured series below that threshold. No exchange-control permit or central-bank approval is required. Recommended BPM6 direct-investment classification: 'Direct investment – Equity capital other than reinvested earnings' (IMF BPM6/Statistics Canada); the remittance narrative should specify 'Capital injection – equity', 'Intercompany loan proceeds', or 'Acquisition consideration'. Funds must be credited to the capital escrow/trust account subject to a tri-party account control agreement in favour of the syndicate agent or senior lender.Outbound settlements execute through the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for wire disbursements to advisers, escrow agents, sellers, and lenders in CAD/USD/EUR/GBP; (ii) MT700/MT707 documentary letters of credit for trade counterparties, subject to UCP600; (iii) Lynx for same-day irrevocable CAD high-value transfers above CAD 50,000; (iv) Fedwire/CHIPS for USD disbursements; and (v) Payments Canada Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for low-value domestic batches. All disbursements require dual-signing authority, a board-approved payee white-list, and bank-side sanctions/OFAC screening. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.00% combined federal-provincial general corporate income tax rate (federal 15.00% + British Columbia provincial 12.00%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% general statutory withholding rate under Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders; reduced to 5% under Canada-US DTA for non-resident holding company owning at least 10% of voting shares and meeting limitation-on-benefits provisions; 5% under Canada-UK DTA for beneficial owner corporation holding at least 10% voting shares

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million or greater in gross revenue triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under subsection 247(4); for a private equity SPV with no intercompany cross-border transactions explicitly with non-arm's length parties, documentation is still advisable where shareholder loans or management fees exceed CAD 50,000; complete Local File and Master File recommended when aggregate intercompany transactions exceed CAD 5 million

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to BVI/Cayman; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 denies tax benefits from transactions lacking a genuine economic purpose; the Canadian substance doctrine requires: real office, full-time management and employees, board governance, bank accounts in Canada, and risk management capacity in Canada; for a leveraged buyout SPV, the CRA will scrutinize whether the Canadian entity has substance distinct from the offshore manager; the 'profit allocation' under Canada's transfer pricing rules (section 247(2)) adjusts the SPV's profits to reflect the functions, assets, and risks actually borne in Canada; a bare nominee SPV with offshore management may trigger a deemed permanent establishment under the treaty and subject full Canadian tax on 100% of acquisition gains; CRA Form T5013 and Schedule 91 requires detailed functional analysis on cross-border financing

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion under Income Tax Act section 238 and section 239(1)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years per count; fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded; civil assessment of abandoned tax plus interest at prescribed rate plus gross negligence penalty (50% of understated tax) per subsection 163(2); CRA may issue Form RC220 for criminal investigation

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1(1) for unremitted withholding taxes and GST; CRA may assess directors personally for 100% of corporate tax unpaid for less than 3 years after cessation of directorship; travel bans under section 238(3) for failure to appear; personal bankruptcy does not discharge wilful tax evasion liabilities
Thin Capitalization and Excessive Interest Financing

Statutory sanction: Interest deduction denied to the extent of loan exceeding the 1.5:1 ratio; reassessment with arrears interest on the additional tax; penalty under subsection 163(2) of 50% on gross negligence where the ratio is deliberately misreported; full adjustment of Part XIII withholding on deemed dividend treatment of excessive interest

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for failure to withhold and remit Part XIII tax on disallowed interest payments within the statutory timeframe; CRA can issue a director's liability assessment and pursue personal assets; travel ban under section 227.1(2) implicated in serious repetition
Treaty Shopping and Abuse of DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief denied retroactively; reassessment at 25% statutory withholding with interest and penalties; GAAR application under section 245 may disallow all treaty benefits and impose a 50% gross negligence penalty; CRA administrative disqualification of the DTA filing; potential double tax due to foreign tax no longer refunded

Director personal liability: Director and corporate officers exposed to civil assessments if they knowingly signed treaty claim forms (NR301) containing false declarations; criminal sanction under section 238(2) for false certificates; personal restitution orders in the amount of the denied treaty benefit
Failure to File Transfer Pricing Documentation and Information Returns

Statutory sanction: Penalty under subsection 247(3) of a minimum of CAD 5,000 to CAD 25,000 per failure, plus CAD 1,000 per month per late month up to 24 months; additional penalty of 10% of net transfer pricing adjustment amounts; no time-limit on reassessment for mispriced intercompany transactions; CRA may impose foreign reporting penalties up to CAD 2,500 per unreported transaction

Director personal liability: Directors liable for knowing misrepresentation of the transfer pricing documentation; criminal liability under section 238 for failure to provide documents upon demand; injunctive relief compelling personal filing where management resides outside Canada
AML, Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime (BC Securities and Proceeds of Crime Act)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to CAD 1 million per count; imprisonment up to 10 years under the Code section 462.31; subject to forfeiture order under the Criminal Code section 462.37; FATF and FINTRAC administrative penalties up to CAD 2 million for non-reporting; the BC Civil Forfeiture Office may seize property where the owner cannot establish lawful origin

Director personal liability: Directors criminally liable where the corporation is a mere instrumentality of fraud; civil conspiracy damages in acquisition litigation; travel bans pending cross-border money laundering investigations; personal bankruptcy not available to shield domestic liability; permanent debarment from serving as director in British Columbia under the Business Corporations Act for wilful misconduct
Shell Company / Sham and Empty Director Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the corporate existence under the 'sham doctrine' and assess the shareholder personally on all income; the corporate veil is pierced; the SPV is deemed to have a permanent establishment of the foreign sponsor under Canada tax treaty Article V; imposition of Canadian branch tax of 25% under Part XIV; deregistration under the BC Business Corporations Act in an administrative strike-off and liabilities deemed in the name of the sole director

Director personal liability: Director's statutory fiduciary duty is breached; the minister may apply to the BC Supreme Court for a compliance order and an injunction preventing further telecommuting directorship; personal liability for all unpaid wages, vacation pay, and taxes under the Employment Standards Act and the Income Tax Act; travel restriction order issued under the Canada Border Services Agency Act where significant unpaid liabilities; imprisonment not exceeding 2 years for failure to comply with a court order restoring proper substance
