Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal electronic filing and registration through CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). For British Columbia provincial indirect taxes (PST), the competent authority is the BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC portal).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a BC limited partnership with non-resident partners) obtains a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (BN/RT) by (i) filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' electronically through CRA Business Registration Online, by mail, or by fax; or (ii) registering through the CRA My Business Account portal. The GST/HST program account is a 9-digit BN followed by an 'RT' program identifier (e.g., 123456789 RT0001). Registration is mandatory under ETA s. 240(1) for any person making taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a commercial activity, including non-residents; a non-resident registrant may be required to post security under ETA s. 240(3). Foreign entities without a Canadian address must appoint a representative (Form RC59) and may register by paper; CRA will assign the BN within approximately 15-20 business days. For BC, the entity must also register for the PT (Provincial Sales Tax) account via eTaxBC if it makes taxable supplies of goods or specified services in BC; international air-freight brokerage services are generally outside BC PST. The LP must also complete BC provincial corporate registry filings (BC Corporate Online) to obtain its incorporation/registration number.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: A BC limited partnership is a hybrid flow-through entity under Canadian federal and BC provincial income tax law. Under ITA s. 96(1), the partnership itself is not a taxpayer; income, gains, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners in proportion to their partnership interests. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: A BC limited partnership is a hybrid flow-through entity under Canadian federal and BC provincial income tax law. Under ITA s. 96(1), the partnership itself is not a taxpayer; income, gains, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners in proportion to their partnership interests. For GST/HST purposes, the Excise Tax Act (ETA s. 272.1) deems the partnership to be a separate 'person' required to register in the partnership name, with each partner jointly and severally liable for any GST/HST liability, penalties, and interest. The territorial scope of taxation follows Canadian-source-income rules: non-resident partners are taxable under Part I ITA only on income from carrying on business in Canada and certain Canadian-source income (ITA s. 2(3)), while the partnership's Canadian-source business income is subject to Part XIII withholding (ITA s. 194) on the non-resident partners' shares.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' or formal entity-classification election; classification is determined by statute and case law. A BC LP is mandatorily treated as a pass-through for income tax. Relevant statutory elections available include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 96(3) aligning the partnership's fiscal period with the taxation year of its members; (ii) reductions in Canadian withholding tax on non-resident partners' profit shares under an applicable Canada tax treaty, or an election to file a Part I return in lieu of Part XIII withholding in specific circumstances; (iii) GST/HST registration in the partnership name under ETA s. 272.1, with partners submitting a joint election to allocate liability and reporting; (iv) election to use the Quick Method of accounting under ETA ss. 227-228 (generally unsuitable for a charter brokerage due to high input purchases, but available to small suppliers); and (v) treaty-based exemption or reduced-rate certificates for Canadian-source service income. Hybrid mismatch restrictions under ITA ss. 18.4(4), 18.6, 20.14 and the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework apply; no domestic check-the-box hybrid election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (CAD) 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 148(1): registration is compulsory once the person's worldwide taxable supplies (including the LP's share of supplies, and including non-resident supplies to the extent the market is Canada) exceed C$30,000 in the current calendar quarter and the preceding three calendar quarters, or, where the person has been deemed not to be a small supplier on an earlier registration, remain over C$30,000. Non-residents without a permanent establishment in Canada may be required to register earlier under the ETA 'carrying on business in Canada' rule and CRA administrative policy; the threshold does not exempt a non-resident that makes taxable supplies in Canada unless the non-resident is a small supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The following supplies made by the air cargo charter broker are zero-rated for GST/HST purposes under Part V of Schedule VI of the ETA: (i) a supply of a freight transportation service in respect of goods shipped from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada, shipped from a place outside Canada to a place in Canada, or shipped from a place outside Canada to a place outside Canada (ETA s. 7(2)-(6)); (ii) a supply of a service of arranging for such a freight transportation service, which is deemed to be a supply of the underlying freight transportation service and thus also zero-rated (ETA s. 7(1) and related deeming rules); and (iii) a supply of an exported service made to a non-resident person where the service is not performed on tangible personal property situated in Canada, is not in respect of real property in Canada, and does not primarily benefit the non-resident's Canadian business (ETA s. 20). To qualify, the broker must maintain contemporaneous documentation demonstrating that the goods physically crossed an international border via air carriage and that the recipient of the arranging service was the non-resident cargo owner, foreign airline, overseas freight forwarder, or other non-resident counterparty. Input tax credits on related expenses (commissions, fuel analysis, telecommunication, office rent) remain fully claimable to the extent the supplies are used in the zero-rated commercial activity, producing a refund position.

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API clearance, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary; CRA accepts electronic invoices provided they are legible, complete, and retained in an accessible format under ETA ss. 286-287. CRA supports voluntary use of the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing framework (piloted 2022-2025) for Government-as-a-Customer procurement, but there is no statutory API clearance obligation. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST34) applies to registrants with worldwide annual taxable revenue exceeding C$1.5M, including non-resident registrants; paper filing is otherwise permitted. No real-time validation, digital stamping, or government-clearing requirement exists for commercial B2B transactions in Canada.

CRA subjects export zero-rating claims, especially those involving related non-resident parties, to heightened audit scrutiny through its International and Large Business Directorate. If the substantiation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent, CRA may assess the 5% GST on the full brokerage commission and deny input tax credits, resulting in a net recovery of tax with arrears interest under ETA s. 280. Civil penalties include: (i) ETA s. 281.1 penalty for failure to remit or knowingly making false statements (the greater of C$500 and 25% of the net tax penalty base); (ii) ETA s. 285 gross-negligence penalty equal to 25% of the understated tax or over-claimed refund; (iii) ETA s. 327.1 penalty of 25% of a falsely claimed refund or credit; (iv) Part XIII withholding penalties under ITA s. 227.1 and s. 235 where non-resident partner profits are unreported; and (v) criminal prosecution for fraud under Criminal Code s. 380 and tax evasion under ITA s. 239(1) and ETA s. 329, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA's normal reassessment period is 4 years from the date of the notice of assessment for a GST/HST registered person (ETA s. 298(1)); where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, or willful default is shown, CRA may reassess without time limit (ETA s. 298(2)), and the clawback of refunds with compound arrears interest predating the reassessment period is permissible. Export zero-rating claims must therefore be supported by auditable contemporaneous export documentation and bank remittance evidence retained for at least 6 years (ETA s. 286).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current-account and capital-account transactions, having accepted IMF Article VIII status. There are no exchange-control approvals, quotas, proportionality rules, or waiting periods applicable to capital injections, profit repatriation, loan repayments, or dividend flows. A British Columbia limited partnership distributing profits to non-resident partners is exposed only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25 per cent, often treaty-reduced) and to provincial income-tax filings; no FX clearance or capital-control authorization is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, forced-conversion, or statutory repatriation timeline for export or service proceeds. Air-cargo charter brokerage commissions earned in foreign currency may be retained offshore or repatriated at treasury discretion. The relevant legal constraints are non-FX: (i) the Export and Import Permits Act for controlled or sanctioned goods/countries; (ii) the Corruption of Foreign Public Officials Act for cross-border payments; and (iii) Part X of the Income Tax Act for foreign accrual property income attribution where a Canadian-resident partner holds a controlled foreign affiliate. None of these creates a CAD conversion or in-Canada surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), the national monetary and central-banking authority; foreign-exchange liberalization and sanctions policy are jointly administered with the Department of Finance Canada and Global Affairs Canada; prudential supervision of chartered banks sits with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF transaction reporting is supervised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium - Canada maintains no distinct EMI licence class; foreign digital EMIs operate as Money Services Businesses registered with FINTRAC or through a Schedule-I bank partner. MSB-registered fintechs (Wise, Airwallex, Corpay, etc.) are acceptable as supplementary cross-border disbursement rails and for FX-layer conversions, but they are not acceptable as the principal clearing account for an ISIC 5229 air-cargo charter broker owing to the Enhanced Risk rating of the logistics sector. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners are routed via SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire in USD/CAD to the LP's clearing account at an OSFI-regulated Schedule-I bank. Canada maintains no prior-approval regime and no mandatory purpose-code permission for FDI capital inflows. The receiving bank will apply an economic-purpose classification in its systemic reporting, and all cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are captured for Statistics Canada's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS). The remitter should reference 'LP Capital Contribution - [Partner Name]' in SWIFT field 70, attach the executed subscription schedule and the BC limited-partnership registration certificate at first funding, and complete FINTRAC-compliant counterparty due diligence before settlement.Aircraft-charter prepayments and cargo-freight settlements are executed through: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wires to the operator's bank; (ii) MT202 COV for third-party FX bank settlement; and (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary LC) where the charter operator requires bank-issued payment security. Canadian-dollar high-value domestic flows clear over Payments Canada's LVTS/Lynx real-time wholesale system; USD flows clear over Fedwire via the bank's correspondent network; sub-CAD 500,000 domestic payments may use the ACSS Automated Clearing Settlement System. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium - Canada maintains no distinct EMI licence class; foreign digital EMIs operate as Money Services Businesses registered with FINTRAC or through a Schedule-I bank partner. MSB-registered fintechs (Wise, Airwallex, Corpay, etc.) are acceptable as supplementary cross-border disbursement rails and for FX-layer conversions, but they are not acceptable as the principal clearing account for an ISIC 5229 air-cargo charter broker owing to the Enhanced Risk rating of the logistics sector. The core USD/CAD wire activity should flow through an OSFI-regulated financial institution to preserve correspondent-banking provenance and FINTRAC compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose a statutory monetary threshold for Local File/Master File; however, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under s.247(4) ITA is expected for all non-arm's length cross-border transactions. The CRA commonly applies a risk-rating materiality benchmark of CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate transaction value.

Economic substance scope: The Income Tax Act's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) denies tax benefits where a transaction or series of transactions lacks a genuine business purpose, economic substance, or bona fide non-tax purpose. For Limited Partnerships, CRA reviews the allocation of profits, partner capital at risk, and contractual arrangements for charter brokerage functions; a BC limited partnership must demonstrate real operational substance (employees, contracts, administrative functions) and cannot arbitrage treaty benefits where the arrangement is artificial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years (s.238 and s.239 ITA).

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner or corporate limited partner who knowingly participate in the fraud may be assessed under s.227.1 ITA for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding, and may be subject to civil restitution, freezing orders, travel bans, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess on an arm's length basis and impose a s.247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the upward transfer pricing adjustment (net of certain language), plus arrears interest. Reassessment cannot be appealed without completing formal objection procedures.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or misleading transfer pricing documentation may be subject to penalties under s.163.2 of the Income Tax Act (planning penalties); they may also face director liability for unpaid withholding amounts and civil prosecutions for participation in the misstatement.
Earnings Stripping / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of excess interest deductions, recalculated taxable income, and interest on arrears. Thin capitalization and earnings-stripping adjustments trigger a 10% penalty under s.247(3) where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate partners may be liable for the resulting withholding tax on Part XIII interest payments if they knowingly cause the partnership to omit withholding, and may be subject to garnishment and personal bankruptcy proceedings for unreported tax liabilities.
AML / Money-Laundering Infraction

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code offence of laundering proceeds of crime (s.462.31): imprisonment up to 10 years; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 1,000,000 for entities per violation; seizure and forfeiture of property used in commission of the offence.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who directed, authorized, or consented to the money-laundering activity may be charged as parties to the offence, subject to fines, asset forfeiture, criminal imprisonment, and applicable travel bans; civil suits for breach of fiduciary duty may also arise.
