Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) as the federal business/TIN/GST-HST registration portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Programs Branch) and eTaxBC portal for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and other provincial accounts.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian Business Corporation obtains its federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account through CRA BRO or automatically via incorporation and corporate taxing filings; a BC-based entity completes registration of its BN with BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC, including a PST vendor number where applicable. Foreign-owned or non-resident corporations with Canadian-source activities apply for a CRA tax account (often using Form RC391) and file the prescribed T2/NR returns as necessary; no additional statutory TIN is issued beyond the BN and, where relevant, the BC PST vendor account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A resident Canadian corporation is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act at the entity level on worldwide income; the operation is not a pass-through entity. British Columbia corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Tax Collection Agreement as an addition to the federal taxable income.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime; corporations are per se taxable entities. Relevant elections and designations include functional currency reporting (ITA s. 261), foreign accrual property income and foreign affiliate election/designations, the GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s. 167), annual GST/HST filing election, and technical rules in the Canadian hybrid-mismatch legislation (ss. 18.4 to 18.6 ITA) that limit rather than expand elective recharacterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in any four consecutive calendar quarters unless the supplier is a small supplier under ETA s. 148; exporters of zero-rated services may elect to register voluntarily to claim input tax credits. BC PST: registration is triggered by engaging in taxable retail sales in BC; the GST small-supplier threshold is not a PST exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of offshore software engineering and development services is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act when made to a non-resident recipient, not in connection with goods located in Canada or real property in Canada, and when the supplier maintains evidence that the recipient is non-resident and the benefit is consumed outside Canada. In BC, exported services are not generally subject to PST.

  • Customs export declaration / CBSA B13A or electronic export reporting for any tangible software media or hardware crossing the border; for pure digital services, a service export invoice and delivery/acceptance evidence.
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill or carrier waybill for physical exports, and for digital deliverables, delivery logs and a signed acceptance certificate from the non-resident client.
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank/SWIFT transfer evidence from the non-resident payer evidencing settlement in foreign currency or deposit into a Canadian bank account.

No real-time fiscal stamping or central invoice clearance mandate exists for GST/HST or BC PST. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework; CRA accepts electronic records but requires retained documentary support; GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA portals, and PST returns and payments are filed through eTaxBC, not invoice-by-invoice.

CRA may claw back zero-rating where the exporter fails to maintain acceptable documentary proof; the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Canada and GST is assessed, with compounding interest. Penalties for false statements in GST returns are generally the greater of CAD 100 or 50% of the tax assessable, plus gross-negligence penalties, administrative fines, deregistration, and in fraudulent cases criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime without exchange controls. Current-account transactions and capital-account transactions are both freely convertible into CAD and from CAD; there are no governmental approvals required for cross-border capital injections, shareholder loans, principal repayments, or dividend profit remittances. Remittance timing is unrestricted, but dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents are subject to Canadian withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaty relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement. Exporters are not obligated to convert foreign currency earnings into CAD, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts with Canadian financial institutions. Cross-border transaction reporting obligations arise only indirectly under the PCMLTFA (large cash and EFT reporting), FATCA/CRS reporting, and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation and supervision of federally incorporated banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange operations and official international reserves are managed by the Bank of Canada under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise Business and Payoneer are feasible as supplementary clearing rails for low-value operational disbursements, employee contractor payroll, and smaller intercompany payments. They are not Schedule I Canadian banks and cannot provide capital escrow custody, commercial letter of credit issuance, or OSFI-insured deposit products. Use as non-bank e-money carriers is legally permissible but is rated Medium for subordinated treasury routing and Low for primary capital and balance-sheet liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections should be executed as SWIFT MT103 wires from the parent's bank to the Canadian bank's CAD or USD account, using the beneficiary corporation's legal name, bank BIC/SWIFT, and the operating account or designated capital escrow account number. USD wires may require a U.S. correspondent bank ABA/CHIPS routing code; CAD wires settle through Payments Canada Lynx. Clearing is initiated through the receiving bank's treasury desk, subject to PCMLTFA AML compliance and FINTRAC EFT reporting thresholds. Since Canada has no binding FX purpose code regime, the purpose field must clearly state 'Share Capital Subscription' or 'Intercompany Equity Injection'; the bank uses this narrative to align with its own onboarding business-intent profile and balance-of-payments classification.Outbound trade disbursements for software services, subcontractors, and operational invoices are cleared through SWIFT MT103 direct payments, Payments Canada Lynx for CAD settlement, and correspondent U.S. rails for USD. For significant procurement or delivery-based contracts, commercial letters of credit are issued through SWIFT MT700/MT710 fields to the beneficiary's bank. CAD ACH-equivalent clearing for low-value domestic payments is handled through Payments Canada's automated clearing settlement system (ACSS).
