Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/taxes/business.html; GST/HST returns filed via NETFILE/GST34. Provincial: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — PST registration and filing via eTaxBC at https://etax.gov.bc.ca.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian or non-resident business corporation obtains a single 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with program accounts (RC for GST/HST) through CRA's Business Registration Online service or Form RC1. Non-resident corporations carrying on taxable activity in Canada must obtain a BN before filing; CRA registration is accepted where a Canadian business nexus exists or where the digital-services simplified regime threshold (CAD 30,000 of worldwide supplies to Canadian consumers) is exceeded. BC PST registration is a separate provincial obligation: the seller must register as a PST collector via eTaxBC (Form TAX-001/eRegistration) before making the first PST-taxable sale to a BC resident; there is no low-value dealer exemption.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian or non-resident business corporation obtains a single 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with program accounts (RC for GST/HST) through CRA's Business Registration Online service or Form RC1. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; domestic corporations are taxed at the entity level on worldwide income (Income Tax Act ss. 2, 89(1)), and foreign entity classification follows legal-character analysis under CRA administrative policy (IT-343R). Relevant elections include: (i) ITA s. 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of eligible property to a Canadian corporation; (ii) ITA s. 88(1.3) winding-up election; (iii) ETA s. 156 election permitting related GST/HST registrants to file consolidated returns; (iv) hybrid-mismatch constraints under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6, which deny deductions for structured payments without any elective curative mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, measured on total worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 148); non-resident digital suppliers exceed this threshold on CAD 30,000 of Canadian consumer supplies in a 12-month period. BC PST: no fixed monetary threshold; every person making taxable sales for consumption or use in BC, including out-of-province and non-resident digital sellers, must register as a PST collector before the first sale, with marketplace facilitators jointly liable for PST on facilitated sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Two zero-rating limbs apply to the royalty-free stock music business: (1) tangible exported goods, such as physical audio media, delivered to an address outside Canada qualify for 0% GST under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 of the Excise Tax Act if the registrant retains satisfactory export documentation; (2) intangible licences — the supply of copyright usage rights to a non-resident person is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 10 if the right is for use wholly outside Canada, and services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under s. 23 unless they relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. If the licence is granted for commercial use inside Canada, 5% GST applies and BC PST may also apply. GST input tax credits on expenses attributable to the zero-rated export stream are refundable through the normal GST/HST net-filing mechanism.

  • CBSA Form B13A / electronic Canadian Export Declaration filed via the Canadian Integrated International Trade Single Window for physical media exports exceeding CAD 2,000 or controlled goods
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list identifying the foreign consignee and destination
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / cross-border transportation manifest evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire advice or payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Non-resident status evidence (Form W-8BEN-E for US clients, Form RC467 or certified non-residency declaration for other jurisdictions, and a signed licence-user declaration confirming use wholly outside Canada)
  • CRA export-evidence checklist consistent with Schedule VI, Part V documentation requirements

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or item-level e-invoice validation. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (Peppol PINT standard, operational 2024); CRA accepts PDF and structured electronic records for audit. GST/HST net tax, including zero-rated export transactions, is reported on periodic GST34 returns via NETFILE, and BC PST is remitted through eTaxBC on a monthly or quarterly cycle. Supporting invoices and export documentation must be retained for six years.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating exposes the taxpayer to: ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax or rebate claimed); ETA s. 452 false-statement penalty (greater of CAD 100 and 5% of the amount); ETA s. 280 prescribed interest on reassessed tax; and reassessment beyond the normal four-year period without limitation where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and Criminal Code s. 380 may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment. Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable for remitted GST/HST under ETA s. 323. CRA cross-matches CBSA CARM export records, billing data, and bank remittances; a billed export without matching cross-border transport documentation and FX inflow will be recharacterized as a taxable domestic supply with penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; no exchange control permits or licensing requirements exist. Current account flows (license fees, royalties, subscription revenue) are unrestricted, and capital account flows (share subscription, loan repatriation, dividend remittance) are also unrestricted. Profit remittance hurdles are solely tax-driven: non-resident dividends and royalties are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% statutory rate, reducible to 5%-15% under applicable tax treaties (e.g., Canada-US Treaty: 5% royalty/use of copyright; 5% dividend for 10% corporate shareholder); the BC corporation must file NR4-NR601/NR602 informational returns and remit withholding within statutory deadlines. No central bank approval is required for any remittance; FINTRAC reporting applies only to individual cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirements exist. Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or licensing proceeds; the entity may freely retain CAD, USD, and other foreign currencies in domestic deposit accounts. The only binding obligations are (i) ordinary record-keeping under the Income Tax Act for revenue recognition in the BC corporate year-end tax filings, and (ii) regulatory cross-border currency and monetary instruments reporting (PCMLTFA Regulations, s.12) when physical instruments valued at CAD 10,000 or more cross the border. Digital settlement via Stripe/PayPal aggregated ACH/SWIFT credits are not subject to any surrender or conversion rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa; administered jointly with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). The Bank of Canada issues currency and operates monetary policy, while the Minister of Finance holds statutory authority under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Canada maintains no separate single-purpose Foreign Exchange Administration; cross-border transactional regulation is effectuated through the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administered by FINTRAC, and foreign investment screening is undertaken by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility, and strictly supplementary. Foreign unlicensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) cannot operate as banks in Canada under the Bank Act and are prohibited from using the 'bank' designation; they may lawfully serve the BC corporation as registered money services businesses if they are registered with FINTRAC and maintain a Canadian compliance officer. Wise Business, Payoneer, and Windmill are viable to streamline USD/GBP/EUR collections from global streaming platforms and to settle royalty payouts; however, they offer no CDIC deposit insurance and no borrowing capacity, and they do not satisfy OSFI regulatory capital expectations for a primary treasury. The recommended design is an EMI as a collection-only conduit with daily sweeps to the relationship Schedule I bank; primary liquidity and all capital injections must reside in an OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: inward share-subscription wire from a non-resident shareholder is sent via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary Schedule I bank in Canada, quoting the BC Business Corporations Act section 188 'share subscription' in the payment reference; the remitting bank must provide source-of-funds evidence to the correspondent bank during OFAC and sanctions screening. The Canadian bank posts funds to the corporation's CAD Operational Account; no capital escrow is legally required. For cash-equivalent or physical currency inflows of CAD 10,000 or more, the carrier must file a FINTRAC cross-border currency report; the receiving bank will file an LCTR only for cash deposits of CAD 10,000+, not for wires. Prior to injection, counsel must confirm the Investment Canada Act thresholds (for WTO investors, the 2024 direct-acquisition enterprise value threshold of approximately CAD 1.325 billion is not met by this transaction, so no net-benefit review is required for a passive minority investment in an ISIC 5920 licensing marketplace). The bank payment form should categorize the funds under the OECD Balance of Payments purpose class A1 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' on the outward leg and C2 'Royalties and License Fees' on future operating receipts. USD inflows are credited directly to the USD signatory account via Fedwire or SWIFT without mandatory conversion, with standalone FX standing instructions permitting conversion only on documented commercial need.Clearing rails in order of priority: (i) domestic CAD disbursements to Canadian licensors and contractors utilise Interac EFT or a CAD credit through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); payments above CAD 200,000 require immediate same-day settlement through Lynx, the Payments Canada high-value system. (ii) Cross-border royalty payments to foreign soundtrack and audio licensors execute via SWIFT MT103 with descriptive text 'Royalty - licensed music, Part XIII WHT applied as applicable'; recurring micro-royalties below CAD 10,000 per beneficiary per month are batched through Stripe Connect or PayPal MassPay and aggregated into a single bank transfer on a T+1 treasury instruction. (iii) One-off buyout license agreements for master recordings may use an ICC documentary collection (URBPO) only where the counterparty insists on payment security; a commercial letter of credit is commercially inappropriate for recurring music licensing and is not recommended. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed no later than 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. For a December 31 year-end, the deadline is June 30 of the following year. Income tax instalments are due quarterly or monthly if the corporation's estimated tax payable exceeds CAD 3,000, with final balance due by the filing deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: aggregate controlled transactions with a non-resident related party in a taxation year trigger Form T106 filing and the CRA's expectation of a contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation package. MNE groups with consolidated revenue above CAD 750,000,000 are required to file Country-by-Country reporting and maintain applicable Master and Local File documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the CRA and Canadian courts apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), substance-over-form, and treaty anti-abuse principles to disregard arrangements lacking real economic substance. A royalty-free stock-music licensing entity must demonstrate actual business purpose, employees, operational presence, and substantive management in Canada to preserve tax benefits and treaty protections.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Omission

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA section 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment, or up to 2 years upon summary conviction; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; compound interest on reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, authorize, or participate in tax evasion can be held personally liable as parties to the offence. Directors risk criminal fines, imprisonment, restitution orders, asset seizure, and travel restrictions imposed as bail conditions. The corporate veil will not shield deliberate wrongdoing.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment at arm's-length amounts, transfer-pricing penalty equal to 10% of the reassessed adjustment where the taxpayer has failed to make reasonable efforts under ITA section 247(3), administrative penalties for late or missing T106, and compound interest on the additional tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve a false transfer-pricing study or sign false information returns may be subject to fraud-based personal liability and criminal sanctions. CRA may also assess directors personally for unremitted amounts in qualified circumstances where withholding or source deductions are implicated.
Shell Company / GAAR / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Application of GAAR to recharacterize the arrangement, denial of deductions or treaty benefits, reassessment of income, interest, and applicable penalties. OECD principal-purpose testing may deny DTA benefits for arrangements undertaken mainly to obtain treaty tax advantages.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where directors operate the shell as an instrument of tax abuse, exposing directors to civil claims, director's liability assessments, and in serious cases criminal liability for fraud and tax evasion.
Part XIII Withholding / Non-Resident Distribution Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Canadian payer becomes liable for the full unwithheld Part XIII amount plus interest and penalties. Failure to remit employee source deductions similarly creates director's liability exposure under federal tax statutes.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholdings and unremitted source deductions if they knew or ought to have known of the failure and did not exercise reasonable care. Directors can be assessed personally even after the corporation is dissolved, and criminal prosecution may follow for deliberate non-remittance.
Money Laundering / AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code section 462.31 laundering proceeds of crime: imprisonment up to 10 years for indictable offences. FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 1.5 million for significant PCMLTFA compliance failures. Funds may be frozen, seized, or forfeited.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or knowingly benefit from money-laundering activity are treated as principal offenders. Corporate limited liability does not protect them; they face asset confiscation, restitution orders, imprisonment, and court-ordered travel bans pending proceedings.
