Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); National portal: GST/HST and corporate tax registration via CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/register-your-business.html)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity requiring national tax identity: (1) Determine whether a CRA Business Number (BN) is required due to carrying on business in Canada, filing corporate income tax, or registering for GST/HST. (2) Complete and submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) via Business Registration Online, by mail, or by fax to the CRA's Business Number Registration Centre, providing the foreign entity's legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered office address (Canadian or foreign), income tax jurisdiction, and fiscal year-end. (3) After BN issuance, register for GST/HST program account (RT) using the same RC1 or online account; select the method of filing (monthly, quarterly, annual) and effective registration date. (4) For corporate income tax, the BN plus program account (RC) is used for T2 corporate income tax return filing. (5) A non-resident entity not carrying on physical business may need to file a special election to be deemed a GST/HST registrant, using Form GST50 or written request under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act. (6) Obtain a provincial PST account with the British Columbia Ministry of Finance via the BC one-stop business registration (Corporate Online) if the entity makes taxable sales in BC, unless the export zero-rating rules eliminate any provincial collection requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under Canada's Income Tax Act, no formal 'check-the-box' election exists; incorporated entities are always taxed at the entity level. However, certain elections apply: (a) Section 216 election for non-residents receiving Canadian-source rental income to be taxed on net taxable income rather than gross withholding; (b) Section 115(2) election for non-residents disposing of taxable Canadian property; (c) GST/HST voluntary registration election under subsection 240(2) of the Excise Tax Act; (d) Provincial PST vendor registration election for unregistered foreign suppliers. No hybrid mismatch election exists specifically; treaties and domestic hybrid mismatch rules (sections 18.2 and 18.3 for interest deduction restrictions) apply automatically. Canadian province of BC has no separate corporate election; federal entity classification governs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (worldwide revenue) per calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters triggers mandatory GST/HST registration for residents and non-residents carrying on business in Canada; however, certain cross-border digital services and intangible property supplies may have a lower or no threshold under the non-resident simplified GST/HST registration regime for 'non-resident vendors' supplying digital products or services to Canadian recipients — threshold is generally still CAD 30,000 for those 'specified digital supplies', except for platform operators.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exported goods under Schedule VI, Part I, and exported services or intangible personal property under Part V of the Excise Tax Act. Eligibility for goods: the exporter must prove that the goods were exported by the supplier or at the direction of the supplier within 60 days of delivery to a common carrier, and the exporter retains shipment documentation. Eligibility for services (including creator MCN agency services): the service must be supplied to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant and the service is performed wholly outside Canada, excluding services delivered within Canada or to Canadian residents. For intangible personal property (IP contracts/TikTok creator agreements), zero-rating generally applies if the IP is supplied to a non-resident and the exploitation is solely outside Canada. A MCN agency receiving fees from a foreign TikTok entity for creator management services to foreign creators should evaluate place-of-supply rules under section 7 of Schedule VI (unrestricted services) and the 'performed in Canada' test; if services are performed from BC offices, zero-rating may be unavailable unless the recipient is a non-resident and the performance is directly linked to foreign entities and no Canadian consumer subscription/payment flow exists. Refunds/input tax credits are substantiated by underlying contracts, creator agreements, invoices, and evidence of consideration from non-resident parties.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CAED e-manifest) for goods exported out of Canada
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or waybill (air waybill / truck / rail / ocean)
  • Evidence of export: port of exit stamp, customs e-manifest confirmation, or carrier certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing conversion/remittance of foreign currency payment from the export sale
  • Contract / service agreement with the non-resident purchaser and documentation of foreign GST status
  • Internal records linking the invoiced supply to the export shipment (for goods) or to the overseas performance (for services/IP)
  • Packing/shipping records and insurance documentation indicating delivery outside Canada
  • Proof of payment of any applicable export taxes/duties, if relevant

Canada does not currently mandate real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance for GST/HST purposes. CRA does not operate a pre-clearance stamping or continuous transaction control (CTO) system. However, all registrants with revenues over CAD 1.5 million per year must use electronic filing for GST/HST returns (GST34 / GST176) via CRA-approved XML/API standards (e.g., via Represent a Client, GST/HST NETFILE, or Internet File Transfer). ESR/electronic invoicing is permitted but not mandatory. Certain data elements are required on invoices (supplier name, BN, date, description, GST/HST paid rate, and total consideration). For BC PST, no real-time clearance mandate exists, but businesses must retain electronic point-of-sale data and provide digital records upon audit.

CRA may conduct exporter audits under GST/HST and impose reassessments denying zero-rating where documentation is insufficient. Penalty exposure: unpaid GST/HST previously refunded may be assessed (clawback) with arrears interest and a 25% gross negligence penalty under Excise Tax Act section 285. Misrepresentation and fraudulent claims may trigger criminal penalties under Income Tax Act and Criminal Code, plus fines up to 200% of the tax evaded. For BC PST, uncollected PST on exported goods/services may be assessed plus interest and penalty at BC's prescribed rates. Export documentation must be retained for six years from the end of the last taxation period to which they relate. Failure to repatriate foreign sales proceeds within 180 days (where commercial practice) may be treated as indirect evidence of domestic supply, resulting in GST/HST assessment at 12% combined (5% GST + 7% BC PST) for BC supplies, plus penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility. Canada accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement on March 25, 1952, and imposes no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, no conversion licensing, and no repatriation approval for services-export proceeds. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely; CAD/USD is the deepest liquidity pair. Profit remittance hurdles are exclusively tax-based, not exchange-control-based: dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% under subsection 212(2) of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1, 5th Supp.), treaty-reduced (e.g., 5% for a 10% corporate shareholder under the Canada-U.S. treaty). Cross-border currency reporting is triggered only at CAD 10,000 or more (CBSA declaration upon import/export of physical currency; FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports for wire transfers).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds. Receipts for creator-incubation and MCN agency services may be retained in USD (or any foreign currency) indefinitely in a Canadian bank foreign-currency account or any offshore account. Documentary retention remains mandatory for tax, FATCA/CRS, and AML audit purposes: underlying TikTok payout statements, invoices, executed creator agreements, and corresponding MT103/MT202 records must be maintained in the corporate treasury archive.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), serving as monetary and financial system authority. Foreign exchange administration is functionally dispersed: FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) enforces reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17); the Canada Border Services Agency administers cross-border currency declaration Form E311; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises Schedule I banks; no dedicated exchange-control authority exists because Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection or shareholder loan from the foreign parent must route via SWIFT MT103 (Fedwire/CHIPS if the remitter is in the U.S., SEPA/CHAPS if in Europe) into the entity's CAD or USD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The payment instruction must include the entity's legal name exactly as registered, the bank's SWIFT BIC, the transit number and account number (Canadian rails do not use IBAN), and a clear remittance reference: 'Capital Contribution – [Entity Legal Name] – Share Subscription Resolution dated [DD/MM/YYYY]'. Supporting verification bundle: certified share issuance resolution, subscription agreement, updated Central Securities Register / ISC register, and proof of source of funds from the remitting entity (bank statement and board resolution). A shareholder loan must be evidenced by a promissory note with arm's-length interest; care is required to avoid thin-capitalization recharacterization under subsection 18(4) and deemed-dividend treatment under subsection 214(3) on repayment. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility with legal boundaries. Wise Canada (registered MSB with FINTRAC), Airwallex, Stripe, and PayPal can serve as peripheral payout rails for global creator disbursements, fulfilling the target rail 'Wise_B2B'. Legal constraint: EMIs/MSBs in Canada are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and cannot take unrestricted deposits; they must maintain FINTRAC money services business registration and file electronic funds transfer reports at CAD 10,000+. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 12% BC); 11% combined rate applies to active business income eligible for the Canadian-controlled private corporation small business deduction up to CAD 500,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; British Columbia imposes no separate provincial dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents; this threshold triggers the mandatory T106 information return and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File / Master File obligations) under CRA administrative practice. Even below this threshold, taxpayers must make reasonable efforts to support arm's-length pricing to avoid transfer-pricing penalties.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime. However, economic substance is relevant under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act s.245), common law substance-over-form, and treaty limitation-of-benefits provisions. A BC corporation acting as a global TikTok MCN agency must maintain real offices, employees, decision-making functions, and genuine creator contracts; otherwise, CRA may disregard the structure, deny treaty benefits, and reassess.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): fine equal to 50% to 200% of the tax evasion and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Criminal Code s.380(1)(a): imprisonment up to 14 years for fraud affecting government revenue. Civil gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(1): 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or acquiesced in the evasion are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for corporate tax debts; they face criminal prosecution, travel restrictions, personal asset seizure, restitution orders, and director disqualification.
Shell Company / Sham and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: ITA s.245(2) General Anti-Avoidance Rule reassessment; s.245(5) penalty of 25% of the tax benefit; CRA may apply the common law sham doctrine, deny treaty benefits, and reassess Part XIII withholding with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for reassessed tax and penalties under ITA s.227.1; participation in sham transactions exposes them to civil conspiracy claims and criminal prosecution if fraud is proven.
Money Laundering and Unlicensed Money Services

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act penalties: up to CAD 1,000,000 per administrative violation for entities; criminal offences punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years; FINTRAC can impose compliance orders and asset freezes.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are personally liable for failure to establish and maintain a compliance program; they may face administrative fines, criminal prosecution, civil liability, and travel restrictions.
Employee/Payroll Misclassification and Unremitted Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: ITA s.227(8): 10% penalty on unremitted source deductions; s.227(9): 20% for knowing failure; late T4 penalties up to CAD 100 per day per slip; CRA payroll assessment with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for all unremitted source deductions under ITA s.227.1, without the due-diligence defence if they knowingly failed; CRA can issue director's liability assessments and garnish personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) transfer-pricing adjustment to arm's-length amount; s.247(3) penalty up to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where reasonable efforts are absent; T106 late-filing penalties and CRA technical penalty assessments; possible gross negligence penalty of 50% under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve transfer-pricing schemes may be held personally liable under ITA s.227.1 for the resulting tax debts; they face CRA collection enforcement, director's warrants, and potential criminal liability for false documentation.
