Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

British Columbia law permits a registered office address to be a registered office service provider, provided it is a physical address in British Columbia at which service of process can be effected and which is not a post office box. A virtual office (mail-drop) that does not have a physical service-of-process location is not acceptable for the registration of a limited partnership. However, an administrative-services provider offering a physical reception office, mailing address, meeting room, and service-of-process agent will satisfy the requirement. For the international raw cotton bales brokerage activity, a fee-based registered agent and virtual physical office in Vancouver or Victoria is acceptable for initial registration and annual filings, but the partnership must maintain substantive commercial substance (records, bank account, trade documentation, and export bills of lading) and must not be a 'brass plate' arrangement only. The Canada Revenue Agency will attribute taxable presence in British Columbia based on the office, bank accounts, employees, and management location; an empty virtual office without local management or records will not shield tax residency or permanent establishment exposure for international trade activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) portal; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export declaration accounts; BC Corporate Registry (OneStop Business Registry) for entity registration under the Partnership Act; BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC) for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administration.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the BC limited partnership under the Partnership Act R.S.B.C. 1996, c. 348 via OneStop Business Registry, obtaining the BC registration number. (2) Apply for the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with CRA through BRO or Form RC1, adding program account 'RT' for GST/HST and 'RM' for the CBSA customs/export account. (3) Non-resident partners must appoint a Canadian representative; the partnership must determine its taxable-supply volume over the trailing four calendar quarters to assess compulsory GST/HST registration (mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CDN $30,000). (4) Register for BC PST via eTaxBC only if the LP makes taxable retail sales or leases of tangible personal property in BC; pure export wholesalers are not required to be PST collectors for goods sold for export. (5) Enrol in the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) using the BN to file electronic export declarations for outbound cotton bale shipments. (6) File the T5013 Partnership Information Return within five months after fiscal year-end where gross revenue exceeds CDN $2M, assets exceed CDN $5M, or any partner is a non-resident, corporation, trust, or partnership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the BC LP is fiscally transparent for federal and BC income tax (ITA s. 96); no entity-level income tax is imposed, and each partner includes allocated income or loss in their own return. Non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on certain payments. The LP itself is a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes and must hold the GST/HST registration. Territorial scope: resident partners taxed on worldwide income; Canadian-source business income allocated to non-resident partners remains taxable in Canada subject to treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; classification follows the entity's legal form. Available mechanisms: (a) ITA s. 216 election allowing non-resident partners with rental or royalty income to file a net-basis Part I return instead of gross-basis Part XIII withholding; (b) partnership income allocation and retirement/retroactive allocation rules under ITA ss. 96(1)(g) to (i); (c) BEPS hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 20.1, and 97.1 (effective July 1, 2022) — no election is available, but the LP agreement and partner composition must avoid 'reverse hybrid' characterization to prevent deduction disallowance; (d) GST/HST small-supplier voluntary registration election under ETA s. 240(2) to claim input tax credits on export-related inputs; (e) ETA s. 167 joint election for taxable supplies between registrants (limited relevance to exports); (f) s. 156 intercorporate close-connection election (only for incorporated entities).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CDN $30,000 — the small supplier threshold: GST/HST registration is mandatory once a person's worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada, computed under ETA s. 148 over the preceding four calendar quarters plus the current quarter, exceed CDN $30,000. A special CDN $150,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of advertising services (ETA s. 147.1). Voluntary registration is permitted for exporters to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of ginned raw cotton bales qualify for the 0% GST rate under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 (property). The supplier must: (i) deliver or ship the goods to a non-resident consignee outside Canada; (ii) ensure the goods are exported as soon after delivery as is reasonable in the circumstances, with CRA administrative practice generally accepting a 60-day window where the supplier arranges export; (iii) if the supplier is the exporter, complete the export within 60 days of the supply; and (iv) retain prescribed documentary evidence under GST/HST Memorandum 4.4.3. Brokerage and commission services on exported cotton are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where supplied to a non-resident and not related to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Input tax credits for GST paid on freight, warehousing, inspection, and brokerage inputs remain claimable.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A) transmitted via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), showing the BN, HS classification, and destination country
  • Commercial invoice issued by the BC LP to the non-resident buyer (Incoterms, unit pricing, HS 5201 classification for raw cotton)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of the cotton bales from the Canadian port to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / documentary collection banker's payment advice evidencing settlement of export proceeds
  • Packing list, weight list, and phytosanitary or fumigation certificates for the cotton bales
  • Written contract and evidence of non-resident status where the supply includes export services or brokerage fees

No real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, or digital stamping mandate exists for B2B invoicing in Canada. The CRA accepts paper or electronic invoices containing the prescribed ETA information (supplier legal name and BN, recipient name, date, description, rate, and tax amount). GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CDN $1.5M must file returns electronically via NETFILE or approved API channels. Canada participates in the voluntary Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing framework; adoption is tax-valid but not compulsory. Export declarations must be transmitted electronically to CBSA through CERS, not through an invoice-clearance system.

CRA may reassess within four years under ETA s. 298, and without limitation where misrepresentation or fraud is established. If export zero-rating is disallowed for insufficient documentation, the LP becomes liable for uncollected GST at 5% on the full consideration, plus arrears interest and penalties under ETA s. 280. False statements or omissions attract penalties under ETA ss. 285 and 285.1, including gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the net tax avoided. Intentional evasion is prosecutable under ETA s. 327, with fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment. In parallel, ITA s. 78(4) disallows deductions for trade payables owed to non-arm's-length non-residents not settled within 180 days after the fiscal year-end, reinforcing the 180-day repatriation discipline. Cross-border sales to non-arm's-length buyers are also subject to transfer-pricing review under ITA s. 247, and CRA may recharacterize understated export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime. No exchange-control law distinguishes current from capital account transactions; profit remittances, dividends, management fees and intercompany loans may be transferred without prior approval. For a BC limited partnership engaged in ginned raw cotton brokerage, capital account convertibility is unrestricted for non-resident partners, provided the transfer is effected through a regulated financial intermediary and source-of-funds documentation is maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain proceeds in CAD or foreign currency, with no obligation to repatriate within any specified period. For Canadian tax reporting, eligible export income is assessable and transfer-pricing documentation should support the LP's brokerage and distribution functions; the only compliance gate is the record-keeping duty under the Income Tax Act and Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Canada has no dedicated foreign exchange control agency; OSFI supervises federally chartered banks; FINTRAC enforces AML/ATF obligations as they intersect with FX remittance channels)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EMI as a regulated category does not exist in Canada. A fintech that issues electronic money or provides cross-border payment services must be registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and may also require registration or authorization as a payment service provider under provincial regimes. Offshore digital EMIs cannot operate into Canada without complying with Canadian payment and securities laws. Viability for this BC LP is Medium-low: acceptable only as a supplementary onboarding channel if the EMI obtains FINTRAC MSB registration and maintains settlement accounts in Canada. Primary clearing must remain at tier-1 domestic banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: $500,000 CAD for related party transactions triggering local file requirements

Economic substance scope: Canada applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form requirements; no formal Economic Substance Regulations but CFC rules and transfer pricing apply to ensure economic substance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file tax returns

Statutory sanction: Penalty of $50 per day up to $1,000 for T3; interest on unpaid tax; possible prosecution

Director personal liability: General partners in LP may face personal liability for partnership debts and tax obligations; civil penalties and potential imprisonment for willful evasion
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income, penalties up to 10% of underpaid tax, interest

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties and interest; potential criminal charges for evasion
Dividend withholding tax non-remittance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of unpaid tax plus interest; possible prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for remittance and penalties; fines and imprisonment possible
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of deductions, GAAR application, penalties

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors face personal tax liability, fines, and in severe cases, criminal sanctions
