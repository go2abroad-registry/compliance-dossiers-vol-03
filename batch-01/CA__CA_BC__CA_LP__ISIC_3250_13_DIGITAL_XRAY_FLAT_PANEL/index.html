Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The BC LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax purposes. The partnership itself is not subject to Part I tax; each partner's allocable share of income and loss is reported by the partner. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada on income from carrying on business in Canada, and certain payments give rise to Part XIII withholding. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is deemed a separate person under Excise Tax Act s. 272.1 and must account for GST in its own right. Territorial Scope: resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source business income and taxable Canadian property dispositions. No Tax_Exempt_Zone regime applies in BC.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election for partnerships; the pass-through classification of a BC LP is mandatory. Relevant elections include: (a) ITA s. 249.1 election to adopt an alternative non-calendar partnership fiscal period where all members are corporations; (b) ITA s. 97(2) rollover election for tax-deferred contribution of property into the partnership; (c) ITA s. 21(1) election to capitalize interest on funds borrowed for X-ray detector production equipment; (d) ETA s. 156 election for supplies between closely related registrants (corporate-partnership structures) to be deemed made for nil consideration; (e) ETA s. 191.1 simplified net-tax computation election for non-resident registrants, where eligible; and (f) mandatory, non-elective hybrid-mismatch deduction-denial rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3, which apply automatically and cannot be bypassed by an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD $30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada, including zero-rated export supplies, measured over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; below this threshold the partnership is a small supplier but may register voluntarily to recover ITCs. BC PST: no monetary registration threshold; a seller making taxable retail sales of tangible personal property in BC must register through eTaxBC before making the first sale, while a vendor exclusively exporting goods should confirm eligibility for PST manufacturing-input exemptions with the BC Ministry of Finance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital X-ray flat panel detector sales qualify for 0% GST under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, s. 20 where the goods are physically exported from Canada: (i) by or on behalf of the supplier via common carrier or the supplier's own vehicle under a contract of carriage, or (ii) by a non-resident purchaser who removes the goods from Canada as soon as practicable and no later than 60 days after delivery, provided the goods are not for consumption, use or supply in Canada. The LP must retain documentary evidence sufficient to prove the export; otherwise CRA will reassess the supply at 5% GST. Related warranty, installation, and engineering services supplied to a non-resident OEM customer may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VII, s. 7, unless the services relate to real property in Canada or to tangible personal property physically located in Canada at the time of the supply. BC PST: a sale of goods delivered to a foreign buyer for export, or shipped outside BC by common carrier, is exempt from PST; qualifying manufacturing machinery and equipment used in the BC production process may be exempt from PST upon proper certification, with a corresponding refund mechanism where PST was paid.

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-clearance, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Electronic invoices are accepted, and the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard is the voluntary national interoperability framework increasingly used in large OEM procurement. Record retention is six years from the end of the relevant tax year. For exports, electronic B13 declarations must be filed through the CBSA Single Window / ARL when the value of the consignment exceeds CAD $2,000. Health Canada MDEL compliance and, for X-ray source systems, Radiation Emitting Devices Act registration impose parallel electronic licensing and record-keeping obligations.

CRA places export zero-rating claims under heightened audit scrutiny, particularly where documentary evidence is incomplete. If the LP cannot substantiate physical export, CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period (unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, wilful default, or fraud), claw back the 0% rate, assess 5% GST on the full invoice value, deny input tax credits, and charge compound daily interest plus penalties. Gross negligence penalties under Excise Tax Act s. 285 can reach 25% of the net tax sought. Criminal prosecution under ETA ss. 327–330 carries fines up to CAD $25,000 on summary conviction or CAD $100,000 on indictment, with imprisonment up to five years in egregious cases. BC PST misuse of the manufacturing or M&E exemption can result in a 10% penalty, prescribed interest, and retroactive assessment. Canada has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the 180-day figure is retained as the commercial benchmark for transfer-pricing and export-credit documentation, and delay beyond arm's-length terms may trigger ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing adjustments on intercompany receivables.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current account: full convertibility; no import deposit, no surrender requirement, and no advance approval for payments to non-residents. Capital account: open, but a non-Canadian acquiring control of an existing Canadian business triggers Investment Canada Act (ICA) notification or net-benefit review; for this BC Limited Partnership formed as a new business with non-resident limited partners, only a post-closing ICA notification is generally required (expanded pre-closing review applies if the investor is a state-owned enterprise). Profit remittance: the LP may freely remit distributions to non-resident limited partners, subject to Part XIII withholding on the taxable-income component of the distribution (25% statutory, typically reduced to 15% or lower under an applicable tax treaty). Return of contributed capital is not subject to Part XIII withholding provided the LP maintains a contemporaneous capital-account ledger segregating contributed capital from accumulated income and the distribution is documented as a capital return.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abandoned formal exchange controls in 1951; there is no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. The LP may retain 100% of export receipts in USD or other foreign currency accounts at a Canadian chartered bank, convert at a freely determined market rate, or leave funds with a foreign clearing institution. Reporting obligations are limited to statistical balance-of-payments filings and tax information returns (Statistics Canada trade reports, CRA Form T1134/T1135 for foreign affiliates and specified foreign property).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC); complemented by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision, FINTRAC for AML/CFT enforcement, and Payments Canada for clearing and settlement (Lynx RTGS, ACSS). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; the FX regime is liberalized under the Currency Act and in compliance with IMF Article VIII.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a secondary disbursement solution; LOW as a primary treasury or clearing bank for the LP. Wise, Airwallex, and similar digital EMIs are registered with FINTRAC as money-services businesses (MSBs) and are becoming subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) as payment service providers; they are not banks under the Bank Act (Canada), cannot accept deposits in the legal sense, cannot issue Canadian-domiciled documentary letters of credit, and cannot hold a true escrow account for capital-injection purposes. They may, however, receive SWIFT MT103 credits and execute economical FX conversion and lower-value outbound supplier payments to Asia. Legal constraint: the LP must always maintain a Schedule I bank account as the master account of record; the EMI account may be used only as an ancillary payment rail so that capital tracking and Canadian regulatory oversight remain with the chartered bank.

27% combined federal + BC general corporate rate (federal 15% + BC 12%). For a BC Limited Partnership there is no entity-level income tax; the LP is fiscally transparent and each partner is assessed on its allocable share. A corporate partner's share of active business income is taxable at the combined 27% rate; qualifying manufacturing and processing profits attract a preferential federal rate of 13.5% (combined 25.5%).
