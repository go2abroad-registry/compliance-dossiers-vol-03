Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible as the statutory registered office of a BC limited partnership for service of process and official correspondence, provided it is a physical street address in British Columbia staffed during business hours. However, the bonded warehouse depot business itself cannot lawfully operate from a virtual location: the Customs Act and CBSA licensing regime require a dedicated physical licensed premises under customs control and custody, with on-site storage, inventory management, bond administration, and goods-handling substance. The registered office therefore serves only as the legal address, while commercial substance must be evidenced at the bonded premises.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Federal tax administration; My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). For British Columbia: BC Ministry of Finance for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and other provincial levies.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must register with the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or by submitting Form RC1. A Business Number (BN) is issued immediately upon registration; GST/HST registration can be made concurrently. Non-residents must provide a Canadian address for service (may appoint an agent), a certificate of incorporation/registration from the home jurisdiction, and, for partnerships, the partnership agreement. The registration is effective from the date of filing or the date the entity commences carrying on business in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act; income and losses flow through to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is a separate ‘person’ and must register for GST/HST independently. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners are subject to worldwide taxation; non-resident partners are taxable only on certain Canadian-source income. No special income tax exemption applies to activities in a customs bonded or free trade zone; only indirect tax relief is available on export.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box election system. An LP is statutorily classified as a partnership. Available elections include: (i) designating a partnership’s non-calendar fiscal period under subsection 249.1(4); (ii) non-resident partners electing under section 216 for net rental income reporting; (iii) section 103 alternative allocation rules; and (iv) anti-hybrid mismatch designations under sections 100.1–104 and the relevant Income Tax Regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual taxable supplies made in Canada (small supplier threshold). A non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register once gross revenues exceed this threshold over a calendar quarter and the last four consecutive quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property, including goods removed from a customs bonded warehouse for direct export, are zero-rated under section 1, Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. The supplier must retain evidence that the goods were exported, such as waybills or customs documents, and the goods must be delivered to a destination outside Canada within a reasonable time. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated if they are provided exclusively for the non-resident’s consumption outside Canada and are not attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form A6 / CUS-021)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Commercial Invoice (Canada Customs Invoice)
  • Proof of Payment (e.g., Swift wire receipt)

Canada has no nationwide real-time invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed information. Electronic filing via GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1 million. The country is adopting Peppol BIS interoperability for e-invoicing on a voluntary basis.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims for four years (or longer for negligent false statements). Penalties under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act include 10% of the tax reported (minimum CAD 10,000) for false or misleading statements, and a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the unreported tax amount. Interest (daily compound) accrues on unpaid amounts. Willful evasion may lead to criminal prosecution and additional fines or imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII and operates without foreign-exchange licensing, prior-approval regimes, or restrictions on profit remittances by non-resident partners of a BC limited partnership. Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) imposes gross-basis withholding tax on dividends, interest, rents, and royalties paid to non-residents (subject to treaty limitation); the CRA requires a T106 information return for the LP's non-resident allocations. No monetary authority approval is required for any inward or outward payment, but all EFTs and currency movements above CAD 10,000 are captured by FINTRAC reporting and are subject to anti-money-laundering verification by the handling institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None — Canada repealed the Exchange Control Act in 1952 and imposes no mandatory surrender ratio, conversion timeline, or repatriation deadline for export proceeds. A bonded depot in a free trade zone may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in a USD or multicurrency account; however, the CBSA Duty Deferral Program requires the depot operator to post acceptable security (bond or cash deposit) covering duties and taxes, and the operator must maintain complete books evidencing the application of export proceeds to satisfy FINTRAC record-keeping and CBSA audit obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) pursuant to the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), acting as monetary authority and settlement supervisor for the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); customs and trade-related control is exercised by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), while fund-movement compliance is supervised by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a replacement for a chartered bank; medium as an ancillary payment corridor only. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act (Canada), do not access Payments Canada settlement directly, and must clear through a Schedule I bank; they must be registered with FINTRAC as money services businesses and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). They are unsuitable for capital escrow, letter-of-credit issuance, CBSA duty-deferral bonding, or high-value CAD/USD clearing, but can support low-value vendor remittances outside sanctioned corridors.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15.00% for CCPC active business income below the annual business limit (CAD 500,000) and 38.00% general corporate rate before abatement; provincial BC 12.00% general corporate income tax rate; combined federal-provincial general corporate rate 27.00% (federal 15.00% net after abatement plus BC 12.00%). For non-CCPC Canadian-controlled private corporations (e.g., a Limited Partnership allocated income to corporate partners), the effective federal general rate is 15.00% after the 10.00% provincial abatement and the 13.00% general rate reduction, subject to refundable and non-refundable credits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid to non-resident individuals and corporations under subsection 212(2) of the ITA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. For a Limited Partnership, no separate partnership-level tax return is required; the partnership files an annual information return (T5013 Partnership Information Return) within five months after the fiscal year-end, and each corporate partner includes its allocated share of partnership income in its own T2 return. A non-resident corporate partner must file a T2 return if it carries on business in Canada through the LP.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Section 247(4) of the ITA, Canada has no monetary threshold for transfer-pricing documentation; taxpayers must maintain complete and contemporaneous documentation for all cross-border transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents. A documentation penalty of 10% of the aggregate adjustments (minimum CAD 1,000) applies under subsection 247(3) if records are incomplete. For transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000, a Form T106 reporting is mandatory. Master File and Local File requirements follow the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (2022), but Canada's domestic rule under Section 247 does not specify a local currency threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have dedicated Economic Substance Regulations like the UAE; however, the 'general anti-avoidance rule' (GAAR) under Section 245 of the ITA denies tax benefits from transactions lacking genuine economic substance where a clear misuse or abuse of the provisions exists. Additionally, the 'Place of Management and Control' test can recharacterize a Limited Partnership as a taxable entity if the central management and control is exercised in Canada. For bonded depot operations, the CRA may pierce the partnership arrangement if the non-resident partner holds only a nominal capital contribution and retains full control, triggering a deemed permanent establishment under Article V of the relevant treaty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act, Section 239)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties on summary conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and up to 2 years imprisonment. On indictment: fine of 100% to 250% of the tax evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment. Civil reassessment without limitation period under subsection 152(4)(a)(i); gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under subsection 163(2); interest on arrears up to 9% compound daily.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1(1) for withholding tax remittance, GST/HST, and employee source deductions. Personal liability for willful evasion under subsection 327(2) of the Excise Tax Act; BC's corporate veil-piercing jurisprudence allows creditors and the CRA to trace trust assets held by the LP and assert claims against the general partner's personal assets. Travel bans under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (Section 36 and 42) can be issued for economic crimes. Personal restitution orders under section 462.33 of the Criminal Code for proceeds of crime.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of treaty benefits under GAAR: denial of the reduced withholding rate, application of the full 25% Part XIII withholding on distributions, and a GAAR penalty of 25% of the benefit (see Canada v. Alta Energy Luxembourg S.A.R.L., 2021 SCC 49). Transfer-pricing reassessment under Section 247(2) with 10% documentation penalty. A shell LP may also be deemed to be a resident entity under de facto 'mind and management' doctrine, subjecting worldwide income to Canadian tax.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the LP is subject to unlimited personal liability for all partnership obligations, including tax liabilities (Section 20 of the BC Partnership Act). Directors of the corporate general partner face joint and several liability for unremitted source deductions; general partner directors may be personally assessed for GST/HST under section 323 of the Excise Tax Act for failure to remit.
AML/ATF and Customs Bonded Depot Violations

Statutory sanction: CRA customs penalties up to CAD 25,000 per violation under the Customs Act and seizure of goods; criminal fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering under subsection 462.31(1) of the Criminal Code; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for reporting violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner are personally liable for customs penalties under section 128 of the Customs Act; the CRA may freeze personal bank accounts via a Section 462.33 restraint order. The BC Securities Commission revokes registrations for principals involved in trade-based money laundering. Imprisonment for up to 10 years for laundering proceeds exceeding CAD 5,000 under the Criminal Code.
Permanent Establishment and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Upon audit, the CRA assesses the non-resident as having a PE and reallocates profits under the 'attribution of profits to a permanent establishment' rule in Part I, adding 12% BC tax plus federal 15% (total 27% on the entire partnership income) and applying a 10% late-filing penalty plus daily interest. Treaty tie-breaker rules are overridden by the saving clause, and the non-resident loses treaty benefits for the PE income.

Director personal liability: Directors of the non-resident corporation become personally liable for penalties if they failed to collect and remit Part XIII withholding on distributions; the CRA may issue a director's assessment under subsection 227.1(1) and obtain a judgment against the foreign director's Canadian assets. Travel bans under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act are applicable for directors who knowingly participate in a PE evasion plan.
