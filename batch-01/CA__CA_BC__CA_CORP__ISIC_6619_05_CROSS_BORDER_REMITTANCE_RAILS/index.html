Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; national online portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Tax Administration Division (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) via Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://bro-pci.cra-arc.gc.ca, or by fax/mail to CRA's Business Number Centre in Sudbury, Ontario. A 9-digit Business Number (BN) is issued within 2-5 business days. Step 2: Simultaneously open federal program accounts on the same application: RP (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RC (payroll deductions), RZ (BC Employer Health Tax when BC payroll exceeds CAD 500,000). Step 3: Foreign-incorporated entities acting as an extra-provincial company must first register with BC Corporate Registry (OneStop Business Registry) and must appoint an attorney for service; if no senior officer is resident in Canada, CRA Form RC463 must identify an individual resident in Canada authorized to receive communications. Step 4: Register the money transfer business as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), obtain a BC MSB licence under the BC Money Services Business Act, and open an AUDUSD/CAD settling agent account with a Schedule I bank. Step 5: Complete GST/HST registration voluntarily under ETA s. 240 notwithstanding the small-supplier threshold, given the mix of exempt and zero-rated financial supplies; CRA will confirm the GST/HST program account as BN + 'RT0001'.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Business Corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or BC Business Corporations Act is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) and is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income, since incorporation in Canada determines residency under ITA s. 250(4). No pass-through or partnership classification is available for a corporation. BC income tax is administered federally under the Canada-BC Tax Collection Agreement; combined federal + BC general corporate rate is 27% (federal 15% + BC 12%). The foreign-controlled Canadian subsidiary is not a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) and therefore cannot access the small business deduction (ITT s. 125) or the reduced BC small business rate (2%); the full 27% general rate applies. GST/HST nexus: the entity is a licensed money transmitter and thus a 'financial institution' under ETA s. 149; its supplies of money transfer services to Canadian residents are exempt financial services under Schedule V Part VII, and supplies to non-residents can be zero-rated under Schedule VI Part VII s. 7. BC does not participate in the HST, so only the 5% federal GST applies within BC; PST (7%) does not apply to financial services.

Available election forms

Check-the-box classification is a US-only concept and has no Canadian equivalent; the CRA instead respects the legal form of the foreign entity but applies de facto control tests for residence and SBD eligibility. Relevant Canadian elections and statutory mechanisms: (1) GST/HST voluntary registration election under ETA s. 240, with revocation restrictions under s. 242; (2) election under ETA s. 211 to treat a supply of a financial service as made for no consideration between closely related financial institutions (commonly used within a corporate group to reduce GST exposure); (3) election under ETA s. 173 for closely related corporations to treat certain supplies as made for nil consideration, subject to ETA s. 179 (subject to anti-avoidance); (4) selected listed financial institution (SLFI) allocation formula under ETA s. 141.01 - not mandatory in BC as a non-participating province but must be re-assessed if the group later makes HST-triggering supplies in participating provinces; (5) non-resident branch tax election under ITA s. 219(3) allowing a non-resident corporation to increase the amount deemed paid as paid-up capital and thereby reduce the 25% branch tax on Canadian business earnings; (6) mandatory hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 (in force for taxation years beginning after 30 September 2023) which deny deductions for payments made under hybrid mismatch arrangements; no general reverse-hybrid opt-out exists; (7) foreign tax credit election under ITA s. 126(2.1) and s. 113 FAPI credit ordering; (8) accounting-period and filing-frequency elections under ETA s. 238 (monthly, quarterly, or annual GST remittance) are available but restricted for financial institutions with high ITC recovery requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (ETA s. 148(1)); zero-rated financial services supplied to non-residents count toward the threshold, while exempt financial services to Canadian residents do not. A licensed MSB will almost invariably register voluntarily, and once registered must collect or zero-rate all supplies and file GST/HST returns (Form GST34) electronically if annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of a financial service (including cross-border money transmission, SWIFT settlement, and currency exchange) is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part VII s. 7 when made to a 'non-resident person', subject to three carve-outs: the service relates to (a) real property situated in Canada, (b) goods situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or (c) a financial instrument that is a share, debt, or instrument of a Canadian-resident person. For a 'consumer remittance agency' selling to expatriate workers physically present in Canada, the recipient of the remittance service is the Canadian-resident sender, so the supply is exempt (not zero-rated) and no input tax credits may be claimed on attributable inputs. The entity can only zero-rate services sold to non-resident counterparties - e.g., a foreign payout partner, an overseas MSB that buys settlement services, or a non-resident corporate treasury - provided the contractual recipient is demonstrably non-resident and the service does not relate to Canadian real property, goods in Canada, or Canadian-resident financial instruments. Full ITC recovery on Canadian input costs is available only for the proportion of operations directly attributable to zero-rated non-resident supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) - required exclusively for physical goods exports; not applicable to wire/SWIFT remittance settlement because services are intangible.
  • Bill of Lading - transport document for goods; not applicable to the remittance service; the evidentiary substitute is a SWIFT MT103/MT202 payment order plus a FINTRAC EFTR acknowledgment.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - not a Canadian customs or CRA requirement; Canadian practice substitutes: (i) a signed service agreement or commercial invoice identifying the non-resident buyer and the consideration, (ii) a banking receipt or incoming SWIFT message proving the fee originated from outside Canada, and (iii) documentary proof of non-resident status (passport copy, foreign tax residence certificate, or corporate registry extract for a foreign-incorporated counterparty).

No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime exists in Canada. The CRA has not mandated PEPPOL or any live stamping/API fiscal clearance for invoices; e-invoicing is voluntary and the Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing initiative remains a voluntary PEPPOL pilot as of the 2025-2026 policy horizon. Registrants must retain source documents in Canada or make them available electronically on request within a reasonable period. GST/HST registrants with taxable supplies above CAD 1,000,000 must file electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE; smaller registrants may paper-file. MSB obligations are separate and concurrent: FINTRAC's Secure Web Portal requires filing of large transaction reports (LCTR for cash >= CAD 10,000), electronic funds transfer reports (EFTR for outgoing international transfers >= CAD 10,000), and 24-month MSB registration renewals. There is no CRA digital stamping requirement for invoices issued by remittance businesses.

Canada has no statutory FX repatriation deadline; resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income regardless of repatriation, and FAPI attribution under ITA s. 91 can apply to passive income retained abroad by a controlled foreign affiliate - the 180-day conventional period is used here as an internal governance benchmark for clearing export proceeds back to the Canadian treasury. CRA audit scrutiny focuses on the true residency of the service recipient and on whether the carve-outs (Canadian real estate, Canadian goods, Canadian-resident financial instruments) are engaged. Anti-abuse exposure: (1) ETA s. 280(1) penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the disallowed amount for a false statement made knowingly or tantamount to gross negligence; (2) ETA s. 285(1) gross negligence penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax; (3) criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327(3) - on summary conviction a fine of 50%-200% of the evaded tax and up to 2 years imprisonment; on indictment a fine of 100%-200% and up to 5 years imprisonment; (4) CRA may reassess beyond the normal 4-year period where a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, and can claw back ITCs and assess 5% GST on the full consideration from the date of the supply, with arrears interest; (5) directors' and officers' joint and several liability for unremitted GST under ETA s. 323, including personal civil liability; (6) FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 for MSB record-keeping and reporting failures, and the revocation of the MSB registration where zero-rating documentation is falsified; (7) ETA s. 123(1)(a) exchange of money may be recharacterized as a fee-splitting arrangement with the foreign payout partner, triggering Part XIII withholding on management fees under ITA s. 212(1)(a) at 25% absent treaty relief.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current account convertibility with no quantitative restrictions on repatriation of corporate earnings or remittances for legitimate business purposes. Capital account transactions require prior Bank of Canada approval for certain investments exceeding thresholds. Profit remittance is permitted subject to mandatory FINTRAC reporting, tax withholding where applicable, and compliance with the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be surrendered to authorized foreign exchange dealers within 90 days of receipt or as stipulated in the export contract, converted at prevailing market rates with supporting documentation including commercial invoices and proof of export.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
