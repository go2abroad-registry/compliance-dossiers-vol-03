E-government filing portal

Foreign entities conducting taxable supplies in Canada must register for a CRA Business Number (BN) with GST/HST account before exceeding CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide revenues in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters; application may be made online via the Business Registration Online service or by mailed RC1 form, requiring legal entity details, non-resident status declarations, estimated revenue, and provincial registration elections.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities conducting taxable supplies in Canada must register for a CRA Business Number (BN) with GST/HST account before exceeding CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide revenues in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters; application may be made online via the Business Registration Online service or by mailed RC1 form, requiring legal entity details, non-resident status declarations, estimated revenue, and provincial registration elections.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Federal and provincial tax laws do not permit check-the-box pass-through elections for limited corporations; corporations are mandatorily taxed at entity level under Income Tax Act; possible elections include Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status, capital cost allowance elections, GST/HST streamlined registration for non-residents, and section 216 elections for investment income, but no hybrid mismatch entity-level elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

5%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exported goods where possession is transferred to a non-resident and goods are exported directly outside Canada as evidenced by shipping documentation; certain services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated when the service is performed wholly outside Canada or is tied to exported property, but legal services related to Canadian real property or litigation are generally taxable unless the recipient is non-resident and the service relates to exported goods or risk.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A or equivalent authorized export report)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing movement from Canada to destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank conversion and wire confirmation evidencing settlement in foreign currency

Canada currently imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants with CAD 1 million+ annual revenues; optional e-invoicing may follow the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL standard for business-to-government invoices.

CRA may reassess zero-rated claims on retrospective grounds: misrepresentations attract gross negligence penalties up to 50% of net understatement plus interest under Excise Tax Act; willful evasion or false certificates in the GST/HST context constitute offenses punishable by fines up to CAD 200,000 and/or imprisonment; export claims must be fully substantiated with documentation for 6 years.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible and freely floating on both current and capital accounts. Canada abolished statutory exchange controls in 1951 with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act; neither the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) nor the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) imposes approval, licensing, or conversion-surrender requirements. There is no legal differentiation between current-account and capital-account flows for FX purposes; profit remittance, dividend distributions, settlement proceeds, capital repatriation, and litigation disbursements may move cross-border without Bank of Canada or OSFI permits. Practical residual frictions are fiscal and compliance-based only: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25% under the Income Tax Act on dividends, interest, and royalties, usually treaty-reduced to 5%-15%; (ii) Regulation 105 withholding of 15% when the firm pays a non-resident contractor for services rendered in Canada; (iii) FINTRAC reporting for large cash transactions at or above CAD 10,000 and for all suspicious transactions; (iv) Global Affairs Canada and UN sanctions screening that can block transfers to designated persons or embargoed jurisdictions; and (v) T1135 foreign-income verification reporting if the firm maintains an account at a non-Canadian institution and aggregate specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000. Legal services exported to non-residents are zero-rated for GST/HST, reinforcing unrestricted cross-border receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Canada. There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, no designated commercial bank requirement for collection, and no obligation to convert export or legal-services proceeds into CAD. A BC commercial litigation chamber may retain USD, EUR, or GBP in a foreign-currency clearing sub-account held at a Canadian Schedule I bank without prior authorization. The only related obligations are: (i) physical cross-border currency reporting to the Canada Border Services Agency for import/export of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) FINTRAC large-cash-transaction reporting for cash receipts at or above CAD 10,000; and (iii) GST/HST zero-rating documentation for services supplied to non-residents, which preserves full convertibility of export earnings without FX friction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole monetary and currency-issuing authority for CAD. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control administration; prudential licensing and supervision of all federally regulated banks is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46). The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border funds-movement surveillance, large-transaction reporting, and suspicious-transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17). Economic sanctions and asset-freezing obligations are administered by Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act (S.C. 1992, c. 17) and the United Nations Act (S.C. 1991, c. 41).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: LOW for IOLTA client trust funds; MEDIUM for ancillary operating payments only. Under Law Society of British Columbia Rules 3-60 and 3-61, client trust funds must be held in an account at a bank, trust company, or credit union authorized in British Columbia. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, and multi-currency platforms hold e-money licences from EU/UK regulators and are not authorized financial institutions under the Legal Profession Act (SBC 1998, c. 9); therefore, retainer funds, settlement proceeds, and security-for-costs deposits cannot lawfully be routed through an EMI. Additionally, Canadian Tier-1 banks routinely decline or freeze inbound and outbound wires connected to EMI wallets due to heightened AML risk, and a law firm holding trust money at an EMI would face professional-misconduct exposure. An EMI may be used only for de minimis operating expenses where no client funds pass through the EMI and the trust ledger remains entirely inside Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended protocol: the remitting client or judgment debtor initiates a SWIFT MT103 wire from any prime international bank to the firm's IOLTA trust account at a Canadian Schedule I bank; correspondent settlement is delivered through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for near-real-time finality, or through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) when batched. SWIFT field mapping: 50K holds the ordering client's full name and account number; 59 holds the beneficiary law firm's legal name and trust-account IBAN-style Canadian routing (transit, institution, and account numbers); 70 contains matter-level remittance information, such as 'RET/FEES - MATTER XX-2024', 'ARBITRAL AWARD SETTLEMENT', or 'SECURITY FOR COSTS'; 71A must read 'OUR' so that all correspondent charges are borne by the remitter and the full contract amount lands in the trust account without deduction. Canada does not use statutory purpose codes; the remittance narrative is the sole purpose descriptor, but it must align with the Law Society trust ledger narrative. Verification steps: (i) pre-transfer clearance call to the receiving bank's AML desk naming the remitter, amount, and purpose; (ii) name-match of the beneficiary against the Law Society of British Columbia professional membership record; (iii) if the inbound is USD or EUR, either retain the funds in a foreign-currency clearing sub-account at the Canadian bank to preserve the FX position, or convert through the bank's FX desk at a separately agreed spot or forward rate; (iv) for any account held at a non-Canadian institution exceeding CAD 100,000 aggregate, file T1135, whereas CAD or USD sub-accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank do not trigger T1135.Primary clearing rail for international disbursements is the SWIFT MT103 wire, used for expert-witness fees, foreign counsel disbursements, court filing fees, cross-border settlement payments, and enforcement payments. Domestic high-value disbursements to the Supreme Court of British Columbia registry or to opposing counsel use LVTS wires for same-day finality; for security-for-costs undertakings, banks issue an irrevocable standby letter of credit or a demand bank guarantee in favour of the court registry, which functions as the commercial LC substitute and is the only standard LC instrument in litigation treasury. Conventional Commercial Letters of Credit are not used for legal-services disbursements and appear only in judgment-enforcement transactions involving documentary sales. Interac e-Transfer and ACSS EFTs are restricted to administrative payments of less than CAD 10,000 because trust funds must never be commingled with firm operating funds. All outbound trust disbursements require: a Law Society-compliant trust ledger entry, a signed client direction or court order, matching beneficiary account details, and a FINTRAC suspicious-transaction report if any indicator arises. Payments to non-resident contractors require Regulation 105 withholding of 15% and appropriate T4A-NR or NR4 filings unless a court order or treaty waiver applies.FX Forward Contracts (plain vanilla): permitted and commonly used to lock CAD/USD or CAD/EUR rates on anticipated settlement receipts, judgment awards, and large multi-year cost disbursements; documented under the Canadian Foreign Exchange Committee FX Master Agreement or ISDA FX Definitions; short-dated interbank forwards are not subject to mandatory clearing in Canada and require only bilateral credit approval.Cross-Currency Swaps: permitted where the litigation chamber has multi-currency cash flows spanning several fiscal years, including long-tail collection of foreign judgments; execution requires an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex on title-transfer collateral and compliance with OSFI margin obligations under National Instrument 93-101.FX Options (plain vanilla calls and puts): permitted for asymmetric hedging of contingent receivables where the existence or timing of a settlement is uncertain; premium is payable in CAD; typically quoted by the bank's derivatives desk and documented under ISDA.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): not applicable for CAD pairs because CAD is fully deliverable through the onshore convertibility window and an NDF would be economically redundant; an NDF is defensible only where a judgment or asset-recovery is denominated in a restricted-currency jurisdiction (e.g., NGN, TWD, or INR), in which case the Canadian bank books the NDF through its offshore affiliate using standard EMTA documentation, while the underlying claim remains deliverable in the restricted currency.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (for goods); CAD 500,000 (for services); CAD 200,000 (for other transactions) – Local File required. Master File required for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires genuine business purpose. For treaty benefits, Limitation of Benefits (LOB) clauses in treaties may require substantive presence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239) with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties include gross negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (including withholding) under s. 227.1 of the Income Tax Act. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under GAAR (s. 245) and reassess taxes with penalties. Fines up to CAD 10,000 for failure to file information returns.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the sham arrangement. Personal assets may be seized.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations per violation. Criminal prosecution for money laundering under the Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly failed to implement compliance programs. Travel bans and extradition possible for serious offenses.
