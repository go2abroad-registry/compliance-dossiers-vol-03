Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal e-services portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); in British Columbia the PST is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Canadian business corporation, the steps are: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act, obtaining a CBCA incorporation number or BC-registry incorporation number; (2) apply for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with the CRA using Form RC1 or the Business Registration Online (BRO) service (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-number.html); (3) open the CRA corporate income tax (RC) program account and GST/HST (RT) program account under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act; (4) if the foreign entity carries on business in Canada directly, it must itself obtain a BN and register for GST/HST with a Canadian representative or agent and file Form RC1. The BN is the statutory TIN for GST/HST, income tax, payroll, and other CRA-administered levies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. A business corporation is taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Available statutory elections include: section 85(1) rollover on property transfers to a corporation; section 88(1) winding-up rollover; subsections 104(13.1) and 104(13.2) trust capital gains elections; subsection 104(14) preferred beneficiary election; section 216 election for non-resident property owners; and structured hybrid-mismatch deductions are restricted and subject to elective provisions under sections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters (GST/HST small-supplier threshold under section 148 of the Excise Tax Act); BC PST has no economic-threshold exemption — registration is required before the taxpayer makes its first taxable retail sale or lease in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of fiduciary trust-administration services to a non-resident offshore trust may be zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if made by a GST/HST registrant, the recipient trust is a non-resident not registered under section 240 in respect of the service, and the service is not supplied in respect of real property situated in Canada nor in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time performed. The zero-rating applies only when the service is rendered for a non-resident's benefit and the documentation supports the non-resident status. If the service is characterized as a financial service, it may otherwise be exempt under Part VII of Schedule V; zero-rating for financial services supplied to non-residents is nevertheless available under the same section 7 rule.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System — required only for physical goods, not for fiduciary services
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill — required only for physical goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank statement evidencing payment from outside Canada
  • Executed deed and trust instrument confirming the non-resident offshore trust and its terms (including spendthrift and discretionary clauses)
  • Service agreement and fee invoices between the Canadian corporate fiduciary and the non-resident trust
  • Declaration or certificate from the non-resident trust that it is not registered under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act in respect of the supply
  • Board resolution or internal records confirming the beneficial recipient of the services and the foreign currency remittance

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is in force in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows PEPPOL/UBL business-to-business standards. GST/HST registrants above the small-supplier threshold are required to file GST/HST returns electronically through NETFILE, and CRA's system-to-system API/EDI reporting can be used for period returns, not per-transaction clearance. Standard paper/digital records must be retained for six years.

The CRA may deny zero-rating retroactively, reassess the supply at 5% GST/HST within the normal four-year reassessment window (extended to ten years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default), charge compound arrears interest, and impose penalties. A false or inflated zero-rated export claim made knowingly or under circumstances giving rise to gross negligence is punishable under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act by a penalty of $250 plus 50% of the understated net tax, and may also attract criminal fines up to $20,000 or 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years under section 239. Fiduciary administrators may additionally face regulatory sanctions from provincial trust regulators and the BC Financial Services Authority for improper documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility; there are zero central-bank approvals or exchange-control licences for capital injections, trust corpus funding, dividend remittances, interest or royalty payments to non-resident beneficiaries, or repatriation of escrow principal. The only frictions are the Income Tax Act (Canada) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on Canadian-source payments to foreign beneficiaries (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 0-15% in most cases) and FINTRAC reporting obligations for cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. The Canadian dollar is freely floating, no convertibility quotas exist, and profit remittance does not require a central bank pass-through account or repatriation certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: Canada imposes no statutory timeline for surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts; exporters and fiduciary service companies may retain, convert, or disburse export or fee proceeds in any freely convertible currency. Obligations are limited to Statistics Canada trade documentation, FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting at the CAD 10,000 threshold, and the bank's contractual foreign-currency balance reporting under OSFI capital and liquidity rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa, Canada; supervision of deposit-taking institutions and banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and cross-border funds flow reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Canada has no dedicated foreign exchange control administration, as the former Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Canada does not licence European-style electronic money institutions (EMIs); fintech remittance and treasury-technology providers operate solely as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Offshore digital EMIs may legally serve as payment-conduit and FX-execution utilities for fee disbursements and settlement bridging, but they are statutorily prohibited from accepting Canadian deposits or holding fiduciary trust capital absent a Schedule I bank charter under the Bank Act (Canada). Use of such rails is therefore viable for low-balance operating flows only, never for custodial trust corpus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound trust corpus and equity capital is routed over SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers (or ISO 20022 pacs.008 where the receiving bank operates modern infrastructure) with the UETR appended for end-to-end traceability. Remitters must complete FINTRAC-sanctioned EFT purpose fields using the Canadian banking purpose code for 'foreign direct investment / equity injection' or 'trust funding / fiduciary capital' as classified by the receiving bank's compliance manual. Beneficiary credit is blocked until the bank completes sanctions screening against the UN, OSFI, and CSEPA lists plus KYC verification of the remitter; funds above CAD 10,000 per day generate an automatic cross-border EFT report to FINTRAC.
