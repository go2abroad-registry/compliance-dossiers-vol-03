Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. British Columbia imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a business corporation; shares may be issued for any consideration fixed by the board, subject to the solvency test under s. 188(3). No escrow account or capital deposit requirement applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and fire safety plan approval under the BC Fire Code (Division C) obtained from the local Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ); structural load and life-safety inspection certificates per the BC Building Code (2024) and the Vancouver Building Bylaw where applicable; occupancy permits for each property; WorkSafeBC Occupational Health and Safety Regulation (B.C. Reg. 296/97) clearance for building maintenance, property management, and workplace safety; asbestos management plan and pre-demolition/remediation asbestos survey under the Workers Compensation Act, RSBC 2019, c. 1 and the Asbestos Regulation.

Paid-in capital rule: None. British Columbia imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a business corporation; shares may be issued for any consideration fixed by the board, subject to the solvency test under s. 188(3). No escrow account or capital deposit requirement applies.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration via Business Registration Online, My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) under the Provincial Sales Tax Act and the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the Canada Revenue Agency. Registration can be completed online through Business Registration Online, by paper Form RC171, or via an authorized representative; the BN is a 9-digit identifier used for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts. To charge and collect GST/HST, the entity must open a separate GST/HST account under the same BN. Registration is mandatory once the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and exceeds the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (worldwide revenues of the individual entity and associated entities over the preceding four calendar quarters). For British Columbia PST, the foreign entity must register through the BC eTaxBC portal and obtain a PST registration number if it makes taxable retail sales or taxable rentals of tangible personal property or taxable services in BC; commercial real property rentals are not generally subject to PST, but GST/HST applies. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada or disposing of taxable Canadian property must also file the T2 corporate income tax return, and may require a separate income tax account number under the BN program.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not apply a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by the Income Tax Act and applicable corporate law. A non-resident corporation with Canadian-source rental income may elect under section 216 of the Income Tax Act to pay Part I income tax on a net rental income basis in lieu of Part XIII withholding. A corporation that qualifies as a REIT but retains corporate form may elect under section 131 to be treated as a mutual fund corporation for Canadian tax purposes, subject to fulfilling the investment fund conditions. For GST/HST purposes, a registrant may elect under section 211 of the Excise Tax Act to treat a closely related branch or corporation as a separate registrant; no voluntary hybrid mismatch election is available, as anti-hybrid rules under sections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act and the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework operate compulsorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies of the entity and associated entities for GST/HST (small supplier threshold); BC PST has no general registration threshold and applies to any person engaged in taxable activity in British Columbia unless specifically exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For a Canadian GST/HST registrant, supplies of goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier sells goods to a non-resident and the goods are exported by the supplier, the non-resident buyer, or a third party at the direction of the buyer; the supplier must retain evidence of export. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI when the service is performed primarily outside Canada or is not related to real estate situated in Canada, tangible personal property located in Canada, or any other enumerated exception. A REIT operating a portfolio of Canadian real property cannot zero-rate commercial property rental income, as real property situated in Canada is taxable; however, the REIT may zero-rate genuine export transactions (e.g., sale of movable assets or offshore services) if the documented statutory conditions are met and the recipient is a non-resident person.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canada Border Services Agency Export Report filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading / Waybill / air waybill showing the foreign destination and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank records evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the non-resident buyer

Canada does not currently mandate national real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000 must file GST/HST returns and prescribed information returns electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE system. British Columbia requires PST returns to be filed and remitted electronically through eTaxBC for registered businesses. The federal government encourages the implementation of a Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing framework, but adoption is voluntary. Record-keeping obligations under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act require invoices, contracts, customs documentation, and financial records to be retained in a form that supports audit and assessment.

The Canada Revenue Agency may verify export zero-rating claims through detailed document reviews and audit programs. Where a registrant fails to meet the documentary requirements established by CRA policy, the zero-rated treatment is reversed, and the tax is assessed with statutory interest and penalties. A false or fraudulent claim may result in a penalty equal to 50% of the net tax evaded under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, gross-negligence penalties under section 163.2 of the Income Tax Act, and, in egregious cases, criminal prosecution. CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period; in cases of misrepresentation or fraud, reassessment is not time-limited. Although there is no fixed federal statutory requirement for FX repatriation within 180 days in GST/HST law, CRA expects exporters to document the receipt of export proceeds within a reasonable commercial timeframe; failure to repatriate or substantiate repatriation may be treated as evidence of non-export for audit purposes, triggering full clawback of any zero-rated benefits or input tax credits previously allowed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Current account transactions, including REIT profit remittances and dividend distributions, face no restrictions. Profit remittance hurdles are confined to standard corporate documentation and Canada Revenue Agency tax filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds repatriation rules under the Export and Import Controls Act require surrender within 180 days for qualifying goods. For real estate investment trusts, this does not apply; investment returns and distributions are managed through domestic banking channels without mandatory conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech-enabled electronic money institutions exhibit high viability for cross-border operations within Canada, subject to FINTRAC registration and compliance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Offshore digital EMIs face legal constraints including mandatory local banking partnerships and regulatory approvals for cross-border transfers.
