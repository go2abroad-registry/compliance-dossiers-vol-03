Competent tax authority

Registration and compliance portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Provincial indirect tax authority for the British Columbia (CA-BC) seat: BC Ministry of Finance, eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca) for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and Employer Health Tax (EHT).

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 — Entity residence: as a corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act (or BC Business Corporations Act), the entity is a Canadian resident under ITA s. 250(4)(a) and a per se taxable corporation. STEP 2 — Business Number (federal TIN): incorporate first with Corporations Canada or the BC Registry; upon incorporation the 9-digit Business Number (BN) is issued, and the entity must activate program accounts through CRA BRO, or by filing Form RC1 or, for non-resident applicants, Form RC1A (Request for a Non-Resident Account), mailbox or online. STEP 3 — GST/HST account: open the RT program account under ETA s. 240 if taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; voluntary registration is permitted (binding 12 months) to claim input tax credits. STEP 4 — Payroll and import/export accounts: open RP payroll account if employees are in Canada, and RM customs account for the import or export of goods; the platform's digital export services typically require no RM account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' entity classification election (unlike U.S. Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3); the Business Corporation is a per se taxable corporation resident in Canada. Available elections material to this AdTech platform: (i) ETA s. 156 election between closely related corporations to treat intercorporate supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) ETA s. 240 voluntary GST/HST registration election for a non-resident or small supplier, binding for at least 12 months and required to recover ITCs; (iii) GST/HST Quick Method of Accounting for eligible small registered suppliers; (iv) ITA s. 249.1 fiscal-period election to shorten/lengthen a taxation year (Canadian resident corporations other than CCPCs may elect a non-calendar fiscal period); (v) ITA s. 125(7) associated-corporations allocation to apportion the small business deduction among associated Canadian-controlled private corporations; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules, ITA ss. 18.2–18.3, are mandatory computerational provisions (BEPS Action 2), with no affirmative election. Cross-border, a foreign seller may elect into the simplified GST/HST registration for non-residents (ETA s. 241.1); the Canadian resident entity does not file such election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold) measured on worldwide taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated exports, in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four calendar quarters; registration is mandatory within 30 days of crossing the limit, otherwise voluntary. BC PST: every 'vendor' making retail sales of taxable goods, software, and specified services into British Columbia must register in eTaxBC — there is no de minimis sales threshold equivalent to the GST small-supplier rule. BC EHT: registration required when total BC remuneration exceeds CAD 500,000 in a calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (A) goods: a supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI, Part V where the supplier exports it by or on behalf of the supplier within 60 days, evidenced by a customs export declaration; and (B) services and intangible personal property: a supply of a service or IP made to a non-resident person is zero-rated unless the supply is in respect of Canadian-situated real or tangible property, is a 'personal service' performed in Canada, or is made to a non-resident who is a GST/HST registrant for a Canadian business. For the programmatic DSP/SSP platform, the following qualify as zero-rated exports: (i) software access, data-reporting, and SSP bidding services supplied to non-resident advertisers, publishers, and agency counterparties not using the services in their Canadian commercial activities; (ii) ad inventory brokering and ad-serving fees charged to foreign media owners where the underlying inventory is sold outside Canada. Services supplied to Canadian-domiciled advertisers, or for campaigns targeting Canadian-resident consumers where the recipient is Canadian, bear 5% GST. The exporter must maintain contemporaneous documentation that the recipient is non-resident and the supply is not consumed in Canada. Registration for GST/HST permits the platform to claim input tax credits and obtain periodic refunds where zero-rated exports exceed Canadian taxable supplies; ITCs must be claimed within four years of the due date of the reporting period in which the expense was incurred (ETA s. 225(2); s. 228(6)).

  • Signed export service agreement / order confirmation evidencing the non-resident counterparty, scope (SSP/DSP software access, bidding and reporting services) and fee schedule.
  • GST/HST zero-rated invoice satisfying ETA s. 223 and SOR/2007-224, noting 'zero-rated export — Schedule VI, Part V'.
  • Non-residency attestation from the counterparty (W-8BEN-E, certificate of residence, or foreign incorporation extract) confirming no Canadian permanent establishment involvement.
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103 / ACH credit adviser and bank statement showing FX repatriation to the Canadian operating account within 180 days.

  • Where tangible promotional goods accompany the service: Canada Customs B13A Export Declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill and inward/outward manifest retained for CRA verification.

Canada does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, government-side digital stamping, or mandatory CRA API validation ('CFDI-style') on B2B invoices. Standard compliance: each GST/HST invoice must contain the supplier's legal or trading name, 9-digit BN/RM number, invoice date, description of the supply, total consideration and either the GST/HST amount, or a statement that GST/PST is included (ETA s. 223; Input Tax Credit Information Regulations SOR/2007-224). GST/HST returns and T2 returns must be e-filed (NETFILE/XML) once the applicable revenue thresholds are reached. CRA is gradually adopting the Peppol/PAN-Canadian e-invoicing framework announced in 2024, but participation remains voluntary as of the current mandate window. For an AdTech platform, each settlement payment to a Canadian media source must also be supported by a complete tax-payment trail, and incoming international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more transit the reporting obligations of the Canadian payment service provider under the PCMLTFA/FINTRAC reporting regime.

The CRA audits export zero-rating on a risk-weighted basis; the registrant bears the burden of proving the export character and non-residency of the recipient. Where documentation is absent or contemporaneity fails, the CRA may deny zero-rating, reassess net tax on the full consideration as if GST had been charged, and impose: (i) arrears interest at the prescribed corporate daily compound rate (currently 8% base for corporate arrears, set quarterly) from the date the GST/HST was otherwise due; (ii) under ETA s. 280, a penalty of 5% of any net tax that the registrant failed to remit, or 25% where the misstatement was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence in respect of an ITC or rebate; (iii) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated on the T2 under ITA s. 163(2), with criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1) for deliberate evasion. The ordinary reassessment window is four years (ETA s. 298(2); ITA s. 152(4)), but the Crown may reassess at any time on misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default. Export declarations for goods made with false statements trigger penalties under Customs Act s. 127 (up to CAD 10,000 and/or 50% of the value of the goods). Provincially, failure to register for BC PST exposes the vendor to the unregistered-vendor assessment equal to 7% PST on taxable sales, plus a 10% late-payment penalty and BC interest on arrears; all amounts remain retroactively assessable for up to four years in the absence of misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with full convertibility of the Canadian dollar for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, no approval gates for dividend or management-fee remittances, and no distinction between resident and non-resident remittance eligibility. Profit repatriation from a British Columbia business corporation is lawful and unrestricted under the federal Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the BC Business Corporations Act. The only material statutory screen is the Investment Canada Act: a foreign non-Canadian acquiring control of an existing Canadian business above the prescribed monetary thresholds may require a net-benefit review filing, but an organic incorporation of a new BC corporation by non-residents is not a reviewable acquisition. ISIC 7310 programmatic advertising is not classified as a cultural business, so the lower cultural-business review thresholds do not apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no statutory conversion timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export or service receipts, including USD revenues earned by an AdTech DSP/SSP platform, may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency bank accounts inside or outside Canada. The only compliance obligations are: (i) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reports for wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a single transaction or within 24 hours, filed by the bank; and (ii) Canada Revenue Agency Form T1135 disclosure on specified foreign property where the aggregate cost of foreign assets, including offshore cash accounts and receivables, exceeds CAD 100,000. Dividend and interest outflows to non-resident shareholders require no central bank approval; only standard withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (the federal central bank and monetary authority), together with the Department of Finance Canada which administers the Exchange Fund Account; Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control administration and no exchange-control licensing regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Canada has no statutory EMI licence or passporting regime comparable to the EU. Cross-border fintech payment providers, including Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and dLocal, generally operate either as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) for foreign-exchange dealing and money transfers or as Payment Service Providers (PSPs) subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada, with mandatory registration and compliance obligations under the deferred-application phase. These platforms offer materially lower-cost multi-currency clearing and are legally viable for ancillary disbursements to overseas publishers and data suppliers. However, a Tier 1 Schedule I Canadian bank remains the only institution capable of providing a segregated capital/escrow account, a domestic CAD clearing framework, OSFI-protected deposit insurance, and a full transactional banking relationship. Recommendation: maintain core liquidity at a Tier 1 bank and use an MSB/RPPA fintech only as a secondary settlement rail for low-value, high-volume programmatic payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: inbound share-capital is delivered by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent's or shareholder's bank to the BC corporation's Schedule I Canadian bank account, referencing the board resolution number, the share subscription agreement, and a clear remittance narrative, e.g. 'Equity Capital Contribution' or 'Share Capital Injection – ISIC 7310 AdTech Treasury'. Canada does not mandate a national purpose-code registry; banks apply their own transactional classification but require the narrative to intelligibly match the flow. CAD-denominated injections settle over Payments Canada Lynx (real-time gross settlement) or the ACSS; USD injections settle via the bank's US correspondent clearing network (typically through New York CHIPS or Fedwire). The receiving bank will file a FINTRAC cross-border EFT report for any wire of CAD 10,000 or more. The client treasury file must hold: the certified board resolution, the updated share register, evidence of the source account, and, where applicable, any Investment Canada Act filing or exemption note. No Canadian central bank authorization or pre-notification is required for the capital injection.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 via Fedwire or CHIPS for high-value USD payouts to media supply partners, data providers, and inventory licences; (2) Automated Clearing House (ACH/NACHA) for recurring low-value USD settlements to US ad exchanges and publishers; (3) Payments Canada Lynx and ACSS electronic funds transfer for domestic CAD disbursements to Canadian suppliers and operating vendors; (4) Commercial Letter of Credit issued through SWIFT MT700 for larger media procurement or data-supply contracts requiring documentary guarantees, with conforming documents presented under UCP600; and (5) a dedicated USD non-resident or Canadian USD clearing account to minimize CAD/USD conversion friction. The treasury architecture must separate client/principal funds from the corporation's own operating funds where the platform holds cash for counterparties, and must maintain audit-ready reconciliation between DSP cash-flow reports, SSP settlement statements, and bank debit/credit advices.CAD/USD outright forwards traded with a Schedule I Canadian bank under standard Master FX Terms, tenors up to 12–24 monthsCAD/USD FX swaps and rollovers for rolling settlement of recurring USD-denominated media purchasesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic-currency payouts to publishers or vendors in restricted-deliverability jurisdictions encountered by the programmatic supply networkVanilla CAD/USD FX options (call and put) to hedge budgeted ad-inventory purchases against adverse CAD/USD movesCross-currency swaps (CAD-USD) to match long-term USD receivables from ad exchanges against CAD operating costs, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with the corporate treasury as a commercial end-user

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium. Canada has no statutory EMI licence or passporting regime comparable to the EU. Cross-border fintech payment providers, including Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and dLocal, generally operate either as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) for foreign-exchange dealing and money transfers or as Payment Service Providers (PSPs) subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada, with mandatory registration and compliance obligations under the deferred-application phase. These platforms offer materially lower-cost multi-currency clearing and are legally viable for ancillary disbursements to overseas publishers and data suppliers. However, a Tier 1 Schedule I Canadian bank remains the only institution capable of providing a segregated capital/escrow account, a domestic CAD clearing framework, OSFI-protected deposit insurance, and a full transactional banking relationship. Recommendation: maintain core liquidity at a Tier 1 bank and use an MSB/RPPA fintech only as a secondary settlement rail for low-value, high-volume programmatic payouts.
