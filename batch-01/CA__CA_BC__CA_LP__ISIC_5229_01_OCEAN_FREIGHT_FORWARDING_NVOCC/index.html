Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) - official online portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); for British Columbia provincial tax, the competent authority is the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through the eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca/).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities and non-resident limited partnerships carrying on business in Canada must obtain a statutory Business Number (BN) from the CRA. The BN is a 15-digit federal tax identification number used for GST/HST, payroll, corporate income tax, and other program accounts. The standard procedure for a non-resident partnership is: (1) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with the CRA, either using the CRA online portal or by mail/fax; (2) where the partnership carries on business in multiple jurisdictions, identify the Canadian business address or appoint a resident representative/agent; (3) include the partnership's full legal name, trade name, and the names of all general partners (limited partners are not listed); (4) select the GST/HST account (program identifier 'RT') if the partnership is required to register or chooses to register; (5) indicate 'non-resident' status and provide the foreign jurisdiction tax identifier or incorporation/registration number; (6) receive the BN and GST/HST account number by mail or electronically; (7) separately register for British Columbia PST via eTaxBC if the partnership makes retail sales of taxable goods or enumerated taxable services within BC. The CRA may issue a temporary BN pending full verification; for nil-activity there is a simplified Fast Form RC148 registration available for non-resident businesses.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a 'check-the-box' election comparable to the U.S. system. A Canadian limited partnership is not a taxpayer for federal income tax purposes; income, loss, and capital gains are allocated to partners. For GST/HST, the partnership itself is deemed a 'person' under the Excise Tax Act and must appoint a representative member (Form RC372) to administer its GST/HST account. A non-resident partner may elect under section 216 of the Income Tax Act to file a Canadian return and be taxed on a net basis instead of being subject to non-resident withholding under Part XIII or Part XIV of the Act. A partnership may elect a fiscal period under subsection 249.1(2) to maintain its non-calendar year-end, and may make an election under section 18.2/18.3 in respect of hybrid mismatch arrangement adjustments. The Canadian hybrid mismatch rules apply non-electively, but certain 'co-operative' elections and 'strong tracking' elections are available to mitigate deferral or deduction adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies made in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents may become small suppliers only if their worldwide taxable supplies do not exceed this threshold, but certain zero-rated supplies are excluded from taxable supply calculations. Voluntary GST/HST registration is permitted below the threshold. BC PST: vendors that make taxable sales at retail in BC are generally required to obtain a PST registration certificate from the British Columbia Ministry of Finance; there is no uniform minimal turnover threshold for PST registration except for specific categories (e.g., temporary summer parking lots or small-scale concessions, which may be exempt).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, a supply of a freight forwarding, arranging-for-carriage, or NVOCC service is zero-rated for GST/HST when the service is supplied in respect of goods transported either (i) from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada, (ii) from a place outside Canada to a place in Canada, or (iii) from a place outside Canada to another place outside Canada. The zero-rating applies to the service itself, including the arranging, brokerage, documentation, and container-handling components, provided the supplier holds evidence sufficient to prove that the goods actually crossed an international boundary. In addition, a supply of a service to a non-resident person who is not registered under GST/HST and is not a Canadian resident may be zero-rated as an exported service under section 23 of Schedule VI, unless the service relates to tangible personal property located in Canada (other than as part of an export transaction) or to Canadian real property. For NVOCC operations, the service is generally zero-rated where the underlying ocean carriage moves container cargo between Canada and a foreign port; domestic rail/truck legs that are not part of an international through-transportation service may be subject to GST/HST at 5%. Zero-ratification is a deduction from output tax, not a VAT exemption: input tax credits receivable on costs attributable to the zero-rated supply remain fully recoverable, and exporters claim refunds through ordinary GST/HST net-filer returns.

Canada has no mandatory country-wide real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance/digital stamping regime. The CRA accepts electronic invoices provided they are legible, preserved, and contain the prescribed GST/HST particulars (vendor name, Business Number, date, transaction description, tax status, tax paid or zero-rated indicator). The federal 'Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing' initiative is progressively deploying interoperability using the PEPPOL format on a voluntary basis; no API-based clearance or government digital stamping mandate applies to NVOCC operators. Suppliers must nonetheless be able to retrieve original invoices electronically upon demand by the CRA.

The CRA may reassess a GST/HST return and recover previously applied zero-rating plus arrears interest if the exporter fails to substantiate the export with the required documentation or if the goods are subsequently diverted for domestic consumption. The statutory reassessment period is four years from the date of the return, but this period is unlimited where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established. Specific penalties include: (a) gross negligence penalty under subsection 285.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act, being the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax evaded; (b) arrears interest under section 280; (c) criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act for intentionally evading or attempting to evade GST/HST, subject to a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment; (d) denial of input tax credits and reversal of the re-documented zero-rated supply. For BC PST, a false statement or omission in a return is subject to a penalty equal to 25% of the net tax owing, plus interest, and intentional evasion may result in prosecution under the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act. All preliminary audit findings are subject to a 30-day response window before a Final Assessment is issued.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no general exchange controls, no requirement for Central Bank approval on profit remittance, and no restrictions on dividend distributions to non-resident partners other than compliance with Part XIII withholding tax and transfer pricing rules. Capital contributions and loan repayments are freely remittable, but all remittances must observe client identification, source-of-funds documentation, and sanctions screening under the PCMLTFA and UN Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, compulsory conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. NVOCC earnings retained in foreign currencies may be held freely; however, for Canadian tax purposes, income and foreign exchange gains must be reported in Canadian dollars on the applicable conversion basis, and transactions must be booked in accordance with Part IV of the Excise Tax Act where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); Canadian foreign exchange policy is market-determined, with OSFI as the prudential regulator of federally chartered banks and FINTRAC as the AML/FTF intelligence unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-to-High feasibility for non-bank payment service providers (EMIs) such as Wise and Payoneer; however, they are not chartered banks and cannot offer full banking services. They must be registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, may be subject to provincial consumer protection laws, and are unsuitable for escrow or credit operations. A dual-banking structure using a Canadian Tier-1 bank for settlement and an EMI for cross-border disbursement is the recommended compliant blueprint.
