Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) - registration and filing portal: Business Registration Online (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-registration-online.html); ongoing corporate and GST/HST administration: My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/manage-business-account.html); British Columbia provincial counterpart: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (eTaxBC at etaxbc.gov.bc.ca); corporate registry: BC Registry Services / Corporate Online (corporateonline.gov.bc.ca) or Corporations Canada for federal incorporations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the business corporation under the BC Business Corporations Act or the Canada Business Corporations Act, obtaining the incorporation number and registered office address in British Columbia. Step 2 - Obtain the CRA Business Number (BN) by filing through Business Registration Online, by telephone (1-800-959-5525), by Form RC1, or through an accountant's Represent a Client portal; the 9-digit BN becomes the corporate income tax program account (RC). Step 3 - Add the GST/HST program account (RT) in the same registration, selecting monthly, quarterly, or annual filing periods; registration is compulsory when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters, and voluntary registration is mandatory for any exporter seeking zero-rating and input tax credits. Step 4 - Register for the BC PST account via eTaxBC or OneStop BC if selling taxable goods or specified services in BC; fractional CFO and bookkeeping services are generally PST-exempt, so a PST account is usually not required for a pure services exporter. Step 5 - Add payroll (RP) and customs (RM) accounts only if employees or physical imports/exports are present; for a virtual CFO with no BC employees, no RP account is needed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian business corporation is a separate resident taxpayer under ITA s. 2 and s. 250, taxed on worldwide income at combined federal (15%) and BC (12%) rates totalling 27% on general income; a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) is eligible for the Small Business Deduction, reducing combined tax to approximately 11% on active business income up to CAD 500,000 per year (federal 9% plus BC 2%, phased out for taxable capital exceeding CAD 15 million). Canada has no US-style pass-through entity regime; all business corporations, including professional service corporations, are taxed at the entity level, and a foreign-owned Canadian corporation is subject to the same entity-level taxation unless the foreign owner elects to treat it as a disregarded branch for Canadian GST/HST purposes (CRA administrative rule).

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or S-corporation pass-through election; entity-level taxation is mandatory for corporations. Relevant elections available to a virtual CFO corporation include: (i) ITA s. 89(11) control deeming election for CCPC status; (ii) ITA s. 249 election to change the fiscal period or adopt a non-calendar taxation year; (iii) ETA s. 156 election treating supplies between closely related Canadian registrants as made for nil consideration; (iv) ETA annual filing and instalment elections for GST/HST; (v) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident rental or timber income (relevant only if the corporation holds Canadian real property); and (vi) BC PST small-seller election where applicable for incidental taxable goods sales below the CAD 10,000 threshold. None of these elections alter the fundamental entity-level taxation of a BC corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1); a person becomes a mandatory registrant if taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in any four consecutive calendar quarters. A virtual CFO exporting services must register voluntarily even below the threshold to zero-rate offshore supplies and to recover input tax credits on Canadian operating costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 for a supply of a service made to a non-resident of Canada, unless the service is supplied to an individual present in Canada, is in respect of real property situated in Canada, or is a service in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. A remote-delivered fractional CFO and multicurrency ledger bookkeeping service performed in BC for a client located offshore qualifies for the 0% rate, provided the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST in Canada and does not carry on business in Canada through a permanent establishment. The zero-rating is available only to a GST/HST registrant; an unregistered small supplier cannot charge zero-rate and cannot claim input tax credits. The actual consumption of the advice offshore and the client's foreign corporate substance must be documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) - required only if physical media, software tokens, or documentation is shipped from Canada; for purely virtual services, a substantive service delivery record (session logs, IP-log evidence of offshore access, and remote ledger delivery reports) is the functional substitute
  • Bill of Lading - for any goods or media exported, the original Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier receipt must be retained; for virtual services, the equivalents are the executed Master Services Agreement and electronic delivery logs showing the place of performance and offshore consumption
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - SWIFT MT103 or bank-issued inward remittance record, or a Wise/Stripe payout record, evidencing the payment originated from the non-resident client's foreign bank account to the Canadian operating account
  • Executed Master Services Agreement with governing law, jurisdiction, fee schedule, and express statement that the services are consumed outside Canada
  • Invoices annotated: 'Zero-rated - ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 - supply to non-resident', showing the client's foreign business address and the CRA Business Number of the vendor
  • Certificate of Incorporation or corporate registry extract of the non-resident client confirming its foreign jurisdiction and non-resident status
  • Time allocation and multicurrency ledger reports demonstrating that all substantive advisory work is performed in Canada for offshore delivery with no nexus to Canadian real property or tangible personal property

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-linked clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada at the federal or BC level. However: (1) Every GST/HST registrant must issue invoices containing the vendor's name, BN, date, description, GST amount or rate, and purchaser identity for supplies over CAD 150, per the ETA and the Invoicing Information Regulations; (2) GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE using certified software or the CRA My Business Account; paper filings are exceptional; (3) Canada participates in the voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (PEPPOL) launched in 2024-2025, allowing interoperable e-invoicing with international networks but imposing no mandate or stamping; (4) BC PST returns, where applicable, are filed via eTaxBC with no fiscal stamping requirement. For export zero-rating, digital records, foreign-currency invoices converted at CRA monthly exchange rates, and documented delivery of services to non-resident clients are the operative compliance artefacts.

CRA may deny export zero-rating and reassess the full invoice value as taxable at 5% GST if the documentation fails to establish non-residency of the client, foreign origin of funds, or offshore consumption; on reassessment, interest accrues daily at the CRA prescribed rate for corporate tax debts, and penalties apply: ETA s. 285 penalty for false statements or omissions (greater of CAD 25,000 and 250% of the nursing tax deficiency), ETA s. 280 failure-to-register penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 1% of the value of taxable supplies), ETA s. 296 failure-to-collect-and-remit penalties, and criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1) for evasion, carrying fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Retrospective clawback follows the standard limitation periods: three years from the original assessment for ordinary reassessment, seven years where misrepresentation or neglect is found, and no limitation period for fraud; additionally, unpaid export receivables aged beyond 180 days without documented collection attempts may be recharacterized as loans or deemed benefits to the shareholder, triggering Part XIII withholding tax or GST recomputation for quasi-Caribbean structures. In BC, because professional bookkeeping services are PST-exempt, no separate PST clawback liability attaches to the zero-rated export revenue.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains de facto and de jure full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the federal foreign-exchange liberalization regime. There is no statutory exchange-control approval for capital injections, profit repatriation, or trade settlement. Practical hurdles are limited to tax compliance, sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act, and reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Profit remittance by a Canadian business corporation is subject to normal Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends and fees, treaty eligibility under the Canada-U.S. or other applicable tax treaties, and thin-capitalization/anti-avoidance rules; no convertibility license or central-bank authorization is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. Exporters and service providers, including a virtual-CFO consultancy with cross-border clients, may retain foreign currency receipts in domestic or offshore foreign-currency bank accounts. The applicable statutory obligations are accurate accrual/revenue recognition for income-tax purposes, GST/HST reporting under the Excise Tax Act, and FINTRAC record-keeping/reporting for individual transactions of CAD 10,000 or more or for wire transfers triggering the cross-border wire-transfer reporting regime.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy is administered under the federal regulatory framework without a dedicated foreign exchange control authority; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises AML/ATF compliance for cross-border payment and money-services activity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-High, but strictly as a regulated money-services business rather than a bank. Foreign electronic-money institutions and fintech providers such as Wise and Stripe cannot take deposits or lend in Canada without authorization under the Bank Act; they may, however, operate through subsidiaries or partner with Canadian Schedule I banks to provide payment facilitation, multi-currency wallets, and settlement services. Any non-resident fintech remitting funds into Canada or disbursing from Canada on behalf of the BC entity must register with FINTRAC as an MSB if it does business in Canada, and the Canadian entity must ensure that its own board-authorized accounts are CDD-compliant. The practical feasibility for a virtual-CFO consultancy is High for ancillary receivables/payables facilitation and Low-to-Medium for primary treasury concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder loans or equity contributions are executed by telegraphic transfer via the SWIFT network to the BC company's Canadian-dollar or US-dollar clearing account at a Schedule I bank. Canada uses no centralized foreign-exchange purpose-code regime; the remittance narrative should succinctly identify the transaction as 'Shareholder capital injection', 'Equity contribution — Corporation BC', or 'Director's loan — CA_Corp', accompanied by a board resolution and updated corporate share register. For USD-origin funds routed through a US intermediary, the correspondent bank must be provided with the Canadian beneficiary's account number, SWIFT/BIC, and ABA routing details, and FATCA/CRS self-certifications must be retained before settlement. FINTRAC reporting applies to wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or above; funding sourced from non-sanctioned jurisdictions must cross the global banking sanctions screen before same-day value.Trade settlements are effected primarily through SWIFT MT103 payment orders for outright vendor disbursements and consulting fees, and through commercial or standby letters of credit issued by a Canadian bank to the foreign beneficiary's confirming bank (SWIFT MT700/MT707).
