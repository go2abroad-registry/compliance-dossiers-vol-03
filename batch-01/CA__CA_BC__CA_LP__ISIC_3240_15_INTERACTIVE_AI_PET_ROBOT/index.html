Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: CRA My Business Account and Canada Business Registration Online (BizReg) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. British Columbia provincial sales tax is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch through the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign limited partnership establishing a BC limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and program accounts. Procedure: (1) appoint (or create) a general partner, ordinarily a BC or extraprovincial corporation, to act as statutory agent for the LP; (2) register the LP with Corporations Canada or BC Corporate Registry under the Partnership Act (BC), obtaining a BC extraprovincial registration number; (3) submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or register via CRA BizReg, disclosing partnership status, principal business activity (NAICS 339930, assembly of bionic companion robotic animals), and expected taxable Canadian supplies; (4) CRA issues a nine-digit BN together with program identifiers, typically RT (GST/HST), RC (customs), and RM (non-resident tax) accounts; (5) each non-resident partner having a Canadian-source allocation must obtain a Canadian tax identification number or non-resident identification number (individual or corporate) for filing obligations; (6) the LP itself, though fiscally transparent for income tax, must file a T5013 Partnership Information Return annually allocating income to partners; (7) if the LP imports components from outside Canada, a customs program account and importer/exporter number are required, and border services declarations will be made through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is an independent 'person' and mandatory registrant under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 240(1) where it makes taxable supplies in Canada through a permanent establishment and is not a small supplier. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For federal income tax purposes, a BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent: the LP computes income at the partnership level, but the partners, including non-residents, are separately taxed on their allocable shares under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is an independent 'person' and mandatory registrant under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 240(1) where it makes taxable supplies in Canada through a permanent establishment and is not a small supplier. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners are subject to worldwide taxation; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income from the LP, comprising active business income under Part I on a net basis and/or Part XIII withholding for passive distributions; eligibility for treaty benefits requires Form NR301 disclosure. BC PST is a separate retail sales tax, not a VAT, and applies on the final consumer sale within BC; the LP will collect and remit PST as a vendor under the Provincial Sales Tax Act unless the sale is an export. No Tax_Exempt_Zone status is available within British Columbia.

Available election forms

Canada has no direct US-style 'check-the-box' election; entity classification is statutorily driven. Available elections include: (a) ITA s. 96(5)/(6) fiscal-period election, allowing a partnership to adopt a non-calendar year-end for income tax; (b) ETA s. 150 and s. 160 elections to use the quick method or cash-method of accounting for GST/HST where annual taxable supplies are below CAD 400,000; (c) ETA s. 273 joint election for closely related corporations to treat supplies as non-taxable, generally unavailable to an LP except where a corporate partner and the partnership meet the specified conditions; (d) ITA s. 115 / s. 216 net-income elections for non-resident partners receiving rental or timber royalties from Canadian partnership property; (e) hybrid mismatch restrictions under ITA s. 18.5, which disallow double-deduction outcomes where the LP routes payments to related foreign entities; (f) for the AI pet robot scope, no industry-specific election or tax holiday regime exists at the federal level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold, ETA s. 148). FT IGN; a non-resident LP selling bionic toy robots online into Canada will cross the threshold once annual Canadian-source taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; thereafter registration is mandatory, or the marketplace platform may be deemed to collect GST under the GST/HST digital economy measures. PST: no registration threshold for remote sellers delivering goods to BC consumers; a non-resident vendor with Canadian or US nexus, or one making sales through a marketplace in BC, must obtain a PST vendor registration regardless of volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of interactive AI pet robots assembled in BC (NAICS 339930 / ISIC 3240; HS code 9503.00) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 of the Excise Tax Act as tangible personal property exported by the vendor. To claim the 0% GST rate, the vendor must prove that the goods were (a) supplied to a non-resident buyer and delivered to a destination outside Canada; (b) delivered by the vendor to a common carrier for export from Canada and subsequently actually exported; or (c) delivered to an address outside Canada, with shipment made within a reasonable time. The export must be unrestricted, with no possibility that goods will be consumed or resold in Canada. The goods are simultaneously exempt from BC PST provided title passes outside BC and shipment occurs directly out of the province. Supplies made through an online platform to Canadian consumers are domestic taxable supplies and must bear GST (5%) plus PST (7%); zero-rating does not apply to that channel.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) electronic declaration (legacy Forms G7/B13A) for shipments valued above CAD 2,000, transmitted to CBSA before departure
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill / road waybill) naming the Canadian exporting LP as shipper and the foreign buyer as consignee, with destination port and shipment date
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank remittance advice (SWIFT MT103, bank statement, or trade finance settlement) evidencing receipt of foreign currency; Canada does not issue a dedicated IRC, and CRA accepts alternative proof of payment
  • Commercial invoice with HS classification 9503.00, description of bionic companion robotic animal with speech synthesizer, quantity, unit value, and purchase order reference

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices. The CRA requires GST/HST returns to be filed electronically (NETFILE) for registrants with annual gross turnover of CAD 1 million or more; paper filing remains available below that threshold. E-invoicing is voluntary and has been piloted through the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing framework since 2023; no API-based clearance is imposed. The BC PST system requires periodic electronic returns via eTaxBC, with invoice-level data retained for audit but not transmitted in real time. Records underlying zero-rating claims must be retained for six years from the end of the relevant reporting period.

Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating; the 180-day parameter is used here as a conservative documentary benchmark consistent with commercial letters of credit. The CRA may audit export zero-rating claims for four years (seven years where misrepresentation is shown) and may reassess to recover uncollected GST together with interest and penalties. Where a vendor knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence claims zero-rating on goods not exported, the ETA and ITA provide: (i) civil penalties of up to 250% of the tax sought to be evaded; (ii) fines on summary conviction of up to CAD 100,000 and on indictment of up to CAD 500,000; (iii) imprisonment of up to 12 months on summary conviction and up to five years on indictment; and (iv) forfeiture or seizure of goods under the Customs Act. Penalties may be mitigated only through the CRA Voluntary Disclosures Program, which must be invoked before the CRA has initiated audit enforcement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating currency regime with full capital and current account convertibility. There are no restrictions on profit remittance, partnership distributions, or dividend repatriation, provided that applicable income tax withholding obligations under the Income Tax Act (Canada) are fulfilled and anti-money laundering (AML) reporting thresholds are observed. No exchange control permits or prior approvals are required for inbound or outbound wire transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no foreign exchange repatriation timelines. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency in Canadian or offshore accounts; however, transactions equaling or exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC as large cash or wire transaction reports, and unrealized foreign exchange gains or losses are taxable/deductible per CRA guidelines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange oversight is conducted through this institution in coordination with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for transactional reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LIMITED. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Wise, or Mercury may be used as ancillary receivables collection tools, but they are not a compliant substitute for a Canadian domestic bank account. Such EMIs are not regulated by the Bank of Canada; when they provide money services in Canada, they must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Offshore EMIs lack direct CAD clearing capabilities, they cannot generate Canadian bank-issued routing numbers, and they are excluded from the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) / Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). Thus, a domestic tier-1 bank account is a mandatory primary clearing rail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident limited partners with 25%+ beneficial ownership interest

Root cause: Non-resident limited partners with 25%+ beneficial ownership interest

Defense evidence: Complete partnership register containing full legal name, date of birth, residential address, tax residence identifier, and percentage interest of all limited partners; source-of-funds declaration; notarized passport copies; home-country bank reference letters; diagram linking each limited partner to the ultimate natural person beneficial owner; confirmation that no partners are Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) or named on Canadian/OFAC/EU sanction lists.
Inbound capital injection from a jurisdiction without adequate AML standards

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from a jurisdiction without adequate AML standards

Defense evidence: Six months of bank statements from the remitting entity; provenance-of-wealth documentation (employment, dividends, property sale, inheritance or corporate distribution); constitutional and commercial registration documents of the sending entity; correspondent-bank or Global Legal Entity Identifier confirmation; board/corporate resolution authorizing the remittance; if a trust or foundation is involved, complete trust deed, settlor/beneficiary listing, and independent legal opinion on legitimacy.
Mismatch between declared ISIC 3240 manufacturing scope and advanced AI/speech-synthesizer componentry

Root cause: Mismatch between declared ISIC 3240 manufacturing scope and advanced AI/speech-synthesizer componentry

Defense evidence: Technical product dossier for bionic companion animals, including bill of materials, engineering schematics, and compliance certifications (FCC, CE, CSA, UL); evidence of physical assembly operations at a rented BC industrial address; supply-chain contracts with component vendors; patent or industrial design filings for the AI speech synthesizer; detailed revenue model and target B2B/B2C distribution demonstrating the low-money-laundering-risk nature of the product; photograph/video verification of assembly line and inventory.
Use of an offshore EMI or digital wallet as the first recipient of export proceeds

Root cause: Use of an offshore EMI or digital wallet as the first recipient of export proceeds

Defense evidence: Treasury flow diagram demonstrating that all end-buyer funds settle directly to the BC operational account via SWIFT MT103/202 with no intermediate unlicensed processors; contractual covenant naming the Canadian bank as the only clearing account; monthly reconciliation of EMI or wallet transactions, if used, with corresponding invoice and customs export documents; legal opinion confirming the EMI is not a conduit for AML/CFT evasion.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LIMITED. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Wise, or Mercury may be used as ancillary receivables collection tools, but they are not a compliant substitute for a Canadian domestic bank account. Such EMIs are not regulated by the Bank of Canada; when they provide money services in Canada, they must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Offshore EMIs lack direct CAD clearing capabilities, they cannot generate Canadian bank-issued routing numbers, and they are excluded from the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) / Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). Thus, a domestic tier-1 bank account is a mandatory primary clearing rail.
