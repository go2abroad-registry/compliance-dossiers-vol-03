E-government filing portal

For a foreign corporation entering Canada: (1) assess mandatory GST/HST registration — any person carrying on business in Canada (including a non-resident with Canadian clients) must register once its worldwide taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; since 1 July 2021 the same CAD 30,000 specified registration threshold also applies to non-resident suppliers of digital services and intangible property to Canadian consumers even without carrying on business in Canada; (2) where no Canadian subsidiary is incorporated, apply directly to CRA using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or Business Registration Online, attaching a certified copy of the foreign certificate of incorporation (notarised and apostilled), certificate of incumbency or status, and director identification; (3) CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number and opens program accounts relevant to the entity, namely a GST/HST program account (marked NR for non-resident where applicable, with the simplified Form RC171 non-resident registrant filing option for a non-resident that does not carry on business in Canada), a T2 corporation income tax account where a permanent establishment exists, and a payroll deductions account if Canadian employees are engaged; (4) in the present structure the delivery vehicle is a Canadian BC business corporation, which after incorporation obtains its own Business Number, GST/HST account, and BC corporate and PST accounts through BC OneStop / BC Tax Account; PST registration is immediate upon the first PST-liable sale of goods or enumerated taxable services in BC; (5) no separate TIN or VAT-ID renewal is required, but any change in residency status, permanent establishment triggering events, or cessation of Canadian activities must be reported to CRA within prescribed periods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian federal or BC business corporation is taxed as a separate person under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada); there is no pass-through or check-the-box status available for corporations. A resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income at the federal general rate of 15%, plus BC provincial corporate income tax at 12% on income allocated to a permanent establishment in BC (combined headline approximately 27%). Non-resident shareholders are taxed separately by Part XIII withholding on dividends paid out of Canadian earnings, and a non-resident corporation operating through a branch is taxed under Part I on Canadian-source business income attributable to a permanent establishment plus specified Canadian-source passive income; Canada does not employ territorial exemption for active foreign business income of resident corporations but grants credits for foreign taxes paid. No tax-exempt free-zone regime applies to virtual office or commercial mail receiving agency services in BC.

Available election forms

Canada has no United States-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections and structuring mechanisms relevant to this taxpayer include: (a) voluntary GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240(2) allowing a non-resident or small supplier to register and claim input tax credits; (b) simplified non-resident GST/HST filing election (Form RC171) for non-residents not carrying on business in Canada; (c) GST/HST reporting-frequency elections under ETA s. 238 (monthly, quarterly, or annual returns); (d) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident to compute Part I tax on net Canadian rental income instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (e) ITA s. 217 election allowing a non-resident to file a full Canadian return for certain pension and social security income; (f) hybrid mismatch legislation under new ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3, operative for taxation years beginning on or after 1 October 2024, which disallows deductions or requires current inclusions on structured hybrid arrangements without any elective opt-out and imposes self-identification and adjustment duties; (g) thin capitalization limitation under ITA s. 18(4), which caps interest deductibility where debt owed to specified non-resident shareholders exceeds a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio; and (h) CRA administrative elections to file consolidated GST/HST returns for closely related registrants and to use simplified accounting methods for specified business inputs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no administrative approval gate, no quantitative restriction, and no licensing requirement for converting CAD to foreign currency or vice versa. Profit remittance hurdles are tax and corporate-law gates, not exchange-control gates: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act applies a 25% non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, rents, royalties, and management fees, subject to treaty reduction (Canada-United States Tax Convention generally 15% portfolio and 5% direct; Canada-China Tax Convention 15% portfolio and 5% direct for 10-per-cent corporate holders); withholding must be reported on NR4 and remitted to the CRA by the 15th day of the month following the calendar month of payment; (ii) the BC Business Corporations Act solvency test permits dividends only when the corporation meets statutory net-asset and liquidity tests, which is a legal remittance hurdle independent of FX; (iii) GST/HST zero-rating under ETA s. 7 applies to supplies of services to non-residents when the CMRA maintains documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and of offshore use, but mischaracterization creates audit exposure; (iv) repatriation of contributed capital or paid-up capital is not subject to withholding; (v) resident shareholders may face T1135 foreign-asset reporting and, where an offshore EMI is used, the funds must be reflected correctly in the Canadian books. British Columbia imposes no provincial currency controls or remittance licensing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export or service proceeds. Income earned by CA_CORP from virtual-office, CMRA, and mail-scanning services (ISIC 8299 / NAICS 561431) may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, CNY, or any other convertible currency, either in a Canadian foreign-currency bank account or in a properly disclosed offshore payment account. The only compliance weights are (i) FINTRAC electronic-fund-transfer reporting for every swap? no, for every transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, including aggregated structuring surveillance; (ii) year-end realization and accrual of foreign-exchange gain or loss under s. 39 of the Income Tax Act if the functional-currency election is not made; (iii) the absence of any export declaration for services under Canadian customs law; and (iv) the OECD/CRA transfer-pricing documentation requirement when intercompany service revenue is retained abroad.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole monetary authority and issuer of Canadian bank notes. Foreign-exchange regulation is distributed among the Bank of Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Department of Finance Canada. Canada has no standalone exchange-control administration analogous to SAFE; the Currency Act and the Bank Act establish a liberal market-based framework. For British Columbia, Consumer Protection BC administers the Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA) licensing under the Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act but has no currency or foreign-exchange jurisdiction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH with conditions. Reputable cross-border payment providers such as Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, and Stripe Treasury are legally accessible to a BC corporation, but each must be a FINTRAC-registered money services business or operate through a Canadian-registered entity to support CA_CORP in a compliant manner. Offshore digital EMIs not registered in Canada are legally permissible as payment conduits, yet they create material constraints: (i) CRA and FINTRAC monitoring can be impaired because foreign virtual IBANs are not automatically subject to Canadian EFT reporting; (ii) deposits at an offshore EMI are not CDIC-insured and are not subject to Canadian insolvency ring-fencing; (iii) the BC CMRA licence requires detailed customer identification, which the offshore EMI cannot lawfully override by creating distance and layered structure; (iv) the target rails (Stripe Recurring and Credit Card) require a receiving account in the merchant's name; it is most compliant to connect Stripe to a Schedule I bank account in Canada and use Wise/Stripe-Treasury only as a treasury sweep. Recommended architecture: RBC or TD primary operating account, plus a Wise Business or Airwallex account for cross-currency settlement, with all foreign-currency balances swept daily to the Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital injection from a non-resident shareholder into CA_CORP is executed in four layers. (1) Corporate authorization: share subscription agreement, directors' resolution authorizing issuance, share certificate, amended Notice of Articles filed with the BC Registry, and the updated share ledger; compliance with the BC Business Corporations Act solvency and filing requirements. (2) Wire protocol: the remitting shareholder instructs its bank to send a SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire from the United States) to the beneficiary Canadian bank's Vancouver branch, using the CA_CORP account number and the bank's SWIFT BIC; the purpose field must carry the transaction reference, for example the SAFE registration code for a Chinese ODI capital contribution using transaction category 621021 (对境外直接投资—资本金), or the US parent reference to the subscription agreement. (3) Verification: the receiving bank performs OFAC/UN sanctions screening, confirms the beneficial ownership schedule, and files a FINTRAC EFT report for any transfer at or above CAD 10,000; source-of-funds evidence is retained in the KYC file. (4) Escrow and conversion: if the injection is structured as a capital escrow, release is conditioned on issuance of the share certificate and a solicitor's undertaking; conversion to CAD can occur at the bank's spot rate without CRA approval; the subscribed paid-up capital is credited in the currency of issue and no Part XIII withholding applies because equity is not income. All inbound capital is reported to the CRA in the T2 corporate return (Schedule 50 ownership information) and, if the shareholder is non-resident, an Investment Canada Act screening determination or counsel's exemption opinion should be on file.Outbound treasury flows for a CMRA/mail-scanning business are predominantly service-based settlements, not goods-based letters of credit. The clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 / SWIFT gpi for direct wire transfers to foreign contractors, software vendors, and non-resident management-fee recipients; (b) commercial letters of credit (UCP600) are legally available for incidental physical mail-forwarding or courier consignments but are only economical above roughly USD 100,000 per transaction; a standby letter of credit or bank guarantee is more apt for performance obligations; (c) domestic disbursements use Payments Canada rails, specifically Lynx for same-day high-value payments and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for EFT and pre-authorized debits; the real-time rail is being phased in and may be used when generally available; (d) low-value recurring disbursements to overseas beneficiaries should be batched through a FINTRAC-registered PSP such as Wise or Airwallex, with daily sweeps back to RBC or TD; and (e) any payment to a non-arm's-length foreign person requires a transfer-pricing file (Form T106) and mandatory Part XIII withholding if the payment is subject to Canadian sourcing rules. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are settled by SWIFT MT103 after the CRA withholding remittance through Form NR4; the defence for the bank is the full board-approved dividend declaration and a current CRA account statement.Plain-vanilla deliverable CAD/USD forward contracts with a Schedule I bank, typically under ISDA FX definitions or the bank's standard FX master terms, available for tenors up to 24 months and requiring no exchange-control authorization.FX swaps (swap-point exchanges) permitted for short-term intercompany funding and liquidity netting between CA_CORP and its foreign parent.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) permissible for hedging shareholder-subordinated debt or funding liabilities under OSFI prudential guidance; the company must designate the hedge and avoid speculative overlay.OTC plain-vanilla European or American FX options, purchased (not written), to protect committed large-dollar receivables such as an enterprise CMRA contract payout.Exchange-traded CME Micro USD/CAD futures and Montreal Exchange cleared derivatives, subject to trade reporting and derivatives risk governance.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) legally available in Canada and appropriate only when the offshore counterparty is situated in a restricted-currency jurisdiction (for example a CNH/USD NDF booked by the parent treasury); operationally unnecessary for CAD itself because CAD is freely deliverable, and the Canadian entity must evidence board approval and a non-speculative rationale.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH with conditions. Reputable cross-border payment providers such as Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, and Stripe Treasury are legally accessible to a BC corporation, but each must be a FINTRAC-registered money services business or operate through a Canadian-registered entity to support CA_CORP in a compliant manner. Offshore digital EMIs not registered in Canada are legally permissible as payment conduits, yet they create material constraints: (i) CRA and FINTRAC monitoring can be impaired because foreign virtual IBANs are not automatically subject to Canadian EFT reporting; (ii) deposits at an offshore EMI are not CDIC-insured and are not subject to Canadian insolvency ring-fencing; (iii) the BC CMRA licence requires detailed customer identification, which the offshore EMI cannot lawfully override by creating distance and layered structure; (iv) the target rails (Stripe Recurring and Credit Card) require a receiving account in the merchant's name; it is most compliant to connect Stripe to a Schedule I bank account in Canada and use Wise/Stripe-Treasury only as a treasury sweep. Recommended architecture: RBC or TD primary operating account, plus a Wise Business or Airwallex account for cross-currency settlement, with all foreign-currency balances swept daily to the Canadian bank.
