Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — GST/HST and income tax administration; portals: Business Registration Online (businessregistration.gc.ca), My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE. Provincial: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) via eTaxBC; BC Registrar of Companies (OneStop Business Registry) for limited partnership registration that triggers the federal Business Number (BN).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the LP in BC through OneStop Business Registry or BC Corporate Online; the Registry issues the LP registration certificate and BC company/registry number. Step 2: BC registration data is electronically transmitted to CRA, which issues a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with program accounts RC (GST/HST), RT (income tax) and RP (payroll) as applicable. Step 3: If automatic BN issuance does not occur, file Form RC1 or register online via Business Registration Online; a foreign LP without a Canadian resident general partner should appoint an authorized representative (Form RC59) and may be required to post security under Excise Tax Act s. 307. Step 4: Apply for the BC PST seller number through eTaxBC once the BN is obtained; PST dealer registration applies to retail sales of tangible personal property delivered in BC without a monetary threshold. Step 5: The partnership files an annual T5013 information return, and each partner reports allocated income (non-resident partners only on Canadian-source business income, with s. 194 withholding/undertaking obligations). No minimum capital is required; non-resident ownership is permitted; BN issuance is typically immediate online or up to 2 weeks by mail.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96; the partnership itself is not a taxpayer for income tax, and partnership income/loss is allocated to partners. For GST/HST, the partnership is a separate person under the Excise Tax Act and may register as a GST/HST registrant in its own right. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident limited partners are taxed only on Canadian-source business income, subject to treaty protection and the s. 194 15% withholding/security regime on partnership profits. The active retail consignment business keeps the LP outside the Specified Investment Flow-Through (SIFT) entity tax. For PST, the LP is the vendor and must collect 7% PST on BC deliveries.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity election; classification follows the Income Tax Act, and an LP is per se a partnership (unless a SIFT, which is not applicable here). Material elections and regimes: (1) Excise Tax Act s. 176 used-goods margin election — a GST registrant dealing in used luxury goods may elect to charge GST only on the difference between the selling price and the dealer's prescribed cost, i.e., the margin scheme, critical for vintage consignment resale; (2) GST Quick Method accounting election — available where annual worldwide taxable supplies (including associates) do not exceed C$400,000, with a 1.8% remittance rate for retail/wholesale sales in BC; (3) Income Tax Act s. 249.1 alternative fiscal-period election for partnerships, with an ordinary-income inclusion for the stub period; (4) Excise Tax Act s. 179 drop-shipment documentation protocol to zero-rate goods delivered in Canada to a non-resident purchaser's designee; (5) hybrid-mismatch planning under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 where a US LLC or other hybrid entity is a partner, with treaty-based relief available to avoid deduction denial.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the GST small-supplier threshold measured over the current calendar quarter plus the immediately preceding four calendar quarters of worldwide taxable supplies excluding sales of capital property. GST registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded for a person carrying on business in Canada; certain non-resident suppliers of digital services and platform sellers have special registration rules, and BC PST dealer registration applies to every person making retail sales of tangible personal property in or delivered into BC regardless of volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 of the Excise Tax Act where the LP exports tangible personal property: (i) goods consigned by the registrant to a non-resident and exported as soon as practicable; or (ii) goods mailed or shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada (international consignment sales of vintage luxury goods to US and overseas customers). The zero-rating attaches only if the supplier retains documentary evidence of export at the time of supply; goods delivered inside Canada are taxable even if destined for resale abroad unless the Excise Tax Act s. 179 drop-shipment conditions are met (non-resident recipient registered for GST/HST or prescribed documentation held). Incidental authentication and platform services supplied to non-resident consignors are zero-rated under Sch. VI, Part V, ss. 10-16 where the applicable conditions are satisfied. BC PST does not apply to goods shipped by the vendor to a destination outside BC or outside Canada; an Export Exemption Certificate or equivalent evidence must be retained. Export refunds arise through the normal GST return mechanism: the zero-rated export supports full input tax credit recovery, and CRA pays any resulting refund after processing.

  • Customs Export Declaration — B13A / CAED filing through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 per export; low-value courier and postal shipments under CAD 2,000 are exempt from export reporting but still require commercial documentation.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — carrier-issued waybill from FedEx, DHL, UPS or Canada Post showing the consignee's non-Canadian address, shipment date and tracking numbers.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — foreign-currency settlement statements from Stripe, PayPal or the escrow agent evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer and the associated USD/EUR/GBP conversion.
  • Commercial Invoice — full export commercial invoice showing the Canadian LP's Business Number, HS classification, quantity, declared customs value and zero-rated status.
  • Proof of Departure — carrier scan history showing the parcel's exit from Canada and delivery abroad.
  • PST Export Exemption Record — BC export exemption certificate or equivalent documentary support evidencing that the goods were shipped outside BC, exempting the sale from 7% PST.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-authority stamping or digital stamp regime exists in Canada. A GST/HST registrant must issue a qualifying sales receipt or invoice for taxable supplies exceeding C$30 showing the supplier's Business Number, the amount of GST charged and the date; e-invoices generated by Shopify, Stripe or ERP systems satisfy Excise Tax Act s. 223 content requirements. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoice Framework (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is voluntary for B2B; CRA has certified PEPPOL service providers, and mandatory PEPPOL e-invoicing for federal public procurement is phased in from 2026-2027. BC PST imposes its own invoice and record-keeping duties under the Consumer Taxation Act, including retention of a sales invoice identifying the PST number, but no pre-clearance of invoices by the Province.

CRA audits export zero-rating by cross-matching courier waybills, customs export declarations, foreign-currency receipts and T5013 allocations. A failure to substantiate that goods physically left Canada within a reasonable time recharacterizes the supply as domestic and taxable, triggering reassessment of unremitted 5% GST, compound daily interest at the prescribed rate (90-day Treasury Bill plus 4%, revised quarterly), and denial of associated input tax credits. Civil penalties include the greater of C$250 and a percentage of net tax for registration/filing failures (ETA s. 280), 25% of the tax sought to be evaded for false statements (ETA s. 281), and up to 50% of income tax for gross negligence (ITA s. 163(2)). CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation, carelessness or willful default is established (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152(4)); criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 or ITA s. 239 can produce fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. BC enforces parallel liability under the Consumer Taxation Act, including penalty assessments on unremitted PST plus interest, with no limitation window for fraudulent misrepresentation. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-currency repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is retained as the pipeline's internal documentary-completion benchmark, and transfer-pricing documentation should corroborate that arm's-length consignment commissions were settled within the customary payment cycle.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate policy. Canada imposes no comprehensive exchange-control law: both current-account and capital-account transactions, including capital contributions into a British Columbia Limited Partnership, are unrestricted. Profit and capital remittances to non-resident partners are free of FX licensing; the sole frictions are Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), FINTRAC reporting for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and enhanced bank due diligence for source-of-funds. The BC LP should nevertheless maintain a documented capital account ledger, the partnership agreement, and an Investment Canada Act notification assessment because non-resident partners are permitted and may constitute foreign control of a new Canadian business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada for a BC Limited Partnership. The LP may retain receivables in USD, EUR or any foreign currency without repatriation or conversion obligations. However, wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more crossing Canada's border trigger FINTRAC reportable-transaction monitoring by financial institutions, and foreign-currency accounts must be revalued at CRA functional-currency or spot-rate conventions for income tax reporting. Documentary evidence of export invoices, airway bills and authentication certificates should be retained to support each foreign-currency receipt.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada; the Bank Act, the Currency Act and the Canadian Payments Act, with FINTRAC administering cross-border currency transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no standalone foreign exchange administration; exchange policy is conducted through the free-floating Canadian dollar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High, subject to FINTRAC registration and licensing constraints. Wise (TransferWise Ltd) and Payoneer offer multi-currency settlement accounts but are not Canadian chartered banks; they operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and, where applicable, as authorized foreign bank service providers under OSFI supervision. Legal constraints: (i) digital EMIs cannot hold escrow balances governed by BC trust law; escrow for high-value consignment resale must be held by a BC-licensed trust company, or by the bank itself; (ii) Stripe settlement and Escrow_Pay disbursements cannot be routed into an EMI as the sole liquidity reservoir because the underlying card-settlement rails require a Canadian acquiring bank; (iii) EMIs trigger additional enhanced due diligence for luxury-goods provenance and sanctions screening; (iv) the LP must not describe an EMI as its 'bank' in marketing or vendor contracts. Recommendation: deploy EMIs exclusively as operational disbursement concentration and FX conversion engines while maintaining primary liquidity, escrow and borrowing facilities at tier-1 Canadian financial institutions.

Root cause: Non-resident beneficial ownership above 25% combined with opaque limited-partner chain

Defense evidence: BC Registries partnership registration certificate; registered address confirmation; partnership agreement showing capital and profit-sharing; 25%+ beneficial ownership declaration; certified ID and proof-of-address for each partner; ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) register if the General Partner is a corporation; signed source-of-wealth statement; notarized copies authenticated by Canadian embassy or consulate for non-resident partners.
Luxury goods provenance and money-laundering via high-value consignments

Root cause: Luxury goods provenance and money-laundering via high-value consignments

Defense evidence: Third-party authentication certificates; itemized purchase invoices from source estates, dealer lots and auction houses; consignment agreements with each consignor; chain-of-custody logs and storage records; insurance valuations; Stripe and Escrow_Pay transaction records; anti-counterfeiting quality-control SOP; resale-price reconciliation demonstrating margin consistent with market data.
Payment-rail mismatch: Stripe card settlement versus delayed consignor payout cycles

Root cause: Payment-rail mismatch: Stripe card settlement versus delayed consignor payout cycles

Defense evidence: Stripe settlement statements mapped to a CAD/USD operating sub-ledger; escrow reserve sub-ledger maintained in the bank account; consignor payout schedule and aging report; cash-flow projection model; monthly reconciliation of gross proceeds against commissions, consignor payables and withdrawal fees.
Sanctions or PEP screening failure on foreign buyers, consignors or auction-house counterparties

Root cause: Sanctions or PEP screening failure on foreign buyers, consignors or auction-house counterparties

Defense evidence: Automated screening logs against OFSI, OFAC, UN and EU consolidated lists; PEP registry checks; geo-location and VPN-detection policy for e-commerce customers; refusal and investigation register; contractual language restricting resale or transshipment into sanctioned jurisdictions; end-customer address verification for consigned pieces.
High-frequency cross-border wires to Geneva, Milan, New York and Hong Kong counterparties

Root cause: High-frequency cross-border wires to Geneva, Milan, New York and Hong Kong counterparties

Defense evidence: Service contracts and invoices with authentication labs and auction houses; SWIFT MT103 remittance-info fields quoting invoice, lot and shipment numbers; customs import/export declarations (B3 entries); airway bills and courier logs; CITES documentation where vintage ivory, reptile skin or endangered materials are traded.
Escrow account class flagged as unlicensed trust-deposit taking

Root cause: Escrow account class flagged as unlicensed trust-deposit taking

Defense evidence: ESCROW PAY service agreement using a licensed trust company or bank escrow wrapper; segregation attestation; partnership undertaking that third-party funds are never held in the LP operating account beyond the 48-hour clearing window; FINTRAC MSB registration certificate if the LP receives and remits funds for consignors in aggregate; legal opinion that the LP is not carrying on deposit business.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Canadian settlement account at a tier-1 bank (preferred: RBC or Vancity). Protocol: (i) wire field 70 (Remittance Information) must recite 'Capital contribution - [LP legal name] - under Partnership Agreement dated [date]'; (ii) receiving bank must match the incoming wire to the LP capital-account ledger maintained under the BC Partnership Act; (iii) where the remittance exceeds CAD 10,000, the FI will record it as a reportable electronic funds transfer and may request the source-of-funds bundle (trading statement, sale agreement, gift instrument or loan agreement); (iv) where a non-resident establishes or acquires control of the Canadian business, Investment Canada Act notification requirements must be assessed and filed within 30 days of establishment; (v) no Bank of Canada exchange-control approval is required; the contribution may be funded in USD or EUR and converted to CAD at the bank's commercial rate, with the FX conversion documented on a CAD value-date receipt. Stripe and Escrow_Pay proceeds must never be treated as substitute capital contributions because platform settlements are operating receipts, not partnership equity.Clearing-rail architecture: (i) card payouts from Stripe are swept automatically to the LP's CAD operational account on T+2; (ii) domestic consignor payouts are executed via Interac e-Transfer or EFT (ACH) batch, with Escrow_Pay releasing funds only against a matched consignment order; (iii) international supplier wires to authentication labs, auction houses and vintage dealers use SWIFT MT103 (GPI) with field 70 reciting invoice and shipment identifiers; (iv) for high-value authenticated pieces exceeding USD 50,000, sellers may require a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) payable against presentation of the authentication certificate, insurance certificate, airway bill and customs duty receipt; (v) CAD domestic large-value clearing rides Lynx and the Real-Time Rail (RTR), while USD commercial settlements clear via CHIPS or Fedwire from the multi-currency USD account; (vi) an internal treasury policy should cap same-day outgoing wires at CAD 100,000 with dual-signature release to reduce fraud loss in the consignment model.Deliverable CAD/USD forward contract with bank dealing desk, tenors up to 24 months, for budgeted Eurozone and Swiss supplier costsFX call/put options (European-style, OTC, CAD/USD) for downside protection on USD-denominated consignment inventoryZero-cost collar (cylinder) combining bought put and sold call on CAD/USD to hedge Est. purchase commitmentsCross-currency swap (CAD/USD floating-to-fixed) to fund multi-season inventory finance with matched interest and principal flowsFX swap (spot-to-forward bridge) for interim liquidity conversion on USD receipt before repatriating into CAD operating account

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-High, subject to FINTRAC registration and licensing constraints. Wise (TransferWise Ltd) and Payoneer offer multi-currency settlement accounts but are not Canadian chartered banks; they operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and, where applicable, as authorized foreign bank service providers under OSFI supervision. Legal constraints: (i) digital EMIs cannot hold escrow balances governed by BC trust law; escrow for high-value consignment resale must be held by a BC-licensed trust company, or by the bank itself; (ii) Stripe settlement and Escrow_Pay disbursements cannot be routed into an EMI as the sole liquidity reservoir because the underlying card-settlement rails require a Canadian acquiring bank; (iii) EMIs trigger additional enhanced due diligence for luxury-goods provenance and sanctions screening; (iv) the LP must not describe an EMI as its 'bank' in marketing or vendor contracts. Recommendation: deploy EMIs exclusively as operational disbursement concentration and FX conversion engines while maintaining primary liquidity, escrow and borrowing facilities at tier-1 Canadian financial institutions.
