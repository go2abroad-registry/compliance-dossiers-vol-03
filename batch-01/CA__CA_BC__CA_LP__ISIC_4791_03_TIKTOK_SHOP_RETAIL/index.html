Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal income tax, GST/HST, and Business Number (BN) administration via Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) via eTaxBC portal; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — customs and export declaration administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a BC Limited Partnership must: (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) for the partnership through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1; (2) apply for a GST/HST account (BN program account RT) once worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or voluntarily under Excise Tax Act s. 240(3) to recover input tax credits and claim export zero-rating refunds; (3) ensure each partner separately obtains an income tax identifier (individual SIN/ITN or corporate BN) for allocation of partnership income under ITA s. 96; (4) register for BC PST via eTaxBC once annual taxable sales in BC exceed CAD 10,000; (5) open a CBSA export/import program account if physically exporting goods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal law. A BC Limited Partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent (Income Tax Act s. 96; BC Limited Partnership Act). Available mechanisms: (i) ITA s. 601.1 late-filed election giving retroactive validity within a three-year window; (ii) ITA s. 216 election permitting a non-resident partner to file a Part I return on Canadian-source rental income instead of Part XIII non-resident withholding; (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 240(3) voluntary registration election for GST/HST; (iv) mandatory hybrid-mismatch reporting and denial-of-deduction rules under Part XII.6 of the Income Tax Act (BEPS Action 2 implementation), which do not provide an elective classification but impose disclosure obligations for structured arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold measured on worldwide taxable supplies (excluding financial services) per single calendar quarter or trailing four quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 148); a non-resident carrying on business in Canada that exceeds this threshold must register. BC PST: vendor registration is compulsory when annual gross revenue from taxable sales in BC exceeds CAD 10,000; effective 1 July 2021, marketplace operators (e.g., TikTok Shop) are deemed the collector for sales made through the platform, which may relieve the underlying BC LP vendor of separate PST collection obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated GST (0%) applies to: (1) tangible goods exported by the supplier from Canada under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 15-16; (2) services supplied to a non-resident where the service is not performed in Canada and does not relate to real property in Canada, tangible goods in Canada, or a Canadian resident's benefit, under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 20-23; (3) in the TikTok Shop retail context, the BC LP must demonstrate that goods are shipped to foreign consumers outside Canada or that the platform-mediated digital facilitation service is supplied to a non-resident platform operator. BC PST does not apply to goods exported by the vendor, but PST applies to goods delivered in BC to Canadian consumers. CRA requires contemporaneous documentary evidence of export and registration to claim net refunds of GST paid on inputs.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) or electronic Canadian Export Declarations (CAED/ECE) manifest confirmation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice, sales contract, and purchase order identifying foreign buyer
  • Packing list and carrier/warehouse receipt proving dispatch and foreign delivery
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate or wire-transfer confirmation evidencing foreign-currency receipt
  • CRA six-year retention records (ETA s. 286) substantiating zero-rating on each export transaction

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime. CRA accepts electronic invoices in EDI/XML and requires electronic records retention for six years. The Pan-Canadian Business e-Invoicing pilot (2025-2026) is voluntary. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, and BC PST is self-assessed and remitted through the eTaxBC portal, with no mandatory electronic document pre-authorization before sale.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims by cross-referencing CBSA export declarations, bills of lading, and inbound FX remittances; missing or false documentary support triggers reassessment at 5% GST with prescribed-rate compound interest, plus a 25% gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 280 for knowingly false statements or omissions, and potential prosecution with fines and imprisonment for GST fraud. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter is a pipeline control metric, although delayed or unexplained inbound remittances may be treated as evidence that no export occurred, exposing the LP to full GST/PST clawback plus penalties. BC PST clawback risk is limited to PST not collected on domestic BC deliveries mischaracterized as exports.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account items; no exchange controls, no prior approval for foreign-exchange transactions, and no restriction on profit remittance by non-resident partners of a British Columbia limited partnership. A non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source profits is, however, subject to the withholding tax regime of Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), which requires the Canadian payor to withhold a statutory 25% (reduced under tax treaties) on distributions; no separate FX permit or repatriation license is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement on export receipts. A British Columbia limited partnership may retain TikTok Shop sales proceeds in foreign currency (e.g., USD, CNY) indefinitely in offshore or domestic accounts; the only obligations are: (i) functional-currency reporting rules under the Income Tax Act for accounts kept in a currency other than CAD, and (ii) FINTRAC transaction reporting obligations (large cash, large virtual-currency, and international electronic funds-transfer thresholds).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision and transaction reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no exchange control authority or foreign-exchange licensing board; the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act govern legal tender and monetary policy, while the federal Foreign Exchange Control regime was dismantled in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium as a supplemental clearing rail, not a primary corporate banking substitute. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Ltd.) and Payoneer hold Canadian money-services-business (MSB) registrations with FINTRAC and can receive TikTok Shop payouts and effect USD/CAD conversions; however, they are not Schedule I chartered banks, do not offer commercial credit, letters of credit, or escrow custody, and are subject to MSB risk-based compliance frameworks. An authorized foreign-financial-institution branch or licensed EMI from the UK/EU is not permitted to act as a primary transactional bank for a BC LP; only provinces and the federal Parliament charter deposit-taking institutions, so any core operational banking must reside with a Payments Canada member institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) from the contributing partner's bank to the BC LP's tier-1 Canadian bank account; the field-70 remittance-info narrative must explicitly state 'Capital contribution — Limited Partnership interest', the LP's BC registration number, and the contributing partner's name. Settlement occurs through Payments Canada's Lynx Real-Time Gross Settlement / ACSS retail clearing for CAD, and via the USD clearing house (Cheque-in-USB / continuous linked settlement) where the account is a USD sub-account. The receiving bank will apply standard inward-wire verification against the KYC/UBO file; no FX conversion approval is necessary from the Bank of Canada because Canada imposes no capital controls. For tax-annexed clarity, the contribution must not be characterized as a distribution; a notarized partners' resolution and contribution agreement should be produced to the bank on request.Outbound trade settlement for TikTok Shop inventory procurement uses three rails in priority order: (1) SWIFT MT103 USD/CAD customer credit transfers for vendor payments below CAD 25,000; (2) documentary Commercial Letters of Credit (sight, irrevocable, confirmed where the supplier requires it) for bulk purchase orders exceeding CAD 50,000 or when transacting with new vendors in Asia; and (3) bulk ACH/NACHA-style payroll and operational disbursements through Canadian clearing for domestic costs.
