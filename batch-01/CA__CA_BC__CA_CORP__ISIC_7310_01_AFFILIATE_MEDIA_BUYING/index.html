Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal corporate income tax (T2), GST/HST, and payroll; BC Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) — British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST); Corporations Canada — federal incorporation; BC Registry Services / OneStop — provincial incorporation. Primary portals: CRA My Business Account, Represent a Client, Business Registration Online (BRO), and BC eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the Business Corporation federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act; obtain the certificate of incorporation and corporation number, and appoint a Canadian resident director/agent if the corporation is controlled by non-residents. (2) Register with the CRA through Business Registration Online to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts: RC0001 (corporate income tax/T2), RT0001 (GST/HST), and RP0001 (payroll if the BC affiliate employs Canadian staff). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and is not eligible for pass-through treatment. If Canadian-controlled and private (CCPC), the first CAD 500,000 of active business income qualifies for the small business deduction (federal 9% + BC 2%); other active income is taxed at approximately 27% combined federal/BC general corporate rates (15% federal + 12% BC). Dividends paid to the non-resident parent are subject to 25% Part XIII withholding tax, generally reduced to 5% by the Canada-United States tax treaty for a US parent owning at least 10% of the voting shares.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification regime. Available elections and mechanisms: (i) CCPC/small business deduction elections and associated-corporation allocation under ITA s. 256; (ii) GST/HST Quick Method election and annual/quarterly filing elections; (iii) ITA s. 250(5) deemed non-resident election to coordinate dual-resident corporation status; (iv) foreign affiliate and exempt-surplus elections under ITA s. 113 and Regulation 5907 for the Canadian shareholder; (v) competent-authority and advance-pricing-agreement procedures to prevent double taxation of cross-border media-buying service margins; (vi) compliance with the Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.21), which operate as deduction-denial and withholding-override rules rather than elective classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: a supplier becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant once consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA small-supplier threshold). BC PST: every vendor selling taxable goods or taxable services in BC must register; an out-of-province or non-resident vendor must register for PST when its taxable BC sales exceed CAD 10,000 in the preceding 12 months, and small-supplier exemptions do not apply to PST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies to: (i) tangible personal property exported by the supplier or delivered to a carrier outside Canada within 60 days of the supply; and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person, provided the service is not in respect of real estate situated in Canada, is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and is not supplied to a non-resident individual present in Canada. Advertising, affiliate media-buying, and lead-generation services sold by the BC affiliate to its foreign parent or foreign advertisers — where the campaigns are executed for offshore audiences — ordinarily qualify as zero-rated exported services. The supplier must hold reasonable contemporaneous evidence of the non-resident recipient status and of the export character of the supplies.

  • Customs export declaration: Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / Automated Export Declaration (CAED) filing for physical goods exported from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing the departure of goods from Canada within the 60-day zero-rating window (goods only)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT / FX settlement records evidencing cross-border payment from the foreign affiliate
  • Commercial invoices and the executed media-buying agreement, insertion order, or affiliate contract identifying the non-resident recipient and the zero-rated supply
  • Non-resident status proof of the foreign counterparty (e.g., foreign certificate of incorporation, registry extract, or CRA non-resident declaration)
  • Digital campaign delivery and geo-targeting reports substantiating that the advertising services were directed to audiences outside Canada

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, and no statutory e-invoicing system at federal or provincial level. The CRA requires GST/HST registrants to file electronically (GST/HST NETFILE/XML), maintain auditable machine-readable business records for six years, and produce invoices evidencing zero-rated exports. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is voluntary. BC PST requires vendors to retain sales invoices and records in Canada for six years and to file periodic PST remittances through eTaxBC.

The CRA may reassess within four years of an assessment and indefinitely where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is shown; if zero-rating is overturned, the 5% GST on the supply is clawed back with compound interest. Inadequate export documentation attracts the gross-negligence penalty under Excise Tax Act s. 280 equal to 25% of the tax sought (minimum CAD 200). Willful evasion or fraudulent zero-rating/refund claims are criminal offences under ETA s. 327 and Income Tax Act s. 239, carrying substantial fines and imprisonment. Cross-border service payments between affiliated entities are also examined for transfer-pricing compliance under ITA s. 247; failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation may trigger adjustments, the 10% documentation penalty, and related Part XIII withholding reassessments. BC PST audits may separately reassess uncollected PST and impose provincial penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the Canadian dollar (CAD, ISO 4217). The federal Foreign Exchange Control Act was terminated in 1952 and no successor exchange-control statute has been enacted, so there are no surrender requirements, dual exchange rates, advance import deposits, FX allocation quotas, or administrative licensing for external payments or profit repatriation. For an ISIC 7310/NAICS 541810 affiliate media buying corporation, all commercial payments to foreign publishers, DSPs, ad networks, and affiliate partners may be freely remitted in any convertible currency. Profit-remittance frictions are exclusively tax-based: Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) imposes a statutory 25% withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents, reduced under tax treaties - for example, the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (1980, as amended by the 2007 Protocol) applies a 5% rate for a US corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% of the voting stock, and 15% for most other US residents; the Canada-UK Convention provides analogous relief. Such withholding is a CRA tax-collection mechanism, not an FX control. The CAD trades in a free float; the Bank of Canada does not restrict purchases of foreign currency for business or investment purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation, deposit, or conversion of export proceeds or cross-border service revenue, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency received by a Canadian business corporation. A British Columbia affiliate media buying entity may retain 100% of USD, EUR, GBP, and other foreign currency receipts in foreign currency accounts at Canadian banks indefinitely, convert to CAD at its own election, or pledge such balances as collateral without central bank approval. The only applicable compliance duties are: (i) FINTRAC cross-border wire transfer reporting (CBWTR) by the financial institution for international wires of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) FINTRAC large cash transaction reporting (LCTR) for cash transactions of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) annual CRA tax filing translated into CAD at the Bank of Canada spot rate or a rate that reasonably reflects market value under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act; and (iv) the requirement not to structure transactions to evade reporting thresholds. There are no export surrender ratios, forced conversion timelines, or central bank ledger controls.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2; original statute 1934, operations commenced 11 March 1935), is the sole monetary authority and manager of Canada's official international reserves. It does not operate an exchange-control consent regime and does not approve individual cross-border payments. Prudential regulation of federally chartered banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing rule-making and supervision rest with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), S.C. 2000, c. 17, as amended. Neither the Bank of Canada nor any British Columbia provincial authority imposes prior authorization for FX transactions or capital movements.
