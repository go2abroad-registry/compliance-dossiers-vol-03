Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; jointly with British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST); CBSA for export customs declarations

E-government filing portal

Non-resident and resident foreign-owned corporations obtain a nine-digit CRA Business Number (BN) before commencing activity. Apply online via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or Form RC459 for a non-resident, identifying program accounts RT (GST/HST), RZ (BC PST), RP (payroll), and RC (corporate income tax). Upon registration, CRA issues a BN and GST/HST registration number (e.g., 123456789 RT 0001). BC PST registration is integrated through the BC Ministry of Finance; foreign entities must appoint a resident agent where required for provincial tax notices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no full check-the-box regime; CRA administrative look-through applies for certain U.S. LLCs treated as fiscally transparent for treaty purposes. Available elections include: Subsection 85(1) rollover of property to corporation on tax-deferred basis; subsection 97(2) partnership-to-corporation rollover; subsection 150(2) non-resident corporation election; GST/HST sections 156 and 157 elections for closely related corporations to simplify reporting; combined GST/HST-PST election for BC declared zero-rated supplies; and treaty-based elections for hybrid mismatch neutrality under the Canada-U.S. Fifth Protocol and OECD BEPS hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold measured over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (and current quarter); BC PST: CAD 10,000 annual gross revenue threshold for vendor registration; CRO activities exceeding either threshold require mandatory registration and collection

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of exported goods under Schedule VI, sections 5-8, and to supplies of services made to a non-resident recipient where the benefit is enjoyed outside Canada and the service is not in respect of Canadian real estate or tangible personal property in Canada (Schedule VI, sections 7-8). Contract Research Organization clinical-trial management services for multinational sponsors qualify for zero-rating when invoiced to and paid by a non-resident sponsor, performed in Canada but for the foreign trial, and evidenced as exported services; ancillary supplies of clinical trial materials exported physically follow goods zero-rating. Registration and collection of input tax credits on zero-rated inputs are mandatory; partial exemption rules apply to mixed-use expenses.

  • CBSA B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration (CESARS filing) for physical clinical trial goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the clinical trial protocol
  • Airway Bill or Bill of Lading showing consignee outside Canada
  • Signed Sponsor CRO Services Agreement / Master Clinical Trial Agreement and Statement of Work
  • GST/HST zero-rated invoice (Schedule VI section 7/8) and proof of export status
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 payment evidence from non-resident sponsor
  • Bank statements evidencing foreign currency receipt
  • CRA Form GST66 (Application or claim) and retained service records

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory clearance API. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA My Business Account or approved EDI software; participants in the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework may exchange B2B e-invoices via PEPPOL BIS 3.0. Federal procurement e-invoicing mandate is being phased in from 2025-2027. BC PST returns use the Ministry of Finance Taxpayer Self-Service portal; CROs must retain transactional records for six years to support audit and e-invoice integrity. No fiscal stamping regime applies.

CRA applies targeted audit review of exported-service claims, requiring documentary substantiation and physical export evidence. Misstated or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger reassessment, interest, and refund clawback with retrospective effect. Civil penalties under Excise Tax Act subsection 285(1) equal 25% of the net tax misstatement, section 280 penalties for failure to file, and section 281(1) gross-negligence penalties of 25%. Willful evasion carries criminal penalties of up to 200% of taxes evaded and imprisonment under Excise Tax Act sections 327-330 and Income Tax Act section 239. CRA may also deny input tax credits and apply joint and several liability against officers; provincial PST assessments and interest compound separately for uncollected PST on taxable BC sales.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada observes IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital transactions; there are no general exchange-control licences, surrender requirements, or repatriation approvals. Profit remittances by non-residents are unrestricted at the FX level, but may attract withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, generally 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty; for direct investment equity injections from a non-resident, Investment Canada Act thresholds may require notification or review depending on transaction size and nature of business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory deadline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. Receipts from CRO service exports may be retained in the invoiced currency (CAD, USD, EUR, etc.) or converted on fully market-based terms. Export documentation is required only for customs statistics and regulatory purposes, typically via the Canada Border Services Agency export declaration framework, and does not serve any exchange-control function.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada has no separate foreign exchange administration; bank prudential supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting is supervised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary cross-border collection and payout channels; Low as a primary treasury clearing hub. Offshore electronic-money institutions cannot operate as Canadian banks or hold Canadian retail deposits under the Bank Act. Such entities may be used as payment facilitators only if they comply with Canadian money-services-business registration requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and applicable provincial payment/remittance laws. An offshore EMI cannot lawfully replace a Schedule I domestic commercial bank for the core capital, escrow, trade settlement, and clinical-trial sponsor disbursement functions of a BC-incorporated CRO; any EMI overlay must settle through a Canadian bank account and maintain full auditability.

Root cause: Non-Canadian ownership with offshore shareholders and no prior Canadian banking history

Defense evidence: Certificate of incorporation and articles; BC extra-provincial or registered office certificate; complete corporate register and shareholders ledger; notarised passports and proof of address for all material owners; home-jurisdiction corporate registry extracts; source-of-funds evidence for initial capital contribution; board resolution opening accounts and designating signing officers; Canadian legal counsel opinion confirming corporate validity and the intended CRO business model.

Defense evidence: Executed clinical trial master/service agreements; Health Canada and research-ethics-board approvals; signed clinical trial protocols; detailed milestone and invoice schedules; KYC and due-diligence files on every site and vendor; enhanced sanctions and politically-exposed-persons screening results; recording of ultimate beneficiaries; board-approved anti-bribery due diligence; sponsor attestation that the payments relate to legitimate contracted research.

Defense evidence: Investigator payment schedule embedded in the protocol; signed clinical trial site agreements; favourable ethics-board approvals; professional qualifications and licences of site investigators; itemised site invoices; treasury reconciliation mapping sponsor funds to each wire; documented no-commingling controls; and periodic sponsor-advance and milestone completion reports submitted to the bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or ultimate shareholder to the Canadian Schedule I bank, with beneficiary account opened in the corporate name. The payment narrative must state: 'CAPITAL INJECTION / EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION — BC BUSINESS CORPORATION' together with the CRA business number and BC registration number. Supporting documentation to be presented at onboarding and on each remittance includes the board resolution approving share issuance, the share subscription agreement, and source-of-funds evidence in the form of audited financial statements or bank statements. The transfer is treated as direct investment in Canada; no exchange-control permission is required. If the investment is notifiable under the Investment Canada Act, a notification must be filed; for ordinary capitalisation below review thresholds, no pre-clearance is required. The receiving bank will complete FINTRAC reporting where applicable and will verify the beneficiary against sanctions and politically-exposed-persons lists before crediting the operating or escrow account.Outbound milestone payments to foreign trial sites and sub-CRO vendors should be settled by SWIFT MT103 using the operating account maintained with the Canadian bank. Domestic Canadian-dollar payments are cleared through the ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) or the Real-Time Rail; same-day CAD wires settle through the Large Value Transfer System. For large, milestone-linked procurement of clinical supplies, laboratory services, or site accommodations, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank provides payment security while preserving auditability. Every disbursement must carry in its remittance detail the clinical protocol number, contract/milestone identifier, invoice reference, and beneficiary entity in full. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (15% federal + 12% British Columbia provincial)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Income Tax Act Part XIII)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., Dec 31 year-end → June 30 filing deadline).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 100 Total revenue (gross receipts from CRO services, milestone payments, and reimbursements) Report all Canadian-source revenue; allocate based on permanent establishment presence. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month (max 12 months); repeated failure: 10% plus 2% per month; daily interest on arrears at prescribed rate (currently 9% for 2024).

Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month (max 12 months); repeated failure: 10% plus 2% per month; daily interest on arrears at prescribed rate (currently 9% for 2024).
Box 300 Taxable income (revenue minus allowable deductions, including SR&ED expenditures) Compute taxable income per Part I of the Income Tax Act; consider treaty non-discrimination clauses. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month (max 12 months); repeated failure: 10% plus 2% per month; daily interest on arrears at prescribed rate (currently 9% for 2024).
Box 400 Federal and provincial tax payable (after credits and surtaxes) Apply treaty withholding rates on dividends, interest, and royalties; claim foreign tax credits if applicable. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month (max 12 months); repeated failure: 10% plus 2% per month; daily interest on arrears at prescribed rate (currently 9% for 2024).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million or cross-border transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million (services) or CAD 250,000 (intangibles).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone ESR, but CRA applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form doctrine; a CRO must demonstrate real operational substance (employees, office, decision-making) to avoid reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of tax evaded plus interest; criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239 with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax under s.227.1; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for gross negligence.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: CRA can reassess under GAAR, disallow deductions, and impose 25% penalty on tax benefit; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they authorized or acquiesced to sham arrangements; asset seizure and travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for knowingly failing to comply with Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.
