Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax-business-number.html). British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (PST, EHT) is the complementary provincial tax authority.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA's Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1, PR1, or PDF/paper forms. The non-resident partnership must provide a Canadian business address and designated representative information; if not carrying on a taxable business requiring GST/HST registration, a voluntary registration can be made under Excise Tax Act (ETA). The same BN is used for GST/HST, corporate/payroll accounts, and import/export accounts. Once the BN is issued, the partnership should separately open a BC provincial PST vendor account via Revenue (BC) eTaxBC or BC Forms 4688/4689 where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under the Income Tax Act, a Canadian limited partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent: the partnership itself is not a taxable entity and income/losses flow through to partners. Non-resident partners are taxed on their Canadian-source partnership business income through required filings (e.g., Form T2122/T2043 for dispositions). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under the Income Tax Act, a Canadian limited partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent: the partnership itself is not a taxable entity and income/losses flow through to partners. Non-resident partners are taxed on their Canadian-source partnership business income through required filings (e.g., Form T2122/T2043 for dispositions). For GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' under ETA, distinct from its partners, and the LP itself must register for GST/HST if making taxable supplies in Canada and not a small supplier.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide US-style check-the-box elections; an LP is a deemed pass-through entity by default. For non-resident partners, an election under ITA s. 216 is available to file a return based on net taxable Canadian income rather than gross withholding. Under the ETA, a partnership may make s. 271 partnership elections for GST/HST account stability, and s. 273 elections can deem supplies between the partnership and a partner to be supplies between separate persons. Hybrid mismatch elections under ITA ss. 96 and 97 are limited by domestic deeming rules; no elective Canadian corporate tax classification of an LP is permissible.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies computed over a calendar quarter and previous four quarters for GST/HST small-supplier threshold. BC PST registration is mandatory for every vendor making taxable sales in BC; a distance-selling vendor has registration obligations upon exceeding CAD 10,000 of BC taxable sales in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For tangible goods, a supply is zero-rated when the supplier transfers ownership or practical control and the goods are exported by the supplier or a common carrier/expediter as part of the supply; goods must be delivered outside Canada within 60 days of the supply or within 60 days after any later consideration due date (Schedule VI Part V ETA). Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated if they are performed wholly outside Canada or, if not, are of a class of services listed under Schedule VI Part V (e.g., advisory, financial). The B2B marketplace operator must maintain conclusive evidence that physical goods left Canada and that payment in Forex was received.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A) with customs broker confirmation
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent common-carrier waybill showing consignee outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (CRA Form RC-44 foreign exchange record or bank SWIFT/FX acknowledgment)
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order matching ISIC 4791 B2B marketplace transaction
  • Packing list, weight/quantity certificate, and booking confirmation

Canada currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing/API necessity. CRA accepts paper and electronic invoices if they meet the prescribed information under ETA s. 221 and Form GST34-1 requirements. Voluntary adoption of the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard (PEPPOL) is expected to become mandatory for federal procurement suppliers; no immediate BC-specific fiscal authorization API exists.

CRA and CBSA audit export zero-rating claims intensively; missing or inadequate export documentation results in denial of zero-rating and reassessment of GST/HST plus insterest. False statements, misrepresentations, or evasion attract penalties under ETA s. 280.1 equal to 50% of the net tax understated/overstated (minimum CAD 100) and gross-negligence penalties; criminal fines and imprisonment can reach CAD 200,000 and 5 years under s. 239. Retrospective reassessments may extend beyond the normal 4-year review period in cases of wilful default, fraud, or carelessness. BC PST fractions on cross-border wholesale sales may similarly be re-assessed if the B2B sale is deemed taxable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible, free-floating hard currency. Current-account transactions (B2B wholesale platform commissions, supplier import payments, subscription and platform fees) and capital-account flows (foreign limited-partner capital contributions, profit distributions, loan repayments, liquidation proceeds) are freely permitted without licensing, ceilings, or forced conversion. Profit remittance by a non-resident limited partner encounters no exchange-control restriction; the applicable hurdles are exclusively tax and reporting in nature: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax imposed by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) on Canadian-source passive income, treaty reduction where available, and FINTRAC reporting obligations for large cash or cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. A BC Limited Partnership may lawfully hold foreign-currency balances in CAD/USD/EUR/CNH sub-accounts and convert at market rates at any time.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no designated-account routing for export or cross-border platform receipts. The BC Limited Partnership may retain USD or other foreign-currency receivables indefinitely in foreign-currency bank accounts. For CRA income-measurement purposes, revenue may be translated using the Bank of Canada daily/annual exchange rate, or the partnership may elect the functional-currency reporting regime where the books are kept in a qualifying foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the monetary authority and operator of the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS)/Lynx RTGS infrastructure, together with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) supervision framework. Foreign-exchange and banking prudential regulation is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally incorporated banks, while anti-money-laundering compliance is enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control administration equivalent to SAFE, and cross-border capital movements require no central-bank approval.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a payment-acceptance rail (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Connect) but Low as the primary treasury or clearing keeper for the BC Limited Partnership. Legal constraints: (i) foreign EMIs authorised in the UK/EU are not licensed under the Bank Act, hold no Payments Canada membership, and cannot provide a Canadian clearing sort code or act as the partnership's settlement bank; (ii) delivering payment services to Canadian residents triggers registration as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, and domestic payment service providers must be licensed by the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); (iii) float balances held with foreign EMIs are not CDIC-insured; and (iv) payout timing lacks the finality of LVTS/Lynx real-time settlement. The recommended treasury architecture uses the EMI only as a client-facing collection node, with all treasury concentration, hedging, and supplier disbursements executed from the Schedule I bank operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Investor funds are transmitted by SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 message to the partnership's CAD or USD account at a Schedule I bank. CAD-denominated transfers require the Payments Canada clearing identity (institution number and branch transit number, e.g., RBC 003-xxxxx), with irrevocable settlement through the Bank of Canada LVTS/Lynx real-time gross settlement system. USD wires are credited via the Canada-US correspondent network over Fedwire or CHIPS. The payment reference field must state 'LP Capital Contribution — [Partnership Legal Name]' and the sending bank must receive, before the test transfer, the signed subscription agreement, source-of-funds questionnaire, and BC LP registration extract to prevent rejection. No Investment Canada Act review or Bank of Canada approval is triggered because the partnership is a domestically registered entity. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a payment-acceptance rail (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Connect) but Low as the primary treasury or clearing keeper for the BC Limited Partnership. Legal constraints: (i) foreign EMIs authorised in the UK/EU are not licensed under the Bank Act, hold no Payments Canada membership, and cannot provide a Canadian clearing sort code or act as the partnership's settlement bank; (ii) delivering payment services to Canadian residents triggers registration as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, and domestic payment service providers must be licensed by the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); (iii) float balances held with foreign EMIs are not CDIC-insured; and (iv) payout timing lacks the finality of LVTS/Lynx real-time settlement. The recommended treasury architecture uses the EMI only as a client-facing collection node, with all treasury concentration, hedging, and supplier disbursements executed from the Schedule I bank operating account.
