Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); for British Columbia provincial sales tax, the Competent Provincial Authority is the BC Ministry of Finance – Consumer Taxation Branch, eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA, which serves as the TIN for income tax and GST/HST. Procedure: (1) determine whether the non-resident entity is carrying on business in Canada or exploiting a Canadian tax presence; (2) submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number (BN)' via CRA's Non-Resident Registration Application, or by fax/telephone under CRA International Tax Services Office protocols; (3) CRA issues BN and GST/HST account program (RT), payroll (RP), and corporation tax (RC) accounts as applicable; (4) for BC PST, register separately through eTaxBC and obtain a PST vendor registration number; (5) foreign corporations must also register extraprovincially under the BC Companies Act before or upon carrying on business in BC. No separate TIN is issued; the BN is the operative tax identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or free-choice entity classification mechanism exists under Canadian tax law. Canadian business corporations are automatic separate taxpayers. Available statutory elections affecting corporate tax include Income Tax Act section 85 corporate rollover elections, section 86 reorganizations, section 93 and 95 foreign affiliate elections, section 216 non-resident rental income elections, and GST/HST Quick Method election for smaller registrants. Hybrid mismatch rules are addressed through ITA ss.18.4–18.5 and arm's-length principles; no direct hybrid entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold) before mandatory registration with CRA. BC PST: If the vendor makes taxable retail sales to BC consumers or is deemed to have a vendor nexus in BC, registration is required; BC has no general small-supplier turnover exemption under the PST Act for retail sellers, though occasional/casual sales may be exempt if not a regular vendor.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero-rated export under ETA Schedule VI applies to: (i) physical goods exported from Canada pursuant to a supply; and (ii) supplies of intangibles or services made to a non-resident where the supply is not acquired for use in Canada and, for services, the service is not substantially performed in Canada. For a mobile utility app sold through Apple IAP/Google Play Billing to users outside Canada, the supply is zero-rated provided the customer is a non-resident, the digital download/access is delivered electronically to an international outlet, and the seller retains credible evidence of non-resident status. Supplies to Canadian residents, including any app-store sales transmitter denominated to Canadian users, are subject to 5% GST plus 7% PST if not specifically zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13; only for tangible goods — not applicable to pure digital downloads)
  • Bill of Lading / Proof of Export (shipping proofs; app-store export logs with IP location and account-origin data)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank proof of receipt of foreign currency or payout from Apple App Store Connect/Google Play Console)
  • Non-resident status evidence (customer records, declaration of non-Canadian address, geolocation data)
  • App Store financial reports and payout statements showing the split of proceeds and commissions
  • Export/service contract and invoice showing supply to non-resident and foreign currency settlement

Canada has no government-mandated real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous tax audit system. GST/HST registrants with taxable supplies above CAD 1.0 million must file electronically via CRA's GST/HST e-services API; all registrants may file through a third-party software, My Business Account, or standardized EDI. BC PST returns must be filed electronically via eTaxBC. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Peppol PINT/Peppol BIS 3.0 standard; no CRA pre-approval or digital signature stamp is required for commercial invoices.

CRA generally has a 4-year statutory reassessment window; in cases of misrepresentation or fraud, reassessment may extend indefinitely. For fraudulent export zero-rating or failure to substantiate the non-resident nature of digital app sales, CRA may reverse the zero-rating and assess GST/HST plus interest. Penalties include ETA s.280(1) penalty of 50% of the tax improperly recovered or claimed; ETA s.285 criminal liability with fines up to 200% of the tax sought to evade or imprisonment; and BC PST assessment under the Provincial Sales Tax Act with interest and a 25% penalty for false statements / tax evasion. In particular, CRA verifies whether app-store recipients are actually Canadian consumers by analyzing payment origination, IP addresses, device carrier country codes, and app-store account territories; inadequate documentation produces clawback of refunds and loss of export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. The Canadian dollar trades freely worldwide, and there are no exchange-control restrictions under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act on the purchase, sale, conversion, or repatriation of funds. Profit remittance and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted but are generally subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax of 25%, reducible to 5%-15% under an applicable income tax treaty. The Investment Canada Act may require a new-business notification where a non-Canadian establishes a new Canadian business above the prescribed financial threshold; no exchange-control approval is required for capital inflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender window, mandatory conversion ratio, or designated-account requirement. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency in Canadian bank accounts, convert at any time at market rates, and make cross-border disbursements without central-bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada operates a free-floating currency regime; there is no specialized foreign-exchange control administration. Ancillary oversight is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for deposit-taking institutions and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High. Canada does not issue an 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licence. Cross-border digital payment platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or through partnership arrangements with Schedule I banks; payment service providers subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) must be registered with the Bank of Canada, with supervision effective November 2024. A British Columbia corporation may lawfully use offshore digital payment platforms for low-value cross-border payouts, but the primary operational treasury account and capital-receipt account should remain with a Canadian Schedule I bank to preserve a clean documentary trail, avoid unnecessary FX margin, and meet CRA substantiation requirements. Legal constraints include limitations on operating a foreign-currency float without an MSB registration and the need to maintain each foreign affiliate as a reporting entity under T1134/T1135 filings where applicable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity from a foreign parent or non-resident investor should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 denominated in CAD or USD to the BC corporation's operational account at a Schedule I bank and settled through Lynx, Canada's high-value payment system operated by Payments Canada. Use a precise wire reference and narrative, e.g., Field 72: '/REC/ EQUITY CONTRIBUTION - SHARE SUBSCRIPTION', and match the wire to the corporate share register. A non-Canadian investor establishing a new Canadian business must file an Investment Canada Act new-business notification within 30 days when above the threshold, and Statistics Canada may require reporting of cross-border fund transfers and direct-investment positions through its international transactions surveys. Equity injection is preferred over shareholder debt to avoid thin-capitalization limitations under subsection 18(4) of the Income Tax Act and withholding-tax leakage on interest. Third-party digital FX corridors should not be used for equity injections; only regulated Schedule I bank channels should execute the capital wire to preserve the documentary audit chain.Outbound settlements should use SWIFT MT103 messages executed through a Schedule I bank over Lynx for high-value CAD/USD vendor payments, and MT101/MT103 sequences for cross-border royalty or licensing disbursements with purpose narrative 'Software Royalty / License Fee'. Commercial import and export letters of credit are issued via SWIFT MT700/MT707 through CIBC, Scotiabank, BMO, or RBC, with CAD clearing for domestic batch items through the Automated Clearing Settlement Service (ACSS) and instant retail credits through Canada's Real-Time Rail (launched 2025) where speed is required. App-store royalty remittances to Apple (Ireland) and Google (Singapore) should be executed as MT103 transactions supported by the platform agreements and a transfer-pricing policy ensuring that no Canadian withholding tax obligation is triggered on outbound software-license royalties beyond treaty-applicable rates, and that appropriate T1134/T106 disclosures are maintained.Deliverable CAD/USD FX Forwards — standard tenors up to 12 months executed under ISDA/CEMA documentation with Schedule I dealing banks; non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally unavailable and unnecessary for CAD because of full onshore convertibility and a liquid deliverable market.FX Swaps — spot-forward and swap combinations for managing near-term US-dollar liquidity from app-store payouts and vendor concentrations.Cross-Currency Swaps (CAD vs USD) — including cross-currency interest rate swaps for medium-term balance-sheet hedging of foreign-parent intercompany balances.Vanilla European-Style CAD/USD FX Options — purchased call or put options from Schedule I banks to cap conversion cost without mandatory execution, subject to board-approved derivatives policy.
