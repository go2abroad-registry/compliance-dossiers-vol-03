Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada), federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; portal: CRA 'My Business Account' and 'Represent a Client'. British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax is separately administered by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a Canadian tax identity by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Tax Accounts) with the Canada Revenue Agency, or by filing a T2 corporation income tax return, to obtain a Business Number (BN). The corporation should activate program account 'RM' for GST/HST and, if employing staff or having a Canadian establishment, a payroll (RP) program account. For British Columbia PST, the corporation registers separately through the BC eTaxBC portal once it carries on business and makes taxable sales in BC. Non-resident corporations may voluntarily register for GST/HST even below the small-supplier threshold; no special pre-approval is required unless the firm is a non-resident carrying on business and subject to extra-provincial corporate registration under the BC Business Corporations Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: a BC-incorporated or Canadian-resident business corporation is a separate taxpayer taxed at the federal and provincial levels on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation is taxable on Canadian-source business income, capital gains on Canadian property, and on Part XIII non-resident withholding items. The BC corporation is not a pass-through entity, and income tax classification is not elective.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; legal personality determines classification. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) GST/HST closely related corporation election under Excise Tax Act s.273 to simplify intercorporate taxable supplies; (ii) non-resident corporation election under Income Tax Act s.216 to be taxed on net Canadian rental or timber rights income in lieu of gross-basis Part XIII withholding; (iii) GST/HST small-supplier optional registration; and (iv) for financial and dual-resident scenarios, Canada applies hybrid mismatch denial rules and the general anti-avoidance rule on a non-elective basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A person is a GST/HST small supplier if its taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies and generally worldwide revenues, do not exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter and in any of the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory on the transaction that exceeds the threshold. BC PST registration does not use a general small-supplier threshold and applies once taxable sales are made in British Columbia and registration is required by the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act and regulations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of a service to a non-resident person is zero-rated if the service is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada and is received for use outside Canada; helpdesk, SaaS, and cloud bot services sold to non-resident enterprise customers therefore qualify as zero-rated supplies. Goods are zero-rated when exported from Canada as commercial goods under the Customs Act, including actual export within the prescribed period and the supplier retains evidence of export. The supplier must be a GST/HST registrant, reflect zero-rated sales in its normal GST/HST net-filing calculation, and any resulting refund of input tax credits is claimed through the regular GST/HST return.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or approved EDI electronic export report) for goods; for services, a contract/service delivery record evidencing non-resident recipient and place of performance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the non-resident consignee, Canadian port/airport of export, and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation showing the non-resident client's payment was received from outside Canada within the 180-day evidence window

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital-stamping system. CRA does not require approval of invoice format or API pre-clearance; GST/HST registrants may issue electronic invoices, but each invoice displaying GST/HST must show the supplier's Business Number, invoice date, recipient name, taxable amount, applicable rate, and tax charged or state that the supply is zero-rated. Invoices and supporting records must be kept in an accessible, legible form in Canada for at least six years and provided to CRA on demand; BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC using prescribed periodic returns.

CRA gives cross-border digital services heightened audit scrutiny; the registrant bears the burden of proving export zero-rating with contemporaneous non-resident status and consumption evidence. If the documentation fails, CRA can claw back the zero rating, reassess GST/HST at 5% on the consideration, disallow or increase related input tax credits, impose arrears interest, and apply civil penalties for false statements and omissions under ETA ss.280-285.1, including a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the misreporting amount in egregious cases. Criminal sanctions under ETA s.327 for knowing evasion can include fines up to 250% of evaded tax and imprisonment. The normal four-year reassessment period is extended without limit where misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default has occurred.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an Article VIII member of the IMF with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The CAD floats freely with no peg, band, or convertibility licensing. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital distributions to non-resident shareholders require no exchange-control approval. The only constraints are (i) Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents (25% statutory rate, potentially reduced to 15% or 5% under applicable tax treaties), (ii) NR4 reporting obligations to the Canada Revenue Agency, and (iii) FINTRAC reporting triggers for cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. A British Columbia helpdesk SaaS corporation may open CAD, USD, and other foreign-currency accounts at Schedule I banks, issue multi-currency invoices, retain foreign receipts, and convert at market rates without prior authorization. Software-as-a-service export revenue is zero-rated for GST/HST purposes, reinforcing unfettered cross-border billing in USD or CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, mandatory repatriation, or forced conversion requirement. SaaS subscription fees and other cross-border receipts may be held indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts, converted at the corporation's discretion, or hedged without restriction. The effective surrender ratio is 0% and free disposition is 100%. FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports apply to cross-border remittances at or above CAD 10,000, but these are informational filing obligations on the remitting/receiving financial institutions, not conversion mandates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the sole central bank and issuer of the Canadian dollar (CAD); the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially regulates all federally incorporated commercial banks; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; the Minister of Finance retains residual authority under the Currency Act. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency because the currency is fully convertible and independently floated.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: MEDIUM — viable only as an ancillary clearing rail, not as the primary banking counterparty. Foreign digital EMI/neobanks (e.g., US-based Mercury, Wise Platform, Payoneer) are not licensed banks under the Bank Act and cannot accept Canadian deposits in the manner of a Schedule I bank. They may operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or as payment service providers subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime administered by the Bank of Canada. For the BC helpdesk SaaS entity, such EMIs are acceptable for low-volume USD/EUR vendor disbursements and payroll contractors, but the primary operating account, capital escrow, and FX hedging relationships must sit with a Canadian Schedule I bank to satisfy bank-grade treasury, escrow, and OSFI lender expectations. Recommended structure: Schedule I bank as principal treasury counterparty; one FINTRAC-registered EMI as a segregated low-value disbursement channel reconciled daily.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 from the remitting shareholder's bank to the beneficiary account of the BC corporation at the chosen Schedule I Canadian bank using standard settlement instructions (SSI). Field 50K must identify the remitting shareholder; Field 57A/56A must contain the Canadian bank's BIC and intermediary chain; Field 59 must show the corporate account name and number; Field 20 must carry a unique reference, e.g., 'CA__CA_BC__CA_CORP_SS1'; Field 70 (Remittance Information) should state 'Direct investment in Canadian business corporation' or 'Shareholder loan — intercompany funding' as applicable; Field 33B must specify CAD or USD amount. No exchange-control permit is required. The receiving bank will perform source-of-funds verification, typically within 1–3 business days for amounts at or above CAD 10,000 and with full enhanced due diligence above CAD 25,000. FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting applies at the CAD 10,000 threshold. Capital injections should land in the Capital Escrow Account and move to the Operational Account only upon a board resolution and proper accounting entry to contributed surplus or paid-up capital.Primary clearing rails: (1) Canadian domestic ACH/ISO 20022 Real-Time Rail for CAD vendor payments under CAD 1 million; (2) SWIFT MT103 for USD, EUR, or GBP vendor settlements executed through the bank's FX desk; (3) commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued under UCP600 by the Schedule I bank's trade finance desk for hardware, software licensing, or infrastructure procurement where the vendor requires bank payment risk mitigation — settlement via SWIFT MT700/MT710 with MT202 reimbursement; (4) Stripe Billing and PayPal payout rails for SaaS subscription refunds, partner disbursements, and marketplace distributions, operating as pre-funded channels reconciled on a T+1 basis. All cross-border outflows at or above CAD 10,000 are reported to FINTRAC under the EFT regime. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: MEDIUM — viable only as an ancillary clearing rail, not as the primary banking counterparty. Foreign digital EMI/neobanks (e.g., US-based Mercury, Wise Platform, Payoneer) are not licensed banks under the Bank Act and cannot accept Canadian deposits in the manner of a Schedule I bank. They may operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or as payment service providers subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime administered by the Bank of Canada. For the BC helpdesk SaaS entity, such EMIs are acceptable for low-volume USD/EUR vendor disbursements and payroll contractors, but the primary operating account, capital escrow, and FX hedging relationships must sit with a Canadian Schedule I bank to satisfy bank-grade treasury, escrow, and OSFI lender expectations. Recommended structure: Schedule I bank as principal treasury counterparty; one FINTRAC-registered EMI as a segregated low-value disbursement channel reconciled daily.
