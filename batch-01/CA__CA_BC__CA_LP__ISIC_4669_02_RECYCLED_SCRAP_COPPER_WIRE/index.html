Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration; e-services portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial sales tax (PST) is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Provincial Tax Division, via the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned BC Limited Partnership: (1) complete BC registration or extra-provincial LP registration with the BC Registrar of Companies; (2) apply to the CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1FF (for non-residents) or Form RC1, requesting a GST/HST account (RT suffix) and, if needed, an Import/Export (RM) account; (3) register for BC PST with the BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC) if collecting PST on taxable BC-consumed goods or maintaining BC nexus; (4) provide supporting documents including partnership agreement, certificate of LP registration, identity/residency details of all general and limited partners, and the foreign corporate registration number; (5) open a CRA My Business Account for filing and compliance administration.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Canadian income tax law, the BC LP is fiscally transparent (Income Tax Act s.96): income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners for taxation in their home jurisdictions. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is treated as a distinct 'person' capable of registering and collecting GST/HST. For BC PST, the LP is the vendor when making taxable sales of goods consumed in British Columbia; non-resident partners do not, by virtue of the LP alone, create Canadian permanent establishment exposure unless the partnership carries on business through a fixed place of business in Canada. — For a foreign-owned BC Limited Partnership: (1) complete BC registration or extra-provincial LP registration with the BC Registrar of Companies; (2) apply to the CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1FF (for non-residents) or Form RC1, requesting a GST/HST account (RT suffix) and, if needed, an Import/Export (RM) account; (3) register for BC PST with the BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC) if collecting PST on taxable BC-consumed goods or maintaining BC nexus; (4) provide supporting documents including partnership agreement, certificate of LP registration, identity/residency details of all general and limited partners, and the foreign corporate registration number; (5) open a CRA My Business Account for filing and compliance administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Canadian income tax law, the BC LP is fiscally transparent (Income Tax Act s.96): income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners for taxation in their home jurisdictions. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is treated as a distinct 'person' capable of registering and collecting GST/HST. For BC PST, the LP is the vendor when making taxable sales of goods consumed in British Columbia; non-resident partners do not, by virtue of the LP alone, create Canadian permanent establishment exposure unless the partnership carries on business through a fixed place of business in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada does not use the US check-the-box regime. Statutory election and designation mechanisms include: (i) ITA s.102 election for a 'Canadian partnership' to compute income at the partnership level; (ii) ITA s.103 allocation election among partners for reasonable income/loss allocation; (iii) Excise Tax Act s.273 joint venture election permitting one member to be the GST/HST registrant for the joint venture; and (iv) anti-hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss.18.2 and 18.4 requiring disclosure on Form RC4655 where cross-border hybrid instruments or pay-through entities are used to create double-non-taxation or deduction/non-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST small-supplier threshold measured by the total reportable worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies for this purpose in certain cases) in the preceding four calendar quarters and the current quarter under ETA s.148. A non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada must register unless it qualifies as a small supplier. BC PST: registration is generally required when the LP sells taxable tangible personal property for BC consumption through a BC place of business or solicits sales in BC; there is no statutory minimum turnover threshold, and the BC Ministry of Finance may require a security deposit from out-of-province vendors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies under Excise Tax Act s.23 only where the supplier (or the supplier's agent) exports the goods as or on behalf of the supplier, delivers them to a common carrier for export in the ordinary course of business, or completes delivery outside Canada within a reasonable time. For recycled scrap copper wire, the physical goods must leave Canada to a foreign destination and be supported by contemporaneous outward shipping documentation; the supplier must also hold evidence that it transferred risk and title to the foreign buyer. Services performed for non-residents may be zero-rated if performed outside Canada or relate to tangible personal property situated outside Canada. BC PST is generally not exigible on goods delivered/shipped out of BC by the vendor for direct export, subject to the vendor's status and documentary proof.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA Form CUS-30/B3 or CARM electronic export declaration (outward cargo report) confirming the physical departure of recycled copper scrap from Canada, including any CBSA-approved outward manifest and permit numbers
  • Bill of Lading — original or electronic ocean/air waybill (e.g., master bill of lading, house bill of lading, or house air waybill) naming the foreign consignee and the destination port/customs territory outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank/financial institution credit advice (SWIFT MT103 or TT advice) evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the overseas buyer into the LP's bank account within the prescribed repatriation window

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, mandatory XML e-invoicing, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. GST/HST compliance is return-based: registrants must file returns and remit net tax through CRA-approved electronic channels (GST/HST NETFILE, CRA My Business Account, or financial institution) by the end of the month following the reporting period. Taxpayers must retain all books and records, including electronic invoices, generally for six years. For exports, the CBSA requires an electronic commercial export declaration (CBSA Form CUS-30/B3 or CARM-compliant export reporting) and outward cargo confirmation before goods leave Canada.

CRA and CBSA audit export claims strictly; failure to produce the mandatory document bundle will deny the 0% rate and trigger reassessment of GST/HST on gross sales with retroactive clawback for up to six years (or longer for misrepresentation), compounded daily interest at CRA prescribed rates, and civil penalties for false statements under ETA s.285 (greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the misstated amount). Intentional evasion subjects the LP and its partners to penalties of 50%–200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment under ETA s.327, as well as Customs Act penalties for failure to report commercial exports. Partners may incur joint and several liability if the LP fails to remit collected or assessed GST/HST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully liberalized current and capital account convertibility. No exchange control restrictions exist under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, or the Investment Canada Act. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to tax compliance only: Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees paid to non-residents (statutory 25%, reduced by treaty, e.g., 15% dividends / 0-15% interest under the Canada-US Treaty), plus Part I tax on Canadian-source business income earned by non-resident partners of the limited partnership. Capital contributions, loan repayments, and operating distributions flow freely across the border. Foreign currency accounts (USD, EUR, CNY) are routinely offered by Canadian banks with no conversion mandate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law does not require surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Receipts from exports of recycled copper scrap may be held indefinitely in foreign currency offshore or in Canadian foreign currency bank accounts. The only reporting obligations are AML-related: FINTRAC Electronic Fund Transfer Reports for transactions of CAD $10,000 or more, and mandatory record-keeping of the trade transaction chain (invoices, bills of lading, weighbridge certificates).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (prudential supervision); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (AML/CFT supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act); Global Affairs Canada (economic sanctions administration). Canada imposes no centralized foreign exchange control authority or licensing regime for FX transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners typically arrive via SWIFT MT103 from the originating foreign bank into the Capital Escrow Account at the chosen Tier 1 Canadian bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO CANADA LP [LEGAL NAME] – PARTNER [NAME]' and field 72 (Sender to Receiver Information) must reference the contribution notice under the partnership agreement. Canada does not use a statutory purpose-code regime; however, the receiving bank will apply EDD and require, for each injection above CAD $100,000: (i) an executed capital contribution resolution; (ii) a source-of-wealth package (bank statements of the contributing partner, business registration, tax reference); (iii) partnership agreement provisions authorizing the capital call; and (iv) confirmation that no FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Report or EFT Report threshold breach occurs without proper filing. The general partner must ensure the commercial invoice and underlying trade documentation align with the stated purpose to avoid misclassification as loan proceeds, which would attract Part XIII interest withholding and TIEWS reporting requirements.Clearing rails for physical scrap copper wire trade settlements: (i) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary LCs) governed by UCP 600, issued by the Canadian buyer's bank in favor of the supplier's bank, with documents comprising commercial invoice, full set of clean on-board bills of lading, SGS or equivalent inspection certificate, weighbridge certificate, packing list, and Canadian export permit from Environment and Climate Change Canada; (ii) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open-account settlements and post-shipment top-ups, containing exact beneficiary bank details, invoice number, and shipment reference in field 70; (iii) Cash Against Documents via remitting bank for medium-size transactions where full LC is disproportionate. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined effective headline rate: 15% federal Part I general corporate rate plus 12% British Columbia provincial general corporate rate; the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and each partner is taxed on its allocated share at the applicable partner-level rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s.212(2) on dividends paid or deemed paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; because the underlying entity is a limited partnership, partnership profit allocations and distributions to non-resident limited partners are generally not 'dividends' and are taxed instead under Part I on the partner's allocated share of Canadian business income; guaranteed payments (rent, interest, royalties, salaries-like draws) to non-resident partners are subject to the 25% Part XIII backstop; DTA reductions typically lower the dividend rate to 5% for corporate beneficial owners holding at least 10% of voting shares and to 15% otherwise

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: aggregate value of controlled transactions during the fiscal period equals or exceeds CAD $5,000,000; Master File: the multinational enterprise group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds CAD $500,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year; Country-by-Country Report: group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds CAD $750,000,000; documentation must be contemporaneous, prepared or obtained before the filing date of the relevant return, and supplied to CRA within 3 months of a written demand

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime analogous to UAE or BVI rules; however, economic substance is embedded in (i) the GAAR under ITA s.245, which denies tax benefits where a transaction is an avoidance transaction lacking a genuine commercial purpose and reasonable economic substance; (ii) the business-purpose test for partnership validity under BC partnership law and common law; (iii) treaty anti-abuse or limitation-of-benefits reasoning administered by CRA in withholding tax certificates; and (iv) the permanent establishment concept for non-resident general partners; a BC LP with no physical premises, no employees, no independent logistics capability, and a passive or single-purpose general partner will be considered a shell or conduit and its intercompany transactions may be recharacterized or reassessed

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False or Deceptive Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under ITA s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment (or up to 2 years on summary conviction); civil penalty under ITA s.163(2) of 50% of the tax sought to be avoided for false statements or omissions made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; the LP may be barred from relief claims

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who direct, assent to, or participate in the false filing can be prosecuted as parties under ITA s.239(1.1), with imprisonment up to 5 years; BC courts may pierce the general partner veil to order civil restitution, and pending criminal charges typically carry travel restrictions or bail-based travel bans
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) adjustment reallocating income at arm's-length levels; s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the adjustment where the aggregate adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD $5,000,000 and 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue and documentation was deficient; additional 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2); compounded interest accrues at CRA prescribed rates on all reassessed balances

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unremitted amounts and penalties if they participated in the mispricing design; criminal exposure under s.239(1.1) arises where the corporate general partner knowingly signed false returns; civil claims by minority limited partners for fiduciary breach and restitution are enforceable in the BC Supreme Court
