Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html, including Business Registration Online and My Business Account. For British Columbia: the BC Ministry of Finance administers the Provincial Sales Tax and Employer Health Tax; BC Corporate Registry administers corporate statute filings.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Canadian business corporation: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the BC Business Corporations Act, and if federally incorporated then register extraprovincially in British Columbia; (2) obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1; (3) activate the GST/HST account, payroll deductions account, and corporate income tax account as applicable; (4) if carrying on a taxable activity in BC, register with BC Corporate Registry and, where required, for BC PST and Employer Health Tax; (5) maintain a Canadian registered office, records of directors, an authorized representative, and a current CRA correspondence address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxable legal person under Part I of the Income Tax Act, taxed on worldwide income if resident in Canada and on Canadian-source income if non-resident. The entity is not pass-through and no US-style check-the-box election exists. British Columbia imposes provincial corporate income tax on income allocated to the province.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch election; entity classification is statutory under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act. Relevant elections and designations include: (i) the GST/HST related-corporation joint election under ETA section 156 to treat supplies between closely related registrants as not a taxable supply; (ii) the non-resident corporation election under ITA section 216 for net-basis taxation of Canadian rental or timber income; (iii) capital dividend account and eligible dividend designations for Canadian-controlled private corporations; (iv) associated-corporation designations affecting the small business deduction. Anti-hybrid and foreign affiliate dumping rules apply automatically without elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a person making taxable supplies in Canada becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant once total worldwide taxable supplies in any preceding four consecutive calendar quarters exceed C$30,000. BC PST registration is separately required for vendors selling taxable goods, software, or services in BC, without a comparable minimum threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered person may zero-rate (0% GST/HST) supplies of tangible personal property exported from Canada, as well as services and intangible property supplied to a non-resident where the supply is not for consumption or use in Canada and is not related to Canadian real property or Canadian goods. Financial services, including many investment and family-office management services, are generally exempt rather than zero-rated and must be characterized with care. The exporter must retain evidence that the recipient is non-resident and, for services, that the benefit is not realized in Canada.

  • Canada Export Declaration transmitted through CERS (Canadian Export Reporting System), including any required export permits
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods or services, quantity, value, and contracting parties
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or other transport document proving physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice, or equivalent financial evidence of receipt of the export consideration

No national real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-authorization, or digital stamping regime exists for invoices in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records; registrants must preserve prescribed invoice and record data in a readable format and e-file GST/HST returns when required. Export declarations must be submitted electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) to the Canada Border Services Agency.

CRA may deny zero-rating, reassess within the normal four-year period, extended to ten years in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect, recover uncollected GST/HST plus statutory interest, and impose civil penalties for false statements or gross negligence. Criminal tax evasion or materially false export claims attract fines and imprisonment under the Excise Tax Act, Income Tax Act, and Criminal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains de jure and de facto full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital account. The Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and customary international law impose no exchange-rate approval, surrender, or repatriation licensing for family office capital. Profit remittance from a British Columbia business corporation is achieved via board-declared dividends; the only meaningful friction is Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (normally 25% absent a treaty, subject to reduced rates under the Canada-US treaty or other applicable tax treaties) and the obligation to remit withholding promptly to the Canada Revenue Agency. FINTRAC cross-border reporting thresholds apply to cash and international electronic funds transfers, but these are reporting obligations, not convertibility restrictions. No hurdles exist under the provincial British Columbia Business Corporations Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender period, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline for Canadian export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency. For regulatory completeness, incoming export payments of CAD 10,000 or more must be discloseable to FINTRAC as international electronic funds transfers by the Canadian financial institution, and customs export documentation must match Canadian trade declarations; however, no proceeds surrender to the Bank of Canada or any regulatory authority is required. Commercial banking counterparties generally require standard supporting invoices and bills of lading for trade settlement but do not impose currency conversion surrenders.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) combined with the federal prudential supervisor, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no central-bank foreign exchange control department; currency policy is free-market oriented and the Bank of Canada does not license or approve individual cross-border treasury transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for a BC single-family office using offshore digital EMIs as a primary treasury rail is Low. Canadian banking law requires a corporation conducting core banking relationships through a regulated Canadian bank or an authorized foreign bank branch under the Bank Act (Canada) and OSFI licensing. Pure EMIs are not members of Payments Canada and cannot offer CDIC-insured deposits. An offshore EMI may be used only as a supplementary, low-value disbursement channel if it is registered as a money services business (MSB) in Canada with FINTRAC, maintains adequate sanctions screening, and avoids the phrase 'bank account' in favor of 'e-money account.' For capital escrow, custody, and cross-currency clearing, a Schedule I Canadian bank or licensed Schedule II authorized foreign bank branch remains mandatory. Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary operating expenses and small disbursements; Low for primary treasury concentration.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial owner classified as or associated with a politically exposed person (PEP) in any jurisdiction.

Root cause: Beneficial owner classified as or associated with a politically exposed person (PEP) in any jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Source of wealth report covering 24 months of independent bank and custody statements; provenance of initial capital from legal establishment of the family office; certified tax returns from residence jurisdiction(s); employment or business-sale contracts; official PEP-assessment questionnaire; an explanation of legitimate government-networked business history; and a written private-wealth professional opinion confirming the absence of unexplained wealth.
Thin capitalization or inconsistent operating purpose of the single-family office investment holding company.

Root cause: Thin capitalization or inconsistent operating purpose of the single-family office investment holding company.

Defense evidence: Board-approved business plan and investment mandate showing family-asset management, not unilateral trade finance; consolidated portfolio list with ISINs and custody evidence; audited or accountant-reviewed financial statements; the intercompany or investment advisory agreement; source of funds traces from grantor/settlor accounts; proof of registered office and substance in British Columbia; and transaction-flow predictions covering projected valuations, lending, and shareholder distributions.
High-risk jurisdictions linked to settlor, trust situs, or underlying portfolio counterparties, including sanctions-listed or FATF-monitored states.

Root cause: High-risk jurisdictions linked to settlor, trust situs, or underlying portfolio counterparties, including sanctions-listed or FATF-monitored states.

Defense evidence: Detailed economic purpose memorandum explaining the family office's presence in Canada; clean global sanctions and EFTA/FATF screening certificates; legal opinions from the jurisdiction of the trust and the jurisdiction of the settlor; assignment of equitable title documentation for trust assets; and confirmation from professional intermediaries that the business activities do not touch prohibited sectors such as nuclear proliferation, arms, or sanctioned commodity trade.
Inbound capital injection from an offshore trust or foreign limited partnership without a visible nexus to Canadian operations.

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from an offshore trust or foreign limited partnership without a visible nexus to Canadian operations.

Defense evidence: Final or certified trust deed and schedule of beneficiaries; trust protector approval; chain-of-funds ledger from the initial gift or family business sale to the Canadian investment holding account; share certificates issued in exchange for the contribution; a board resolution for issuance at par or stated capital; register of share transfers; tax-domicile certificates; and a response memorandum demonstrating that no unreported foreign source income is being laundered through the BC corporation.
High-frequency cross-border wires or structured transfers inconsistent with the family office investment mandate, triggering FINTRAC large value and management-flagged reporting.

Root cause: High-frequency cross-border wires or structured transfers inconsistent with the family office investment mandate, triggering FINTRAC large value and management-flagged reporting.

Defense evidence: A treasury and investment policy statement approved by the board, limiting counterparties and instruments to low-leverage private/public equities, fixed income, and permitted hedging; monthly reconciliation of SWIFT messages to custodian confirms and trade tickets; an organizational chart separating the treasury officer from beneficial ownership; an external audit trail of the clearing route; and escalation protocols to ensure all MT103/MF202 transfers are documented with written investment or flow-of-funds contracts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a compliant inbound capital injection into the British Columbia single-family office, the preferred rail is SWIFT MT103 from the settlor/beneficial owner's high-street bank to the Canadian bank account using the corporate SWIFT BIC, Canadian bank transit number and account number. Payment messages should state 'Issued Share Capital Contribution' or 'Equity Subscription' in SWIFT field 70 where permitted, accompanied by the board authorization and share allotment. The corresponding SWIFT purpose tag should align with Canadian balance-of-payments accounting as a direct investment equity transaction rather than trade-in-goods. The Canadian receiving bank applies OSFI-mandated sanctions screening and FINTRAC reporting when the transaction is CAD 10,000 or greater. Verification packet: notarized share subscription agreement, corporate resolution, statement of compliance with the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia), register of directors and beneficial owners, source-of-funds documentation, and a letter from the remitter's bank confirming the remitter's identity. To avoid an unclean credit, remitters must remit from an account bearing the name of the subscribing entity or the disclosed beneficial owner.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and treasury transfers are executed through SWIFT MT103 customer transfers, SWIFT MT202 cover payments for bank-to-bank funds positioning, and, where letters of credit are used, commercial or standby letters of credit issued under UCP600 and curated by a licensed Canadian bank's trade finance department. Domestic CAD clearing is provided by Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or, for smaller payments, the retail Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); United States dollar settlements clear through the Federal Reserve's Fedwire funds service or CHIPS. For family-office outward investment purchases, use institutional wire guidance with settlement instructions from the broker/custodian, including DTC settlement codes and ISIN references.
