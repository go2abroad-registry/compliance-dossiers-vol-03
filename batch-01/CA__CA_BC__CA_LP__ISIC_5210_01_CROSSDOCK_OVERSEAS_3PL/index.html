Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal administration of GST/HST and income tax; CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO). BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is separately administered by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch via the eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign LP must obtain a Business Number (BN) and register for GST/HST once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available below the threshold to recover input tax credits. Step 2: Submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Accounts) online via BRO or by mail/fax to the CRA International and Non-Resident Tax Services Office, appending the partnership registration certificate, partnership agreement, and a Canadian representative with authority; no minimum capital is required for an LP. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax: income and losses flow to partners pro rata with no entity-level tax; non-resident limited partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their allocable share of Canadian-source business income (treaty-relievable), while Part XIII withholding applies to passive-type payments (interest, rents, management fees) absent treaty relief. For GST/HST and PST the LP is a separate person/registrant in its own right and must self-assess and collect taxes independent of its partners.

Available election forms

Canada has no general check-the-box election, but the following statutory mechanisms are available: (i) GST/HST joint election under s. 156 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) allowing closely related registrants to supply taxable services/goods to each other for nil consideration; (ii) closely related corporation/partnership GST election under ETA s. 273; (iii) proposed Budget 2024 elective regime (draft legislation released Aug 2024 and revised Feb 2025, not yet in force) that would allow a qualifying Canadian partnership to elect deemed-corporation status for income tax, modelled on the US check-the-box rules; (iv) election under s. 216 of the Income Tax Act for a non-resident partner/LP to file a Canadian return and pay net-basis tax on Canadian rental/income property instead of gross-basis Part XIII withholding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in one calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters (mandatory); voluntary registration permitted below the threshold to claim ITCs. BC PST: mandatory registration when annual taxable retail sales in BC exceed CAD 10,000; 3PL warehouse storage and cross-dock handling charges are generally PST-exempt services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating under s. 1, Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) applies where a registrant supplies tangible personal property delivered to a carrier or consignee for direct export and the goods are actually exported from Canada within 60 days of the supply without intermediate use in Canada (CRA may extend the period on request). Services: zero-rating under ss. 6-9, Part V, Schedule VI applies to services supplied to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST, provided the service is not performed to an individual present in Canada and does not relate to real property in Canada; however, a service relating to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time performed (e.g., cross-dock storage, order-picking, and handling of e-commerce inventory inside the BC warehouse) does not qualify for zero-rating under the s. 9 carve-out and remains taxable at 5% even when billed to a non-resident merchant; 0% treatment on 3PL service lines can be sustained only where the services are identified to an immediate export transaction or performed in respect of goods not situated in Canada (title/export-obligation mapping is required). Exported goods sold by the LP to foreign buyers are creditable through ITCs on the GST inputs of the Canadian operation.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA ACI/eManifest electronic export report for consignments valued above CAD 2,500 (below the de minimis, retain commercial invoice, carrier manifest and proof of exit instead)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air/truck) or comparable transport document naming the Canadian exporter of record, the foreign consignee, and the destination port with carrier proof of delivery/exit
  • Inward remittance and payment evidence: SWIFT MT103, Airwallex/wire debit confirmation, or bank credit advice — Canada issues no statutory Inward Remittance Certificate, but the CRA requires documentary proof of consideration flowing from the non-resident buyer to substantiate the export transaction

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, mandatory e-invoicing, or digital duty-stamp regime exists in Canada at the federal or BC provincial level. CRA accepts electronic invoices, EDI, and scanned records provided they meet the books-and-records requirements of ETA s. 286 and are retained for six years (CRA GST/HST Memorandum 15-4). Canada is voluntarily adopting the Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing framework (Digital Governance Council) but participation is not legally mandated; the only electronic transmission mandate relevant to exports is the CBSA ACI/eManifest pre-departure export declaration filing, which is customs clearance, not fiscal invoice clearance.

If zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA reassesses the supply as taxable at 5% GST with compounded daily interest from the due date; normal reassessment is 4 years, extended to 7 years for misrepresentation or neglect and unlimited for fraud. Penalties under ETA ss. 280 and 280.1: 25% of the tax sought where a false statement or omission is made, plus CDN 250 for a false certificate; fraudulent evasion under ETA s. 327/327.1 attracts a penalty of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be avoided, fines up to CDN 200,000, and imprisonment up to two years, with directors and general partners jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST (ETA s. 323). For BC PST, failure to register or remit triggers a 10% penalty on the unpaid tax plus arrears interest under the Provincial Sales Tax Act, with director/partner liability under PSTA s. 78. Note: Canadian law sets no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier is an administrative evidentiary cycle for completing export documentation, while the legal retention period for export records is six years under ETA s. 286.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency with no exchange-control legislation. Both current-account (trade in goods and services) and capital-account (foreign investment, loan repayments, profit repatriation) flows are fully convertible without prior ministerial or central-bank approval, and there are no quotas, surrender requirements, or currency-pair restrictions on CAD. Remittance constraints arise only from tax and AML rules: (i) as a pass-through entity under the Income Tax Act (Canada), the BC Limited Partnership allocates Canadian-source business income to its non-resident Limited Partners, who are taxed under Part I on a net basis where the LP carries on business through a Canadian permanent establishment (the BC cross-dock warehouse constitutes a PE); distributions of previously taxed business income are not subject to Part XIII withholding, but separate payments to non-resident partners (management or administration fees, interest on partner loans, royalties) attract Part XIII withholding tax of 25%, reduced by applicable tax treaties; and (ii) every international transfer at or above CAD 10,000 in a 24-hour window is reportable by the financial institution to FINTRAC as an electronic funds transfer report (EFTR).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. The LP may retain foreign-currency receipts (USD, EUR, CNH) indefinitely, onshore or offshore, and may settle overseas warehouse and freight obligations directly in the billing currency. The only compliance duties are: (i) FINTRAC EFTR filings for all international wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more aggregated within 24 hours; (ii) sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, and the Freezing Assets of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act; and (iii) standard corporate reporting of FX gains/losses and foreign-source revenue in the partnership's financial statements (ASPE/IFRS) and the annual CRA partnership information return.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising foreign-exchange market operations and Exchange Fund Account management as fiscal agent for the Minister of Finance; banking prudential supervision: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); financial-transaction reporting and cross-border wire surveillance: Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium - permissible only as a secondary payment rail, not as a substitute for a Tier-1 bank account. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Platform, and comparable multi-currency account providers) cannot take retail or corporate deposits in Canada without a federal banking license under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46), and their Canadian activity is conditional on: (i) sponsorship or settlement through a federally regulated Schedule I bank; (ii) registration with the Bank of Canada as a retail payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (transitional registration regime effective as of November 2024); and (iii) mandatory FINTRAC registration as a money services business (MSB) to the extent they execute cross-border EFTs. Legal architecture for the BC LP: maintain the primary CAD/USD operational and escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank, and use the EMI solely for merchant payouts, refunds, multi-currency collection pages, and low-value supplier disbursements, with a contractual daily-sweep zero-balance arrangement and auditable transaction-ID mapping back to the underlying e-commerce order.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from foreign Limited Partners are received into the separately designated Capital Escrow Account at the Schedule I bank via SWIFT MT103 (direct transfer) or MT202 COV (cover payment), with the payment reference field populated as 'Capital Contribution - [Partner Name] - [CA BC LP Legal Name]' and the partnership's domestic account identifiers (transit and institution number for CAD, or the bank's SWIFT BIC for cross-border instruction). The compliant routing protocol requires: (i) a notarized capital call notice issued by the General Partner specifying currency, amount, and the subscribed capital percentage; (ii) a source-of-funds statement from each contributing partner identifying the originating bank, account-holder identity, and economic purpose; (iii) an FX conversion instruction with a binding quote where the contribution arrives in USD, EUR, or CNH and must be booked in CAD; and (iv) automatic FINTRAC EFTR filing by the receiving bank for any transaction at or above CAD 10,000. No Bank of Canada or FINTRAC pre-approval is required, and no conversion ratio applies. The credited amount is recorded against each partner's capital account in the LP's books under ASPE or IFRS and reconciled to the partnership information return.The clearing rails for trade settlements are: (i) SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers for direct supplier payments, with the underlying sales order or purchase order and invoice number quoted in field 70; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 rules, transmitted as SWIFT MT700, with documents (bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin) negotiated against the paying Canadian bank; (iii) Canadian domestic ACH through Payments Canada's Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for CAD-denominated payments to domestic freight, warehouse, and customs-broker counterparties; (iv) USD-denominated payables cleared through the bank's U.S. correspondent network (e.g., RBC's U.S. clearing arm or TD Bank, N.A.) to U.S. suppliers without FX conversion; and (v) low-value cross-border merchant payouts and refunds executed as API-driven payments through the licensed EMI aggregator, each carrying a merchant transaction-ID audit string that maps to the source order. All outbound international wires at or above CAD 10,000 per 24-hour period are reported by the bank to FINTRAC, and the partnership must retain the invoice-voucher-cash match for CRA examination.Outright deliverable FX forwards (typically 1-24 month tenors) for CAD/USD and CAD/CNH conversion hedging on trade receivables and supplier payables, documented under an ISDA 1998 FX Definitions master confirmation with the Canadian banking counterparty.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-dated USD/CAD treasury exposure arising from the overseas warehouse lease obligations, governed by an ISDA Master Agreement with a credit support annex.Vanilla FX options and zero-cost collar structures (purchased options only) to protect minimum revenue conversion rates on USD-denominated 3PL receivables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to currency pairs without an accessible deliverable forward market, including CAD/CNY reference NDFs settled against the offshore CNH fixing where physical CNY delivery is unavailable.Spot outright FX transactions executed strictly for commercial conversion of operating receipts and disbursements, with no speculative or uncovered margin trading permitted under the LP's constitutive documents.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium - permissible only as a secondary payment rail, not as a substitute for a Tier-1 bank account. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Platform, and comparable multi-currency account providers) cannot take retail or corporate deposits in Canada without a federal banking license under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46), and their Canadian activity is conditional on: (i) sponsorship or settlement through a federally regulated Schedule I bank; (ii) registration with the Bank of Canada as a retail payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (transitional registration regime effective as of November 2024); and (iii) mandatory FINTRAC registration as a money services business (MSB) to the extent they execute cross-border EFTs. Legal architecture for the BC LP: maintain the primary CAD/USD operational and escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank, and use the EMI solely for merchant payouts, refunds, multi-currency collection pages, and low-value supplier disbursements, with a contractual daily-sweep zero-balance arrangement and auditable transaction-ID mapping back to the underlying e-commerce order.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (15% federal + 12% British Columbia) is the reference combined corporate rate. The CA Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96(1) and is not a taxpayer; each partner's allocable share is taxed in the partner's hands. Individual BC resident partners face graduated marginal rates up to 53.5%; non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on taxable Canadian earnings from carrying on business in Canada (ITA s.115(1)(a)).
