Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia), then obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1. Program accounts are attached to the BN: RT (corporate income tax), RP (GST/HST), RC (payroll). Non-resident corporations without a Canadian incorporation must file Form RC1425 for Part I tax. As of January 2025, CRA mandatory registration rules apply to certain non-resident suppliers, including digital platform operators deemed registrants.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - Canadian tax-resident business corporations are taxed at the entity level (federal general corporate rate 15%, small business rate 9%; BC provincial corporate rate 12% general, 2% small business rate subject to BC budget adjustments). No pass-through regime; worldwide income is subject to Canadian corporate income tax with foreign tax credit relief; BC imposes an additional provincial corporate income tax on allocated taxable income. — Foreign entities incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia), then obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - Canadian tax-resident business corporations are taxed at the entity level (federal general corporate rate 15%, small business rate 9%; BC provincial corporate rate 12% general, 2% small business rate subject to BC budget adjustments). No pass-through regime; worldwide income is subject to Canadian corporate income tax with foreign tax credit relief; BC imposes an additional provincial corporate income tax on allocated taxable income.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada. Available elections include: subsection 85(1) rollover for qualifying property transfers; section 216 election for non-residents to compute Part I tax on net Canadian rental income; section 219 non-resident extra-provincial election; GST/HST section 267 election for closely related corporations; hybrid mismatch rules under Part VI.1 of the Income Tax Act (in force for 2022+) limiting deduction mismatches; Regulation 4301(6) and (7) foreign entity classification elections for tax treaty purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold calculated on total worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters under ETA s. 148; mandatory registration regardless of threshold for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Canada under the post-2025 digital economy registration rules

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI: s. 20 and 22 for goods exported where the exporter retains documentary proof of export; s. 21 for services supplied to a non-resident (excluding services performed in Canada or services relating to real/immovable property in Canada); s. 23 for professional, management, or administrative services supplied to a non-resident provided the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and the services do not relate to a permanent establishment in Canada; ESG carbon auditing and offset brokerage services qualify under s. 23 where the recipient is non-resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA Form B13A or CAED (Canadian Automated Export Declaration) for goods valued over CAD 2,000 or controlled items
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated GST/HST invoice referencing ETA Schedule VI provision
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC), SWIFT MT103, or bank statement proving foreign currency receipt into a Canadian financial institution
  • Non-resident status evidence: certificate of residence, foreign tax registration confirmation, or non-resident GST/HST registration record

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API clearance exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1 million must file electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol-based) is voluntary as of 2024-2025; CRA accepts EDI and API transmissions for return filing but does not mandate invoice pre-clearance.

CRA audits export substantiation under ETA s. 296; insufficient documentation triggers reassessment of uncollected GST (5%) plus interest under ETA s. 280, and denial of input tax credits. Gross negligence penalty is 25% of understated net tax under ETA s. 285.1; Income Tax Act s. 163.2 third-party penalty can reach CAD 25,000 plus 50% of claimed benefit. Willful evasion is a criminal offence under ETA s. 327.1 punishable by fine up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years. The normal 4-year reassessment window extends to 7 years for misrepresentation; fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective clawback of refunds, GST/HST rebate disqualification, and loss of the Voluntary Disclosures Program relief.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with full current-account and capital-account convertibility under a freely floating CAD; there are no central-bank conversion permits, no approval gates on capital inflows or outflows, no surrender requirements, and no profit-remittance licensing hurdles. The only material friction for outbound profit remittances is the federal Part XIII withholding tax — statutory 25% on dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees, treaty-reduced under the Canada-US Tax Convention and similar treaties where a form NR301/NR302 or composite certificate is filed. BC provincial income tax and PST do not create additional remittance barriers, and non-resident ownership of a BC business corporation is unrestricted under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export or service-export proceeds; receipts may be retained in CAD, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency indefinitely. Compliance obligations are limited to FINTRAC reporting — every electronic funds transfer or cash transaction of CAD 10,000 or more, inbound or outbound, must be disclosed by the reporting entity (bank or MSB) through a Large Cash Transaction Report or Large Virtual Currency Transaction Report where applicable — and to PCMLTFA-mandated five-year record retention supporting the audit trail of each receivable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the federal monetary authority and fiscal agent; the Bank of Canada Act governs currency and payment-system stability, though Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange-control licensing office. Prudential oversight of Schedule I banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), cross-border AML/CTF supervision is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and capital-movement policy rests with the Department of Finance Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium — Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Platform, Mercury, and Revolut Business are legally viable only as supplementary FX-conversion and disbursement channels, not as primary liquidity custodians. Under the Bank Act, a foreign corporation cannot accept deposits in Canada without an authorized Schedule III banking licence, and unlicensed EMIs must instead register as money services businesses with FINTRAC and comply with provincial consumer-protection rules administered by Consumer Protection BC under the Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act. For a BC service company, this means the main treasury position should sit at a Schedule I bank, while an offshore EMI may be used for cost-efficient CAD/USD/EUR conversions, provided counterparty onboarding, funds-holding caps, and PCMLTFA reporting obligations are mapped in the treasury policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is injected by SWIFT MT103 wire from the remitting bank into the Schedule I Canadian bank account bearing the exact registered legal entity name and commercial account number; the payment narration should cite contribution to share capital or shareholder loan. Canada imposes no statutory purpose-code classification, but the receiving bank will record a FINTRAC Large Cash/EFT Report for every transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, and commercial underwriters typically request a board resolution authorizing the capital call, the remitter's KYC profile, evidence of the funding chain, and a closing statement before crediting funds. Large-value CAD payments settle in real time over Payments Canada Lynx; USD payments clear through the Canadian bank's US correspondent network and ACH equivalents, avoiding reliance on cheque clearing.
