Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and Corporate Income Tax administration via the CRA Business Registration Online portal and My Business Account; BC Ministry of Finance (Provincial Sales Tax) administered through the BC Tax Account portal; Corporations Canada for federal incorporation.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Canadian business corporations obtain a federal/BC Business Number (BN) with CRA program accounts: RT (T2 corporate income tax), RC (payroll), and RP (GST/HST). Procedure: 1) Incorporate the entity under the Canada Business Corporations Act or BC Business Corporations Act and receive the incorporation number; 2) File T2 registration using Form RC1 or online through CRA Business Registration Online, providing Articles of Incorporation, directors' details, registered office address, and parent/residency declarations; 3) Register separately for a BC PST account through the BC Tax Account portal if PST-remittable supplies are made; 4) For non-resident vendors supplying digital services to Canadians, register under the GST/HST Non-Resident Simplified Registration framework. The GST/HST account is effective from the earlier of the registration date or the date on which the CAD 30,000 threshold was exceeded. ETA/CRT registration is generally completed within 5–10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box'; foreign entity status under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act follows continuity-of-existence and common-law tests. Available elections: ETA s. 156 election to treat closely related corporations as not making taxable supplies; quick method election for GST/HST (Form GST74); voluntary GST/HST registration by non-resident digital suppliers using the simplified non-resident framework; BC PST seller's license election where applicable; hybrid mismatch disallowance under s. 18.2 and s. 18.6 of the Income Tax Act for structured payments, with mandatory self-compliance filings. No discretionary corporate classification election is available to a BC-incorporated corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide annual taxable supplies (excluding financial services) over any single calendar quarter or the last four consecutive quarters; once exceeded by a supplier with Canadian nexus, mandatory GST/HST registration applies. There is no separate distance-sales threshold; a non-resident digital supplier must register when taxable supplies made to Canadian customers, including through app stores, exceed the specified CAD 30,000 calibre. BC PST registration is required where taxable tangible personal property or enumerated digital services are sold to BC consumers by a vendor with property or solicitation nexus, or where the vendor is a non-resident' marketplace operator deemed a vendor.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, items 1, 7 and 23, and s. 135, a supply of services to a non-resident person is generally zero-rated unless the supply is made in respect of Canadian real/immovable property or tangible personal property situated in Canada for consumption/use in Canada, or the supply is physically performed in Canada to the extent it is not exported. Voice-streaming and interactive-application subscriptions sold by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident users, and digital enablement services supplied to a non-resident platform operator, qualify as zero-rated exports provided the recipient is genuinely a non-resident and the service is not consumed in Canada. CRA place-of-supply rules for digital supplies deem the supply to be made in the jurisdiction of the recipient's ordinary residence; therefore the exporter must evidence user residence, geolocation/device data, and contractual terms indicating offshore delivery. Where Apple IAP or Google Play Billing is used, the Canadian exporter should ensure the distribution agreement identifies whether the platform is the deemed supplier to the consumer and documents the exporter's supply to the non-resident platform as a zero-rated service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / CAED) — required for tangible media exports, generally not applicable to pure digital voice-streaming services
  • Commercial invoice or tax invoice identifying the supply, stating 'zero-rated export under ETA Schedule VI Part V', and naming the non-resident recipient
  • Export service contract / platform distribution agreement with Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, including tax and payment terms
  • Inward remittance / FX repatriation evidence, such as bank credit advices, SWIFT MT103, Apple/Google settlement statements, or PayPal/payment-rail treasury reports
  • Technical delivery evidence, including CDN/server logs, IP-geolocation reports, and user-residence records demonstrating that the service is provided from Canada to users located outside Canada

Canada does not currently mandate a nationwide real-time electronic invoicing or continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance regime; there is no pre-clearance tax stamp or fiscal clearance API for digital service providers. The CRA accepts electronic invoices if the records are complete and legible, and PEPPOL e-invoicing is voluntary rather than statutory. Non-resident registrants are required to issue tax invoices containing the supplier, BN/GST registration number, date, consideration, and GST/HST charged; CRA may require electronic submission of GST34 simplified returns and supporting schedules via My Business Account. For app-store distribution models, the marketplace platform (Apple/Google) may be deemed the supplier for GST/HST collection at the point of sale, and the Canadian exporter must maintain platform settlement dashboards, billing proofs, and export-backup documentation sufficient to support zero-rating of the B2B supply to the platform operator.

CRA audits exporters' documentary evidence for zero-rated supplies, focusing on non-resident status, place-of-supply, and commercial substance of platform arrangements. Where export substantiation is deficient, the CRA may reassess the supply as taxable at 5% GST (plus PST if scope extends to BC consumers deemed not exported), deny input tax credits, and impose penalties under s. 280 of the Excise Tax Act (false statements or omissions) and s. 285.1 (gross negligence: up to 25% of the net tax amount) plus arrears interest at prescribed rates. Misrepresentations extend the normal 4-year reassessment period indefinitely. Deliberate evasion attracts criminal fines, director liability exposure, and possible prosecution under s. 327 ETA. Retrospective clawbacks of zero-rated treatment at the CA-BC provincial level may be asserted by the BC Ministry of Finance where PST-eligible digital entertainment services were sourced to BC consumers, and vendor non-resident parties may lose marketplace relief provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both capital and current account transactions. Canada maintains no foreign-exchange controls, no requirement for prior authorization on profit remittance, and no central-bank permission for cross-border flows. Dividends, royalties, and branch profits may be remitted to non-residents after corporate taxation and subject to applicable Part XIII withholding taxes. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC reporting obligations, but do not require exchange-control permission. A British Columbia voice-streaming application business may freely maintain CAD and foreign-currency treasury accounts; any non-resident parent investment must comply with the Investment Canada Act and applicable income-reporting provisions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency in chartered bank accounts and convert at market rates. However, all payments received for exported services and digital content must be supported by contracts and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. FINTRAC requires reporting of incoming or outgoing wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. For Canadian-controlled private corporations using a functional currency other than CAD, an election under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act may apply, but no mandatory conversion of receipts is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border transaction monitoring and reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are generally not authorized to carry on banking business in Canada. An offshore EMI may be used only as an ancillary payment-processing vendor and cannot be the primary treasury settlement rail. A FINTRAC-registered Canadian money services business or a Schedule I / Schedule II chartered bank is required for cross-border liquidity clearing. Therefore, using an offshore EMI as the main corporate operating account is assessed as LOW feasibility; using a regulated Canadian bank is HIGH feasibility.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for services, royalties, rents and interest with a non-arm's length non-resident; CAD 5,000,000 for tangible goods transactions. Mandatory Local File is required for Canadian corporations with annual revenue over CAD 50,000,000; Master File is required when Canadian revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000 and consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations codes akin to UAE/offshore hubs, but CRA enforces economic substance through the Canadian transfer pricing reallocation rule, the general anti-avoidance rule, and treaty-benefit denial principles. The Canadian voice-streaming entity must employ the people, own the app-related IP and control the functions, assets and risks that generate income; otherwise CRA may disregard pricing, reassess the arrangement and impose a 15% GAAR penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Understatement

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239 criminal evasion: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Civil gross negligence penalty under s.163.2 is 50% of the understated tax; CRA also charges daily late-filing and information penalties and stautory interest on all reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign a false T2 may be personally assessed for unremitted withholding taxes and source deductions; Canadian courts can pierce the corporate veil where the filing is fraudulent. Criminal proceedings may impose travel restrictions, restitution orders and director disqualification.
Transfer Pricing and Incomplete Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA can reassess to arm's length, apply a 10% penalty on the net transfer-pricing adjustment due to failure to maintain documentation, and assess arrerars interest. Offending amounts may also be recharacterized as deemed royalties/dividends subject to Part XIII withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding on deemed payments. Where transfer-pricing statements are knowingly false, directors may face criminal prosecution; courts have lifted the veil to hold directors personally liable for negligent transfer-pricing governance.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule disallows the tax benefit and imposes a 15% GAAR penalty plus interest; treaty benefits can be denied under the abusive-avoidance exception. CRA may impose 25% non-resident withholding on disguised distributions and apply transfer-pricing reallocation to the BC entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a shell are treated as principals when the shell is a facade; BC courts pierce the corporate veil, imposing restitution and disgorgement orders. Travel bans and passport holds may be ordered where the arrangement involves offshore money movement.
AML and Money Laundering Through In-App Currency

Statutory sanction: Violations of Canada's Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act expose the corporation to administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation and criminal money-laundering penalties with imprisonment up to ten years; FINTRAC can suspend the business and CRA may also assess unreported revenue.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly operate or steer an unregulated payment scheme face criminal liability, travel bans, extradition exposure and personal fines. The corporate veil is pierced for intentional money laundering; directors may also be disqualified from holding directorships in Canada.
