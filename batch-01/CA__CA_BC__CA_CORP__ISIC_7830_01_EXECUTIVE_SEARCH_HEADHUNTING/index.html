Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital services portals: My Business Account and Business Registration Online at www.canada.ca/revenue-agency. British Columbia provincial tax administration: BC Ministry of Finance (BC PST via eTaxBC portal; BC corporate income tax is administered federally by CRA under the Canada-BC Tax Collection Agreement).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities: (1) determine whether the non-resident corporation carries on business in Canada or has a permanent establishment under the Canada-BC tax framework and applicable treaty; (2) obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or CRA telephone registration; (3) activate program accounts under the same BN: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RM (customs/import-export), RC (corporate income tax); (4) register for BC PST through BC eTaxBC and obtain a BC PST number; (5) file extra-provincial registration with the BC Registrar of Companies under the Business Corporations Act before carrying on business in BC; (6) maintain a Canadian books-and-records location or accessible electronic records and appoint an authorized representative/custodian if no physical presence.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian-resident Business Corporation is taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide income at federal and BC provincial corporate rates (combined general rate 27% in BC); territorial scope for non-resident corporations: Canadian-source income only, subject to permanent establishment and transfer-pricing rules under applicable tax treaties. — Foreign entities: (1) determine whether the non-resident corporation carries on business in Canada or has a permanent establishment under the Canada-BC tax framework and applicable treaty; (2) obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or CRA telephone registration; (3) activate program accounts under the same BN: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RM (customs/import-export), RC (corporate income tax); (4) register for BC PST through BC eTaxBC and obtain a BC PST number; (5) file extra-provincial registration with the BC Registrar of Companies under the Business Corporations Act before carrying on business in BC; (6) maintain a Canadian books-and-records location or accessible electronic records and appoint an authorized representative/custodian if no physical presence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian-resident Business Corporation is taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide income at federal and BC provincial corporate rates (combined general rate 27% in BC); territorial scope for non-resident corporations: Canadian-source income only, subject to permanent establishment and transfer-pricing rules under applicable tax treaties.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box or flow-through election; the Business Corporation is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer. Statutory elections available: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration as a small supplier (Excise Tax Act subsection 240(2)); (ii) Quick Method of accounting for small GST registrants; (iii) subsection 216 Income Tax Act net-basis election for non-resident rental income; (iv) treaty-based withholding-reduction claims (not an election but a treaty claim); (v) hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act sections 18.4-18.6) are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions with no election, denying deductions for imported hybrid mismatch arrangements in cross-border fee structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory GST/HST registration applies when the corporation's worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies and supplies by associated persons, aggregated) exceed CAD $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, Excise Tax Act section 148). BC PST registration is mandatory from the first taxable retail sale or lease in BC (no monetary threshold); headhunting retainer fees invoiced to foreign clients count as taxable supplies for threshold purposes even when zero-rated, and entitlement to input tax credits on Canadian overhead begins upon registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Goods physically exported from Canada qualify for 0% GST/HST under section 6, Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act, provided prescribed evidence of export is retained. (2) Services supplied to a non-resident, including cross-border C-suite executive search and headhunting retainers, are zero-rated under section 7, Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act provided the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or rendered to a non-resident individual present in Canada; retainer fees invoiced to foreign corporate clients with delivery of candidate introduction/placement services abroad therefore qualify for 0% GST/HST. (3) The supplier must obtain and retain evidence of the non-resident status of the client and the foreign settlement of fees; input tax credits on Canadian operating costs attributable to zero-rated exports are claimable, resulting in net GST refunds payable by CRA (subject to enhanced verification for large or frequent refunds).

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Declaration / GCA customs reporting for goods)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill naming the foreign consignee and shipper of record
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or bank wire record evidencing foreign currency settlement of the export invoice
  • Commercial invoice and executed headhunting retainer/services agreement with the non-resident client
  • CBSA export report or carrier inward/outward manifest for goods shipped abroad
  • Wise B2B or correspondent-bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of funds from the non-resident customer

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing clearance regime as of the date of this dossier. GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account and issue tax invoices containing the prescribed information under Excise Tax Act section 223 (including BN, date, vendor name, consideration, tax charged). CRA supports voluntary Pan-Canadian electronic invoicing aligned with the PEPPOL standard; BC PST imposes standard sales-invoice/record requirements without digital clearance.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims strictly: if export evidence is missing or the non-resident supply is recharacterized as domestic, zero-rating is reversed, output GST is reassessed at 5% plus prescribed interest compounded daily, and refunds may be clawed back. The normal reassessment window is four years (Excise Tax Act section 298), extended without limitation where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, or fraud exists. Gross-negligence penalties apply under Excise Tax Act section 285.1 (greater of CAD $250 and 25% of the net tax understatement) and Income Tax Act section 163(2) (50% of understated income tax on related adjustments); failure-to-remit penalties under Excise Tax Act section 280 (3% of unremitted amounts, 6% on repeat failures) and criminal prosecution for fraudulent export claims (Excise Tax Act section 327; Income Tax Act section 239) with seizure of records/goods and mandatory refund offsets. BC PST non-collection or non-remission similarly triggers provincial penalties, interest, and audit assessments within BC's four-year limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally require foreign-exchange approval for the incorporation capitalization, intercompany funding, acquisition of foreign currency, payment of foreign suppliers, or repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, or other legitimate corporate proceeds. Banks must still verify the legal purpose, source of funds, beneficial ownership, sanctions status, and supporting contracts or invoices. Payments to non-residents may trigger Canadian withholding tax, generally 25% absent a treaty reduction or exemption, and treaty relief requires appropriate residency and beneficial-ownership documentation. Dividends paid by a Canadian corporation are generally subject to Part XIII withholding unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty; interest and royalties may have separate statutory and treaty outcomes. Related-party financing and service payments should be supported by arm's-length pricing, transfer-pricing documentation, and applicable information returns. A corporation carrying on business in British Columbia must also consider provincial tax, payroll, sales-tax, and non-resident payment compliance where relevant.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, convert export receipts within a prescribed period, or convert a mandatory percentage of export proceeds into Canadian dollars. Export receipts may ordinarily be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the corporation's discretion. The corporation should preserve contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery evidence, customer remittance records, and bank records. Large or unusual transactions remain subject to bank monitoring, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering reporting, and possible FINTRAC reporting. Cash or monetary instruments transported into or out of Canada with a value of CAD 10,000 or more must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency; this rule does not generally apply to ordinary electronic bank transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing capital controls. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions supervises federally regulated deposit-taking institutions, while the British Columbia Financial Services Authority supervises applicable provincially regulated financial services. FINTRAC administers anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing obligations for reporting entities, including money services businesses.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for supplementary collections, foreign-exchange conversion, and supplier or contractor payments; Low as a sole treasury bank. An offshore electronic-money institution is not automatically authorized to accept deposits, provide Canadian payment services, or operate a Canadian client-money account. The provider should be authorized in its home jurisdiction and, where it performs in-scope payment functions for end users in Canada, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act registration and operational requirements administered by the Bank of Canada when applicable. A provider performing foreign-exchange dealing, remittance, or payment activities may also require Canadian FINTRAC money-services-business registration or may need to rely on a regulated partner. Client funds may be safeguarded rather than insured deposits, and funds can be delayed or frozen by sanctions, transaction monitoring, source-of-funds reviews, or account-risk rules. Wise Business and similar services should be used only after confirming Canadian availability, permitted business activity, settlement account ownership, limits, beneficiary restrictions, and tax-record retention. They should not replace a Canadian operating account for payroll, tax remittances, client-money segregation, or primary treasury control.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity subscriptions or shareholder loans from an account held in the legal investor's name directly to the corporation's Canadian operating or designated funding account through a bank subject to Canadian anti-money-laundering controls. Use a complete wire reference identifying the legal entity, transaction type, agreement date, and purpose. Canada does not use a universal statutory foreign-exchange purpose-code system for ordinary corporate wires; the bank may nevertheless require an internal purpose classification and supporting documents. Before remittance, provide the bank with the subscription agreement or loan agreement, corporate authorization, cap table, beneficial-owner information, remitter identity, source-of-funds evidence, currency and amount, expected transaction frequency, and any applicable tax analysis. Reconcile the incoming wire to the general ledger and issue shares or record the loan only after funds clear. Do not route capital through employee, director, nominee, unrelated third-party, or personal accounts. For non-resident lenders and related parties, assess thin-capitalization rules, transfer pricing, interest withholding, imputed-interest issues, shareholder-benefit rules, and required Canadian tax filings. For material foreign direct investment, assess applicable Investment Canada Act notification or review requirements; this does not ordinarily create a bank remittance approval requirement.Use Canadian bank wire transfers, correspondent-bank SWIFT payment messages such as MT103 or their ISO 20022 equivalents, and bank-issued commercial letters of credit where counterparty or country risk justifies documentary settlement. For ordinary executive-search services, pay against verified invoices and executed mandates, with the beneficiary legal name, bank details, country, invoice number, service period, and contract reference recorded. For a commercial LC, define applicant, beneficiary, issuing and confirming banks, currency, amount, expiry, presentation period, governing rules such as UCP 600 where agreed, and required documents; documentary compliance must be checked before release. Use wire templates with maker-checker approval, independent callback verification for changed beneficiary details, sanctions screening, segregation of duties, and payment-limit controls. SWIFT payment release does not eliminate Canadian sanctions, AML, tax withholding, transfer-pricing, payroll, or sales-tax obligations. Maintain invoices, contracts, proof of service, tax forms, bank confirmations, and FX conversion records for audit and CRA purposes. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate intercompany transactions in a single fiscal year activates the CRA administrative expectation for contemporaneous Local File/Transfer Pricing Documentation; no statutory de minimis exists, and CRA may request documentation for transactions below this threshold where risk indicators arise.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under subsection 245(3) of the Income Tax Act to disallow tax benefits arising from avoidance transactions lacking genuine economic substance, business purpose, or transactional integrity; separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as found in offshore financial centres are not enacted in Canada, but CRA will recharacterize sham arrangements and deny treaty benefits to conduit or artificial structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine equal to not less than 50% but not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded; civil reassessment beyond the normal 3-year limitation period where misrepresentation is established; gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax plus daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participated in or directed the evasion may be personally prosecuted criminally and can be assessed civilly for unremitted source deductions under section 227.1; CRA may seek civil fraud judgments, asset freezes, and travel bars through court orders; for willfully causing false returns, directors face concurrent imprisonment and fines, with the corporate veil pierced for restitution.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch and Offshore Shell Intermediaries

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under subsection 247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts or to maintain contemporaneous documentation; CRA can deny deductions entirely under GAAR or recharacterize the transaction, resulting in, e.g., Part XIII withholding of 25% plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve an intercompany arrangement without economic substance may be jointly and severally liable for resulting withholding or source-deduction shortfalls; directors who sign false returns or make false transfer-pricing statements are subject to gross-negligence penalties and possible criminal proceedings, including personal fines and imprisonment.
Treaty Shopping and Artificial Dividend-Stripping

Statutory sanction: CRA will deny treaty relief under GAAR and domestic treaty-shopping rules; withholding tax is reassessed at the full 25% statutory rate, additional penalties for false declarations under section 163.2 apply, and interest accrues from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly create or maintain conduit structures face civil assessments up to the amount of benefit obtained plus penalties; in egregious cases, a court may impose restitution orders through in rem proceedings and enjoin directors from further corporate management, with personal liability for fraudulent distributions under corporate law.
AML and Unlicensed Executive Search Operation

Statutory sanction: BC administrative penalties up to CAD 10,000 for an individual and CAD 50,000 for a corporation per contravention, plus statutory surcharges and potential deregistration; under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 100,000 for an individual and CAD 500,000 for a corporation; criminal laundering of proceeds carries imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate, or fail to implement reasonable supervision, in unlicensed operations or AML breaches are personally liable for regulatory fines and may be banned from serving as directors; fraud or laundering findings permit clients and creditors to pierce the corporate veil and sue directors personally for civil restitution.
