Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via My Business Account portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration via eTaxBC portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a British Columbia limited partnership must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by submitting Form RC1 or through the online registration service. For GST/HST purposes, the entity must register for a GST/HST account under a separate number or the BN assigned. For British Columbia PST, registration is mandatory for taxable sales of certain goods, including wholesale distribution of eyewear frames, through the BC Tax Account (eTaxBC). The BN serves as the statutory TIN/VAT ID, while the BC PST number is an additional provincial tax identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Canadian federal law, no direction for a check-the-box election exists; rather, a limited partnership is classified by domestic tax statute as a fiscally transparent partnership, not a separate taxpayer. Non-resident partners may elect under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act to pay tax on net Canadian-source business income, effectively isolating their Canadian tax liability. Partnerships may also make a technical SIFT election if the entity becomes a publicly traded partnership treating substantial Canadian investment as a specified investment flow-through.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada; mandatory GST/HST registration once a non-resident registrant exceeds this threshold anywhere in Canada. For BC PST, registration is required immediately for a wholesaler/retailer carrying on business in BC, irrespective of a monetary threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate a supply of goods, including wholesale optical frames classified under ISIC 4646, if the goods are delivered to a non-resident customer and physically exported from Canada within the prescribed export period. The supplier must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence proving export, such as an export declaration, transport document, and proof of foreign payment. Services that are performed for a non-resident and relate to the export of goods may also be zero-rated where the recipient is outside Canada and the contract confirms export usage.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada imposes no real-time government fiscal clearance, pre-approval as a digital stamp mandate. All GST/HST-registered suppliers must issue plain electronic invoices containing prescribed fields and file periodic GST/HST returns electronically. The CRA requires tax records, including electronic export evidence, to be retained in a readable format and submitted upon demand. No API-based clearance systems akin to Latin American fiscal clearance are applicable to Canada.

The CRA may audit export claims and deny zero-rating where evidence is insufficient. In the absence of proper documentation, the GST/HST is reassessed at the standard 5% rate, plus interest and a penalty of up to 25% of the net tax amount under the Excise Tax Act, and for false statements penalties can escalate to 25% of the excess tax alleged. A knowingly fraudulent zero-rating claim constitutes wilful evasion attracting criminal fines and imprisonment. The CRA can reassess beyond the normal three-year period in cases of misrepresentation or fraud, permitting full retrospective clawback. For BC, the Provincial Sales Tax Sustainability Act similarly imposes interest and penalties for unpaid provincial tax arising from mischaracterized sales regulated as BC domestic sales.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Bank of Canada Act. Both current and capital accounts are freely convertible with no restrictions on profit remittances to foreign partners, subject solely to standard KYC, AML, and tax compliance obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds required. Receipts may be repatriated without restriction; all transactions remain subject to FINTRAC reporting and anti-money laundering verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High
