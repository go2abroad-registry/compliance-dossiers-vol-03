No statutory minimum paid-up capital; capital contributions are set by the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be cash or property. No escrow account requirement under British Columbia law. Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign owner of a B.C. limited partnership obtains a federal Business Number (BN), including a GST/HST registration number, by filing Form RC1 with CRA or applying online through My Business Account; the partnership, as a separate person for excise tax purposes, must then register for B.C. PST by submitting the B.C. Provincial Registration Application (FIN 418) via eTaxBC before making taxable retail sales in British Columbia.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign owner of a B.C. limited partnership obtains a federal Business Number (BN), including a GST/HST registration number, by filing Form RC1 with CRA or applying online through My Business Account; the partnership, as a separate person for excise tax purposes, must then register for B.C. PST by submitting the B.C. Provincial Registration Application (FIN 418) via eTaxBC before making taxable retail sales in British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; a limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes. For GST/HST, a partnership may make a joint election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act for supplies to be deemed to be made by the partnership as a separate person. Hybrid mismatch constraints under sections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act and applicable treaty anti-hybrid rules restrict cross-border deferral planning.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold based on worldwide taxable supplies over a calendar quarter or the trailing four consecutive quarters; B.C. PST: CAD 10,000 of taxable sales to B.C. customers over the previous 12 months for remote/out-of-province sellers, with registration required before exceeding that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act applies to goods exported by the vendor as part of a commercial transaction (Schedule VI) and to certain services provided to non-residents or performed outside Canada (Schedule VII). A beauty DTC retailer may claim 0% GST/HST when cosmetics are sold, paid for, and shipped from Canada to a foreign customer, with export documentation demonstrating that possession and risk pass in Canada and delivery occurs outside Canada; the registrant must file GST/HST returns to claim input tax credits against zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canada Customs Automated Export Declaration - CAED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Sales Contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign currency settlement
  • Carrier and freight forwarder records evidencing physical export

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, mandatory digital stamping, or API-based invoice clearance in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices and requires most registrants to file GST/HST returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE; B.C. PST returns are filed through eTaxBC. E-invoicing interoperability follows Peppol/EDI as an optional standard.

CRA may disallow zero-rating, reassess the GST/HST due without limitation for misrepresentation, reverse input tax credits, and make retrospective clawbacks with compound interest. Civil penalties include a 25% penalty for false statements or omissions, a separate gross-negligence penalty, and criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act for deliberate evasion, with fines and imprisonment exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility without material statutory restrictions. The CAD/USD and CAD/CNY interbank markets are deep, freely accessible, and permit unrestricted profit remittance by a British Columbia Limited Partnership to foreign partners. There is no conversion surcharge or licensing requirement for a merchant converting trade proceeds. The principal statutory hurdle is non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (default 25%, generally reduced under applicable income tax treaties) on distributions of Canadian-source income and on certain payments; capital repayments are not taxable. The Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), administered by the Bank of Canada, requires registration of payment service providers, but a cosmetics DTC retailer acting for its own account does not require RPAA registration unless it operates payments services for third parties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds; a BC LP may retain USD, CNY, or other foreign-currency revenues freely in offshore accounts or in domestic foreign-currency (U.S. Dollar) bank accounts at Schedule I banks. No FINTRAC report is triggered by currency conversion per se, and no settlement account is mandated for export receipts. For accounting and tax purposes, the LP's financial statements must be prepared in CAD under IFRS/ASPE, and unrealised FX gains or losses are recognised per the applicable accounting standard; this is a measurement rule, not a convertibility restriction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Anti-Money Laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); payments-policy oversight by the Department of Finance Canada. Canada operates a liberalised, non-restrictive foreign-exchange framework; no standalone foreign-exchange administration body or exchange-control permit regime exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — legally constrained. Canada does not recognise an 'Electronic Money Institution' bank-licence category; offshore EMIs (e.g., EEA-licensed) cannot hold settlement accounts in the Canadian retail payment system and are not considered banks by OSFI. An offshore EMI may operate only if it registers with FINTRAC as a money services business and, where applicable, with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act; it cannot provide insured deposit accounts. For capital escrow and operational liquidity, a Schedule I bank is mandatory; a foreign EMI is viable only as a secondary disbursement conduit through a Canadian-registered PSP partner and should not be used for fiduciary custody of LP capital because of tax-at-risk and securities law exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Preferred rail: SWIFT MT103/103P from the foreign remitter's bank to the LP's Canadian account, cleared via the correspondent network into LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) for CAD, or into the bank's internal USD ledger for foreign-currency accounts. Remittance information must state 'Capital Contribution — CA-BC Limited Partnership' with ISO20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (equity capital) or 'GFCD' (investment in financial asset); use 'TRCP' for working-capital injections. Compliance packet: (1) certified BC Corporate Registry filing; (2) executed capital-contribution schedule approved by the General Partner; (3) source-of-funds evidence (asset sale agreements, dividend vouchers, savings statements); (4) legal opinion or nil-withholding certificate under Part XIII where interest or deemed income arises; (5) FINTRAC record-retention for a 5-year period. Beneficiary account name must exactly match the LP's registered legal name; the bank will deny credits to mismatched names. No FINTRAC pre-approval is required for inbound equity wires.Dominant rails: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT103P for supplier payments to China, U.S., and EU cosmetics manufacturers, with MT202 cover for USD final settlement through the bank's New York correspondent; (2) documentary Letter of Credit (UCP600, Incoterms 2020 CIF or FOB) issued by the Vancouver branch and advised through the exporter's bank for large purchase orders; and (3) card-network settlement via Shopify Payments (Visa/Mastercard payouts to the LP's CAD/USD accounts) and PayPal Business for DTC micro-transactions. 25% statutory Part XIII withholding rate, applicable to a non-resident limited partner's share of Canadian-source partnership income; the limited partnership is not a dividend-paying corporation, but the 25% federal cap is the relevant withholding benchmark for profit distributions to foreign partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate threshold for mandatory T106 reporting on non-arm's-length cross-border transactions; a contemporaneous Master File and Local File are required when the corporate partner is a 'large taxpayer' with revenues exceeding CAD 50 million (Level 1) or CAD 500 million (Level 2).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act functions as an economic-substance doctrine to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking a genuine business purpose or non-tax economic substance. CRA may also disregard the limited partnership if it is a sham or agency arrangement; the partnership must maintain actual premises, management, real inventory, and operational employees in British Columbia.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of up to 50% of the tax under subsection 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1 for unremitted taxes; travel bans, civil forfeiture, and director disqualification can be imposed.
Failure to File Return / Cross-Border Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Information return penalties of CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500 (T5013); T106 penalties of CAD 100 per violation, with a minimum of CAD 1,000 and a maximum of CAD 100,000 for repeated gross negligence.

Director personal liability: The CRA can assess directors of the corporate general partner for the partnership's unpaid penalty amounts and interest, and may pursue personal assets after issuing a demand.
General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of the tax benefit, full reassessment with arrears interest, and a 5% civil penalty under subsection 163(9) where the primary purpose was avoidance; in egregious cases, criminal fraud charges may be brought.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations face civil liability for arrears and interest, potential criminal prosecution for fraud, and regulatory disqualification from serving as directors on Canadian corporations.
AML / Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is an indictable offence under the Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 10 years. FINTRAC non-compliance penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act can reach CAD 500,000 for a first violation and CAD 1,000,000 for repeat failure.

Director personal liability: Corporate general-partner directors who knowingly direct laundering face asset forfeiture, extradition proceedings, and personal criminal liability; limited partners who actively participate in laundering lose their limited liability shield.
Non-Resident Part XIII Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The partnership is liable for unremitted Part XIII tax plus interest and a 10% late-filing penalty; wilful failure may lead to summary conviction with a fine up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 12 months.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner become jointly and severally liable for the unremitted withholding, and serious cases can trigger criminal proceedings and travel restrictions.
