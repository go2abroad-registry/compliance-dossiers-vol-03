Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada for GST/HST, federal corporate income tax, and payroll source deductions; BC Ministry of Finance – Consumer Taxation Branch (eTaxBC) for Provincial Sales Tax (PST); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs export controls; Health Canada – Medical Devices Directorate for the required Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) and Device Licence. Primary federal portal: CRA 'My Business Account' at canada.ca/revenue-agency; provincial registry: BC Corporate Online / OneStop; provincial tax portal: eTaxBC at etax.gov.bc.ca.

E-government filing portal

A BC Limited Partnership (LP) must first be registered under the Partnership Act through BC Registry (Corporate Online), then obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The BN is expanded with a 'RT' program account for GST/HST; the LP itself, as a 'person' under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 240, registers for GST/HST, files T5013 Partnership Information Return, and issues T5013 slips to partners. Non-resident partners file Canadian returns via T2121/ETC flows, receive NR4 or T5013 allocations, and must quote the LP's BN and partner-specific ITN on all filings. PST registration is obtained separately under BC's Social Service Tax Act where the LP has a place of business or makes taxable sales of tangible personal property in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A BC LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96: income, capital gains, and losses are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners; no entity-level federal or BC income tax. The general partner is jointly and severally liable; limited partners have liability capped by capital contributions plus undistributed income but are subject to at-risk and limited-recourse loss restrictions under ITA s. 96(2.1)/96(2.2). The LP is also deemed a 'person' for GST/HST purposes, so registration, collection, and reporting obligations run at the entity level while income tax flows through. Territorial scope: Canadian resident partners are subject to worldwide taxation; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income allocated from the LP and may obtain treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election. Statutory levers for an LP include: (1) ITA s. 103 election to re-allocate partnership income/loss among partners where the allocation is bona fide and not primarily tax-motivated; (2) ITA s. 96(3) election for a non-resident partner to compute Canadian partnership income on a deemed-resident separate basis; (3) ITA s. 216(1) election for non-residents to report Canadian-source passive/rental or certain income on a net basis; (4) GST/HST quick method, annual filing, and selected financial institution elections under the ETA. For hybrid mismatch (BEPS Action 2), Canada applies the EIFEL rules (ITA s. 18.2/18.21) with a CAD 250,000 EBITDA threshold; no partnership-specific hybrid election exists, so a foreign hybrid classification mismatch is resolved through domestic transparent treatment and applicable treaty tie-breaker clauses.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: mandatory registration for any person whose worldwide taxable supplies exceed the 'small supplier' threshold of CAD 30,000 per calendar quarter and CAD 30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters (CAD 50,000 for public service bodies); a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is subject to the same threshold but loses small-supplier status if it makes taxable supplies through a permanent establishment in Canada. BC PST: no monetary threshold — registration as a PST vendor is mandatory where the taxpayer has a place of business in BC or solicits sales in BC unless the supply qualifies as an exempt export (goods shipped from BC to a destination outside Canada by the vendor or common carrier).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ultrasound console qualifies for zero-rating under Part V, Schedule VI to the ETA: s. 1 zero-rates tangible personal property exported by the supplier, or delivered to a common carrier for export, 'as soon as practicable' after delivery, with documentary evidence satisfactory to the Minister. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident and performed wholly outside Canada may qualify under s. 9, subject to non-use-in-Canada conditions. The exporter must hold a valid Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence and applicable Device Licence before the sale; HS classification 9018.12 applies. Zero-rating also requires that the supplier retain evidence of physical export within a reasonable period (CRA administrative guidance treats 60 days from delivery as the benchmark; failure to prove export results in re-rating at 5% GST plus potential PST exposure).

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (or eManifest export report) evidencing exit from Canada and foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice in English/French stating HS 9018, Canadian origin, quantity, value, and export terms
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued to the non-resident buyer showing Canadian port of loading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice proving payment in CAD/USD from abroad within the 180-day treasury benchmark
  • Packing list and CBSA cargo control documents evidencing physical clearance
  • Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) and applicable Device Licence certificate
  • ETA evidence log per CRA Memorandum 17.4.1 (shipping records, contracts, freight invoices, and carrier certificates)

Canada has no national continuous transaction control, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate. CRA requires a GST/HST invoice containing supplier name, BN, invoice date, description of goods/services, tax rate, and tax amount for taxable supplies over CAD 150 (ETA s. 223). Electronic invoicing via EDI, XML, or API is permitted subject to audit trail and CRA record retention requirements (6 years). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (NETFILE/GST34) for all registrants; CBSA mandates eManifest submission for export shipments, including a B13A export declaration for goods valued above CAD 2,000 or controlled goods. Health Canada licensing (MDEL/Device Licence) is a prerequisite to exporting the medical device.

CRA audit of export zero-rating focuses on documentary proof of export; failure to substantiate within the normal reassessment period (4 years; 7 years for misrepresentation) triggers re-rating at 5% GST plus BC PST, interest, and penalties. ETA s. 285 imposes a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax sought to be evaded where a false statement is made knowingly or in circumstances amounting to gross negligence; late-filing penalties run at 1% plus 0.25% per month to a maximum of 8%, with interest accrued daily. Fraudulent zero-rating claims also expose the LP and its partners to criminal liability under Criminal Code s. 380 and to director/officer liability under ETA s. 323, plus BC PST penalties of 25% of tax due under the Social Service Tax Act; CRA may claw back refunds and ITCs for the full statutory period where the export evidence is false or backdated.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a freely convertible currency regime with unrestricted capital account transactions. Current account remittances, including dividends, interest, management fees, and partnership distributions, are not subject to conversion controls or prior central bank approval. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity contributions to a British Columbia limited partnership and repatriation of capital, are likewise permitted freely. The only compliance hurdles are (i) accurate characterization of remittances to respect the Canada Revenue Agency's rules on non-resident withholding tax, generally 25% on Canadian-source passive income unless reduced by tax treaty; (ii) FINTRAC and bank-level anti-money laundering verification of beneficial ownership and source of funds; and (iii) compliance with sanctions legislation under the Special Economic Measures Act and the UN Act, which may freeze any payment routed through restricted jurisdictions. No mandatory conversion of foreign currency proceeds or surrender of export receipts exists; all clearing may occur in CAD, USD, or any permitted foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or to any other governmental authority. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed repatriation timeline, and no minimum holding period for export receipts earned by a Canadian exporter. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency in registered foreign currency accounts with Canadian chartered banks. The only applicable obligations pertain to (i) export/import statistical declarations under the international merchandise trade reporting framework (Statistics Canada, with monthly thresholds); (ii) FINTRAC money laundering declaration thresholds for foreign exchange and EFTs exceeding CAD 10,000; and (iii) customs documentation (AES/ACE if exporting from or through the United States, and CBSA requirements within Canada). Corporate treasury may therefore maintain USD settlement balances, convert at spot market rates, or execute hedging transactions without regulatory limitation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration functions are integrated into the OSFI/FINTRAC framework, as Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange control authority.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A British Columbia limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and does not pay corporate income tax at the partnership level. Allocated income is taxed at partner level. If a corporate partner is subject to Canadian corporate tax, the general combined federal and British Columbia rate is generally 27% (15% federal and 12% British Columbia); the eligible small-business combined rate is generally 11% (9% federal and 2% British Columbia), subject to statutory eligibility and business-limit rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic Canadian Part XIII withholding rate on taxable dividends paid or credited to a non-resident is generally 25%. An LP distribution is ordinarily not a dividend; its Canadian tax treatment depends on the character of the underlying partnership income, the partner's status, Canadian-source rules, and any applicable treaty. A corporate partner distributing after-tax profits may create a separate dividend withholding event.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 information return is generally due within five months after the end of the partnership fiscal period. T5013 slips and relevant information must generally be provided to partners by the same statutory deadline. A partnership fiscal period generally cannot exceed 12 months. A corporate partner normally files T2 within six months after its taxation year-end; any balance is generally due two months after year-end, or three months for qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations.

Deadline: · Frequency:

File within six months after the corporation's taxation year-end. Corporate income-tax balances are generally payable within two months after year-end, with a three-month balance-due period available to qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations subject to statutory conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose a general monetary threshold below which contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is unnecessary. Under Income Tax Act section 247, Canadian taxpayers must make reasonable efforts to determine arm's-length prices and maintain contemporaneous documentation for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions. Form T106 generally applies when the statutory reporting conditions are met, including the commonly applicable CAD 1 million aggregate threshold. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million in the preceding fiscal year. A formal local-file or master-file requirement is not triggered solely by a Canadian-dollar transaction threshold, although CRA may request detailed local-file, master-file, and benchmarking material.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to the United Kingdom or United Arab Emirates economic-substance regimes. Substance remains relevant under the general anti-avoidance rule, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty anti-abuse provisions, sham principles, partnership characterization, and permanent-establishment analysis. A Canadian LP should maintain genuine Canadian commercial activity where claimed, including appropriate personnel, manufacturing or quality-control functions, premises, equipment, decision-making, books, bank accounts, regulatory controls, and risk assumption. A conduit LP with contractual profits unsupported by functions and risks is exposed to reassessment, treaty denial, transfer-pricing adjustments, withholding tax, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Records

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act offences may result in fines generally ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years. Reassessment, gross-negligence penalties, arrears interest, and civil collection also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, general partners, and persons exercising effective control may face criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, restitution, and director liability for certain unremitted amounts. Corporate separateness does not protect a person who personally participates in fraud or evasion.
Failure to Withhold or Remit Payroll, GST/HST, or Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the unremitted tax, interest, remittance penalties, director-liability assessments, denial of treaty relief, and potential criminal sanctions for deliberate conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for corporate source deductions under Income Tax Act section 227.1 and GST/HST amounts under Excise Tax Act section 323, subject to statutory due-diligence defenses and limitation periods.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing reassessment, secondary adjustments, withholding tax, interest, penalty under section 247, denial of deductions, and possible thin-capitalization income inclusions.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may face civil claims, shareholder or creditor claims, and personal exposure where they knowingly approve fraudulent records, unlawful distributions, or transactions breaching fiduciary duties.
Shell Company, Treaty Shopping, or Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty benefits may be denied under the principal-purpose rule or other anti-abuse provisions; the 25% domestic withholding rate, interest, penalties, and reassessment may apply.

Director personal liability: Individuals directing sham arrangements may face civil restitution, director disqualification consequences in applicable proceedings, criminal tax charges, and imprisonment where fraud or evasion is proven.
Partnership Mischaracterization and Limited-Partner Overreach

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization, denial or suspension of losses, partner-level reassessments, interest, penalties, and possible loss of limited-liability protection under partnership law.

Director personal liability: A general partner is ordinarily liable for partnership obligations. A limited partner may lose statutory protection to the extent it improperly participates in management or commits independent wrongdoing. Persons controlling the LP may face civil restitution and, for fraud, criminal sanctions.
Medical-Device Regulatory Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Food and Drugs Act and Medical Devices Regulations may support compliance orders, product seizure, recalls, licence suspension or cancellation, administrative enforcement, prosecution, fines, and imprisonment for serious offences.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be prosecuted where they authorize or tolerate violations. Personal civil exposure may arise from defective products, negligence, consumer claims, and regulatory non-compliance.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Sanctions

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties can be substantial and depend on the violation and compliance history; serious laundering or sanctions offences may lead to significant fines, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and related legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may face prosecution, forfeiture, travel restrictions imposed by court or enforcement process, and imprisonment for knowing participation or willful blindness.
Unlawful Distributions and Insolvency Fraud

Statutory sanction: Civil recovery, preference and transfer-at-undervalue remedies, tax reassessment, creditor claims, bankruptcy proceedings, and potential fraud or fraudulent-preference offences.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may face restitution, contribution claims, fiduciary-duty damages, disqualification, asset tracing, and imprisonment for intentional fraud. Travel bans are not an automatic tax sanction but may be imposed through court orders or criminal proceedings where legally justified.
