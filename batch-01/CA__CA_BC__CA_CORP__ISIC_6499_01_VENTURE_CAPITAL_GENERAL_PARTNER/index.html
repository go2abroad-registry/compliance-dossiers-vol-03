Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — sole federal administering authority for federal corporation income tax (Part I of the Income Tax Act), GST/HST (Part IX of the Excise Tax Act) and payroll; BC provincial corporate income tax is also collected by CRA under the Canada–BC tax collection agreement. The BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, separately administers the 7% Provincial Sales Tax (PST) via the eTaxBC portal. Primary CRA digital portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); returns are filed through GST/HST NETFILE and e-file services; customs and export accounts are managed by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia); CRA assigns the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) upon incorporation. 2) Register online via CRA 'Business Registration Online' (Form RC1) to open program accounts: corporation income tax (RC), GST/HST (RT — mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a trailing four-quarter period; voluntary earlier), payroll (RP) and import/export (RM with CBSA). 3) Register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance for a PST vendor (retailer) account via eTaxBC. 4) Non-resident GPs and foreign investors should appoint a Canadian tax representative under ETA s. 218 and obtain a BN; a non-resident without a Canadian permanent establishment may nonetheless be required to register for GST if it makes taxable supplies in Canada. 5) The BN plus the two-letter program identifier (e.g., RT) constitutes the statutory tax ID for invoicing and filing. The GST number format is BN + 'RT' + 4 digits.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a federal or BC business corporation is always a separate 'taxable Canadian corporation' (ITA s. 89) taxed on worldwide income under Part I; Canada does not offer US-style check-the-box or pass-through classification for corporations. A BC venture-capital GP management entity normally qualifies as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) if not controlled, directly or indirectly, by non-residents or public corporations; CCPC status grants the small business deduction (federal 9% plus BC 2% low rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income) and access to the lifetime capital gains exemption on qualified small business corporation shares. Territorial scope: resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source active business income (Part I) and passive income (Part XIII). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a federal or BC business corporation is always a separate 'taxable Canadian corporation' (ITA s. 89) taxed on worldwide income under Part I; Canada does not offer US-style check-the-box or pass-through classification for corporations. A BC venture-capital GP management entity normally qualifies as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) if not controlled, directly or indirectly, by non-residents or public corporations; CCPC status grants the small business deduction (federal 9% plus BC 2% low rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income) and access to the lifetime capital gains exemption on qualified small business corporation shares. Territorial scope: resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source active business income (Part I) and passive income (Part XIII). No tax-exempt zone or free-zone corporate income tax holiday applies in British Columbia.

Available election forms

ITA s. 85(1) rollover on transferring assets to a corporation; s. 86 share reorganization; s. 97(2) partnership-to-corporation rollover; s. 87 amalgamation; s. 88(1) wind-up rollover; s. 89(1) capital dividend account election; s. 256(2) T2S31 associated-corporation designation to allocate the CAD 500,000 small business limit; s. 216 election for non-resident rental income; ETA s. 177 closely related elections; ETA s. 273 joint group registration; ETA s. 218 tax-representative appointment. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.2/18.3) are enacted but their coming-into-force is deferred; until then, hybrid outcomes are governed by the general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245) and FAPI attribution (s. 91/95). A US entity holding or controlling a Canadian feeder can make a check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3, which CRA respects for mapping the US branch for foreign accrual purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1), measured on worldwide taxable supplies over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding it mandates GST registration. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies must register from the first dollar; non-residents without Canadian nexus may register voluntarily to claim input tax credits. BC PST vendor registration begins with the first taxable sale or lease of tangible personal property, with a small-PST-registrant concession when the monthly PST liability is below CAD 3,000. For a VC GP, management fee invoices attract 5% GST unless the supply is zero-rated to a non-resident fund or constitutes an exempt financial service.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the ETA where the supplier exports tangible property by shipment or delivery to a destination outside Canada, with documentary proof retained under ETA s. 167.1; the supplier charges 0% GST and remains entitled to full input tax credits. Services: Schedule VI, Part V s. 7 zero-rates a supply made to a non-resident person unless it relates to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, is rendered to an individual present in Canada, or the non-resident is a GST registrant. A BC VC GP's management and administration services supplied to a non-resident fund are zero-rated when the fund is not resident in Canada, is not registered for GST/HST and does not carry on business in Canada, provided no part of the consideration is in respect of Canadian property or services performed in Canada; carried interest and partnership profit allocations are not consideration for a taxable supply and are outside GST. Management fees charged to Canadian resident portfolio companies attract 5% GST. CRA accepts invoicing in a foreign currency using the CRA official exchange rate; no mandatory currency conversion applies.

  • CBSA G13 Export Declaration filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) or the Canadian Single Window / eManifest portal for goods exports
  • Certified commercial export invoice describing goods, value, destination and terms of sale
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill issued by a common carrier evidencing shipment to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank transfer advice substantiating the receipt of export proceeds
  • Executed service agreement and work-papers evidencing the non-resident recipient, place of performance and absence of Canadian nexus for zero-rated services
  • Non-resident status certificate or equivalent documentation (e.g., Form W-8BEN-E for US fund investors) retained in GST/HST e-records

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), API clearance or digital stamping regime exists federally or in BC. GST reporting is periodic; invoices must contain prescribed information under ETA s. 223 and SOR/90-1 — supplier name, BN, date, description and tax amount. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE (mandatory once taxable sales exceed CAD 1.5M), with tax payments made by electronic transfer to CRA. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (PEPPOL-BIS) is voluntary except for suppliers invoicing the federal government; BC does not adopt Quebec's planned e-invoicing mandate (2027–2028). Records — including e-invoices and supporting export evidence — must be retained for six years.

CRA's normal reassessment window for corporations is four years from the mailing date of the original notice of assessment; for gross negligence, fraud or misrepresentation the limitation period is indefinitely suspended, permitting retrospective clawback of ITCs and zero-rating benefits. Monetary penalties: ITA s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax (100% for a second offence); ITA s. 163(1) false statements or omissions CAD 100 plus 50% of understated tax; ETA s. 280.1(1) 10% of the ITC/rebate claimed for careless misstatement; ETA s. 285 failure to file or provide information CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 per failure. Criminal tax evasion under ITA s. 239 and ETA s. 327.1 carries fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years on summary conviction or up to 14 years on indictment. Interest on reassessed amounts accrues at prescribed rates, compounded daily. CRA zero-rating audit programs specifically target cross-border management fees and export schemes; a pre-audit voluntary disclosure (named or 'no-name') may mitigate penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains no exchange controls, no CAD convertibility licensing, and no approval requirement for profit repatriation, dividend remittance, capital withdrawals, or liquidation proceeds. Remittance friction is limited to tax compliance: Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents (default 25%, generally reduced to 5% or 15% under the Canada-US Treaty and analogous instruments), the solvency test under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) before any shareholder distribution, and COR-required reporting for certain cross-border transactions. Capital calls from foreign LPs are fully convertible into CAD for deployment without central bank authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in Canada. The former Foreign Exchange Control Act was suspended in 1951 and definitively repealed; exporters are not required to repatriate, convert, deposit, or declare export proceeds to the Bank of Canada. The operative obligations are reporting and recordkeeping: FINTRAC disclosure of cross-border electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, declaration of physical currency of CAD 10,000 or more at the border, and retention of trade-settlement documentation for seven years under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Commodity exporters in British Columbia must additionally satisfy CBSA customs documentation requirements, but no CAD conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising FX oversight jointly with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of federally chartered banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border transaction monitoring; provincial supervision for non-prudential entities rests with the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium viability as a primary clearing rail; High viability only as a supplementary disbursement channel. Canadian law does not recognize 'electronic money institutions' as deposit-taking institutions; under the Bank Act, only Schedule I and II banks may accept deposits. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise for Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud) may operate in Canada solely as Money Services Businesses under the PCMLTFA, requiring FINTRAC registration, registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada (effective September 2024), and BCFSA licensing where applicable in BC. These entities cannot hold LP capital in escrow without breaching the LPA segregated-account covenant and the Bank Act deposit prohibition. Recommended architecture: use a Schedule I chartered bank as the settlement spine; engage an EMI solely for low-value portfolio-company invoices, contractor remittances, and fund-administration payments, with the GP maintaining its own source-of-funds and AML file for each EMI transaction.

Defense evidence: Approved investment committee minutes authorizing each portfolio company disbursement, fund cash-flow model and liquidity budget, side-letter interpretations, executed term sheet and share purchase agreement, and, where applicable, notarized director resolutions evidencing the investment mandate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital calls must route via SWIFT MT103 from the LP's own funded account to the GP's segregated escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The wire narrative must contain the structured purpose code and reference string: 'VENTURE-GP CAPITAL CALL — [FUND NAME] — [LP ID] — [CALL NOTICE REFERENCE]'. The bank applies FINTRAC EFT reporting for transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, sanctions and PEP screening on both originating and beneficiary parties, and UBO re-verification on each new wire message where the LP's underlying ownership has changed. Any wire failing source-of-funds confirmation or displaying a prohibited counterparty association is quarantined in a suspense account pending counsel-reviewed remediation. Interac e-Transfer and ACH are not viable for cross-border capital calls.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 for single portfolio-company investments, bridge-loan draws, and follow-on rounds, with MT101 or MT202 as batch and interbank settlement alternatives through the Schedule I bank's proprietary system. A commercial letter of credit (sight or standby) is permissible where the GP guarantees portfolio-company obligations or contributes to a cross-border M&A closing, but requires a dedicated facility agreement and board-approved mandate letter. Every outbound transfer over CAD 10,000 generates a FINTRAC report; each instruction must carry a board-approved disbursement memo and a wire narrative matching the deal code, ISIN, or CUSIP of the target portfolio company. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium viability as a primary clearing rail; High viability only as a supplementary disbursement channel. Canadian law does not recognize 'electronic money institutions' as deposit-taking institutions; under the Bank Act, only Schedule I and II banks may accept deposits. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise for Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud) may operate in Canada solely as Money Services Businesses under the PCMLTFA, requiring FINTRAC registration, registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada (effective September 2024), and BCFSA licensing where applicable in BC. These entities cannot hold LP capital in escrow without breaching the LPA segregated-account covenant and the Bank Act deposit prohibition. Recommended architecture: use a Schedule I chartered bank as the settlement spine; engage an EMI solely for low-value portfolio-company invoices, contractor remittances, and fund-administration payments, with the GP maintaining its own source-of-funds and AML file for each EMI transaction.
