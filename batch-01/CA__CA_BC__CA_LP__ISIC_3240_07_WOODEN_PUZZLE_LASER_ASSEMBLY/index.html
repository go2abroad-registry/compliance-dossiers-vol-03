Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. A BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes: income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners (ITA s. 96), with no entity-level federal income tax. For GST/HST the partnership is a distinct registrant. Available statutory elections include: (i) ETA s. 156 election for closely related suppliers to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) appointment of a 'representative partner' to account for GST/HST on behalf of the partnership; (iii) ITA s. 249.1 fiscal-period election for partnerships; (iv) section 216 optional deemed-non-resident election for provincially-incorporated corporations (not applicable to the LP itself). Hybrid mismatch arrangements are neutralized by ITA s. 18.4 following BEPS Action 2, with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters, excluding financial services and goodwill); BC PST: registration required for every seller making taxable sales in BC — no universal small-supplier exemption for remote sellers of tangible goods; marketplace facilitators are separately accountable with their own registration/collection thresholds

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the registrant supplies tangible personal property, ships/delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, and the goods are exported 'as soon after delivery is complete as is reasonable in the circumstances'. Services: laser cutting/assembly services performed on movable wooden goods for a non-resident recipient qualify under ETA Schedule VI Part V where the property is exported after the service is performed and the non-resident is at arm's length; the supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence that the goods left Canada (carrier receipts, customs export declarations, shipping waybills) and that payment came from a non-resident account. Zero-rated supplies must be recorded separately in the GST/HST return to claim net-tax refunds where input tax credits exceed output tax.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or Canadian Automated Export Declaration 'CAED' record) where the shipment exceeds CAD 2,000
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order describing the goods, stated zero-rating rationale, and name/address of the non-resident purchaser
  • Bank/remittance evidence (SWIFT credit advice, foreign-currency conversion record, or inward remittance certificate) proving settlement from a non-resident account

Canada does not operate a real-time statutory e-invoice clearance, fiscal stamping, or XML-clearance regime at federal or BC level. GST/HST registrants must issue and retain prescribed invoice content (ETA ss. 169 and 169.1) and e-file GST/HST returns; mandatory e-filing applies to all GST/HST registrants (CRA Business Number / NETFILE), and BC PST returns must be filed electronically through eTaxBC above the BC e-filing threshold. Canada participates voluntarily in Peppol-based e-invoicing for B2G transactions, but no approval/certification of the tax authority before invoice issuance is required.

CRA may reassess outside the standard 4-year limitation period (7 years or longer for false representations, fraud, or wilful misstatement), retroactively disallow zero-rated treatment, and recover the unreported 5% GST with daily compounded prescribed-rate interest. Civil penalties include the ETA s. 285 false-statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax evaded, and gross-negligence penalties where export documentation is fabricated. Criminal conviction for deliberate export zero-rating fraud carries fines up to twice the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. BC PST penalties (interest, penalty of up to 25%, registration revocation) apply where goods are sold for consumption in BC rather than exported.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has a freely convertible Canadian dollar and no general exchange-control regime. Current-account payments, including imports, exports, services, royalties, interest, dividends, and ordinary business remittances, may generally be made without prior central-bank approval. Capital-account transactions, including foreign investment, partnership contributions, intercompany loans, acquisitions, and capital repatriation, are generally permitted without exchange-control approval, subject to tax, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, beneficial-ownership, securities, competition, investment-screening, and financial-reporting requirements. A British Columbia limited partnership may remit distributable cash to non-resident partners subject to the partnership agreement, solvency and creditor protections, withholding-tax rules, treaty eligibility, CRA reporting, and bank verification of the payment purpose and source of funds. Non-resident withholding may apply to Canadian-source interest, dividends, rents, royalties, management fees, and certain other payments; partnership allocations may create Canadian filing and withholding obligations depending on the partners, income type, and applicable treaty. Foreign investment in Canada may be reviewable under the Investment Canada Act, particularly where thresholds, cultural-business rules, national-security considerations, or sensitive sectors are involved. The described wooden-puzzle manufacturing activity is ordinarily not a regulated financial, defence, or otherwise specially controlled sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the holder's discretion. Exporters must retain adequate commercial and customs records and comply with applicable CBSA export reporting, GST/HST, income-tax, transfer-pricing, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Banks may request invoices, shipping records, customs documentation, contracts, and beneficial-owner information under FINTRAC and internal risk procedures. Foreign-currency balances, receivables, and hedging positions must be recorded and reported under the applicable Canadian accounting and tax framework.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; foreign-exchange reporting and anti-money-laundering oversight also involves the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and foreign-exchange execution; Low for replacing a full Canadian operating bank account. Canada does not provide a single general electronic-money-institution licence equivalent to the European Union EMI regime. An offshore digital EMI or payment institution may provide services only within the scope of its home-country authorization and applicable Canadian legal requirements. Canadian payment-service activities may trigger the Retail Payment Activities Act and Bank of Canada registration requirements when the provider performs regulated payment functions for Canadian end users, subject to statutory exclusions and implementation status. Foreign providers may also face FINTRAC registration, money-services-business obligations, sanctions screening, consumer-protection, tax, privacy, Canadian securities or derivatives, and correspondent-bank requirements. Stripe and Amazon Pay availability, settlement currencies, reserve policies, chargeback exposure, merchant underwriting, and cross-border settlement eligibility must be confirmed for the precise Canadian LP, beneficial owners, products, and sales channels. Customer funds should not be treated as legally segregated trust funds unless the provider's contractual and regulatory framework expressly provides that protection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner contributions, equity-like capital, or partnership loans through a Canadian commercial-bank account held in the exact legal name of the British Columbia LP. Use a SWIFT customer credit transfer, normally MT103 or the applicable ISO 20022 equivalent, from an account controlled by the contributing partner or documented lender. Include the legal name and address of the LP, Canadian account number, transit and institution numbers, SWIFT/BIC, originating party, ultimate beneficial owner where requested, and a precise remittance description such as 'limited-partnership capital contribution under agreement dated [date]' or 'intercompany loan under agreement dated [date]'. Canada generally has no universal statutory FX purpose-code system for ordinary corporate wires; the bank may nevertheless require an internal purpose classification and supporting documents. Provide the partnership agreement, contribution or loan agreement, partner resolution, source-of-funds evidence, ownership chart, tax-residency information, and expected-use schedule before transmission. Do not route capital through a personal account, unrelated third party, undisclosed nominee, or payment processor. Confirm whether the contribution is capital, debt, or revenue before remittance because classification affects accounting, withholding, transfer pricing, interest deductibility, partnership allocations, and tax reporting. Maintain wire confirmations, bank advices, contracts, and ledger reconciliation. Consider applicable Statistics Canada foreign-investment or international-transaction surveys and CRA information returns; these are reporting obligations rather than exchange-control approvals.Use Canadian bank domestic rails for Canadian-dollar payments and SWIFT cross-border transfers for foreign-currency suppliers. An MT103, or the bank's ISO 20022 pacs.008 equivalent, is appropriate for a single commercial payment and should contain complete ordering-customer, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, invoice, and purpose information. Use a commercial documentary letter of credit where supplier, jurisdiction, performance, or documentary risk justifies bank intermediation; define the issuing bank, confirming bank if required, UCP 600 rules, document requirements, shipment terms, inspection evidence, insurance, partial-shipment rules, expiry, discrepancy procedures, and sanctions clauses. For routine suppliers, use documented open-account transfers supported by purchase orders, invoices, shipping records, and three-way matching. For Canadian domestic clearing, use the Payments Canada systems and bank-specific EFT, wire, or real-time payment capabilities available to the account; availability and limits depend on the bank and account profile. For marketplace and card receipts, maintain processor-to-bank settlement accounts in the LP's legal name, segregate settlement reconciliation from operating disbursements, and control refunds and chargebacks. Screen counterparties, banks, vessels, jurisdictions, goods, and payment messages against applicable Canadian sanctions and AML requirements. Retain transaction records and supporting documents for the statutory and bank-prescribed retention periods.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with a Canadian bank or appropriately regulated dealer, documented under the bank's master agreement and supported by a demonstrable underlying exposure such as forecast supplier payments, marketplace receipts, or foreign-currency receivables.Foreign-exchange spot transactions and scheduled conversions through a Canadian commercial bank or regulated dealer, with transaction confirmations, rate records, settlement instructions, and segregation of trading and payment approval duties.Non-deliverable forwards where the currency is restricted, non-deliverable, or commercially appropriate, subject to dealer eligibility, applicable Canadian provincial derivatives rules, trade-reporting or recordkeeping requirements, collateral terms, close-out provisions, and documented underlying exposure. NDFs are generally cash-settled and do not create a right to deliver the restricted currency.Currency swaps or cross-currency swaps for documented recurring funding or liquidity exposures, subject to counterparty credit limits, master documentation, valuation, collateral, accounting, and applicable derivatives regulation.Exchange-traded currency futures or options through an authorized futures commission merchant, where the LP satisfies account-opening, margin, suitability, and reporting requirements.Purchased foreign-exchange options and structured option strategies only after confirming the LP's sophistication, risk limits, dealer documentation, accounting treatment, and capacity to absorb premium, margin, liquidity, and termination risks. Avoid speculative trading unrelated to identifiable business exposures.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment collection and foreign-exchange execution; Low for replacing a full Canadian operating bank account. Canada does not provide a single general electronic-money-institution licence equivalent to the European Union EMI regime. An offshore digital EMI or payment institution may provide services only within the scope of its home-country authorization and applicable Canadian legal requirements. Canadian payment-service activities may trigger the Retail Payment Activities Act and Bank of Canada registration requirements when the provider performs regulated payment functions for Canadian end users, subject to statutory exclusions and implementation status. Foreign providers may also face FINTRAC registration, money-services-business obligations, sanctions screening, consumer-protection, tax, privacy, Canadian securities or derivatives, and correspondent-bank requirements. Stripe and Amazon Pay availability, settlement currencies, reserve policies, chargeback exposure, merchant underwriting, and cross-border settlement eligibility must be confirmed for the precise Canadian LP, beneficial owners, products, and sales channels. Customer funds should not be treated as legally segregated trust funds unless the provider's contractual and regulatory framework expressly provides that protection.
