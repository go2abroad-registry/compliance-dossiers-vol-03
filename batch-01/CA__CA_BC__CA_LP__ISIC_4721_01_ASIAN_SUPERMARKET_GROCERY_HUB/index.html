Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, and payroll; portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at register-cr.gc.ca. For British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST): BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, administered via eTaxBC; jurisdiction code CA-BC.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity obtains a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 with CRA or registering online through BRO. The Limited Partnership must open a GST/HST program account (RT) once annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or when it carries on a commercial activity in Canada; Form RC1 also creates the BC PST account if the BC box is selected. The LP files a T5013 Partnership Information Return annually; non-resident partners are tracked for Part XIII withholding and Part I tax remittances. A non-resident partnership is required to designate a resident agent for service of process under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) and the Income Tax Act (ITA).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; an LP is statutorily pass-through under ITA s. 96. Available elections: non-calendar fiscal period-end election under ITA ss. 249.1(1)-(2); specified member election and deemed-period rules under ITA s. 96(3); hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.7 (BEPS Action 2) restrict deduction of hybrid payments and may recharacterize mismatches; GST/HST elections for closely related entities under ETA s. 273.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST small supplier threshold); non-residents carrying on business in Canada are required to register regardless of threshold. BC PST threshold: CAD 30,000 for retail vendors; grocery sales of basic food are PST-exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export eligibility under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 applies where a supplier delivers goods to a draught of conveyance or ships/delivers goods outside Canada under a contract for placement outside Canada; the LP must retain satisfactory evidence of export. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under Part V ss. 5-7 if performed wholly outside Canada. Basic groceries under Schedule VI Part III are already zero-rated domestically; exported Asian condiments and vegetables qualify at 0% GST if the export documentation bundle is complete and the sale is made in the ordinary course of commercial activity.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration via GCA for goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more; Form B13A for non-CSA filers)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Ocean Waybill naming the Canadian LP as exporter
  • Commercial invoice stating Incoterms, foreign destination address, and consideration in foreign currency
  • Proof of delivery: carrier waybill tracking, terminal receipt, or postal/parcel customs label retained by the supplier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident purchaser
  • Sales contract or purchase order and insurance certificate (for CIF/CIP sales)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API clearance mandate exists in Canada as of 2025. E-invoicing is voluntary and permitted if the supplier retains records satisfying ETA s. 286; electronic GST/HST returns are mandatory via GST/HST NETFILE when annual revenue exceeds CAD 1.5 million. BC PST returns are filed electronically through eTaxBC; no third-party clearance engine applies.

CRA may deny zero-rating, reassess GST at 5%, and charge interest under ETA s. 280 if export substantiation is incomplete; retrospective clawback periods are four years from the assessment date, extendable to seven years for negligence or fraud. Penalties for false statements or omissions include up to CAD 25,000 or 25% of the net tax amount under ETA ss. 285 and 285.1, plus ITA s. 163.2 gross negligence penalties. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may trigger criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 with fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment, and cancellation of the GST/HST registration number. FX repatriation imposes no statutory deadline in Canada; the 180-day benchmark reflects the foreign exchange control contract limit, not an ETA/ITA threshold.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with no current-account or capital-account restrictions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely; there is no approval quota, no prior-verification requirement, and no license for inward or outward remittances, profit distributions, or capital repatriation. For a British Columbia Limited Partnership with non-resident limited partners, profit remittance is subject to tax compliance rather than FX approval: the LP must file a partnership information return and issue T5013 slips allocating Canadian-source business income; non-resident partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on their share of that business income and may be required to file a Canadian non-resident return. Passive payments (interest, rents, royalties, management fees) attract Part XIII withholding tax at 25%, reducible under applicable tax treaties (e.g., Canada-US, Canada-China). Upon exit, a disposition of the partnership interest that constitutes taxable Canadian property requires a Form T2062/T2062B clearance certificate and may trigger a compliance deposit before funds are released to the non-resident seller.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender deadline, no mandatory conversion ratio, no designated-account requirement, and no repatriation quota for export or foreign-currency receipts. A BC grocery importer may freely retain USD, CNY, JPY, or other currencies in foreign-currency accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Nevertheless, CAD-equivalent reporting at Bank of Canada daily exchange rates is required for income tax and GST/HST purposes. Under FINTRAC rules, cash deposits of CAD 10,000 or more require a Cash Transaction Report, and inbound telegraphic transfers exceeding internal bank thresholds (commonly CAD 100,000) require source-of-funds confirmation. Transfers connected with sanctioned jurisdictions or listed entities must be blocked pursuant to Global Affairs Canada's Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA) regulations and FINTRAC terrorist-property reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercised in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision, the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy, Payments Canada for systemic clearing (Lynx, ACSS, Real-Time Rail), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CTF enforcement under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange control ministry or exchange-control licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Stripe, Airwallex) are viable only as FX settlement sub-wallets after the provider has registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). These EMIs cannot replace a Schedule I bank account: they do not provide Interac POS acceptance, do not hold Payments Canada clearing membership (Lynx/ACSS), cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and do not carry CDIC deposit insurance. The treasury policy should cap aggregate EMI balances below CAD 250,000, wire-sweep balances daily to the primary bank, and verify in writing that the EMI's settlement agent is a Canadian-regulated Schedule I bank, otherwise FINTRAC may reclassify the LP as an unregistered money services business.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all inbound capital injections via SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022 pacs.008) using purpose code 'CAPT' (Capital Payment) or 'CFIN' (Corporate Finance) in the remittance field. Mandatory remittance narrative: 'Equity contribution per LP Agreement dated [DATE]; BC LP registration no. [X]; Part XIII withholding not applicable — equity, not interest or dividend.' The receiving bank will validate the remitter against the account's beneficial-ownership register. Do not split wires to circumvent internal controls, as this raises FINTRAC structuring flags. For injections above CAD 500,000, pre-clear the transaction with the bank's treasury desk and provide a source-of-funds attestation and a capital-call resolution adopted by the general partner. Maintain a written equity-versus-loan classification ledger to support the Canada Revenue Agency's thin-capitalization rules (1.5:1 controlled-foreign debt-to-equity) and the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) regime.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 / pacs.008 direct credit for supplier payments below CAD 100,000 per transaction using purpose code 'TRIN' (Trade Payment). (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued by the Canadian bank through its foreign correspondence banks (e.g., Bank of China Hong Kong for CNH, MUFG for JPY, KEB Hana for KRW) for large recurring shipments; required documents are commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and independent inspection certificate. (3) Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) as an intermediate step when migrating suppliers to open-account terms. (4) Domestic CAD settlement via Payments Canada ACSS or Real-Time Rail (RTR) for local BC wholesalers and staff disbursements. For Chinese renminbi, settle on the offshore CNH axis through the bank's Hong Kong clearing (CMU) rather than triangulating via USD; for JPY and KRW use SWIFT MT202 cover. Include the beneficiary bank BIC and precise account identifiers (Canada does not use IBAN; China requires the 12-digit account number plus CNAPS code). T5013 Partnership Information Return and T5013 Summary No later than five months after the end of each partnership fiscal period; a calendar-year LP with December 31 year-end must file by May 31 of the following year. $0 USD Late filing penalty is CAD 25 per day for each day late, from CAD 100 to a maximum of CAD 2,500; CRA may additionally assess gross-negligence penalties for false or incomplete partner information.
GST/HST Annual Return (if annual GST/HST remitter election is in effect) For an annual GST/HST filing period, the return and annual remittance are due no later than three months after the end of the partnership's fiscal year; for a December 31 year-end this is March 31 of the following year. $0 USD Late filing and late remittance attract interest under the Excise Tax Act plus a late-filing penalty equal to 1% of the amount of tax owing for the first month and 0.25% for each additional complete month, up to 12 months; habitual omission may trigger higher penalties.
British Columbia Employer Health Tax (EHT) Annual Return For every year in which the LP is an employer with BC payroll remuneration exceeding the EHT exemption threshold (CAD 500,000 for 2024), the annual return is due on or before March 31 of the following calendar year. $0 USD Failure to file or pay by March 31 triggers interest, a late-filing penalty equal to 2% of the tax due plus 1% per complete month up to 12 months, and possible collection enforcement by the BC government.
