Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital. The BC Business Corporations Act permits incorporation with one or more shares without par value; no escrow account or capital deposit is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: occupancy permit and fire inspection clearance under the BC Fire Code for the registered office premises; structural load inspection is the landlord's obligation; occupational health and safety compliance under the Workers Compensation Act, RSBC 1996, c. 492, with WorkSafeBC, including ergonomic workstation assessments and a written workplace safety plan.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital. The BC Business Corporations Act permits incorporation with one or more shares without par value; no escrow account or capital deposit is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll and excise tax administration; principal portal is My Business Account via canada.ca/revenue-agency. British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation and Programs Branch, administers Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in British Columbia, or incorporating a Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or British Columbia Business Corporations Act, obtains a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) through the one-stop business registration process. For a BC-incorporated corporation, the BN is generated automatically upon incorporation through BC Corporate Registry and is activated as the corporate income tax account (RC), GST/HST account (RT), and payroll account (RP) as required. Non-resident suppliers without Canadian incorporation may register directly with CRA using Form RC1 and receive a BN with a GST/HST program account. The BN serves as both the statutory tax identifier and the indirect tax VAT/GST identifier; there is no separate national tax card.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box regime exists. A Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer and cannot elect pass-through treatment under the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST purposes, closely related corporations that are registrants may jointly elect under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act to treat supplies made between them as being made for nil consideration, subject to conditions, and certain restructuring elections are available for transfers of business on a GST-neutral basis. These elections do not alter the entity-level income tax classification and Canada does not provide hybrid mismatch entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST, mandatory registration is triggered once a person carrying on commercial activity in Canada makes CAD 30,000 or more of taxable supplies in Canada during the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in the current calendar quarter; this is the small-supplier threshold. For BC PST, no equivalent small-supplier turnover exemption applies; a vendor selling taxable goods, taxable software or taxable services at retail in British Columbia, or a non-resident vendor with B.C. sales of taxable digital products or software, must register as a PST vendor.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST applies to a supply of goods exported by the supplier where the goods are exported as soon as is reasonable after the supply and documentary evidence is retained. For an SEO content analytics and keyword analytics SaaS supplied to a non-resident, zero-rating applies to intangible personal property and services supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under the Excise Tax Act and who does not acquire the supply for use, consumption or enjoyment in Canada. The supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence establishing the non-resident status of the recipient, the place of supply, and the absence of Canadian use. Qualifying zero-rated exports entitle the registrant to claim input tax credits and obtain a net refund where GST/HST credits exceed GST/HST collected.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A), applicable to physical media or tangible components exported from Canada; for pure digital delivery, this item is not mandatory but IP-geolocation and download evidence must be preserved
  • Bill of Lading, electronic transport document, or carrier certificate of export for goods; for cloud SaaS, server and access logs demonstrating delivery of services or intangible property outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice and signed service contract identifying the non-resident customer, billing territory, and recipient's GST/HST registration status
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank transfer advice, or payment settlement records showing foreign collected proceeds attributable to the zero-rated supply

Canada does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax clearance, or digital stamping for GST/HST or BC PST. Electronic invoices are permitted and acceptable to CRA providing they contain all prescribed information under sections 169 and 169.1 of the Excise Tax Act, including vendor name and BN, date of supply, description and GST/HST amount or rate. Voluntary electronic invoicing may use PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standards, but CRA approval or integration with a fiscal central clearinghouse is not required.

CRA will audit zero-rating claims and may deny the 0% rate where evidence fails to establish export or non-resident use; the result is reassessment of GST/HST at 5% plus interest under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act and possible civil penalties under sections 280 and 285. The normal reassessment period is four years, but misrepresentation, fraud, neglect or wilful default removes the limitation period, leaving the export zero-rating exposure open indefinitely. Deliberate false or misleading statements can lead to criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, with fines and imprisonment, and corresponding PST penalties may be imposed by the BC Ministry of Finance under the Provincial Sales Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII. There are no statutory approvals for profit remittance, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation for Canadian corporations, subject to solvency tests under corporate law and applicable withholding taxes. Foreign exchange transactions are freely conducted by chartered banks and regulated financial institutions. Domestic and cross-border payments settle in CAD without quota restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds in Canada. Canadian-resident exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in non-resident or domestic foreign-currency bank accounts; conversion to CAD is voluntary. The cross-border movement of funds is monitored under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for AML/ATF reporting, and customs declarations apply for physical currency, but no export surrender or exchange control requirement exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); monetary authority, with the Department of Finance Canada responsible for exchange rate policy and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulating federally incorporated banks; no standalone foreign exchange administration agency exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Fintech EMI entities incorporated outside Canada may provide ancillary cross-border payment facilitation, but they cannot accept CAD deposits or operate as deposit-taking institutions in Canada without being licensed as banks or credit unions. Non-resident EMIs providing money transfer, virtual currency dealing, or payment services to Canadian clients are required to register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and are subject to compliance oversight. Onerous MSB registration, Canada's restricted access to domestic clearing infrastructure, and correspondent banking hurdles materially reduce viability relative to tier-1 banks; they are best used as auxiliary collection or payout rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route for a parent/shareholder capital contribution is an inbound SWIFT MT103 wire designated as payment for 'share capital' or 'contributed surplus,' with a clear remittance reference and the BC company name. For CAD settlement into a domestic account, the bank routes via the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or Real-Time Rail (RTR). No Bank of Canada approval is required for foreign direct investment or capital inflows under current Canadian exchange control rules; however, the Investment Canada Act may require post-activity notification if a non-Canadian acquires ownership of a Canadian entity above asset thresholds (generally higher for WTO investors, but commonly only a notification for SaaS development where no cultural/sensitive business is involved). The receiving bank performs FINTRAC reporting for electronic funds transfers and must verify the commercial purpose; accordingly the corporate treasury should prepare the shareholder resolution, subscription agreement, and bank source-of-funds evidence.Trade settlements may be executed via SWIFT MT103 for supplier invoices and recurring software licensing payments; commercial letters of credit (documentary LCs) are used for high-value contractual deliveries requiring documentary control. Canadian banks provide foreign currency disbursement accounts in USD, EUR, GBP and mainstream Asian currencies. No export proceeds surrender timeline applies, and CAD is freely convertible at spot.
