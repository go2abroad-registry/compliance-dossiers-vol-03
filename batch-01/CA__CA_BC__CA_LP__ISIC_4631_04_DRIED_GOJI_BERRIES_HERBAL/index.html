Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Canadian income tax treats the limited partnership as a flow-through entity; each partner reports allocable income. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is treated as a registrable person; BC PST applies separately on domestic taxable supplies.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election for domestic limited partnerships; pass-through classification is mandatory. No hybrid mismatch entity-classification election is available under Canadian federal domestic law. Certain withholding and reporting elections for non-resident partners exist under sections 194 and 216 of the Income Tax Act, but these do not change the entity's fundamental pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the current or preceding four consecutive calendar quarters before registration becomes mandatory; voluntary registration is available for exporters. BC PST: no general registration threshold; a seller, lessor, or vendor making taxable sales or leases in British Columbia must register before the first taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property, such as organic dried goji berries and herbal botanicals, are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the recipient is a non-resident and the property is exported outside Canada by the supplier or the recipient in accordance with Canadian export laws within a reasonable time after the supply. The supplier must retain commercial records demonstrating the export. Complementary export-related services may also be zero-rated when received by a non-resident or supplied in respect of property being exported. BC PST does not apply to goods delivered outside British Columbia.

  • Customs export declaration filed via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or the B13A Export Declaration where prescribed, including the export reference number.
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent air waybill / highway equivalent document signed by the carrier evidencing physical delivery of the dried goji berry products outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT / bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the foreign currency export proceeds into the partnership's Canadian bank account.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government invoice pre-approval regime is currently imposed in Canada. CRA accepts electronic returns and digital records; export declarations must be submitted electronically through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or CARM where applicable. Voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing follows the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard, but no federal API clearance mandate is yet in force.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST return within four years of the filing due date, or without time limit where misrepresentation or fraud is established. False zero-rating claims and fabricated export documentation attract penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the amount claimed, plus gross-negligence penalties, interest, and possible criminal prosecution for wilful evasion. Directors and partners can be jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST under section 323 of the Excise Tax Act. BC tax authorities may reassess PST, charge interest and penalties up to 10% of the unreported or incorrectly claimed PST, and claw back false export exemptions. Exporters are expected to maintain contemporaneous shipping, customs, and foreign currency receipt evidence; failure to repatriate export proceeds substantially within 180 days may trigger heightened audit scrutiny and denial of the zero-rating where the transaction is recharacterized as domestic.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member; the Canadian dollar is freely floating and both current-account and capital-account items are convertible without central-bank approval. Capital injections from non-resident limited partners into a British Columbia limited partnership are unrestricted, provided the LP is properly registered and the general partner is empowered to carry on business in BC. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is effected through periodic distribution; the main hurdle is Part XIII withholding tax at 15% (or reduced treaty rate) levied on distributions exceeding the partner's contributed capital. Passive non-resident limited partners do not acquire control of the Canadian business under the Investment Canada Act where the general partner is Canadian-controlled, so no net-benefit review is triggered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign-currency receivables in domestic or offshore accounts, and no minimum conversion ratio applies. The only obligations are administrative: an export declaration must be filed with the Canada Border Services Agency within 15 days for goods valued above CAD 2,000 under the Reporting of Exported Goods Regulations, and goods included on the Export Control List under the Export and Import Permits Act require an export permit. Organic dried goji berries (HS 0813.40.30) and common herbal botanicals are not on the Export Control List.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); cross-border foreign-exchange administration in Canada is decentralised, with the Department of Finance Canada holding policy authority over the Exchange Control Act and international financial relations, the Canada Border Services Agency enforcing export declarations, and FINTRAC conducting AML/ATF surveillance of cross-border transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for adjunct receivables collection (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer for UK/EU/USD marketplace payments), but Low as a primary treasury or settlement rail. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (Canada), a payment service provider operating for a Canadian LP must be registered with the Bank of Canada; most offshore EMIs hold no Canadian banking licence, lack access to Lynx/ACSS clearing, are not CDIC-insured, and create FINTRAC reporting friction for unlicensed inbound sweeps. The legally compliant pattern is to use EMIs solely for foreign customer collections, with automatic daily sweeps into the LP's Schedule I operational account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined (federal corporate income tax 15% + British Columbia provincial corporate income tax 12%). The Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; income is allocated to partners and taxed at the partner's applicable federal and BC rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII non-resident withholding on taxable dividends paid by Canadian corporations). Treaty relief: Canada-US 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders with at least 10% voting control; 15% for portfolio dividends; most DTAs provide 5%-15%. For a Canadian LP, profit repatriation occurs by allocation to partners; allocated active business income is not subject to Part XIII but is taxed under Part I on the partner's T1/T2 return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 or more in aggregate reportable transactions with any single non-arm's length non-resident in one fiscal year triggers Form T106 filing; contemporaneous Master File and Local File documentation must be prepared and retained by the filing due date for all reportable transactions; record-keeping threshold per transaction is CAD 25,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime (as in UAE/Bermuda); however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA), the sham doctrine, and treaty Limitation on Benefits clauses deny benefits to conduit arrangements lacking genuine economic activity. The LP must maintain real operational substance in British Columbia: physical office, employees, warehouse for dried goji berry inventory, purchase/sale contracts executed in Canada, and management decisions taken in BC. Absent such substance, the CRA may reallocate income to the LP, apply GAAR penalties, or deny treaty benefits for non-resident partners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: s.239(1) Income Tax Act: fine of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; BC provincial penalties mirror federal; CRA gross negligence penalty: 50% of the understated federal and BC tax; statutory interest accrues from the date of assessment.

Director personal liability: The General Partner bears unlimited liability for all LP debts and tax obligations; directors of a corporate General Partner may be assessed under s.227.1 ITA for the LP's unremitted employee source deductions and GST/PST (100% of unremitted amounts plus interest); criminal conviction may result in imprisonment, court-imposed travel restrictions, asset freezes, and civil restitution orders.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: s.247(3) penalty: 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment, capped at the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; GAAR penalty: 25% of the tax benefit; CRA may reallocate the full profit to the Canadian LP and assess arrears interest; failure to file T106 adds CAD 100 per failure up to CAD 7,500.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate General Partner who approve or execute non-arm's-length pricing without defensible comparables may be held personally liable for unremitted corporate obligations and gross-negligence penalties; deliberate falsification of TP records exposes directors to criminal prosecution under s.239 and civil restitution.
Shell Company / Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke GAAR (s.245) to deny the tax benefit, apply a 25% penalty, disregard the LP for tax purposes, and reassess with interest; Canada-US treaty benefits are denied under the Limitation on Benefits clause; the LP risks administrative strike-off and loss of BC registration for continued non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil when the LP is found to be a sham or a bare conduit; the General Partner and its directors become jointly and severally liable for the LP's tax debts and creditor claims; directors may face travel restrictions, worldwide asset freezes (Mareva injunctions), and civil restitution for diverted profits.
AML / FINTRAC Violations

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation (entity) and CAD 500,000 per violation (individual); criminal conviction under the PCMLTFA carries up to 5 years imprisonment; proceeds of crime are subject to civil forfeiture; penalties are assessed per incident, creating cumulative exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors and the General Partner are principals of the reporting entity and face personal fines, imprisonment, and confiscation orders; courts may impose travel restrictions as bail or anti-fleeing conditions in serious money-laundering prosecutions; asset forfeiture actions may reach personal residences and foreign bank accounts of directors.
