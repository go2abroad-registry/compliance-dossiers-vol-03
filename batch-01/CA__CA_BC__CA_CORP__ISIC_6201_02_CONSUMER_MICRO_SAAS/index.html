Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal tax administration; electronic filing via Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html). British Columbia's 7% Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, via the eTaxBC vendor portal.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate as a Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (Corporations Canada online) or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act (BC Corporate Online) - no minimum capital; foreign ownership permitted. (2) Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RC (corporate income tax) and RP (payroll, if employees) through Form RC1 filed with CRA; the T2 corporation tax account is activated at incorporation. (3) GST/HST registration is mandatory once the 'small supplier' threshold (CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the current calendar quarter or preceding four quarters) is exceeded; voluntary registration is available from the first dollar to claim input tax credits and to zero-rate export supplies. (4) For BC PST: register as a PST vendor in eTaxBC before making taxable retail supplies (including SaaS/licensed software) to BC consumers; BC has no PST registration threshold. (5) If Paddle or Lemon Squeezy acts as Merchant of Record (MoR / seller of record), the platform normally collects and remits GST/HST and PST on BC consumer sales; the entity should still maintain its own BN/GST account for direct supplies and document the MoR's principal role in the master services agreement. (6) A foreign-controlled Canadian corporation is taxed as a resident taxable Canadian corporation (ITA s. 89(1)); it is NOT a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), so the CAD 500,000 small business deduction and the reduced BC small business rate are unavailable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election: every incorporated entity is a taxable Canadian corporation taxed on worldwide income (ITA s. 2(1)); the only classification variants are CCPC vs non-CCPC (CCPC requires Canadian-resident control). Available elections relevant to this SaaS vehicle: (a) ETA s. 156 joint election - supplies between closely related registrants deemed made for nil consideration; (b) voluntary GST/HST registration election for small suppliers (ETA s. 240(1)); (c) corporate fiscal year-end election for a non-calendar T2 taxation year (ITA s. 249.1); (d) commercial MoR election appointing Paddle/Lemon Squeezy as principal to discharge consumer tax collection obligations. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4 and 12.28) deny interest/deductions in connection with hybrid mismatch arrangements and provide no elective carve-out; the consumer micro-SaaS model with pure equity funding and no intra-group debt avoids hybrid structures.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current and capital account purposes. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, and there are no general exchange controls, surrender requirements, or Central Bank approval mechanisms for dividends, capital repatriation, or cross-border profit remittance. Dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax (default 25%, potentially reduced under an applicable tax treaty) and must be reported on Form NR4. For capital transactions, the Investment Canada Act imposes a net-benefit review only for acquisitions of control of a Canadian business exceeding statutory thresholds; it does not restrict ordinary profit remittances or equity injections.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Canada. Exporters and cross-border SaaS providers may retain foreign currency in Canadian or offshore accounts without obligation to convert into CAD. All cross-border electronic fund transfers in excess of CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC, and exporters must maintain books and records consistent with the Income Tax Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank); no dedicated foreign exchange administration exists; prudential supervision of banks and clearing systems is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering supervision is conducted by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low Feasibility. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) generally cannot lawfully provide Canadian-dollar clearing or deposit-like payment services to British Columbia residents without registering as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, with the BC Registrar under the Money Services Business Act. Unregistered offshore EMI operations attract heightened scrutiny, often cause bank de-risking, and create legal exposure for the Canadian corporation. A viable route is partnering with a FINTRAC-registered Canadian MSB or using a regulated Canadian Schedule I bank that has an approved fintech banking partnership.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions or shareholder loans must be routed through a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the Canadian corporate operating account. The bank will apply name, reference, and sanctions screening, so the remittance instructions must identify the sending bank, the shareholder as the ordering customer, the Canadian corporation as the beneficiary, and include a clear narration such as 'Shareholder capital contribution pursuant to share subscription' or 'Due shareholder loan re capital structure'. The Payments Canada LVTSE framework applies for high-value domestic transfers once the funds arrive; no FX approval or purpose-code verification is required by the Bank of Canada, but the sending and receiving financial institutions must retain records for FINTRAC's EFT reporting threshold of CAD 10,000. For subscription software revenue collected through Paddle or Lemon Squeezy as merchant of record, payouts are typically consolidated and remitted by the merchant of record to the corporate bank account via SWIFT MT103 or an integrated payment rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods transactions and CAD 500,000 for services transactions with non-residents per year; below these thresholds, documentation is not mandatory but recommended.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits. Transactions lacking commercial rationale or substance may be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties up to 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax under s.227.1 if they failed to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits under GAAR; penalties up to 50% of tax benefit; potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for restitution and face civil fraud claims; travel bans and imprisonment up to 14 years under Criminal Code.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal charges for money laundering up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for AML failures if they knowingly participated; travel bans and asset forfeiture possible.
