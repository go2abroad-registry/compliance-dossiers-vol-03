Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal tax authority; registration, filing and account administration via the My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca. British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through the eTaxBC portal (etaxbc.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian business corporation obtains a single nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or using Business Registration Online, after which it activates the required CRA program accounts on the same BN: RC0001 (corporate income tax), RC15 (GST/HST) and RP0001 (payroll source deductions). The application must attach the BC Certificate of Incorporation or extra-provincial registration certificate issued under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia), state the registered office address in BC, identify directors, and appoint an agent for service where the corporation has no Canadian residence. GST/HST registration is confirmed electronically within 5-10 business days, with the BN letter issued by CRA. BC PST registration is a separate filing via eTaxBC; payroll administration, staffing and employment services are generally not subject to BC PST, so PST registration is only required if the corporation makes PST-taxable sales in BC.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A federal/provincial Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and there is no pass-through or transparent classification for corporate income tax purposes. Because the shareholder is non-resident, the corporation is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) and is therefore taxable at the general corporate rates: federal 15% plus British Columbia provincial 12%, producing a combined 27% headline rate, with no small-business deduction available. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A federal/provincial Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and there is no pass-through or transparent classification for corporate income tax purposes. Because the shareholder is non-resident, the corporation is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) and is therefore taxable at the general corporate rates: federal 15% plus British Columbia provincial 12%, producing a combined 27% headline rate, with no small-business deduction available. Canada subjects resident corporations to tax on worldwide income with foreign tax credits, and BC levies provincial corporate income tax on income attributable to a BC permanent establishment under the federal/BC harmonized allocation rules.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election and a corporation cannot elect pass-through treatment. Statutory elections relevant to a foreign-owned BC staffing/EOR corporation include: (i) the joint venture election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) for GST/HST; (ii) group registration election under ETA subsection 240(1.1) for related registrants; (iii) fiscal-period election under section 249 of the Income Tax Act (ITA); (iv) the section 216 ETA election for non-resident corporations earning Canadian rental income; and (v) permanent-establishment allocation elections between provinces under the federal Income Tax Act and BC Income Tax Act. Canada's anti-hybrid 'Maple Leaf' rules (ITA subsections 12(8.12), 18(4) and 20(4.2)) and section 245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule constrain hybrid-mismatch structures; no hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the GST/HST small-supplier threshold under ETA section 148, measured on worldwide taxable supplies other than zero-rated supplies, financial services and capital property made over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and in any single calendar quarter. Zero-rated export supplies to non-resident clients are excluded from the threshold, so an EOR serving only non-resident clients may technically remain a small supplier; however, voluntary GST/HST registration is standard commercial practice to claim input tax credits on Canadian overheads and to establish the zero-rating of exported payroll services. Once domestic taxable staffing fees exceed CAD 30,000, mandatory registration applies and 5% GST must be charged on all taxable Canadian supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act and Part V of Schedule VI. An EOR/staffing agency may zero-rate fees for payroll administration, HR, recruitment and employer-of-record services supplied to non-resident clients where the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not rendered to an individual situated in Canada. Zero-rating is denied where the non-resident recipient is registered or required to be registered for GST/HST in Canada or carries on a Canadian permanent establishment to which the supply relates. Staffing placements performed by personnel physically present in Canada for the non-resident's Canadian operations are generally taxable at 5% GST. Export of physical deliverables (e.g., HR policy packs, software media) may qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, section 6 if the goods are exported as part of the supply and supported by customs evidence.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration (form B13A / Canadian Export Declaration via the single-window export reporting system) for any physical goods
  • Commercial invoice and executed master services agreement with the non-resident client evidencing the exported payroll/EOR service or deliverable
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or courier waybill evidencing export movement from a Canadian gateway (where goods are shipped)
  • Signed zero-rating attestation referencing Excise Tax Act section 23 and Schedule VI, Part V, together with the CRA vendor file evidencing the non-resident status and non-registration of the client
  • Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent: SWIFT MT103 credit advice or bank receiving statement confirming the foreign-currency invoice proceeds are credited to the corporation's BC bank account within the repatriation benchmark

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, CRA digital stamping, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0) is voluntary for B2B transactions. However, mandatory electronic filing applies to all GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding C$1.5 million, to all non-resident registrants, and to all newly registered commercial registrants; returns must be filed through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or the GST/HST XML API. Registrants must retain books, records, invoices and payroll source-deduction documents for six years in readable electronic or paper format, and CRA may require the mapping of payroll/general-ledger data to GST/HST reporting under ETA section 286.

CRA may reverse zero-rated treatment on audit, reassess within the normal four-year reassessment period (unbounded where misrepresentation is fraudulent or grossly negligent), and impose: (i) a penalty of C$8,000 or 50% of the understated GST/HST or overstated refund under ETA section 285 for false statements or omissions; (ii) gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the deficiency; (iii) daily compound interest under ETA section 280; and (iv) clawback of refunded GST/HST with rescission of input tax credits claimed in respect of the disputed export supply. Where payroll source deductions are involved, Income Tax Act section 227.1 director's liability applies, and a fraudulent zero-rating or refund scheme may attract criminal liability under the Criminal Code of Canada as well as sanctions under the Tax Rebate Discounting and Fee Prohibition Rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with a fully convertible Canadian dollar. No exchange controls, capital movement restrictions, or central bank approval requirements apply to current or capital account transactions. Inbound equity injections, cross-border loan repayments, dividend distributions, and profit remittances are freely executable; the only structural hurdle is Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, which imposes a 25% statutory non-resident withholding tax on dividends and certain payments, generally reduced to 5% to 15% under an applicable tax treaty (including the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement). There is no repatriation waiting period, no investment-conversion requirement, and no restriction on maintaining foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds or cross-border services receipts. EOR payroll collections and service fees invoiced in foreign currency may be retained in USD/EUR operating accounts or converted to CAD at commercial discretion. The Bank of Canada does not intervene to require conversion of export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federal prudential supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT administration; provincially, the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) regulates non-bank financial services companies in British Columbia.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise (formerly TransferWise) and Payoneer are FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) operating legally in Canada. Feasibility rating: High as a supplemental payout rail for low- to mid-value cross-border contractor remittances; Medium-Low as a primary treasury counterparty. Legal constraints: MSB funds are not CDIC-insured, EMIs are not licensed banks under the Bank Act, and user funds must be segregated. An EOR platform must avoid unlicensed payment-intermediation by keeping client payroll float and fiduciary cash in a Tier 1 Canadian depository institution; the EMI integration should be limited to the payout layer for small contractor disbursements where local clearing rails offer superior speed and cost.
