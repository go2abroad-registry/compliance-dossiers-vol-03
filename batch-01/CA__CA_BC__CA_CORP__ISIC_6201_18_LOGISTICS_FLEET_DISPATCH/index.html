Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administration; national web portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and CRA non-resident digital services registration portal; British Columbia provincial income tax administered by CRA under the Tax Collection Agreement; BC PST and Employer Health Tax administered by the BC Ministry of Finance (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes).

E-government filing portal

The national statutory taxpayer identifier is the nine-digit Business Number (BN), issued by the CRA and used for GST/HST (RT account), corporation income tax (RC account), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM/RE). For a federal or extra-provincially registered BC Business Corporation, the procedure is: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act and file an extra-provincial registration with the BC Registrar of Companies to obtain a corporate registry number; (2) complete Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or register online through the CRA's Business Registration Online service; (3) request the GST/HST RT account with the effective date aligned to the commencement of Canadian commercial activity; (4) for non-resident controlling entities with no Canadian fixed place of business, use the CRA simplified GST/HST digital-services registration route after crossing the CAD 30,000 Canadian-source threshold; (5) receive the BN confirmation letter and maintain supporting records including certificate of incorporation, registered office address, officers/directors identification, and evidence of Canadian business nexus. Online registration is encouraged because CRA real-time validation typically issues the BN and RT account within one to five business days, while paper Form RC1 processing can take six to eight weeks. The BN must be quoted on all GST/HST invoices, customs declarations, payroll remittances, and corporate income tax returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian federal/provincial Business Corporation is a separate legal person deemed resident in Canada under subsection 250(4) of the Income Tax Act and is subject to entity-level federal Part I tax and BC provincial corporate income tax. It is not a pass-through vehicle; no check-the-box or partnership election is available under Canadian law. The combined headline rate on active business income is 27% (federal general rate 15% after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction, plus BC general corporate rate 12%). Because the entity is foreign-controlled, it is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation and cannot access the small business deduction. Canada employs worldwide taxation for resident corporations, with relief through foreign tax credits and an exempt-surplus territorial regime for active foreign affiliate business income under subsection 113(1). No tax-exempt zone applies to this software/logistics business; available incentives are limited to federal SR&ED expenditure credits and the BC SR&ED provincial tax credit.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box pass-through classification for federal or provincial business corporations; corporate status is mandatory and non-elective for tax purposes. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) subsection 85(1) rollover election permitting a tax-deferred transfer of property to the corporation in exchange for shares or debt; (ii) subsection 85(2) election for property received by a corporation; (iii) subsection 88(2) winding-up election for amalgamations of a Canadian subsidiary into its Canadian parent; (iv) subsection 216(1) net-basis election for non-residents deriving Canadian rental or timber royalties; (v) subsection 39(5) joint election for non-resident vendors disposing of taxable Canadian property; and (vi) GST/HST elections prescribed under the Excise Tax Act, including stream-lined registration and section 24.1 elections for charities and public-sector bodies. For hybrid mismatch arrangements, Canada enacted Part II.1 of the Income Tax Act effective for taxation years beginning after June 30, 2022, requiring automatic set-off and deduction-denial adjustments where Canadian entities are parties to hybrid mismatch outcomes; there is no elective exclusion or check-the-box avoidance mechanism under Canadian federal law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — under subsection 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act, a person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when total consideration for taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) exceeds CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-resident suppliers of digital services, the mandatory simplified GST/HST registration threshold is CAD 30,000 in Canadian-source taxable consideration over a 12-month period.

Canada has no mandatory national e-invoicing platform, no real-time fiscal clearance, and no digital stamping or API-based pre-clearance requirement. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices, paper or electronic, containing prescribed information: supplier legal name and Business Number, date of issue, description of the supply, GST/HST amount charged or a notation that the supply is zero-rated, and the total consideration. Electronic record-keeping is permitted under the CRA's Information Circular IC03-1, and records must be readable and available on demand in Canada. The Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing specification (PEPPOL) is voluntary and used mainly for public-sector and B2B interoperability; it does not constitute tax clearance. For BC PST, separate vendor record-keeping and exemption-certificate retention rules apply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility with no foreign exchange controls. Canadian dollar (CAD) and foreign currency accounts are freely permitted. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and royalty payments are unrestricted, subject to Part XIII withholding tax for non-residents and, at the federal level, the Investment Canada Act for large foreign investments and reviewable transactions. Exported SaaS and telematics services are zero-rated for GST/HST purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation deadline applies to export receipts. A registered exporter may retain proceeds in any currency. FINTRAC reporting is required for physical cross-border currency flows or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000, and for large virtual currency transactions on a record-keeping basis. GST/HST zero-rating applies to exported services regardless of timing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority and lender of last resort); Department of Finance Canada (national economic policy); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border transaction monitoring under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate to High for ancillary cross-border payments only. Retail Payment Service Providers (including electronic money institutions such as Wise for Business, Payoneer, or Airwallex) are subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) framework administered by the Bank of Canada, with mandatory registration and risk management requirements. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% aggregate federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 12% provincial)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, unless reduced by an income tax treaty (0% / 5% / 15% typical for arm's-length portfolio and direct shareholdings)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File for taxation years beginning after December 31, 2020, where the Canadian taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million, or the aggregate value of controlled cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 5 million for goods or CAD 1 million for services; for smaller entities the CRA applies an administrative expectation to prepare reasonable documentation for transactions above CAD 1 million to avoid penalty exposure

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to UAE or Barbados; however economic substance is a decisive factor in the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the transfer-pricing recharacterization rules in section 247, the determination of corporate residence under subsection 250(4), and the 'benefit' analysis for treaty access; a logistics-tech SaaS entity carrying out functions (software development, fleet telematics algorithm maintenance, contractual risk) in British Columbia must demonstrate that its directors and decision-makers exercise real management and control in Canada; substance deficiency can result in recharacterization of income as borne by a foreign agent or trust, triggering Part XIII withholding and GAAR penalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239 criminal conviction: fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the misreported amount; CRA can also levy a stop-filing order and require immediate payment of the entire disputed tax; failure to remit source withholding of 25% on a dividend can trigger assessment under Part XIII plus interest and prohibitive late-payment charges

Director personal liability: Under section 227.1 ITA, directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes and GST/HST owed by the corporation if they did not exercise due diligence; criminal tax evasion prosecutions may result in personal fines, restitution orders, prohibition from serving as director, and actual imprisonment for up to 5 years; an order can also impose travel bans seizing passports pending trial and freeze personal bank accounts under proceeds-of-crime rules
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Subsection 247(15) penalty: 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment amount where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 1 million or 10% of the taxpayer's revenue; additional GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit if the arrangement lacks bona fide business purpose; failure to provide documentation after a request within 90 days triggers a penalty equal to 25% of the ‘reasonableness’ amount (a régime of CAD 8,000 per month, capped at CAD 96,000? [see subsection 247(15) and ICD 96-41])

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the T2 return without reasonable assurance that transfer-pricing data is accurate may face gross-negligence penalty under s.163; in flagrant cases, directors are also liable for the corporation’s tax debt through director’s liability extensions under provincial securities and occupational safety legislation, and can be subject to civil restitution orders for lost tax revenue owed to the Crown
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s.245 nullifies the tax benefit and imposes a 25% penalty of the disallowed tax benefit; treaty-shopping arrangements may be disallowed under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in the OECD Multilateral Instrument with a resulting unexpected 25% Part XIII withholding; CRA can retroactively reassess up to 10 years under s.152(4) for willful omission or false representation

Director personal liability: Directors of an offshore conduit engaged in aggressive tax-avoidance can be assessed as non-resident owners of a ‘controlled foreign affiliate’ under s.17 (FAPI provisions), triggering personal income inclusion in Canada; a director who actively participates in the sham may be civilly liable for restitution, incur an overseas travel ban under the Criminal Code s.462.332 money-laundering provisions, and face director’s residence restrictions under the Canada Business Corporations Act
Unpaid Source Deductions / AML

Statutory sanction: Section 227.1 liability equal to the full unremitted amount plus interest; Excise Tax Act s.327(2) for GST/HST evasion: fine up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to 5 years; AML failure under PCMLTFA can trigger administrative penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 for certain entities and reputational remediation orders; CRA can freeze the corporation's bank accounts and issue a 'non-compliance' bulletin to other financial institutions

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST; where failing to act prudently, a director can be held personally responsible for the full amount of the corporation’s withholding debts, and a court can issue an order requiring the director to surrender their passport for 21 days pending investigation, with a further ban of up to two years upon conviction under the Criminal Code provisions respecting fraud and public money theft
