Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A BC business corporation must maintain a registered office and a records office in British Columbia. A mailbox-only virtual address does not satisfy the statutory requirement. A virtual office provided by a licensed BC corporate services agent that maintains a physical street address in BC, has personnel able to receive service of process, and provides registered-office and records-office services is generally accepted by the BC Registry. For the AI/LLM API platform, a virtual office is permissible for corporate registration purposes, but it does not create Canadian tax residence or commercial substance; Canada Revenue Agency will look to central management and control, board meetings, personnel, and substantive business activities when determining tax residence and whether the entity has a permanent establishment in BC.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online portal and CRA My Business Account

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on taxable GST/HST activity in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) with an RT program account. Registration is initiated via Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), through Business Registration Online, or by calling the CRA non-resident GST/HST registration line. The foreign entity must provide its constitutional documents, foreign tax identification number, Canadian address or authorized representative/local agent, and details of anticipated Canadian taxable supplies. CRA issues a 9-digit BN and the separate 2-letter/digit program account for GST/HST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification system. A business corporation is always an entity-level taxpayer subject to federal Part I tax and provincial corporate income tax. For foreign legal entities, CRA applies a domestic-law characterization test, not elective classification. Relevant statutory elections include the section 216(1) election for non-resident lessors, capital cost allowance elections, and the hybrid-mismatch rules under sections 18.2 and 18.8 which disallow excessive interest payments in cross-border arrangements and operate as an anti-avoidance election-limitation regime. No pass-through classification is available for a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter, or CAD 50,000 in the last four consecutive calendar quarters of total taxable worldwide supplies after intercompany sales reduction; BC PST registration is required before making taxable retail sales in British Columbia with no general small-supplier exemption

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rated exports under section 21 of the Excise Tax Act apply where the exporter satisfies CRA that the goods were delivered to a destination outside Canada and exported as part of a sale. For services, section 22 zero-rates a supply made to a non-resident person unless the supply is made to a non-resident who is registered for GST/HST or the service relates to real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. AI/LLM API platform services supplied to foreign API customers are generally zero-rated if the contract, payment, and place of use are outside Canada; the supplier must retain documentary evidence proving non-resident recipient status and off-Canada consumption.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) for physical goods exported from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and non-resident service agreement demonstrating the place of supply
  • Bill of Lading or other shipping/carrier evidence for tangible property exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank payment advice, SWIFT/transfer records proving inflow of foreign currency
  • Technical usage logs and IP-address evidence for AI API platform services consumed outside Canada

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API digital stamping, or PEPPOL e-invoicing mandate. CRA accepts electronic invoices; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically for registrants with annual taxable turnover over CAD 1,500,000, and certain registrants must use GST/HST NETFILE. In BC, PST vendors maintain their own records but there is no fiscal clearance API requirement.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims with heightened scrutiny on AI, digital, and cross-border API arrangements. If the 0% GST/HST rate is applied incorrectly, CRA may assess uncollected GST/HST plus interest and civil penalties. Fraudulent export transactions attract gross negligence penalties up to 30% of the assessed amount, false-statement penalties, prosecution, and a general four-year reassessment window extended to ten years in cases of neglect or misrepresentation. Failure to retain required export documentation destroys the zero-rating justification and triggers immediate clawback of input tax credits and refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility with zero exchange controls; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951. There are no restrictions on dividend remittances, interest payments, equity liquidation proceeds, or capital repatriation. Non-resident shareholders of a British Columbia corporation receiving dividends are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at a 25% statutory rate, generally reduced to 5–15% under an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada–US Treaty). The Canadian dollar is freely deliverable on global markets; no government approval is required for payment routing, foreign-currency account maintenance, or profit repatriation. The only administrative gate for a non-resident founder establishing a BC operating company is the Investment Canada Act notification for new Canadian business establishment, which is a filing requirement rather than an FX or capital-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export receipts. Any entity may retain USD, EUR, CNY, or other foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting (record-keeping and transmission of EFT reports), but this is a transparency measure, not a conversion or surrender obligation. No BC-specific invoicing or export-license rules apply to software/API exports.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary and financial-system authority; foreign-exchange policy operates through a free-floating regime rather than administrative controls. Currency issuance and exchange-rate framework rest on the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52). Prudential supervision of banks is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); payments-system oversight is shared between the Bank of Canada and Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for payment collection and FX intermediation layers; MEDIUM as a full treasury-banking substitute. Legal constraints: (1) Canadian fintech EMIs — e.g., Wise Canada, an MSB registered with FINTRAC, and Stripe Canada as a payment facilitator — are not deposit-taking institutions; CRA payroll program accounts, Part XIII withholding settlement, and corporate tax payments must be made through a Schedule I chartered bank account; (2) if the BC entity itself accepts USDC as customer payment, it likely triggers MSB registration under the PCMLTFA's 'dealing in virtual currency' provisions unless the collection and conversion are contractually performed by a registered MSB payment processor; (3) the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) imposes registration on the payment service provider, but the merchant must demonstrate contractual and operational segregation of client funds to the Bank of Canada; (4) treasury risk limits — fintech rails should be capped below 30% of monthly FX flows to maintain banker confidence and liquidity redundancy.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate rate (federal 15% + British Columbia provincial 12%); 11% combined small-business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income for Canadian-controlled private corporations (federal 9% + BC 2%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; withholding is federally administered with no additional BC provincial layer

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory registration threshold for transfer-pricing documentation, but contemporaneous records are mandatory under ITA s.247(4) and Information Circular IC87-2R; Form T106 must be filed where the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length transactions with any one non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the fiscal year. Country-by-country reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 789,000,000. The s.247(3) adjustment penalty applies where the upward international transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s.245 denies tax benefits from avoidance transactions lacking genuine economic substance. ITA s.247, IC87-2R, and CRA administrative policy require a documented functional and risk analysis evidencing that the Canadian entity has the people, assets, and decision-making authority to support its intercompany profits. For an AI/LLM API platform, the Canadian corporation must own the customer contracts, employ the developers and risk functions, retain pricing discretion, and pay arm's-length royalties for any foreign-resident licensed IP. A hollow Canadian cash-box that passively routes USDC/token revenue to a foreign parent while claiming SR&ED credits and treaty benefits will be recharacterized under GAAR, the sham doctrine, and transfer-pricing anti-abuse rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ITA s.239(1): fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment up to five years, and full restitution of evaded tax plus interest; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) equal to 25% of the understated tax, stacked on the 5%/1% late-filing penalty regime

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or participated in false filings face personal assessment under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted Part XIII withholding and source deductions notwithstanding corporate insolvency; criminal proceedings permit asset freezes, forfeiture orders, bail conditions restricting travel, and director-veil-piercing where corporate funds were diverted
Transfer Pricing and Foreign Information Return Failures

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment penalty under ITA s.247(3): 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; T106 late-filing penalties of CAD 100 per day per form up to CAD 2,500, accumulating until corrected; additional 25% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2); interest on reassessed amounts runs from the original filing due date

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically exposed for corporate transfer-pricing penalties, but personal exposure arises where they knowingly authorize false documentation, breach fiduciary duties by diverting IP value, or participate in wilful collusion with a foreign parent, triggering criminal s.239 charges and director-level restitution orders
Shell Company, Sham, GAAR, and AML (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial under ITA s.245 recharacterizing the arrangement, disallowing deductions and treaty benefits; sham doctrine reassessments; administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA up to CAD 500,000 for a non-registered money services business dealing in virtual currency; potential strike-off under the BC Business Corporations Act for abandonment; Criminal Code laundering charges carrying up to ten years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham or shell corporation risk veil-piercing where the corporation acts as a mere agent of a foreign principal, personal liability for corporate income tax and GST/HST under ITA s.227.1, statutory liability under the BC Business Corporations Act for false corporate records, and personal AML penalties where compliance functions were under their control; egregious cases support director disqualification orders and travel watch-listing where outstanding criminal warrants are issued for evasion or money laundering
