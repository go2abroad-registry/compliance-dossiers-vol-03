Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and income tax; British Columbia Ministry of Finance for British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST). Primary portals: CRA Business Registration via canada.ca; BC eTaxBC and BC OneStop Corporate Online.

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnerships establishing a Canadian branch or permanent establishment must: (1) register the extraprovincial limited partnership with the BC Corporate Registry under the Partnership Act, obtaining a BC registration number; (2) apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online, indicating the need for GST/HST and Import/Export account; (3) create a BC PST account through eTaxBC before making first taxable sale in BC; (4) if the LP has specified non-resident partners, submit any required partnership information return (Form T5013) and obtain a CRA trust/partnership account; (5) appoint a deemed resident attorney or streamed representative if no single office in Canada, consistent with section 128.1 of the Income Tax Act.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign limited partnerships establishing a Canadian branch or permanent establishment must: (1) register the extraprovincial limited partnership with the BC Corporate Registry under the Partnership Act, obtaining a BC registration number; (2) apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online, indicating the need for GST/HST and Import/Export account; (3) create a BC PST account through eTaxBC before making first taxable sale in BC; (4) if the LP has specified non-resident partners, submit any required partnership information return (Form T5013) and obtain a CRA trust/partnership account; (5) appoint a deemed resident attorney or streamed representative if no single office in Canada, consistent with section 128.1 of the Income Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian tax law permits partnership classification elections: filing of partnership return and selection of fiscal period under subsection 99(2) of the Income Tax Act; making an election under subsection 216(1) or 216(4) for non-resident partners to file a separate joint tax return on Canadian-source income; an election under subsection 97(2) to transfer property to the partnership on a rollover basis; GST/HST election under subsection 273 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related registrants to charge no GST/HST; and an election under section 156 of the Income Tax Act for concurrent corporate taxation of a fixed-share partnership. British Columbia PST does not provide a check-the-box election; registration is mandatory once taxable activity in BC commences.

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for federal GST/HST (small-supplier threshold over four consecutive calendar quarters); no monetary threshold for British Columbia PST registration when taxable goods or services are sold at a location in BC or supplied to a customer present in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of stevedoring and cargo-handling services at a BC bulk terminal may be zero-rated for GST/HST where the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient or provided directly in respect of goods that are being exported, provided the supplier maintains contemporaneous evidence that the goods have been or will be delivered outside Canada within a reasonable period (generally 150 days for consumable goods or as shown on export documents). Tax counsel should confirm whether the services qualify as a zero-rated supply under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act rather than a taxable domestic handling service.

  • Completed service agreement or stevedoring contract

No statutory real-time electronic invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or government API fiscal receipt regime is in force in Canada as of the current date. The CRA accepts electronic invoices under the electronic records requirements (section 88 of the Excise Tax Act); BC PST requires that each invoice separately set out the PST amount charged or, where appropriate, the 'PST included' statement with the vendor's registration number.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST registrant within the normal four-year limitation period and without limitation if false statements or misrepresentations are made. Penalties for fraudulent export zero-rating include a 25% gross-negligence penalty under subsection 285.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act, compound interest, revocation of GST/HST and PST registration, and criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, together with civil fraud or aiding-and-abetting charges under the Criminal Code. BC PST audits may assess 25% penalty plus arrears interest for suppression or misclassification; failure to remit may lead to certificate of lien and collection enforcement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada provides full current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and a fully open capital account; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no exchange-control statutes, repatriation quotas, profit-remittance permits, or currency-conversion approvals exist today. The CAD floats freely and USD/CAD is the deepest traded Americas cross after EUR/USD; a British Columbia limited partnership may receive foreign capital, hold CAD/USD/EUR multi-currency accounts, and remit earnings to non-resident partners via SWIFT without central-bank authorization. Real-world profit-remittance hurdles are limited to non-FX compliance obligations: Part XIII withholding tax on certain distributions and deemed income of non-resident partners under the Income Tax Act; FINTRAC reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; OSFI correspondent-bank de-risking pressure; and Global Affairs Canada sanctions screening for any cargo, vessel, or counterparty nexus with prohibited jurisdictions. Residual transfer risk for CAD is negligible given Canada's AAA sovereign profile.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies anywhere in Canada. A B.C. bulk-stevedoring terminal is free to hold USD export receivables onshore in a domestic Canadian USD clearing account or offshore, indefinitely, and may convert to CAD at commercial-bank dealing rates. The only binding statutory interfaces are: (i) FINTRAC declarations for cross-border movement of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) CRA reporting of worldwide business income converted to CAD using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate; (iii) sanctions compliance — proceeds from or involving listed persons, vessels, or jurisdictions must be blocked and reported; and (iv) Port Authority tariff settlement rules under the Canada Marine Act, which do not impose FX conversion requirements. Export Development Canada offers trade credit and political-risk insurance but mandates no conversion of proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority and retail-payment-system oversight under the Retail Payment Activities Act; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) — AML/ATF regulator and reporting centre under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — prudential regulator of federally chartered banks and foreign-bank branches; Global Affairs Canada — administrator of economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, and the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act; Department of Finance Canada — policy lead on the Bank Act, the Currency Act, and exchange-fund arrangements. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency; the Bank of Canada floats the CAD and intervenes only to maintain orderly markets.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for treasury concentration and capital custody; MEDIUM only as supplementary low-value collection rails. Canada does not recognize the EU/UK electronic-money institution (EMI) licence class; an unlicensed foreign EMI cannot lawfully accept deposits, hold partnership capital, or issue e-money balances, and doing so violates the Bank Act deposit-taking prohibition and exposes the LP to OSFI enforcement and FINTRAC penalties. Canadian payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (registration regime in force since late 2024) and, where engaged in money-services business, with FINTRAC; however, such registrants are not banks and offer no deposit insurance. The compliant architecture is to concentrate all capital and treasury liquidity exclusively with Schedule I banks and to employ registered PSPs only for ancillary collections such as demurrage, gate fees, and small port tariffs, with mandatory daily sweeps into the bank operating account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Canada: 15% federal general corporate rate; British Columbia: 12% provincial general corporate rate; combined statutory headline rate 27%. The Limited Partnership itself is generally a pass-through entity and is not subject to tax; income is allocated to partners and taxed at their respective rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. For a Limited Partnership, distributions of Canadian-source partnership income to non-resident partners are generally subject to this rate on the non-resident partner's allocated share.

Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid amount plus 1% per month for up to 12 months. Repeated failure on assessment may trigger 10% penalty. Gross negligence penalty is 50% of understated tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation and T106 filing are triggered when the aggregate value of transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceeds CA$1,000,000 annually. Country-by-country reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue above CA$750,000,000. Consistent with CRA administrative practice, Master File and Local File documentation is expected for large taxpayers with reportable cross-border transactions.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate standalone Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, the Canada Revenue Agency applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Income Tax Act s.245, and economic substance is a central factor in determining whether an arrangement avoids tax in a manner contrary to the object, spirit, and purpose of the statute. A BC limited partnership must demonstrate genuine business purpose and actual stevedoring operations to preserve pass-through treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239, fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, plus gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes; courts may impose travel bans, seizure orders, and personal bankruptcy orders for unpaid assessments.
Transfer Pricing Mis-Application

Statutory sanction: CRA adjustments to income, 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the adjustment amount, plus interest and compound interest. Criminal sanctions possible for fraudulent misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved or participated in false transfer-pricing documentation may be assessed for penalties and held personally liable under the corporate general partner structure.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under GAAR, deny the pass-through allocation, impose gross negligence penalties, and the BC Registrar of Companies may administratively dissolve or strike the entity for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling unitholders can face personal liability for the full amount of taxes, interest, and penalties where corporate veil is pierced; criminal fraud charges under Criminal Code s.380 are possible.
AML / Sanctions Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CA$1,000,000 per violation, prosecution under the Criminal Code and Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and risk of losing port terminal licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers may be subject to personal fines, prohibition orders, and imprisonment up to 14 years for involvement in laundering proceeds of crime through the terminal.
Profit Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is liable for the full unpaid amount plus interest, a 20% recalcitrant penalties under s.227.2, and criminal prosecution on repeated offences.

Director personal liability: Directors of the withholding corporate partner are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII amounts under ITA s.227.1; tax authorities may issue freeze or garnishment of personal bank accounts and travel prohibition orders.
