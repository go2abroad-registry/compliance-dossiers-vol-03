Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) for federal income tax and GST/HST; Business Registration Online portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Programs Branch for PST; BC eTaxBC portal at https://etax.gov.bc.ca; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export reporting infrastructure and customs enforcement.

E-government filing portal

A British Columbia limited partnership engaged in cross-border battery-metals trading must obtain federal tax identity through the CRA Business Registration Online process: (1) register the partnership in its own name to obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN); (2) open the applicable CRA program accounts, including the RC4601 non-resident income tax account for foreign-owned partnerships and the RT registrant account for GST/HST; (3) determine mandatory GST/HST registration under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act, which requires registration when the LP carries on business in Canada or when the LP's worldwide taxable supplies (excluding financial services, capital property, and goodwill) exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold over any four consecutive calendar quarters. If the LP's only Canadian supplies are zero-rated exports and it does not carry on business in Canada, registration remains voluntary but is commercially essential to claim input tax credits; (4) file a T5013 Partnership Information Return for each fiscal period, disclosing each partner's allocation and, for a non-resident partner, the applicable Part I and Part XIII withholding position; (5) register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST via eTaxBC before the first taxable sale of tangible personal property in British Columbia, since no small-supplier exemption applies to PST; and (6) maintain a registered address for service in BC pursuant to the BC Partnership Act, with non-resident partners providing a non-Canadian address and a BC-based agent where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under ITA section 96, a BC LP is fiscally transparent and is not a taxpayer for federal income tax purposes; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of partnership income or loss on the partnership's fiscal period basis, with a deemed year-end for non-resident partners unless an alternate fiscal period is elected. For GST/HST, the LP is a separate registrable person unless a subsection 273(1) Excise Tax Act election is filed; for BC PST, the LP is itself the vendor liable for collection and remittance. Territorial scope: the non-resident partners are subject to Canadian Part I tax only on Canadian-source income attributable to the LP's Canadian business, subject to income tax treaty relief, while exported goods are outside the BC PST base and zero-rated under the GST/HST.

Available election forms

(a) Subsection 273(1) ETA partnership election on Form GST25, whereby the LP and all members jointly elect that the partnership is not a separate person for GST/HST, rendering the partners jointly and severally liable for registration, net tax, and input tax credit adjustments; (b) subsection 273.1(1) ETA large-partnership election allowing eligible large partnerships to have each member file and remit GST/HST separately; (c) hybrid entity relief under Article IV(6)(c) of the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention (Fifth Protocol), permitting a U.S. member of a Canadian LP to be treated as fiscally transparent where the LP is a partnership under Canadian law and the member is a U.S. limited liability company or limited partnership; documentation is provided through Form W-8BEN-E and treaty position statements; (d) ITA section 102 Canadian-partnership designation, which is automatically unavailable where a non-resident partner is admitted, unless the partnership qualifies under the specified exceptions, with consequential Part XIII withholding consequences; (e) no general check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime exists under Canadian domestic income tax law; hybrid mismatch countermeasures under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3, including the excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL), apply at the partner level and cannot be affirmatively elected out; and (f) BC PST offers no election for export sales, but a vendor may apply for a PST direct-export exemption certificate and must maintain prescribed exemption records.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, calculated on the aggregate value of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies but excluding financial services, capital property, and goodwill, made in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding the threshold compels registration, and a non-resident that carries on business in Canada or makes any taxable supply in Canada other than zero-rated supplies must register regardless of the CAD 30,000 test. A non-resident whose only Canadian supplies are zero-rated and who does not carry on business in Canada may remain unregistered but should register voluntarily to recover input tax credits. BC PST: no monetary registration threshold; every vendor, including a foreign or extraprovincial vendor, must register with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC before making its first PST-taxable sale of tangible personal property in BC, subject to the remote-vendor rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 144 and Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, the LP may zero-rate the export of battery-grade lithium carbonate (HS 2836.91) where: (1) the supply is made to a non-resident person, meaning a purchaser who is not resident in Canada and, if a corporation, is not incorporated under Canadian law; (2) the registrant exports the goods from Canada in a continuous journey out of Canada, using a common carrier with a destination outside Canada stated in the transport document; (3) the goods are physically removed from Canada within a reasonable time after the sale, with the CRA administrative benchmark of approximately 180 days applied for continuous-journey purposes; (4) for sales to a related foreign buyer, the transaction is bona fide, at arm's-length pricing, and accompanied by evidence of actual movement of the lithium carbonate across the border; (5) the registrant assembles and retains complete proof of export in accordance with CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4.6.1, covering transport documents, customs documents, and commercial evidence; and (6) any ancillary logistics, brokerage, or trading services rendered to the non-resident qualify as zero-rated offshore services under Part VII of Schedule VI. For BC PST, a sale is exempt where the goods are delivered outside British Columbia by the vendor's own vehicle or by a common carrier under an export sale evidenced by shipping records.

  • CBSA B13A / Canadian Export Declaration filed electronically through the G7 Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Commercial invoice showing HS 2836.91 classification, INCOTERM (FOB, CIF, or DAP), and express endorsement of export sale to a non-resident
  • Bill of lading for ocean carriage or air waybill for air carriage showing the destination outside Canada and continuous journey movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire transfer / letter of credit documents evidencing settlement in foreign currency from the non-resident purchaser
  • BC PST export exemption certificate or equivalent record evidencing direct shipment out of British Columbia
  • Consolidated zero-rated export evidence file per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4.6.1, cross-referencing transport, customs, and commercial documentation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC). The CRA accepts paper and electronic invoices in EDI, PDF, or XML format, and section 223 of the Excise Tax Act requires a registrant to issue an invoice for each taxable supply containing the vendor's name, business number and GST/HST registration number, the date, a description of the goods or services, the consideration, and the tax amount or a zero-rated designation. Registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or EDI. The Pan-Canadian e-invoice standard based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0 has been implemented voluntarily through the Ocean digital exchange network, but adoption is optional and no Canadian authority operates a pre-clearance or stamping API. Exporters must submit electronic export declarations through the CBSA G7 eManifest / Canadian Export Reporting System, and BC PST vendors must retain export exemption evidence and shipping records supporting the removal of goods from BC.

The CRA treats zero-rated export claims as a high-risk audit area. Where the documentary bundle is deficient or the continuous-journey requirement is not met, the CRA may recharacterize the supply as taxable at 5% GST and, if the goods were consumed in BC, the BC PST becomes payable, with the following consequences: (i) reassessment of the GST/HST net tax and denial of associated input tax credits; (ii) arrears interest at the CRA prescribed rate, currently 8% per annum for Q4 2025, compounded daily under section 279 of the Excise Tax Act; (iii) a false-statement penalty under section 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax that would have been payable, where a false statement or omission is made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; (iv) a gross-negligence penalty of 20% of the tax sought to be evaded under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act; (v) criminal prosecution for tax evasion under sections 280 and 281 of the Excise Tax Act and section 239 of the Income Tax Act, with fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years; (vi) indefinite reassessment periods under section 298(4) of the Excise Tax Act and subsection 152(4) of the Income Tax Act where misrepresentation is attributable to carelessness, negligence, wilful default, or fraud; (vii) application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and transfer-pricing adjustments under section 247 where the arbitrage price, circular cash flow, or related-party terms are non-commercial, with retroactive clawback to the original filing date; and (viii) late-filing penalties for the T5013 partnership return of CAD 25 per day, minimum CAD 100 and maximum CAD 2,500, together with heightened audit scrutiny on recurring export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency under a floating exchange-rate regime. There are no exchange controls under the Currency Act or the former Foreign Exchange Control Act. Current-account and capital-account transactions are freely permitted, subject to tax compliance (e.g., NR4 withholding for non-resident partners) and FINTRAC reporting. Profit remittances are unrestricted but may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose statutory surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in any foreign currency or repatriated freely. Only FINTRAC large-transaction reporting ($10,000+) and customary banking monitoring apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI))

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Moderate. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) may operate in Canada under money-services-business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC, but they cannot act as deposit-taking institutions. Legal constraints include the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, the Bank Act (Section 529 – unauthorized banking), and BC's Financial Institutions Act. For an LP with commodity-trade exposure, direct banking with a Tier-1 Schedule I bank is advised; EMIs may serve as ancillary payment corridors, not primary operational accounts.
