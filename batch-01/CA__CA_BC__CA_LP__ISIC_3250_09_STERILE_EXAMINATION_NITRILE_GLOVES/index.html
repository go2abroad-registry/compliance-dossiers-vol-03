Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada generally imposes no approval requirement, quota, mandatory conversion, or repatriation restriction for foreign investment, intercompany funding, dividend payments, loan repayments, or trade receipts. Canadian banks may require source-of-funds, beneficial-ownership, sanctions, tax-residency, and transaction-purpose evidence. Payments to non-residents remain subject to applicable Canadian withholding tax, treaty relief conditions, partnership allocation rules, transfer-pricing requirements, and lender or investor contractual restrictions. A British Columbia limited partnership should document capital contributions, partner identities, allocation mechanics, foreign ownership, and any general-partner authority before accepting material cross-border funding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or general deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into Canadian dollars. Exporters may retain foreign-currency balances or convert receipts at commercially agreed rates. Banks may request invoices, customs records, bills of lading, contracts, and end-use information for compliance and transaction monitoring. Foreign-currency proceeds should be reconciled to sales ledgers, receivables, customs documentation, and tax records.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general exchange controls. Financial institutions and money-services businesses are subject to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and regulations administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection, foreign-exchange execution, and treasury administration; Low as a substitute for a Canadian primary operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution cannot automatically provide a Canadian deposit account, Canadian payment access, or Canadian regulatory status. Use only a provider that is appropriately licensed in its home jurisdiction and transparently identifies the regulated entity, safeguarding model, settlement bank, permitted services, sanctions controls, and insolvency treatment. Confirm whether funds are safeguarded e-money or bank deposits, whether Canadian residents are served lawfully, and whether the provider reports or supports required Canadian tax and AML records. Canadian banks may reject or delay funds originating from opaque fintech wallets, nested payment providers, personal accounts, mixers, or high-risk jurisdictions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Unclear limited-partnership ownership, unidentified limited partners, unexplained nominee arrangements, or inability to identify the general partner and ultimate controlling persons

Defense evidence: Certified British Columbia partnership registration and certificate; limited-partnership agreement; amendments and partner register; general-partner constitutional documents; organizational chart to natural persons; beneficial-ownership declarations; passports and residential-address evidence for controlling persons and signatories; board or partner resolutions authorizing the account; explanation of nominee or fiduciary roles.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (combined federal 15% and British Columbia provincial 12% general corporate rate; note: Limited Partnership is pass-through, taxed at partner level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners; not a dividend but treated as income distribution).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013 must be filed within 5 months after the partnership's fiscal year-end (e.g., if fiscal year-end is December 31, due by May 31). Partners' returns due April 30 (individuals) or 6 months after fiscal year-end (corporations).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Business Income) Gross revenue from manufacturing and sale of sterile nitrile gloves. Non-resident partners must report Canadian-source business income on their Canadian tax return; T5013 allocates income based on partnership agreement. Late filing penalty: 5% of the amount owing plus 1% per month up to 12 months; for T5013, a separate penalty of $25 per day per partner (max $2,500) applies. Failure to file may also result in criminal prosecution.
Box 2 (Expenses) Deductible expenses including raw materials, labor, depreciation, and overhead. Expenses must be allocated to non-resident partners proportionally; ensure arm's length pricing for cross-border transactions. Late filing penalty: 5% of the amount owing plus 1% per month up to 12 months; for T5013, a separate penalty of $25 per day per partner (max $2,500) applies. Failure to file may also result in criminal prosecution.
Box 10 (Net Income/Loss) Net income or loss for the fiscal period. Non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on their share of Canadian-source income unless treaty relief applies. Late filing penalty: 5% of the amount owing plus 1% per month up to 12 months; for T5013, a separate penalty of $25 per day per partner (max $2,500) applies. Failure to file may also result in criminal prosecution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if total value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million; Master File required if total value exceeds CAD 50 million; Country-by-Country Report for groups with consolidated revenue ≥ CAD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine apply. The CRA may recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance. For LPs, the partnership must have real business operations, employees, and assets to avoid GAAR challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for unpaid withholding taxes (Part XIII) and GST/HST under s.227.1; travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete), plus interest; double taxation risk without MAP.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held liable for penalties if they knowingly participate in TP mispricing; personal liability for gross negligence.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit; CRA may also impose a 'sham' transaction penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for GAAR penalties if they authorized the arrangement; potential criminal charges for conspiracy to defraud the Crown.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 for AML violations; travel bans and seizure of assets if involved in money laundering.
