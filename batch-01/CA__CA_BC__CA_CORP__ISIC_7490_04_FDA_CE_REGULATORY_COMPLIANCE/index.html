Competent corporate registry

BC Registries and Online Services – Corporate Registry, Ministry of Finance, Province of British Columbia (corporateonline.gov.bc.ca); postal: PO Box 9431 Stn Prov Govt, Victoria, BC V8W 9V3

Governing statute

British Columbia Business Corporations Act, S.B.C. 2002, c. 57 (BCBCA), ss. 10-21 (incorporation), ss. 32-41 (corporate records), ss. 99-118 (share capital), and ss. 173-200 (directors and officers); Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44 (available for federal incorporation but not elected for this BC provincial filing); Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. I-5 as amended (tax registrations ancillary to incorporation).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notice of Articles (Form 1) and Articles (Form 2) filed under the BCBCA; the Registrar issues a Certificate of Incorporation as conclusive evidence of the company's existence.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no capital escrow requirement under the BCBCA; a nominal subscription (e.g., 1 common share per incorporator at CAD $1 par value) is sufficient. Consideration for shares may be paid in money, property, or past services; the directors must determine the fair value of non-cash consideration and record it in the share subscription ledger.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire safety certification and occupancy permit under the BC Fire Code; structural load/occupancy compliance under the BC Building Code for the leased office; WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety clearance for desk-based and remote consulting employees (ergonomic evaluation, emergency exit plan, first aid kit); building insurance and business interruption continuity plan maintained as standard professional practice.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no capital escrow requirement under the BCBCA; a nominal subscription (e.g., 1 common share per incorporator at CAD $1 par value) is sufficient. Consideration for shares may be paid in money, property, or past services; the directors must determine the fair value of non-cash consideration and record it in the share subscription ledger.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable – ISIC 7490 (FDA 510(k) filing and CE Mark regulatory consulting) is an office-based professional service and is not a reviewable industrial undertaking in British Columbia. If the company later expands into medical device manufacturing, importation or distribution, Health Canada's Medical Devices Bureau (Medical Devices Regulations, SOR/98-282) and the BC Ministry of Health must be engaged as competent authorities.

Category: No statutory industrial or manufacturing operating licence applies to the consulting scope. The only operational permit needed is a municipal business licence (e.g., City of Vancouver Business Licence) for the office premises; a health product establishment licence would be required only if the company physically handles or distributes medical devices.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online tax account portal is 'My Business Account'. Provincial parallel authority: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, accessible via the BC Taxpayer Portal, administers the 7% Provincial Sales Tax (PST) independently of CRA. The federal GST/HST is exclusively administered by CRA.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the BC Business Corporations Act; the corporate registry transmits incorporation data to CRA via the integrated OneStop Business Registry, which issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically. (2) Activate the CRA Business Number and enrol for the mandatory program accounts: Corporate Income Tax (RC), GST/HST (RT), and Payroll (RP) via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by calling the CRA Business Window (1-800-959-5525). (3) If the corporation was incorporated provincially in another province and carries on business in BC, file an extra-provincial registration with BC Corporate Online; no separate tax ID is created. (4) Open a BC PST account only if the company makes PST-taxable sales; professional MedTech regulatory consulting services are not PST-taxable. (5) Appoint an authorized representative by filing Form RC59 to enable third-party tax accountants to file electronically. (6) Obtain the BN confirmation letter containing the taxpayer PIN within 10 business days; non-resident directors are permitted and do not delay issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Business Corporation is a fully separate taxable person with limited liability and is not pass-through eligible. A resident Canadian corporation is taxed on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act (IT Act); no territorial scope carve-out applies to BC-headquartered corporations. Tax_Exempt_Zone status does not apply anywhere in mainland Canada.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' entity election. Applicable statutory elections: (1) Voluntary registration as a GST/HST small supplier before exceeding the threshold (ETA s. 240); (2) Closely related corporation joint election treating inter-corporate taxable supplies as nil for GST/HST purposes (ETA s. 156); (3) Quick Method accounting election for eligible small businesses; (4) BC PST vendor election to treat sales exclusively for resale as exempt; (5) A US check-the-box election could be filed with the IRS to treat the Canadian corporation as a disregarded entity, which creates hybrid entity exposure and must be reviewed against Canada's anti-hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) to avoid denial of deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (worldwide taxable supplies of goods and services over any 12-month rolling period; once exceeded, mandatory registration with CRA for GST/HST. BC PST registration is separately triggered only if the business makes PST-taxable sales of enumerated goods or specified professional services; this corporation's consulting output is outside the PST tax base).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, section 9 of the Excise Tax Act: a supply of a service made to a non-resident recipient is taxable at 0% if the recipient is not registered for GST/HST and is not carrying on a commercial activity in Canada at the time of supply, unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. MedTech regulatory consulting (FDA 510(k) filings and CE Mark / ISO 13485 dossiers) is performed in BC for non-resident medical device manufacturers, has no connection to Canadian situs property, and therefore qualifies as zero-rated. The exporter must demonstrate via contractual terms, delivery records, and foreign-currency settlement that the recipient and the place of commercial activity are offshore; invoicing a foreign address alone is insufficient to sustain a zero-rating claim on audit.

  • Signed consulting agreement identifying the non-resident recipient's legal name, registered address, and country of tax residence
  • Commercial invoice issued in CAD, USD, or EUR with express notation 'Zero-rated export supply - Schedule VI, s. 9 ETA'
  • Foreign-currency payment evidence, including bank MT103 wire confirmations, SWIFT remittance records, or credit-card gateway settlement reports from foreign issuers
  • Milestone completion certificate or deliverables acceptance report identifying the offshore principal and confirming no direct link to Canadian real property or Canadian-situs tangible goods
  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A) applicable only if physical test samples or regulatory specimens cross the border; pure IP and consulting deliverables require no customs entry
  • CRA Form GST106 (GST/HST Paid on Taxable Purchases) retained with the file to substantiate full input-tax-credit recovery against zero-rated output

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API pre-approval of invoices. Mandatory requirements: invoices must be issued at the time of supply and contain the supplier's name, trading name, BN, date, recipient identity, rate, tax amount, and a 'zero-rated' notation for exports (ETA s. 221). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants with worldwide revenue exceeding CAD 10 million; paper filing is permitted below that threshold. Books and records, including electronic invoices, must be retained in machine-readable format and kept in Canada for audit (ETA s. 286; ITA ss. 230 and 230.1). A voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing pilot is available but no clearance stamp or SOAP/API accreditation is required.

Canada imposes no formal statutory deadline that requires physical FX repatriation within 180 days; the 180-day window is used as an administrative benchmark for export documentation freshness, and unrepatriated receivables must nonetheless be translated and accrued in Canadian dollars in the year earned. CRA audit scrutiny is elevated for consulting registrants whose input-tax-credit claims exceed the 5% net tax on domestic supplies; where export zero-rating documentation fails, CRA may claw back all claimed ITCs, deny zero-rated treatment retrospectively, and assess: (1) 25% of the net tax deficiency as a misstatement penalty (ETA s. 281.1); (2) 200% of the evaded tax for knowingly false statements (ETA s. 280); (3) criminal fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment (ETA s. 327); and (4) a separate 40% penalty on evaded BC PST if the PST exemption is misapplied. Reassessment periods are normally four years, extended to ten years where misrepresentation or fraud is established; corrections can be mitigated through CRA's Voluntary Disclosures Program before audit contact.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account for residents and non-residents. There are no exchange-control permits, no prior-approval gates for profit remittance, and no convertibility limitations. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, reduced by applicable treaty; under the Canada-US Convention generally 5% for a US corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of the voting stock, 15% otherwise; interest typically 0%; royalties 0%-10%), and require the payer to file NR4 returns and obtain treaty-residency documentation (NR301/NR302) before remittance. Cross-border consulting fees paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident contractors are generally not subject to Part XIII withholding if no Canadian permanent establishment exists, but transfer-pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act is required where related parties are involved. For exported consulting services (US FDA 510(k) and EU CE Mark advisory work), GST/HST is zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, Section 7, provided the non-resident recipient is outside Canada and the service is not connected to Canadian real property or goods. No Bank of Canada authorization is required to convert or remit profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises Schedule I chartered banks under the Bank Act; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and receives Large Electronic Funds Transfer Reports and Large Cash Transaction Reports; the Department of Finance Canada is responsible for exchange-rate and capital-flow policy. Canada operates no restrictive Foreign Exchange Administration: FX transactions are executed freely through federally regulated banking entities, and no prior central-bank approval is required for payments, capital flows, or corporate account opening.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 12% BC)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; BC imposes no separate dividend withholding tax and the 25% rate may be reduced under an applicable tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the end of the corporation's fiscal period. The balance-due day for corporate income tax is generally two months after the fiscal year-end, extended to three months for Canadian-controlled private corporations eligible for the small business deduction. Instalments and final payment are tied to the balance-due day, not the filing deadline.

Late filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax, plus 1% per complete month late to a maximum of 12 months; a repeat failure raises the base penalty to 10% and the monthly surcharge to 2% to a maximum of 20 months. The CRA charges daily compound interest on unpaid balances at the prescribed rate plus applicable federal and provincial income tax. Failure to file Form T106 or to prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation can trigger separate penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in total annual fair market value of reportable transactions with each non-arm's-length non-resident triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. Multinational groups with consolidated global revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000 must file Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting with the CRA.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the CRA may ignore, recharacterize, or adjust transactions under section 247 and the section 245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule if a BC consulting corporation lacks real economic substance. A BC corporation providing FDA 510(k) and CE Mark services must maintain a physical office, qualified professional employees, board oversight, client contracts, technical manuals, project records, invoicing, and bank accounts in BC to prove real business risk, decisions, and operational substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act equal to 50% of the tax understated; criminal prosecution under subsection 239(1) may result in a fine of between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. The CRA may also reassess beyond the normal reassessment period where misrepresentation is due to neglect, fraud, or wilful default.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize, or participate in evasion are personally exposed to criminal charges, fines, restitution to the CRA, imprisonment, and travel restrictions as a condition of bail or criminal proceedings. The corporate veil may be pierced where the corporation is a facade for personal profit.
Shell Company / Artificial Corporate Residence

Statutory sanction: The CRA may recharacterize intercompany payments at arm's-length prices under section 247(2), impose the section 247(3) transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment, deny treaty benefits under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, and reassess disguised dividends as taxable income. Directors may be assessed for unremitted source deductions and withheld Part XIII taxes under section 227.1.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for corporate withholding tax failures, are exposed to penalties for knowing participation in a sham conduit arrangement, and may face personal tax reassessments, civil fraud claims, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion if they acted in bad faith.
FDA 510(k) / CE Mark Regulatory Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under US FDA law, false 510(k) submissions may trigger civil money penalties, product seizure, import refusal, debarment, and criminal penalties including imprisonment. Under EU MDR 2017/745, misleading CE marking can result in market withdrawal, fines, and criminal prosecution in member states. Under Canada's Food and Drugs Act and Medical Devices Regulations, false or misleading device information is an offence punishable on indictment with fines and imprisonment. Fines or penalties imposed for regulatory convictions are not deductible for Canadian income tax purposes.

Director personal liability: The corporate form does not shield a qualified individual who signs or submits a false regulatory declaration; directors who knowingly participate in medical device misrepresentation can face personal liability, professional disqualification, product recall costs, client indemnity claims, and criminal prosecution both abroad and in Canada.
Anti-Money Laundering / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative penalties may reach CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation per violation; laundering the proceeds of crime under section 462.31 of the Criminal Code is an indictable offence punishable by up to ten years imprisonment. The illegal fees remain taxable income, and unreported income from criminal activity is subject to ordinary tax, interest, and gross negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly allow the corporation to process tainted funds can be prosecuted as parties or conspirators, face personal fines and imprisonment, and be subject to asset freezes, restraint orders, and travel bans in the course of a proceeds-of-crime investigation.
