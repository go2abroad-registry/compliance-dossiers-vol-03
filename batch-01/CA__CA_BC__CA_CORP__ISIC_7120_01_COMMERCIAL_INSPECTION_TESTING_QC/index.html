Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; business portal: CRA My Business Account; registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Canadian business corporation or commencing taxable activity in Canada obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA, then activates the GST/HST account via BRO, mail, or phone. The corporation must also register with the British Columbia Corporate Registry for provincial status if incorporating provincially, and with BC Ministry of Finance for BC PST if applicable. Non-resident corporations are required to provide a head office address and authorized representative/attorney in Canada for service if carrying on business.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a Canadian business corporation or commencing taxable activity in Canada obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA, then activates the GST/HST account via BRO, mail, or phone. The corporation must also register with the British Columbia Corporate Registry for provincial status if incorporating provincially, and with BC Ministry of Finance for BC PST if applicable. Non-resident corporations are required to provide a head office address and authorized representative/attorney in Canada for service if carrying on business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. A CRA-registered business corporation is automatically a resident of Canada if incorporated in Canada and is taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Available elections relevant to the foreign-owned corporation include the GST/HST group registration election (Excise Tax Act ss.273-275), the closely related corporation election for GST/HST, and various income tax elections under section 85 or section 256.1 for capital restructuring; hybrid mismatch rules under Part VI.1 may apply to cross-border branch payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters for GST/HST small supplier threshold; BC PST registration is required for non-resident vendors making taxable sales through a BC permanent establishment or selling taxable goods into BC under the stated vendor tax collection rules

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for property or services exported by a GST/HST registrant under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For on-site factory inspection and pre-shipment QC services, the service must be supplied to a non-resident person and must not be supplied to a resident of Canada; the service must also be for consumption in the course of commercial activity outside Canada or in relation to goods that are exported, provided adequate documentary evidence proves the foreign supply. The full ITC entitlement is preserved on the 0% supply.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (form B13A) or Automated Export Declaration (AEB) confirmation
  • Signed commercial invoice identifying the foreign service recipient and scope of inspection services
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or courier waybill evidencing movement of any inspected goods or reports to the foreign destination
  • Foreign service contract or purchase order stating the testing/QC mandate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire transfer receipt showing foreign currency payment from the non-resident customer
  • CRA-specified documentary record under Regulations 120-122 of the Excise Tax Act (e.g., ETA s.218 documentation evidence)

Canada does not mandate a real-time e-invoicing fiscal clearance system. The CRA requires all GST/HST registrants to issue invoices for taxable supplies and maintain electronic records in an accessible format. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST TELEFILE for larger registrants; no digital stamping or API real-time clearance requirement exists at the federal level, although the CRA may issue CSV/XML specifications for certain ledger data in audits.

The CRA may treat an export zero-rating claim as invalid if the required shipping/payment evidence is absent or if the service is actually consumed in Canada. Consequences include disallowance of the 0% rate, reassessment to impose GST/HST at 5% on the supply, recovery of related ITCs, interest, and arrears penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act. Fraudulent claims attract additional penalties up to 15% under ETA s.273.1, gross negligence penalties under Income Tax Act s.163.2 if reflected on income filings, and criminal liability under the Criminal Code for wilful evasion; export documentation must be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely floating, fully convertible, IMF Article VIII freely usable currency. Both current-account and capital-account convertibility are unrestricted for a BC commercial inspection and testing company (ISIC 7120 / NAICS 541380). No exchange-control approval, surrender certificate, or permit is required for service-export receipts, equity injection from a US parent, dividend/interest remittance, or reinvestment. Practical constraints: FINTRAC must be notified of any cross-border EFT of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period; sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act are the only hard blockers; Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, reduced to 5% - 15% under the Canada-US tax treaty) applies to dividends and requires a valid W-8BEN-E and a corporate dividend resolution at the paying bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriation of export-of-service proceeds. CA_CORP may hold USD receipts indefinitely in a foreign-currency operating account at a Schedule I bank and convert at commercial rates. Income Tax Act, s. 39 governs foreign exchange gains and losses for tax purposes; IFRS IAS 21 governs financial statement translation. No company-level balance-of-payments filing is required; the clearing bank reports Statistics Canada purpose-of-payment codes as part of the wire origination.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority; there is no dedicated central foreign-exchange-control body in Canada. Administering institutions: Department of Finance Canada (Exchange Fund Account, Currency Act, Financial Administration Act); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of the QC agency's Schedule I clearing bank; FINTRAC for cross-border EFT and AML reporting; Bank of Canada for Retail Payment Activities Act supervision of payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for low-value cross-border disbursements and staff expense flows; Low as the principal treasury or capital-escrow rail. Wise Business and Payoneer operate as FINTRAC-registered money service businesses (MSBs) holding pooled CAD balances at a Schedule I bank; they are not chartered banks, are not Payments Canada clearing members, and provide no CDIC deposit insurance or commercial documentary credit (LC) capacity. Legal constraints for the client are indirect: the PSP must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada, and the corporate client must complete equivalent KYC/CDD through the PSP. Recommended use: sub-threshold supplier wires, multi-currency FX conversions, and rapid settlement to overseas inspectors. Primary clearance and capital escrow must remain at a tier-1 domestic bank.

The T2 return must be filed no later than 6 months after the taxation year-end; final tax balance is generally due 2 months after the fiscal year-end, extended to 3 months for an eligible CCPC claiming the Small Business Deduction. Example: December 31 year-end requires T2 filing by June 30 of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions triggers mandatory Form T106 reporting and CRA contemporaneous documentation expectations; comprehensive Local File and Master File documentation is strongly indicated where related-party revenues or transaction values exceed CAD 10,000,000, including functional analysis, risk analysis, economic substance, transfer pricing method selection, and arm's-length range benchmarking.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute; however, CRA requires real economic substance for corporate residence, permanent establishment status, and general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) purposes. A BC corporation acting as a shell with no office, no staff, no management meetings in Canada, and no substantive business activity in BC is vulnerable to CRA residence re-attribution, treaty denial, and recharacterization of its factory inspection revenues.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Concealment

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1) provides criminal liability of a fine of from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment; civil gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) is 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted corporate tax amounts, may be personally assessed for payroll and GST/HST amounts, face criminal fines, restitution orders, travel restrictions in connection with extradition where fraud is serious, and imprisonment.
Chronic Late Filing / Information Return Default

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month up to 12 months; daily compounded interest; T106 penalties of C$25 per day up to C$2,500; failure to comply with a CRA demand may result in a court order compelling compliance and criminal prosecution under ITA s.238(2) with fines up to C$25,000 and imprisonment up to 12 months.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct non-filing or who knowingly allow repeated default may be personally liable for employer withholding and GST/HST under statutory deemed agency provisions; convictions can result in personal fines and imprisonment for the directing mind.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to deny the tax benefit, reassess with interest, impose a 50% gross negligence penalty, add penalties under transfer pricing rules of 10% of the net adjustment, and deny treaty benefits by setting aside the corporate residence or permanent establishment claim.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign returns based on artificial structures without real economic substance may be liable for gross negligence penalties personally; where the shell is used to launder or conceal assets, directors may face civil restitution, anti-money laundering penalties, and criminal charges carrying travel bans and imprisonment.
Money Laundering / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for corporations and C$50,000 for individuals; Criminal Code s.462.31 possession of proceeds of crime is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years; s.354 also imposes up to 5 years for possessing property obtained by crime.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for corporate AML failures if they knowingly authorize, participate in, or fail to correct suspicious transactions; personal criminal conviction results in fines, forfeiture orders, imprisonment, and international arrest or travel restrictions.
