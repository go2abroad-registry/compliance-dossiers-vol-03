Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada; taxpayer portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/business-account.html); provincial BC portal: BC eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and/or a BC incorporation number. For a federal CBCA corporation, incorporate through Corporations Canada; for a BC provincial corporation, file incorporation with BC Registry Services. After incorporation, apply to the CRA for the BN and GST/HST (RT) account by Form RC1, or for non-resident-only economic presence by Form RC4590 (Application for a Non-Resident Corporation Business Number). Provide certified board resolution, registered office address in Canada or BC, agent for service, director identification, and foreign tax domicile documentation. Complete the GST/HST registration online if threshold exceeded or voluntarily to obtain a RT/BN-15 identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No United States-style check-the-box election is available under Canadian federal tax law; a business corporation is always a separate taxable legal entity. Available statutory elections include: (i) Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) designation under Income Tax Act subsection 125(7) if 50% or less foreign controlled and the family office meets active business criteria; (ii) GST/HST group registration election under Excise Tax Act section 150 for closely related resident members; (iii) qualifying joint venture election under Excise Tax Act section 273; (iv) optional streamlined accounting for financial services if the multi-family office provides non-custodial portfolio advisory; and (v) hybrid mismatch ordering elections under Income Tax Act section 17.1.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

30,000 CAD (small supplier threshold; a registrant must register and collect GST/HST once worldwide taxable supply revenues exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; BC PST registration threshold is 10,000 CAD)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI Part V (goods supplied outside Canada for export) and Part VII (services supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada). A GST/HST registrant must establish that the supply meets the place-of-supply rule and obtain documentary proof of export within the required timeline, generally within 60 days after the goods are exported or the service supplier has written evidence that the service is to be performed and received outside Canada. Portfolio management and investment advisory services rendered to non-resident family members may qualify as zero-rated financial services only if the services are in the nature of arranging for or advising on securities trading and are supplied to a non-resident (subject to the financial services carve-outs).

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13 or approved electronic export reporting through CBSA
  • Bill of Lading or Equivalent Waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/crediting evidence showing payment in foreign currency from a non-resident
  • Commercial invoice containing complete description of goods/services, ultimate destination, and contract terms
  • Signed service agreement where applicable, confirming the recipient is a non-resident and the place of supply is offshore

No national real-time fiscal clearance or transaction digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants exceeding the mandatory electronic filing threshold must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or TELEFILE using CRA-certified software or through My Business Account. Invoices must contain prescribed registration rules, GST/HST registration number, consideration, tax rate and amount, and be retained either in Canada or in a CRA-accessible format. BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC; BC does not operate a real-time pre-clearance fiscal e-invoice system.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims under the Excise Tax Act using documentation matching and third-party bank information. A claim unsupported by contemporaneous export evidence is reassessed with GST/HST payable, arrears interest, and a false-refund penalty equal to the greater of $500 and 25% of the amount claimed under section 285.0.1. Where the taxpayer has knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence made false statements, a 50% gross negligence penalty may apply under section 327. Ordinary reassessment windows generally cover four years, but are open-ended where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act or Criminal Code may also follow, with additional fines and potential imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII. A British Columbia multi-family office may freely receive, hold, and disburse CAD or foreign currency via licensed financial institutions. Profit repatriation is permitted without exchange-control licence; the withholding tax rate on dividends paid to non-resident owners is 25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act unless reduced to 5% or 15% under an applicable income tax treaty. Beneficial ownership reporting, CRS/FATCA registration, and T1135 foreign-asset reporting remain the operative compliance hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in any currency and settled freely. FINTRAC reporting obligations may apply to cross-border wire transfers of C$10,000 or more, and CRA foreign-source reporting (Form T1135) applies where the cost amount of specified foreign assets exceeds C$100,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority) with foreign exchange policy coordination by the Department of Finance Canada and prudential banking supervision by OSFI; no dedicated foreign exchange control agency exists because Canada maintains open capital and current account convertibility.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for core treasury operations. Offshore e-money institutions (EMIs) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions or clearing members of Canadian payment systems and cannot provide the custodial/trust account capacity required for discretionary portfolio advisory services. They may be used only as ancillary payment corridors for low-risk operating expenses provided funds never hold client assets or settle securities; legal constraints arise under PCMLTFA, NI 31-103, and Canadian banking restrictions on 'banking' activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Incoming shareholder capital should be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the Schedule I bank operating account or a segregated capital escrow account in the entity's name. The remitter must use explicit purpose codes such as 'Q001 equity injection' or a bank-approved narrative stating 'contribution to share capital'; remittance instructions must identify the MFO, the underlying shareholder/trust, and the intermediary chain. For CAD clearing, domestic value transfers use Lynx (large-value system) or ACSS; for USD/cross-currency transfers, the receiving bank must pre-verify the source of funds and match inbound wires against the submitted KYC file. Any equivalent aggregate receipt of C$10,000 or more is reportable to FINTRAC as a cross-border wire transfer.Disbursements for trade settlements and service fees should use the SWIFT MT103 message for cross-border payments, with commercial letters of credit (UCP600) for documentary trade settlements. CAD domestic cash clearing is done through Lynx/ACSS; cross-border USD transfers may route through our Canadian bank's correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire at the US correspondent) or CLS for the settlement of eligible FX currencies.
