Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax authority; taxpayer portal: CRA My Business Account; BC corporate registry: BC Registry Services (bcregistry.ca); BC consumer tax administration: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch via the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

1) Register the limited partnership under the BC Partnership Act at BC Registry Services to obtain the BC registration number and certificate of limited partnership; 2) Apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via the online 'Register a business' service or Form RC1, selecting 'Partnership' as the legal structure and opening the required program accounts — RT for GST/HST and RP for payroll if employees exist; 3) Use the BN/RT account for all CRA filings on behalf of the LP, including the T5013 Partnership Information Return and GST/HST returns; 4) Because non-resident limited partners are permitted, confirm whether Part XIII withholding under ITA ss. 212 and 135 applies to any non-resident allocations and whether the T5013 must be filed with schedules for non-resident partners; 5) Register separately for BC PST through eTaxBC using the federal BN, receiving a distinct BC PST collection account; 6) Notwithstanding the small-supplier threshold, the LP should consider voluntary GST/HST registration to recover input tax credits on export-related inputs because subscription-box exports are zero-rated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists under the Income Tax Act. A Canadian LP is statutorily transparent: income, gains, deductions and credits are computed at the partnership level under ITA s. 96 and flow through to partners each fiscal period. Limited partners are generally 'specified members' (ITA s. 125(8)) and therefore cannot claim the small business deduction on their shares. The LP may elect non-calendar fiscal periods (ITA s. 249.1(7)) and may use specific GST/HST elections under the Excise Tax Act, such as s. 156 (closely related persons) and s. 179 (joint supplies). Hybrid-mismatch rules in ITA ss. 94.1-94.3 mandatorily restrict tax deferral where the LP is treated as opaque by a foreign jurisdiction; a US check-the-box election is not recognized by the CRA and cannot override Canadian classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold based on the total value of taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in Canada by the LP during any four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA ss. 240(1) and 241). BC PST registration: a vendor must register with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch as soon as it begins selling tangible personal property in BC, with no minimum revenue threshold for a continuing vendor; limited exemptions exist for occasional sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical subscription-box merchandise qualifies for the 0% GST/HST rate when the LP ships the goods to a destination outside Canada (ETA Sch. VI, Part V, item 1) or the recipient exports the goods as soon as is practicable and at latest within 60 days after delivery (ETA s. 23(1)); related transportation, shipping-handling, and insurance services supplied to the non-resident customer are also zero-rated (ETA Sch. VI, Part V, item 2). Digital subscription benefits or ancillary electronic services delivered in connection with the box are zero-rated only if supplied to a non-resident and not performed in Canada (ETA Sch. VI, Part V, item 7); tangible goods must not be excluded or prescribed by the regulations.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for consignments with a value of CAD 2,000 or more, or for controlled/prohibited goods
  • Commercial invoice showing HS tariff classification, unit and total value, country of origin, destination country, and a clear zero-rated export statement
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier waybill, or Canada Post receipt evidencing delivery to a foreign destination
  • Proof of payment or inward remittance certificate from the non-resident buyer (e.g., SWIFT MT103, Stripe payout records, or bank statement showing FX or CAD proceeds received from abroad)
  • Export packing list, carrier manifest, and destination routing documentation sufficient to support an ETA s. 169(2)/CRA policy audit

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API pre-validation, or digital stamping regime applies to GST/HST invoices in Canada. The CRA promotes voluntary adoption of the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 e-invoicing standard. Statutory GST/HST invoice content (ETA ss. 169 and 223) requires the supplier's BN, invoice date, supplier and recipient names, a description of the goods, and either the GST amount charged or a statement that the tax is included in the price for taxable supplies sold for more than CAD 30 (tax-included); commercial invoices for zero-rated subscription-box exports should explicitly state zero-rated. BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC with separate e-remittance and record-keeping duties independent of the federal GST/HST regime.

If the exporter cannot substantiate timely export, the CRA may retroactively deny the zero-rating, assess GST/HST at 5% on the full selling price, and charge arrears interest; a false statement or omission connected with the export claim attracts a civil penalty of up to CAD 25,000, or up to 25% of the tax sought to be avoided under ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence rules. Goods diverted back into Canada or sold domestically after a zero-rated export immediately become taxable, and the resulting assessment is retroactive. Wilful evasion of GST/HST is an offence under ETA s. 99(2) and, on conviction, can result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment; systematic documented-export fraud is also indictable as fraud under the Criminal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current account and capital account purposes. No exchange control licenses, prior approvals, or surrender requirements apply to foreign direct investment, partnership capital contributions, operational inflows, or profit and distribution remittances. Non-resident partners may receive distributions in CAD or any freely convertible currency, subject only to routine withholding tax compliance under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and applicable income tax treaties, as well as FINTRAC large transaction reporting for amounts equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000. Repatriation of capital and current earnings is considered unrestricted, although financial institutions may require architected source-of-funds evidence for regulatory compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds. Subscription box sales receipts retained in foreign currency may be held, converted at market rates, or used directly to pay foreign suppliers. Cross-border electronic fund transfers and large cash transactions above CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC; authorized banks may ask for supporting invoices or contracts to align with their own compliance programs.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); no separate foreign exchange administration is maintained because Canada operates a liberalized floating-rate regime under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High with regulatory constraints. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut Business) can provide operational multi-currency collection and vendor disbursement accounts, but they generally rely on a Canadian Schedule I bank sponsor or a Payment Canada clearing member to obtain native CAD settlement. Direct access to Canadian clearing rails is not available to foreign EMIs that do not hold a Canadian bank license or an authorized payment service provider registration. A British Columbia Limited Partnership may use an offshore EMI for secondary treasury concentration, but the primary capital escrow account and auditable inter-entity flows should remain with a domestic Schedule I bank to satisfy lender, supplier, and tax authority expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended compliant rail: SWIFT MT103 (international wire) or Canadian domestic large-value transfer via Payments Canada. Purpose codes should follow ISO 20022 / SWIFT conventions — use 'CAPT' for capital increase or 'BGRP' for a business-related capital transfer, and include the LP agreement contract number in the remittance information field. The receiving bank must file any required Large Electronic Funds Transfer report to FINTRAC for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more.
