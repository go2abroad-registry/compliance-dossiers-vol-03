Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/small-businesses/register-your-business.html); for British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST), the competent authority is the British Columbia Ministry of Finance via eTaxBC (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst/register-for-pst)

E-government filing portal

After incorporation, the corporation must obtain a 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) either through CRA Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1 with the articles of incorporation. The BN is the statutory tax identification number. Program accounts must then be opened as applicable: RC for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST when annual taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000, and RP for payroll if employees are engaged. A non-resident-owned corporation must provide a Canadian business address and a CRA access code to file and manage accounts. In British Columbia, the entity must separately register as a PST vendor with the BC Ministry of Finance before making PST-taxable sales, using eTaxBC and providing legal name, BN, business address, and supply details.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' election; a business corporation is mandatorily classified as a separate taxable person subject to Part I income tax on worldwide income if it is resident in Canada (de jure by incorporation or de facto by central management and control). No hybrid mismatch election is available; hybrid mismatch outcomes are corrected by mandatory ordering and disallowance rules under section 18.6 of the Income Tax Act. Available elections that may apply include voluntary GST/HST registration while under the small supplier threshold and, for non-resident corporations, treaty residence/PE and branch-relevant elections. There is no elective entity reclassification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada during the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in any single calendar quarter (small supplier threshold); BC PST: no general small supplier turnover threshold for vendors making PST-taxable sales in British Columbia, so PST registration is required before such sales are made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of advisory/consultancy services made to a non-resident is zero-rated (0% GST/HST) where the service is supplied to a person who is a non-resident, the service is not a supply to an individual present in Canada at the time the service is supplied, the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the supply is not otherwise an excluded service. An overseas market-entry feasibility study and regulatory licensing consultancy performed for a non-resident client, with analysis delivered offshore or consumed wholly outside Canada, will generally qualify as zero-rated. The supplier must maintain credible evidence of the non-resident identity and foreign consumption as required by CRA GST/HST Memorandum 8-2.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or CBSA electronic export reporting for any tangible deliverables shipped out of Canada
  • Bill of Lading / Waybill evidencing delivery outside Canada where goods are exported
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / MT103 SWIFT payment evidence or Wise B2B remittance statement showing receipt of funds from the non-resident client
  • Executed consulting agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, scope of the overseas market-entry consultancy, and place of supply
  • Commercial invoice annotated 'Zero-rated for GST/HST' with the supplier's Business Number and a clear description of the offshore service
  • Certificates of Residence, incorporation records, or equivalent documentary evidence confirming non-resident status of the client

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, or digital stamping. CRA accepts any electronic or paper invoice that contains the required information set out in the Excise Tax Act regulations (business number, date, supplier/recipient identification, description, consideration, and tax status). Participation in the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing network is voluntary. Periodic GST/HST returns are filed with CRA, while BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC; neither system performs transaction-level pre-clearance or API-based fiscal stamping.

If CRA disallows zero-rating because export or non-resident status evidence is incomplete or fraudulent, it may reassess the supply at the applicable GST/HST rate and, where relevant, the BC PST rate, add prescribed quarterly interest, and impose penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 10% of the net tax (20% for repeat violations). The normal reassessment period may be extended to six years for misrepresentation or negligence and remains unlimited for fraud. Gross negligence penalties may also apply under the Income Tax Act where false statements affect income tax. Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Excise Tax Act applies to intentional evasion or falsification of records, and directors may be held personally liable for unremitted GST/HST under section 323. Retroactive clawback of input tax credits previously claimed is also possible where zero-rating is overturned.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a regime of full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions pursuant to IMF Article VIII obligations. For a British Columbia-incorporated business corporation engaged in foreign market feasibility study and regulatory licensing agency consultancy services, there are no capital controls, no restrictions on profit remittances, and no hurdles to repatriation of service fees or consulting revenues. All FX transactions are subject solely to FINTRAC reporting and tax compliance obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service exports such as overseas market entry consultancy fees are not subject to any mandatory surrender requirement to the Bank of Canada. Proceeds may be retained directly in foreign currency accounts without conversion. No statutory timeline, conversion ratio, or surrender mechanism applies to legitimate service receipts; however, entities must maintain adequate documentation for tax filing and FINTRAC AML monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for cross-border electronic money transfers via regulated platforms such as Wise Business or equivalent licensed providers; legal constraints include mandatory registration with FINTRAC as a money services business, ongoing transaction monitoring, and adherence to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for all digital EMI activities.
