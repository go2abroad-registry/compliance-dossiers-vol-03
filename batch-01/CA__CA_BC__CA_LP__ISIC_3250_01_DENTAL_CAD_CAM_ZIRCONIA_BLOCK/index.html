Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) through the Business Registration Online portal and CRA My Business Account. British Columbia provincial tax registration is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance / Consumer Taxation Branch (myTAXBC).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forming or registering a BC limited partnership must first register the partnership as a limited partnership or extraprovincial limited partnership with the British Columbia Corporate Registry. The registry information is communicated to the CRA for issuance of the 9-digit Business Number (BN), which is then used to open the GST/HST programme account (e.g., RT/RM/RP) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1. The LP must also register as a PST collector with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch through myTAXBC where taxable sales in BC are made. Non-resident partners should provide their home-jurisdiction TIN, attest to their permanent establishment status in Canada and, where required, appoint a service-of-process representative in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes and is not entitled to any check-the-box election; income and losses pass through to partners under the partnership agreement and sections 96 and 103 of the Income Tax Act. The partnership may file a partnership information return (TP-501), and special allocations may be supported by a signed election under subsection 96(3). For GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate person and may elect with a registered member under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to treat certain intercompany supplies as taxable. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA sections 18.15 to 18.2) apply automatically and are not elective; no US-style entity classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (exclusive of zero-rated supplies of specified financial services and certain capital assets), for which registration is compulsory once the threshold is exceeded in a calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. PST: registration is triggered by making taxable retail sales in BC; no broad general turnover exemption exists, although a limited small-seller de minimis exemption may be available for negligible or irregular BC sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks and milling discs supplied to non-resident customers qualify for the 0% GST rate under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act and the Zero-Rated Supplies (GST/HST) Regulations if the goods are exported as part of the supply. The exporter must establish that title passes to a non-resident, the goods are shipped or delivered outside Canada within 60 days of the supply, no use, consumption, or installation occurs in Canada, and contemporaneous evidence of physical export is retained. PST is not payable on goods sold in BC for export when the seller retains proof of exportation. Related design and engineering services supplied to a non-resident and performed wholly outside Canada for use by the recipient may also qualify for zero-rating under the services provisions, provided they are not directly performed on tangible property located in Canada.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A / Export Reporting Capture System) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency or Statistics Canada when required by export control or statistical value
  • Commercial invoice and packing list showing the dental zirconia block description, quantity, value, and declaration of export
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing movement of the goods from Canada to the foreign buyer
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT/TT payment settlement confirmation matching the export invoice value

Canada does not mandate continuous transaction controls, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. Electronic invoices are accepted if they contain all statutory information required by CRA record-keeping rules, including supplier name, Business Number, date, description of the supply, amount, and GST/HST charged. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via CRA My Business Account, and PST returns are filed via myTAXBC. Peppol-based e-invoicing may be adopted voluntarily, particularly for government procurement and supply chain automation.

CRA and the BC Ministry of Finance may audit export zero-rating claims by requiring contemporaneous customs, shipping, and payment records. If a supply is recharacterized as domestic because export documentation is missing, defective, or fraudulent, the supply is reassessed at 5% GST (plus 7% PST where applicable), input tax credits are denied, and any refund or rebate is clawed back. Civil penalties apply for failure to register, failure to file, false statements, and gross negligence, including percentage penalties on net tax plus statutory interest. Criminal prosecution may follow for wilful evasion or the use of false documents. BC PST violations attract additional penalty assessments of up to 100% of the tax evaded, plus interest, and CRA may also impose penalties for knowing or grossly negligent misrepresentation in export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange rate with no exchange-control regime. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current account (trade in goods, services, royalties, interest) and capital account (equity injections, partnership distributions, loans, portfolio flows). A British Columbia limited partnership may freely accept foreign capital, convert at market rates, and remit profits abroad without prior Bank of Canada or OSFI approval. The only remittance hurdle is fiscal, not currency-based: Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on distributions/interest to non-resident partners, typically reduced to 15% under the Canada-US treaty (5% for certain interest), reported via NR4 or T2 filings. The Investment Canada Act may apply to acquisitions of control above statutory thresholds but does not restrict convertibility or profit repatriation. No pre-export license or invoicing permit is required for dental zirconia block sales.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory repatriation timeline. Exporters, including the BC LP, may retain receipts in any convertible currency and in any country. Recommended treasury practice is to route export settlements to the LP's Canadian bank account within standard trade settlement terms to satisfy PCMLTFA record-keeping (minimum 5-year retention) and to support Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting when annual cross-border settlements exceed CAD 100,000. FINTRAC large-transaction reporting is triggered for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, but this is a reporting obligation, not a surrender or conversion duty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority and issuer of legal tender; Department of Finance Canada (Minister of Finance) as administrator of the Exchange Fund Account and custodian of foreign exchange reserves; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator for FX and derivatives; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/ATF supervisor. Governing statutes include the Bank of Canada Act, Currency Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplemental collection and payment rail; LOW as the primary corporate treasury bank. Fintech firms and electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) operating in Canada must hold a FINTRAC Money Service Business (MSB) registration and comply with PCMLTFA; however, they are not Schedule I or Schedule II banks and therefore cannot accept CDIC-insured demand deposits, issue commercial letters of credit, open bankers acceptances, or provide Lynx/ACH clearing membership. A BC LP may employ EMIs for low-value cross-border FX remittances and supplier collections, but must maintain its capital escrow, operating liquidity, trade finance, and derivative relationships with chartered Canadian banks. AML constraint: EMI balances should be treated as unsecured exposures, not core treasury balances, and must be included in the bank's sanctioned-corridor review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the LP's initial foreign capital: the non-resident investor's remitting bank transmits a SWIFT MT103 (TT payment) or Fedwire/CHIPS wire for USD through its correspondent to the LP's designated account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The beneficiary name must exactly match the registered BC LP name, and the account number must be pre-validated to avoid Lynx clearing rejects. The remittance reference should state Capital Contribution per LP Agreement followed by the date and partner name, with no accompanying goods or services description. Canada does not operate a mandatory purpose-code regime, so no central-bank approval is required; however, the receiving bank must complete FINTRAC large-transaction reporting for any CAD 10,000 or more credit, and the LP must file the Statistics Canada balance-of-payments survey if the transferred sum exceeds CAD 100,000. Foreign currency proceeds must remain in the multi-currency escrow, and any USD-to-CAD conversion must be executed same-day at the host bank's institutional rate, with an FX confirmation voucher retained for audit.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 wire/TT for open-account supplier payments, primarily for zirconia powder and dental milling consumables; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (ICC UCP600, SWIFT MT700) issued by the Canadian bank and advised or confirmed in the supplier's country, required for contracts exceeding CAD 50,000 or first-time counterparties; (3) Documentary collections (DP/DA, SWIFT MT4xx) for stable repeat vendors with a satisfactory payment history; (4) Domestic CAD payments clear through Lynx (formerly LVTS, operated by Payments Canada) using SWIFT MT202 or ISO 20022 messages. USD disbursements settle through the US Federal Reserve/CHIPS corridor via the bank's US-dollar clearing centre. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No mandatory statutory audit applies to a private BC limited partnership under the Limited Partnership Act (BC) or the Income Tax Act (Canada). A compulsory independent audit is triggered only if the partnership becomes a 'reporting issuer' under BC Securities Act, or the partnership agreement/lender covenants require an audit, or the partnership qualifies as an 'advisive financial entity' under specified regulations. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. A limited partnership does not pay dividends; distributions of current or accumulated partnership income to non-resident limited partners are generally taxed as Canadian-source business income under Part I, not as Part XIII dividends, although withholding may apply to interest, rents, royalties, management fees, or other fixed or determinable annual gains paid to non-residents.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$5,000,000 in aggregate reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions triggers mandatory Form T106 disclosure and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation expectations; Country-by-Country Reporting becomes mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of C$750,000,000 or more. Canada does not issue separate 'Local File' and 'Master File' forms, but the CRA applies the OECD transfer pricing guidelines and expects supporting local-file-style documentation for any material intercompany transaction, regardless of the C$5 million threshold if audit risk is present.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no formal economic substance regulations analogous to offshore financial centres, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), judicial 'sham' doctrine, permanent establishment jurisprudence, and Canada–US treaty limitation-on-benefits provisions require the limited partnership to maintain substantive operations in British Columbia. The LP must own or lease tangible premises, employ or retain qualified personnel, perform manufacturing and distribution functions, carry real commercial risk, and keep books and records in BC. A limited partnership used solely as a conduit or shell to access treaty benefits is vulnerable to CRA recharacterization, GAAR reassessment, and treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) is 50% of the understated tax payable, plus arrears interest and the 50% general anti-avoidance penalty under section 245 where GAAR applies.

Director personal liability: General partners of the LP and directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted withholding taxes under subsection 227.1(1); CRA may also pursue directors for unpaid Part XIII and Part I amounts, obtain civil restitution orders, register liens, issue travel restrictions under section 226, and initiate criminal charges against knowingly culpable individuals.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and T106 Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the taxpayer to arm's-length amounts under section 247(2) and impose a penalty equal to 10% of the aggregate net upward transfer-pricing adjustment under subsection 247(3). If misstatements are grossly negligent, an additional 50% gross-negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) may apply, and repeated non-filing of T106 carries separate information-return penalties.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner's directors can be held personally liable for the unpaid penalty and tax if the corporation fails to remit after being assessed; the general partner of the LP is also stripped of limited liability protection if it participates in the management or pricing arrangements without arm's-length substance.
Shell Company / Lack of Substantive Operations

Statutory sanction: CRA can apply the GAAR under section 245 to deny the tax benefit, reallocate income, and impose a 25% penalty on the benefit amount; anti-treaty-shopping rules and domestic permanent establishment rules may additionally deny treaty relief. The transaction may be reviewed as a sham, resulting in full reassessment, interest, and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be assessed personally for the resulting tax liabilities and penalties; the limited partner's liability shield is lost under BC partnership law if the limited partner takes part in management; travel bans and asset freezes are possible under the Income Tax Act's collection powers for serious unresolved assessments.
Part XIII Withholding and Non-Resident Distribution Failures

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the full amount of unwithheld Part XIII tax, plus arrears interest and a penalty of 10% of any withholding error if not due to negligence; if negligence is gross, the penalty rises to 20%, and knowing failure can trigger prosecution under the Income Tax Act. Branch tax of 25% applies to non-resident corporate partners under Part XIV unless reduced by treaty.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under subsection 227.1, irrespective of whether the LP has assets; CRA may enforce through director assessments, garnishments, seizure of personal assets, and restrictions on leaving Canada.
