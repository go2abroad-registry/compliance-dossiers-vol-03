Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal corporate income tax (Part I of the Income Tax Act), Goods and Services Tax / Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST), and Business Number (BN) registry, administered via CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); British Columbia Ministry of Finance — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration, filing and remittance through the eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca); BC corporate income tax is collected by the CRA under the Federal-Provincial Tax Collection Agreement with the Province of British Columbia.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the entity under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the British Columbia Business Corporations Act; the articles/certificate of incorporation generate the base CRA Business Number (BN) of 9 digits plus 2-character program account codes (RC for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RT for corporate income tax). Step 2 — Register for GST/HST online through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), by telephone (1-800-959-5525), or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts); a non-resident foreign entity must file Form RC1 with the foreign registration certificate, a completed Form RC2 (Non-resident account request) where applicable, and evidence of Canadian nexus (carrying on business in Canada or holding registrable property). Step 3 — Register for BC PST through the eTaxBC portal using Form REG 108Q; registration is mandatory for any seller making taxable retail/wholesale sales in BC unless the administrator grants a small seller exemption under the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act (ACP 153) for episodic low-volume activity. Step 4 — For non-resident suppliers of digital services (automated subtitle transcription and AI voice cloning), the CRA offers a simplified non-resident GST/HST registration class for specified digital services and software; a foreign entity without a permanent establishment in Canada may still register voluntarily to zero-rate exported services and claim input tax credits (ITCs) on Canadian inputs. Step 5 — File annual corporate income tax returns (T2) with the CRA and PST returns (REG 400) periodically with the BC Ministry of Finance; no separate provincial corporate registration certificate is required beyond the BN and PST account.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian federal or provincial business corporation is a separate taxpayer for federal Part I corporate income tax and for BC provincial corporate income tax; Canada does not permit US-style check-the-box pass-through classification. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian federal or provincial business corporation is a separate taxpayer for federal Part I corporate income tax and for BC provincial corporate income tax; Canada does not permit US-style check-the-box pass-through classification. Territorial scope: a Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation engaged in or carrying on business in Canada is taxed under Part I on Canadian-source income (with the permanent establishment and sourcing rules of ITA ss. 115 and 2(3)) and on gains from disposition of taxable Canadian property. This entity qualifies as a specified service business and is a general corporate taxpayer, not an eligible small business corporation unless ownership tests under ITA s. 125(6) are met; the 9% small business deduction and the 9% BC small business deduction may apply to active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), while non-resident owners do not disqualify CCPC status provided 25% or more of the shares are not owned by non-residents.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box entity classification election; the principal statutory elections relevant to this structure include: (i) GST/HST s. 150 election (Form GST25) to treat related resident corporations as a single registrant for GST/HST purposes, thereby simplifying intercompany invoicing; (ii) the foreign affiliate election under ITA s. 93(1) to reduce capital gains on disposition of shares of a foreign affiliate; (iii) hybrid mismatch arrangement rules under ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 20.5 and 18.21, effective for taxation years beginning after September 30, 2023, which disallow deductions for hybrid mismatch payments (structured with no generic entity-level hybrid election); (iv) the s. 216 election for a non-resident to file a net-basis return on Canadian rental income (not applicable to service income); and (v) for GST/HST, the annual filing election (Form GST113A) and the election to file fiscal-year installments. No partnership or trust pass-through election is available to a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the GST/HST small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1): a person is a small supplier if the total taxable worldwide revenues (including associated persons) are CAD 30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and in the aggregate of the preceding four calendar quarters; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory with effect from the day of exceedance, and the entity must collect 5% GST on taxable supplies within Canada. BC PST has no general monetary registration threshold — every vendor selling taxable goods or services in BC must register for PST, except that the administrator may exempt a small seller (ACP 153) whose activity is occasional, temporary or short-term; non-resident sellers of digital products may apply for the simplified non-resident PST registration program.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V where the supplier exports tangible personal property in accordance with the Export of Goods Regulations (B13A declaration or direct shipment evidence). Services: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part VII when supplied to a non-resident customer, provided the service is not performed wholly or partly in Canada for the customer's use in Canada; for an automated subtitle transcription and AI voice cloning studio, subtitling, dubbing, audio mastering and post-production services delivered electronically to foreign distributors, streaming platforms or broadcasters qualify for 0% GST/HST when the invoiced customer is a non-resident and the benefit is consumed abroad. Specified digital services (SDS) supplied to a non-resident individual (consumer) are zero-rated or outside the scope of Canadian GST/HST. BC PST: a direct export is exempt where tangible deliverables are shipped to a destination outside BC, and digital goods/software delivered electronically are not subject to PST when the place of consumption is outside BC; the vendor must retain a completed BC direct-export exemption record (Form FIN 453) or equivalent third-party evidence of offshore delivery. Documentary substantiation of non-resident status must be obtained prior to or at the time of invoicing, including foreign GST/VAT registration numbers, offshore billing addresses, IP geolocation logs and delivery metadata.

  • For physical deliverables (master tapes, hard drives, optical media): Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or Automated Export Declaration through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), plus Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • For pure digital deliverables (no customs transaction): commercial invoice, signed service agreement or statement of work identifying the foreign customer and zero-rated supply, digital delivery metadata (server logs, timestamped IP geolocation evidence of export from Canada and receipt outside Canada)
  • Non-resident status evidence: foreign business registration certificate, foreign VAT/GST identification number, or completed CRA Form GST11 (Certificate of a Non-Resident Person) / equivalent exemption certificate
  • Foreign exchange and settlement evidence: inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103 confirmation, or Stripe/Wise payout reports showing funds credited from a non-Canadian account
  • BC PST direct-export exemption form (Form FIN 453) and delivery records demonstrating that possession or control of the goods or digital content transferred outside British Columbia

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime (unlike Mexico CFDI, Italy SDI or Brazil NF-e). CRA accepts electronic invoices in PDF, EDI, XML and PEPPOL BIS 3.0 formats; since July 2021, the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is published for voluntary adoption by federal, provincial and private-sector entities. GST/HST returns (Form GST34) must be filed and remitted electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or a CRA-approved EDI service provider once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million, or when total revenues exceed CAD 65 million for electronic filing of T2 returns. BC PST returns (Form REG 400) must be filed via eTaxBC with remittance monthly, quarterly or annually depending on PST collection volume. Statutory records (ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230) must be retained for six years, including invoices, signed service contracts, delivery logs and foreign exchange records, and CRA may conduct electronic-record audits requiring machine-readable formats.

GST/HST: failure to remit collected tax triggers a penalty of 5% of the unremitted amount plus 1% per month for up to 12 months (ETA s. 280), with prescribed-rate interest (currently 6% plus compounding) under ETA s. 280.1. Misstatements or omissions attributable to carelessness, neglect or wilful default attract a penalty of up to 25% of the understated tax (ETA s. 285.1), and gross negligence on the corporate T2 return invokes an additional 25% penalty under ITA s. 163(2). Criminal tax evasion (ETA s. 327.1 and ITA s. 239) carries fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years. Export-zero-rating claims are audited under the CRA International Tax Audit Program, which cross-references B13A filings, bank SWIFT records, foreign exchange documentation and third-party industry data; wrongfully claimed zero-rating or refunds are reassessed within the normal four-year statutory period, which is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud or wilful default is established, and the CRA may apply large-supplier penalty multipliers under ETA s. 281.1 for repeat or late remittance issues. Clawbacks include repayment of the erroneous refund plus statutory interest computed from the date the refund was issued, and PST export exemptions are similarly reassessable for four years by the BC Ministry of Finance with penalties of 10% plus interest on unreported tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for current and capital account transactions under a floating exchange rate regime. No currency exchange controls implemented by the Currency Act or Bank Act. Profit remittance is unrestricted but subject to withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, frequently reduced under tax treaties), arm's length transfer pricing documentation, and factual substance reviews.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Canadian corporations may maintain foreign currency accounts and hold receipts indefinitely. FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply to cross-border currency movements above CAD 10,000 and international electronic funds transfers as set out in the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and related Cross-Border Currency Reporting Regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High for ancillary payment processing such as Wise Business, Payoneer, or Stripe Treasury; Medium-Low as a substitute for a primary bank account because non-bank EMIs are registered only as money service businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, lack Canadian deposit insurance under CDIC, cannot lend in Canada without a banking licence, and rely on partner bank clearing rails in Canada. Offshore EMI onboarding can comply, but certain high-value escrow mandates and commercial LCs normally require a tier-1 Canadian Schedule I bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Utilize SWIFT MT103 remittance from the foreign investor's bank to the Canadian entity's CDS or Schedule I bank settlement account. ISO 20022 purpose codes to be applied as 'BCP' (Business and Corporate banking services) for share premium and 'G20' (Equity and Investment Fund Shares) for paid-up capital, with reference 'Share Subscription Agreement – [CA corp name]'. Prior to remittance, complete Investment Canada Act notification screening if applicable (not expected for a post-production subtitle studio); record the direct investment with Statistics Canada via the quarterly survey; and for incoming wire amounts above CAD 10,000, ensure FINTRAC international EFT report is filed by the receiving Canadian bank. Funds then become available as undistributed treasury capital, subject to compliance with British Columbia Securities Act restrictions if issuing shares to foreign investors.For vendor and licence fees, use SWIFT MT103 with pain.001 mandate and purpose code '6531' (Licensing fees) or '1308' (Digital deliverables) as relevant. For procurement of high-value software/hardware and licensed audio corpora, use Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) with the Canadian bank as advising and confirming bank, payable at sight, with shipping/invoice/insurance documents presented. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is expected where the aggregate value of specified transactions with non-resident non-arm's-length persons exceeds C$1,000,000 in the taxation year; Form T106 must then be filed. The OECD-style Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least €750,000,000 (approximately C$1,050,000,000).

Economic substance scope: British Columbia and Canada do not operate Cayman/UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, economic substance is tested through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), common-law substance-over-form principles, and treaty limitation-of-benefits/beneficial-ownership rules. A Canadian PE or treaty claimant must maintain real management, personnel, assets, functions, and business risk allocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: On indictment, ITA s. 239 imposes imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of 50%–200% of the tax evaded; on summary conviction, imprisonment up to 2 years plus similar fines. The civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) is 50% of the understated tax, plus arrears interest and compounded daily penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or sign false returns may be prosecuted criminally as parties to the offence. Although they are not automatically liable for corporate income tax, they are jointly liable for unremitted source deductions or Part XIII withholding under ITA s. 227.1, and can face civil restitution, travel restrictions imposed as bail conditions, or imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke the GAAR (ITA s. 245) to disregard the arrangement and recharacterize the payments, resulting in 25% Part XIII withholding assessments, arrears interest, and penalties. Transfer pricing adjustments attract a 10% penalty under ITA s. 247(3) where the adjustment exceeds the prescribed threshold. False or misleading information penalties apply under ss. 162/163.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or execute royalty, dividend, or interest payments without remitting Part XIII withholding are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1. If the scheme is part of a fraudulent design, directors can be prosecuted under Criminal Code fraud provisions (maximum 14 years), be subject to personal restitution orders, and face travel bans through court-ordered conditions.
AML / Cross-Border Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Failure to file T1135 can trigger a penalty of CAD 25 per day, minimum CAD 100, up to CAD 2,500, with an additional 5% of the cost amount of unreported property where the default exceeds 24 months. T1134 failures carry CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500; CbC reporting failures attract CAD 2,000 per month. Money laundering is punishable under the Criminal Code (s. 462.31) by imprisonment up to 14 years, and under the PCMLTFA by fines up to CAD 500,000 for each offence.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or participate in the non-filing or laundering are personally liable for prosecution, imprisonment, penalties, and forfeiture of personal assets. Travel bans and court-ordered restrictions may be imposed during money-laundering investigations. Where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud, directors are exposed to civil restitution claims and personal collection of evaded taxes.
