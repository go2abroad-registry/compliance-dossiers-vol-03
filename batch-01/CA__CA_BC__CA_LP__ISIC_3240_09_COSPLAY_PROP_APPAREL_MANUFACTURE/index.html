Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal income tax, GST/HST, and Business Number registration (portals: CRA My Business Account and the Register a Business service). British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and BC Employer Health Tax (EHT) (portal: eTaxBC). Federal corporate income tax and BC provincial corporate income tax are administered together by the CRA under the Tax Collection Agreement. Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) is the customs and export-reporting authority operating the Single Window / Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: file or register the Limited Partnership under the BC Partnership Act and Limited Partnership Act at the BC Corporate Registry through OneStop Business Registry, or submit an Extraprovincial Limited Partnership registration if formed outside BC. Step 2: obtain a CRA Business Number (BN), a 9-digit statutory identifier, for the partnership itself; complete Form RC1 (or Form RC348 for non-resident businesses) or use the CRA online business registration wizard, designating the representative partner (normally the general partner) as the person authorized to make elections and receive CRA notices. Step 3: activate the required CRA program accounts under the BN: RT (GST/HST) for goods and services tax, RP (payroll) if the LP employs BC staff, and RM (import/export) for customs transactions. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or from the first taxable supply in Canada if the partnership has ceased to be a small supplier. Step 4: register with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch as a PST vendor via eTaxBC; BC has no small-supplier turnover exemption for ordinary vendors. Step 5: if the LP is a non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada, provide a Canadian address for service or appoint an accredited representative under Form RC59. The LP is fiscally transparent for income tax and files only a T5013 Partnership Information Return; the CRA BN/RT number must appear on GST/HST filings, invoices, and export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is mandatory and follows the legal form. Available elections for the LP include: (a) GST/HST representative member election, under which the partners appoint one member to hold the BN/RT account and to file, remit, and claim input tax credits on behalf of the partnership; (b) partnership fiscal-period election under Income Tax Act subsection 249.1(4), allowing a non-calendar partnership year where the LP maintains a shared fiscal period with its partners; (c) voluntary GST/HST registration election for a small-supplier LP below CAD 30,000, permitting the charging of GST and recovery of input tax credits; (d) hybrid mismatch adjustment elections introduced by Canada's 2023 hybrid mismatch rules (applicable to payments made on or after July 1, 2022), which allow alignment of the deductible amount where a fiscally transparent LP is involved in a cross-border hybrid arrangement; and (e) BC PST registration is mandatory and not elective for vendors making taxable retail sales in BC, except for casual and occasional vendors. All elections must be filed with the CRA in prescribed form and within specified timelines.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 - the CRA small-supplier threshold, calculated on the partnership's worldwide revenues from taxable supplies, including revenues of associated persons, in any single calendar quarter or across the most recent four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, registration is mandatory. Below the threshold, registration is voluntary. BC PST: no CAD-denominated small-supplier threshold; every vendor making taxable retail sales of tangible personal property in BC in the ordinary course of business must register, while casual or occasional vendors are exempt. BC EHT registration applies only where the LP's annual BC remuneration exceeds CAD 500,000 and is a separate payroll registration from GST/PST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating is available where the cosplay merchandise is supplied in Canada by the registered LP and is exported by the supplier or by a common carrier acting on the supplier's instructions, with the goods delivered to a foreign consignee outside Canada and exported within a reasonable time after the supply; the LP must retain documentary evidence satisfying the Excise Tax Act and CRA export-evidence standards. Offshore services, such as custom tailoring or design services performed in BC for non-resident recipients, are generally zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the service is not for use in Canada and the recipient is not a Canadian GST/HST registrant. The LP may recover input tax credits for GST paid on Canadian manufacturing inputs even when all outputs are zero-rated. BC PST exemption applies concurrently only where the vendor ships or delivers the goods directly to a destination outside Canada and retains shipping and export records; a sale in which the BC purchaser takes possession in BC and later exports the goods remains PST-taxable. Export proceeds should be repatriated to a Canadian bank account within the 180-day documentary benchmark; invoices, e-commerce order logs from Stripe or Shopify Payments, shipping records, and customs declarations must reconcile to the foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA Form B13A / Automated Export Declaration filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or Single Window, mandatory where the export shipment value exceeds CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled or regulated.
  • Bill of Lading / airway waybill / international carrier manifest evidencing the physical outbound movement of the merchandise from Canada to the foreign consignee's address.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank or fintech payment proof (SWIFT MT103, wire confirmation, Stripe payout record, or Shopify Payments settlement statement) evidencing receipt of foreign currency and its conversion into Canadian dollars.
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract showing Incoterms, the foreign delivery address, the LP's Business Number, and an explicit zero-rated export annotation.
  • Carrier-signed proof of export, such as final tracking records showing customs exit, or a warehouse receipt confirming removal from Canada.
  • Foreign exchange conversion record and Canadian bank deposit confirmation documenting the repatriated CAD proceeds within the 180-day window.

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, no government invoice pre-clearance, and no digital stamping regime. Electronic invoices are valid for GST/HST if they contain the statutory elements prescribed by the Excise Tax Act (supplier identity and BN/RT program account, transaction date, description of goods, consideration, and the GST/PST amount or an indication that tax is included; invoices over CAD 150 must show tax and total separately). The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework, built on the Peppol network, is voluntary and not yet mandated. The only mandatory electronic clearance relevant to exports is the CBSA export declaration transmitted through the Single Window / Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), which is required where the value of exported goods exceeds CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled or regulated; low-value export shipments may proceed without a CERS declaration, provided documentary evidence of export is retained.

Export zero-rating is a CRA audit priority because of e-commerce and cross-border retail exposure. CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, or without time limit where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation or committed fraud, to reverse the 0% rate, re-apply 5% GST, deny input tax credits, and charge compound prescribed rate interest on the net tax from the original due date. Gross negligence penalties under Excise Tax Act subsection 280(1) may reach 50% of the net tax understated, with a minimum penalty of CAD 100, where a false statement or omission is made knowingly or under circumstances of negligence. Comparable Income Tax Act subsection 163(2) penalties may apply where the flow-through income of the partners is affected. BC PST is independently reassessable: an unsubstantiated export claim is recharacterized as a taxable BC retail sale, triggering 7% PST, interest, and penalties. In aggravated cases, CRA may cancel the GST/HST registration retroactively, require security, or pursue criminal tax evasion charges under Excise Tax Act section 327 or Income Tax Act section 239, with substantial fines and imprisonment available. Missing or inconsistent export documentation, stale bills of lading, or an absence of FX repatriation beyond the 180-day benchmark are treated as badges of a domestic sale, converting the full supply into taxable net tax plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada imposes no general exchange controls; both current-account and capital-account transfers may be made freely, subject to AML/CFT reporting, economic sanctions screening, and Canadian income tax compliance. Profit remittances and capital repatriations by a BC limited partnership are legal without central-bank approval; any withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act and the relevant treaty must be satisfied before funds leave Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada, and no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation deadline applies. A BC LP may retain foreign-currency export earnings in a Canadian or offshore account. Exports must be reported through customs/statistical channels, and physical currency or wire transfers in excess of prescribed FINTRAC thresholds remain reportable, but no percentage-based surrender requirement exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), supplemented by the Minister of Finance for exchange administration, OSFI for prudential supervision, and FINTRAC for AML/CFT supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderately feasible with legal constraints. An offshore digital EMI has no statutory passport into Canada and cannot be treated as a bank under the Bank Act (Canada). A foreign fintech must, if carrying on business in Canada, register as a money services business with FINTRAC and satisfy any applicable BC provincial MSB licensing and consumer-protection rules. For treasury-critical inflows and outflows, the recommended structure is a Schedule I bank account as the account-holding layer, with the EMI used only for settlement acceleration, card acquiring, or payout facilitation through an approved Canadian PSP such as Stripe or Shopify Payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required if: (a) gross revenue exceeds CAD 10 million, or (b) transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million (for services) or CAD 5 million (for goods).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act applies to disallow transactions lacking economic substance. For partnerships, the CRA may scrutinize artificial allocations or lack of commercial rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Section 239 of the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: 50% gross negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner may be personally liable for unpaid withholding taxes (Section 227.1). Travel bans and seizure of personal assets possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties up to 50% of tax avoided. CRA may also impose third-party penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate general partners may be held liable for unpaid taxes if they participated in the scheme.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly fail to implement compliance programs.
