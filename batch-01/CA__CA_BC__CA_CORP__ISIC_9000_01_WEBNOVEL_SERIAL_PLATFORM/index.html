Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration for corporate income tax and GST/HST; provincial BC Ministry of Finance for PST; registration portal: CRA 'Business Registration Online' (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-registration-online.html

E-government filing portal

For a registered federal/provincial Business Corporation: after incorporation (via Corporations Canada or BC Corporate Registry), obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). The BN serves as the corporate TIN. If taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters, a separate GST/HST account must be opened (via BRO, My Business Account, or Form RC1). For non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada without a Canadian incorporation, a non-resident account with CRA is required; a BN and GST/HST registration are obtained in the same manner. BC PST registration (if applicable) is completed through the BC eTaxBC portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act; it files T2 corporate income tax returns and is subject to federal and provincial (BC) income tax. It may qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) if not controlled by non-residents. For GST/HST, the corporation is a registrant if over the threshold; BC PST applies separately.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box regime for entity classification. Available elections include: (i) s. 256 associated corporation elections affecting loss/credit sharing; (ii) GST/HST quick method or simplified accounting elections for small suppliers (gross revenue ≤ CAD 400,000); (iii) voluntary registration election for GST/HST refunds of input tax credits; (iv) NR301/NR302 elections for treaty-based relief on taxable Canadian property; (v) for non-resident corporations, a Part XIV election to file based on branch taxable income. Hybrid mismatch rules under s. 17.1 and s. 18.3 may also require election or disclosure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including online/electronic services to Canadians) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration allowed below threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital services (including web novel subscriptions) supplied to a non-resident individual or business are zero-rated for GST/HST where the supply is considered made to a person abroad (place-of-supply rules for digital services) and the service is consumed entirely outside Canada. Certain §§ of Schedule VI to the ETA (e.g., Part V, sections 7-13 for services) also zero-rate exported services if the recipient is a non-resident and the services are not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. For subscriptions via app stores, revenue attributable to non-Canadian users qualifies for zero-rating with documentation. BC PST does not apply to exports.

  • Signed service agreement or end-user license terms with non-resident subscriber
  • Geo-IP logs, billing address, and account registration data demonstrating residence outside Canada
  • Payout statements from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing showing settlement currency and foreign source of funds
  • Internal GST/HST zero-rated supply records (export ledger) and general ledgers
  • Payment processor or bank statements confirming receipt of foreign consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoice stamping mandate in Canada. Businesses must retain commercial records for six years. Invoices must show vendor BN/GST/HST number, date, brief description, and consideration. For platform-based sales (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing), the platform may be deemed the supplier for GST/HST purposes; if not, the app provider self-assesses/collects GST/HST. No API clearance or digital stamp duty is applied.

CRA scrutinizes zero-rating claims. If a supply is recharacterized as taxable in Canada, the full GST/HST plus interest is assessed. Negligence penalties up to 25% of the tax at issue apply under ETA s. 285; gross negligence may trigger 50% penalties. Fraudulent refund claims or false statements may be prosecuted with fines up to 200% of the tax and imprisonment (up to 5 years). Records must be retained six years; failure will result in denial of ITCs and reversals. Clawback of input tax credits previously claimed on exports may also be triggered.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible floating currency with no general exchange control restrictions on current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittance and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25%, reducible under applicable tax treaties) and must comply with standard corporate distribution documentation. There is no repatriation permit, no capital export license, and no foreign exchange rationing. In British Columbia, no separate provincial exchange control applies; however, cross-border wire transfers, beneficial ownership disclosures, and source-of-wealth substantiation remain subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds and AML compliance expectations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency, held in domestic or foreign bank accounts, and converted freely. No statutory timeline for repatriation exists; nevertheless, invoice settlement proceeds received by a Canadian business corporation must be remitted to its Canadian operating account to support accurate GST/HST, income tax, and financial statement reporting. For digital content exports (webnovel subscriptions and in-app purchases), the commercial settlement via Apple and Google payment rails is treated as export revenue and may be received in foreign currency without any mandated conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central monetary authority; cross-border foreign exchange administration and transaction monitoring governed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with legal restriction notice. Legally, an offshore electronic money institution (EMI) holding client funds in Canada is not a chartered bank and may not accept deposits or provide Canadian retail banking services unless authorized under the Bank Act or operating as a licensed money services business in each applicable province. For treasury use, an offshore EMI may be used only as a supplementary FX conversion and international payment conduit, not as the primary account where subscription revenues are settled. The EMI must be registered with FINTRAC as a foreign money services business if it provides services to Canadian residents or businesses, must maintain segregation of client funds, and must comply with Canada's Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) record-keeping and reporting obligations. The primary operational and escrow accounts must remain at a Schedule I Canadian bank to preserve corporate transparency, banking stability, and auditability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be transmitted by SWIFT-compliant wire or a verifiable digital transfer from the shareholder's own bank account. The wire should include the purpose code 'Equity Injection / Capital Contribution' and the corporate legal name, and must be accompanied by a board resolution authorizing the share issuance or the deemed capital contribution. If funds are delivered in Canadian dollars, a currency conversion may be executed internally by the receiving bank; for significant injections, the bank will require an explanation of the transaction's economic substance. In British Columbia no import permit is required; however, the receiving Canadian bank must verify the beneficial ownership of the incoming payment and may request the sender's banking KYC package.
