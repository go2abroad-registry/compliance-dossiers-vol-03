Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permissible subject to BC Business Corporations Act s. 53 and the Memorandum of Association amendments. The company must maintain a registered office in British Columbia, at a physical address where documents can be served during regular business hours. A virtual office (a third-party service provider physical address with office-visual and mail-forwarding services) is generally accepted by the BC Registry as a valid registered office, provided the address is not a postal box and is capable of receiving service of process. However, for a digital health app company, trade-name and privacy regulators do not require a physical operational presence in BC; a 'registered office' can be a mail-forwarding service. Nevertheless, revenue authorities (CRA) may assess commercial substance under the common-law residency rules; a virtual office without a place of business, business bank account, and decision-making functions in Canada may result in non-resource operations or permanent establishment risk. For GST/HST purposes, CRA requires a Canadian address for registration; a virtual office suffices for correspondence but not for establishing statutory residency if central management and control is outside Canada. Given Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, jurisdictional Nexus may be created through app stores for digital services taxation; BC PST registration may require a physical presence or 'taxable distribution' in BC. The commercial substance requirement is satisfied by maintaining a director resident in Canada, a Canadian bank account, and a registered office, but for a wholly foreign-owned company with a Canadian-resident director, the company may still be considered a resident of Canada for tax purposes under the Income Tax Act if the director exercises management and control in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian-resident business corporation is separately taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), on worldwide income; the entity is not a pass-through vehicle. BC imposes provincial corporate income tax at the entity level, and no check-the-box pass-through classification is available under Canadian law.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election. Available statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s. 85(1) rollover for tax-deferred transfers of property to a corporation; (ii) s. 216(1) election for non-residents to pay Part I tax on Canadian rental income or timber royalties; (iii) s. 219 election allowing a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada to elect Part I taxation in place of Part XIV branch tax, subject to statutory conditions; (iv) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related registrants to exclude intercompany supplies from GST/HST; and (v) GST/HST annual-filing and quick-method elections for eligible small registrants. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 18.6-18.7 operate automatically without elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: mandatory registration for a supplier, including a non-resident digital-economy supplier, when worldwide taxable supplies (excluding financial services and goodwill) exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. BC PST: mandatory registration as a seller/collector once gross revenue from taxable sales in British Columbia exceeds the small-seller threshold of CAD 10,000 in the preceding 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Goods exported by the supplier as part of a supply to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is a personal service rendered in Canada to an individual. The health-fitness app tracker (digital software license/subscription and offshore biometric telemetry services) supplied to a non-resident customer qualifies for 0% GST/HST where the recipient is a non-resident, consideration is attributable to the offshore supply, and contemporaneous evidence of non-resident status is retained. If distributed through Apple App Store or Google Play, the marketplace operator may be the deemed supplier for GST/HST, but the Canadian corporation must still document export sales to avoid recharacterization.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A Trading Exports process / Canadian Automated Export Declaration CAED) where a physical export of goods occurs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing departure of goods from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement record (SWIFT, wire transfer, or foreign-currency conversion report) evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Digital export evidence: signed contract or order record, IP address/geolocation logs, app-store export sales reports, and customer non-resident status confirmation for electronically supplied services

None. Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, government e-invoicing API validation, or digital stamping. GST/HST registrants must issue documents satisfying Excise Tax Act s. 221 content requirements and retain complete books and records, including electronic records, accessible to the CRA. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol/UBL, ISO 20022) is voluntary and not tax-cleared by the CRA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted for non-residents: profit remittances, royalty payments, and repatriation of invested capital require no conversion approval or central-bank licence. Statutory constraints are exclusively fiscal and corporate-law based: (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding on outbound non-resident dividends at 25% statutory, reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US, Canada-China, and Canada-UK treaties; (ii) the solvency test under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) (SBC 2002, c. 57) for all distributions; and (iii) mandatory reporting of electronic funds transfers of CA$10,000 or more to FINTRAC. No capital-gating, prior-approval, or exchange-certificate regime exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts or app-store settlement proceeds. USD received from Apple and Google may be held indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at any Canadian Schedule I bank without conversion. Residual obligations: FINTRAC EFT reporting for aggregated intra-day inflows of CA$10,000 or more; CBSA B13 export declarations are inapplicable to digital services; and standard bank sanctions/PEP screening applies to all inbound remittances.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential supervisor of Schedule I/II banks; FINTRAC as financial intelligence unit under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Payments Canada as clearing operator (Lynx RTGS and ACSS). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange administration; the Minister of Finance holds residual policy authority under the Currency Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium - permissible only as a payment facilitator, not as a substitute for a Schedule I depository account. A foreign electronic-money institution (EMI) such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, or Currenxie must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA before lawfully onboarding a BC-domiciled corporate client; unregistered offshore EMIs cannot settle Apple or Google disbursements in Canada. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate: 27.0% (15% federal + 12% BC). Canadian-controlled private corporations are eligible for the small business deduction on qualifying active business income, yielding an effective combined rate of 11.0% on the first CAD 500,000 of taxable income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the end of the taxation year. For a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is June 30 of the following calendar year. Income tax balances generally are due two months after year-end (for most CCPCs) or three months after year-end (for other corporations), subject to monthly instalment requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and retained where the aggregate value of controlled transactions subject to the arm's length standard reaches or exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year, and the CCA/Country-by-Country and Master File threshold applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue equal to or in excess of CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general Economic Substance Regulations in the Caribbean format, but CRA may disregard profit-shifting arrangements lacking economic substance through GAAR under ITA s. 245 and the transfer-pricing anti-avoidance rules. A Canadian-resident or non-resident company acting as a holding or licensing shell in BC must demonstrate real decision-making, substantive employees, and business risks in Canada to retain treaty eligibility and tax losses.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Income Tax Act s. 239: imprisonment for up to five years and fines of 50% to 250% of the taxes evaded; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; deemed interest and compounding penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorize false returns face joint and several liability for unremitted source deductions, GST/PST, and unpaid taxes; personal criminal prosecution, civil restitution, travel embargoes, and director disqualification orders are available.
Transfer Pricing Failures and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: CRA readjustment to the arm's length price; transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the adjustment (minimum CAD 10,000); denial of deduction; interest and late-payment surcharges; potential gross negligence penalty if documentation is false or absent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause to be signed false transfer-pricing documentation are exposed to civil restitution, severe monetary sanctions, and prosecution for fraudulent returns affecting their personal assets.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: GAAR may deny treaty and deduction benefits; CRA may impose a penalty on the value of the transaction and reassess tax as if the arrangement did not exist; failure to maintain a registered office and records in BC can lead to administrative dissolution under the Business Corporations Act (BC).

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a cloak for fraud or abusively avoids tax; directors incur personal liability for unpaid taxes and can be subjected to travel bans under tax-fraud enforcement frameworks.
AML and Payment Rails Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: BC civil forfeiture actions under the Civil Forfeiture Act; monetary penalties under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; CRA may disallow expenses not supported by credible payment gateway records; criminal liability for money laundering under the Criminal Code, s. 462.31.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or knowingly benefit from tainted payment flows face criminal prosecution, forfeiture of personal property, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as a director.
