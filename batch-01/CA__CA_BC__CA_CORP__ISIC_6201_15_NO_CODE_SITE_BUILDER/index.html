Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes/register.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian corporation must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via the CRA Business Registration Online portal. The incorporating jurisdiction (federal or British Columbia) provides the incorporation file number, which the CRA validates before issuing a BN. The corporation must then register for GST/HST (program account RT), payroll deductions (RP) if employees are engaged, and corporate income tax (RC). Non-resident directors and shareholders are permitted; the corporation must designate an authorized signatory resident in Canada for tax correspondence unless a non-resident authorized representative is appointed. A completed RC151 GST/HST registration request and Form RC152 (non-resident corporation registration) may be required if the corporation is a non-resident or has non-resident ownership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer the United States 'check-the-box' regime. However, the Income Tax Act permits certain elections: section 216 election for non-resident rental and timber income, section 217 election for non-resident employment income, and provincial allocation elections for interprovincial taxable income. For tax treaty purposes, a corporation can obtain a Part XIII waiver under section 215(6) and may make a deemed residency election under the Canada-US Convention. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed under subsection 18.4(4) and 18.5(1) of the ITA, which disallow deductions where foreign hybrid entities or instruments produce a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 gross worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration retroactively from the first day of the quarter of excess

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian supplier may zero-rate exports of tangible goods where the goods are delivered by the supplier or its agent to a non-resident customer outside Canada for export, subject to proper documentary evidence. Services supplied to a non-resident are generally zero-rated under section 23.5 of the Excise Tax Act if the supply is not a service relating to real property located in Canada, not a service delivered to an individual present in Canada, and not a service that is reasonably expected to be performed in Canada. For low-code web application builder services and digital platform subscriptions provided to non-residents with no Canadian nexus, the supply will be zero-rated if the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and the Canadian supplier maintains adequate evidence of the recipient's non-resident status. Corporate income tax transactions are not themselves zero-rated; GST/HST refunds are available for excess input tax credits attributable to zero-rated export supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian B13A) for goods; not required for pure digital services
  • Commercial invoice or service agreement identifying non-resident customer
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for hard-good software media if physically exported)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/foreign exchange record showing receipt of payment in foreign currency
  • Non-resident customer declaration or registration status certificate (if GST/HST-registered, provide valid registration number)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or government-operated digital stamping regime exists federally. Electronic invoices are permissible and must be retained in an auditable format usable by the CRA. The CRA offers API integration for business number validation and GST/HST return filing; GST/HST returns are transmitted via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI. British Columbia PST returns and remittances can be filed online through eTaxBC. Quebec and select sectors have additional e-document requirements, but those do not apply to a BC-incorporated corporation operating outside Quebec.

CRA may reassess zero-rated claims within four years (or later in cases of misrepresentation). If an exporter claims zero-rating without possessing the required export documentation, the supply becomes taxable and CRA may assess unremitted GST/HST, interest, and penalties. Wilful participation in false export documentation constitutes tax evasion under section 327.1 of the Excise Tax Act and section 239 of the Income Tax Act, leading to penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded or amounts withheld, potential fines up to CAD 10,000 per count plus imprisonment for up to five years. BC PST clawbacks are also possible where software or services are wrongly excluded and audited by the Ministry of Finance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Profit remittance, dividend payment, interest, royalty, and branch-profit transfers to non-residents are unrestricted by exchange-control rules; the sole statutory impost is non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (default 25%, generally reduced to 15% or 5% under the Canada-US and Canada-UK tax treaties). No pre-approval hurdle exists for repatriation, but cross-border wire transfers of CAD or foreign currency equivalent to CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the financial institution, and certain large transfers trigger source-of-funds verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Canada: there is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to surrender export proceeds. A British Columbia business corporation may lawfully maintain foreign-currency accounts (USD, EUR, GBP) with Canadian banks or foreign banks and may choose to convert or retain receipts without central-bank approval. The only applicable obligation is the general FINTRAC large-transaction reporting threshold and the CRA requirement to translate foreign-currency income into CAD for tax reporting at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is responsible for monetary policy and lender-of-last-resort functions; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally chartered banks; FINTRAC administers AML/CFT supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada maintains a market-determined floating exchange rate regime and has no designated foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control statute equivalent to SAFE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions with non-residents per fiscal period generally requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation in the form of a local file and economic analysis; multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least C$750,000,000 are additionally subject to country-by-country reporting and notification requirements.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations as seen in zero-tax jurisdictions. However, CRA applies Canadian transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, and treaty-shopping doctrines to deny benefits where a BC no-code builder lacks real management, personnel, decision-making, risk bearing, or operational substance in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Suppression of Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 239 of the Income Tax Act carries fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years. CRA may also apply a 50% gross negligence penalty on the understated tax plus compound interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or assent to the false return can be personally assessed for unremitted payroll and GST/HST, held liable on fraudulent conveyance theories, subjected to travel restrictions, and prosecuted personally where the corporation is used as a shield for fraud.
Transfer Pricing and Shell Company Arrangements

Statutory sanction: CRA may reprice the cross-border transaction under section 247, disallow deductions, and impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the net adjustment. Egregious cases attract gross negligence penalties and criminal evasion charges.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding on disguised distributions, and courts may pierce the corporate veil where the arrangement is a sham, an agency relationship, or a fraudulent preference.
Treaty Shopping and Withholding Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the full 25% Part XIII withholding, apply interest and gross negligence penalties, and recharacterize the transaction under the general anti-avoidance rule. Administrative penalties for making false statements may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false beneficial-ownership representations can be assessed personally for withheld amounts, face misleading-statement penalties, and be named in tax fraud investigations carrying asset freezes and travel bans.
AML and Fraudulent Money Movement

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, Criminal Code money-laundering provisions, and cross-border reporting rules can produce monetary penalties, forfeiture, fines up to 200% of avoided tax, and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in laundering revenues can be prosecuted personally, subjected to asset freezes, denial of removal or travel bans, and ordered to restore diverted funds to the corporation for payment of tax liabilities.
