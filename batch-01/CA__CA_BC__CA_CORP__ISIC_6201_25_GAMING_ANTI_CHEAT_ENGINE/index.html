Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC), federal competent authority for income tax and GST/HST; online portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. British Columbia administers its separate Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, BC eTaxBC portal: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the business corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (federal) or the BC Business Corporations Act via Corporations Canada / BC Corporate Online and obtain the incorporation number. Step 2: Apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'. Step 3: Open the GST/HST program account (BN suffix 'RT') either mandatorily when taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or voluntarily when the entity needs to zero-rate exports and recover input tax credits (ITCs). Step 4: A foreign-incorporated or non-resident-owned corporation without Canadian presence files Form RC160 'Non-Resident Application for GST/HST Registration' (or uses the CRA-approved online intake) and provides its foreign registration/TIN documents, articles of incorporation, and a Canadian contact or agent address. Step 5: If selling taxable goods or software to BC customers, register separately for BC PST through BC eTaxBC to obtain a PST vendor number. CRA issues a standardized 9-digit Business Number; the RT program account functions as the VAT/GST identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer for federal Part I income tax and BC provincial corporate income tax; if resident in Canada (corporations incorporated in Canada are statutorily deemed resident), it is taxed on worldwide income. No U.S.-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists for corporations. A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) may claim the small business deduction; a foreign-controlled private corporation (FCPC) does not qualify as a CCPC. Territorial_Scope: non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Canadian income tax law; classification follows actual incorporation and residence. Available statutory elections include: (a) Income Tax Act (ITA) s.85 rollover election to transfer eligible property to the corporation at agreed tax values; (b) ITA ss.86/51 reorganization, amalgamation and share-conversion elections; (c) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s.156 election for nil-consideration taxable supplies between closely related registered corporations; (d) ETA Quick Method election available to small GST/HST registrants; (e) administrative official-intermediary / branch-accounting elections for corporate groups. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss.18.18–18.21 apply automatically and cannot be overridden by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST registration is mandatory when total taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) made in Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters exceed CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is available below the threshold to claim ITCs and issue zero-rated invoices. BC PST registration is separately required for vendors making taxable sales of goods or software into British Columbia; small-seller and occasional-seller reliefs generally apply until PST taxable revenue exceeds approximately CAD 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. Goods: tangible personal property shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada is zero-rated when export is completed and evidenced within a reasonable time. Services and intangibles: a service, software licence or intellectual-property right supplied to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated unless the supply is directly in connection with real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or a service performed in Canada for an individual. Cross-border anti-cheat middleware/SaaS supplied to foreign gaming companies therefore qualifies where the recipient is a non-resident and does not acquire the supply for Canadian in-situ use or consumption. The exporter must maintain contemporaneous evidence of non-resident status, the signed contract/PO identifying the zero-rated supply, delivery/export records, and foreign-currency settlement; full ITC entitlement is retained for zero-rated export supplies.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) export declaration / e-export number (required for physical goods exports; digital-only deliveries may rely on download logs and server-access evidence instead).
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / carrier destination proof, and for digital supplies the contract, IP logs and server-delivery evidence showing supply to a non-resident destination outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank credit confirmation evidencing foreign-currency settlement (Canadian banks issue transaction advices rather than formal 'inward remittance certificates'; the item is retained to satisfy the standard export substantiation bundle).
  • Residency certification and foreign tax identification of the non-resident gaming licensee, together with the executed licence/SLA containing the zero-rating and export-delivery terms.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, transactional e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA accepts paper, PDF, EDI/XML and API-generated invoices. A valid GST/HST tax invoice under ETA s.169 and the supporting regulations must show: supplier name, 9-digit BN/RT account, date of supply, description, consideration, and tax amount or rate; simplified invoices are permitted for amounts below CAD 30. Canada participates in the Peppol network on a voluntary basis; there is no CRA pre-clearance or fiscal-intermediary mandate for foreign suppliers.

CRA requires exporters to retain substantiation records for six years from the end of the relevant taxation year. On audit failure, zero-rating may be disallowed, giving rise to reassessment of uncollected 5% GST plus any applicable provincial component, denial of input tax credits, statutory interest and penalties. Applicable penalties include: ETA s.285 failure/penalty provisions (CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 plus additional daily penalties), ITA s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax, false-statement penalties, and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ITA s.239. Retrospective clawback is permitted beyond the ordinary four-year limitation period in cases of misrepresentation, fraud or wilful default; fraudulent export zero-rating arrangements are referred to CRA's International, Large Business and Criminal Investigations programs. The Voluntary Disclosures Program is the standard route to limit penalty and interest exposure for non-fraudulent corrections.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalised exchange control regime. Neither the Bank of Canada nor any federal agency imposes exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. The CAD is freely convertible, profit remittance is permitted without exchange control approval; however, outbound dividends and royalty payments are subject to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and to standard FINTRAC reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender of export proceeds applies in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency and service receivables in offshore or domestic foreign-currency accounts. For corporate income tax filings, all receipts must be converted to CAD using the Bank of Canada published exchange rate on the applicable transaction date. Export declarations under the Customs Act are required above the prescribed statistical threshold, but no conversion ratio or repatriation timeline is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and financial stability authority; foreign exchange administration oversight executed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML and cross-border transaction reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderately feasible with caveats. Foreign digital EMI providers must be registered as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and maintain a domestic compliance framework under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Pure offshore EMIs without Canadian MSB registration cannot lawfully open settlement or operational accounts with Canadian banks nor clear payments into the Canadian banking system. Domestic fintech banking partners (e.g., Wealthsimple, Koho, or Payfare) offer ancillary treasury functionality but are not recommended as primary clearing infrastructure for cross-border trade.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan injections from non-resident investors are executed via SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire equivalent, with purpose code 'D' or 'BON' (capital contributions) or '700' (direct investment in a Canadian entity). The beneficiary must nominate the Canadian operating account and provide to the receiving bank a share subscription agreement, board resolution, and source-of-funds declaration.
