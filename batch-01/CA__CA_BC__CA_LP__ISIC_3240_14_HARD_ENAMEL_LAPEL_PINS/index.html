Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office address (registered agent's office or mail-forwarding service) is acceptable as the registered office address for the BC Limited Partnership under Partnership Act, RSBC 1996, c 348, s 49. However, the LP must maintain a physical operating address in BC for actual manufacturing, warehousing and inventory activity, which must be disclosed to the municipal business licensing authority and WorkSafeBC. The registered office cannot be a P.O. box; it must be a street address in BC at which the registered agent accepts service of process. For an e-commerce enamel pin manufacturer using Etsy and Stripe payment rails, the virtual office does not satisfy the business licence requirement; a physical production facility is mandatory for commercial substance and for CRA and provincial sales tax registration. The risk that a virtual-office-only arrangement is challenged as a sham is elevated if no tangible BC business assets or employees exist.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a Canadian limited partnership. A valid Canadian partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes: the partnership computes income, and partners are taxed on their allocated shares under the applicable partnership agreement and Canadian tax rules. The LP may have T5013 partnership information-return obligations. Available mechanisms include partnership income-allocation rules, a subsection 97(2) tax-deferred rollover for qualifying transfers of property to a Canadian partnership, subsection 85 elections for qualifying transfers to a taxable Canadian corporation, GST/HST joint-venture elections where statutory conditions are met, and elections or designations applicable to specific transactions. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they do not provide an entity-classification election. The legal classification of a foreign entity is determined under Canadian law and the relevant facts.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: generally CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding supplies of financial services and certain other excluded amounts, measured under the small-supplier rules over the applicable single-calendar-quarter or preceding four-consecutive-calendar-quarter period. Voluntary registration is permitted in many cases. British Columbia PST: no general threshold applies to a business required to collect PST; the CAD 10,000 small-seller threshold may apply only where all statutory small-seller conditions are satisfied. The threshold does not automatically apply to every manufacturer, wholesaler, marketplace seller, out-of-province seller, or business making taxable sales in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For GST/HST purposes, qualifying goods supplied in Canada and exported from Canada may be zero-rated where the statutory export conditions are satisfied and the supplier retains prescribed evidence that the goods were delivered or made available outside Canada. Documentation must establish the purchaser, goods, destination, shipment, export date, and the supplier's role in arranging or completing export. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where the specific place-of-supply and export-service provisions are met; non-resident status alone is insufficient. Services relating to tangible personal property situated in Canada, services connected with Canadian real property, and other statutory exclusions may remain taxable. A GST/HST registrant may claim input tax credits for eligible GST/HST incurred in making zero-rated supplies, subject to documentary and timing rules. British Columbia PST generally does not apply to goods purchased for export and delivered or shipped out of British Columbia where the applicable exemption conditions and evidence are satisfied, but the transaction must be analyzed separately from GST/HST.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, purchaser or consignee, complete goods description, quantity, value, currency, origin, tariff information where applicable, and delivery terms
  • Canadian export declaration through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), where required by the Export and Import Permits Act, customs rules, destination, value, commodity, or other applicable exception rules
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, postal record, or equivalent transport document showing shipment from Canada to a destination outside Canada
  • Packing list and proof of delivery or carrier tracking evidence sufficient to substantiate export and receipt outside Canada
  • Export permits, certificates, or controlled-goods documentation where required for the product or destination
  • Sales contract, purchase order, customer correspondence, and payment records linking the exported goods to the zero-rated supply
  • GST/HST and British Columbia PST books and records supporting the zero-rated or exempt treatment; an inward-remittance certificate is not generally a mandatory Canadian export-zero-rating document

Canada and British Columbia do not generally impose a universal real-time e-invoice clearance or digital tax-stamping mandate for ordinary business-to-business or business-to-consumer sales. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices or receipts containing prescribed information when required by customers and must retain reliable electronic records. CRA electronic-filing requirements apply to specified registrants and returns, including GST/HST returns in prescribed circumstances. Electronic records must be complete, readable, retrievable, and retained for the statutory period. British Columbia PST invoices and records must identify taxable and exempt amounts and support tax collection and remittance. No general API pre-clearance of each invoice is required, although marketplace, payment-processor, customs, and government electronic-reporting systems may impose separate technical requirements.

Canada has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days and no general mandatory inward-remittance certificate for GST/HST zero-rating. If export conditions are not met or evidence is inadequate, the CRA may reassess the supply as taxable, deny related input tax credits, assess tax, arrears interest, and applicable administrative penalties. Gross-negligence penalties can generally equal 50% of the understated tax or improperly claimed credit, subject to statutory computation; other penalties may apply for false statements, repeated failures, late filing, or failure to maintain records. Knowingly false claims may create criminal exposure under the Excise Tax Act. British Columbia may reassess PST, impose interest and penalties, and recover tax collected or improperly exempted. Customs non-compliance may result in duties, penalties under the Administrative Monetary Penalty System, seizure, export-reporting sanctions, or prosecution. Voluntary disclosure relief may be available only if the statutory program conditions are satisfied and does not automatically eliminate all tax, interest, or penalty exposure.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Bank of Canada Act. Current account transactions, including profit remittances and repatriation of funds for legitimate corporate operations, face no restrictions. Capital account transactions are subject to general reporting obligations but permit unrestricted repatriation for Canadian limited partnerships and non-resident owners provided tax filings are current and beneficial ownership is disclosed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds is required under Canadian foreign exchange regulations. Export receipts must be recorded for Canada Revenue Agency tax reporting purposes only. Conversion to CAD or other currencies occurs at the exporter's discretion without prescribed timelines or ratios, subject solely to anti-money laundering transaction reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% + BC provincial 8% = 23% combined general corporate rate; the BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level income tax — income is allocated to partners and taxable at partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents. A BC limited partnership allocating profits is not a 'dividend'; non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their share of Canadian-source business income, and a non-resident corporate partner may be subject to Part XIV branch tax of 25% on after-tax earnings (reduced to 15% under the Canada-US treaty; branch tax is generally not reduced by the Canada-China treaty).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before the 5th month after the partnership's fiscal year-end. If the LP's fiscal year-end is December 31, the filing deadline is May 31 of the following year.

T106 — Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents Schedule of reportable cross-border transactions between the LP (or its partners) and non-arm's length non-residents, including intercompany purchases, royalties, interest, and management fees. The LP must file T106 when aggregate reportable transactions exceed C$1,000,000 in a year; non-resident partner transactions must be disclosed at arm's length values. Late-filed T5013: C$25 per day (minimum C$100, maximum C$2,500) under ITA s.162(7.1); knowingly false return: C$500 per person per return (s.162(7.2)) plus a fine of 50%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded (s.239). Late T106: C$100 per day, up to C$2,500 per form.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 must be filed when aggregate reportable non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceed C$1,000,000 in a taxation year. Contemporaneous OECD-aligned Local File documentation is expected by the CRA for transactions exceeding C$1,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the C$750,000,000 consolidated group revenue level.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) voids abusive transactions lacking a real business purpose, imposing a 25% GAAR penalty on undisclosed tax benefits. A BC LP must demonstrate real economic activity (employees, premises, inventory, manufacturing and sales functions in BC) to avoid recharacterization of its status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years (ITA s.239(1)); gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (ITA s.163(2)).

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted payroll, GST/HST, and Part XIII withholdings (ITA s.227.1; ETA s.323); criminal conviction can result in a Canadian inadmissibility order, travel bans, and deportation proceedings under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act.
Shell Partnership / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization (ITA s.245): the tax benefit is denied, gross negligence penalty 50%, and a GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit; CRA can also reassess beyond the normal 3-year limitation period for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner bears unlimited liability for all LP debts; a nominee director who signs false T5013 returns faces personal fines and restitution orders; section 239 prosecution may lead to seizure of assets and prohibition from re-entering Canada.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, failing to report suspicious transactions or international electronic funds transfers can attract administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 for individuals; money laundering indictable offence: imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly launder proceeds through the LP's bank accounts can be prosecuted personally; the CRA can issue a requirement to pay to the payment processors, freezing funds; a criminal record triggers mandatory inadmissibility to Canada.
