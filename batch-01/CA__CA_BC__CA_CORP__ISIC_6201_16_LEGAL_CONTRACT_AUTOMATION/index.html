Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html for GST/HST and federal corporate income tax; BC Consumer Taxation Branch, Ministry of Finance - https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst for BC Provincial Sales Tax

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated corporation planning to carry on business in British Columbia must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); if annual taxable supplies worldwide exceed CAD 30,000 (the GST/HST small-supplier threshold), it must open an RT (GST/HST) account. Registration is also made through BRO. To collect BC PST, including on remote-delivered software/SaaS where applicable, the supplier must apply for a BC PST vendor number through BC eTaxBC (Ministry of Finance Consumer Taxation Branch); BC has no general PST registration threshold and every supplier making taxable sales in BC must register before making such sales. The BC corporate income tax account is administered by the CRA under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement; no separate provincial corporate tax account is required except for the provincial Registrar of Companies registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a British Columbia business corporation is a separate taxpayer; resident corporations pay federal and BC provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income, with non-resident corporations taxed only on Canadian-source business and capital gains; no check-the-box flow-through for ordinary business corporations)

Available election forms

Canada does not use US-style check-the-box entity classification. Ordinary business corporations are taxed at the entity level. Relevant elections include: (i) GST/HST non-resident election under ETA s.273 for supplies to a permanent establishment; (ii) GST/HST filing quarterly/annual elections and streamlined accounting for small suppliers; (iii) Income Tax Act tax-deferred elections for incorporations/rollovers (e.g., s.85) and treaty-based deemed-permanent-establishment decisions (hybrid mismatch rules under s.18.4, s.18.18-18.23 for excessive interest and hybrid arrangements); (iv) BC PST vendor consolidation or separate filing elections if using eTaxBC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made worldwide by the entity over any four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold; mandatory registration once exceeded, or voluntary registration available). BC PST: no monetary registration threshold - any person selling taxable goods or services in BC (including remote software/SaaS deemed taxable where applicable) must register as a PST vendor before making taxable sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, exports are generally zero-rated. For goods, zero-rating applies where the goods are exported by the vendor in compliance with Schedule VI Part V (e.g., B13 customs export declaration, commercial invoice and shipping evidence). For services and intangible property (e.g., LegalTech SaaS and e-signature authentication), zero-rating generally applies where the recipient is a non-resident at the time the supply is received, the supply is not made to a Canadian permanent establishment of that recipient, and the service or intangible property is not in respect of real or tangible property situated in Canada at the time of performance. A cloud-based legal document automation service supplied to a foreign counterparty with no Canadian nexus can therefore be billed at 0% GST/HST; the supplier should obtain a signed declaration of non-residence, a business address outside Canada, and a license/contractual restriction against use in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13) filed via the Canadian International Trade Single Window / customs broker for physical exports
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air/road) or other proof of export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 showing receipt of export proceeds
  • For zero-rated SaaS: executed SaaS subscription agreement/order form, invoice showing 0% GST/HST, evidence of foreign beneficial ownership/use, and non-residence declaration

Canada has no national real-time e-invoicing clearance mandate, no API-based fiscal stamping, and no digital stamping requirement for GST/HST or BC PST. A GST/HST registrant must issue a prescribed invoice (ETA s.169(1)) containing supplier and recipient names and addresses, the supplier's GST/HST registration number, the date, the total consideration, and the tax amount; electronic invoices are valid if legible and retained. Returns are filed through CRA's NETFILE/EDI channels (My Business Account or EDI). For e-procurement, Canada has adopted PEPPOL BIS in public-sector invoicing but this does not constitute a tax clearance obligation. BC PST vendors must issue a sales slip or invoice showing PST charged, and maintain records for audit.

CRA can reassess beyond the normal four-year period in cases of misrepresentation, fraud or omission under ETA s.298(1)/(2) and can recover uncollected GST/HST together with interest (s.280) and penalties (s.280.1, s.285) of up to 25% (or more for gross negligence and criminal fines). BC PST assessments may carry penalties under the Provincial Sales Tax Act, up to 50% for intentional evasion or assisting evasion, plus interest and restoration of unremitted tax; input tax credits are clawed back if exports are not substantiated. Documentary insufficiency, source-code audit, or non-repatriation of foreign exchange within a reasonable period may trigger a reassessment of export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; the Canadian dollar is freely convertible, and Canada maintains no exchange-control regime restricting current-account or capital-account transactions. Under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) the CAD is full legal tender, and there are no requirements for prior approvals, exchange-rate surrender, or import/export licenses for payments or repatriation. Profit remittance is unrestricted: non-resident shareholders may receive dividends, and non-residents may repatriate capital and liquidation proceeds without central-bank clearance. The only friction is fiscal, not exchange-control: Part XIII withholding tax of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) at the 25% statutory rate, reduced to 5% or 15% on dividends under the Canada–US Tax Convention (1980, as amended), 15% branch tax on remitted branch profits, and 0% royalty withholding for US residents under treaty. Payments originating from OFAC/US-, EU-, or UN-sanctioned jurisdictions remain blocked under Canadian sanctions law administered by Global Affairs Canada, and the Banking Act AML rules require SWIFT EFT reports to FINTRAC for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriating export proceeds and no mandated conversion ratio to CAD. A British Columbia SaaS exporter (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541199) may invoice in USD, CAD, or any freely convertible currency, may hold foreign-currency balances in Canadian Schedule I bank accounts or offshore accounts, and may remit such proceeds at the commercial spot rate through an authorized FX dealer. Bookkeeping and tax-filing obligations apply (CRA functional-currency reporting permitted under s. 261 ITA), and the bank must file PCMLTFA large-EFT reports for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. Export revenue from cloud services is treated as a services supply under the GST/HST rules; zero-rated if exported, but no FX surrender is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); AML/ATF enforcement is vested in FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17); payments oversight is administered by Payments Canada (Lynx, ACSS, and Real-Time Rail), with the Bank of Canada regulating retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for ancillary treasury, Low as a primary clearing rail. Canadian law does not recognize an independent 'EMI' licence; all non-bank payment providers must hold a federal retail-payment registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23) and a provincial money-services business (MSB) registration where applicable (FICOM in British Columbia). Wise (Wise Payments Canada) and Stripe Treasury operate as sponsored MSBs with transactional clearing through a Schedule I bank, not as direct participants in Payments Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer) in Canada are treated as money-service businesses, must register with FINTRAC, and are legally barred from holding client funds in Canada except through a regulated Canadian depositary; their virtual accounts do not enjoy Lynx accession or CDIC insurance and create sub-custody risk. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (i) adopt a board resolution authorizing the capital injection as paid-up share capital or a shareholder loan; (ii) issue shares and update the Central Securities Register and BC Reg. transparency appendix; (iii) transmit the funds via SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 (Remittance Information) reading 'CAPITAL INJECTION / SHARE CAPITAL' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN', the ordering customer name exactly matching the KYC profile, and the beneficiary account held at the receiving Schedule I bank; (iv) for CAD wires executed domestically, clear through Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS) sweeps from the correspondent, or for USD wires through the bank's US presence (RBC/TD/BMO operate onshore US bank subsidiaries, enabling Fedwire settlement without intermediary deductions); (v) the receiving bank files a FINTRAC large-EFT report for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and executes standard OFAC-screening. No central-bank approval is required, but the Investment Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 28 (1st Supp.)) requires a post-establishment Notification where a non-Canadian establishes a new Canadian business — indistinguishable for a newly incorporated BC corporation controlled by non-residents — and the notification form must be filed online within 30 days. If routed as debt rather than equity, the shareholder loan must respect the ITA thin-capitalization ratio (1.5:1 debt-to-equity vs. specified non-resident shareholders). All closing documents (subscription agreement, issued share certificates, funds-provenance letter, and the MT103) must be retained in the bank's CDD file and in the corporate records book; the capital injection itself is non-taxable with no GST/HST or provincial tax reconciliation.Primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for cross-border vendor, license, and royalty payments, with charge code OUR for full-beneficiary-credit routing; CAD-denominated domestic payments via Payments Canada Lynx with same-day finality; (ii) USD cross-border payments through the bank's US dollar segment — best executed by instructing the same bank's US-incorporated subsidiary to clear over Fedwire/CHIPS, eliminating correspondent float; (iii) Commercial documentary letters of credit (LC) for procurement of goods and hardware, issued under UCP 600 with advising/confirming bank in the seller's jurisdiction — confirmed LCs recommended for non-OECD suppliers; (iv) Stripe and credit-card acquiring receivables settle on a T+2 Visa/Mastercard cycle into the Operational Account in CAD or USD, with the merchant acquirer holding no treasury authority; (v) intercompany settlements and SaaS subscription payouts via Wise or other FINTRAC-registered MSBs permitted only as zero-balance ancillary rails with the sponsor-bank audit trail preserved. The bank will apply charge-back and return-billing rules consistent with PCMLTFA for outbound EFTs above CAD 10,000, and the company must maintain an annual treasury schedule documenting all outbound MT103s with purpose codes (e.g., 'LICENSE FEE', 'INVOICE 2024-091') to satisfy FINTRAC and CRA transfer-pricing documentation.Deliverable FX Forward (CAD/USD; CAD/EUR; CAD/GBP) for tenors up to 24 months, documented under the ISDA 1998 FX and Currency Options Definitions and a bank Master FX Agreement, with no CSA required for non-speculative commercial hedgesFX Swap (simultaneous spot and forward contract) executed with the relationship bank as dealer, utilized for rolling currency conversion of USD subscription receivables into CAD operating balancesVanilla FX Option (CAD call / USD put and CAD put / USD call) — purchased options permitted for hedging forecast subscription revenue; written options generally disallowed for unhedged corporates without an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and a fully executed Credit Support AnnexCross-Currency Swap (CAD/USD) exchanging fixed or floating interest and principal, available for term-debt alignment or long-dated capex liabilities, subject to ISDA Master Agreement, Schedule, and CSA with a collateral threshold and posting mechanics negotiated at closingNon-Deliverable Forward (NDF) — not market standard for CAD because the Canadian dollar is fully convertible and a robust deliverable forward market exists; NDFs would be permissible only for exotic-pair settlements where physical delivery is legally impossible, and the bank would price them as an offshore non-Canadian booking

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for ancillary treasury, Low as a primary clearing rail. Canadian law does not recognize an independent 'EMI' licence; all non-bank payment providers must hold a federal retail-payment registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23) and a provincial money-services business (MSB) registration where applicable (FICOM in British Columbia). Wise (Wise Payments Canada) and Stripe Treasury operate as sponsored MSBs with transactional clearing through a Schedule I bank, not as direct participants in Payments Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer) in Canada are treated as money-service businesses, must register with FINTRAC, and are legally barred from holding client funds in Canada except through a regulated Canadian depositary; their virtual accounts do not enjoy Lynx accession or CDIC insurance and create sub-custody risk. For a LegalTech SaaS entity targeting Stripe and credit-card receivables, the compliant structure is Stripe Payments Canada (a FINTRAC-registered MSB) settling into a Schedule I Operational Account, supplemented by a cross-border payout channel via Wise only for zero-balance contractor disbursements with a documented sponsor-bank audit trail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined; federal Part I general corporate tax at 15% and British Columbia provincial general corporate tax at 12% (active business income subject to general rates).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% – Part XIII of the Income Tax Act requires a Canadian resident corporation paying dividends to a non-resident individual or corporation to withhold 25% of the gross dividend, unless a tax treaty applies to reduce the rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is mandatory where the Canadian taxpayer and all non-arm's-length persons have aggregate gross revenue of at least CAD 1,000,000 in the year; separate CRA Master File (Form T5023) and Local File (Form T5024) obligations apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 50,000,000 or more. For a smaller LegalTech SaaS entity, documentation is still advisable when any cross-border intercompany transaction (e.g., royalty payment to a parent, platform licence fee, or management charge) is present.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime akin to offshore centres; however, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) and the substance-over-form doctrine when entities and transactions lack economic substance. For a BC corporation, a critical mass of Canadian payroll, cloud infrastructure contracts, board meetings, bank accounts, and CRS/FATCA indicia must exist. If the entity is a mere corporate shell that derives profits from a marketing platform but has little or no local function, the CRA may reassess under GAAR and apply the Principal Purpose Test (MLI Article 7) to deny treaty benefits, and provincial corporate registries may strike the company for non-compliance with the BC Business Corporations Act.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Filing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, and/or imprisonment of up to 2 years on summary conviction (up to 5 years and a discretionary fine on indictment). Civil gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2) may reach 100% of the misreported amount plus 50% of understated tax. Reassessment periods are extended for misrepresentation (12 years).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve a false T2, or who are knowingly involved, are personally liable for the penalty and as parties to an indictable offence may face concurrent criminal liability. The corporate veil is pierced to obtain restitution from directors' personal assets if the corporation is a mere shell; a director may also be prevented from leaving Canada where CRA commences prosecution.
Transfer Pricing Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.247(3) imposes a penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (i.e., the excess amount adjusted at the tax rate), plus applicable interest. The CRA may also deny deductions for the adjustment under GAAR. For a big multinational group, failure to file the local/master file may lead to a penalty of CAD 500 or more per day, limited to CAD 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute the TP scheme with knowledge of the mispricing risk assessment may be found grossly negligent; the CRA can assess penalties jointly against the director as a 'non-arm's-length person' if the director is the controlling mind. If the corporation is insolvent, directors' liability for unpaid TP penalties may be asserted under ss.227.1 and 169.
