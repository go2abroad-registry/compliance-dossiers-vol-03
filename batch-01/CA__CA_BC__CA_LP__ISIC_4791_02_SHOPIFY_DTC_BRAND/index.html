Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, Ottawa, and its digital portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services.html); British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Revenue Division, for the 7% provincial sales tax (PST), administered via the eTaxBC portal (https://etax.gov.bc.ca/). Federal GST/HST and Income Tax Act compliance are centralized in CRA, while PST is a separate provincial levy collected directly by BC.

E-government filing portal

For the Canadian-resident BC Limited Partnership: (1) procure a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) in the LP's exact registered name through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or paper Form RC1, attaching the BC Registry certificate of limited partnership; the BN is the statutory TIN for all federal program accounts and is issued to the partnership as a 'person' for GST/HST and as a reporting entity for income tax. (2) Register for a GST/HST program account (BN + suffix 'RT0001') at the moment taxable activity in Canada commences; registration is compulsory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters, and voluntary below that small-supplier threshold; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must also appoint a resident representative for service and filings under ETA s. 311. (3) Register for BC PST via eTaxBC before the first taxable sale in BC; a vendor with a place of business in BC must register without threshold, while a remote/non-resident vendor becomes registrable once PST-taxable sales to BC consumers exceed CAD 10,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters under BC's July 1, 2021 e-commerce rules. (4) File the T5013 partnership information return annually within five months of fiscal year-end, allocate income to partners, and issue T5013 slips to resident partners and T5013/NR schedules for non-resident partners. Where the general partner is a foreign corporation, that partner must itself obtain a CRA BN and file Canadian corporate returns on its Canadian-source income under ITA s. 115; the LP nevertheless registers under its own BN. Each partner's separate foreign TIN is captured in T5013 schedules; there is no separate municipal income-tax identifier in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and provincial income tax; income/loss flows to partners under ITA s. 96 and no entity-level income tax is imposed. For GST/HST, the partnership is a distinct 'person' under ETA s. 123(1) and must register, collect, and remit GST/HST under its own BN. Territorial scope: the LP is a Canadian-resident partnership when central management and control is exercised in BC; non-resident partners are subject to Part I tax on their Canadian-source shares (ITA ss. 2(3), 115), with Part XIII withholding generally not applying to partnership business-income allocations but applying to certain passive Canadian-source amounts. Canada maintains no tax-exempt zones, and there is no elective entity-level corporation tax classification available without converting the legal form to a BC company.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; classification follows the legal form, so the BC LP is mandatorily a pass-through partnership and cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. Available statutory elections: (i) a partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 to establish a consistent non-calendar year-end for T5013 reporting where all partners are taxable residents; (ii) the GST/HST Quick Method election under ETA s. 281.1 for small suppliers; (iii) the joint election under ETA s. 167 to treat an otherwise exempt supply between related/registered persons as taxable (useful for intercompany cost recovery); (iv) income/loss allocation elections in the partnership agreement, subject to ITA s. 96 and the arm's-length/substantiality test; and (v) protective elections related to provincial PST exemptions on goods shipped out of BC. Hybrid-mismatch outcomes are governed by mandatory OECD-BEPS measures (notably ITA s. 18.25), which disallow deductions for structured hybrid arrangements; there is no opt-in or opt-out election under that regime.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

High convertibility. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible; there are no exchange-control permits, no central-bank approval for profit remittances, and no restrictions on holding foreign currency. Dividends or partnership profit distributions to non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (treaty-reduced where applicable) but not to exchange approval. Cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in any permitted currency or converted freely. However, international wire transfers and currency exchange transactions of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC; same-day, sequential, or otherwise connected transactions are aggregated.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Department of Finance Canada. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange control administration; FINTRAC supervises cross-border transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium as a settlement conduit, Low as a primary treasury account. An offshore digital EMI cannot act as a principal depository or clearing account for a BC limited partnership without carrying on money-services business in Canada, which requires FINTRAC MSB registration and, depending on activities, provincial licences. Regulated Canadian payment facilitators such as Shopify Payments and Stripe are viable for e-commerce settlement but are not substitutes for a federally regulated bank account.
