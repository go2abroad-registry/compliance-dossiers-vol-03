Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A physical street address in Canada is statutorily required under section 19 of the CBCA; the registered office must be situated in the province designated in the Articles and must be a genuine, accessible location for service of process during normal business hours; corporate records must be kept at the registered office or at another location in Canada designated by the directors. A registered agent's physical office may serve as the registered office and is permissible; a pure virtual office, post-office box, digital mailbox, or mail-forwarding-only address is not acceptable for federal corporate registration. For a digital PC/console game publisher operating via Steam and PlayStation Store with no brick-and-mortar presence, the use of a registered agent's commercial address or a shared physical office that accepts service of process is compliant, provided that the address is a real Canadian street address and the service capacity is maintained.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital filing and registration portal: My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic entity incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincial law obtains a federal Business Number (BN). The BN is acquired through the CRA Business Registration Online portal or by submitting Form RC1 with certified corporate documents (articles of incorporation, certificate of corporate status). For GST/HST purposes the entity opens a program account RT; for corporate income tax, an RC account; for border transactions, an RM account. A non-resident corporation making taxable supplies in Canada may register for GST/HST on a simplified basis using Form RC1 or the online non-resident registration pathway. The BN is typically issued within five to ten business days. Digital game publishing revenue through Steam and PlayStation Store payout rails is included in taxable supply revenue for registration threshold determination.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a CBCA business corporation resident in Canada is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income. No federal pass-through election exists for business corporations. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada through digital platforms is taxable on Canadian-source business income and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on certain Canadian-source payments. Territorial scope is worldwide for resident corporations and source-based for non-resident corporations.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a check-the-box entity classification regime. Relevant statutory mechanisms include: treaty-based relief under subsection 115(1) via Form NR301; an election under section 216 for non-resident rental income; the ETAct section 156 election for closely related corporations to exclude inter-corporate supplies from GST; the ETAct section 179 election for a foreign supplier to designate a Canadian agent for GST remittance; and hybrid mismatch outcomes addressed through the general anti-avoidance rule and the Multilateral Instrument rather than by affirmative domestic election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over any twelve-month rolling period. For non-residents, registration is mandatory when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 and taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000. Digital game sales via Steam and PlayStation count toward the taxable supply threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of intangible personal property including digital video game content made to non-residents, provided the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant and the supply is not for consumption in Canada. The applicable provisions are subsection 25.1(2) and Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act. Steam and PlayStation Store payout receipts are treated as zero-rated export revenue (GST/HST line 103 to 110) and permit full input tax credit recovery. Substantiation requires evidence of non-resident customer status, foreign payment settlement, and absence of Canadian consumption. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is adopted as an internal compliance benchmark for repatriation of Steam and PlayStation payouts, since Canadian tax reporting follows the accrual method.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) when physical media or hardware is exported; digital delivery server logs otherwise
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill for physical export legs
  • Inward remittance report or Steam Valve / PlayStation Store payout statement evidencing foreign-source settlement
  • End-user license agreement and publisher distribution agreement showing non-resident rights
  • GST/HST NETFILE return summary identifying zero-rated export amounts

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API validation, or digital stamping regime applies. Canada maintains an optional Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on Peppol interoperability. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information under section 169 of the Excise Tax Act and the related regulations, including supplier identity, BN, tax status, and tax amount. Invoices may be electronic or paper and must be retained for six years.

The CRA may reassess within four years of the relevant due date, with no time limit in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or failure to file. Incorrectly claiming zero-rating for supplies made to Canadian consumers results in assessment of understated GST/HST at the applicable rate, compounded daily interest at the prescribed rate (approximately 9% in early 2025), and potential penalties. The ETAct section 285 penalty for omission ranges from CAD 250 to CAD 25,000; section 327 criminal penalties for false statements extend to fines of up to 200% of the tax amount and imprisonment of up to five years. Export substantiation records must be retained for six years under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act and section 230 of the Income Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada has no general capital controls, no mandatory approval for ordinary foreign investment funding, and no general restriction on conversion between CAD and foreign currencies. Profit, dividend, interest, royalty, and service-fee remittances may generally be made offshore, subject to corporate solvency requirements, applicable withholding tax, transfer-pricing support, treaty eligibility, thin-capitalization rules, anti-avoidance rules, sanctions, AML review, contractual restrictions, and any applicable Investment Canada Act or competition review. Dividends must be declared in accordance with the applicable corporate statute and may not be paid if statutory solvency tests are failed. Canadian-source payments to non-residents can attract Part XIII withholding tax, generally at 25% absent a treaty reduction or exemption; the Canada-U.S. treaty and other treaties may reduce the rate depending on payment type, beneficial ownership, residence, and entitlement to treaty benefits. Interest, royalties, software-related payments, and platform payments require transaction-specific characterization. Banks may request evidence of ownership, tax residence, beneficial ownership, board authorization, source of funds, and the commercial basis for recurring remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into CAD or repatriated through a Canadian bank. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or received through foreign payment platforms, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, sanctions, AML, and reporting obligations. Physical goods exports are subject to applicable Canada Border Services Agency reporting and customs requirements. Digital publishing and platform receipts must be classified for tax and accounting purposes, including whether the company is earning royalties, licensing income, digital supplies, advertising income, or services. GST/HST treatment depends on the supply and customer location. Banks and payment providers may nevertheless impose internal documentation, settlement, and source-of-funds requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Foreign exchange in Canada is principally market-determined and is not administered through a general exchange-control authority. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML and terrorist-financing compliance for reporting entities, while the Canada Border Services Agency, Canada Revenue Agency, Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, provincial securities regulators, and Global Affairs Canada may have transaction-specific roles.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for collections and treasury support, but not a complete substitute for a Canadian bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution may provide multi-currency wallets, foreign exchange, virtual IBANs, card acquiring, or payout collection, but its ability to receive Steam or console-platform proceeds depends on platform acceptance, beneficiary-name matching, payment corridor availability, local account details, and the EMI's licensing perimeter. An offshore EMI is not automatically entitled to provide deposit-taking, payment, money-services, or foreign-exchange services in Canada. Canadian users, Canadian payment flows, or Canadian domestic transfers may engage the Payment Services Act framework, FINTRAC money-services-business obligations, provincial rules, or bank-partner requirements. Funds held with an EMI may not have Canadian deposit insurance and may be safeguarded under a foreign regime rather than treated as Canadian bank deposits. Use a Canadian operating account as the primary treasury account and document the EMI's licensing, safeguarding, ownership, sanctions controls, settlement terms, chargeback exposure, and insolvency treatment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity funding or shareholder loans from the verified remitter's institutional bank account directly to the Canadian corporation's account, preferably using SWIFT or the bank's approved international-wire rail. Use the legal corporate name, Canadian account number, institution and transit numbers, SWIFT/BIC, currency, and a precise payment reference such as 'share subscription' or 'shareholder loan under agreement dated [date]'. Do not describe a loan as equity or use vague references. Maintain the subscription agreement, loan agreement, board resolutions, capitalization records, lender ownership evidence, source-of-funds evidence, and bank confirmations. Canada does not use a universal statutory FX purpose-code regime comparable to certain exchange-controlled jurisdictions; the originating bank or intermediary may require an internal purpose code, payment classification, tax declaration, or sanctions information. Confirm whether the funding creates related-party interest, withholding-tax, thin-capitalization, transfer-pricing, or beneficial-ownership consequences. Screen the remitter, intermediary banks, directors, shareholders, and payment narrative against Canadian sanctions and AML requirements. Reconcile the receipt to the general ledger and retain records for the applicable legal and tax retention periods.Use the Canadian operating account for ordinary vendor, developer, licensor, marketing, cloud, platform, and professional-service payments. Use SWIFT MT103 or the bank's successor ISO 20022 cross-border payment message for ordinary wires, with complete originator, beneficiary, invoice, contract, and purpose information. Use commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, or bank guarantees only where counterparty or shipment risk justifies the cost; align the instrument with Incoterms, documentary requirements, sanctions screening, insurance, inspection, and discrepancy procedures. For digital publishing, platform settlements are generally contractual receivables rather than documentary trade transactions, so platform payout reports, invoices, royalty statements, and contract records should support payment reconciliation. Apply Canadian withholding-tax analysis before remitting interest, royalties, software rights, management fees, or other Canadian-source amounts to non-residents. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for collections and treasury support, but not a complete substitute for a Canadian bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution may provide multi-currency wallets, foreign exchange, virtual IBANs, card acquiring, or payout collection, but its ability to receive Steam or console-platform proceeds depends on platform acceptance, beneficiary-name matching, payment corridor availability, local account details, and the EMI's licensing perimeter. An offshore EMI is not automatically entitled to provide deposit-taking, payment, money-services, or foreign-exchange services in Canada. Canadian users, Canadian payment flows, or Canadian domestic transfers may engage the Payment Services Act framework, FINTRAC money-services-business obligations, provincial rules, or bank-partner requirements. Funds held with an EMI may not have Canadian deposit insurance and may be safeguarded under a foreign regime rather than treated as Canadian bank deposits. Use a Canadian operating account as the primary treasury account and document the EMI's licensing, safeguarding, ownership, sanctions controls, settlement terms, chargeback exposure, and insolvency treatment.
