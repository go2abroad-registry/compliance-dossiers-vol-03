Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Federal corporate income tax (T2), GST/HST, and payroll administered centrally. Primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MBA), and GST/HST NETFILE at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; incorporation registry administered by Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada).

E-government filing portal

On incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA), Corporations Canada transmits registry data to the CRA, which automatically assigns a 9-digit Business Number (BN) as the federal TIN; the BN with suffix RC0001 constitutes the corporate income tax account. The corporation must separately register for GST/HST by filing Form RC1 or using Business Registration Online (BRO) once annual worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 (the small-supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act, s. 123(1)); the CRA then appends the GST/HST program account (suffix RT0001) and fixes the effective registration date. A non-resident parent company or a non-resident vendor of digital services may obtain its own BN under the CRA Non-Resident Business Registration pathway or, for remote digital-supply vendors, the July 1, 2021 simplified GST/HST registration procedure, which requires no permanent establishment in Canada and permits quarterly filing via GST/HST NETFILE. A non-resident registrant relying on ITA sections 216 or 217 must also appoint a Canadian agent for service under a signed power of attorney. An entity whose supplies are exclusively zero-rated may register voluntarily to recover input tax credits, with excess refunds typically payable quarterly.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a CBCA business corporation is deemed resident in Canada under ITA s. 250(4)(a) and is taxable under Part I on worldwide income; no look-through or pass-through characterization is available; non-resident shareholders remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, rents, and royalties). — On incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA), Corporations Canada transmits registry data to the CRA, which automatically assigns a 9-digit Business Number (BN) as the federal TIN; the BN with suffix RC0001 constitutes the corporate income tax account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a CBCA business corporation is deemed resident in Canada under ITA s. 250(4)(a) and is taxable under Part I on worldwide income; no look-through or pass-through characterization is available; non-resident shareholders remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, rents, and royalties).

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election; a business corporation is imperatively an entity-level taxpayer. Available elections relevant to the corporate tax identity include: (i) non-calendar taxation year election (ITA s. 249.1(1)); (ii) functional-currency election for corporations whose relevant activities are primarily conducted in a foreign currency (ITA s. 261); (iii) capital dividend account election (ITA s. 83(2)); (iv) associated-corporation waivers affecting Canadian-controlled private corporation small-business threshold allocation (ITA s. 256(2)); and (v) hybrid-mismatch reporting and deduction-disallowance elections under the OECD BEPS Action 2 rules implemented in the Income Tax Act (ss. 18.4(3)-(4), 18.6(2)) effective July 1, 2022, which require participating Canadian corporations to file the hybrid mismatch return (Form RC4646) where cross-border structures exploit duplicative deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes (e.g., a US LLC treated as fiscally transparent in the US but opaque in Canada). The CRA administrative position on US LLC classification is stated in Income Tax Folio S4-F15-C1.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide revenues from supplies made in Canada (small-supplier ceiling, Excise Tax Act s. 123(1)); the same CAD 30,000 global-revenue threshold applies to non-resident vendors of digital products and services under the July 1, 2021 simplified GST/HST registration regime, computed on a calendar-quarter rolling basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, a supply of a service or intangible personal property (including software subscriptions, SaaS, and digital analytics) is generally made outside Canada - and hence not subject to GST/HST - where the recipient is a non-resident not present in Canada at the time tax becomes payable and the supply does not relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property located in Canada (ETA ss. 142.1-143.2). Where a supply to a non-resident is nonetheless treated as made in Canada (e.g., the non-resident accesses the platform while temporarily present in Canada), zero-rating is available under Schedule VI, Part V (s. 2 for services supplied to a non-resident; s. 10 for intangible personal property licensed to a non-resident), provided the supply is not an exempt financial service and is not in respect of Canadian real or tangible property. Caution: for an ISIC 6201 financial-portfolio-tracking platform, the provision of neutral data, charting, and bookkeeping tools is a taxable supply zero-rated on export; but if the platform supplies portfolio management advice, trade arrangement, or custodial services, it may be an exempt financial service under ETA s. 123(1) and Schedule V, Part VII, in which case exports are exempt (no GST/HST charge, restricted input tax credits) rather than zero-rated. The exporter must documentaryly substantiate the recipient's non-resident status (contract, tax form, foreign billing address) and the conditions of supply to sustain zero-rating.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A Form or Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) electronic export report) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency for any tangible goods component
  • Commercial invoice and binding supply agreement (SaaS order form or subscription terms) identifying the non-resident recipient, the nature of the digital service, and the place of supply
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or digital-delivery record (server access log, IP geolocation, license-key issuance) evidencing export or offshore consumption
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice) showing receipt of consideration from outside Canada in the contracted currency
  • Non-resident status evidence (e.g., IRS Form W-8BEN-E, foreign incorporation certificate, or equivalent attestation) for the recipient

Canada operates a voluntary e-invoicing environment. There is NO mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate as of the 2025 tax year. The CRA accepts electronic tax invoices (PDF/XML/EDI) provided the supplier BN, invoice date, recipient identification, description and quantity, consideration, and GST/HST rate and amount are stated in compliance with Excise Tax Act ss. 169(1)(g), 169(2) and 169(3). The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol-based, administered with provincial partners) may be adopted voluntarily. GST/HST returns (Form GST34) must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or certified tax software for the majority of registrants, with paper filing restricted to specific exceptions; the CRA offers API-based filing integration for certified vendors. Registrants must retain books and records for six years (ETA s. 286), including fintech payment-rail data (Stripe, Apple IAP, card acquirer records) sufficient to substantiate gross revenue and place-of-supply determinations.

Export zero-rating and non-resident supply claims are a priority audit area of the CRA International and Large Business Directorate and the Ottawa Tax Services Office (for non-resident registrants). Where substantiation is inadequate, the CRA will claw back the 0% treatment by reassessing the supply as taxable at 5% GST (or applicable HST), with retroactive prescribed-rate interest (ETA s. 280) and penalties: (i) failure to collect - 25% of the uncollected tax (ETA s. 285.01); (ii) false statements or omissions - the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA s. 285(1)); and (iii) tax evasion offences under ETA s. 327, carrying fines up to CAD 100,000 on summary conviction or, on indictment, fines and imprisonment of up to two years. The normal four-year reassessment window (ETA s. 298(1)) is unlimited where a return reveals misrepresentation attributable to negligence, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud (ETA s. 298(2)). In the financial-services context, a mischaracterized zero-rated export of an exempt financial service triggers input tax credit clawbacks under ETA s. 296 with the same interest and penalty overlay.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII and the Bank Act. There are no FX restrictions on dividend remittance, interest payments, brand royalties, or capital repatriation; the only legal constraints are Part XIII non-resident withholding taxes (25% statutory rate, treaty-reduced where applicable) and regular income/corporate capital tax calculations. A pure-software subscription is treated as a taxable Canadian royalty only if the payer is Canadian-resident and the consideration is made for the use of IP; no prior bank or central-bank authorization is required before transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for Canadian export proceeds. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency with any Canadian Schedule I bank, and conversion to CAD is at the holder's discretion at market rates. Cross-border transfers equal to or above CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC electronic funds-transfer reports, but no central-bank licensing or repatriation filing is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border reporting; no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority exists under the Currency Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for auxiliary use and Low as a primary treasury provider. An offshore EMI without a Canadian banking licence cannot accept deposits in Canada; such deposit-taking is an offence under the Bank Act. An offshore EMI may only operate as a payment processor or as a FINTRAC-registered money-services business (MSB) for FX dealing or money transfer, and never for holding client funds or the company's own CAD treasury. For this low-risk software enterprise, an offshore EMI is acceptable only for cross-border payouts to vendors and for merchant acquiring through partner banks; operating accounts and liquidity buffers must remain at Canadian regulated banks.
