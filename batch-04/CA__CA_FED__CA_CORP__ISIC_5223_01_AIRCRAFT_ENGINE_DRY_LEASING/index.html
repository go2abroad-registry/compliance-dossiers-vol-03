Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The CBCA imposes no minimum paid-up capital and no statutory escrow deposit at incorporation; the 1,000 common shares may be subscribed for lawful consideration (money, property or past services) under ss. 25(1). Where aircraft lease proceeds are routed through an escrow account, such escrow is a commercial banking arrangement to be maintained at a Canadian Schedule I chartered bank, separate from operating funds, and is not a condition of incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire marshal fire-safety clearance for the registered office (fire-alarm certificate per CAN/ULC-S536 and annual portable extinguisher inspections); landlord-provided building-code and structural load certificates for the leased office premises; provincial occupational health and safety compliance (workplace inspection, first-aid and hazard communication), and workers' compensation coverage clearance (e.g., WSIB in Ontario) for all employees and compensated officers.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Transport Canada — Civil Aviation (TCCA), Commercial Flight Standards Division (advisory oversight only for aviation asset leasing; no industrial or manufacturing license is required because the Canadian entity establishes no production or overhaul facility in Canada).

Category: None — dry leasing of a turbofan aircraft engine without crew is not a 'commercial air service' under the Canada Transportation Act, S.C. 1996, c. 10; the Canadian lessor is not required to hold a Canadian Aviation Document for the leasing activity itself. If the leased engine is installed on a Canadian-registered aircraft, the aircraft operator must hold the applicable air operator certificate and maintain the engine under an approved maintenance control manual (CARs Part VI and VII).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration portals: My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html.

E-government filing portal

A corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act is automatically issued a 9-digit Business Number (BN) at incorporation; the GST/HST account (RT program code) is opened by filing Form RC1, either online via BRO or by mail; non-resident aircraft engine lessors carrying on business in Canada may register voluntarily using Form RC1 and Form RC243 where Part IX of the Excise Tax Act applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian-resident corporation under Income Tax Act sections 2 and 250(4) taxed on worldwide income; no tax-exempt zone designation applies; US-style check-the-box classification is not recognized in Canada. — A corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act is automatically issued a 9-digit Business Number (BN) at incorporation; the GST/HST account (RT program code) is opened by filing Form RC1, either online via BRO or by mail; non-resident aircraft engine lessors carrying on business in Canada may register voluntarily using Form RC1 and Form RC243 where Part IX of the Excise Tax Act applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian-resident corporation under Income Tax Act sections 2 and 250(4) taxed on worldwide income; no tax-exempt zone designation applies; US-style check-the-box classification is not recognized in Canada.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or free-standing entity classification election exists under Canadian law; available elections relevant to a cross-border aircraft engine lessor include: (i) Income Tax Act s. 216 election for non-resident lessors to report net rental income from Canadian real property (limited relevance to zero-rated aircraft engine dry-leases); (ii) joint election under ITA s. 216.1 for a non-resident partner; (iii) instalment-basis estimate election under ITA s. 156; and (iv) hybrid mismatch and GAAR adjustments under the Budget 2019 measures and ITA s. 245, which recharacterize arrangements lacking economic substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold based on aggregate worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated leases) of the person and associated persons in the preceding four calendar quarters; registration is mandatory once exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A dry lease of a turbofan aircraft engine is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, sections 7 and 8 of the Excise Tax Act where the engine is supplied to a non-resident for use exclusively outside Canada and is exported as part of the supply; leases to resident air carriers operating international flights may also qualify as zero-rated supplies of exported goods; documentary evidence of export and foreign use must be retained to support the zero-rating claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) transmitted via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or an approved service provider
  • Air Waybill or Bill of Lading evidencing shipment to the non-resident lessee and referencing the engine serial number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/wire transfer receipt proving payment of consideration in foreign currency
  • Signed dry-lease agreement confirming exclusive use outside Canada and engine return obligations
  • GST Form GST11 — zero-rating certificate or equivalent foreign-acquisition attestation

Canada imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping; electronic invoices are acceptable if legible and retained in machine-readable format; the federal government participates in the voluntary Peppol OpenPEPPOL framework for B2G e-invoicing; records must be kept for seven years under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act.

CRA may reassess within four years, and without limitation where misrepresentation or fraud is involved; zero-rating is clawed back with tax, interest and penalties if exported goods are not substantiated, return to Canada, or are used in Canada within the statutory period; culpable conduct attracts a penalty of 20% of the federal tax evaded plus a minimum $250 penalty under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act; corporate fines up to $50,000 and imprisonment of directors up to two years on summary conviction may apply; gross negligence penalties under section 163(2) of the Income Tax Act may apply in parallel.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current account and capital account convertibility. The Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act confirm the Canadian dollar is legal tender, and there is no statutory restriction on conversion, remittance of dividends, repatriation of capital, or liquidation proceeds. Non-resident investors may freely convert CAD to any eligible currency at market rates via Schedule I or II chartered banks. Profit remittance is subject to Canadian federal non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (typically 25% unless reduced by an income tax treaty), but no exchange control hurdle or central bank clearances apply. For corporate treasury purposes, capital injections and working capital transfers are treated as ordinary international financial flows reportable only under large-transaction thresholds. There is no mandatory approval from the Bank of Canada for cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Aircraft engine dry leasing rental proceeds and trade payments may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely in offshore or domestic non-resident bank accounts. However, all cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC as international electronic funds transfers (IEFTs). The operating lessor should also maintain accurate books and records in Canadian dollars for tax compliance; any foreign currency receipts must be translated into CAD at the prevailing Bank of Canada exchange rate for income tax filing purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and monetary authority. Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; currency-related regulatory oversight is shared with the Department of Finance Canada, OSFI, and FINTRAC. Foreign exchange transactions are not generally subject to licensing or case-by-case administrative approval.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Moderate. Canada does not authorize offshore fintech digital EMI institutions as deposit-taking banks. A foreign electronic money institution cannot open an operating account as a pure EMI without becoming a registered money services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and registering with FINTRAC. Even then, an MSB is not a bank and cannot provide chequing, clearing, or settlement privileges. For an aircraft engine dry lessor, offshore EMIs may be used for ancillary collections or treasury visibility, but the primary clearing rail for cross-border operating cash must be through a Canadian chartered bank, a Schedule I bank, or a licensed branch of a foreign bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Canadian federal aircraft engine dry leasing corporation, capital injections should be routed through the Canadian Payments Association's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day CAD settlement or via an international SWIFT MT103 wire to a Schedule I bank account. To eliminate Part XIII withholding exposure on dividends, the injection should be structured as share capital contributed free of income tax. The wire should cite a clear remittance information field: 'Equity contribution, aircraft engine leasing, CBCA account' or the bank-specific purpose code corresponding to 'Intercompany capital injection' or 'Equity investment'. For any shareholder loan, the interest rate must be arm's-length and documentation filed with the Canada Revenue Agency if cross-border. All inbound wires exceeding CAD 10,000 will be reported to FINTRAC as an IEFT. The receiving bank will require a valid CRA business number, certificate of incorporation, and a resolution confirming the signatories authorized to accept the deposit.The primary clearing rail for cross-border trade settlements and lease-related outflows is the SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer. For engine maintenance, lease security, and sublease disbursements, the treasury should use the LVTS for same-day CAD settlements to Canadian counterparties and SWIFT MT103 for USD or multi-currency wires to the lessee, engine OEM, MRO providers, or technical managers. For substantial lease deposits requiring conditional release, an escrow account with a Canadian Schedule I bank and enforceable escrow agreement is the standard clearing vehicle. Commercial letters of credit can be used as import or export instruments when the lessor provides security for performance, but the ordinary recurring rental and operational payments should be executed as direct SWIFT MT103 transfers.
