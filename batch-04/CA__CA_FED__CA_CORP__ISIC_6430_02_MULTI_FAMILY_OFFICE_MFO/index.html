Incorporation processing time

⚡ 89 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or capital prepayment requirement under the CBCA for incorporation. The articles may establish one or more classes of shares with unlimited or fixed maximum authorized capital (no-par value permitted). At least one director is mandatory; unless an exemption under CBCA s. 105(1) applies, at least 25% of the directors must be resident Canadians (or, for foreign-controlled corporations, the board must satisfy the special residence rules). Provincial harmonization may impose a nominal share-capital filing requirement only in Quebec.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the National Fire Code of Canada (NFC) Part 2 (local fire marshal inspection and annual means-of-egress verification); structural load inspection and certificate of occupancy issued by the municipal building department; workplace health and safety clearance under Part II of the Canada Labour Code (hazard identification, first aid, fire drills and emergency preparedness); if premises are in Ontario, compliance with the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act, 2005 (AODA) and the Ontario Fire Code is also required for staff and client access.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or capital prepayment requirement under the CBCA for incorporation. The articles may establish one or more classes of shares with unlimited or fixed maximum authorized capital (no-par value permitted). At least one director is mandatory; unless an exemption under CBCA s. 105(1) applies, at least 25% of the directors must be resident Canadians (or, for foreign-controlled corporations, the board must satisfy the special residence rules). Provincial harmonization may impose a nominal share-capital filing requirement only in Quebec.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Federal corporate income tax (Part I, Income Tax Act) and GST/HST (Part IX, Excise Tax Act) administration. Primary portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) via canada.ca/revenue-agency; federal incorporation through Corporations Canada; sub-federal corporate income tax and Quebec QST administered separately by Revenu Québec where a Quebec establishment exists.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) via Corporations Canada (online Articles of Incorporation, NUANS name search, no minimum capital); (2) Obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) — the statutory TIN — automatically upon incorporation or by filing Form RC1 (Request to Open a Business Number Program Account) through Business Registration Online (BRO); (3) Open program accounts: corporate income tax (RC0001), GST/HST (RT0001) where taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters, or voluntarily to claim input tax credits and zero-rate exports; (4) A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must file Form RC148 (Information Return for Non-Resident Corporations) and maintain a Canadian address for service; (5) File annual T2 corporation income tax return within six months of fiscal year-end; GST/HST returns filed annually (small suppliers), quarterly, or monthly depending on taxable supply volume.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident corporation is a separate taxpayer under ITA s. 2; no check-the-box pass-through in Canadian law; eligible for CCPC status if Canadian-controlled, providing the small business deduction — federal rate 9% on active business income up to CAD 500,000; general federal Part I rate 15%; provincial corporate rates 0%–16% additional; inter-corporate dividends deductible under ITA s. 112; foreign income shielded through foreign affiliate surplus regime, not territorial exemption).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Available elections include: ITA s. 85(1) tax-deferred rollover of property into a corporation; s. 85(6)/(7) paid-up capital elections; s. 89(5) capital dividend election; s. 93(1) foreign affiliate surplus designation; s. 216 (non-resident rental/investment income election); GST/HST: voluntary registration (ETA s. 240(2)), Quick Method (ETA s. 225.1, generally unavailable to financial institutions), and selected listed financial institution (SLFI) rules; hybrid-mismatch outcomes are governed by non-elective ITA provisions (ss. 18.4, 18.6, 95.1 and the MLI rules) — no elective hybrid-mismatch mechanism exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any rolling four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148(1), small-supplier rule); a person ceases to be a small supplier and must register within 30 days of exceeding the threshold; for a Multi-Family Office, discretionary portfolio management fees are generally exempt 'financial services' (ETA s. 123(1), Schedule V Part VII), so registration may be unnecessary for exempt Canadian-resident client fees, but registration is required (or advisable) where administrative/custodial fee components are taxable or where zero-rating of services to non-residents is claimed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1–6 with proof of export (title passing outside Canada). Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 unless the service is rendered (a) in the course of the non-resident's commercial activity carried on in Canada, (b) in relation to real property situated in Canada, (c) in relation to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or (d) as a personal service rendered to an individual present in Canada. For the MFO's discretionary portfolio advisory services to non-resident family clients, zero-rating applies where the non-resident recipient is not GST/HST-registered, is not present in Canada at performance, and the advice does not relate to Canadian-situated property; zero-rated supplies confer full input tax credit entitlement, whereas exempt supplies to Canadian residents trigger partial ITC restriction.

  • Customs export declaration — verified electronic export filing in the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), successor to Form B13A, for controlled/monitored goods
  • Commercial invoice / Canada Customs Invoice with HS classification, valuation, and stated zero-rating basis
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Proof of export — carrier certificate, terminal receipts, or customs release stamp
  • Inward remittance / SWIFT interbank wire evidence showing foreign consideration actually received by the Canadian MFO (FINTRAC-compliant bank record)
  • GST/HST invoice (ETA s. 223) or management agreement documenting non-resident recipient status and satisfaction of the ETA s. 7 conditions

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) API mandate. Registered suppliers must issue invoices containing the vendor's BN, invoicing date, GST/HST registration status and amount of tax charged (ETA s. 223). Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for all monthly filers and for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million, via CRA GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account; payments exceeding CAD 10,000 per remittance must be made electronically by EFT; otherwise paper filing and cheque payment remain permissible.

Canada imposes no statutory 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline for GST/HST zero-rating; the 180-day field is a schema default reflecting internal bank/FINTRAC payment-rail controls and FATF-aligned KYC practice — CRA instead requires contemporaneous documentary proof of receipt and may apply a four-year reassessment window (10 years where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect exists) to retroactively claw back refunds with prescribed interest. On failure to substantiate zero-rating, CRA reassesses the supply as taxable at 5%/HST, denies ITCs, applies ETA s. 280 failure-to-file penalties (1% of net tax plus 0.25% per month, max 12 months), ETA s. 285 gross-negligence penalties (greater of CAD 250 and 50% of misstated net tax, plus CAD 250 per false statement), ITA s. 163(2) 50% gross-negligence penalties for income-tax adjustments, and ETA s. 327 / ITA s. 239 criminal-evasion sanctions (fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years). For an MFO, CRA additionally scrutinizes beneficial ownership, source of funds, and FINTRAC reporting on SWIFT rails, which can suspend refunds independently of tax law until AML documentation is complete.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no currency conversion restrictions, exchange licences, or repatriation controls. Dividends, interest, management fees, and other profit remittances are freely payable in any convertible currency, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced where applicable) and FINTRAC reporting thresholds for international electronic funds transfers (CAD 10,000 or more).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or retention timeline for export proceeds. Exporters and service providers may maintain foreign currency earnings in Canadian and offshore accounts. For the Multi-Family Office context, no export surrender obligations apply to inbound investment capital or management fee income.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank oversight; FINTRAC for cross-border AML/FT transaction monitoring. Canada has no dedicated foreign exchange administration because exchange controls were abolished.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary clearing rail. Foreign non-resident Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not eligible for bank accounts without Canadian registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and Money Services Business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC. Unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot open Canadian accounts or provide settlement services; they may serve only as ancillary FX quotation counterparts after full Canadian licensing and regulatory compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.009 credit transfer from the remitting institution to the Canadian MFO's operational or escrow account via a major correspondent bank (often in New York or London). The recommended SWIFT purpose code is 'CAPT' (capital contribution) for equity injections or 'INVC' (investment income) for client-family investment proceeds. The originating bank must provide a detailed remittance instruction, and the receiving bank in Canada will apply FINTRAC reporting for international electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate (38% basic rate minus 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction; combined federal-provincial headline generally ranges from 23% to 31% depending on province).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid to non-residents. Tax treaty reductions: 5% for a corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% of voting stock; 15% for all other treaty-protected shareholders, including non-arm’s-length individuals and trusts.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within six months after the end of the taxation year (Income Tax Act, s.150). Balance due is due two months after year-end, or three months after year-end for a CCPC eligible for the Small Business Deduction. Instalment payments are required quarterly.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm’s length cross-border transactions within the taxation year (triggering contemporaneous T106 documentation and Schedule 88 disclosure). Multinational groups with consolidated revenue over €750 million must additionally file a Country-by-Country Report.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the same manner as defined offshore regimes; however, economic substance is central to the Canadian corporate-residence test (mind-and-management jurisdiction), the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s.245), and treaty-based Limitation on Benefits. For an ISIC 6430 multi-family office, real employees, actual discretionary-decision-making, physical office, and board meetings in Canada are mandatory; a structuring shell with no Canadian substance can be disregarded, leading to non-residency, GAAR re-characterization, and loss of treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Section 163(2) gross negligence penalty: 50% of understated tax; criminal sanction under s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment to 2 years on summary conviction, or up to 5 years on indictment; full back taxes plus compound interest.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in or authorizes false filings is a party to the offence. Conviction can result in personal criminal fines, imprisonment, a travel ban, disqualification from board service, and personal civil liability for restitution of the under-collected tax under s.239 and s.227.1.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA can adjust revenues and deductions under s.247(2), deny related deductions entirely under s.247(1)(b), and levy a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the understated taxable income where reasonable documentation is not provided; late-filing penalties for T106 forms also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who grossly neglect their duty to obtain transfer-pricing documentation may be jointly and severally liable for corporate penalty under the 'willful default' language of s.247(3); direct director assessments are also possible under s.160 where property is transferred for less than fair value to a family trust entity.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / GAAR

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization under GAAR (s.245) with the denied tax benefit plus interest; no GAAR penalty exists for transactions before 2023, but since 2023 a 25% penalty on the avoided tax applies under s.245(4.1) for transactions entered after April 6, 2022; also income may be attributed back under s.75(2) trusts rules.

Director personal liability: The doctrine of corporate veil piercing applies in sham transactions; directors and controlling shareholders can be assessed for unpaid tax via s.160 third-party liability, subjected to a CRA seizure and travel ban, and charged with quasi-criminal criminal negligence if client funds were knowingly placed in worthless shell vehicles.
AML / Unlicensed Discretionary Portfolio Management

Statutory sanction: Unregistered discretionary advice violates provincial securities acts (e.g., Ontario Securities Act) carrying administrative penalties up to C$1 million and maximum fines up to C$5 million; the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) provides penalties up to C$2 million for record-keeping and reporting failures, plus confiscation and immovable-asset restraint.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly assist unregistered advisory activity or fail to report suspicious transactions to FINTRAC are criminally liable; penalties include imprisonment up to five years, personal fines, permanent prohibition from acting as a director in the financial sector, and release of personal assets for victim restitution.
