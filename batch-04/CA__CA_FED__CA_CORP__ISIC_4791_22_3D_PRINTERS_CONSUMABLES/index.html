Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime; foreign and domestic corporations are taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Relevant statutory elections: (i) Income Tax Act s. 216 election (non-resident may elect to file a Part I return on net Canadian-source rental income in lieu of Part XIII withholding at 25%); (ii) ITA s. 217 election (non-resident electing to file a Part I return for certain pension/benefit income); (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 167 joint election (closely related corporations may elect to have inter-corporate taxable supplies deemed made for nil consideration, eliminating GST/HST frictions on internal restructurings/fulfilment cost transfers); (iv) ETA s. 273 election (non-resident registrant joint election with related registrants); (v) hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA Part 6.3 — ss. 12.3, 18.6, 18.7, 20.2) apply mandatorily with attendant designation rules for 'structured arrangements'; no elective opt-out exists for entity classification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible floating currency with full current account and capital account liberalization. There are no exchange controls on inward or outward cross-border payments, profit remittances, dividend distributions, or capital repatriations. Profit remittance is subject only to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (statutory 25% on dividends, reducible to 5-15% under applicable tax treaties) and normal transfer pricing rules. No statutory balance-of-payments approval, prior license, or central bank clearance is required for external payment transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no forced conversion ratios. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency proceeds in foreign or domestic accounts indefinitely. The only relevant statutory obligation is accurate GST/HST zero-rating of exported goods (using the exporter's Business Number) and proper record-keeping for 6 years under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act to support the zero-rated characterization.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration; FX policy and reporting authority reside with these agencies under the Currency Act, the Bank Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair-market value of reportable non-arm's length cross-border transactions during the taxation year triggers mandatory Form T106 reporting; contemporaneous Canadian transfer-pricing documentation (Local File/Master File-equivalent information) should be prepared and available to the CRA upon request regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore financial centres; however, the CRA enforces substance through the general anti-avoidance rule (subsection 245(2)), the sham doctrine, the artificial transaction doctrine, and treaty limitation-of-benefits provisions. An e-commerce entity selling desktop 3D printers and resin filaments in Canada must evidence real Canadian commercial activity — employees, procurement decisions, inventory risk, logistics contracts, customer contracts, financial records, and management oversight — to support the arm's length allocation of profit. A Canadian company acting as a 'letterbox' or front without actual substance remains exposed to recharacterization, gaar cancellation of tax benefits, and reassessment of non-resident ownership.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by E-Commerce Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: Reassessment for unreported income plus gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under subsection 163(2) and interest at the prescribed rate compounding daily; criminal conviction under section 239 of the Income Tax Act carries fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize, tolerate, or participate in the omission may become personally liable for unremitted amounts under subsection 227.1(1) and for false statements; criminal sentencing may include imprisonment, fines, and, in serious cases, travel bans pending arrest or surrender where an extraction or warrant is issued by the CRA/prosecution authorities.
Part XIII Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: The payor becomes liable for the entire unwithheld Part XIII amount under subsection 215(1); failure to deduct can result in a 10% penalty under subsection 227(8) plus daily compound interest, and an additional 20% penalty for repeated contravention under subsection 227(9).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under subsection 227.1(1) for the unremitted Part XIII withholding tax unless they exercised a standard of care to prevent failure; CRA can pursue civil enforcement against directors, freeze accounts, and file security interests; directors may also face criminal liability if they knowingly failed to withhold.
