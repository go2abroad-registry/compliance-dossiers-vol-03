Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax authority; CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal is the primary e-service gateway. Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) is the co-competent authority for customs, AEO certification (Partners in Protection / Customs Self-Assessment).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act via Corporations Canada online filing centre; CRA automatically assigns a 9-digit Business Number (BN) upon incorporation completion. 2) Activate program accounts through BRO: RC (corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST), RM (import/export), RP (payroll); non-resident corporations may file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or register upon filing their first T2 return. 3) A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada that makes taxable supplies exceeding the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold must register for GST/HST (Form RC1, RC4601 for non-residents) before issuing taxable invoices. 4) For AEO/export activities, activate the RM account with CBSA and enrol in Partners in Protection (PIP) to obtain C-TPAT/AEO mutual recognition status with CBSA. 5) Foreign shareholders/parents file T2 Schedule 57 (Part IX.1. REOP) and Form T106 (reporting transactions with non-arm's length non-residents).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian federal Business Corporation is a separate taxable person subject to Part I corporate income tax; resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income (with foreign tax credits), non-residents only on Canadian-source income; no pass-through default; a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) qualifies for the small business deduction on the first C$500,000 of active business income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian federal Business Corporation is a separate taxable person subject to Part I corporate income tax; resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income (with foreign tax credits), non-residents only on Canadian-source income; no pass-through default; a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) qualifies for the small business deduction on the first C$500,000 of active business income. No tax-exempt-zone status applies to federally incorporated commercial entities.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election under the Income Tax Act. Available elections: (i) ITA s. 256(2) associated-corporation election allocating the C$500,000 small business limit among related Canadian corporations (T2 Schedule 4, Form T1005); (ii) ETA s. 211 election to treat closely related corporations as a single person for GST/HST; (iii) ETA s. 156 nil-consideration election for inter-affiliate supplies; (iv) capital dividend account election under ITA s. 83(2) (Form T2054) for tax-effective dividend payments; (v) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4–18.6, effective 2021) apply mandatorily to cross-border payments with no elective carve-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies — below this a person is a 'small supplier' and exempt from registration; non-residents supplying services in Canada are assessed on the same worldwide threshold; provincial HST add-ons raise the combined rate to 13%–15% once registration is triggered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V, item 7 to a supply of advisory, consulting, or professional services (including AEO supply-chain compliance audits) made to a non-resident person, provided: (i) the non-resident is not present in Canada at the time the service is performed; (ii) the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada; (iii) the service does not relate to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; (iv) the service is not a financial service and is not rendered to an individual in Canada. Services consumed or enjoyed in Canada, or performed in Canada for a Canadian arm's-length client, remain taxable at 5%–15%. Continued compliance with CBSA AEO/PIP qualifications substantiates the offshore-export nature of the audit deliverables.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CAED) filed with CBSA via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) — required only for physical goods; not required for electronically delivered consulting reports
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill — only if hard-copy reports or audit exhibits are physically exported (not required for digital deliverables)
  • Commercial contract between the Canadian entity and the non-resident client specifying the AEO audit scope, offshore benefit, and delivery outside Canada
  • Foreign-settlement evidence: SWIFT MT103, Wise transaction receipt, or inward remittance advice showing payment from abroad; Canadian-dollar equivalent at CRA's published exchange rates
  • Tax return back-up: T2/GST/HST line-item mapping, intercompany agreements (if non-resident affiliate), Timesheets/workpapers showing labour performed in Canada for offshore consumption

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control (CT) system in Canada. Electronic GST/HST filing (GST/HST NETFILE) is compulsory for registrants whose annual revenue exceeds C$1.5M; CRA accepts API-enabled EDI/XML bulk filing from accounting software; e-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Peppol PINT standard in private networks. Invoices must contain the BN, supply date, supplier legal name, GST/HST charged, and be retained with workpapers for the 6-year Canadian record-keeping period.

CRA may reassess within 4 years of the original assessment, extended to 7 years for misrepresentation, neglect, or willful default. Fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger: ETA s. 285.1(1) penalty (up to 25% of net tax + interest), ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated, interest charges on arrears, and potential criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and Criminal Code s. 380 (tax evasion) with imprisonment for directors/officers. Retrospective clawbacks of input tax credits, added §160 assessments, and director's liability under ETA ss. 323/ITA s. 227.1 may follow; voluntary disclosure (CRA VDP) before the audit reduces but does not eliminate penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible free-floating currency with no foreign exchange control statute on either current or capital account transactions. CAD can be freely purchased, sold, held, and remitted through any authorized financial institution. Profit repatriation via dividends requires no regulatory pre-approval or currency license; the only statutory burden is Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 15% for portfolio and 5% for substantial corporate shareholders under the Canada-US Treaty). There is no limitation period for holding foreign currency, no netting restriction, and no requirement to convert export invoicing into CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory repatriation window, no minimum conversion ratio, and no requirement that export receipts be invoiced in CAD. Export proceeds may be retained in the invoiced currency, held in a foreign-currency account at a domestic Schedule I bank, or settled directly to overseas suppliers. The substantive compliance obligations are restricted to FINTRAC record keeping, suspicious transaction reporting, and the corporate income tax treatment of unrealized foreign exchange gains (mark-to-market for functional currency reporting).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting alongside the Department of Finance Canada; prudential banking supervision by OSFI; AML/CTF reporting governance by FINTRAC; wholesale payments infrastructure operated by Payments Canada (Lynx and ACSS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise Business earns a High ancillary rating for low-value disbursements to overseas contractors and suppliers (per-transaction values under CAD 25,000) and a Medium rating as a primary treasury rail. Wise operates in Canada as a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business rather than a bank, holding money services licenses in select provinces; its balances are not CDIC insured and its settlement is routed through Canadian partner banks (RBC, National Bank of Canada, Peoples Trust) without direct Lynx or ACSS membership. Foreign-licensed EMIs (UK or EU) face Canadian licensing friction unless they rely on a Canadian MSB registration; such non-Canadian EMIs must not hold end-value capital, escrow money, or permanent float. Recommended legal limit: use fintech rails only for expense payments, audit document delivery, and small vendor disbursements; keep the operational and escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% federal general corporate rate, structurally derived from Part I tax computed at 38% of taxable income, less a 10% provincial abatement and a 13% general rate reduction; provincial corporate income tax (and branch tax for non-resident corporations) applies separately where a permanent establishment is allocated to a province.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on all dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed no later than 6 months after the fiscal period end; the tax balance must be paid no later than 2 months after fiscal year-end (3 months for CCPCs eligible for the Small Business Deduction); quarterly instalments are due on the last day of each quarter of the fiscal period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of reportable transactions per transaction class per fiscal year with non-arm's length non-residents triggers the mandatory preparation of contemporaneous Local File documentation (T106, economic analysis, and comparability study); an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year must additionally file a CRA Country-by-Country Report and maintain a Master File, consistent with BEPS Action 13.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the sham transaction doctrine, and section 69 intercompany pricing rules to recharacterize transactions lacking genuine commercial substance. Part I of the ITA thin capitalization rules (s. 18(4)) cap interest deductions on debt owing to specified non-resident shareholders at a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio on the maximum of the shareholder's cost amount of shares and retained earnings; excessive or back-to-back financing, circular flows, and conduit arrangements are audit targets; an AEO certification firm must be able to demonstrate that its Canadian operation has real offices, employees, decision-making, and revenue risk to avoid recharacterization as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful False Statements

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1): on conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the amount of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment (2 years on summary conviction), plus full reassessed taxes and arrears interest at the prescribed rate; Criminal Code s. 380 fraud upon conviction additionally permits penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years; CRA may also proceed with a civil action for three times the amount of the tax in question and freeze bank accounts through an advance ruling or court order.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false returns, authorized the omission, or acquiesced are personally prosecutable; CRA pierces the corporate veil under ITA ss. 160 (transfers to related persons) and 227.1 (unremitted withholdings), permitting restitution from personal assets; courts may issue travel bans, asset freezes, passport restrictions during ongoing fraud prosecutions, and imprisonment for individual directors upon conviction.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the consideration to fair market value under ITA s. 247(2) and impose a penalty under s. 247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the adjustment (net of bona fide rebuttals) exceeds the prescribed threshold; the entire adjustment remains taxable with daily compounding arrears interest; failure to produce documentation may also lead to an adverse inference in litigation and Justice Canada enforcement actions.

Director personal liability: Although the transfer pricing penalty is assessed to the corporation, directors who knowingly signed false T106 declarations risk gross negligence penalties (s. 163(2)) and may be made personally liable in cases of shamming, fraudulent conveyances, or willful blindness; travel bans and imprisonment arise where the transfer pricing mispricing is prosecuted as tax evasion or fraud.
Shell Company / GAAR Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) denies the tax benefit and reassesses tax, interest, and the 50% gross negligence penalty where there is a misuse or abuse; CRA may require production of board minutes, internal emails, and staffing records; failure to maintain adequate books and records in Canada is separately actionable; a finding of sham or conduit corporation allows the CRA to disregard the corporate form and attribute business income directly to the controlling foreign principal.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and controllers may be jointly and severally liable for assessed taxes under the fraudulent conveyance rules; courts may order civil restitution, injunctive relief freezing Canadian assets, and the lifting of the corporate veil; where the scheme involves a criminal fraud, travel bans and imprisonment up to 14 years under the Criminal Code can be imposed.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance and False Trade Security Attestation

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation for entities and CAD 100,000 for individuals (up to CAD 1,000,000 and CAD 250,000 under recent amendments for contraventions of certain provisions); failure to report large cash or e-transfers may result in fees and penalties; Criminal Code s. 462.31 money laundering is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years; consumer border and customs fraud penalties under the Customs Act can reach CAD 50,000 per count and result in immediate revocation of AEO authorization and disqualification from the Partners in Protection / C-TPAT equivalency program.

Director personal liability: FinTRAC and criminal authorities consider senior officers personally responsible for instructing, authorizing, or acquiescing in non-compliance; individuals may suffer personal fines, forfeiture of proceeds, travel restrictions pending trial, and imprisonment up to 10 years; CRA and FINTRAC may issue restraining orders and publish compliance notices affecting cross-border movement of directors.
