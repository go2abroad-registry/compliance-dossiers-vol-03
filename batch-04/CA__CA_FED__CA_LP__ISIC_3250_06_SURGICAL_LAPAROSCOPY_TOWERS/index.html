Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); business registration and GST/HST account administration via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and the My Business Account portal (canada.ca); customs export reporting is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned or non-resident limited partnership manufacturing in Canada must obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account before commercial activity. Procedure: (1) file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online via BRO or by mail, or file Form RC171 (Request for a Business Number - Non-Resident) where no Canadian address exists; (2) attach the partnership agreement, provincial registration certificate, and proof of a registered Canadian address for service or a named Canadian agent; (3) complete a GST/HST application (Form RC1 section G or the non-resident GST/HST registration form) declaring the estimated annual Canadian taxable supplies; (4) for a non-resident person, the CRA applies the 'carrying on business in Canada' test under ETA subsection 123(1) before issuing an RT-type account; (5) registration is compulsory once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, but is also available voluntarily to claim input tax credits on Canadian inputs and to zero-rate exports; (6) after issuance, the BN/RM account is used for import-export reporting with the CBSA.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes: the LP itself is not a taxpayer; each partner (resident or non-resident) reports its allocable share of income or loss under ITA section 96, and the partnership files an informational T5013 return. For GST/HST purposes the LP is treated as a separate person under ETA subsection 272.1, and the general partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST liabilities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes: the LP itself is not a taxpayer; each partner (resident or non-resident) reports its allocable share of income or loss under ITA section 96, and the partnership files an informational T5013 return. For GST/HST purposes the LP is treated as a separate person under ETA subsection 272.1, and the general partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST liabilities. Non-resident partners earning manufacturing business income through the LP are subject to Part I tax on Canadian-source business income under ITA sections 115 and 180; no Part XIII withholding applies to ordinary trading income, but treaty benefits require compliance with the fiscally-transparent entity rule in Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Tax Convention.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box entity-classification regime at the federal level. Available statutory elections: (1) ITA subsection 96(3) election to establish a common fiscal period for the partnership; (2) ETA section 156 joint election for closely related corporations (not available to a limited partnership); (3) non-resident rental-income election under ITA subsection 216(1) (inapplicable to manufacturing income); (4) agency or undisclosed-principal elections under ITA section 216.1 where the LP acts as an agent for a non-resident principal; (5) for US-resident corporate members, the entity's US federal classification (Form 8832) determines whether the member can claim treaty relief under the fiscally-transparent provision; (6) hybrid mismatch rules under the MLI and domestic recharacterization rules (ITA section 107.1) may deny treaty benefits or reclassify payments flowing through transparent entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold under ETA section 148, measured on total consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada in any single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; supplies of capital property and financial services are excluded, and special rules for non-residents limit the count to supplies made in Canada in the course of a Canadian business; zero-rated exports of goods are included in the threshold unless excluded by the non-resident specific rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated supply of goods under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, and related services under Part VI. For Ultra-HD 4K laparoscopy towers manufactured in Canada: (1) the supply of tangible personal property must be made to a non-resident recipient or exported by the supplier, or by a carrier or customs broker on the supplier's behalf; (2) goods must be exported from Canada as soon as is reasonably possible after the supply is made (ETA subsection 16(2)); (3) the exporter must retain contemporaneous documentary evidence of export, including CBSA export declarations, bills of lading, and foreign customs import records; (4) goods installed or delivered to a Canadian hospital, clinic, or distributor for domestic resale do not qualify; (5) warranty, repair, and technical services performed on exported goods may also be zero-rated provided they are separately invoiced and supported by service documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA Form B13A or Canadian International Trade Single Window (CITSW) export reporting for goods exceeding CAD 2,000 in value or for controlled/regulated goods; includes the exporter's business number, HS classification, and destination country
  • Bill of Lading - original through-carrier ocean bill, air waybill, or truck manifest showing the foreign port of discharge, foreign consignee, and shipping date
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice, wire transfer confirmation, letter-of-credit settlement statement, or customs-broker payment record demonstrating receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident buyer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing regime exists at the federal level. E-invoicing is voluntary; the CRA endorses the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework (Peppol PINT standard) and certifies Access Points, but participation is optional. Registrants must retain complete invoices, books, and records for six years under ETA section 286, and electronic records must be readable and convertible into a format acceptable to the CRA.

If export substantiation is defective, the CRA may reverse the zero-rating, assess uncollected 5% GST on the full invoice value, and charge compound interest at the prescribed quarterly rate. Civil penalties: false statements in a GST/HST return attract a penalty of 25% of the tax avoided, minimum CAD 250 (ETA section 285); wilful evasion under ETA section 327 is punishable by fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to five years. The normal four-year reassessment period is suspended indefinitely for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default (ETA subsection 298(6)). The General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA section 245) may recharacterize artificial export structures. There is no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline under the ETA; the 180-day figure is the CRA audit benchmark for matching foreign-currency remittances to export shipments, and longer settlement periods require documented trade terms (e.g., 360-day letters of credit). Input tax credits attributable to zero-rated exports are fully recoverable and may be refunded in cash, but are subject to enhanced verification, and the CRA may require security or revoke registration under ETA section 299 for high-risk non-resident registrants.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Canada operates a floating-rate regime (no exchange controls) and imposes no approval requirement on inbound equity, trade payments, partner distributions, or repatriation of capital. The only structural hurdles are: (i) Income Tax Act s.212 withholding tax at 25% on dividends/interest to non-resident partners (treaty-reducible); (ii) CRA reporting forms (NR4/NR301) for distributions; and (iii) FINTRAC disclosure for currency/monetary instruments ≥ CAD $10,000 crossing the border. The Investment Canada Act does not trigger for the passive holding structure of the LP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; a Canadian LP may retain USD or other foreign currency indefinitely in a domestic or offshore account. For tax, receipts are recognized in CAD at the Bank of Canada daily rate on the revenue recognition date unless a s.261 functional-currency election is filed with the CRA. Export proceeds routed through non-Canadian banks remain subject to FINTRAC large-transaction reporting and attract enhanced due diligence when individual wires exceed CAD $10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa. The Bank of Canada Act and Currency Act govern the monetary regime; there is no separate foreign-exchange administration agency. FINTRAC exercises AML/ATF surveillance over all cross-border transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium. Canada has no 'electronic money institution' licence class; non-bank cross-border providers operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses and/or Payment Service Providers registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (registration regime phased from November 2024). Such platforms (Wise, Convera, Airwallex) are viable for execution-only settlement of trade disbursements in USD/CNY/EUR, but cannot lawfully hold the LP's capital escrow, issue guarantees, or provide deposit protection. Escrow must reside at a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital is wired via SWIFT MT103 to the designated escrow account at the Schedule I bank. The remitter's SWIFT field 50K must match the name in the LP subscription ledger; field 72 must carry the purpose narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO CANADIAN LIMITED PARTNERSHIP — DIRECT INVESTMENT IN CANADA' plus the Canadian LEI. The bank maps the flow to Statistics Canada's balance-of-payments direct-investment equity category for a non-financial Canadian enterprise and screens the remitter and ordering bank against OFAC/UN/OSFI sanctions lists. No prior Bank of Canada approval is required; the only operational friction is the 1–3 day clearing window via Payments Canada's Lynx and US correspondent networks.Two rails are available: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account prepayments, sending CAD or USD via Lynx (domestic leg) then through the bank's US correspondent/CHIPS network; and (ii) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 subject to UCP600, typically at sight, with documents negotiated by the supplier's bank against invoice, packing list, B/L, and certificate of origin. CAD/USD conversion for either rail is executed on the bank's spot desk; value-date FX is settled through CLS where counterparties are members. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate after the 10% provincial abatement and the 13% general tax reduction, before provincial corporate allocation; a Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and is not itself liable to Part I corporate income tax on its allocable net income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory Part XIII withholding tax rate is 25% on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. A Canadian limited partnership is not a corporation, so a distribution by the LP to a non-resident partner is not a dividend for withholding tax purposes; instead the non-resident partner is taxable under Part I on its allocated share of Canadian-source partnership income. If the LP invests in or owns a Canadian subsidiary that pays dividends, that subsidiary must withhold 25% Part XIII tax unless a treaty reduction applies.

The T5013 return must be filed within five months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period; for a December 31 fiscal period-end, the return is due by May 31. T5013 slips must be provided to partners and filed with CRA by March 31 for calendar-year periods.
