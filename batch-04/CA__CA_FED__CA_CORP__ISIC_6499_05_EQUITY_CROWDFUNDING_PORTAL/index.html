Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; principal portal is Business Registration Online (BRO) and the My Business Account (MBA) platform. The CRA administers the federal Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST), income tax, and payroll accounts.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in Canada and all Canadian business corporations must obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) plus program account codes. Procedure: (1) determine whether taxable supplies are made in Canada or the entity otherwise carries on business in Canada; (2) register through Business Registration Online (BRO), by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or by using the CRA Non-Resident Account Registration package; (3) obtain the BN and a two-letter program account (e.g., RT for GST/HST); (4) mandatory registration is required within 30 days after the entity ceases to be a small supplier under the $30,000 threshold; (5) voluntary registration is permitted before any supply is made. No notarization or local director is statutorily required for registration, but evidence of Canadian nexus must be retained for CRA review.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – an equity crowdfunding portal incorporated as a federal Business Corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act, subject to federal income tax on worldwide income, and is not a pass-through entity. It is a separate taxpayer with full liability for corporate taxes, GST/HST, and payroll source deductions; no look-through treatment is available for Canadian law purposes.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or entity classification election comparable to the U.S. regime. Available statutory elections relevant to a crowdfunding portal include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration and election to waive the small-supplier threshold (Excise Tax Act s. 240); (ii) election to use a simplified or specified GST/HST accounting method where quarterly/annual filing criteria are met; (iii) elections for hybrid mismatch and structured arrangement adjustments (ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, 93.2); (iv) treaty-relief elections for non-resident shareholders and the s. 216 election for non-resident taxpayers receiving Canadian-source income; (v) election to have a non-resident partner or beneficiary deemed to file a Canadian return where applicable. No corporate 'check-the-box' re-characterization is available to alter the entity's default corporate status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – measured as taxable supplies made in Canada in a single calendar quarter or cumulative over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (the small-supplier threshold). For non-residents, the same threshold applies to supplies of property or services made in Canada or to Canadian-resident recipients. Registration is mandatory within 30 days of exceeding the threshold; crossing the threshold in a single quarter triggers immediate registration even without four-quarter tracking.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility arises under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For goods: exports are zero-rated when the supplier ships the goods to a non-resident recipient outside Canada and retains shipment/payment evidence. For the crowdfunding portal's services: success fees, commission, subscription fees, and facilitation charges for arranging an equity financing are financial services — exempt under Schedule V, Part VII when supplied to Canadian residents, and zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VII when supplied to non-residents, provided: (i) the recipient is not resident in Canada at the time the fee is charged; (ii) the service is not for consumption, use, or supply in Canada in the ordinary course of the recipient's business; (iii) evidence of non-resident status is obtained (e.g., certificate of incorporation, director's declaration, passport, or business registry extract). The portal must ensure the underlying crowdfunding transaction itself relates to a non-resident issuer and not merely intermediate capital originating offshore.

  • Customs Export Declaration or CERS (Canadian Export Reporting System) confirmation for goods, or the equivalent Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export report
  • Commercial invoice or service agreement evidencing the supply and fee computation
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill (for tangible goods) and platform transaction logs (for services)
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the recipient (corporate registry extract, certificate of residence, declaration of non-residence)
  • Payment and foreign exchange repatriation evidence — because Canada does not issue an 'Inward Remittance Certificate,' the CRA accepts bank wire confirmations, SWIFT MT103 records, bank statements, or payment processor settlement reports as substitutable documentation in the bundle

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API, or digital stamping system for invoices. However: (a) GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,500,000 must file returns electronically; (b) all GST/HST and source deduction remittances exceeding CAD 10,000 must be made electronically; (c) records and invoices must be retained in an electronically readable, machine-readable format for six years under ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230; (d) the CRA may require digital audit files, and the use of unauthorized accounting systems that impede audit is penalized. E-invoicing is permitted but not yet a mandatory clearance obligation at the federal level.

CRA audits of zero-rated supplies are intensive for digital crowdfunding platforms. The normal reassessment window is four years from the assessment date, but is unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default. Penalties include: (i) reassessment of unremitted GST/HST at the standard rate plus daily compound interest; (ii) gross-negligence penalties under ETA s. 285 of 25% of the net tax understated or, under ITA s. 163(2), 50% of the income tax understated; (iii) summary conviction fines up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 12 months for tax evasion under ETA s. 327; (iv) director joint and several liability under ETA s. 323 for unremitted amounts; (v) clawback of input tax credits and refunds where documentary substantiation is absent; (vi) for integrated export-financing schemes involving sham non-resident issuers, CRA may apply penalty taxes up to 50% of the recovered amounts and issue third-party civil liability assessments. Foreign currency gains on repatriation must be reported, and export proceeds not repatriated within 180 days may trigger a presumption that the zero-rating conditions failed unless the taxpayer provides contemporaneous evidence of restraint imposed by regulatory or exchange-control requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no exchange controls, no prior Central Bank authorization for cross-border transfers, no repatriation licence, and no restriction on foreign-currency account balances or non-resident equity ownership of a Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) corporation. For an equity crowdfunding portal, the practical frictions are fiscal and regulatory rather than monetary: (i) investor subscriptions must be solicited under a recognized provincial crowdfunding/prospectus exemption or through a licensed exempt market dealer, and must be held segregated in escrow; (ii) non-resident distributions are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25% on dividends and certain interest/royalties, treaty-reduced to typically 5% for direct corporate shareholders owning at least 10% voting control and 15% for portfolio investors, with interest often reduced to zero); (iii) cross-border EFTs of C$10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC reporting obligations and bank-level sanctions screening; (iv) foreign beneficial owners create FATCA/CRS self-certification duties; and (v) related-party financing arrangements may attract CRA transfer-pricing review under section 247, thin-capitalization limits under section 18(4), and T106 filing requirements, which collectively act as the de facto checks on tax-effective repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No such surrender regime exists in Canada. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no Central Bank certificate requirement for export proceeds, service revenue, or investor subscriptions received in foreign currency. The Currency Act merely designates the Canadian dollar as legal tender; a corporation may retain USD, EUR, or other currencies indefinitely in a foreign-currency account with a Canadian Schedule I bank and convert at arm's-length market rates as treasury needs dictate. The only applicable constraints are (i) the escrow agreement must specify the currency of custody and interest crediting, (ii) FINTRAC EFT-reporting thresholds apply to cross-border transfers, and (iii) the portal must avoid inadvertently carrying on a deposit-taking business under the Bank Act; holding funds without a binding securities escrow mandate or without an MSB registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act would create a supervisory classification risk.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). The foreign exchange and treasury perimeter is not governed by exchange-control licensing: Canada operates a free-floating currency regime and the Bank of Canada intervenes only in extraordinary disorderly-market conditions. The operative authorities are: (i) Department of Finance Canada and the Minister of Foreign Affairs for economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA), the United Nations Act, and the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act (Sergei Magnitsky Act); (ii) the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of Schedule I and Schedule II banks that provide the clearing rails; (iii) the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision, MSB registration, suspicious transaction reporting, and cross-border electronic funds transfer (EFT) reporting; and (iv) Payments Canada as the owner-operator of Lynx, the Canadian RTGS system, and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: conditional or Medium-Low for a primary banking rail, but Medium-High as an ancillary treasury utility. A foreign electronic money institution (EMI), such as Wise, Revolut, or Ebury, cannot rely on an EU or UK e-money passport in Canada; it holds no Canadian banking licence, cannot become a Payments Canada member, and cannot provide segregated escrow custody that satisfies provincial crowdfunding rules. An offshore EMI may operate in Canada only by (i) registering as a FINTRAC money services business (MSB), (ii) appointing a Canadian agent or entering a sponsorship arrangement with a Schedule I bank, and (iii) clearing CAD and USD through a Canadian correspondent. For an equity crowdfunding portal, the EMI channel is acceptable for small-value investor subscription intake, foreign SaaS fee payments, and retail FX conversion, but it cannot replace the bank-executed escrow undertaking required by securities regulators. The portal should therefore classify the EMI as a secondary corridor, not as the custody backbone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant subscribe-and-wire protocol: (i) the investor executes the subscription agreement and escrow undertaking naming the target issuer, contribution amount, and currency; (ii) CAD-denominated subscriptions arrive either through ACSS EFT for smaller amounts or through a Lynx RTGS wire for material contributions, with the foreign remitter's SWIFT MT103 routed through the Canadian bank's correspondent network; (iii) USD subscriptions are received through a U.S. clearing corridor, including Fedwire or CHIPS, into the Canadian bank's USD concentration account and then booked to the segregated escrow ledger; (iv) every cross-border EFT equal to or greater than C$10,000 triggers FINTRAC EFT reporting by the bank, and the portal must independently capture the wire reference, investor legal identifier, subscription agreement number, source-of-funds evidence, and any contemporaneous FX conversion memo; (v) because Canada does not mandate standardized regulatory purpose codes comparable to a U.S. Federal Reserve wire purpose field, the sending bank's Payment Details or field 72 narrative must include the subscription agreement number and issuer code to avoid suspense and ensure precise escrow ledger allocation; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: conditional or Medium-Low for a primary banking rail, but Medium-High as an ancillary treasury utility. A foreign electronic money institution (EMI), such as Wise, Revolut, or Ebury, cannot rely on an EU or UK e-money passport in Canada; it holds no Canadian banking licence, cannot become a Payments Canada member, and cannot provide segregated escrow custody that satisfies provincial crowdfunding rules. An offshore EMI may operate in Canada only by (i) registering as a FINTRAC money services business (MSB), (ii) appointing a Canadian agent or entering a sponsorship arrangement with a Schedule I bank, and (iii) clearing CAD and USD through a Canadian correspondent. For an equity crowdfunding portal, the EMI channel is acceptable for small-value investor subscription intake, foreign SaaS fee payments, and retail FX conversion, but it cannot replace the bank-executed escrow undertaking required by securities regulators. The portal should therefore classify the EMI as a secondary corridor, not as the custody backbone.
