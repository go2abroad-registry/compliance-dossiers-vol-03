Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); taxpayer portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), CRA My Business Account (MyBA), and the non-resident account registration desk; customs licensing counterpart is the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for bonded carrier authorization.

E-government filing portal

Any corporation carrying on business in Canada obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts. A federal or provincial corporation receives its BN automatically upon Articles of Incorporation and activates program accounts online (browser-based BRO or MyBA). A foreign non-resident corporation without a Canadian physical presence obtains a BN by filing the CRA non-resident registration package (RC1/RC2 series), accompanied by certified certificate of incorporation or equivalent, registered/for-service address in Canada, list of directors, and senior officer identity attestation. The GST/HST account is program account 'RT'; the corporate income tax account is 'RC'. Registration is mandatory once the entity is carrying on business in Canada or is required to charge/collect GST/HST; a nominee or agent in Canada may complete the filing. There is no paid-in minimum capital or share capital filing fee for the BN registration itself.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box regime; a Business Corporation is categorically taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act, and its residence is determined by central mind and management. Statutory elections available: (i) section 216 election for non-residents earning Canadian rental income to be taxed on a net basis; (ii) section 216.1 election to trigger a deemed disposition and arm's-length reacquisition of treaty-protected rental property; (iii) elections under sections 18.4, 18.5, and 256.1 to realign hybrid mismatch arrangements and treaty-shopping structures; and (iv) GST/HST elections for closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration. No passthrough or transparent election is available to a limited liability Business Corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold based on worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current calendar quarter; mandatory registration is required once the threshold is exceeded or where the non-resident otherwise carries on business in Canada)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Road feeder trucking of goods from a Canadian airport/warehouse to an international gateway or U.S. point of continuation qualifies for zero-rating under Part VI, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act as 'transportation of goods' to a place outside Canada, provided the movement forms part of a single continuous export journey and is performed under a contract of carriage (or sub-carriage) for the international carrier. The local airport feeder leg must be contractually linked to an international air waybill or through bill of lading; purely domestic cartage disconnected from an export move is taxable at the standard GST/HST rate. The service must be supplied to the exporter or the international carrier, and the goods must physically leave Canada within a commercially reasonable time. Proof of the export status of the goods is the primary audit test.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A or electronic CADEX/ECI equivalent) where an export declaration is required
  • CBSA Highway Cargo Control Document (A8A / eManifest cargo data at time of border crossing)
  • Contract for inland transportation naming the exporting consignor and the international air carrier
  • Master Air Waybill or through Bill of Lading evidencing the continuous international journey
  • Commercial invoice and freight bill/pro-forma invoice
  • Proof of payment settlement: bank wire, ACH, or Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Proof of delivery to the ocean/air export terminal or to the connecting inland carrier, including terminal receipts or proof of gate-out

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API settlement, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime applies federally. CRA requires periodic GST/HST net tax remittance and filing via GST/HST NETFILE and imposes digital record-keeping obligations under ETA section 286 and ITA section 230; registrants must retain and produce an electronic audit file (EAF) on request. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol PINT standard is being phased in for B2B transactions, but adoption is not compulsory. CBSA eManifest/ACI filings, not CRA clearance, govern the customs bonded trailer movement aspect.

CRA may reassess a zero-rating claim within the four-year normal assessment period and may reassess without time limit if the return contains misrepresentation attributable to negligence, carelessness, or wilful default (ETA section 298(1)). Substantiation failures trigger: denial of the zero-rating claim, reversal of associated input tax credits, interest on the assessed net tax (daily compounded at the prescribed CRA rate), and a 25% gross-negligence penalty under ETA section 280; false or fraudulent statements expose the registrant to criminal liability under ETA section 327.5 and ITA section 163.2 with fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to two years. A 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation benchmark is applied operationally by CRA examiners to test whether the consideration is genuinely settled; materially longer receivables must be justified. Repeat offenders are disqualified from the Large Exporter Net Tax Refund program and accelerated refund procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely convertible currency under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Canada maintains no general exchange-control restrictions under the Canada Business Corporations Act or the federal Income Tax Act on current-account payments; capital-account inflows and outflows are likewise unrestricted for non-resident equity and debt placements. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily documentary and tax-compliant in nature: outbound dividends require a board-declared resolution, audited or reviewed financial evidence of surplus, a Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) T2 corporate return position confirming tax status, and withholding-tax compliance under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. Treaty-based withholding-tax reductions require a valid Canada non-resident declaration and beneficial ownership documentation. Realized FX conversion is available on demand through Schedule I chartered banks at market rates; no surrender, approval, or licensing is required for customs bonded trucking operational receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds under Canadian federal law. A Canadian customs bonded truck operator may retain export earnings in CAD or foreign currency in a bank account in Canada or abroad without surrender requirements. For ISIC 5222 bonded trucking services rendered to international air carriers, the proceeds are treated as export service revenue for GST/HST zero-rating purposes under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, but no FX surrender applies. Nevertheless, for corporate treasury efficiency, all inbound EUR/USD payments should be converted within one to two business days using competitive bank or forward transaction benchmarks to avoid FX drift.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential regulatory supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/ATF transaction monitoring by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); there is no standalone foreign-exchange control ministry for routine CAD current-account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border digital EMI / e-money institutions such as Wise, Payoneer, OFX, and Airwallex may be approved as secondary settlement wallets but are rated Medium-Low viability for core treasury rail in Canada. They can facilitate low-value customs-bonded feeder expense disbursements and cross-border driver settlements, but they are not authorized deposit-taking institutions and do not provide Canadian CDIC deposit insurance, cheques, or commercial credit facilities. FINTRAC-registered money services businesses may be used as pass-through service providers, provided the operator maintains separate onboarding records, source-of-funds documentation, and 90-day transaction histories. For capital escrow, aircraft-operator payments, and CRA remittances, CAD clearing must remain at a Schedule I bank with SWIFT BIC and Payments Canada settlement access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loans from non-resident sponsors are efficiently routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the collecting Schedule I bank using CAD or USD settlement account. For capital injection in USD, the funds may be booked directly to a Canadian USD domestic settlement account or converted to CAD at the bank's corporate FX rate. The remittance must designate the appropriate Canadian purpose code for Statistics Canada reporting: equity injection should be referenced as direct investment-equity capital, and the bank must record the transaction as a foreign-direct-investment flow for balance-of-payments purposes. The bank will complete FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting for transactions at or above CAD 10,000 and maintain provenance evidence; the operator's counsel should prepare a signed directors' resolution, updated share register and subscription agreement for share issuance, or a formal shareholder-loan agreement with arm's-length interest rate if debt. Payments should be cleared through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System for same-day finality; wire buffers are generally absent in Canadian clearing, so the receiving bank must be notified in advance for amounts above CAD 1,000,000.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements shall use SWIFT MT103 via LVTS for CAD vendor payments and via SWIFT MT103 for USD/EUR cross-border payments. Recurring domestic driver settlements, fuel payments, maintenance invoices, and airport-landside fees should be batched through ACH direct payment or pre-authorized debit, or wire transfer depending on amount and urgency. Customs-bond top-ups and U.S. carrier disbursements are executed by wire transfer to the U.S. surety company or customs-broker account; if the counterparty supports it, a commercial letter of credit is used only for large equipment leases; for ordinary operating expenses, open-account wire settlement is more efficient. All outbound MT103 messages must contain structured remittance information with invoice number and U.S. Customs entry number to avoid quarantine holds. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate (after 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction under s. 123.4 ITA); CCPCs may qualify for 9% on first C$500,000 of active business income; provincial corporate tax (8%-16%) applies separately and is administered by CRA under Tax Collection Agreements.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax (Income Tax Act s. 212) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders; payable by the payer and remitted by the 15th day of the month following payment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 filing threshold: aggregate cross-border non-arm's length transactions exceeding C$1,000,000 in a fiscal year; CRA expects contemporaneous Local File documentation for such transactions and Master File for MNE groups with C$750M+ consolidated revenue; CbC Report (Form RC5220) at the same C$750M group revenue threshold. There is no statutory C$ threshold for preparing transfer pricing documentation, but the s. 247(3) penalty defence is premised on 'reasonable efforts' evidenced by complete contemporaneous documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations akin to UAE/OECD ESR; however, s. 245 GAAR re-characterizes transactions lacking bona fide purpose, courts apply the 'economic substance' doctrine (Shell Canada, Copthorne), and treaty benefits are denied under Limitation on Benefits clauses (Canada-US Protocol V) and MLI Principal Purpose Test where applicable. A customs bonded carrier must demonstrate substance through CBSA 'Bonded Carrier' authorization, Canadian vehicle registration and insurance, terminal lease, payroll, and active GPS-seal control; a paper entity with no Canadian risk-taking faces reclassification and treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / wilful misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: s. 239 ITA: criminal liability - on summary conviction, fine of 50%-200% of tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, fine of 100%-200% plus imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2): 50% of the understated tax; valuation misstatement penalties are added under s. 163(1).

Director personal liability: Under s. 242 ITA, directors and officers who directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the offence are parties to the crime and face the same imprisonment; CRA may obtain Mareva/freezing orders and criminal bail terms can impose passport surrender and travel bans pending trial.
Failure to withhold and remit Part XIII taxes

Statutory sanction: s. 227(8) ITA: the payer is liable for the full unreported withholding amount, interest at the prescribed rate, plus 10% penalty where the failure was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence. CRA may assess Part XIII tax on the gross remittance, resulting in economic double taxation.

Director personal liability: s. 227.1 ITA: directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted Part XIII withholdings and GST/HST if they failed to act with reasonable care; civil collection may target personal bank accounts, liens on director residences, and cross-border enforcement under reciprocal tax collection treaties.
