Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — official federal tax authority; primary portal: Canada.ca/taxes, Business Registration Online (BRO), and My Business Account (MyBA).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters, or upon loss of 'small supplier' status; registration is effected by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) with supporting corporate documentation (certificate of incorporation, home-jurisdiction TIN, Canadian agent for service), or online via Business Registration Online for eligible non-resident business corporations. The BN is the statutory TIN and the GST/HST registration number is expressed as BN-RTxxxx; there is no separate VAT ID. The same BN activates the T2 corporate income tax program account, the GST/HST account, and any payroll (RP) account. A non-resident may register voluntarily below the threshold where a reasonable expectation of taxable business activity in Canada exists, and the CRA assigns the non-resident filing channel and effective registration date in its notice of registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Business Corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or a provincial statute is resident in Canada under ITA s. 250(2)(a) and taxed on worldwide income under ITA Part I; no pass-through or check-the-box transparency is available at the federal level).

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; the CA_CORP is irrevocably an entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory and administrative mechanisms include: (i) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident corporation to file Part I net-basis tax on Canadian real-property/rental income instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (ii) ITA s. 219 branch tax filing (Part XIV) for a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada, with the branch tax rate treaty-reduced to 5% for US-resident corporations under Article V of the Canada-US Tax Convention; (iii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related GST/HST registrants to treat inter-company supplies of the platform's internal services as made for no consideration; (iv) the GST/HST 'Remote Goods and Services' / Specified Digital Products registration regime for non-resident suppliers of digital services, with an election to use an authorized representative/agent for registration and remittance; (v) hybrid-mismatch deduction-disallowance rules under ITA ss. 18.2/18.3 (domestic implementation of BEPS Action 2) which operate automatically and contain no elective opt-in mechanism; related disclosure is made on Form T106, and treaty-protected income elections may be filed under the applicable tax treaty; (vi) ETA s. 240(2) input tax credit (ITC) allocation elections for mixed-use financial/zero-rated supplies; and (vii) CRA advance income tax rulings for entity-classification and export zero-rating certainty.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, royalties, or on the liquidation of capital for a federally incorporated business corporation. Cross-border payments to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%; reduced under applicable tax treaties, e.g., 5%–15% for dividends and 10% for interest/royalties under the Canada–China treaty). Intercompany payments must satisfy the transfer-pricing standard of section 247 of the Income Tax Act, and any artificial routing of funds is vulnerable to the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists in Canada. There is no mandatory timeline, no requirement to convert export proceeds into CAD, and no prescribed conversion ratio. A Canadian exporting entity may lawfully retain USD, CNY, EUR, or any other foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts; the only binding requirements are accurate bookkeeping, correct income inclusion for corporate tax purposes, and FINTRAC monitoring of electronic funds transfers exceeding CA$10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential regulator for federally chartered banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF supervisor under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; Payments Canada as the operator of the national clearing and settlement infrastructure (Lynx/SWIFT). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency equivalent to SAFE or the Exchange Control Department; the Canadian dollar operates under a free-floating regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as a supplemental collection rail for Alibaba Pay and Asian corridor settlement, but not as the principal treasury or escrow repository. Offshore electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not authorized banks under the Bank Act; unless licensed as an authorized foreign bank, they cannot accept deposits from Canadian residents. Such providers may operate in the payments space only as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate; combined federal plus provincial rates range from 23% to 27% depending on province of permanent establishment (e.g., Alberta 23%, Ontario 26.5%, British Columbia 27%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder, subject to treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due no later than 6 months after the end of the fiscal period. Balance of tax due 2 months after fiscal year-end (3 months for a CCPC eligible for the Small Business Deduction). Instalments due on the last day of each quarter of the fiscal period.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Identification header and BN CRA Business Number (9-digit BN plus 4-digit RP program account), legal name, registered office address, fiscal period end date, province of permanent establishment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation and Form T106 filing are required where the aggregate value of reportable non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceeds C$1 million in a taxation year. Transfer pricing penalty protection requires that reasonable efforts be demonstrated; the section 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment applies where the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue or C$5 million. Canada does not prescribe a separate C$10 million Local/Master File threshold; the CRA requests OECD-compliant Local File and Master File in the course of an audit, and all such documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and provided within three months of a written CRA request.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute in the offshore sense. Substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA section 245), the legal doctrine of corporate residence (central management and control), permanent establishment agency rules, and treaty Limitation-on-Benefits clauses. A B2B marketplace corporation must maintain substantive activities in Canada: local board meetings with real decision-making, local employees and management, Canadian contracts and bank accounts, and genuine operating infrastructure. Absence of substance permits CRA to disregard the Canadian entity as a conduit, reattribute income to the foreign parent, deny treaty benefits, and assess Part XIII withholding on deemed distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: ITA section 239: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment; CRA gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or authorize the evasion are personally liable for the corporate tax debt under ITA section 227.1 for unremitted withholdings, subject to civil restitution, seizure of personal assets, travel bans under court enforcement orders, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: ITA section 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue or C$5 million; CRA may recharacterize transactions and impute interest, royalties, or dividends; T106 late-filing penalties of C$100 per day up to C$2,500 and C$1,000 per month for continued failure.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or incomplete T2 returns with transfer pricing misstatements may be assessed penalties as preparers under ITA section 162 and exposed to criminal liability for tax evasion if the misstatement is willful; personal assets subject to civil recovery and director travel restraints under cross-border enforcement.
Treaty Shopping and Surplus Stripping

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA section 245 denies the tax benefit and recharacterizes the transaction; deemed dividend treatment under ITA section 84 triggers 25% Part XIII withholding; treaty Limitation-on-Benefits clauses deny relief; the 50% gross negligence penalty applies to the resulting under-withholding; reassessment remains open for three years or longer in cases of misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Canadian entity who execute artificial financing or dividend-stripping arrangements face joint and several liability for unremitted Part XIII tax under ITA section 227.1, civil restitution orders, and potential criminal prosecution for participating in a fraudulent scheme under the Criminal Code.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA disregards the shell entity, attributes its income to the foreign principal under substance-over-form and GAAR, assesses the foreign principal on a Canadian permanent establishment basis, denies treaty relief, and reclassifies remittances as taxable dividends or royalties subject to 25% Part XIII; administrative penalties and interest accrue from the date the tax should have been paid.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who permit the corporation to operate without substance are personally liable for the corporation's unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholdings; courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose travel bans, issue civil forfeiture orders, and convict directors of counselling fraud where the structure is used to evade Canadian tax.
AML and Money Services Business Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Unregistered MSB activity: administrative monetary penalties up to C$1 million for an individual and C$2 million for an entity per violation; laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code section 462.31: imprisonment up to 10 years on indictment and fines at the discretion of the court; FINTRAC penalties for failure to report suspicious transactions, large cash transactions, and virtual currency transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct, authorize, or acquiesce in unregistered payment facilitation and money laundering are personally indictable, subject to imprisonment, seizure of personal and corporate assets, travel bans, and director disqualification orders; civil liability extends to restitution of laundered amounts and the corporation's AML penalties cannot be indemnified by insurance for intentional misconduct.
