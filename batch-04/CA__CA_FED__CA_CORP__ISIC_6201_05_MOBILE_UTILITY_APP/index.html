Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free and unrestricted convertibility regime for both capital and current account transactions under the Bank of Canada Act. Capital account inflows and outflows, including equity investments and profit remittances, face no quantitative restrictions or prior approval requirements for most corporate entities. Current account transactions, including royalty and fee remittances from foreign operations, are fully convertible at market rates with no capital controls or surrender mandates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is required under Canadian law. Foreign currency export receipts may be retained offshore or converted at prevailing spot rates. Entities must comply with FINTRAC reporting obligations for large or suspicious transactions and beneficial ownership disclosure under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, but no fixed timeline or ratio applies to standard software development or app-related receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for fintech EMI partnerships with Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, subject to FINTRAC AML/KYC compliance and potential registration as a money services business. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada applies a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under ss. 245 ITA to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking bona fide economic substance. The 2024/2025 GAAR amendments modernize the substance analysis, expressly disregarding transactions whose primary purpose is to obtain a tax benefit and lacking real economic substance or a reasonable business purpose. While Canada does not impose OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore holding jurisdictions, cross-border mobile app IP structures must satisfy Canadian residency, permanent establishment, and beneficial ownership rules to access treaty benefits, and transfer pricing documentation must evidence substantive activities in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Concealment

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s. 239 Income Tax Act: fines from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded (or up to C$25,000 on summary conviction), imprisonment up to 5 years, and full reassessment with gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax plus arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers jointly liable under s. 160/227.1 ITA for unremitted withholdings and corporate tax amounts in case of neglect, with civil and criminal exposure including restitution orders, travel bans, director penalty assessments, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR disallowance of tax benefits, reassessment, 50% gross-negligence penalty, interest on outstanding amounts, and potential denial of treaty relief under beneficial ownership doctrine.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false certificates or authorized sham structures may be assessed personally for the full tax avoided, faces disqualification, travel restraints, and criminal liability if fraudulent misrepresentation is proven.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, penalties up to C$1,000,000 for first administrative violations and criminal fines up to C$500,000 plus imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved in money laundering face criminal conviction, asset freezing, travel bans, personal monetary fines, and custodial sentences; civil suits for conspiracy and unjust enrichment may pierce the corporate veil.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment plus a transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the adjusted amount (minimum C$5,000) if documentation is inadequate, plus gross-negligence penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly participate in or authorize false documentation or incomplete TP filings may be personally liable for penalties and, in aggravated cases, prosecuted for tax evasion.
