Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html (My Business Account).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada, obtain a Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' via the CRA's online Non-Resident Business Registration service, by telephone, or by mailing the form to the appropriate CRA tax centre. Simultaneously apply for the RT Program Account (GST/HST). The CRA issues a 9-digit BN with an 'RT' suffix; registration is effective as of the requested date, but liability to collect tax may arise earlier if the small supplier threshold is exceeded. Foreign entities must provide incorporation/registration documents from their home jurisdiction, a permanent establishment address (or Canadian agent/mandate), and information respecting directors and beneficial ownership.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a non-resident foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada, obtain a Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' via the CRA's online Non-Resident Business Registration service, by telephone, or by mailing the form to the appropriate CRA tax centre. Simultaneously apply for the RT Program Account (GST/HST). The CRA issues a 9-digit BN with an 'RT' suffix; registration is effective as of the requested date, but liability to collect tax may arise earlier if the small supplier threshold is exceeded. Foreign entities must provide incorporation/registration documents from their home jurisdiction, a permanent establishment address (or Canadian agent/mandate), and information respecting directors and beneficial ownership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity classification election is available under Canadian federal income tax law; a Business Corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) is per se a taxpayer subject to Part I income tax. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2–18.8) apply non-electively. Available statutory elections are limited to transactional tax elections (e.g., ITA s. 85 corporate asset rollover, s. 86 share exchange, s. 89 CCAA/CCPC status elections) and do not alter the entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated financial services and certain supplies) in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and in the current quarter; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supplier is a GST/HST registrant, the supply is made to a non-resident recipient, and the goods are exported by or on behalf of the supplier as part of the transaction (export must be substantiated by shipping documents, typically within 60 days). Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated when the services are not 'in connection with' real estate situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; services performed wholly outside Canada to a non-resident are also zero-rated. Online sales of maternity and infant care commodities via e-commerce platforms will qualify where the goods are shipped to non-resident customers from Canada or to a foreign destination under a single contract, and the seller retains proof of shipment, customs export declaration, and receipt of foreign currency.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or electronic transmit via CAED
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (proof of export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Bank Confirmation (if applicable)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-API authorisation is in force. Registrants must issue prescribed information (invoice) under Article 3 of the Excise Tax Act; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or EDI) for registrants with annual taxable revenues above CAD 1.5 million. E-invoicing is optional; CRA accepts standard electronic records. There is no centralized e-invoicing clearance mandate.

CRA may issue a Notice of Assessment reassessing the supply as taxable (5% GST/HST) if export documentation fails audit scrutiny, with clawback of the zero rating and interest. For misrepresentation, the limitation period is extended (no time limit for fraud). Penalties: (i) failure to collect/remit – up to 5% of the unremitted amount plus 1% per month (max 10%); (ii) false statements or omissions – penalty of 25% of the amount of tax that would have been payable, or greater of $250 and 25% of the net tax if gross negligence; (iii) criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment (up to 5 years). Retrospective clawback of input tax credits previously claimed on export goods may also apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and applies no exchange restrictions, no central bank conversion approvals, and no repatriation quotas. Dividends, management fees, royalties, interest, and loan repayments to non-residents may be remitted freely, subject only to (i) Part XIII withholding tax of generally 25% (treaty-reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty or other applicable comprehensive agreements for eligible treaty residents); (ii) Part 219 branch tax of 25% where the Canadian entity is a non-resident-owned branch; (iii) thin-capitalization and section 212.3 ITA rules limiting deductible interest on excessive non-resident debt; and (iv) FINTRAC EFT reporting for cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. For the online sale of maternity and infant care commodities, no foreign-investment screening approval, import license, or controlled-goods permit is required at the federal level; normal CBSA import and GST/HST registration obligations apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian federal law imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. Exporters, including e-commerce operators, may retain, convert, or reinvest foreign currency receipts in Canadian-domiciled foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts without restriction. The only applicable obligations are statistical and AML-related: (i) authorized banks report cross-border transactions to Statistics Canada under the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) using IMF BPM6 purpose codes; (ii) FINTRAC receives EFT reports for outgoing and incoming transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) physical cross-border movements of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported under the Cross-border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); the Bank administers the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime for payment service providers and acts as monetary authority, while exchange control and monetary instruments policy rest with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52). Prudential supervision of banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act; AML/CFT enforcement is performed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17); cross-border trade and customs compliance is enforced by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Canada maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration; the former Foreign Exchange Control Board was dissolved in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Offshore fintech EMIs, such as UK or EU e-money institutions, cannot accept deposits or operate as banks in Canada without a banking license under the Bank Act; accepting deposits without such license is unlawful (section 539 Bank Act) and funds held at an EMI are e-money claims, not bank deposits eligible for Canadian deposit insurance (CDIC). An offshore EMI may only act as (i) an unlicensed payment facilitator channeling funds into a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank account, or (ii) a registered Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and a registered Payment Service Provider (PSP) under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada, which applies to retail payment functions involving Canadian end-users. Feasibility rating: Low for treasury consolidation or as a primary liquidity clearing rail; Medium for niche collection-only corridors such as receiving USD, GBP, or EUR payouts from offshore marketplaces, subject to daily zero-balance sweeps into the Canadian bank account and customer-facing disclosures that the EMI is not a bank. Accordingly, the corporate treasury stack should treat the Canadian Schedule I bank account as the single source of clearing truth and use fintech EMIs only as auxiliary collection rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity or shareholder loans exclusively via SWIFT MT103 between the shareholder's bank and the Canadian Schedule I bank. For equity: Field 50K must carry the shareholder's legal name; Field 59 must carry CA_CORP's account in standard Canadian format (institution number, transit number, account number, or equivalent clearing identifier); Field 70 Remittance Information must state 'Share capital subscription - CA_CORP - ISIC 4791 e-commerce'; the receiving bank records the BPM6 capital purpose code (Equity Capital) for Statistics Canada ITRS reporting and FINTRAC EFT reporting where the transfer equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. For shareholder loans: execute a dated loan agreement at the CRA prescribed rate, enter Field 70 as 'Intercompany loan - CA_CORP', and confirm that the lending shareholder is a treaty resident eligible for reduced or zero Part XIII withholding; maintain debt-to-equity within the thin-capitalization ceiling to preserve interest deductibility. Cash injections must never be made in physical currency; crypto funding or mixed-purpose senders will trigger enhanced due diligence. GST/HST treatment: subscription for shares is an exempt financial service; no GST/HST applies to the capital injection. The bank may request a letter of undertaking and beneficial-ownership confirmation before crediting the first inbound wire.Use (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account settlements to foreign suppliers, with Field 70 remittance information containing invoice and purchase order references; (ii) commercial letters of credit governed by UCP600 for larger or higher-risk inventory bookings, particularly where the supplier requires a confirmed LC, routed through the bank's trade finance desk with compliant transport document presentation; (iii) SWIFT MT101 bulk payment files for recurring disbursements involving multiple beneficiaries; and (iv) the bank's online FX and payments platform for sub-threshold settlements. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (gross Part I rate 38%, less 10% provincial abatement, less 13% general rate reduction; applicable to the taxable income of a Canadian Business Corporation not claiming the Small Business Deduction)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder (s.212(2) ITA), subject to reduction under an applicable income tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is six months after the taxation year-end for both resident and non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada (e.g., a December 31 year-end must be filed by June 30 of the following year). Tax instalments are generally due quarterly for corporations whose taxable income exceeds C$3,000 (instalment threshold); interest on late instalments is compounded daily at the CRA prescribed rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian transfer-pricing law (s.247 ITA) imposes no monetary de minimis for the requirement to maintain contemporaneous documentation; every cross-border non-arm's-length transaction with a foreign affiliate (including inventory purchases of maternity supplies) must be documented before the T2 return is filed. Practical thresholds: Form T5806 (Transfer-Pricing Arrangements Summary) must be filed when aggregate covered transactions exceed C$5,000,000; Country-by-Country Report under BEPS Action 13 is mandatory for ultimate-parent MNE groups with consolidated revenue of C$1 billion or more; local file and master file documentation must be produced within 3 months of a CRA written request to obtain relief from the s.247(3) penalty of 10% of net mispricing adjustments that exceed the greater of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply standalone offshore Economic Substance Regulations comparable to UAE/Barbados; however economic substance is a critical legal doctrine under the federal General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) reinforced by the Supreme Court of Canada decision in Deans Knight (2023): a corporation lacking genuine business purpose, staff, premises, or active decision-making cannot support a tax-motivated arrangement (e.g., treaty-based dividend stripping, conduit financing, or loss-trading) and the benefit will be denied with penalties and interest. The 'sham transaction' doctrine and single-purpose corporate veil principles similarly permit CRA to disregard an artificial e-commerce shell and re-characterize income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239(1) ITA: imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of not less than 50% and up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded; the civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under s.163(2), plus compound interest, applies independently.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorized or participated in the falsification may be criminally charged as parties to the offence; in civil context, the corporate veil is pierced where fraud or deceit is established, and CRA may assess s.160 transferee liability against directors who extracted funds, with travel bans under s.226 ITA for federal tax debts exceeding C$100,000.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping Conduit

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under GAAR (s.245 ITA) to deny the treaty benefit, apply the 25% Part XIII withholding tax, and impose arrears interest; penalties up to 25% of tax avoided; administrative monetary penalties where inaccurate information returns are filed, and transfer-pricing penalties if intercompany markups are artificial.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or execute conduit activity risk personal criminal liability for tax evasion where the arrangement is a wilful deceit rather than aggressive planning; civil personal liability may be imposed for distributions made to shareholders while the corporation is insolvent, and CRA may issue an assessment against directors under s.227.1 for any unremitted withholding stream; travel bans may be imposed for large arrears.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA adjusts the taxpayer to the arm's-length price under s.247(2), increasing taxable income and Part I tax; s.247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net mispricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the greater of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; the documentation deficiency (absence of contemporaneous records) forfeits the taxpayer's right to reduce the penalty under s.247(4); interest is compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Although the penalty is corporate, a CFO/director who signs false transfer-pricing documentation may be prosecuted under s.239(1); directors may also be personally liable if mispriced payments are structured as dividends/return-of-capital extracted by the director, or where the mispricing constitutes fraudulent conveyance; s.160 transferee assessments are common against related shareholders.
Failure to Remit Source Deductions and GST/HST (Trust Monies)

Statutory sanction: Section 227(8) ITA and s.280(1) ETA: penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount (increased to 20% for intentional failure), plus daily compound interest; the amounts are deemed trust property of the Crown ranking ahead of all other claims; CRA may issue a lien or garnishment and pursue collection immediately.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1(1) ITA for all unremitted withholding and GST/HST amounts; the 'reasonable care' defence under s.227.1(3) is extremely narrow; statutory assessments against directors are personally enforceable; serious delinquency (federal debt exceeding C$100,000) can trigger s.226 travel-ban action preventing the individual from leaving Canada.
Money Laundering / Unregistered Money-Service Activity / AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA offences: on indictment, fine up to C$2,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for a corporation (C$250,000 for an individual) per violation; CRA may also invoke gross-negligence penalties for unreported income traces.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly direct, authorize, or acquiesce in a non-compliant AML program are subject to personal administrative penalties under the PCMLTFA and to criminal prosecution as accessories; the corporate veil does not shield intentional AML breaches, and CRA/CBSA cross-border seizure and travel prohibition powers apply.
