Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The CBCA imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement. The Articles of Incorporation must authorize at least one class of shares; if more than one class is authorized, the rights, privileges, restrictions and conditions attaching to each class must be stated. Shares may be issued for consideration fixed by the directors, including cash, property or past services, and no escrow account is required at the federal level. The corporation must maintain a registered office situated at a location within Canada designated in the Articles.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - national authority for corporate income tax (Income Tax Act, Part I) and Part IX GST/HST (Excise Tax Act); portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO). Customs export controls are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (eManifest/CAED).

E-government filing portal

A federally incorporated CBCA Business Corporation obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Corporations Canada at incorporation; the BN is the statutory TIN, and program accounts (RP for income tax, RT for GST/HST, RC for payroll) are activated through BRO or Form RC1. A foreign (non-resident) corporation carrying on business in Canada must file Form RC476 or RC1 with the certificate of incorporation, foreign registry extract, proof of Canadian registered office/resident agent, and CRA authorization, after which an 'NR'-prefixed BN is issued with required program accounts. Voluntary GST/HST registration is available immediately; mandatory registration applies once the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded. Online processing is instantaneous; paper applications take 2-8 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a CBCA Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer and cannot be a pass-through. Canadian-resident corporations (including corporations incorporated in Canada under ITA s. 250(4)(a)) are taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits; non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a CBCA Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer and cannot be a pass-through. Canadian-resident corporations (including corporations incorporated in Canada under ITA s. 250(4)(a)) are taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits; non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income. No territorial system election and no tax-exempt zone is available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists for Canadian corporations. Available elections: (1) GST/HST closely-related group election under ETA s. 273; (2) ITA s. 216(1) election for a non-resident to file a Part I net-basis return on Canadian rental income instead of Part XIII withholding; (3) ITA s. 93(1) election on dispositions of shares of foreign affiliates; (4) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2-18.7, which override contractual elections and deny deductions on certain hybrid arrangements. CRA Folio S4-F6-C2 confirms that a U.S. check-the-box election may determine the Canadian tax classification of a foreign hybrid subsidiary, but a domestic Canadian corporation has no entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) in the current calendar quarter and preceding four calendar quarters; exceeding the small-supplier threshold triggers mandatory registration under ETA ss. 148(1) and 240(1). Voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits (ITCs). A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada without carrying on business in Canada must register under ETA s. 240(1.1).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST/HST) applies under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. Goods: supplies exported by the vendor outside Canada qualify under Parts I and VI of Schedule VI upon proof of export. Services (media relations, crisis public relations, communications retainers): Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 zero-rates a service supplied to a person who is not resident in Canada, unless the service relates to real property in Canada, relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time performed, is a personal service rendered to an individual in Canada, or is rendered in connection with Canadian criminal or civil proceedings. An overseas PR communication firm supplying services to non-resident clients qualifies for zero-rating where none of those exclusions applies; advertising production/publishing services for non-resident markets are similarly zero-rated under Part V. The supplier claims the resulting refund (excess ITCs over output tax) on its GST/HST return (GST34/NETFILE), subject to CRA verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA G13/B13A filed electronically through eManifest/CAED (mandatory for controlled goods or goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more where physical promotional materials are exported)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement and foreign destination of exported goods
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate: bank-issued inward remittance advice confirming receipt of export proceeds from abroad (not a statutory CRA form but standard audit substantiation)
  • Commercial invoice or signed retainer contract identifying the non-resident recipient, description of services, place of supply, and zero-rating basis
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the client (foreign business registry extract or W-8-type certificate)
  • GST/HST return (GST34) working papers reconciling zero-rated supplies and input tax credit claims

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API clearing mandate exists. E-invoicing and EDI are permitted; the CRA's PEPPOL-based e-invoicing adoption (Pan-Canadian Sprint, 2023-2026) is voluntary. Mandatory invoice content under ETA s. 223 and the Information Respecting Supplies by Registrants Regulations: supplier name and GST/HST registration number (BN + RT), invoice date, total consideration, tax charged, and description of supply; zero-rated export invoices should expressly state 'zero-rated' and cite the Schedule VI basis. Records must be kept in English or French for six years in an approved format; verification is ex post through audit, not pre-clearance.

Zero-rated export refunds are subject to CRA desk audits and post-refund review; the normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years (ETA s. 298(1)), extended to seven years for misrepresentation and unlimited for fraud. Unsupported or inflated zero-rating claims trigger clawback of the refund and ITCs plus daily compound interest at the prescribed rate plus 4% (ETA s. 280). A false or careless statement attracts a 25% contingent penalty under ETA s. 285(1); failure to file or remit incurs penalties under s. 280(1); and deliberate evasion prosecuted under ETA s. 327 carries fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier is an internal audit-control target for export documentation, and prolonged deferral of export proceeds without commercial justification may invite source/situs and transfer-pricing scrutiny under ITA Part XVI and s. 247.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a freely floating Canadian dollar (CAD) regime with no exchange control legislation restricting current or capital account convertibility. Non-resident direct investment in a federal corporation is permitted, and profit/dividend remittance is subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, generally reduced under the Canada-US Tax Treaty or other applicable income tax treaties. FINTRAC requires reporting of all cross-border electronic fund transfers (EFTs) and large cash transactions meeting regulatory thresholds, but no prior exchange-control approval is required for ordinary capital injections or repatriation. Companies should maintain transfer-pricing documentation and corporate records to support intercompany payments and avoid audit challenge.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Exporters and service providers, including PR/communications firms, may retain foreign currency revenue in designated foreign-currency bank accounts. Traveler currency reporting and FINTRAC cross-border reporting apply to physical currency, monetary instruments, and EFTs equal to CAD 10,000 or more. Corporate tax filings require foreign-source income and foreign currency gains to be reported in Canadian dollars under the functional currency rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange and AML surveillance co-administrated by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium with legal limitations. Canadian federal corporations may hold accounts with FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs) such as Wise, Payoneer, or specialty payment gateways, but these entities are not banks under the Bank Act and cannot offer commercial letters of credit, bank guarantees, or high-value SWIFT correspondent settlement. Digital EMIs are viable as supplementary rails for small retainer ACH/international remittance collections and employee expense reimbursements, provided the provider's custody model is audited and beneficial ownership does not trigger deemed residency or withholding exposure. Principal corporate treasury, clearing-grade settlement, and hedging lines must remain with a Schedule I chartered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder capital is wired to the Canadian operating account via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank, with remittance information citing 'Equity / Contributed Surplus' or 'Shareholder Loan'. Canada has no foreign-exchange approval or designated purpose-code requirement at the central bank level; banks will rely on the SWIFT sender and receiver fields for FINTRAC EFT reporting if the value is CAD 10,000 or more. Injected funds should be supported by a board resolution, a subscription agreement, and an entry in the federal corporation's authorized capital ledger. For USD wires, the Canadian bank's correspondent (usually in New York) will apply OFAC screening; the entity must ensure no listed party appears in the wire chain. For amounts exceeding CAD 1 million, the bank may require a certified capital declaration or evidence of the transaction's commercial substance.Outbound retainer settlements to overseas media, research vendors, and PR agencies use SWIFT MT103 clean payments with full beneficiary account and bank details. For high-ticket campaign purchases, a commercial letter of credit issued under UCP600 on the Canadian bank's blank is used, especially where the vendor requires documentary compliance. Domestic and cross-border ACH is available only for CAD-denominated payments; USD outflows are cleared via CHIPS/Fedwire through the Canadian bank's US correspondent. All outbound EFTs above CAD 10,000 are reported to FINTRAC. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (Part I of the Income Tax Act), combined with provincial corporate rate yielding 11.5% to 16.5% depending on province

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax under Income Tax Act subsection 212(2); applicable to all dividend payments from a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders unless reduced by a bilateral income tax treaty

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the end of the corporate fiscal year (calendar year for most PR agencies); balance of tax payable due 2 months after year-end (3 months for qualifying CCPCs); non-resident corporations must file within 6 months of the end of the taxation year in which they were carrying on business in Canada

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 6 months of the corporation's taxation year-end; no instalment obligation attaches to the information return itself

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: CAD 1,000,000 or more of reportable non-arm's length transactions with any single non-resident related party; Master File: CAD 50,000,000 or more of gross revenue reported by the Canadian MNE group (consolidated group revenue also triggering); T106 must be filed at the CAD 1,000,000 threshold

Economic substance scope: Canada enforces economic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (subsection 245(2) ITA) and the BEPS Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test (PPT) which overrides Canadian treaties; a foreign-owned PR agency must demonstrate real activity: local employees, leased office premises, active client negotiation in Canada, substantive decision-making, and bank accounts holding business revenue; a passive or shell conduit vehicle with no business functions will fail treaty benefits and attract recharacterisation under the GAAR; the CRA 2025 mandatory disclosure rules also require Form RC312 to be filed for reportable tax avoidance transactions that lack substantive commercial purpose

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 239(1) Income Tax Act: summary conviction – fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment – fine of 100% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA additionally issues gross negligence penalties under subsection 163(2) equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly direct evasion face criminal liability, personal restitution equal to the whole tax shortfall and penalties, seizure orders over personal assets, prohibition on travel under immigration enforcement upon criminal warrant, and potential imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced where the entity was used as a vehicle of fraud
Sham Conduit / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: The CRA may re-attribute the income to the actual beneficial owner, deny the entire deduction base of the shell, disallow treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, and impose a GAAR penalty of 50% of the federal tax avoided (subsection 245(3) ITA); late filing and gross negligence penalties aggregate; a court can order the taxpayer to disclose all transactions and authorise CRA seizure

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the sham are jointly and severally liable for at least 50% of the corporate tax liability and can be assessed personally; travel bans are imposed where evasion exceeds CAD 25,000 in unassessed tax; the penal verdict activates a Canadian criminal record triggering border-control screens
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment under subsection 247(3) ITA where the adjustment exceeds CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue and reasonable efforts were lacking; a secondary penalty of 100% interest on the tax attributable to the adjustment applies; reassessments can reach back 7 years for gross negligence, otherwise 3 years for normal reassessment windows

Director personal liability: Directors who signed fraudulent documentation to justify inflated charges become personally liable for the penalty and the adjustment interest; a director's reasonable efforts test is evaluated strictly; in egregious cases, CRA applies subsection 227.1(1) for unremitted amounts and seeks civil restitution orders against the individuals involved
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Failures in Business Process

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and Criminal Code: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per occurrence for failing CDD; criminal money laundering offence under section 354 Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to 10 years and forfeiture of property that is proceeds of crime; the Canada Border Services Agency places sanctioned persons on the immigration travel prohibition index

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly accept tainted funds face criminal prosecution, repatriation and confiscation of assets, travel bans and interception at the border, disqualification from serving as directors, and direct civil liability for resulting fines; the veil is pierced as money laundering is considered an intentional criminal act
Unremitted Withholding and Payroll Liabilities

Statutory sanction: Directors' liability provisions (subsection 227.1) allow recovery of the amount not remitted, plus interest and penalties; withholding penalties equal 10% of the amount plus 5% increment per month up to 50%; the CRA can file a lien for the amount and seize corporate inventory, accounts receivable, and bank accounts without prior court order

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly and severally liable for 100% of unremitted GST/HST, payroll CPP/EI, and Part XIII amounts, even where the corporation is bankrupt or dissolved; no rationalisation of 'acting in good faith' excuses failure to verify remittances; directors who resigned within a one-year period pre-receivable are still liable; travel bans apply where the CRA certifies a criminal charge of failing to remit under section 239(1.1)
