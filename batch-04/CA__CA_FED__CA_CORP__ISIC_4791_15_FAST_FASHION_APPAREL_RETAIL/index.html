Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and Corporate Income Tax registration via the CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity must apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with the CRA, typically using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Online registration through the CRA portal is available where an authorized representative with a Canadian presence exists; otherwise, the foreign entity files by mail with certified proof of incorporation (certificate of corporate status, Articles, or equivalent), full name and address of all directors, authorized signatory powers, a Canadian contact of record for service of documents, and details of intended Canadian GST/HST accounts. After CRA validation, the BN plus GST/HST account suffix (RT) is issued; no notarised foreign TIN is required, but intergovernmental tax information exchange references may be requested.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident foreign entity must apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with the CRA, typically using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Online registration through the CRA portal is available where an authorized representative with a Canadian presence exists; otherwise, the foreign entity files by mail with certified proof of incorporation (certificate of corporate status, Articles, or equivalent), full name and address of all directors, authorized signatory powers, a Canadian contact of record for service of documents, and details of intended Canadian GST/HST accounts. After CRA validation, the BN plus GST/HST account suffix (RT) is issued; no notarised foreign TIN is required, but intergovernmental tax information exchange references may be requested.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian tax law has no check-the-box election comparable to U.S. Treasury Reg. §301.7701-3. A federally or provincially incorporated Business Corporation is automatically subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada). Available elective mechanisms include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration (effective immediately, with input tax credit recovery) for a non-resident vendor whose taxable supplies otherwise remain below the small supplier threshold; (ii) the s.216 election for a non-resident to file Canadian net-basis tax on rents; (iii) the group or associated corporation election for Corporation Tax rules in certain provincial regimes; and (iv) the hybrid mismatch alignment rules under ss.18.2/18.4 of the Income Tax Act for payments that might otherwise be treated inconsistently by Canada vs. the counterparty jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies attributable to Canada (small supplier threshold); however, e-commerce platform operators and certain non-resident vendors selling through prescribed marketplace platforms may be required to register and collect GST/HST regardless of volume, subject to special separate thresholds and deemed supplier rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property from Canada qualify for zero-rating (0% GST/HST) if: (i) the goods are sold to a non-resident purchaser; (ii) the goods are loaded aboard a vessel, aircraft, or export carrier in Canada; (iii) the goods are exported as soon as practicable after sale; and (iv) the vendor retains documentary evidence of export. Services and intangible personal property supplied directly to a non-resident can also be zero-rated if the supply is not made to a resident of Canada and is not substantially connected to Canadian real property or Canadian-source intellectual property use. No express customs export declaration or formal exit certificate is statutorily required for GST/HST zero-rating if alternative export evidence (e.g., airway bill, truck bill of lading, ocean bill of lading, Canada Border Services Agency customs coding) is maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency – B13A Export Declaration or voluntary CAED record)
  • Bill of Lading (airway, marine, or truck shipment document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or FX credit advice showing payment receipt from the foreign buyer within prescribed banking timelines

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system at the federal level. E-invoicing is voluntary; CRA accepts electronic filing of GST/HST returns through the CRA portal or commercial software. A mandatory e-filing obligation applies for businesses with GST/HST taxable turnover exceeding CAD 1.5M. There is no national continuous transaction control (CTC) import/export e-invoice clearance. Certain provincial measures (e.g., Quebec’s Revenu Québec payments) require digital remittance, but no national mandate exists.

A claim for zero-rating or GST/HST refund on export transactions is subject to retroactive audit by CRA, with the exporter bearing the burden of proof. Failure to maintain the required export documentation renders the supply taxable at 5% GST and subjects the vendor to reassessment of uncollected GST/HST, plus interest and penalties. Under the Excise Tax Act, penalties include: a 25% gross negligence penalty under s.285.1 for false or omitted statements where the exporter has knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence failed to reflect the true value of exported goods or the eligibility of the export; a further 20% penalty for repeat violations; cancellation or clawback of any refund or rebate paid; and criminal prosecution where deliberate misrepresentation is established, including fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment for up to 6 months.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada's currency is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control restrictions on the CAD/CAD cross-border remittance of dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, or capital repatriation, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act and any treaty reductions. Inward direct investment by non-residents is likewise unrestricted at the federal level, except for sectoral national-security reviews under the Investment Canada Act. E-commerce retail receipts may be collected in CAD or foreign currency without Bank of Canada approval; however, every financial transaction of C$10,000 or more is subject to record-keeping and reporting under the PCMLTFA, and clearing and settlement must be effected through licensed Canadian banks, authorized foreign bank branches, or registered money services businesses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists in Canada. Exporters may retain receivables in any currency, and there are no mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Revenue recognition for tax purposes follows CRA rules; evidence of export invoices, advance contracts, and freight/customs documentation must be retained in accordance with the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act (GST/HST), but no repatriation timing or conversion ration is imposed by the federal government.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange regulatory functions are otherwise dispersed across the Department of Finance Canada, OSFI for prudential supervision of federally regulated financial institutions, and FINTRAC for AML/CTF supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated SAFE-style foreign-exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Canada does not issue European-style electronic money institution (EMI) licences and does not recognise passporting into Canada. A foreign digital EMI that wishes to provide money-transfer, payment-processing, or virtual-asset services to Canadian residents must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and appoint a Canadian agent, unless it is acting exclusively as a software provider or payment facilitator on behalf of a regulated Canadian bank. Foreign EMIs do not have direct access to the Bank of Canada wholesale settlement system (Lynx) and therefore generally rely on a Schedule I bank or an authorized foreign bank branch as settlement correspondent. For a low-risk fashion e-commerce treasury stack, a foreign EMI is not a viable primary account provider but may be used solely for marginal cross-border disbursements where the underlying bank holds segregated funds and FULLING AML/KYC is centralized in Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections must be wire-transferred in CAD (or converted immediately) via SWIFT MT103, with originator name and account number matched to the capital-active investor, and purpose code in Field 70 coded as 'CORPCAP' or 'CAPIT' where the bank accepts standardized payment descriptors. The receiving corporate bank will reconcile the wire to the operating or escrow account only after source-of-funds verification, which is supported by a board share subscription resolution, executed subscription agreement, evidence of international bank path, and in cases of arm's-length lending an executed term loan agreement with a reasonable market interest rate. The Bank of Canada Lynx real-time gross settlement system is used for high-value CAD flows between Canadian banks, but the retail client sees the credited balance in a regular operational account; any deposit exceeding C$10,000 triggers mandatory FINTRAC record-keeping but no prior approval requirement.Outbound trade settlements for imported apparel and footwear are cleared through bank wire transfers (SWIFT MT103) in CAD or foreign currencies, international commercial letters of credit (LC) under UCP600 rules, documentary collections, and Payment Canada retail batch rails when applicable to domestic suppliers. For cross-border e-commerce disbursements to suppliers in China, Bangladesh, Vietnam, and other garment-producing jurisdictions, a high-volume importer should maintain USD and CNY accounts with its Canadian bank only after determining the correspondent-bank fee and settlement risk profile. Where contractual documents are synchronous, an irrevocable LC at sight may be opened against the seller's invoice and transport documents; for recurring activity, MT103 direct wires with unique end-to-end transaction references are the main liquidity-release mechanism and must be matched against the approved master data of the supplier to prevent fraud. 15% federal general corporate income tax rate. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation may generally access a 9% federal small business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of eligible active business income, subject to the associated-corporation, capital-business-limit, passive-income, and other statutory restrictions. Provincial or territorial corporate income tax is additional and depends on the province or territory and income allocation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid or credited to a non-resident, subject to treaty reduction. Common treaty outcomes include 5% where the beneficial owner is a qualifying corporate shareholder meeting ownership thresholds and 15% for other qualifying recipients. The Canada-United States treaty commonly provides 5% for qualifying corporate ownership and 15% otherwise.

The T2 return is due six months after the end of each taxation year. Any balance is generally due two months after year-end. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation may generally pay its balance three months after year-end if it satisfies the statutory conditions, including the applicable taxable-income and associated-corporation requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no general transaction-value threshold below which contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is automatically unnecessary. Section 247 of the Income Tax Act requires reasonable efforts to determine arm's-length terms and contemporaneous documentation must be prepared by the filing deadline and provided to CRA within three months of a written request. Form T106 generally applies to reportable non-arm's-length non-resident transactions, commonly where the applicable aggregate transaction threshold exceeds CAD 1 million. Country-by-country reporting, Master File, and Local File obligations apply to qualifying multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million, implemented in Canada through the corresponding CAD threshold published for the relevant year; the Canadian Local File and Master File requirements apply to relevant Canadian constituent entities and material controlled transactions.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Economic substance remains relevant through the transfer-pricing arm's-length requirement, the principal-purpose test and other treaty anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, the general anti-avoidance rule, sham-transaction principles, permanent-establishment analysis, and corporate-residence tests. A Canadian corporation should maintain Canadian decision-making, personnel, premises, records, operational risks, inventory controls, and commercially coherent functions proportionate to its reported activities. A foreign parent cannot convert a routine distributor or ecommerce operator into a non-taxable commissionaire merely through contractual labels.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act criminal conviction may result in a fine generally ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, or a minimum statutory fine where applicable, and imprisonment of up to two years. Civil reassessment, arrears interest, gross-negligence penalties, and fraud penalties may apply independently.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability where they personally participate, authorize, assent to, or acquiesce in the conduct. The corporate veil is not routinely pierced solely because of office, but personal liability may arise for statutory amounts, knowing participation, fraudulent conduct, or tortious conduct. Imprisonment is possible after criminal conviction; a general automatic tax-related travel ban does not apply.
Failure to Remit Payroll Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the unremitted amount, prescribed interest, remittance penalties, and possible criminal prosecution. Director liability under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act may apply where statutory conditions are met.

Director personal liability: Current and former directors may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions unless they establish the statutory due-diligence defence. Liability is not normally discharged merely by corporate insolvency or resignation.
GST/HST Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Assessment of tax, interest, administrative penalties, input-tax-credit denial, gross-negligence penalties, and potential prosecution under the Excise Tax Act for serious conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST where the statutory director-liability rules apply. Fraudulent or knowing conduct may create direct criminal and civil exposure.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may make a section 247 adjustment, deny or reduce deductions, impose transfer-pricing penalties generally equal to 10% of the relevant adjustment where the statutory threshold and conditions are met, and assess interest and additional penalties. Treaty relief may be limited by anti-abuse rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure for knowing participation in fraudulent documentation or tax evasion. Ordinary transfer-pricing reassessment generally remains a corporate liability unless separate misconduct or statutory liability exists.
Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for the required withholding, interest, penalties, and information-return failures. Treaty reductions are not available merely because a recipient presents an invoice or is incorporated in a treaty jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors may become personally liable for certain unremitted amounts where statutory director-liability provisions apply or where they knowingly participated in the breach. A contractual gross-up does not eliminate the Canadian payer's withholding obligation.
Shell Company, Sham, and Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, apply the general anti-avoidance rule, reassess income, deny deductions, impose penalties and interest, and refer intentional conduct for criminal prosecution. A shell company is not unlawful solely because it has limited operations, but sham documentation and concealment are high-risk conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face civil restitution, corporate-law claims, criminal fraud or tax charges, and asset seizure where they participated in the scheme. Travel restrictions are not automatic and generally require a court order, bail condition, enforcement process, or separate criminal proceeding.
Books, Records, and Electronic Evidence Failure

Statutory sanction: Records may be compelled, deductions may be denied, penalties may apply, and persistent non-compliance may lead to compliance orders, contempt proceedings, or criminal prosecution under applicable tax legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face orders to produce records, contempt consequences, and personal criminal exposure for obstruction, destruction, or falsification. The corporation should generally retain relevant records for at least six years after the end of the related taxation year, subject to longer periods and litigation or CRA directions.
Customs, Origin, and Import Valuation Fraud

Statutory sanction: Canada Border Services Agency may reassess duties and taxes, impose administrative monetary penalties, seize goods, suspend import privileges, and prosecute serious offences under customs and related legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face prosecution, fines, imprisonment for serious offences, seizure-related losses, and personal liability for fraudulent participation. Corporate insolvency does not automatically eliminate customs liabilities.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Sanctions Exposure

Statutory sanction: A standard apparel retailer is generally not a FINTRAC reporting entity merely because it sells clothing, but separate obligations may arise from regulated payment, financing, remittance, or virtual-currency activities. Proceeds-of-crime and sanctions breaches can trigger asset forfeiture, administrative penalties, substantial fines, and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face direct criminal liability, forfeiture, imprisonment, and regulatory disqualification for knowing participation, authorization, or wilful blindness. Travel restrictions may arise from criminal bail or court orders but are not an automatic consequence of ordinary corporate tax non-compliance.
Corporate Insolvency and Fraudulent Preference

Statutory sanction: Transactions may be set aside under federal or provincial insolvency and corporate statutes, directors may be assessed for statutory liabilities, and fraudulent conduct may result in criminal charges and asset recovery.

Director personal liability: Directors may face oppression, misfeasance, breach-of-duty, fraudulent-conveyance, and statutory claims, together with restitution, asset recovery, director disqualification, fines, and imprisonment for criminal conduct.
