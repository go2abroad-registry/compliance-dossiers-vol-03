Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Federal tax administration; online portal: CRA Business Registration (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/) and My Business Account

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) using Form RC1, or register online through the CRA Business Registration portal. For GST/HST purposes, the non-resident must open a GST/HST account with the BN if it makes taxable supplies in Canada. The BN is the statutory taxpayer identification number; no separate corporate TIN is issued at the federal level. Registration requires a Canadian domestic mailing address or authorized representative with a Canadian address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have check-the-box entity classification. A federally incorporated or registered business corporation is a tax entity under the Income Tax Act. Non-resident corporations may elect under section 216 or section 219 to compute Canadian tax on certain Canadian-source income, and corporations may file consolidated returns only in limited specified circumstances. Hybrid mismatch elections under the OECD Multilateral Instrument are not applicable as a domestic check-the-box; anti-hybrid rules apply under the Income Tax Act and must be reviewed separately.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) per calendar quarter; mandatory registration once threshold exceeded. Special rules apply to non-resident businesses making supplies in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act applies to direct and immediate exportation of tangible personal property, and to certain supplied services performed wholly or partly outside Canada or rendered to non-residents if the supply is not delivered to a Canadian recipient and qualifies under section 9 or 7 of Part V of Schedule VI. Video game anti-cheat middleware supplied as digital software to foreign gaming operators generally qualifies as a zero-rated export if supplied to a non-resident and effectively used wholly outside Canada.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (Form B13 or Canada Border Services Agency equivalent in CASL/EDI)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract (including proof of non-resident purchaser)
  • Packing list and transport documents (Bill of Lading/Airway Bill)
  • Evidence of dispatcher or carrier instructions showing export destination
  • Bank transfer or SWIFT Wire inward remittance with foreign exchange record
  • Signed statement of use outside Canada for digital services or intellectual property transfers

There is no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no government-run pre-clearance API, and no digital stamping requirement for GST/HST. Registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically if annual revenue exceeds CAD 1 million, but e-invoicing remains voluntary and business-to-business integrity is governed by CRA record-keeping rules. Payments via SWIFT Wire or Stripe do not require CRA clearance.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to CRA audit scrutiny; falsified export documentation or failure to substantiate within the reassessment period can result in reassessment of tax, interest, penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act (including penalty of 25% of net tax evaded for gross negligence), and possible recovery of input tax credits previously claimed. Fraudulent export claims may also trigger criminal prosecution under section 327. Non-arm's-length offshore transactions face transfer-pricing adjustments and additional penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada imposes no foreign exchange control statutes, no central bank approval for cross-border capital movements, no inconvertibility restrictions, and no registration requirements for inbound equity or debt contributions. Profit remittance is governed solely by the Income Tax Act: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (generally 25% statutory, reduced by applicable tax treaty, commonly 5% for treaty-qualified corporate dividend recipients holding significant ownership, 15% in other cases), s.215 withholding, remittance and reporting obligations, and the s.212 restrictions on management fees, rents, royalties, and interest. FATCA/CRS information reporting and FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting apply to the bank as an intermediary, not as exchange controls. In practice, remittance friction arises principally from sanctions screening (OFAC, SEMA, UN Act) and enhanced bank due diligence rather than from currency convertibility hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not require exporters to surrender foreign-currency proceeds, impose mandatory conversion ratios, or mandate the repatriation of export receipts within any statutory timeline. The former federal exchange-control regime was dismantled in the early 1950s, and no successor surrender obligation exists. Export proceeds may be held indefinitely in foreign currency or freely converted into CAD at market rates. Applicable boundaries are statutory rather than exchange-control based: FINTRAC reporting of electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, PCMLTFA client identification and record-keeping, sanctions screening under the United Nations Act and Special Economic Measures Act, transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany sales under s.247 of the Income Tax Act, and Part XIII withholding on any related-party cross-border dividends, royalties, or interest actually remitted.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act, responsible for monetary policy, financial system stability, currency design and issuance, and registration and oversight of retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administration agency; exchange-related regulatory functions are distributed across FINTRAC (anti-money laundering), OSFI (prudential bank supervision), and the Department of Finance international sanctions division (Special Economic Measures Act, United Nations Act).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as the banker of record, acceptable only as a supplementary collections and disbursement rail. An offshore digital EMI may legally onboard a Canadian federal corporation and supply multi-currency virtual IBANs, but it is not a Schedule I or Schedule II bank under the Bank Act and cannot take deposits, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide CDIC-insured accounts. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, in force as of November 16, 2024, payment service providers offering services to Canadian customers must register with the Bank of Canada; offshore EMIs without such registration, and without a licensed entity in Canada, pose legal viability concerns for statutory tax payments, escrow, and high-value treasury clearing. A TIER-1 Canadian chartered bank remains mandatory for the core treasury ledger, payroll, tax remittance, and documentary trade instruments; the EMI rail is limited to low-value, high-frequency SaaS subscription collections and developer payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 internationally to the Canadian beneficiary's account; Canadian payment rails do not use IBAN, the beneficiary must be identified by bank BIC, the 3-digit institution number, the 5-digit transit/branch number, and the 7-to-12-digit account number, with the legal name of the CBCA federal corporation in field 50K/59 exactly as shown on the corporate profile. Field 70 must state the purpose narrative, e.g., 'Share subscription payment for common shares per board resolution dated YYYY-MM-DD' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement per loan agreement dated YYYY-MM-DD'. No central bank approval or exchange registration is required in Canada, but all transfers of CAD 10,000 or more obligate the receiving financial institution to file an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC; wires from high-risk jurisdictions will require source-of-funds corroboration. If the injection is structured as debt, the loan agreement, interest rate, and economic substance documentation must be retained for transfer-pricing and tax-filings purposes.Outbound disbursements use the SWIFT MT103 for operating payments, licensing fees, royalties, cloud-hosted services, and dividend remittances; field 70 must identify the underlying contract and, for interest, royalty, or dividend payments, the Part XIII withholding basis. For documentary trade settlements, the Canadian chartered bank issues a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) with documents against payment or acceptance terms, and USD/CAD settlement occurs through correspondent clearing. High-value Canadian-dollar disbursements may clear in real-time finality through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) operated by Payments Canada; SWIFT membership of the issuing bank ensures global correspondent reach. Outgoing wires of CAD 10,000 or more are reported by the bank to FINTRAC; where a payment is made to a non-resident payee in Canada, the remitter must withhold the required Part XIII tax under s.215 of the Income Tax Act and remit it to the CRA on the prescribed date.
