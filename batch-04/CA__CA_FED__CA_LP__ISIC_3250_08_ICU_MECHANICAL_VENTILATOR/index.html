Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Gateway: My Business Account via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership (LP) must first obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA by completing Form RC348 (Request for a Business Number for a non-resident) or applying through the CRA's digital onboarding service. After issuance of the BN, the partnership must open a separate GST/HST account using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Account) if registrable. Foreign legal documentation (partnership agreement, articles of limited partnership, foreign tax registration certificate) must be attached. For corporate-level income tax, the LP (if treated as a partnership) is fiscally transparent at the federal level; an electing trust or corporation may be required for certain cross-border structuring. The registration is electronic-only and generates a 9-digit BN plus a four-character program account code (e.g., RT for GST/HST).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and the Excise Tax Act (Canada), a limited partnership can elect to be treated as a disregarded entity for selected tax purposes by filing a partnership election (subsection 10? forms such as T301 or T353? In fact, there is no direct 'check-the-box' regime comparable to the US; however, the partnership itself may file a joint election under section 96 of the Income Tax Act to allocate income, and for GST/HST the partnership may elect to have a member be the registrant remitter (subsection 273(2) election) or use the 'election by non-resident registrants' to report on a simplified basis. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canadian LPs may make elections pursuant to the OECD MLI to defer to Canadian entity classification where a foreign jurisdiction imports the entity's tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual world-wide taxable revenues (if the non-resident makes taxable supplies in Canada) or a lower 'permanent establishment' threshold when the non-resident is deemed to carry on business in Canada; additionally, non-resident suppliers of selected property and services may trigger mandatory simplified registration if selling into Canada via a digital platform.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods (including critical care mechanical ventilators) physically exported from Canada by the exporter are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule IX to the Excise Tax Act, provided the supplier (a) has possession of evidence that the goods were exported (e.g., customs export declaration, bill of lading, transport waybill), (b) removes the goods from Canada within the prescribed period (generally 60 days), and (c) is a GST/HST registrant. For services supplied to a non-resident, zero-rating applies to 'contract in respect of movable property' or 'services supplied outside Canada' if the service is delivered to the non-resident outside Canada and is not for use in a Canadian permanent establishment. The standard zero-rating is available both to domestic exporters and to non-resident registrants who export goods from Canada in the course of a commercial activity.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A/B13-Focus)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank/processor confirmation of consideration receipt in convertible currency)

Canada does not operate a continuous transaction control (CTC) real-time fiscal clearance model. There is no API-based tax authority clearance or digital stamping for invoices. Paper and electronic invoices are both accepted provided they contain the registrant's BN, transaction date, vendor and buyer identification, description, consideration, and tax amount. Mandatory e-invoicing is being phased in for suppliers to the federal Government using the Pan-Canadian Peppol network (effective fiscal year 2026-2027 for large suppliers). GST/HST returns and information are filed electronically through CRA 'My Business Account' (or EDI for high-volume filers). Vendors are required to retain transactional data for six years.

CRA may review export support documentation within a six-year reassessment window. If a zero-rating or refund claim is fraudulent or lacks documentary backing, the supplier is liable to reassessment for uncollected GST/HST (5% federal component) plus provincial HST as applicable, interest, and a penalty of at least 50% of the tax sought to be evaded (Excise Tax Act, section 285), with gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the understated tax, civil fines, and criminal prosecution potential. Furthermore, a supplier that knowingly makes a false claim may be barred from refunds and be subject to cancellation of its GST/HST account.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible currency under IMF Article VIII. Both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible. No prior approval or exchange control permit is required for dividend remittances, profit repatriation, royalties, or capital injections by non-residents. The only substantive hurdles are commercially driven: banking compliance, documentation of the underlying transaction, and OSFI-regulated prudential review for high-risk counterparties. For a Limited Partnership (CA_LP), non-resident partners may remit distributable income without exchange control consent, provided the partnership maintains proper audited financials and files T5013 schedules with the Canada Revenue Agency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in any eligible bank account or converted to CAD at the exporter's discretion. No surrender requirement exists under Bank of Canada directives or OSFI rules. For invoicing, Canadian medical device exporters must comply with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) reporting thresholds (typically CAD 2,000 per shipment) and use the Canada Customs Invoice or commercial invoice, but no FX conversion obligation arises.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no designated foreign exchange control administration; exchange control policies are administered under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act, and AML/ATF supervision is under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the core banking layer and Medium only as an auxiliary payment channel. Canadian regulation does not recognise the European-level 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) concept. Offshore EMIs must either partner with a Schedule I chartered bank to use the LVTS clearing system or obtain a federal payment service provider licence under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. An RPAA registration covers payment services only and does not permit deposit-taking or credit granting. For capital escrow and government LC settlement, a regulated chartered bank is mandatory; fintech EMIs cannot serve as custodians under medical device procurement contracts that require Canadian bank guarantees or letters of credit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners may transfer capital in CAD or foreign currency to the LP's Canadian capital escrow account through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). The sending bank must issue an SWIFT MT103 with clear remittance information: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO CA_LP' and the partnership's registered account number. Canadian receiving banks under OSFI guidelines require the beneficial owner of the inbound amount to be the same as the LP's ultimate beneficial owner, or the reason must be documented as 'LOAN' or 'SERVICES' to prevent money-laundering layering. For payments above CAD 10,000, the LP must file a Large Cash Transaction Report with FINTRAC, and for wires above CAD 100,000, standard internal AML threshold monitoring applies. No exchange-control permission is needed; however, the bank will require an executed capital contribution deed or limited partnership agreement referencing the exact amount.Primary clearing rail for international supplier settlement is SWIFT MT103 through the LVTS to the beneficiary's offshore correspondent bank. For government procurement of ICU ventilators, payment is typically via a commercial letter of credit (LC) issued as SWIFT MT700, irrevocable and confirmed, payable at sight. Payments to overseas component manufacturers in non-CAD currencies are settled on the FX spot market via the Canadian bank's own dealing desk within a T+2 standard convention. For inbound government purchase orders from provincial health ministries or Crown corporations, the LP can also accept domestic electronic funds transfer (EFT) through Payments Canada - ACSS and RTGS.
