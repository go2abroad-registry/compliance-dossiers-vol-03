Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - federal administrator of Part I corporate income tax and Part IX GST/HST; primary portals: CRA 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; provincial tax authorities (Revenu Quebec, Ontario Ministry of Finance, etc.) administer separate PST/QST/RST regimes where applicable.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Form RC1/RC1A filed with the Sudbury Tax Centre (International and Large Business Directorate) or through BRO online; a GST/HST program account (BN suffixed with 'RT0001') is mandatory within 30 days after ceasing to be a small supplier (ETA s. 240) or before making a taxable supply where the small supplier threshold is exceeded; non-residents supplying digital services to Canadian consumers may register under the Simplified GST/HST regime (ETA ss. 242.1-242.4); federally incorporated entities receive their BN automatically from Corporations Canada, after which GST/HST and payroll accounts are activated via BRO.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - Canadian-resident federal business corporation taxed at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act (general federal rate 15% plus provincial rates 11.5%-16%); no pass-through eligibility; a foreign-owned subsidiary with permanent establishment in Canada is taxed on Canadian-source business income with an additional Part XIV branch tax of 25% for non-resident branches (treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% under Canada-US Article V/VI allocations); GST/HST territorial scope is determined by place-of-supply rules (ETA s. 142). — Foreign entities obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Form RC1/RC1A filed with the Sudbury Tax Centre (International and Large Business Directorate) or through BRO online; a GST/HST program account (BN suffixed with 'RT0001') is mandatory within 30 days after ceasing to be a small supplier (ETA s. 240) or before making a taxable supply where the small supplier threshold is exceeded; non-residents supplying digital services to Canadian consumers may register under the Simplified GST/HST regime (ETA ss. 242.1-242.4); federally incorporated entities receive their BN automatically from Corporations Canada, after which GST/HST and payroll accounts are activated via BRO.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - Canadian-resident federal business corporation taxed at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act (general federal rate 15% plus provincial rates 11.5%-16%); no pass-through eligibility; a foreign-owned subsidiary with permanent establishment in Canada is taxed on Canadian-source business income with an additional Part XIV branch tax of 25% for non-resident branches (treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% under Canada-US Article V/VI allocations); GST/HST territorial scope is determined by place-of-supply rules (ETA s. 142).

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada; entity classification follows domestic law (ITA s. 248, ETA s. 123) and treaty 'liable to tax' analysis. Available elections: (1) ETA s. 156 nil-consideration election between closely related corporations; (2) ETA s. 227 Quick Method (turnover up to CAD 400,000; unavailable to listed financial institutions); (3) ETA s. 238 annual filing and deemed-return elections for qualifying non-resident registrants; (4) ETA s. 169(5) annual input tax credit reconciliation election; hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.7-18.9) are mandatory, non-elective, and apply to structured arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small supplier turnover threshold (ETA s. 148): taxable supplies below CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter and over the preceding four calendar quarters; non-residents supplying digital services or e-commerce products to Canadian consumers must register once gross specified supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period (ETA s. 242.1).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the supplier (ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 1) or delivered to a common carrier for export (s. 2): 0% GST/HST where the supplier ships goods outside Canada and retains evidence of export. Services supplied to non-residents (s. 7) and intangible personal property / software licenses supplied to non-residents (s. 10): zero-rated for LegalTech SaaS and e-signature document automation, except where the service relates to Canadian real estate or goods situated in Canada, or the recipient is a GST/HST registrant acquiring the supply for a Canadian commercial activity; CRA guidance per GST/HST Memorandum 4-5-2 requires contemporaneous residency and destination evidence.

  • Commercial invoice / service contract with non-resident recipient declaration and place-of-supply reference
  • Customs export declaration or exporter's accounting evidence compliant with CBSA export reporting (A48/CUSCAT or carrier's cargo control manifest)
  • Bill of lading / airway bill / truck waybill evidencing physical movement outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank SWIFT MT103, or payment gateway settlement proof showing consideration received from outside Canada

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or mandatory API reporting framework; e-invoicing is permissive under CRA electronic records policy (ETA s. 286; six-year retention in legible, auditable format); Canada participates in a voluntary Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing interoperability initiative, not yet a statutory mandate; statutory minimum invoice content per ETA ss. 169-169.1 (supplier name and BN, recipient identification, date, description, tax rate and amount).

CRA may reassess within 4 years (extended to 7 years for misrepresentation and unlimited for fraud, ITA s. 152(4)); failure to substantiate export status on a document-by-document basis triggers full clawback of the zero-rating with GST/HST assessed at 5%-15% of gross consideration plus compounded daily interest at the CRA prescribed rate; civil penalty of 25% of net tax deficiency (minimum CAD 500) under ETA s. 327 plus late-remittance penalties of 1%-1.25% per month; criminal fraud under ETA s. 327.1: fines up to CAD 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; no statutory 180-day FX repatriation clock exists federally, but CRA examines delayed or non-repatriation as an indicator of Canadian-consumed supply and applies commercial reasonableness analysis.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating currency regime under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Both current-account (royalties, subscription fees, dividend remittances) and capital-account (intercompany loans, equity injections) flows are fully convertible without advance approval or FDI-related conversion restrictions. Profit repatriation is unrestricted for Canadian Business Corporation shareholders; dividends and capital returns require no exchange-control permit. The sole preconditions are (i) due-diligence compliance by the remitting financial institution, (ii) FINTRAC reporting obligations for cross-border EFT of CAD 10,000 or above, and (iii) the Investment Canada Act notification/review thresholds for larger controlling acquisitions, which do not apply to the ordinary treasury flows of this LegalTech SaaS entity. No surrender or conversion-ratio requirement applies to any class of transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law (Bank of Canada Act, Currency Act, and PCMLTFA) does not impose any mandatory surrender, retention account, or conversion ratio on export receipts or cross-border trade proceeds. A Federal Business Corporation is fully entitled to hold, convert, or disburse USD/CAD proceeds via any Canadian chartered bank. Obligations are limited to transactional record-keeping (books and records under the Income Tax Act sections 230(1) and the Foreign Reporting rules may not require export-specific surrender), FINTRAC reporting of EFTs equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, and customs/currency declarations only where physical cash crosses a border.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate reportable non-arm's length transaction value with a single non-resident triggers mandatory Form T106 filing; contemporaneous taxation documentation under ITA section 247 must be prepared before the T2 filing deadline to avoid transfer-pricing penalties. No separate statutory Master File/Local File threshold exists, but CRA expects OECD-aligned documentation for material cross-border related-party flows.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations of the type used in zero-tax offshore jurisdictions; however, economic substance is an operative doctrine through the GAAR under ITA section 245, sham doctrine, beneficial-ownership analysis for treaty claims, and OECD BEPS anti-avoidance rules. A LegalTech SaaS holding company must have real decision-making people, office premises, payroll, and operational substance in Canada; otherwise treaty relief and deductions may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under ITA section 163.2; criminal conviction under ITA section 239 carries a fine of not less than 50% and up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to five years. CRA will also assess unpaid tax, interest, and any related penalties and may require audited financial statements for contemporaneous correction.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participated in or authorized evasion may be personally assessed for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under ITA section 227.1 and ETA section 323; courts disregard the corporate veil in fraud cases; controlling individuals risk criminal imprisonment and, if foreign nationals, inadmissibility to Canada that operates as a travel ban.
Shell Company / Sham Conduit / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the GAAR under ITA section 245 to deny the tax benefit and impose a 15% GAAR penalty; common-law sham findings allow the arrangement to be recharacterized; transfer-pricing adjustments under ITA section 247 may be applied with a 10% transaction penalty; Form T106 late/false filing penalties compound the assessment.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly designed the shell can be jointly and severally liable for the resulting Part XIII tax and interest if corporate assets are stripped; director civil liability may arise under the oppression remedy and fraudulent conveyance provisions of the CBCA; criminal liability for tax evasion is a personal continuing liability even if the shell has no assets.
AML / FinTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act imposes registration obligations and administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations; Criminal Code section 462.31 makes money laundering an indictable offence carrying imprisonment up to ten years, with asset seizure and forfeiture orders; false reporting to CRA can add tax evasion penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed, authorized, or acquiesced to AML violations are personally exposed to prosecution and penal consequences; conviction on an indictable money-laundering offence is an inadmissibility ground under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act, preventing re-entry to Canada; corporate directors may also be liable for unremitted source deductions and GST where the shell has been stripped of funds.
