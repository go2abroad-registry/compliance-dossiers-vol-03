Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST Registration and Business Number (BN) administration; primary portal: CRA My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) and, if applicable, a GST/HST registration account. The non-resident corporation files Form RC1 (Request for Business Number/GST/HST Registration) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by mail, accompanied by certified corporate constitutive documents, a Canadian registered office address, and identification of an authorized representative. GST/HST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is recommended for exporters seeking zero-rating and input tax credit recovery.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election for federal corporate tax. Relevant elections include: Income Tax Act section 216 election for non-resident owners of Canadian rental real property, section 219 election regarding branch tax for non-resident corporations, and various GST/HST elections for closely related corporations and joint venture operators. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies to goods exported from Canada where the registrant supplier ships the goods directly by common carrier, freight forwarder, or through a consignee for delivery outside Canada, or where the goods are delivered to a customs officer for export. Offshore transportation and services directly related to exported goods, including deep-sea Ro-Ro vehicle shipping to non-resident recipients, may qualify if the recipient is non-resident and not registered under the GST/HST. Substantiation must demonstrate physical export, transfer of title, and settlement of foreign-currency proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada currently imposes no federal real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no mandatory digital stamping, and no compulsory API transaction clearance for GST/HST. E-invoicing is legally permissible provided records are kept in a prescribed format consistent with Excise Tax Act section 286. Voluntarily adopted interoperable electronic invoicing may use Peppol standards; CRA may request electronic records in audit and taxpayers must retain comprehensive transactional metadata.

CRA audits export zero-rating with heightened scrutiny where documentation is incomplete or inconsistent. A failed zero-rating entitlement results in reassessment of GST/HST at the full applicable rate, statutory interest, and administrative penalties. Knowingly false or grossly negligent statements attract penalties under Excise Tax Act section 285.1, including a minimum penalty of CAD 100 and up to 50% of the tax or credit at issue. Fraudulent export schemes may expose directors, officers, and agents to criminal charges under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code, with retrospective reassessment periods extended beyond four years where misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange rate regime for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada imposes no exchange-control restriction on conversion of CAD, acquisition or retention of foreign currency, offshore banking balances, or cross-border credit operations. The only material profit-remittance hurdle is tax-sided, not FX-sided: dividends paid by the Canadian RoRo subsidiary to its US parent attract Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25%, reduced to 5% under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention where the beneficial owner is a US company holding at least 10% of the voting stock, with 15% applicable to portfolio shareholders; arm's-length interest on intercompany debt is generally exempt from Canadian withholding subject to treaty and domestic conditions. No Bank of Canada or OSFI approval is required for dividend repatriation, equity injection, loan repayment, or head-office remittances, although the payer must complete NR4 information returns and remit Part XIII withholdings to the Canada Revenue Agency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation requirement for export proceeds. RoRo freight revenue earned by the Canadian entity may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or any freely usable currency, in Canadian, US, or other offshore accounts, at the treasury's discretion. The only binding informational obligation is that financial intermediaries must file FINTRAC International Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for cross-border wire transfers of C$10,000 or more; the corporate obligor must retain underlying bills of lading, charter-party invoices, load/discharge certificates, and bank statements to evidence the commercial substance of each freight transaction for audit, tax, and CBSA purposes. No state-mandated conversion ratio exists for export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; foreign exchange policy competence is statutorily vested in the Department of Finance under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Prudential regulation of Canadian Schedule I and foreign-bank-branch deposit-taking institutions is discharged by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while anti-money-laundering supervision and cross-border transaction reporting are administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control administration, exchange-control license, or central bank approval gate for cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Cross-border fintechs and foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer may operate in Canada only as money-services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and, where providing payment services, as registered payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (in force since 1 November 2024 with a 12-month transition for legacy providers). Such entities are not chartered under the Bank Act, cannot take Canadian deposits or extend credit in the Canadian sense, and do not possess direct settlement membership in Payments Canada Lynx or ACSS. Their treasury utility for an ocean RoRo carrier is limited to ancillary corridors, such as collection of small-value freight payments from dispersed shippers, pre-funding disbursement wallets for Asian port agencies, and acceleration of low-value supplier payments. The primary account spine, USD freight concentration, sanctions screening, documentary LC capability, and high-value clearing must remain with a Schedule I bank; any fintech channel must be restricted by a board-approved counterparty policy and must be reconcilable to the lead bank's SWIFT infrastructure.
