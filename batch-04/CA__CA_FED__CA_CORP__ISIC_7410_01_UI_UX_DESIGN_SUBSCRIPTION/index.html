Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and corporate income tax administrative portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); Business Registration Online; Corporations Canada for federal charter registration.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity first obtains a federal Business Corporation charter via Corporations Canada (online incorporation; no minimum capital). After incorporation, the entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering with the CRA through Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1. During registration, the taxpayer may open a Corporate Income Tax (T2) account and, where taxable supplies exceed the threshold, a GST/HST account. A non-resident corporation with Canadian nexus must use Form RC1416 or applicable CRA registration procedures; the 9-character BN is used for all CRA tax accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not generally permit a check-the-box or pass-through election for federally incorporated business corporations. Canadian resident corporations are subject to entity-level taxation under the Income Tax Act. Available practical elections include: (i) GST/HST 'closely related corporation' elections under ETA s.156/157, allowing non-taxable supplies between qualifying related corporations; (ii) GST/HST annual or quarterly filing elections; (iii) ITA allocation rules for provincial margin after federal base exercise; and (iv) hybrid mismatch rules do not create an entity classification election but may restrict deductions under ITA s.18.2/18.3 for arrangements exploiting dual characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (an entity that makes no quarterly threshold excess is a 'small supplier' and is not required to register). Non-residents supplying taxable services in Canada may also have a registration obligation if they are engaged in a commercial activity and have permanent establishment or economic nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Where a Canadian-resident business corporation provides UI/UX design subscriptions to a recipient outside Canada, the service is zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act if: (i) the recipient is a non-resident, (ii) the recipient is not registered for GST/HST, (iii) the recipient is not in Canada at the time the service is rendered, and (iv) the service does not relate to real property in Canada, tangible personal property in Canada, or a service performed in Canada for a person physically in Canada. The supplier must retain documentary evidence that the service was exported, including the contract, invoices indicating the foreign jurisdiction, and proof of payment from overseas.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CRA Form B13A for goods, or automated export reporting; for services, a service-export declaration is replaced by transaction records)
  • Bill of Lading (for goods, not typically applicable to digital design services; for services, deliverable records and server/time-outside-Canada logs are used)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank/FX record showing foreign-source payment received from the non-resident client)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is required in Canada. CRA does not impose mandatory real-time API clearance. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants whose total annual revenue exceeds CAD 1.5 million, and all GST/HST returns must be filed electronically if certain thresholds apply. E-invoice formats, including Peppol BIS and PINT implementation, are commercially adopted in Canada but not statutorily mandated at the federal level.

If CRA determines that a zero-rating claim was invalid or that required export documentation (foreign contract, remote service delivery proof, foreign exchange remittance) is not maintained within the normal retention period or within the 180-day evidentiary support window, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST (or applicable HST rate). CRA may assess unremitted tax, apply interest, and impose penalties: under Excise Tax Act s.285 for gross negligence, 25% of the tax that would have been assessed; under s.280.1 for false statements or misrepresentations, the greater of CAD 250 and 10% of the understated net tax; in severe cases, criminal prosecution is possible. Input tax credits previously claimed may be clawed back and assessed if the export zero-rating is reversed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility in respect of both residents and non-residents; the Canadian dollar floats freely and CAD/USD exchange is unrestricted. Profit remittances (dividends, interest, royalties, management fees) require no regulatory approval or FX license. Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes a 25% gross withholding tax on passive payments to non-residents, generally reduced to 15% under the Canada-US tax treaty and to 0% on arm's-length treaty-protected interest in qualifying circumstances. Inbound equity capital is not subject to exchange control; only the Investment Canada Act national-security or WTO-investor review above the prescribed significance threshold can gate an acquisition. GST/HST on exported design services supplied to non-residents is zero-rated under Section 7 of Part V, Schedule IX of the Excise Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation obligation, and no export-receipts escrow for design services or other exports. Exporters may retain, convert, or hedge foreign-currency proceeds freely. The only compliance obligations are FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer reports for single or linked operations at or above CAD 10,000, anti-money-laundering travel-rule data, and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially supervises Schedule I banks; FINTRAC administers the AML/CFT regime under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; currency and convertibility obligations arise from the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, IMF Article VIII, and the OECD Codes of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and Current Invisible Operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility only as a secondary payment corridor, not as the primary treasury bank. Wise (Wise Payments Ltd.) is registered with FINTRAC as a money services business under the PCMLTFA, holds customer funds in segregated accounts with regulated deposit-takers, but is not a Schedule I bank and is not CDIC-insured. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No monetary threshold for ordinary contemporaneous documentation; CRA requires Local File/Master File to support all related-party transactions. Mandatory Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) applies when consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750 million; penalty relief under s.247(3) is conditional on contemporaneous completion of documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies GAAR (s.245) and sham/substance-over-form doctrines; a passive design-subscription shell with no employees, premises, or real business can be rebutted as a sham and lose deduction/treaty benefits; CRA also applies an OECD permanent establishment analysis to foreign affiliates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.238/s.239: conviction liable to a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded amount and/or up to 5 years imprisonment; civil penalties up to 50% under s.163(2), plus interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: CRA can assess directors personally for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST (s.227.1, s.323); directors who authorize false returns face criminal liability, travel bans, and restitution orders.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: s.247(3) penalty: 10% of the understated transfer price up to a maximum of CAD 5 million (or 10% of gross proceeds); CRA adjusts taxable income and assessable withholding tax; no deduction allowed.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly sign inconsistent TP documents may be jointly liable for gross negligence penalties; CRA can seek civil fraud damages via court.
Shell Company / Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR could disallow treaty relief, tax on deemed profits, apply 50% gross negligence penalty, and impose withholding tax on deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors may be sued for breach of fiduciary duty if they used shell architecture to avoid tax; CRA can use asset-tracing and seek personal judgments.
AML/Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA penalties: up to CAD 500,000 for non-reporting, plus forced review and administrative monetary penalties; Criminal Code s.462: money laundering is independently prosecutable with 5-10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as persons for purposes of FINTRAC compliance; knowingly structuring subscription payments to avoid CAD 10,000 reporting triggers criminal liability and personal fines.
