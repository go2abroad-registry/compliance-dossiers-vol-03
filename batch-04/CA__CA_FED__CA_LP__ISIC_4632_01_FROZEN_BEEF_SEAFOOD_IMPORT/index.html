Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Federal Jurisdiction (CA-FED); principal portals: CRA (My Business Account) for BN/GST/HST administration, GST/HST NETFILE for return filing, and CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) plus eManifest for customs accounting.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service; the application identifies the LP as a partnership and names the general partner (or an ETA s. 287 Canadian representative where the LP has no establishment in Canada) as the responsible person; the GST/HST program account (RC0001) is attached to the BN, with registration effective from the application date and limited retroactive effective dates permitted under ETA s. 242; the BN15 inclusive of program identifier serves as the LP's federal tax identification number for GST/HST, payroll, customs and income tax obligations; a separate CBSA importer/exporter account (RM0001) is required under the Customs Act for cross-border clearance.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96(1): income, gains, losses and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners; the LP is not a taxpayer for Part I income tax; for GST/HST, the LP is a separate deemed person under ETA s. 272(2) and is the registrant for its own supplies; partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's net tax under ETA s. 272(2.2); non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source business allocations and on dispositions of taxable Canadian property. — A foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service; the application identifies the LP as a partnership and names the general partner (or an ETA s. 287 Canadian representative where the LP has no establishment in Canada) as the responsible person; the GST/HST program account (RC0001) is attached to the BN, with registration effective from the application date and limited retroactive effective dates permitted under ETA s. 242; the BN15 inclusive of program identifier serves as the LP's federal tax identification number for GST/HST, payroll, customs and income tax obligations; a separate CBSA importer/exporter account (RM0001) is required under the Customs Act for cross-border clearance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96(1): income, gains, losses and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners; the LP is not a taxpayer for Part I income tax; for GST/HST, the LP is a separate deemed person under ETA s. 272(2) and is the registrant for its own supplies; partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's net tax under ETA s. 272(2.2); non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source business allocations and on dispositions of taxable Canadian property.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election; classification follows statute. Available elections: (i) ITA s. 96(3) — partnership fiscal-period election; (ii) ITA s. 103(1) — joint election for a reasonable alternative allocation of partnership income/loss among partners, subject to CRA adjustment if unreasonable; (iii) ITA s. 216/217 — non-resident partner election to file on a net basis for Canadian-source rental or employment income; (iv) ETA s. 273(1) — joint election between a registrant supplier and a registrant recipient to treat goods delivered in Canada and subsequently exported as supplied outside Canada; (v) ETA s. 156 — election for closely related registrants (e.g., corporate general partner) to deem taxable supplies to be for nil consideration, preserving ITC neutrality; hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.3 and 18.4 admit no elective relief and apply to payments routed through hybrid instruments or hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 under ETA s. 148 (small-supplier threshold); a non-resident LP is a small supplier only if the value of its taxable supplies made in Canada in the preceding four calendar quarters does not exceed CAD 30,000; registration is mandatory once this threshold is exceeded, but an LP engaged exclusively in zero-rated export trading is entitled (and practically required) to register voluntarily to recover input tax credits on imported frozen beef and seafood inventory and domestic cold-chain operating costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may zero-rate: (i) frozen beef carcasses and pelagic fish exported by the LP as supplier (Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1); (ii) goods sold to a GST/HST registrant who will export them, provided the buyer's export can be substantiated (s. 5); (iii) services performed in respect of exported goods or performed wholly outside Canada, including international freight and transportation services (s. 7). Prerequisites: the LP must be a GST/HST registrant; the goods must be removed from Canada within a commercially reasonable period; the LP must obtain and preserve prescribed export evidence under ETA Regulations ss. 40-41 and CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17-4.1; CFIA export certification for beef and fish is required for market access and is examined alongside trade documents during audit.

  • Canada Customs Coding Form B3-3 or CBSA CARM/eManifest release document showing GST/HST and duties paid at importation
  • Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A) transmitted via the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) system where the export value exceeds CAD 2,000
  • Clean on-board Bill of Lading (ocean/air) issued to the non-resident buyer or its order
  • Commercial Invoice, packing list and certificate of origin (HTS classification, Incoterms, weights, shipping marks)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation evidencing export proceeds credited to the LP's Canadian bank account
  • CFIA health certificates and export inspection records for deep-frozen beef carcasses and pelagic fish

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal invoice clearance or digital-stamping regime; no mandatory e-invoicing API is imposed by CRA. Soft obligations: every registrant must satisfy ETA s. 223 invoice content standards (supplier name and BN, date, description, value, tax rate and tax amount) to support the recipient's ITC claim; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or EDI/API where annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5M; CBSA mandates electronic advance commercial information (eManifest/ACI) and, for importers, the CARM Single Window Initiative data set; export declarations are transmitted through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) system.

Canada imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day dossier convention is a documentary warranty, while CRA's record-keeping rule under ETA s. 286 requires retention of export evidence for six years. If zero-rating is denied, the LP must remit GST/HST on the full invoice value plus compound interest at prescribed rates and penalties under ETA ss. 280 (failure to remit), 285 (false statements or omissions) and 327 (evasion: fines up to double the tax avoided and imprisonment); CBSA AMPS penalties of CAD 500 to CAD 25,000 per infraction apply to customs declaration errors; CRA may reassess within four years under ETA s. 298(2), and without limitation where misrepresentation arises from neglect, carelessness or wilful default under ETA s. 298(4); associated ITCs on exported inventory and operating expenses are clawed back accordingly.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on profit repatriation, and no requirement for prior governmental approval for inward or outward foreign-exchange transfers. Remittances of dividends or distributions are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory rate, often reduced by tax treaty, e.g., to 15% or less under the Canada-US Treaty).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender, repatriate, or convert export proceeds to Canadian dollars. Foreign-currency receipts may be retained, held, or converted freely; there is no mandated timeline or conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank; foreign exchange control policy is administered by the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with significant constraints. Canadian-registered or FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (such as certain virtual EMIs) can provide cross-border payment and multi-currency accounts, but the Limited Partnership must verify that the EMI is compliant with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Offshore digital EMIs without a Canadian MSB registration are generally non-viable for core corporate treasury due to compliance and banking partner limitations. Onboarding may be remote, but documentation requirements mirror those of domestic banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inward capital injections to the Limited Partnership must be made via an international wire transfer using the SWIFT network (MT103) in CAD or USD. The routing should be structured as a 'Capital Contribution' to the LP, with precise beneficiary details, including or for further credit to the LP's account at the Canadian bank. The remittance should include Purpose Code 'CAP'-related annotations (e.g., 'Deposit - Equity Participation' in SWIFT field 70) and should be supported by the LP's capital call notice and the executed subscription documents. Where required, the Canadian bank will issue or confirm the LC through an advising bank in the exporter's jurisdiction. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate; the Limited Partnership itself pays no entity-level federal income tax under Canadian flow-through partnership rules, and each partner is taxed on its allocated share of net income at its own applicable statutory rate (up to 26.5-31% combined federal-provincial for corporate partners and up to 53.5% for high-income individual partners).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents; distributions from the Canadian LP to its non-resident partners are generally treated as business-income allocations subject to Part I tax, not Part XIII dividend withholding, unless the distribution is legally characterized as a dividend-like payment (for example, guaranteed passive income flowing through a specified investment flow-through partnership or a disguised preferred-return arrangement).
