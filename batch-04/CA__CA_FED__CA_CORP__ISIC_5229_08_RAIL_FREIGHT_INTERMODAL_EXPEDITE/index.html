Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Number registration portal and GST/HST Registry, accessible via My Business Account and the CRA website (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation must first determine whether it carries on business in Canada. If it does, it must apply for a Business Number (BN) using RC1FORM, elect for a corporation account (RC104), and open a GST/HST account if taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold. Non-resident suppliers of taxable digital or remote services must also register for GST/HST using the non-resident simplified procedure. The application can be made electronically, by mail, or through an authorized representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident corporation must first determine whether it carries on business in Canada. If it does, it must apply for a Business Number (BN) using RC1FORM, elect for a corporation account (RC104), and open a GST/HST account if taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold. Non-resident suppliers of taxable digital or remote services must also register for GST/HST using the non-resident simplified procedure. The application can be made electronically, by mail, or through an authorized representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada applies entity-level taxation without a U.S.-style check-the-box regime. Available elections include: Income Tax Act subsection 216(1) election for non-resident rental income; Excise Tax Act subsection 156(1) election for closely related corporations; Excise Tax Act subsection 167(1) election for nil consideration; and hybrid mismatch adjustments under Income Tax Act subsection 18.4(1). No general transparent-entity election exists for a federally incorporated business corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated export supplies) unless the supplier is a non-resident carrying on business in Canada or a non-resident supplier of digital services above the same threshold; registration is generally mandatory from the first supply exceeding CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or after four consecutive calendar quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada and transportation services that begin and/or end outside Canada, including rail freight and intermodal carriage services supplied to non-residents. The supplier must be registered for GST/HST, retain documentary evidence of export, and satisfy the conditions in the Excise Tax Act (sections 21, 22, 23 and Schedule VI, Part V). Services must be for the exclusive benefit of non-resident recipients or the transportation must constitute movement of goods from Canada to a destination outside Canada.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration / Automated Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or equivalent digital transmission via the Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading or rail waybill evidencing shipment from Canadian origin to a foreign destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or wire transfer/letter of credit settlement proof confirming payment for the exported goods or services

Canada does not currently impose a mandatory federal real-time e-invoicing clearance regime. There is no continuous transaction control API, digital stamping, or government clearance requirement for GST/HST. Registered businesses must issue prescribed invoices containing all CRA-required information, and electronic invoices are accepted if they remain legible and accessible in Canada. Quebec imposes separate invoice content rules, but for federal GST/HST no real-time fiscal clearance is mandated.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict CRA audit scrutiny. If documentary evidence is missing or insufficient, CRA may deny the zero-rating, reassess the GST/HST at 5%, disallow input tax credits, and impose interest on unpaid amounts. False claims may attract penalties under subsection 280.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the amount claimed, plus gross negligence penalties under the Income Tax Act if applicable. Directors and officers may also be liable. Goods exported without proper export reporting may be detained or subject to administrative monetary penalties under the Customs Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible across both current and capital accounts under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Canada imposes no exchange control legislation, no prior approval requirement on capital flows, and no restriction on dividend remittance, intercompany loan repayment, or equity repatriation. The only financial friction on profit remittances out of Canada is Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%–15% depending on the recipient jurisdiction and shareholding threshold). Inbound and outbound transactions exceeding CAD $10,000 in cash or negotiable instruments trigger FINTRAC large-transaction reporting by the financial intermediary, not by the corporate client; wire-based equity injections and trade settlements require no regulatory pre-authorisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada mandates no surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Freight-forwarding and intermodal logistics receivables invoiced in USD, EUR, or CNY may be held freely in offshore accounts, retained in foreign-currency Canadian bank accounts, or converted at market rates. There is no statutory deadline for repatriating export earnings, and no Central Bank approval is required for holding or investing such proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) serves as the monetary authority and fiscal agent for foreign exchange operations; formal foreign exchange administration rests with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52), while FINTRAC and the Canada Border Services Agency administer cross-border currency reporting. No separate foreign exchange control commission exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium as a legal matter. No EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) passport right exists in Canada. An offshore EMI may not take deposits or clear CAD in Canada without a sponsoring Schedule I bank or an authorized foreign bank branch. Non-bank payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and may only provide payment services, not deposit-taking or settlement account custody. For this freight-forwarding treasury, an EMI is viable solely as an FX conversion and cross-border disbursement conduit; the primary corporate operating and escrow accounts must sit at a Tier-1 Canadian bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous documentation threshold: CAD 1 million in gross revenue for the tax year (or CAD 250,000 for specific transaction categories: interest, royalties, management fees, and services) if the taxpayer has reportable transactions; master file is required when total revenue exceeds CAD 1 million and transactions with non-resident related parties exceed CAD 5 million, or when significant transactional activity is reportable.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the GAAR under s.245 and the transfer-pricing rules under s.247. For intermodal freight forwarding, economic substance is assessed by the CRA through functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed (FAR analysis). A Canadian-resident intermodal expediting corporation with actual rail booking, freight consolidation, customs logistics, and terminal operations personnel must exist; a shell entity routing revenues through a tax-haven billing centre will be recharacterized under s.247(7) and denied foreign subsidiary treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal)

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under s.239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax plus daily compound interest; the right to a future NOL carryback may also be denied.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid tax, interest, and penalties; personal assessment under s.160 for transfers to shareholders; travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and extradition treaties apply in egregious cases.
Shell Company / Sham (GAAR and Substance)

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment to disregard or recharacterize the transaction; a 100% gross negligence penalty if the avoidance transaction is misreported; denial of treaty benefits under the DTA's principal-purpose test; interest and penalties compound daily.

Director personal liability: Director and shareholder liability for the full amount of the recharacterized tax, penalties, and interest under the normal reassessment; a court may pierce the corporate veil where the company was a pure façade; personal travel restrictions if a warrant is issued for non-payment.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 2 million for a corporation; criminal laundering of proceeds of crime under the Criminal Code: imprisonment up to 10 years; forfeiture of assets; directors/officers individually liable for consenting or negligent supervision.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers may be held criminally liable under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for failure to implement adequate compliance controls; personal travel bans and asset freezes are applied under restraints in laundering investigations.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (uncapped, but reduced if the taxpayer makes a 10% correction before assessment); separate fines for each T106 late/missing form up to CAD 2,500; documents may be inadmissible in court if not contemporaneous.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false T106 declarations or fail to ensure the company has a proper transfer-pricing policy can be assessed personally for the penalty amounts if the company cannot pay and the director is a party to a deliberate act; CRA may issue a director's liability assessment on a source-withholding or similar basis.
Dividend Stripping / Withholding Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Withholding penalty of 10% of the unreported amount plus interest; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the unreported tax; the corporation is liable for the unpaid Part XIII tax even if the non-resident shareholder has disappeared; Part XIII tax can be grossed up and assessed directly.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid withheld amounts under s.227.1 of the Income Tax Act, including any Part XIII withholding tax that the corporation failed to remit; the personal liability covers tax, interest, and penalties; directors can be prohibited from leaving Canada for repeated non-remittance.
GST/HST and Customs Violations

Statutory sanction: Penalties equal to 10% of the unreported GST/HST plus interest; failure-to-register penalties of CAD 1,000 or 5% of net tax, whichever is greater; customs penalties for rail cars carrying undeclared freight under the Customs Act up to CAD 25,000 per incident; repeated contraventions trigger criminal fines.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for unremitted GST/HST (same standard as source deductions) if they were aware of collection and failed to remit; civil restraining orders may freeze rail trailers and freight rolling stock before a full assessment is completed.
