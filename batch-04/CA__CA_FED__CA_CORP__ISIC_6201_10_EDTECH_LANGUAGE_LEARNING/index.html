Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Ottawa, ON; primary portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO) co-administered with Corporations Canada (https://www.ic.gc.ca/eic/site/125.nsf/eng/home).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) via Corporations Canada to obtain a federal incorporation certificate and corporation number; non-resident-controlled corporations must identify a Canadian-resident director (CBCA s. 105(3)). 2) Complete single-window CRA registration (Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and certain program accounts' or online BRO) to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus program accounts: RC (corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll deductions), RM (import/export). 3) The EdTech corporation selling digital language-learning subscriptions must register for GST/HST immediately after worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; non-resident simplified registration streams A/B/C apply only to suppliers without a Canadian permanent establishment, which is not the default posture of a CBCA-resident subsidiary. 4) File the T2 Corporation Income Tax Return within 6 months after fiscal year-end via EFILE; remit instalments monthly/quarterly per CRA notice; file the EIFEL information return for interest deductibility limitation for taxation years beginning on or after 1 October 2023.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a CBCA federal corporation is a separate taxpayer and is resident in Canada when central management and control is exercised in Canada, ITA s. 250(4)). Federal Part I tax: base 38%, less 10% provincial abatement = 28%, less 13% General Rate Reduction = 15% general corporate rate; Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) pay 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (Small Business Deduction), with the business limit phased out between CAD 10M and CAD 15M of taxable capital employed in Canada. A subsidiary controlled by non-residents is a non-CCPC ineligible for the SBD. Investment income carries refundable Part I tax and a non-refundable Part IV tax of 38.33% (federal/provincial combined) refundable when eligible dividends are paid. Non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding at 25% (treaty-reduced, commonly 5%-15%) on dividends/royalties. Provincial corporate income tax (e.g., ON 11.5% general / 3.2% SME, AB 8%/2%, BC 12%/2%) is assessed separately by permanent establishment allocation.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; classification follows CBCA status and the ITA. Available statutory elections for the foreign-owned EdTech subsidiary: (1) ITA s. 216 — non-resident may elect to file a Part I return on Canadian rental/timber income instead of Part XIII withholding; (2) ITA s. 48(1) — deemed-disposition election on ceasing residence (limited relevance to a Canadian-resident CBCA subsidiary); (3) ETA s. 22 — single-supply election treating the bundled subscription (software licence + tutoring support) as one supply, enabling clean export zero-rating or domestic tax treatment; (4) ETA s. 279 — closely-related corporations election for nil-consideration taxable supplies between a parent and a 90%-owned subsidiary; (5) ETA s. 238 — election of GST/HST reporting frequency (monthly/quarterly/annual); (6) ETA s. 156/157 — simplified net-tax remittance for small suppliers (inapplicable above the CAD 30,000 threshold); (7) the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL, ITA s. 18.2/18.21) and hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4) apply mandatorily without elections; no elective carve-out exists for foreign-parented EdTech groups except the statutory exclusion for regulated financial institutions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (ETA s. 148(1) small-supplier threshold): mandatory GST/HST registration is triggered when worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters; registration is due within 29 days and the supplier must collect GST/HST on the first supply made after ceasing to be a small supplier. Non-resident digital suppliers cross the same CAD 30,000 threshold on supplies to Canadian-resident consumers; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI Part V for supplies to non-residents: (i) a supply of digital software subscriptions and electronic tutoring services made to a non-resident recipient whose ordinary residence is outside Canada is zero-rated under s. 7(1) unless the service is an excluded supply (rendered to a GST/HST-registered non-resident for consumption in Canada, performed in respect of Canadian real/tangible property, or acquired for an adventure in the nature of trade carried on in Canada); (ii) for sales via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the offshore platform acting as merchant of record is deemed under s. 208.1 ETA to make the supply to the Canadian consumer, while the developer's supply to the platform is outside Canada and not subject to GST/HST; (iii) direct B2B sales to Canadian corporate customers are taxable at 5% GST or HST per the province of consumption. Digital export substantiation requires W-8BEN-E/residency certificates, merchant-of-record agreements, IP/geolocation download logs, billing-country evidence, and confirmation of no Canadian consumption; pure digital downloads do not require a customs export declaration, whereas co-packaged tangible goods require a B13A export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System) — mandatory for tangible goods leaving Canada (e.g., USB or hardware-kit packages accompanying the software licence); not applicable to pure digital downloads, where platform delivery logs and download geolocation records serve as export evidence.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — required for physical export shipments; for digital EdTech exports, substitute Apple/Google payout statements confirming merchant-of-record status, CDN delivery records, software subscription agreements, and IP-address evidence of foreign download destinations.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank SWIFT MT103 or FINTRAC-reportable foreign exchange records evidencing collection of export consideration in the Canadian bank account within 180 days; CRA links the zero-rated supply to the FX remittance chain to distinguish a true export from a domestic supply.

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory government API validation, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. Registrants must issue invoices containing the supplier's BN, invoice date, description, and GST/HST amount or a clear zero-rating/exemption indicator; paper and electronic invoices are legally equivalent, and supporting records must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286; ITA s. 230). The Pan-Canadian B2G e-Invoicing Standard (Peppol BIS 3.0 with Canadian extension) is mandatory only for invoices to federal procurement; CRA operates OECD Model Rules digital-platform reporting (effective 1 January 2023) requiring platform operators to report seller income. Voluntary Peppol/EDI networks are permitted without CRA pre-clearance.

CRA reassessment windows: 4 years from the GST/HST return due date (ETA s. 298) and 4 years from mailing of the T2 notice of assessment (ITA s. 152(4)); extended to 7 years for neglect/misrepresentation and to 10 years for non-arm's-length non-resident transactions. An improperly zero-rated export is clawed back at the applicable 5%/13%/15% rate with compound daily arrears interest computed from the original remittance date. Statutory penalties: ETA s. 280 (failure to register or collect) — 5%-10% of the unremitted net tax; ETA s. 285 (false statement or omission) — greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the assessed amount; ETA s. 327 (tax evasion) — fines up to CAD 20,000 on summary conviction or unlimited on indictment, plus imprisonment of 2-5 years; ITA s. 163(2) — gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated income/tax. CRA GST/HST Memoranda 11.2 and 12.1 require documented 'reasonable export' indicia; failure to maintain the substantiation bundle is treated as fraudulent zero-rating, exposing the exporter to retrospective refund/ITC clawback and criminal referral.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized and freely convertible currency system with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar trades freely; profit remittances such as dividends, royalties, and interest may be repatriated without FX approval, subject to applicable withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. No Canadian statutory rules restrict timing or amounts of dividend payment to non-resident shareholders, and no FX licensing is required for ordinary cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained in the original foreign currency, deposited into domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts, or converted at market rates without surrender obligations. Reporting to Statistics Canada under the Balance of Payments/International Transaction Reporting System (BOP/ITRS) is required for statistical purposes only.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low—as a primary treasury banking channel for a Canadian operating corporation, offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) have minimal viability because they generally do not hold Canadian banking licenses, cannot provide local deposit-taking or LVTS clearing, and may violate the Bank Act's deposit-taking prohibitions unless depositly registered with OSFI or authorized exemptly. They may supplement payment operations only if they are FINTRAC-registered money services businesses (MSBs) and maintain a partnership clearing arrangement with a Schedule I or Schedule II bank for CAD settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

General federal Part I corporate rate 15% (38% less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction); Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) rate 9% on the first C$500,000 of active business income eligible for the Small Business Deduction; combined federal-provincial marginal rate typically 22% to 31% depending on province and permanent-establishment allocation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident Canadian corporation to a non-resident individual, parent, or foreign shareholder, subject to reduction under an applicable income tax treaty (s. 212(2)).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due within 6 months after the fiscal period-end (e.g., December 31 year-end files by June 30); final balance of Part I tax due within 2 months after year-end (3 months for CCPCs claiming the Small Business Deduction); quarterly installments due on the last day of each quarter-end month; interest accrues from the statutory tax-payment due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 mandatory where total reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a single non-resident exceed C$1,000,000 in a taxation year; documented contemporaneous transfer-pricing (Local File) evidence required at or above that threshold; Master File and Local File are compulsory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of C$1,000,000,000 or more for taxation years beginning after 2023; TP penalty threshold triggers where the net TP adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose OECD-Stage-2 Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as applied in the UAE/Bermuda. Substance nevertheless governs: (i) Canadian corporate tax residency is determined by central management and control ('mind and management'); and (ii) GAAR under s. 245 ITA nullifies avoidance transactions lacking bona fide business purpose, and treaty-benefit provisions (Beneficial Ownership analysis) strip shell IRAs. An EdTech operator must therefore conduct real board meetings, payroll, product development (SR&ED), data-server decision-making, and commercial negotiations in Canada to preserve Canadian residency and treaty-reduced rates on outbound dividends and royalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal Offence)

Statutory sanction: s. 239(1) ITA: on summary conviction, fine of 50%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; on indictment, fine of 50%–200% and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; prosecution referral to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada (PPSC); CRA may also seize goods/real property under the Seizure and Restraint provisions and revoke business number registrations.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who directed, participated in, or acquiesced in the evasion are treated as principal offenders (s. 239(4) ITA); the corporate veil is pierced where the corporation acts as a mere facade, exposing personal civil restitution for the evaded tax plus interest; court orders may restrict travel pending prosecution or compel surrender of passports; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 5 years and personal forfeiture of proceeds of crime.
Gross Negligence and Knowingly False Statements

Statutory sanction: s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty: 50% of the understated tax for each false statement or omission; s. 162(5) failure to provide information: C$100 per false statement (minimum C$1,000); full reassessment of tax; daily interest at CRA prescribed rate compounding on the reassessed balance.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for the corporation's s. 163 penalty, but a director who signs or certifies false financial statements is exposed to personal liability under CBCA s. 122 (duty of care), CRA can assess a director as a preparer of the false return under joint and several liability rules where the director personally endorsed the filings, and indemnification insurance may be voided for knowing participation.
Transfer Pricing / Profit-Stripping Misallocation

Statutory sanction: s. 247(2) reassessment to the arm's-length price plus adjustment of correlative relief; s. 247(3) transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment where the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; additional interest from the balance-due date; denial of the deduction in full.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the mispricing and thereby conferred a benefit on a non-arm's-length shareholder are personally exposed to CRA assessments under s. 160 ITA where funds are transferred to the shareholder; breach of fiduciary duty (CBCA s. 122(1)(a)) may mandate personal restitution; if the pricing is a sham, criminal conspiracy under s. 239 also applies, and directors face travel restrictions pending prosecution.
Shell Company / GAAR Artificial Avoidance

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s. 245 (and provincial equivalents) denies all tax benefit derived from the avoidance transaction, reassesses the corporation, and imposes interest; the offending royalty deductions are disallowed and the low-tax intermediary is ignored; repeated GAAR findings can trigger the 15% GAAR penalty under the Québec equivalent and enhanced CRA audit classification.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly orchestrated the shell structure may be sued by shareholders for breach of fiduciary duty; CRA can apply the corporate-veil doctrine to recover tax from directors where corporate assets have been stripped into the offshore vehicle (civil restitution); directors resident in Canada who manage the offshore entity risk personal taxation on the foreign entity's income and potential criminal exposure if the arrangement is characterized as a sham.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) up to C$625,000 per violation for corporations and up to C$125,000 for individuals; criminal penalties for money laundering under s. 462.31 Criminal Code: up to C$2,000,000 fine and 10 years imprisonment; FINTRAC also has authority to suspend or revoke an MSB registration.

Director personal liability: FINTRAC may issue AMPs directly against named responsible persons or directors where the director directed, authorized, acquiesced in, or failed to prevent the violation; directors who knowingly participate in laundering are liable as parties to the offence under Criminal Code s. 21 and face personal forfeiture of proceeds, imprisonment up to 10 years, and travel restrictions as a condition of judicial interim release.
