Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); official operations portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE; federal corporate income tax and GST/HST are administered by the CRA, with provincial corporate income tax administered federally for all provinces except Quebec.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Canadian tax presence applies to the CRA for a Business Number (BN), which serves as the statutory TIN, and registers for the required program accounts, including the GST/HST account (RT program identifier) and corporate income tax account. The application is made on Form RC1 or RC1A, or through the CRA online registration service. A non-resident corporation must evidence carrying on business in Canada, provide board and constitutional documents, a Canadian address for service, and the anticipated date of first taxable activity; the CRA issues the BN and GST/HST registration upon nexus confirmation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a Canadian tax presence applies to the CRA for a Business Number (BN), which serves as the statutory TIN, and registers for the required program accounts, including the GST/HST account (RT program identifier) and corporate income tax account. The application is made on Form RC1 or RC1A, or through the CRA online registration service. A non-resident corporation must evidence carrying on business in Canada, provide board and constitutional documents, a Canadian address for service, and the anticipated date of first taxable activity; the CRA issues the BN and GST/HST registration upon nexus confirmation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Canadian federal tax law. A Canadian Business Corporation is taxed at entity level with no pass-through election. Available elections include: optional GST/HST registration for small suppliers under the Excise Tax Act, section 216 optional net-basis return for non-resident property income, and federal-provincial permanent-establishment allocation elections for corporate income tax. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.4 through 18.6 may restrict deductions on certain hybrid instruments and disregarded branches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

30,000 CAD. A person, including a non-resident carrying on business in Canada, is a small supplier if total taxable supplies in Canada in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four calendar quarters do not exceed CAD 30,000. Exceeding this threshold creates mandatory GST/HST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating is available for exports of goods where possession is transferred outside Canada by a registrant; for services supplied to non-residents and delivered or rendered outside Canada; and for certain intangible personal property exported or granted to non-residents. The supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence that the supply is exported and that the recipient is a non-resident.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill showing foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming foreign consideration settlement and FX repatriation
  • Commercial contract, invoice, and purchase order identifying the non-resident buyer
  • Carrier or freight forwarder export documentation
  • Bank statements and currency conversion records evidencing receipt in Canadian or foreign currency

Canada does not impose a federal real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate at the CRA level. Electronic invoices are accepted if they satisfy CRA record-keeping and audit requirements. Registrants above CRA volume thresholds must file GST/HST returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or an approved third-party service; no continuous fiscal API clearance is required.

CRA may deny zero-rating, reassess GST/HST with interest, and claw back input tax credits where export substantiation is incomplete or inconsistent. Failure to maintain adequate documentation can trigger penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including gross negligence penalties, and criminal prosecution for false statements or tax evasion where intended. The normal reassessment period may be extended beyond four years for misrepresentation, and retrospective clawback may apply to previously allowed zero-rated treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a liberalised current-account and capital-account framework. There are no exchange controls, surrender requirements, or prior central-bank licensing for capital inflows or outflows. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful without government approval, but is subject to Canadian Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty), CRS/FATCA reporting, and FINTRAC anti-money-laundering thresholds. Practical convertibility constraints arise only from sanctions screening, source-of-funds verification, and the commercial bank's internal compliance requirements; no material transfer-pricing or capital-blockage regulation applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into Canadian dollars. Exporters may invoice in any freely convertible currency and maintain foreign-currency operating or settlement accounts. Reporting obligations are limited to trade-statistics declarations with Statistics Canada/Canada Border Services Agency and to FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports for cross-border transactions of C$10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada has no separate federal foreign-exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. A foreign electronic money institution is not a Canadian chartered bank and cannot independently act as the primary treasury account or capital-escrow depository. To serve Canadian customers, an offshore EMI must ordinarily register with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB) and comply with applicable provincial money-transfer licensing. Such an entity may provide payment-orchestration rails (subscription billing, payout APIs) and may maintain a settlement account at a Canadian Schedule I bank, but the core corporate treasury account, capital injection account, and FX execution account should remain at the chartered bank. Foreign EMIs are acceptable for secondary collection routing, not for statutory treasury custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound capital via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the Canada domiciled Schedule I bank's correspondent network, crediting the corporate operating or escrow account. The remitter must populate SWIFT field 70 with a compliant remittance narrative specifying 'Equity capital contribution' or 'Shareholder subordinated loan' and reference the corporate account and board resolution number. Canada does not maintain a central-bank purpose-code table, but the receiving bank will map the transaction to its internal reason-for-payment classification and may require a signed certificate of source of funds for transactions equal to or exceeding C$10,000. Beneficiary and remitter details are reportable to FINTRAC as electronic funds transfer reports. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, subject to withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act; rate reduced to 15% for direct-qualifying treaty shareholders under Canada–US treaty, 5% if the shareholder holds at least 10% voting interest and meets treaty conditions; domestic inter-corporate dividend exemption applies for Canadian-resident corporations

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) administratively requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) for transactions with non-arm's-length parties if the aggregate value of transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 (gross) in a taxation year; for intercompany services intangibles, a lower threshold of CAD 100,000 may trigger full documentation under CRA's Transfer Pricing Manual; T106 information return must be filed for reportable transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents when the total amount exceeds CAD 100,000 (for payments or receipts) and all such transactions are subject to penalty, even if below the CAD 1,000,000 documentation threshold; CRA requires a complete 'contemporaneous' transfer pricing policy to be prepared before filing deadline; failure to prepare and provide documentation within 3 months of request attracts a CAD 1,000 penalty plus fines up to CAD 10,000, and the Tax Court may impose a 10% gross adjustment penalty under subsection 247(3)

Economic substance scope: Canada has no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in offshore jurisdictions (such as Bermuda, BVI or UAE); instead, under the Income Tax Act (section 247), transactions must be undertaken for 'bona fide purposes which do not circumvent' the Act; CRA uses general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under subsection 245(2) to recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance; the Canada-US Treaty LOB (Limitation on Benefits) provisions impose substantive-tests requiring Canadian 'functional economic substance' (active business or capital ownership) for treaty eligibility; for software ERP/CRM companies, CRA requires substantive Canadian presence: real office, full-time employees in Canada making management decisions (board meetings), development/research activities, operational staff, and invoicing substructure; without these activities, a Canadian 'policy-only' or 'agent-only' structure may be disregarded and profits re-attributed to the foreign principal under OECD transfer pricing guidelines (2017 TPG Chapter VI); the CRA applies the 'anti-conduit' rules for treaty shopping under treaties signed post-2005, including a general 365-day ownership holding condition for capital gains exemption on shares of Canadian corporations

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act, section 238, 239, and Excise Tax Act 327.1)

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion is punishable by prosecution by CRA on indictment: imprisonment up to 5 years, fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded (additional civil penalties of 50% of the evasion amount under subsection 163(2)); civil penalties for false statements may be up to the greater of CAD 100,000 and 50% of the tax that would have been payable; failure-to-file penalties as detailed above; CRA may also issue formal demand for full tax arrears plus interest compounded daily at prescribed rate

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (Part VIII or Part XIII) if they fail to exercise 'due diligence' under subsection 227.1(1); personal civil liability for the company's payroll withholding (income tax, CPP, EI), GST/HST, and employee source deductions; CRA may issue a director's liability letter and obtain civil judgment; willful evasion or gross negligence can lead to director travel restrictions (e.g., denial of departure authorization under Excise Tax Act), criminal prosecution, and imprisonment for up to 5 years; restitution orders compelling personal repayment; prohibition on acting as director pursuant to section 494 of Canada Business Corporations Act for up to 5 years
Transfer Pricing Penalty (ITA subsection 247(3))

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty: 10% of the net upward adjustment (grossed-up under subsection 247(3)) if the taxpayer does not make reasonable efforts to use arm's-length data, plus re-assessment of tax at full statutory rates (15% federal + provincial top rates) plus daily interest; CRA can impose a 100% penalty for deliberate overvaluation of software intangibles; the adjustments can be applied retroactively for 3 to 7 years (ordinary reassessment period for non-arm's-length: 3 years; 7 years for misrepresentation or negligence under subsection 152(4))

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the corporation's tax liability if the transfer pricing adjustment results in a withheld remittance under subsection 227.1 (category of source deductions); in extreme cases of transfer pricing fraud or evasion, directors can face criminal liability, personal restitution orders, travel restrictions (if CRA obtains a court order under section 240), and imprisonment; civil director's liability for failing to require CFO/controller to comply with TP documentation duty
Shell Company and Conduit Arrangement (GAAR, subsection 245(2), and Treaty Shopping)

Statutory sanction: GAAR applies to disallow the tax benefit, recharacterize the transaction, and reassess tax plus interest; treaty LOB clauses can deny the reduced withholding rates; CRA may impose the 10% transfer pricing penalty and treat the Canadian entity as a permanent establishment of the foreign principal (subjecting full foreign income to Canadian taxation); potential civil penalties of up to CAD 10,000 for false information in treaty-based disclosure (Form NR302); permanent establishment attribution could add 15% federal tax plus provincial tax on all Canadian-related profits

Director personal liability: Directors as corporate officers of a shell entity may be treated as the 'real controller' for GAAR penalty; if the shell company is not properly funded or dissolved, directors can be personally liable for unpaid tax under the 'look-through' principle of the CRA's taxpayer relief policy; criminal liability applies if the shell is used to commit tax evasion; travel ban and seizure of personal assets (bank accounts, real estate) is possible under the criminal code search and seizure provisions
AML / Money Laundering and Inconsistent Reporting (Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, subsection 4(2))

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) up to CAD 100,000 for non-compliance with AML record-keeping requirements; CRA can impose a 50% gross negligence penalty on unreported amounts; criminal offence for laundering proceeds: up to 10 years imprisonment; failure to report suspicious transactions: fine up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment for 1 year; cross-border criminal seizures under the Criminal Code; tax evasion penalties as above may aggregate with separate AML penalties

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted for money laundering if they knowingly participate or are wilfully blind to illicit flows; personal liability for AMPs applies under the PCMLTFA if the director was 'directing' or 'authorizing' the offending act; travel ban may be imposed pre-charge; personal assets including third-party property may be frozen under criminal anti-money laundering legislation; professional director disqualification (up to 10 years)
Incomplete Foreign Reporting and Off-Balance-Sheet Intangibles (ITC 240, 241, T1134 Non-filing)

Statutory sanction: Penalty for failure to file T1134: CAD 100 per day, max CAD 8,000 (if not filed within 24 months, max CAD 2,500 per month up to CAD 12,000); for T106 failure: CAD 100 per day, max CAD 2,500, plus an additional CAD 1,000 for each month of continued failure (up to 24 months); gross negligence penalty: CAD 1,000 per form; higher penalties apply for unreported foreign source income (10% of unreported amount or 50% if misrepresentation); CRA may extend reassessment period to 7 years if a return is incomplete or contains misrepresentation; indefinite or 10-year limitation in cases of fraud

Director personal liability: Directors signing false declarations on foreign affiliate filings may be criminally liable; civil director's liability can be assessed for failure to ensure corporate compliance with information returns; repeated non-filing can trigger a CRA criminal investigation, leading to director arrest, travel restrictions, and criminal fines; if the company is dissolved to avoid liabilities, directors are personally liable for pre-dissolution tax debts
