Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no partnership or trust flow-through status is available for a corporation. Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source income (business carried on in Canada, taxable capital gains on Canadian properties, and Part XIII passive income). Canada has no federal tax-exempt zones.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election and no U.S.-style entity classification election exists under Canadian federal tax law; CRA disregards the U.S. check-the-box classification for domestic tax characterization. Available statutory elections include subsection 85(1)/85.1 rollovers for tax-deferred transfers to corporations, section 88 amalgamation/winding-up elections, subsection 83(2) capital dividend election, section 216 and 217 elections for non-resident passive income, and ETA section 156 election for GST/HST relief between closely related corporations. Hybrid mismatch rules apply through Canada's adherence to the OECD BEPS Action 2 minimum standard, limiting deductions on hybrid arrangements, but no elective corporate transparency regime is offered.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no general exchange-control regime under the Currency Act, no investment approval requirement for inward or outward capital movements, and no restriction on dividend, interest, royalty, or management-fee remittances to non-residents. Current and capital account transactions are effectively free, subject only to standard tax withholding on outbound non-arm's length payments and to FATF-oriented AML compliance reporting for large cross-border wire transfers (CAD 10,000 or more).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio in Canada. Exporters of goods and services, including ship-crew recruitment/crew-management services, may invoice, collect, and retain proceeds freely in any convertible currency. Foreign currency earnings may be held offshore or repatriated through a domestic authorized dealer without Bank of Canada approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange policy and payments infrastructure are additionally overseen by the Department of Finance Canada, with prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/FINTRAC reporting to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: RELIABLE BUT NOT FULLY EQUIVALENT to a Canadian chartered bank. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, OFX, and Revolut Business can serve as secondary cross-border payment/payroll rails. They are not authorized deposit-taking institutions in Canada; they generally operate as money-services businesses (MSBs) and must be registered with FINTRAC, and where active in the U.S., with FinCEN. The legal constraint is that corporate clients cannot receive Deposit Insurance (CDIC) protection; funds held by an EMI are not guaranteed and are held via pooled omnibus or safeguarded accounts. For crew-payroll clearing, an additional sponsored domestic bank account is recommended for statutory source deductions (CPP, EI, income tax) and to satisfy crew visa/embassy requirements for proof of employment income.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital contributions to the Canadian federal corporation must be routed via a SWIFT MT103 wire to the designated CAD/USD account held by the licensed Schedule I bank. The reference line must include the corporate name/BN and show 'Capital Contribution—Share Capital' to satisfy the bank's automatic EFT-reporting rules. The remitter must provide: corporate resolution, share subscription agreement, passport copy of investor, source-of-funds bank statement, and if above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC reporting is mandatory. For amounts exceeding CAD 100,000, the receiving bank will perform source-of-wealth confirmation but no Bank of Canada approval is needed. Cross-border payroll is optimized by using same-bank book transfers between foreign currency accounts or a global multi-currency sweep to reduce correspondent fees. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

38% statutory federal rate; after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% General Rate Reduction, the net federal general corporate rate is 15%. Combined federal and provincial general corporate rates range from approximately 23% to 27% depending on the province.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 information return is required when the aggregate fair market value of specified non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year. Canada also requires Country-by-Country reporting for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be maintained for all controlled transactions regardless of amount, consistent with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone statutory ESR regime comparable to UAE Economic Substance Regulations, but the CRA applies general anti-avoidance rules under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the doctrine of substance over form, and sham transaction analysis. A maritime crew recruitment agency must demonstrate real operational substance in Canada, including the presence of employment contracts, payroll records, physical management functions, insurance coverage, and economic risk-bearing, before treaty benefits or transfer pricing arrangements are respected.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Underreporting Canadian-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of unreported income plus gross negligence penalties under Income Tax Act section 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal prosecution under section 239 carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in falsifying corporate records are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholdings and payroll remittances under section 227.1; the corporate veil can be pierced for fraud, and courts may order civil restitution, travel restrictions on bail, and imprisonment.
Shell Company Arrangement and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR under section 245 and disallow the tax benefit entirely; the transactions may be recharacterized as Canadian-source business income, with penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit and reassessment of all non-arm's length payments.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false declarations or causes the shell arrangement is exposed to civil clawback, personal penalty assessments, director liability under section 227.1, and Canadian criminal prosecution for tax evasion, with potential imprisonment and international travel controls.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 1,000,000 per violation for an entity; intentional money laundering is a Criminal Code offense under section 462.31 punishable by imprisonment for up to ten years and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally subject to FINTRAC penalties if they knowingly participate in unregistered money transmission, and criminal liability for money laundering carries serious imprisonment terms plus confiscation orders; civil liability may extend to all laundered payroll amounts.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment and T106 Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess the transfer price under section 247(2), increasing Canadian taxable income. A 10% transfer pricing penalty under section 247(3) applies where documented pricing efforts were not reasonable and the taxpayer did not retain contemporaneous TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for knowingly signing false returns; continued non-filing of information returns attracts separate court compliance orders, and deliberate transfer pricing manipulation will likely be treated as tax evasion, exposing directors to civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
