Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC), 4th Floor, 555 Mackenzie Avenue, Ottawa ON; statutory tax filing and registration portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes.html); GST/HST registration portal: 'Business Registration Online' (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-business/business-number-registration-online.html; customs and export reporting portal: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)/Canadian Export Reporting System.

E-government filing portal

For a federal business corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) and held by non-residents: (1) file Articles of Incorporation with Corporations Canada / Innovation, Science and Economic Development (ISED) — no minimum capital requirement; (2) upon issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation, the CRA automatically issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN), which is the statutory TIN, together with the RC corporate income tax program account; (3) before commencing taxable commercial activity, open the GST/HST RT program account via BRO or Form RC1, and for non-resident applicants via Form RC4601 ('Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'); (4) where the corporation will have Canadian-resident employees, open the RP payroll account; where goods are embedded in consultancy deliverables, open the RM customs/export account; (5) the combined 15-character program account identifier (e.g., BN '123456789' + 'RT0001') must appear on GST/HST returns, invoices, and customs documents. Registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold, and the BN/RT account application must be filed within 30 days after ceasing to be a small supplier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: CA_CORP is a resident corporation under subsection 250(4) ITA by virtue of federal incorporation and is taxed as a separate Part I taxpayer on worldwide income (federal general corporate rate 15.0%; 9.0% small business rate only where the corporation is a Canadian-controlled private corporation — unavailable if non-resident-controlled); no pass-through, transparent, or S-corporation-type treatment exists for CBCA corporations; the same entity-level taxation applies provincially/territorially (e.g., Ontario 10.0% general corporate rate); non-resident-owned CBCA corporations remain resident Canadian corporations and therefore are not taxed on a purely territorial basis.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election; classification follows legal form. Available elections relevant to this cross-border consultancy: (i) subsection 256(2.1) ITA election for a non-resident corporation to be deemed resident for the loss-utilization and associated-corporation rules; (ii) subsection 216(1) ITA election for a non-resident to file a Part I return on Canadian-source property/rental income instead of Part XIII withholding; (iii) the Part XIV branch-tax 'investment allowance' election computed under Income Tax Regulations 808-810 to reduce the 25% branch tax base of a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada; (iv) GST/HST nil-consideration election under section 156 ETA (Form RC4129) for supplies between closely related corporations; (v) GST/HST joint group-registration election among closely related residents under Part IX Excise Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch consequences are non-elective and are governed by ITA sections 18.4 and 18.5 (BEPS Action 2).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (CAD) — GST/HST registration is mandatory for a person carrying on a commercial activity in Canada whose total taxable supplies made in Canada exceed C$30,000 either in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, subsections 240(1) and 240(1.1) ETA); zero-rated export supplies are excluded from the threshold calculation; a non-resident supplying services into Canada is subject to the same threshold, and once exceeded the RT account application must be filed within 30 days after ceasing to be a small supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The overseas market-entry consulting services supplied by CA_CORP to non-resident clients are zero-rated under section 23 of Schedule VI to the ETA where: (1) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada with a foreign business address and economic substance; (2) the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada; (3) the supply is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at any time; and (4) the service is not rendered to a non-resident who is present in Canada at the time and for whose benefit the service is performed with a connection to Canada. Any tangible goods physically exported as part of a deliverable (e.g., demonstration kits, printed reports) qualify for zero-rating under section 7 of Schedule VI and must be supported by a CBSA export declaration. The registered supplier must retain contemporaneous proof of non-resident status, the place of performance, and actual receipt of consideration; mischaracterized domestic services (where the foreign client directs or benefits from the consultancy within Canada) will be reclassified as taxable at 5% GST plus applicable provincial HST/PST.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System / authorized carrier cargo declaration evidencing departure of any tangible deliverable from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill issued by a common carrier evidencing shipment of physical media, prototypes, or printed reports to the overseas client
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — SWIFT MT103, Wise B2B transaction receipt, or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of consideration from the non-resident payer
  • Written market-entry advisory contract identifying the non-resident recipient, offshore subject matter, scope of work, and place of performance
  • Itemized commercial invoice bearing the notation 'Zero-rated export supply — section 23, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act (Canada)'
  • Foreign client address and non-resident status attestation confirming the consulting output is received and used outside Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API tax-stamping, or continuous-controls e-invoicing mandate is currently in force in Canada at the federal level. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1.5 million must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE / My Business Account), and electronic remittance is mandatory where monthly payroll withholdings average more than C$50,000. CRA administers the voluntary 'Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing' PEPPOL-based interoperability framework, but participation is optional; regardless, subsection 286(1) ETA requires all registrants to retain books, contracts, invoices, and transaction logs for six years, and CRA may demand contemporaneous zero-rating substantiation on audit.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export claim within four years of the GST/HST filing date (subsection 298(1) ETA) and within four years for corporate income tax (subsection 152(3.1) ITA); where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, willful default, or fraud is established, the reassessment window is unlimited. Penalties for fraudulent export zero-rating include: (i) a false-statement penalty of the greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax assessed under subsection 280(1) ETA; (ii) a gross-negligence income tax penalty of 50% of the understated Part I tax under subsection 163(2) ITA; (iii) criminal prosecution under section 327 ETA and section 239 ITA — fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment; (iv) daily compounded arrears interest and recovery of over-credited input tax credits; and (v) application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 ITA) where the zero-rating claim lacks substantive economic reality. A transaction file failing the 180-day repatriation substantiation benchmark of this dossier will be treated by CRA as presenting elevated audit risk and presumed insufficiency of documentary evidence for the zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada accepts Article VIII obligations; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible on current and capital account. Profit remittance does not require exchange-control permission, but Part XIII withholding tax (typically 25% statutory, up to 5% or 25% reduced under the Canada-U.S./double tax treaties) and transfer-pricing rules are the material hurdles for overseas market-entry consultancies repatriating earnings. Investment Canada Act review may impose notification or net-benefit conditions when a non-resident acquires control of a Canadian business, but it does not block currency conversion or lawful dividend flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender period, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no exchange-control filing for the retention of export foreign currency. Export receipts may be held in foreign-denominated bank accounts; only PCMLTFA-based threshold reporting applies to international electronic funds transfers in excess of CAD 10,000, which is discharged by the financial institution.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration exists, and exchange control functions are subsumed by Finance Canada, OSFI, and FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium viability as supplemental payment rail. Offshore digital EMIs and specialized providers such as Wise B2B are lawfully available for low-value cross-border disbursements, but cannot hold corporate deposits as a Canadian bank; Canadian-licensed deposit-taking institutions are required to act as the primary treasury layer. Fintech EMIs must be integrated only for small ticket consultancy payments and fast FX settlements, with the main operating and escrow accounts at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank to satisfy CBCA oversight, FINTRAC reporting, and bank comfort requirements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Foreign shareholder injection structured as share capital while operational expenses are paid in multiple overseas currencies

Root cause: Foreign shareholder injection structured as share capital while operational expenses are paid in multiple overseas currencies

Defense evidence: Corporate organizational chart, CBCA certificate and articles, board resolution authorizing share subscription, share certificates, source-of-funds documentation from the remitting bank, audited or reviewed financial statements, and a written treasury plan explaining the commercial purpose of each cross-border movement.
Ultimate Beneficial Ownership (UBO) opacity, especially if a trust, unregulated holding vehicle, or nominee shareholder is in the ownership chain

Root cause: Ultimate Beneficial Ownership (UBO) opacity, especially if a trust, unregulated holding vehicle, or nominee shareholder is in the ownership chain

Defense evidence: Completed corporate registry UBO register filed under CBCA, identification for every individual holding 25% or more, trust deed or settlement deed if applicable, sworn beneficial ownership declarations, and a Canadian legal opinion confirming that no undisclosed indirect owner exists.
PEP status or adverse media on directors, shareholders, or high-level employees

Root cause: PEP status or adverse media on directors, shareholders, or high-level employees

Defense evidence: Point-in-time commercial PEP/media screening reports, government-issued notarized identity documents, professional reference letters from a regulated Canadian or foreign financial institution, biographical resume, and documented legal source of wealth (tax returns, inheritance documents, property sale contracts, or publicly audited corporate payouts).
Substantial incoming wire from a low-transparency jurisdiction or an exchanger/high-risk jurisdiction that does not align with cash-flow nature of a consultancy

Root cause: Substantial incoming wire from a low-transparency jurisdiction or an exchanger/high-risk jurisdiction that does not align with cash-flow nature of a consultancy

Defense evidence: Counterparty due diligence questionnaire, client engagement agreement, deliverables schedule with client-approved invoices, remittance breakdown showing payment for specific advisory services, exporter/importer details on the SWIFT message, and where necessary a Canadian counsel memorandum on the legitimacy of the funding source.
Large volume of outbound disbursements for sub-contracting or licensing fees with mismatched invoice references

Defense evidence: Executed sub-consultancy or license agreements, invoices cross-referenced to payment dates and SWIFT MT103 reference fields, proof of registered GST/HST account or evidence that supply is zero-rated for non-resident clients, timesheets or milestone certifications, and periodic reconciliation statements submitted to the bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for a Canadian federal consultancy are executed by International Central Securities no; the practical route is a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wire to the beneficiary's Tier-1 Canadian bank account using the bank's BIC, institution number, branch transit, and account number. Field 70 (Remittance Information) should explicitly cite 'Share capital contribution pursuant to board resolution dated [date]' or 'Intercompany shareholder loan' so the wiring-bank narrative matches the corporate treasury record. Canada does not maintain a statutory purpose-code or forex approval regime for incoming capital; the bank must simply complete FINTRAC's large-international-funds-transfer report where the amount exceeds CAD 10,000. The corporate must pre-position the share subscription agreement, director resolutions, and CBCA share register for presentation during any hold-funds review. If the inbound transfer is from a non-resident parent or private shareholder, treat it as capital for CBCA solvency purposes only if issued shares with consideration; otherwise, document an intercompany loan with arm's-length interest and written treasury support.Outbound trade and consultancy disbursements should clear via SWIFT MT103 for beneficiary banks outside Canada, particularly for licensing, sub-consultancy, and overseas market-entry registrations. Documentary trade counterparties may enforce Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600 where escrow payments or milestone deliveries require bank-verified documentation. For domestic Canadian-dollar flows, Payments Canada's Lynx high-value system handles same-day settlement, while the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) processes batched EFTs and pre-authorized debits for non-urgent vendor payments. FX conversion is executed at the ordering bank in the same working day before the MT103, with the corporate treasury maintaining a separate CAD operating account and a multi-currency settlement account to minimize conversion slippage. Where only small disbursements are needed and supplier contracts allow, Wise B2B can be used as an approved payment rail; otherwise all material treasury-grade payments must route from the primary bank through SWIFT.Outright FX forwards for CAD against USD, EUR, CNY, GBP, and JPYFX swaps and cross-currency swaps for smoothing intercompany cash-flow timingNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) where the referencing currency lacks deliverable settlement or statutory supportOTC FX options (call/put structures) for asymmetric protection on large licensing or M&A-related one-off paymentsExchange-traded FX futures via recognized Canadian and U.S. exchanges for short-dated hedge rollovers
