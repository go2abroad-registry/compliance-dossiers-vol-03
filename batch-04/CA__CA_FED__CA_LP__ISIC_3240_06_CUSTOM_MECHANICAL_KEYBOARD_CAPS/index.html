Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); operational tax account management through the CRA 'My Business Account' portal, business registration via the CRA Business Registration Online service, and GST/HST filing through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE system.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or resident limited partnership is a deemed person for Canadian excise tax purposes and must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and a dedicated GST/HST account before making taxable supplies in Canada. The LP must submit CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or use the CRA online business registration service, identifying the partnership name, the Canadian general partner and its SIN/BN, the registered business address, the expected date of first taxable supply, and the nature of export-oriented sales of physical goods. The GST/HST account number, formatted as BN plus the 'RT' program identifier, is the sole VAT-style tax identifier; Canada does not issue a separate VAT ID. A foreign LP that does not yet exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits and to zero-rate export sales, but voluntary registration is not advisable for pure export businesses unless Canadian input credits exist. Records of the foreign entity's constitutive documents, powers of attorney, and proof of the general partner's Canadian connection must be filed with the registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no statutory check-the-box election analogous to the U.S. regime. A domestic limited partnership is automatically fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act section 96(1) as a 'Canadian partnership' where every member is a resident of Canada or where the partnership carries on business in Canada; non-resident limited partners are permitted and ownership is not restricted. The limited partnership agreement must specify the allocation of income, losses, and capital gains, and allocations must be reasonable and consistent with the partners' economic interests; no advance CRA election is required for pass-through treatment. For hybrid mismatch protection, ITA sections 18.2, 18.4, 20.4, and 94 operate to disallow deductions for structured payments that create deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; the LP should document all cross-border payments from foreign limited partners and avoid hybrid instruments. There is no SIFT partnership election available because the LP is engaged in an active manufacturing business rather than a specified investment flow-through activity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export sales), measured on taxable supplies made in a single calendar quarter and over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; the same threshold applies to non-resident LPs selling custom mechanical keyboard keycaps through Shopify Payments and Stripe into Canada or from Canada to foreign customers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for the sale of novelty double-shot PBT plastic mechanical keyboard keycaps is available under section 23(1) of the Excise Tax Act and Schedule VI, Part V. The LP must prove that the goods were shipped or delivered by the supplier, the supplier's agent, or a common carrier to a destination outside Canada, exported in substantially the same form as when supplied, and dispatched no later than two days after delivery or as soon as reasonably possible under the mode of transportation. Online orders routed to non-resident customers through Shopify Payments and Stripe qualify where the customer's foreign shipping address is recorded and the carrier waybill confirms actual cross-border export. The supply of associated offshore services strictly ancillary to the exported goods is also zero-rated under Part V where separately disclosed and billed. Goods that are merely sold to a non-resident purchaser but remain in Canada for subsequent processing or resale do not qualify.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration, including the Canadian Export Declaration B13A form or authorized Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) transmitted record where the shipment value exceeds the CBSA reporting threshold
  • Commercial invoice generated by Shopify Payments or Stripe showing the supplier's GST/HST registration number, the zero-rated 'Z' tax code, the foreign buyer name and address, and Incoterms
  • Transport document, being a Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier shipping manifest (Canada Post, Purolator, DHL, FedEx, or UPS) evidencing departure from Canada and foreign destination delivery

  • Carrier tracking history showing export timing, destination country, and delivery confirmation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government e-invoicing clearance. The CRA requires all GST/HST registrants whose annual net tax exceeds CAD 10,000 to file GST/HST returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE and, in most cases, to remit payments electronically. Canada's Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing framework is voluntary and aligns with the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 international standard. Invoices generated by Shopify or Stripe satisfy the CRA informational content rules provided they identify the supplier and its GST/HST BN, the date of supply, the consideration, and the applicable tax rates; export sales must be coded as zero-rated with tax code 'Z' and a note describing the export shipment.

CRA export audits focus on documentation adequacy and the physical reality of export. Where zero-rating is disallowed, the LP is assessed uncollected GST at 5% on the selling price plus daily compound interest at the prescribed CRA rate. False statements or omissions in GST returns attract a penalty under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax shortfall. Wilful evasion, fraud, or intentional concealment is prosecutable under section 281, with fines up to CAD 25,000 on summary conviction or up to CAD 200,000 on indictment and possible imprisonment. The normal reassessment period is four years from the filing date; misrepresentation by negligence extends the period to eight years, and fraudulent conduct leaves the assessment period unlimited. CRA may also claw back input tax credits previously claimed on the exported goods, reclassify the goods as taxable domestic supplies, and assess the general partner personally where the partnership's failure to remit arises from gross negligence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates under a fully open capital and current account system. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible with no exchange control approval mechanism for either current account transactions (trade in goods and services, interest, dividends, royalties) or capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, financing, profit repatriation). Profit and capital remittance by a Canadian limited partnership to foreign partners is not subject to FX-control clearance, but is subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (normally 25% unless reduced by an applicable treaty) on deemed or actual distributions of income. Direct capital injection from abroad into a Canadian LP likewise requires no Bank of Canada permission; however, investments exceeding review thresholds trigger notification or review obligations under the Investment Canada Act. Sectoral FDI restrictions do not apply to double-shot PBT plastic injection manufacturing of keyboard keycaps (ISIC 3240/NAICS 334118), which is considered a non-sensitive industrial activity. All cross-border financial transactions are routed through the Canadian Payments Association (CPA) or SWIFT, with settlements processed in Canadian dollars or foreign currencies without mandatory CBC reporting unless FINTRAC threshold records apply (e.g., large cash or EFT records above $10,000).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, repatriation, or proportional conversion requirements for Canadian export proceeds. Exporters of keyboard keycaps may retain sales denominated in USD or other currencies in non-resident bank accounts, and may convert them at any time through Canadian financial institutions. There is no prescribed timeline, minimum conversion ratio, or central bank registration for export receipts. Nonetheless, all import/export of currency or monetary instruments above $10,000 CAD must be reported to FINTRAC, and routine documentation of export invoices, customs declarations, and underlying sales contracts is advisable to maintain audit trails for banking and tax compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the primary monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential banking regulator; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF financial intelligence unit; and the Department of Finance Canada for residual exchange control policy under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. A foreign fintech operating as an electronic money institution (EMI) is not chartered as a Canadian bank under the Bank Act and therefore cannot accept deposit liabilities subject to OSFI oversight. However, foreign money services businesses (MSBs) may offer cross-border payment and currency exchange services to a Canadian LP if they register as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC and comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. The LP could use a registered EMI for operational payments, but funds held with such entities do not enjoy Canadian Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protection and may be subject to grey-list jurisdictions, making them unsuitable for capital escrow or large settlement balances. For inbound capital and trade settlement, tier-1 Canadian bank accounts are preferred to ensure statutory creditor safeguards, SWIFT connectivity, and smooth clearing through the CPA.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment into the Canadian LP must be wired via SWIFT to the LP's Canadian operational or escrow bank account using a clearly defined purpose code. For regulatory verification, the commercial wire narrative should state the nature of the transaction, for example: 'CONTRIBUTION TO CANADIAN LIMITED PARTNERSHIP - ISIC 3240 KEYBOARD KEYCAP MANUFACTURING' and reference the foreign partner's name and the partnership's account number. The remitter should use the applicable business/investment purpose code under the SWIFT instruction category (e.g., 'Investment in Canadian business' or 'Advances in capital contributions'). Canadian banks generally do not require a specific central-bank code, but they will verify that the payment is not a remittance of earning subject to Part XIII tax, and will ask for a supporting capital contribution agreement or board resolution. To avoid misclassification, the sending institution should indicate the sender's capacity (general partner) and the recipient as 'Business - Limited Partnership' under FATF/OECD recipient-partner type codes. Upon receipt, the bank may place a temporary hold for enhanced due diligence if the amount exceeds CAD $150,000, and will complete a FINTRAC report. The LP should be prepared to produce: partnership registration certificate, trade licence (if any), tax identification number, and an anti-money-laundering acknowledgement from all general partners.Outbound trade settlements for purchased raw materials (powder pigments, plastic pellets, packing materials) and reimbursements to overseas suppliers are executed through SWIFT MT103 or, separately, via Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) when suppliers require documentary credit. For routine high-volume trade payments, a Canadian Schedule-I bank will offer corresponding clearing rails through the Clearing and Settlement System of the Canadian Payments Association (LVTS for CAD) and SWIFT/CHIPS/Fedwire via their New York correspondent for USD. Each MT103 must include the business reason code (STO, CHP, or MRC), the Canadian BIC (Business Identifier Code) of the beneficiary and the reverse invoice reference. For commercial L/C transactions, the LP must present a full set of shipping, customs, and quality certificates; the issuing Canadian bank will execute reimbursement against a compliant document presentation. Disbursements to related parties or non-residents in currencies other than CAD must respect Part XIII tax withholding or, alternatively, be structured as payment for expenses under a documented contract to avoid deemed-dividend recharacterisation. All outgoing wires must be coupled with proof of expenditure to clear triggers.Deliverable plain-vanilla foreign exchange forward contracts (USD/CAD and EUR/CAD) executed with a Canadian OSFI-regulated bank under ISDA or a short-form FX master agreement, permissible for hedging raw material purchases and sales exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) against an exotic counterparty currency (e.g., CNY, KRW, INR) are permissible via Canadian banks, though the CAD is a freely convertible currency so an NDF is typically limited to hedging illiquid ultimate settlement currencies.Cross-currency swaps and simple USD/CAD currency swaps are permissible, with swap transactions requiring board approval and reporting to counterparty banks and, if the aggregate notional exceeds OSFI thresholds, regulatory reporting to the Bank of Canada under the Derivatives Reporting Regulations.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal Part I headline rate 15%; M&P manufacturing rate 13%; Small Business Deduction rate 9% (first CAD 500,000); LP is fiscally transparent — the LP itself pays no Part I tax; each partner is taxed on their allocated share (individuals: federal marginal up to 33%; corporate partners: 15% federal)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII on deemed dividends allocable to non-resident partners under s. 212(13.1) of the ITA where the LP carries on business in Canada; actual cash dividends paid by a Canadian corporate manufacturer: 25%; capital distributions upon LP wind-up are non-taxable returns of capital unless in excess of the partner's ACB

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate gross proceeds per controlled transaction category — Form T106 trigger; contemporaneous CRA transfer-pricing documentation (Local File logic) required for every controlled cross-border transaction; s. 247(3) penalty 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of the reporting LP's gross revenue

Economic substance scope: Canada imposes no statutory Economic Substance Regulations in the Caribbean/UAE sense; however, the GAAR in s. 245 ITA void tax-benefit transactions lacking real economic substance; the Limited Partnership carrying passive 'specified investment flow-through' income is taxed as a corporation under s. 122.1 unless it carries on an active business; treaty shopping is rejected via the LOB clause (Canada-US Treaty Art. XXIX-A) and s. 94.1/120.4/212(13.1) anti-conduit rules; provincial LP statutes require the LP to maintain real business premises, payroll, and registrations in the province to preserve limited-liability status

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine equal to 50%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded and up to 2 years imprisonment (s. 239(1)(a) ITA); indictment: 200% fine and up to 5 years imprisonment (s. 239(1)(b)); civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (s. 163(2)); full re-assessment with arrears interest (s. 161)

Director personal liability: General partners are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII and Part I withholding under s. 227.1 ITA; directors who sign false returns face criminal prosecution and arrest-warrant flags; while Canada has no standalone tax travel ban, an indictment restricts cross-border travel through immigration enforcement and Federal Court certificate liens attach to all Canadian realty
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; arbitrary reassessment under s. 247(4); T106 late-filing penalty CAD 500/month capped at CAD 5,000; interest under s. 161 from the original due date

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or acquiesce in false transfer-pricing documentation are exposed to s. 239 criminal liability and civil claims by the corporate partner; a foreign parent's directors may be extradited under the Canada-US MLAT where documentation fraud is established
Shell Company / GAAR / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization (s. 245(5)) disallows the tax benefit and realigns the transaction to its true commercial substance; s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty applies; mandatory-disclosure reporting under s. 237.3 with CAD 500–2,000 monthly penalties; de facto entity-level taxation where SIFT rules (s. 122.1) treat the LP as a corporation

Director personal liability: A limited partner who participates in management control loses the statutory limited-liability shield and becomes personally liable for the general partner's debts; directors approving the scheme face s. 227.1 withholding liability and fraudulent-transfer claims from the CRA; the Federal Court may issue a certificate freezing the partnership's bank accounts and preventing asset dissipation
AML / Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 (money laundering) / CAD 2,000,000 (terrorist financing); Criminal Code s. 462.31 — imprisonment up to 10 years for laundering proceeds of crime; asset forfeiture under provincial civil forfeiture statutes; sanctions contravention under the Special Economic Measures Act with imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Director/general partner liability for knowingly causing the LP to transact with sanctioned persons; criminal record triggers inadmissibility to the US/Canada under IRPA s. 36(1); the veil is pierced where the limited partner exercises control over day-to-day payment routing; foreign government seizure of the LP's e-commerce platforms
