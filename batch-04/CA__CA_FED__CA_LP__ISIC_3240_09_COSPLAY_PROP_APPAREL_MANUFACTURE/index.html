Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery, Companies and Personal Property Security Branch, operating through the Ontario Business Registry (ServiceOntario); federal business number issued by the Canada Revenue Agency.

Governing statute

Canada has no federal limited partnership statute. Formation is effected under provincial limited partnership legislation; for a cross-provincial footprint the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.7, ss. 2, 9-12 and the Extra-Provincial Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. E.27, s. 3 are the governing statutes.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form 1) under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act; Extra-Provincial Limited Partnership Registration where the limited partnership carries on business outside Ontario.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a limited partnership. Capital contributions may be in cash, property, or services and must be recorded in the partnership agreement. No escrow or trust account is required, but contributed capital must be separately tracked to preserve limited liability status.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire safety inspection and fire code certificate are required for the manufacturing premises. Structural load inspection is required for storage racks and mezzanines. Occupational health and safety clearance must be obtained through the workplace safety regulator in the province of operation, and the business must register with the applicable workers' compensation authority before commencing production.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a limited partnership. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO); compliance portal: My Business Account (GST/HST).

E-government filing portal

A Canadian-resident limited partnership (LP) must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Register through BRO (online) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The BN is issued at the partnership level; the LP then opens a GST/HST account under the same BN. For non-resident owners, the LP may register as a non-resident registrant using CRA Form RC1 with Form RC459 (GST/HST Non-Resident Registration), or apply online where the designated partner holds valid Canadian identification. Voluntary registration under ETA s. 240(3) is permitted before the threshold is reached to recover input tax credits (ITCs) on startup and pre-revenue costs. The LP file a T5013 Summary and T5013 slips for each fiscal period; the BN is referenced in all returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. The LP is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (ITA ss. 96–103): the partnership itself pays no federal income tax; income, gains, losses and credits flow through to partners pro rata under the partnership agreement. The LP is a Canadian partnership if it is established under provincial law (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act) and carries on business in Canada; its partners are taxed on worldwide income if Canadian-resident. Non-resident partners are taxable on Canadian-source income from the partnership and may be subject to Part XIII withholding (25%) on distributions of other income. For GST/HST, the partnership is nevertheless a separate 'person' (ETA s. 48) requiring its own registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The LP is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (ITA ss. 96–103): the partnership itself pays no federal income tax; income, gains, losses and credits flow through to partners pro rata under the partnership agreement. The LP is a Canadian partnership if it is established under provincial law (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act) and carries on business in Canada; its partners are taxed on worldwide income if Canadian-resident. Non-resident partners are taxable on Canadian-source income from the partnership and may be subject to Part XIII withholding (25%) on distributions of other income. For GST/HST, the partnership is nevertheless a separate 'person' (ETA s. 48) requiring its own registration. No federal tax-exempt zone applies to cosplay prop apparel manufacturing.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists; classification follows juridical form and CRA administrative position. Available elections: (1) Voluntary GST/HST registration (ETA s. 240(3)); (2) Partnership fiscal-period election (ITA s. 249.1(8)) permitting a non-calendar taxation year where no corporation holds a majority partnership interest; (3) Hybrid-mismatch timing and allocation election under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 to neutralize deduction/non-inclusion differences involving non-resident partners; (4) Election under ITA s. 96(1) to compute income at the partnership level in Canadian currency; (5) Net-filing election to use GST/HST NETFILE. For non-resident partners, an election under Part XIII can be made to file a corporate tax return instead of withholding (where applicable).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: total value of taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) made in Canada by the LP and its associates in the current calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148(1)). Exceeding this threshold makes GST/HST registration mandatory; below it, registration is voluntary.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

COSPLAY APPAREIL EXPORT: Finished goods (synthetic wigs, costume robes, props) sold to non-resident purchasers and exported by the LP qualify for 0% GST under ETA Sched. VI, Part V, s. 2 (goods exported by the supplier) and s. 1 (goods delivered to a residence outside Canada pursuant to the supply). Services performed entirely outside Canada (e.g., pattern licensing, offshore design) are zero-rated under Part V, s. 7. To claim 0%, the LP must show that the goods were exported 'as soon as is reasonably possible' after sale and must retain the required documentation. Zero-rated supplies retain full ITC eligibility.

  • Export Reporting: Canadian Export Declaration via CAED (Canadian Automated Export Declaration) or carrier e-manifest for shipments valued at CAD 2,000 or more; low-value shipments evidenced by courier waybill
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Express Courier Waybill showing consignee address outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice with HS classification (e.g., HS 6114, 6704, 9505), original value, and stated 0% GST
  • Proof of Foreign Exchange Settlement: bank statement, SWIFT MT103, or Stripe/Shopify settlement record evidencing receipt of funds from the foreign purchaser (functional equivalent of an inward remittance certificate; Canadian banks do not issue 'inward remittance certificates' as a standard product)

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory API-based invoice authorization. Business invoices must meet ETA s. 169 and Regulation 238 (information content), and may be issued and stored electronically. PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is voluntary. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE, GSTR, or an accredited service provider once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million. Payment rails (Stripe, Shopify Payments) must collect GST at the point of sale and remit through the LP's GST/HST account; no separate fiscal clearance API is prescribed.

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; however, CRA audit practice treats settlement delays beyond 180 days as raising substantive doubt on the export sale, potentially triggering a reassessment to remove zero-rating. Assessment window: generally 4 years from filing (ETA s. 298(2)); extended to 10 years+ for misrepresentation or fraud. Reassessed GST (full 5%) is clawed back with arrears interest (ETA s. 280). Penalties: ETA s. 281.1(1) imposes a minimum CAD 250 plus 25% of the avoided tax for false statements made knowingly or under gross negligence; criminal evasion under ETA s. 327 carries fines of 50%–200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years. Under ETA s. 272.1, every partner is jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST debts, including clawbacks and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction; no approval is required for profit remittances, distribution of partnership earnings, or capital repatriation. Non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on distributions, with treaty relief available; CAD floats freely with no surrender or conversion obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Exporters must retain commercial documentation for CBSA/Statistics Canada reporting and file the Canadian Export Declaration (via Canadian Export Reporting System) for controlled goods and goods exceeding listed thresholds. All cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); with exchange-control authority historically vested in the Minister of Finance and no separate Foreign Exchange Control Board; cross-border AML oversight by FINTRAC.
