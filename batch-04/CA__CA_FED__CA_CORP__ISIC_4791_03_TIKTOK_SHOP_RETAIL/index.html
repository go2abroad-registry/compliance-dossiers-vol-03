Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online portal and My Business Account

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and, where applicable, register for GST/HST via the CRA's Business Registration Online. The entity must submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), provide corporate identification, Canadian director information, and details of Canadian place of business or representative. A separate GST/HST account is established when taxable supplies in Canada exceed or are expected to exceed the registration threshold, or when the entity voluntarily registers and satisfies the non-resident registration criteria.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. A federal/provincial business corporation is mandatorily taxed as a separate entity under the Income Tax Act. Elections relevant to hybrid arrangements include: joint election under subsection 216(1) for non-resident rental income, section 216 re-election for deemed disposition under subsection 128.1(4), and explicit declarations under section 245(5) for avoidance transactions. No pass-through election is available for a Business Corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies of property and services performed in Canada; once the threshold is exceeded, the entity must register within 30 days

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for GST/HST applies to supplies of goods made by way of sale that are exported by the supplier or delivered to a non-resident outside Canada, provided the supplier retains evidence of export within prescribed timeframes. Services that are supplied to a non-resident and that are performed entirely outside Canada, or that relate to certain export-related activities, may also be zero-rated. Qualifying exports must be supported by commercial invoices, proof of shipment, customs documentation, and foreign exchange records.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Proof of Foreign Payment / Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Transportation and Insurance Documentation
  • Non-Resident Customer Identification Records

Canada does not currently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping infrastructure for electronic invoices. GST/HST registrants are generally required to file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million. E-invoices are acceptable if they meet CRA readability and content requirements under the Books and Records Retention requirements; API-facilitated reporting is optional, not compulsory.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal reassessment period of 4 years (or longer in cases of misrepresentation). False or fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract penalties under subsection 285(1) of the ETA, including a 'false statements' penalty of 25% of the tax amount or CAD 100 (whichever is greater), plus compound interest. In gross negligence or fraud cases, the penalty may be increased to 50% of the understated tax, and the exporter may lose zero-rating privileges on future supplies. Repayment of incorrectly claimed input tax credits and retrospective clawbacks are also assessed using a cash-flow methodology.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control regulations restricting profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders, provided corporate solvency tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act are met and the relevant non-resident withholding tax on dividends and certain cross-border payments is addressed. CAD is freely tradeable in global markets, and bank accounts may be held in CAD, USD, EUR, or other major convertible currencies without central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Canada does not impose any foreign-currency surrender requirement on exporters. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts indefinitely, repatriated as needed, or used for cross-border trade settlements, subject solely to financial reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), which require FINTRAC reporting of inbound and outbound electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, as well as reporting of large cash transactions and prescribed accounting records.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, with foreign exchange policy coordination by the Department of Finance, prudential bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/financial intelligence oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border FinTech EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) are operationally viable for TikTok Shop transactional flows such as influencer payouts, international supplier disbursements, and small-value payment batch runs. They may act as supplementary payment rails and can offer multi-currency accounts with local receiving facilities in USD, EUR, GBP, and CNY. Legal constraints: these entities are not Canadian-chartered banks under the Bank Act; they are typically authorized as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, or operate under foreign jurisdictions with EMI licenses, and funds held with them are not CDIC-insured. They also do not provide the full scope of letters of credit, documentary collections, or formal SWIFT correspondent-bank settlement finality required for larger trade operations; therefore, a regulated Tier-1 Canadian bank account remains the primary treasury anchor.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) opacity with tiered shareholdings held through foreign holding companies

Defense evidence: Complete organizational chart showing each intermediate entity and the natural persons exercising control; notarized share register, trust deeds, declarations of trust, certificates of incumbency, and passport copies of all individuals holding 10% or more of voting rights; bank reference letters and source-of-funds evidence for the capital being advanced.
Non-resident directors or directors domiciled in high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., sanctions-sensitive regions)

Root cause: Non-resident directors or directors domiciled in high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., sanctions-sensitive regions)

Defense evidence: Form 5409 or equivalent attestation of director identity; Canadian registered agent confirmation; documented evidence of substantive Canadian operations (lease agreement, employee payroll, physical presence, contracts); police certificate or criminal background check from the director's country of residence; clear source-of-wealth narrative for any personal activities.

Unclear source of funds for cross-border capital injection from a related overseas trading entity

Root cause: Unclear source of funds for cross-border capital injection from a related overseas trading entity

Defense evidence: Board resolutions authorizing the capital contribution; share subscription or subscription agreement; remitter bank statements evidencing the commercial origin of funds; group entity organizational chart; commercial invoice or underlying trade contract for the related-party funding; tax identification number of the remitter and a written representation confirming the transfer is an equity injection and not a loan subject to Canada's thin capitalization rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for the Canadian operating entity must be effected as a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to a designated account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. The remitter's bank must include a structured purpose code reflecting an equity contribution, such as the OECD classification item for 'Purchase of shares' or the Canadian bank's internal capital-contribution narrative (e.g., 'Share Subscription Proceeds'). The beneficiary account must exactly match the corporate legal name as registered under the Canada Business Corporations Act, and the receiving bank will obtain a copy of the board resolution and subscription agreement. Transactions with a value of CAD 10,000 or more automatically trigger FINTRAC reporting obligations; no Bank of Canada approval is required. The bank may place the funds in an escrow account pending compliance verification and issuance of shares in the corporation's share register.Outbound disbursements for TikTok Shop merchandise purchases, logistics settlement, customs duties, and influencer payouts are cleared through a combination of SWIFT MT103/MT202 direct wires, the Canadian Lynx real-time gross settlement system for CAD, ACSS for batch CAD, and the bank's domestic wire network for USD through a US clearing correspondent. International inventory purchases are often financed through a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) with the seller's advising bank, typically in China, Hong Kong, or the United States, with documents under the LC presented via SWIFT MT798. For smaller cross-border payments, high-volume disbursements to overseas suppliers can be batched through Wise Business or Payoneer using local payment rails where permissible, provided that the Canadian corporate bank account remains the primary settlement node and FINTRAC reporting obligations are satisfied on every cross-border electronic transfer above CAD 10,000.Outright deliverable foreign exchange forwards (up to 24 months) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Schedule with a Canadian Schedule I bank dealer, providing mandatory USD/CAD and CNY/USD hedging.Cross-currency swaps (MTM with bank) to align operating cashflow mismatches between CAD, USD, and CNY settlement cycles.European-style foreign exchange options (call/put) on USD/CAD, permitted for hedging a forecasted revenue stream, subject to board-approved hedge policy and IFRS 9 documentation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or non-deliverable currencies such as CNY where an offshore deliverable CNY market is unavailable to the Canadian entity; NDFs are permissible if entered directly with an authorized FX dealer and the reference rate is set using a recognized offshore CNY fixing.FX swaps (spot/forward combination) to convert temporary USD surpluses from TikTok Shop sales into CAD while preserving deterministic future re-conversion at a fixed rate.
