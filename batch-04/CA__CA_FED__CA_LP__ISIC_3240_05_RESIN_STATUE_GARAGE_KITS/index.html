Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and income tax registration via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-registration-online.html)

E-government filing portal

Foreign LP must first obtain a 15-character CRA Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1; register for a GST/HST program account (Account Type 'RT') at the partnership level; a non-resident LP must name a resident tax representative or appoint an additional agent under Excise Tax Act section 306; registration becomes mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single quarter or over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; CRA issues immediate confirmation and establishes an RT0000 program account; provincial registrations (QST in Quebec, PST in British Columbia/Saskatchewan/Manitoba) follow separately based on physical presence nexus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (T5013 Statement of Partnership Income allocated to partners); no entity-level federal income tax on Canadian-source income; non-resident partners remain taxable on Canadian-source income and are subject to Part XIII withholding on passive distributions; for GST/HST purposes the partnership itself is the registrant and the unit of taxation, and partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's net tax liabilities.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box exists; CRA characterises a partnership by de jure legal features. Available elections include: (i) subsection 97(2) ITA partnership property rollover election on contribution of resin casting equipment and intangible design IP at undepreciated capital cost; (ii) hybrid mismatch safeguards under sections 18.6 and 95(2)(m.1) ITA and section 13.4 of the Income Tax Application Rules disallowing deductions for payments routed through hybrid LPs or reverse hybrids; (iii) GST/HST Quick Method election under sections 170 and 170.2 ETA for registrants with taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000; (iv) section 156 ETA election to treat closely related registrants as separate branches for internal supply relief; (v) election under section 177.1 ETA to treat a partnership and its partners as a single taxable entity in prescribed circumstances.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies measured over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold under ETA section 148); mandatory registration from the first supply that exceeds the threshold; a non-resident LP with no permanent establishment must additionally register if it solicits orders in Canada or makes taxable supplies to Canadian persons in the ordinary course of business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating is claimable under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act: section 5.1 zero-rates goods exported directly by the supplier or transported by the supplier out of Canada within 60 days of sale; section 7 zero-rates services performed wholly outside Canada for non-residents where the services are acquired exclusively for use outside Canada; sections 10–12 cover drop-shipments from Canadian warehouses to registered non-resident persons and services such as moulding, packing, loading, and freight that facilitate an export; section 23 zero-rates intangible personal property (artistic design files, casting master IP) supplied to non-residents for use outside Canada. The LP must procure documentary evidence at the time of supply; input tax credits for GST/HST embedded in resin, moulds, pigments, studio equipment, freight, and utilities remain available on the ordinary ITC recovery rules supporting the zero-rated export stream.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CBSA Single Window Canada export reporting declaration evidencing physical departure of the goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill/Cargo Control Document evidencing continuous physical movement from Canada to the non-resident consignee
  • Commercial invoice stating the HS classification (typically HS 3926.40 or 3920.40 for polyresin statuettes) and the explicit 0% GST/HST export indicator
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank receipt or SWIFT/electronic funds transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of the export consideration in convertible foreign currency
  • Carrier delivery proof (Proof of Delivery), cargo manifest, and the underlying sales contract naming the non-resident buyer as owner entitled to possession.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital fiscal stamping, or API-authorization regime is currently mandated by CRA; registrants may lawfully issue standard electronic invoices. The 2024 Federal Budget announced a phased mandatory e-invoicing obligation for GST/HST registrants — large businesses (revenue over CAD 20 million) by 2026 and all other registrants by 2027 — using the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol BIS 3.0) over a four-corner interoperability model. CRA retains the power to demand invoice-level electronic data (Formal Demand template GST 13-010) and to audit ERP sales journals, bank feeds, carrier shipping records, and foreign-exchange remittance ledgers.

CRA auditors scrutinise export dossiers for continuous physical shipment and enforceable receipt of export consideration; missing documents cause zero-rating to be denied, GST/HST to be reassessed on the full selling price, and past ITCs to be clawed back. Statutory interest accrues at 6% per annum compounded daily on any net tax deficiency. Statutory penalties include: ETA section 285(2) negligence penalty of 25% of net tax; ETA section 285(1) false statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax; ETA section 329 criminal provision for willful evasion with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; and ETA section 323 joint and several director/partner liability for the partnership's net tax. Retrospective reassessment windows extend four years ordinarily, seven years where negligence or misrepresentation is established, and without limitation in cases of fraudulent export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital and current account convertibility with no foreign exchange controls. There is no requirement for central bank approval for capital contributions, profit repatriation, dividend distributions, or trade settlements. The Income Tax Act may impose Part XIII withholding tax on certain payments to non-residents, and treaty relief is available; an LP's pass-through status allocates Canadian-source income to partners, and non-resident partners may have Canadian filing or withholding obligations depending on whether they are active in a business carried on in Canada. No currency conversion licence is required for operating current or capital account transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Canada. Exporters may lawfully retain proceeds in CAD, USD, or other foreign currencies, in Canadian or offshore bank accounts. The only outward-facing filing is the B13 export declaration required for customs and statistical purposes; this is not an FX control mechanism and does not mandate conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority under the Bank of Canada Act); Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange control administration; FINTRAC supervises AML/ATF obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for banks and money services businesses.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs without a Canadian licence are not viable as primary treasury rails. Any foreign electronic-money institution operating payment services, FX dealing, or e-money issuance in Canada must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and must comply with PCMLTFA, Canada's AML/ATF requirements, and applicable provincial consumer-protection laws. An EMI with Canadian MSB registration and a physical compliance presence is moderately feasible for low-risk preorder settlement via Stripe/PayPal, but it is not appropriate for capital escrow, trade finance, or large outbound supplier disbursements; the LP should maintain a Schedule I Canadian bank account as its liquidity anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Investor capital must be remitted by wire transfer using the SWIFT MT103 message format from a licensed foreign banking institution. The payment narrative must state 'LP capital contribution' and reference the Canadian LP's bank account. Canada has no exchange-control permission requirement for inward FDI or partnership capital calls. The receiving Canadian bank must report any international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC. The bank will require a capital-account subscription agreement, a general-partner resolution evidencing the capital call, a source-of-funds questionnaire for each investor, and proof that the remitting bank has performed customer due diligence. For USD-denominated injections, the LP may credit a US-dollar commercial account, with CAD-equivalent entries recorded at the institution's prevailing exchange rate; no central-bank approval is needed.Supplier payments are made via SWIFT MT103 wires through the Canadian bank's correspondent network. For domestic CAD settlement, the bank will clear through Payments Canada's Lynx system previously known as LVTS; for USD clearing, funds move through the US Federal Reserve's Fedwire or CHIPS via the Canadian bank's US correspondent. Trade settlement with overseas resin, pigment, and packaging suppliers can be structured as an irrevocable documentary letter of credit opened by the Canadian bank; the beneficiary presents commercial invoices, bills of lading or airway bills, certificates of origin, packing lists, and inspection certificates to obtain payment.
