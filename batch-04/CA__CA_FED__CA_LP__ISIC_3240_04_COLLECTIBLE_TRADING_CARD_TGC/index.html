Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) — GST/HST and Business Number registration administered through CRA's My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/gst-hst.html).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must register for a federal Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST account using CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) service or by mailing Form RC1 (‘Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts’). For non-resident registrants without a Canadian presence, Form RC151 (‘GST/HST Application for Non-Resident Registrant’) and Form RC1 are used. CRA may require security under subsection 296(2) of the Excise Tax Act before issuing a GST/HST number. The statutory tax identifier is the 9-digit BN plus the two-letter program identifier (e.g., ‘RC’ for GST/HST). The partnership must provide its BN on all GST/HST returns, invoices, and customs documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian limited partnerships are flow-through entities under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act; no check-the-box election exists. Partners may make a partnership property contribution election under subsection 97(2) to defer tax on capital property transfers. The GST/HST regime, however, treats the partnership as a separate person in its capacity as a partnership under subsection 123(1) of the Excise Tax Act. For cross-border hybrid mismatch arrangements, Part XVI.I and subsection 26(2) of the Income Tax Act apply; there is no US-style check-the-box election available at the federal level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold measured on worldwide taxable supplies (including GST/HST and provincial PST) in a calendar quarter and the four preceding calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available to non-residents, and registration becomes obligatory where a non-resident carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment and makes taxable supplies in the course of that business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property (including collectible trading cards) supplied by a GST/HST registrant and exported from Canada as part of commercial export is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier exports the goods, delivers them outside Canada to a common carrier for export, or ships by mail or courier; the goods must be exported within a reasonable time, not be acquired for use in Canada prior to export, and the supplier must obtain and retain documentation establishing export. Services performed on tangible personal property for a non-resident, such as finishing and packaging for export, may also be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency export documentation or authorized customs broker confirmation)
  • Commercial Invoice / Sales Contract
  • Bill of Lading (or airway bill / courier manifest evidencing physical export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer evidence (SWIFT MT103 or payment gateway settlement record)
  • Packing List and cargo movement records
  • Carrier or warehouse export confirmation / certificate of shipment

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices or receipts containing the information required by section 223 of the Excise Tax Act; electronic invoicing is permitted without prior CRA authorization. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to most registrants, and all registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 150,000 must file electronically using CRA-approved software or the GST/HST NETFILE service.

Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-currency repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating eligibility; however, CRA auditors generally expect export consideration to be received within a commercially reasonable period not exceeding approximately 180 days, and zero-rating may be denied where payment evidence is absent or inconsistent. False or inflated zero-rating claims are subject to reassessment recovering GST/HST, interest (via the Income Tax Act / Excise Tax Act interest provisions), and penalties under subsection 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act (gross negligence penalties, the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the claimed credit or refund), failure-to-report penalties under section 285, and criminal sanctions under section 327 for making false or deceptive statements, with fines and imprisonment. The CRA may also disregard transactions lacking economic substance and impose penalties on non-resident registrants who do not pay assessed amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible fiat currency with no exchange-control restrictions on either current-account or capital-account operations. Cross-border capital contributions, trade payments, loan servicing, dividend distributions, and liquidation repatriations generally require only standard bank KYC/AML documentation and tax-compliant regulatory reporting. Profit remittances to non-resident partners remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% base rate, subject to reduction by treaty, but the actual currency conversion and wire transfer are unrestricted. CRS/FATCA self-certification and source-of-funds documentation will be collected at onboarding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts for collectible trading cards and holographic cold-foil printing may be retained in either Canadian or foreign currency accounts, used directly for offshore trade settlement, or remitted abroad. Export transactions remain taxable under the Income Tax Act, and intercompany pricing with foreign buyers must comply with Canadian transfer-pricing rules. Export documentation, including customs declarations, bills of lading, and commercial invoices, must be retained to support banking compliance and tax filings.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Currency Act; there is no separate foreign-exchange control administration. Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally chartered banks, and FINTRAC enforces AML transaction-reporting rules under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability Rating: Medium for ancillary payment collection and low-value cross-border disbursements, but Low for primary treasury and capital-escrow functions. Canada prohibits unlicensed offshore e-money institutions from accepting deposits or carrying on retail banking activities under the Bank Act. A foreign EMI license alone is not sufficient to open a Canadian settlement account. A regulated Canadian money-service business or authorized foreign-bank branch may provide FX conversion and payment routing, but high-value trade settlements and statutory capital preservation should remain on Tier 1 bank SWIFT/LVTS rails.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax rate on taxable income allocated to the federal jurisdiction, before provincial allocation and general corporate rate reductions; effective federal rate for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) on active business income eligible for the small business deduction is 9%. For a Limited Partnership, the entity is fiscally transparent under federal tax law: the partnership itself does not pay income tax; each partner is liable for tax on their allocated share of partnership income. Non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on certain passive allocations and Part I tax on income from carrying on business in Canada through the permanent establishment of the partnership.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory Canadian withholding tax rate under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder is 25%. For a Limited Partnership, there is generally no concept of dividend withholding from partnership distributions because the profit allocation is taxed to partners on a flow-through basis; however, if the partner is a corporate shareholder and the LP earns Canadian business income through a Canadian permanent establishment, distributions of after-tax Canadian earnings to a foreign parent corporation or foreign limited partner may be recharacterized as deemed dividends in certain cross-border restructuring scenarios under the Canadian paid-up capital and deemed dividend rules. In the common scenario where the LP has a Canadian-resident corporate general partner that is a subsidiary of a non-resident parent, and the general partner distributes its allocated partnership income as a dividend to the foreign parent, the withholding tax rate is 25% reduced by treaty; for a Canada-US parent, the treaty rate on a portfolio dividend is 15% and on a 10% voting share corporate shareholder is 5%. For treaty-reduced rates, the Canadian payor must obtain an NR301/NR302 form or an automatic treaty relief arrangement; the CRA's treaty relief application protocol requires the non-resident beneficial owner to submit Form NR301 (Application for a Refund of Part XIII Tax Withheld) or Form NR302 (Application for a Reduced Rate of Part XIII Tax Withheld) and provide a certificate of residence from the treaty country's competent authority; under the Multilateral Instrument and US-Canada Protocol, a Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause must be satisfied. Since the underlying entity is an LP, the allocation of income to the foreign limited partner is direct business income and is taxed via a non-resident return for a permanent establishment, not via withholding; on actual cash distributions of previously taxed income no additional tax is withheld. To the extent the partnership pays fixed or determinable income such as rents, royalties, or interest to non-resident partners, the 25% withholding applies to those gross amounts unless a DTA reduces it.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Canadian transfer pricing documentation requirements under s.247(4) of the Income Tax Act apply to transactions between the taxpayer and a non-resident with whom the taxpayer is not at arm's length, regardless of a minimum transaction value, if the taxpayer (or a partnership of which the taxpayer is a member) is required to file a Canadian tax return. However, the CRA's administrative penalty waiver threshold, articulated in Information Circular IC87-2R and s.247(5), provides a 'cap' on penalties: the penalty for failing to make or obtain reasonable efforts to establish arm's length transfer prices on multiple transactions is computed as a percentage (10% of the net adjustment, plus 10% of the gross adjustment for multiple years) unless the aggregate amount of the gross transfer pricing adjustments for a taxation year is less than CAD 5 million or less than CAD 7.5 million for the cross-border group? Actually, the safe harbor threshold is CAD 1 million of associated-party gross revenue for a taxpayer with less than CAD 50 million in annual revenues; for those with revenues above CAD 50 million, the threshold is CAD 5 million. The CRA requires contemporaneous documentation (within 6 months of the tax filing) for each transaction. The mandated local file and master file thresholds in Canada are administrative: the CRA has adopted the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. For large taxpayers, CRA expects a transfer pricing policy and documentation; filing Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) is mandatory where the total value of reportable transactions exceeds CAD 10,000 (subject to exemptions); a failure to file T106 results in penalties under s.162(10.1) of CAD 100 per day (min CAD 1,000, max CAD 25,000). Under CRA's updated administrative guidelines (2021), taxpayers with revenues over CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions with associated enterprises above CAD 50 million may be asked for a master file; however, the statutory threshold for penalties for failure to provide documents under s.231.6 is CAD 1,000 per day for failure to respond to a requirement, plus judicial enforcement. For the LP specifically, the partnership is required to file T106 for any non-arm's length transactions with non-residents, and the partners are jointly and severally liable for penalties in proportion to their partnership allocation. The economic substance threshold is not a simple monetary amount but requires analysis of the functions performed, risks assumed, and assets used; the CRA applies the OECD refined framework to determine if the entity has substantive economic activity and a commercial rationale. The documentation must be in English or French and retained for six years after the fiscal year-end.
