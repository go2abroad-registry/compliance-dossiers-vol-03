Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification system. A Canadian limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes; no election exists to convert the LP into an entity-level taxed corporation. For GST/HST, the partnership is itself a separate 'person' and may elect under Excise Tax Act subsection 156(1) to treat eligible related registrants as a single registrant for filing and administrative purposes. Income allocations among partners may be adjusted under Income Tax Act subsection 103(2) with CRA consent. Anti-hybrid rules apply automatically; there is no bespoke hybrid mismatch election for passthrough partnerships.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold; worldwide taxable supplies in the current calendar quarter and after deducting the prior four consecutive calendar quarters do not exceed CAD 30,000 per Excise Tax Act subsections 148(2)-(4))

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for tangible personal property, including UHT plant-based oat milk products, supplied in Canada for export where the supplier or the supplier’s agent delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada and retains satisfactory evidence of export. Certain services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, but the service must be supplied to a non-resident who is outside Canada at all times and the service must be performed wholly or substantially outside Canada, or be ancillary to a zero-rated export. The supplier must keep contemporaneous documentation sufficient to demonstrate the physical export and the true consideration received.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or CBSA electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or foreign exchange/settlement advice confirming export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Evidence of physical export (terminal receipts, warehouse release, customs control documents)

No real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-stamping, or CRA API-based digital stamping is mandatory at the federal level for GST/HST. Registered suppliers must issue invoices containing the prescribed information under Excise Tax Act subsection 221(1), and electronic records must be complete and readable. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing initiative is based on Peppol and remains voluntary; there is no national mandate requiring pre-clearance of electronic invoices.

CRA may reassess and retroactively claw back zero-rating if satisfactory export evidence is not produced, converting the supplies to taxable 5% GST and charging interest from the original due date. Gross negligence penalties under Excise Tax Act section 280 may be imposed up to 50% of the tax sought to be evaded; willful evasion can lead to criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. The normal reassessment period is four years, but it is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect has occurred.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD, currency code 124) is a fully convertible currency on both current account and capital account transactions. Canada applies a liberalised post-Bretton Woods regime: there is no general approval requirement for inward investment, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation. However, significant non-Canadian acquisitions of control of Canadian businesses are reviewable under the Investment Canada Act, and a limited partnership with non-resident partners receiving Canadian-source income is subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act on certain amounts such as management fees, interest, royalties, and distributions characterised as dividends. A Canadian limited partnership that is properly structured with a Canadian general partner may be eligible for pass-through treatment, but non-resident limited partners can become exposed to Canadian withholding tax or deemed permanent establishment issues if the LP carries on business in Canada. Bank remittances for dividends and other distributions are not subject to exchange control, but must satisfy FINTRAC reporting at CAD 10,000 or above and the FATF travel rule for wire transfers. Profit and capital remittance is therefore operationally unrestricted, with the compliance burden focused on tax withholding, source-of-funds documentation, beneficial ownership disclosures, and Investment Canada Act notification thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters and trading entities may invoice in CAD, USD, EUR, or any agreed currency, may retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts within Canada or at approved foreign banks, and may convert into CAD at negotiated commercial rates. The applicable regulatory duties are (a) filing export declarations with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System for controlled or regulated goods, including agricultural and food products such as oat milk where CFIA labelling and plant-based food export certificates may apply; (b) reporting of cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, or foreign currency equivalent, to FINTRAC, including the originator and beneficial owner information under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; and (c) maintaining accounting records sufficient to support the underlying trade transaction for tax audits under the Income Tax Act. There is no prescribed statutory deadline within which export proceeds must be repatriated to Canada, and no percentage of export earnings must be converted into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Department of Finance Canada for cross-border payment and financial integrity policy. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control administration analogous to an emerging-market central bank; FX oversight arises principally through anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing obligations, investment and trade reporting, and prudential rules for authorised institutions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate tax rate under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 123(1); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and not subject to federal corporate tax, but each general and limited partner is taxed on its allocable share of Canadian-source business income at the applicable federal plus provincial general corporate or individual rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) for the deemed remittance of the non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source net income of the limited partnership; the rate is reduced to 5% or 15% under certain income tax treaties depending on the relevant percentage ownership in the partnership and applicable treaty article.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared for each cross-border transaction with non-arm's length non-residents exceeding this aggregate threshold per non-resident in a taxation year; Form T106, 'Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents', must be filed with the CRA even if the LP has no taxable income; for large Canadian taxpayers with consolidated revenue of CAD 50 million or more, a full Master File and Local File must be maintained in accordance with CRA Information Circular IC-87-2R.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the OECD Economic Substance Regulations in force in offshore jurisdictions, but the CRA and the Federal Court of Appeal may deny treaty benefits, apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), or disregard the partnership under s. 96(2) if the LP or the non-resident partner lacks genuine business purpose, employees, or operational assets in Canada. The Limited Partnership must have an actual manufacturing presence, including a UHT plant, licensed food-processing authorizations under the Safe Food for Canadians Act, and real employment to support its allocation of Canadian-source profits; a passive or shell LP in Canada will be subject to reassessment with penalties and interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act ss. 238 and 239, prosecution by indictment attracts a fine of up to CAD 250,000 for a first offence and up to CAD 1,000,000 for a second or subsequent offence, and imprisonment up to 5 years; on summary conviction the penalty is up to a CAD 25,000 fine and 6 months imprisonment; in addition, civil penalties under s. 163.2 may reach 100% of the tax evaded plus late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: CRA may issue a subsection 227.1(1) assessment against each director or senior officer of a corporate general partner who failed to exercise due diligence; the director is jointly and severally liable for the LP's unpaid taxes, withheld taxes, penalties, and interest, and the CRA may file a lien or garnishment against the individual; in fraudulent cases a ban on cross-border travel and an order prohibiting directors from leaving Canada may be sought under s. 226.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA applies GAAR under s. 245, which imposes a penalty of 50% of the tax benefit, plus interest and any gross negligence penalty under s. 163.2 if there is contemporaneous evidence of scheming; the courts can also disallow the entire partnership allocation and require the same tax to be paid as if the transaction had never occurred.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in the design or authorization of a sham partnership may be personally liable for restitution to the Crown for unpaid corporate tax where the LP was a sham under corporate-law veil-piercing principles; upon criminal conviction, a court may order restitution under s. 739(1) of the Criminal Code, prohibit serving as director under s. 220.1 CBCA, and impose an administrative travel ban furthered by subsection 227(5) of the Income Tax Act.
AML / Money Laundering / Unlicensed Cross-Border Transaction Flows

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), non-compliance with reporting, record keeping, and compliance program requirements may lead to an administrative monetary penalty of up to CAD 1,000,000 for a financial entity and up to CAD 2,000,000 on prosecution by indictment for serious offenders; penalties are issued to the Person and to every partner who participated, and the courts may order forfeiture of the profits.

Director personal liability: The LP's general partner and its directors as 'persons in charge' may be investigated by FINTRAC; officers can be personally fined up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisoned for both a federal offence and for a violation of the Corruption of Foreign Public Officials Act if false books or criminal payments are used; a director may also be personally liable for unpaid AML penalties under successor corporate-type liability if the entity is a corporate general partner with no assets.
