Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; business portal: My Business Account (CRA ‘My BA’) and the GST/HST NETFILE / GST/HST Registry.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not otherwise carrying on business in Canada must register with the CRA through Form RC1 (‘Request for a Business Number’) or through the online Business Registration service, submitting the entity’s proof of legal existence, foreign registration/TIN details, and Canadian resident agent contact information. The CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST account number where registration is required. Non-resident partnerships and limited partnerships may obtain a BN and GST/HST account; form RC4455 or RC4607 may be relevant depending on the entity’s activities. The LP must also provide a completed Schedule 50 for Canadian income tax filings if Canadian-source income is expected.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity not otherwise carrying on business in Canada must register with the CRA through Form RC1 (‘Request for a Business Number’) or through the online Business Registration service, submitting the entity’s proof of legal existence, foreign registration/TIN details, and Canadian resident agent contact information. The CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST account number where registration is required. Non-resident partnerships and limited partnerships may obtain a BN and GST/HST account; form RC4455 or RC4607 may be relevant depending on the entity’s activities. The LP must also provide a completed Schedule 50 for Canadian income tax filings if Canadian-source income is expected.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Canadian Income Tax Act, a limited partnership is generally a fiscally transparent partnership for federal income tax purposes; no ‘check-the-box’ election exists analogous to the US. However, the partners may elect, under subsection 96(8) or by filing a prescribed form, to have the partnership’s fiscal period and allocation of income computed on a specified basis. For GST/HST purposes, a qualifying partnership may elect under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to have the partnership itself, rather than the partners, register and report. Non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding rules, and hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.1 and 18.2 may apply to certain cross-border arrangements involving partnerships.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies made in Canada in the preceding four calendar quarters (or at any time where reasonable expectation to exceed threshold), being the small-supplier threshold under the Excise Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Canada are zero-rated as ‘zero-rated exports’ under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supplier maintains documentary evidence that the goods are exported by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for export within prescribed timeframes. Services performed on goods destined for export and certain offshore services provided to non-residents may qualify as zero-rated. The action figure mecha model kit goods must be shipped from Canada to a foreign destination and title/risk of loss must pass to the non-resident purchaser; the supplier must retain proof of export sufficient to satisfy CRA’s Section 223 of the ETA and ETA Form GST66.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13 / CAED or electronic export reporting via CBSA’s Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/financial receipt demonstrating foreign proceeds received in convertible currency and traceable to the export sale

No federal real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance/digital stamping mandate. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically for registrants with annual revenues above CAD 1.5 million; CRA has introduced digital reporting and optional e-invoicing through the ‘Business Gateway’ API for GST/HST returns and assessments. There is no mandatory CRA pre-clearance of invoices; taxpayers must retain prescribed books and records, and zero-rated export substantiation may be audited through CRA’s e-services.

CRA may deny zero-rating and assess unreported GST/HST, plus arrears interest and penalties, if export substantiation is inadequate or false; penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a penalty of CAD 500 or 8% of the amount assessed (if due to negligence) and up to 250% of the tax avoided for gross negligence; persons who knowingly make false statements may face criminal prosecution under section 312 of the ETA and Income Tax Act, with fines of up to CAD 50,000 and/or imprisonment. Retroactive tax assessments can be issued within a 4-year normal reassessment period, extended to 8 years for misrepresentation, and no time limit where fraudulent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1952, and the wartime Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951; the CAD trades freely with no restriction on inbound or outbound transfers in any currency. Profit remittance from a Canadian-resident LP: non-resident partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their share of Canadian-source business income carried on through the partnership and must file Canadian returns; Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced) applies only to interest, rents, royalties and management fees, not to business-income partnership distributions. The practical remittance hurdles are documentary only: CRA Business Number, T5013 information return and partner allocations, non-resident filings, and a clean audit trail of capital-account funding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No currency surrender or mandatory conversion obligation exists. Exporters may retain export proceeds in CAD or foreign currency in any Canadian-chartered bank account and convert at market rates. Statutory duties are limited to: (i) CBSA electronic export declarations (G13) under the Customs Act for goods valued above CAD 2,000; (ii) FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) CCMIR written declaration for physical crossing of the border with currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more. None of these rules imposes repatriation or conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential regulation of banks delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); anti-money-laundering supervision by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17). Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate policy; there is no FX-control authority, no exchange-control licence requirement, and no approval requirement for current or capital transactions.

Late filing: 5% of the unpaid balance due plus 1% per full month, capped at 12 months; repeated or gross negligence misstatements can attract a 50% penalty; intentional non-filing creates criminal liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents triggers Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s. 247(4); C$750,000,000 or more consolidated group revenue triggers Country-by-Country Reporting obligations.

Economic substance scope: Canada's anti-avoidance doctrine is the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s. 245, which disregards transactions lacking genuine economic substance and bona fide business purposes. There is no separate Economic Substance Regulations decree as in some jurisdictions. The physical injection-molding plant, employees, steel inventories, and mold tooling constitute robust economic substance; an LP holding only contracts without operational activity would be vulnerable to GAAR recharacterization and monetary penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Non-filing of T5013

Statutory sanction: Civil gross negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2): 50% of the understatement amount. Criminal summary conviction: fine of C$10,000 to C$50,000 and up to two years imprisonment. Indictable conviction: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and up to five years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: If the LP's general partner is a corporation, directors may be personally liable for unremitted tax withholdings or GST/HST under ITA s. 227.1; fraudulent misrepresentation or conspiracy exposes directors to civil restitution, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
Transfer-Pricing Misreporting and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under s. 247(2) to an arm's-length amount; penalty under s. 247(3) equals 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment; late T106 filing penalty is C$25/day (minimum C$100, maximum C$2,500).

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to liability if they personally orchestrate a fraudulent mispricing scheme; the corporate general partner may be assessed the 10% penalty; directors who fail to remit payroll/GST/HST are jointly liable, though transfer-pricing tax itself is not a director liability absent willful misrepresentation.
Abusive Tax Avoidance / GAAR (Shell Limited Partnership)

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s. 245 voids the tax benefit and authorizes reassessment; the 2023 amendment imposes an additional 25% penalty on the avoided-benefit amount; CRA can apply interest, and repeated abusive transactions can attract criminal exposure.

Director personal liability: Where a court pierces the LP veil, the general partner and its directors may be liable to restore diverted funds and pay penalties; travel may be restricted by court order during fraud or insolvency examination; imprisonment is possible in egregious cases.
AML / FINTRAC Reporting Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 for a legal person; Criminal Code money-laundering conviction can yield a C$1,000,000 fine and up to 10 years imprisonment; asset seizure and freeze orders under proceeds-of-crime instruments.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable where they knowingly direct or participate in laundering; prosecutorial agencies may obtain travel bans, asset freezes, and restitution orders; CRA and FINTRAC coordinate with CBSA at ports of exit.
Part XIII Withholding Evasion on Non-Resident Royalties/Interest/Service Fees

Statutory sanction: CRA assesses the Canadian payor for the full withheld amount under ITA s. 227(8), plus daily interest on arrears and penalties under s. 227(9); treaty benefits may be denied retroactively if NR301 fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholdings under ITA s. 227.1 after CRA registration of the assessment; civil recovery can freeze personal accounts; criminal conviction follows synthetic circumvention.
