Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); corporate register: Corporations Canada (https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en); export/customs authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) through Corporations Canada and obtain the certificate of incorporation and corporate key. Step 2: Register for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1. Step 3: Activate program accounts: Corporation Income Tax (RC0001), GST/HST (RT0001), Import/Export (RM0001) for CBSA linkage, Payroll (RP0001) if applicable. Step 4: Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST once gross taxable supply revenue in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters; use Form GST1 or the simplified non-resident registration stream for digital/e-commerce supplies. Step 5: Maintain the CBCA individuals-with-significant-control (ISC) register, file annual corporate returns, and appoint a Canadian representative for service of process if not resident in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. A CBCA business corporation is a separate taxpayer resident in Canada for Part I income tax purposes (treaty tie-breakers may override); it is taxed on worldwide income, while non-resident corporations are taxed only on Canadian-source business and property income (net basis) plus Part XIII withholding at 25% reduced by treaty; no pass-through classification exists for corporations, and no tax-exempt zone status applies to online resale activities.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Available federal elections include: (i) ITA s. 256(9) deemed non-control election to shed Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status and related small-business deduction limits; (ii) ITA s. 216 election for non-residents to report Canadian rental income on a net basis instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (iii) GST/HST elections under ETA: quick method (s. 229), annual filing (s. 238), and election for selected listed financial institutions (s. 225.2) where relevant; (iv) ITA s. 247(4) compensating transfer-pricing adjustment election; (v) hybrid mismatch characterization elections under ITA ss. 18.2/18.3, which recharacterize deduction/timing outcomes for cross-border arrangements rather than alter entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - gross worldwide taxable supplies (including deemed supplies) in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once this small-supplier threshold is exceeded (ETA s. 240); non-resident suppliers of digital products/services to Canadian recipients use the simplified registration stream under the same CAD 30,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible personal property (including vintage luxury goods) is zero-rated under item 1, Part V, Schedule VI ETA where the registrant sells the goods for export, the goods are exported as soon as reasonably possible by the supplier or an agent, and the supplier holds export evidence; deliveries to a common carrier for foreign destination also qualify. Zero-rating of services/digital intangibles supplied to non-residents applies under Part V, Schedule VII unless the recipient's primary benefit is in Canada or the service relates to Canadian real property or goods located in Canada. The supplier must remit net tax accordingly and retain documentary proof of export before the CRA will accept a refund/rebate of related ITCs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA export declaration filed via Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for exports over CAD 2,000 or controlled goods at any value)
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill/courier consignment note) evidencing foreign destination and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank/FX settlement evidence, e.g., SWIFT MT103 or escrow payout record, demonstrating receipt of the export consideration from the foreign buyer)

Canada operates no real-time continuous-transaction-control (CTC) e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API mandate; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE/TEI) where taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5M, and payments above CAD 10,000 must be made electronically; exports to CBSA require electronic CERS/eManifest filing for shipments valued above CAD 2,000 and for controlled goods at any value; CRA mandates auditable electronic recordkeeping (ETA s. 286, ITA s. 230), including support for CRA web-services APIs for return/payment integration, but no fiscal e-invoice repository is operated.

CRA may redetermine GST/HST liability within the standard 4-year assessment period (extended to 10 years where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, willful default, or fraud in filing) and claw back zero-rating with arrears interest at prescribed rates. Penalties include ETA s. 285.1 gross negligence (greater of CAD 100 and 50% of net understatement), ETA s. 280 late-filing (1% of net tax plus 0.25% per full month, capped at 8%), ETA s. 280.1 failure-to-file information (CAD 250 up to CAD 1,000), ETA s. 327 criminal evasion (fines up to CAD 25,000 and/or 5 years imprisonment); ITA s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty (50% of understated tax) and ITA s. 247(3) transfer-pricing penalty (10% of adjustment with contemporaneous documentation, 50% without, minimum CAD 1,000); although Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, sustained non-repatriation beyond 180 days is an audit red flag triggering transfer-pricing review, deemed dividend/benefit assessments, and possible forfeiture of zero-rating evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full currency convertibility under both current and capital account provisions for CAD and all reserve currencies. There are no statutory restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distributions, or repatriation of invested capital for resident or non-resident shareholders. Transactions are subject only to ordinary tax withholding, FINTRAC 24-hour reporting for receive/less-than-commercial payments above CAD 10,000, and anti-money laundering due diligence standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandated conversion, or holding-period constraint on export receipts. Exporters may hold foreign currency proceeds freely in domestic or offshore accounts, subject to Canadian tax reporting for controlled foreign affiliates and financial statement classification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Bank for international operation policy) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border transaction reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium - Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) and neo-banks cannot operate as standalone banks in Canada without a bank charter. They may provide cross-border payment and FX solutions through a regulated Canadian MSB partnership, which requires FINTRAC registration, under prevailing AML protocols. Legal constraints include client fund safeguarding limitations, no deposit insurance (CDIC) coverage, and mandatory fund routing through a chartered Canadian bank for escrow or settlement activity.
