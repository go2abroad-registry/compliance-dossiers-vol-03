Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); online portal: CRA My Business Account — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a statutory Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing a paper application with the relevant tax services office. The BN, supplemented by program accounts, functions as the Canadian corporate TIN. For federal corporation income tax, the non-resident entity must register for an RC program account and file a T2 return if it carries on business in Canada. For GST/HST, the entity must register for an RT program account through the same BN process when it is not a small supplier (i.e. worldwide taxable supply revenue exceeds C$30,000) and makes taxable supplies from or in Canada. Non-resident digital service suppliers may use CRA's simplified GST/HST registration procedure. The CRA requires certified corporate documents from the foreign jurisdiction, registered office/agent details, and evidence of authority for the Canadian representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a check-the-box or pass-through election regime. A corporation incorporated federally is a resident taxpayer by default and is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income, subject to treaty tie-breaker rules. Non-resident corporations are taxed on Canadian-source income with no elective classification override. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act s. 18.6 may deny deductions involving hybrid entities or hybrid transfers and do not provide elective carve-outs. Limited statutory elections exist for fiscal period selection under s. 249.1 and for certain Part I returns by non-residents, but none alter entity-level tax treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (small supplier threshold); compulsory GST/HST registration if a non-resident carries on business in Canada and exceeds this threshold. Non-resident digital economy suppliers to Canadian consumers are subject to simplified registration with a similar C$30,000 Canadian-source revenue threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of programmatic ad exchange/SSP services to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 if the recipient is not registered under GST/HST and the service is not in respect of real property in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or intangible personal property for use in Canada. Services delivered electronically from Canada to foreign trade counterparties with documented non-resident counterparties generally qualify. Goods exports are eligible for zero-rating when the supplier exports the goods on a shipment-by-shipment basis and retains prescribed documentary proof declared to CRA.

  • For goods: CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) or eManifest export declaration number
  • Commercial invoice and export/supply contract
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or Cargo Control Document
  • Bank payment evidence (SWIFT MT103, FX conversion confirmation, or inward remittance certificate)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or a national continuous transaction control (CTC) system. E-invoices are accepted for GST/HST compliance provided they satisfy CRA record retention requirements. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA Business Number portals. PEPPOL standards may apply to public-sector e-procurement, but this is procurement interoperability, not a tax clearance requirement.

CRA audits may test whether the entity has contemporaneous proof of foreign provision, foreign non-registration status, and payment for exported services. Missing documentation or payment received after the 180-day benchmark without commercial justification can cause zero-rating to be denied, leading to reassessment, interest, cancellation of input tax credits, and potential clawback of refunds. Penalties for false statements under the Excise Tax Act can be up to 25% of the tax at issue, gross-negligence penalties up to 50%, and willful evasion may result in criminal fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Canada imposes no foreign-exchange controls, no capital-account approval thresholds for inward equity investment, and no restrictions on maintaining foreign-currency deposits in Canadian Schedule I banks or on repatriating profits abroad. Profit remittance frictions are purely tax and compliance driven: a 25% Part XIII dividend withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally reduced to 0-15% under an applicable income tax treaty), the anti-money-laundering and large-transaction reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and sanctions screening requirements applicable to all OSFI-regulated financial institutions. None of these frictions abridge the lawful convertibility of CAD or the free movement of capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: not applicable. Mandatory conversion ratio: none. Canada does not impose any export-proceeds surrender, forced conversion, or minimum holding period for foreign-currency receipts. The Canadian entity may retain export revenues in CAD or any foreign currency, in Canada or abroad, indefinitely. For this AdTech DSP/SSP programmatic advertising platform, cross-border revenue from foreign exchanges and demand sources is not subject to repatriation timing rules. The only applicable duties are general tax filing obligations (foreign accrual rules, GST/HST on Canadian services) and PCMLTFA reporting thresholds for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority and issuance entity for the Canadian dollar; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury repository, Medium as a supplementary payment-facilitation rail. Legal constraints: there is no EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) regime in Canada, and no offshore EMI passporting arrangement exists. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully take deposits or issue electronic money in Canada unless it is authorized by OSFI or properly registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under PCMLTFA, and, depending on the province, licensed as an MSB by the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) in Quebec or under the Money Services Business Act in British Columbia. Reliance on an unregistered offshore EMI for main operating liquidity is inadvisable: Canadian clearing banks will typically reject or suspend transfers sourced from unregulated payment intermediaries. The recommended architecture uses a foreign EMI only for small-value cross-border receipts, with all material balances swept to a Schedule I Canadian bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity funding is routed by SWIFT MT103 wire from the parent or shareholder's bank to the Corporation's Canadian bank account. The wire's field 70 remittance information should state 'Equity Capital Contribution' or 'Share Subscription - CAD' to differentiate the injection from trading revenue. No foreign-investment authorization, exchange-control permit, or regulatory pre-approval is required in Canada for a non-resident capital injection. CAD-denominated settlement occurs over Lynx, Payments Canada's real-time gross settlement system, or a correspondent bank's overnight clearing arrangement. The receiving bank must verify the remitter's identity and source of funds; wires of CAD 10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC. For injections exceeding CAD 100,000, the bank will typically require a board resolution, the subscription agreement, pro forma financial statements, and a director's certificate confirming the corporate capacity of the recipient.Outbound trade and vendor disbursements are executed through SWIFT MT103 high-value wires for non-resident counterparties, cleared via the correspondent banking network, using CHIPS or Fedwire for USD and Lynx for CAD; recurring small-value payments to US sub-publishers and data vendors are effected through ACH (US ACH for USD, ACSS Automated Clearing Settlement Service for CAD). Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary LC under UCP600) and standby LCs are permissible for material media-acquisition contracts requiring documentary security. Each outbound payment should include the invoice number and campaign reference in the remittance field, and the beneficiary's full BIC/SWIFT, account number or IBAN, and clearing address must be validated to prevent misdirection.
