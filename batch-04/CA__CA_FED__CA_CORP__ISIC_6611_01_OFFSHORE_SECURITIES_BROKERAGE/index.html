Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA); provincial tax authorities apply for QST and provincial retail sales taxes where the entity carries on business through a permanent establishment in a province.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA by registering through the Business Registration Online portal, by completing and filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number', or through an authorized third-party registrant. The BN is a unique 9-digit identifier with program accounts, including the GST/HST account and income tax account. Registration becomes mandatory when the entity is no longer a 'small supplier', the standard being CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies worldwide over any trailing 12-month period, or when the entity is required to register under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act. For a federal corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act, the Corporations Canada record can be linked to the CRA, but the corporation must still file Form RC1 or register the BN accounts. A non-resident securities broker must also verify whether it is deemed to carry on business in Canada through solicitation by an agent or representative, which triggers the registration duty, and must appoint an address for service in Canada and, where necessary, a GST/HST agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a Canadian Business Corporation is categorically an entity-level taxpayer for federal and provincial income tax. Relevant statutory elections and elections of convenience include the following: (i) the functional currency reporting election under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act; (ii) the associated-corporations election to reallocate the small business deduction; (iii) the subsection 216(1) election for a non-resident to pay tax on net Canadian rental income; (iv) the section 115.2 election to treat a non-resident with Canadian-source business income as taxable on a net basis with the same credits and deductions as a resident; (v) the GST/HST joint election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act, permitting closely related corporations to be treated as a single registrant; (vi) the section 177 election treating a taxable supply between closely related registrants as made for no consideration; and (vii) the documentation-based zero-rating treatment for financial services supplied to non-residents under section 7.1 of Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. No hybrid mismatch election exists: Canada applies mandatory hybrid mismatch rules under sections 18.4 and following of the Income Tax Act, and a corporate taxpayer cannot elect out of those rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies worldwide in any trailing 12-month period; below this amount the entity is a small supplier and is not required to register for GST/HST. A retail securities broker supplying only exempt financial services normally has no mandatory registration, but registration may be elective to recover input tax credits where zero-rated supplies are made. A non-resident that carries on business in Canada, solicits orders through an agent, or operates a Canadian permanent establishment is treated as making supplies in Canada and the ordinary threshold applies with added deemed-nexus rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, a registrant zero-rates goods exported from Canada under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI when it retains proof of export. Financial services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated under section 7.1 of Part V of Schedule VI where the supply is made to a person who is not a resident of Canada and the service is not for consumption in Canada or is not in respect of goods or real property located in Canada. For an offshore securities brokerage under ISIC 6611, commission income derived from executing trades, clearing, custody, and margin services for non-resident clients may qualify as a zero-rated financial service if the following criteria are satisfied: the recipient is a non-resident at the time of the supply; the service is provided exclusively from a Canadian or foreign broking desk without physical touchpoints in Canada; the trade execution, market access, and settlement occur on offshore markets; and contemporaneous documentary evidence evidences the offshore destination of the service. Fee-based investment advice or account administration for Canadian-resident clients remains taxable or exempt as the facts require. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for zero-rating, but the CRA expects export documentation and payment evidence to be assembled within a 180-day period after the transaction for audit certainty; failure to maintain the documentation will defeat the zero-rated treatment. A registrant that zero-rates a financial service may claim input tax credits and file for a net refund; the refund claim is substantively governed by the export documentation bundle described below.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or Canadian Export Declaration (CAED) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill for goods; for financial services, a detailed executed service agreement and commission invoice issued to the non-resident client
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103/MT202 records evidencing foreign-currency receipt from the non-resident client
  • Non-resident status evidence, including passport copies, certificate of incorporation, or residence certificate issued by the client's foreign tax authority
  • Brokerage contract, order execution tickets, clearing statements, and custodial records demonstrating that the service was performed for an offshore account
  • Foreign-exchange and settlement reports matching the contract value and payment date with the order tickets and segregated client account records

Canada does not operate a federal real-time fiscal clearance system with digital stamping; there is no API-based tax clearance gateway for invoices. The CRA requires GST/HST registrants to issue and retain invoices containing prescribed information, file returns electronically or by paper, and preserve electronic books and records for six years. The Pan-Canadian e-invoice framework based on Peppol BIS 3.0 is being phased in, but as of the current compliance year there is no statutory mandate for universal e-invoicing or online tax stamping. For a high-risk securities brokerage, the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization requires electronic trade records, order routing logs, and client account documents to be preserved in a retrievable format, but no CRA fiscal clearance applies to those records.

The CRA may reassess a GST/HST return within four years of the filing date, and without any time limitation where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful neglect is established. A disallowed zero-rating claim results in full tax reassessment, statutory interest under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, and penalty exposure. Knowingly making, or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence making, a false statement in a return, certificate, or application attracts a penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act of 25% of the tax attributable to the false statement, subject to specified minimums. Wilful evasion is an offence under section 327, punishable by a fine, by imprisonment for up to five years, or both, and a conviction does not excuse the underlying tax or interest. The Income Tax Act imposes parallel penalties under section 163 and criminal sanctions under section 239. Directors and officers can be held jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST under subsection 323 of the Excise Tax Act. The CRA's offshore compliance projects and the OECD automatic exchange of information network specifically target cross-border securities brokerage, suspected artificial export arrangements, and fabricated non-resident status; when the invoice or the 180-day documentation bundle is incomplete, retrospective clawback of the zero-rating plus penalties is the normal outcome.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full and free convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) for both current account and capital account transactions under its IMF Article VIII obligations. There is no general exchange control law or requirement for central bank approval to convert, remit, or repatriate funds. Dividends, management fees, royalties, and other profit remittances to non-residents are permitted without any prior authorization or conversion restriction. However, reporting obligations exist: statutory reporting under the Income Tax Act (e.g., Form T106 for certain non-resident transactions), FATCA/CRS reporting thresholds, and FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers (ETFs) over CAD 10,000 in a 24-hour period. No surrender or repatriation requirements are imposed on capital inflows or outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into CAD. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in freely denominated offshore or onshore accounts and use them for settlement without central bank approval. The only applicable compliance measures are standard customs declaration, GST/HST reporting, and anti-money laundering documentation requirements under PCMLTFA for any cross-border fund movements exceeding the prescribed threshold.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with delegated cross-border transaction oversight to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low to medium viability for non-bank foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) and neo-banks. Digital-only providers such as Wise, Revolut Business, or similar EMIs may offer convenient multi-currency settlement and lower cost FX, but they are not Schedule I Canadian banks and cannot serve as the primary custodian for client trust funds, securities, or margin balances. For an offshore retail securities broker registered under ISIC 6611, Canadian securities regulatory rules and CIRO (formerly IIROC) requirements mandate that client cash be held in a segregated trust account at a Canadian bank or an approved financial institution. EMIs can be used only for pre-funded operational expense accounts, treasury visibility, or third-party disbursement facilitation, provided the EMI has a Canadian Money Service Business (MSB) registration and AML/CFT compliance. Legal constraints also include the prohibition on deposit-taking without a Bank Act authorization, meaning EMIs generally cannot offer CDIC-insured deposit accounts or settle securities trades through CDS directly.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital wire transfers must be routed through SWIFT MT103 or domestic LVTS/ ACSS rails using the beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC and a unique corporate reference. The payment purpose code should be stated in the transaction narrative as 'Capital Injection — Equity Subscription / Share Capital Contribution' for the Canadian receiving bank. The corporate legal name, registered office address, and corporate registration number must be present in the order. For securities broker capitalization, the incoming funds should be credited to a capital escrow account and be traceable to the exact shareholder of record, with supporting board resolutions, signed subscription agreements, and proof of source of funds available for FINTRAC and bank AML compliance. For funds from an affiliated offshore entity, the Canadian broker must prepare a T106 disclosure if applicable, and the bank must ensure no third-party obfuscation. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate under ITA s.123(1); provincial corporate rates range from 8% to 16% (e.g., Ontario 11.5%, British Columbia 12%, Alberta 8%); combined federal plus Ontario effective rate is approximately 26.5%. A foreign-controlled brokerage corporation is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) and is therefore ineligible for the 9% federal small business deduction.
