Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); self-service portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); GST/HST e-filing via GST/HST NETFILE and Business Registration Online (BRO).

E-government filing portal

A Canadian Limited Partnership (LP) is treated as a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes and as a flow-through vehicle for income tax. (1) Determine whether the LP carries on business in Canada or makes taxable supplies in Canada; the $30,000 CAD small-supplier threshold in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters triggers mandatory GST/HST registration. (2) Apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or via BRO; the GST/HST program account receives an RT suffix. (3) For a non-resident partnership without Canadian physical presence, engage a Canadian representative or file through the CRA non-resident services; address the requirement to post security (RC1 interpretation) where CRA deems collection at risk. (4) Concurrently register for the provincial income tax program account (e.g., Quebec Revenu Québec if carrying on business in QC; otherwise the federal BN suffices) and, if the LP has a permanent establishment in an HST province, account for HST accordingly. (5) The partnership itself must file the T5013 Partnership Information Return where it earns Canadian-source income or carries on business in Canada; each partner reports allocated income using their own tax identifier (SIN for individuals; BN for corporate partners).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax — income, gains, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners under the partnership agreement, subject to subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST, the partnership is a distinct registrant (person) separate from its partners. Territorial scope: Canadian-source business income carried on through a permanent establishment (PE) is taxable; non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source income via Part XIII withholding or Part I tax on PE income, with no federal branch tax on partnerships, but applicable provincial allocations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax — income, gains, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners under the partnership agreement, subject to subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST, the partnership is a distinct registrant (person) separate from its partners. Territorial scope: Canadian-source business income carried on through a permanent establishment (PE) is taxable; non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source income via Part XIII withholding or Part I tax on PE income, with no federal branch tax on partnerships, but applicable provincial allocations. Grain/soybean/corn brokerage is not a tax-exempt activity; no tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' regime; entity classification follows the legal form of the partnership under provincial law (limited partnership is per se flow-through). Available elections include: (a) partnership fiscal year-end election under subsection 249.1(4) — an off-calendar year-end (e.g., January 31) may be elected by Canadian-resident partners to defer income; (b) the GST/HST quick method election (section 229, Excise Tax Act) permitting simplified output tax remittance at reduced effective rates, with a 1% credit on qualifying purchases; (c) the section 156 election for closely related corporations/partnerships to treat supplies as made for nil consideration for GST/HST; (d) the de minimis/hybrid mismatch rule under section 18.6 of the Income Tax Act excluding deduction for amounts arising in hybrid mismatch arrangements, and the election to treat a partnership as a 'reporting entity' for the purpose of the 'structured arrangement' carve-out — foreign partners should assess rules under sections 18.4 and 18.5 (hybrid mismatch) to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in annual worldwide taxable supplies from carrying on business in Canada (or $30,000 CAD in a single calendar quarter); voluntary registration permitted; a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada without PE must be registered even below threshold where CRA requires security

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports of goods is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (section 1 — goods exported by the supplier) and for services supplied to a non-resident (section 7 — but not where the service is performed in Canada in respect of real property). Bulk soybeans and yellow corn sold under a vessel brokerage agreement qualify for 0% GST/HST if: (i) title passes outside Canada, (ii) the grain is exported in substantially the same state, and (iii) the broker/principal retains documentary proof of export. Commissions earned by the LP for arranging ocean carriage of soybeans/corn from Canadian ports to a non-resident buyer qualify for zero-rating as a service supplied to a non-resident, provided the service is not delivered to a person in Canada and is not in respect of goods situated in Canada at the time of supply (careful analysis required: if the commission relates to goods that remain in Canada at supply, the supply is taxable; therefore the LP should ensure contractual language establishes the supply of the brokerage service as occurring when goods cross the border or when risk passes to the foreign buyer). Where zero-rating is denied, GST/HST at 5% applies; HST province additions (e.g., 13% Ontario, 15% Prince Edward Island) apply only where the supply is made in a participating province (s. 144.1)

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CES (Canadian Export Reporting System) transaction record confirming actual export of the soybeans/corn
  • Bills of lading (ocean master and negotiable/order bill) and corresponding shipping instructions/data (e.g., Fiata FCR, mate’s receipt)
  • Commercial invoice and sales/purchase agreement evidencing transfer of title to a non-resident buyer
  • Inward remittance certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103/FX conversion evidence demonstrating conversion of export proceeds to CAD and repatriation into Canada
  • Vessel nomination and freight/settlement documents (charterparty fixture recap, demurrage/despatch agreement) for the bulk cargoes

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. Statutory e-filing mandate applies to GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding $1,000,000 CAD (must file electronically). Peak Adoption of the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoice standard (voluntary B2B, phased federal adoption) is increasing, but there is no API-based clearance obligation or CRA pre-approval of invoice stamps. Registrants must retain electronic records supporting invoices (section 1, s. 286 of Excise Tax Act, and ETA section 287). Use of EDIFACT/XML for B2B cross-border grain transactions is commercially customary but not statutorily mandated.

CRA generally audits export zero-rating claims on a two-year (normal reassessment) to four-year (ordinary) window; where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established, reassessment is unlimited by statute and penalties escalate. Gross negligence penalty under subsection 285.1(2) of the Excise Tax Act is 25% of the amount of tax sought to be evaded (or reassessed); false statements or omissions carry a penalty of C$2,000 plus 25% of the tax; plus compound daily interest. Retrospective clawbacks apply where the exporter fails to retain required export documentation for six years (s. 286, ETA); the zero-rated supply is re-taxed at 5% (or applicable HST) with interest and penalties. Cross-border grain referral cases attract enhanced CRA enforcement through the GST/HST Large Business and International Directorate; CRA may also disallow input tax credits claimed on purchases supporting zero-rated exports if no valid export evidence is retained.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a liberalized foreign exchange regime with full current account convertibility for trade-related transactions including commodity imports and exports. Capital account transactions are subject to prior Bank of Canada approval only for investments exceeding CAD 1 million per transaction or certain portfolio investments. Profit remittances from operations are unrestricted provided corporate tax filings and beneficial ownership disclosures are complete.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds required under Canadian law. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in designated accounts. All FX transactions must be reported to the Bank of Canada via authorized dealers if exceeding reporting thresholds; no conversion ratios apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Low viability for offshore digital EMIs due to FINTRAC registration and Bank of Canada oversight requirements.
