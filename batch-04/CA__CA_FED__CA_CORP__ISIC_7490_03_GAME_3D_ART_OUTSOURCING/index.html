Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), administered through Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MBA), and GST/HST NETFILE; customs border services are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Customs Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially under applicable corporations legislation; Step 2: Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with program accounts by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts) via BRO, fax, or mail; Step 3: Open an RT (GST/HST) account if taxable supplies of the corporation in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or voluntarily register to recover input tax credits on zero-rated export supplies; Step 4: Open an RM (Import/Export) account with the CBSA/CRA where physical media or tangible deliverables cross the border; Step 5: Open an RP payroll account if Canadian employees are engaged; non-resident corporations may register directly through the CRA non-resident stream and must designate a Canadian agent for service where required; the CRA issues a written notice of confirmation containing the BN and RT program account to be quoted on all returns, invoices, and customs filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian-resident business corporation is a separate taxable corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act, equivalent to a C corporation for tax purposes; no pass-through or S-corp-type classification exists at the federal level; the corporation is taxed on world-wide income with foreign tax credit relief under section 126, and provincial corporate tax is allocated by permanent establishment formula in the province(s) of business; for GST/HST purposes, the corporation is a distinct registrant and all domestic taxable supplies are subject to GST/HST unless zero-rated or exempt.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election mechanism; relevant statutory elections and mandatory hybrid rules include: (i) the GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) available to registrants with annual taxable supplies of CAD 400,000 or less; (ii) voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier to claim input tax credits attributable to zero-rated export supplies; (iii) capital dividend account and paid-up capital reduction elections under subsections 83(2), 86(1), and 88(1) of the Income Tax Act when structuring shareholder distributions; and (iv) Canada's hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act and Regulations, aligning with BEPS Action 2, which apply mandatorily to cross-border branch deduction/omission mismatches (effective 2021) and import mismatches (effective 2023) and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); once the threshold is exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory and takes effect on the date the threshold was crossed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, the supply of custom 3D game art assets, character rigs, textures, and related digital files is zero-rated where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered under the Act; (ii) the supply is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada; and (iii) the service is performed and delivered digitally outside Canada or the intangible personal property is assigned or licensed to the non-resident; physical exports of goods also qualify at 0% under Part V of Schedule VI if exported pursuant to a sale contract and supported by CBSA export documentation; sufficient substantiation requires a written contract evidencing the offshore recipient, digital delivery or shipment records, evidence of non-resident status, and corresponding payment receipts.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A filed via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System) for any physical media shipped, or a digital delivery log and intellectual property assignment ledger for purely electronic game asset uploads
  • Commercial invoice or service contract quoting the exporter's GST/HST BN and expressly stating that the supply is zero-rated under Schedule VI, with full description of the 3D art deliverables, licensing terms, and non-resident purchaser details
  • Inward remittance certificate, direct wire transfer credit advice, or bank statement evidencing FX repatriation, accompanied by the non-resident buyer's status certificate (foreign corporate registry extract, W-8 series form, or tax residence certificate)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system, digital stamping regime, or mandatory continuous control transaction (CCT) engine; electronic invoicing is voluntary and PEPPOL-based interoperability has been adopted as a shared standard; registrants must issue valid GST/HST invoices containing the supplier's legal/trading name, BN and RT account, date of supply, description, and tax status or a zero-rated notation; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE where annual gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,500,000; electronic records and invoices must be retained in legible accessible format for six years from the end of the relevant fiscal year.

CRA may reassess beyond the ordinary four-year limitation period and retrospectively claw back zero-rated treatment where export documentation is missing, misleading, or fraudulent; a denied claim results in net tax reassessment, arrears interest at the prescribed rate (enhanced by up to 4 percentage points for large corporate arrears), and a gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the additional tax assessed; wilful evasion is prosecutable under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code, carrying fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment for up to five years; section 323 imposes joint and several director liability for unpaid GST/HST, interest, and penalties; CRA may also suspend refund programs and impose a fresh eight-year audit window on repeat offenders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full CAD and USD convertibility under a free-floating exchange-rate regime consistent with IMF Article VIII commitments. Canada maintains no general exchange-control legislation, no capital-account authorization regime, no profit-repatriation permit system, and no restrictions on dividend or management-fee outflows. The Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) permits unrestricted capital base; non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (25% statutory rate, treaty-reduced to 5% for a treaty parent corporation holding 25% or more of voting shares and 15% for portfolio shareholders under the Canada-US Treaty) is the only meaningful remittance hurdle and is administrative in nature rather than discretionary. OSFI does not apply convertibility limits, and the Bank of Canada's Overnight Rate only influences the marginal cost of CAD liquidity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Receipts in USD or other foreign currencies may be retained indefinitely in domestic foreign-currency segments of a corporate bank account, applied directly to offshore vendor disbursements, or converted to CAD at negotiated commercial rates at any time. The only regulatory threshold is FINTRAC reporting of international EFTs equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 (or equivalent) within a 24-hour window, a reporting obligation executed by the financial entity rather than by the exporting corporation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF and international EFT reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium feasibility under Canadian law. Non-bank payment entities must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and, where providing retail payment functions, obtain authorization under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. Offshore fintech and E-Money Institutions (EMIs) domiciled outside Canada hold no passporting right into Canada; they may legally provide ancillary collection and FX services only through FINTRAC-registered MSB status or a licensed PSP partnership with a Tier-1 chartered bank. For a low-risk creative game-art BPO, such providers are acceptable as supplementary collection rails (PayPal Business and comparable PSPs), but the primary liquidity spine must remain with an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No prior Bank of Canada or FINTRAC approval is required for equity injection into a CBCA business corporation; however, Canadian chartered banks apply sanctions screening and internal competence checks, and FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting is triggered at CAD 10,000 or more. The treasury should append BOP-consistent purpose references under 'Equity securities issued' and keep current CRS and FATCA self-certification forms (Canada Revenue Agency RC518 and RC520). Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate for active business income earned by a non-Canadian-controlled federal business corporation; provincial/territorial corporate rates are additional and vary from 8% to 16%, producing a combined statutory headline rate of 23% to 31%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end, regardless of the due date for payment of tax. For a calendar-year corporation, the filing deadline is June 30 of the following year. Any taxes owed must be paid no later than 2 months after the fiscal year-end for non-CCPCs (or 3 months for CCPCs with certain criteria and eligible for the small business deduction at any time in the year).
