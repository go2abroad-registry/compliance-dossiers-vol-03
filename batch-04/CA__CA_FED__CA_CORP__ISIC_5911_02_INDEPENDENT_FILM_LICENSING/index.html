Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — official federal tax authority; business registration portal: CRA My Business Account via 'Represent a Client' or 'My Business Account' at canada.ca/revenue-agency.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign corporation conducting a taxable business activity in Canada must apply for a Canada Revenue Agency business number (BN) and GST/HST account using Form RC1 or through the CRA 'Represent a Client' portal. The application requires certified corporate constitutional documents, registered office/agent information in Canada if applicable, details of the Canadian commercial activity, and, if using a representative, an authorizing form. Registration is free; the BN plus GST/HST program account is issued upon successful processing, typically within 15–30 business days for paper filings and faster for online applications.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident foreign corporation conducting a taxable business activity in Canada must apply for a Canada Revenue Agency business number (BN) and GST/HST account using Form RC1 or through the CRA 'Represent a Client' portal. The application requires certified corporate constitutional documents, registered office/agent information in Canada if applicable, details of the Canadian commercial activity, and, if using a representative, an authorizing form. Registration is free; the BN plus GST/HST program account is issued upon successful processing, typically within 15–30 business days for paper filings and faster for online applications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box or pass-through elections exist for Canadian business corporations. A corporation is an entity-level taxpayer under the Income Tax Act. Limited statutory elections are available for specific purposes — e.g., section 216 net-basis election for non-residents, associated corporation joint elections, and GST/HST voluntary registration for businesses below the CAD 30,000 threshold — but none alter the default entity-level corporate tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies from an active business in Canada; non-residents may voluntarily register without a permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act Schedule VI applies to exports of tangible goods and to certain supplies of services and intangible personal property (including copyright licences) provided to non-residents for use predominantly outside Canada. For film licensing, the licence grant to a non-resident licensee for distribution and exploitation outside Canada generally qualifies as a zero-rated supply under Part V of Schedule VI provided it is not a supply made in Canada and the copyright rights are not substantially used in Canada. Documentary evidence must demonstrate the non-resident status of the licensee, ownership of the exploitation rights, foreign territory scope, and that the royalty proceeds relate to the foreign licensing activity.

  • Customs export declaration (CBSA Form B13A or equivalent) — required only when tangible film prints, encoded media, or masters are physically exported
  • Bill of lading or airway bill — required only for physical shipment of film materials to foreign licensees
  • Executed film licensing agreement with foreign licensee, containing territory, rights granted, royalty structure, and duration
  • Commercial invoice or royalty statement issued to the non-resident licensee, evidencing the zero-rated supply classification
  • Bank/foreign-exchange support — e.g., SWIFT inward remittance advice or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of royalty proceeds (used as substantiating export evidence)
  • Full contemporaneous records (books and records) retained under the Excise Tax Act showing customer identity, destination, nature of the licensed rights, and foreign-use facts

No statutory e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate. Electronic invoices are permitted and recognized for GST/HST purposes if the taxpayer retains accessible business records. CRA provides digital filing and account-management services (e.g., NETFILE, Represent a Client), but there is no API-based real-time invoice clearance, digital stamping, or government fiscal repository requirement for Canada.

The CRA may reassess to claw back input tax credits and reverse zero-rating treatment where export documentation is incomplete or fraudulent. Unpaid GST/HST becomes subject to compound daily interest and penalties: a 10% penalty on net tax deficiency is common for failure to collect/remit; knowingly making false statements may attract a 25% misrepresentation penalty. Serious export zero-rating fraud can lead to criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, with fines up to 200% of evaded tax amounts and imprisonment, together with high-risk audits, third-party liability for promoters, and suspension of GST/HST refunds. Note: Canada imposes no specific statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is used as a conservative administrative benchmark for substantiating the receipt of export consideration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada does not generally require approval for foreign investment, dividend payments, interest payments, royalty payments, service fees, loan repayments, or repatriation of capital. Principal practical hurdles are bank compliance review, beneficial ownership and source-of-funds verification, Canadian withholding tax, transfer-pricing support, thin-capitalization rules, taxable shareholder benefits, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, corporate-law solvency requirements, and documentary evidence supporting the commercial purpose of related-party payments. Payments to non-residents may require Canadian withholding tax, generally at 25% absent a treaty reduction or exemption; the Canada-foreign jurisdiction treaty position must be verified transaction by transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no general Canadian statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, convert export receipts into Canadian dollars, or repatriate export proceeds within a prescribed period. No mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts may generally be retained in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual banking terms, sanctions, AML requirements, tax reporting, and accurate accounting. Foreign-currency transactions must be recorded and reported in accordance with applicable Canadian accounting and tax rules. Cross-border monetary transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a single transaction, or linked transfers meeting the threshold, may trigger reporting obligations for financial institutions and other reporting entities under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and related regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no separate foreign exchange administration imposing general exchange controls; foreign exchange activity is also subject to federal AML rules, sanctions administered by the Department of Foreign Affairs, Trade and Development, tax rules administered by the Canada Revenue Agency, and applicable provincial securities and derivatives regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection, foreign-exchange conversion, and international payouts, but not equivalent to a Canadian deposit-taking bank account. Offshore electronic-money institutions may be used only after confirming that their Canadian service model is legally permitted and that the provider is authorized in its home jurisdiction. A provider conducting payment functions for end users in Canada may fall within the federal Retail Payment Activities Act and may require Bank of Canada registration, subject to statutory scope and exemptions. Money-services-business obligations, FINTRAC registration, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, safeguarding, data-transfer, privacy, settlement-risk, and counterparty-bank requirements must be assessed. Client funds may not be represented as Canadian deposit-insured funds unless held through an eligible deposit-taking institution and covered by applicable Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation rules. Offshore EMI accounts are unsuitable as the sole treasury account for a Canadian corporation without a Canadian bank backup.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through a Canadian bank account in the exact legal name of the corporation, preferably from an account held by the documented shareholder or investor. Use the wire narrative to identify the transaction as equity subscription, paid-in capital, or shareholder loan; Canada has no universal statutory FX purpose-code system, so the bank's internal purpose classification and compliance questionnaire must be completed accurately. Provide the subscription or loan agreement, board resolutions, capitalization table, beneficial ownership information, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence, investor tax-residency information, and expected-use schedule before remittance. Reconcile the incoming wire to the corporate ledger and issue appropriate share or debt records. Do not route capital through directors, unrelated third parties, personal accounts, or unapproved payment intermediaries. For non-resident loans, document principal, interest, maturity, arm's-length terms, withholding tax, thin-capitalization implications, and any applicable related-party reporting.Use the Canadian operating account for payments to distributors, rights holders, production suppliers, platforms, sales agents, insurers, and professional advisers. For ordinary invoices, use bank wire or electronic funds transfer with a complete beneficiary name, account number, SWIFT/BIC, intermediary-bank details where required, invoice reference, contract reference, and documented payment purpose. A SWIFT MT103 is a legacy customer-credit-transfer format; banks increasingly process cross-border payments under ISO 20022 while preserving equivalent payment data. For documentary trade settlement, use a commercial letter of credit issued or advised by a regulated bank under UCP 600, with documentary requirements matching the licensing or delivery contract. For copyright licensing, verify whether an LC is commercially appropriate because many royalty and platform payments are better settled by contract-based wire, escrow, or collection arrangements.
