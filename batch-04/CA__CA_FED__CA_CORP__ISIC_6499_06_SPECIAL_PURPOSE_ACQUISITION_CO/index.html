Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: a resident Canadian public corporation is taxable on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no check-the-box or pass-through classification is available for a CBCA business corporation; no tax-exempt zone applies; SPAC investment income held in escrow/trust is taxable to the corporation or trust at ordinary corporate/trust rates.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Applicable statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 85 and s. 86 rollover elections for tax-deferred property transfers into or out of the corporation; (ii) ITA s. 186(2) Part IV tax reduction/elimination election for dividends received from connected corporations; (iii) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 150 election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as non-supplies for GST/HST; (iv) ETA s. 211 and s. 225(4) selected listed financial institution and attribution-method elections for GST/HST on financial services; (v) ITA s. 104(5.3) election to deem a foreign trust resident in Canada for hybrid-mismatch and foreign-accrual-property-income mitigation; (vi) functional-currency election under ITA s. 261 for reporting in USD, relevant to US-dollar SPAC trust assets.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1): GST/HST registration is mandatory when worldwide taxable revenues exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; for a SPAC whose only activities are exempt financial services under ETA s. 123(1) 'financial service' (share issuance, trust investment, escrow holding), registration is not compulsory; voluntary registration is possible under ETA s. 240 but is generally disadvantageous because input tax credits are denied on exempt supplies under ETA s. 169(1).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V when the supplier delivers tangible personal property to a destination outside Canada, or transfers possession or ownership to a common carrier or forwarder for export, and retains documentary evidence satisfactory to CRA. Services: zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VII, Part VII to financial services supplied to non-residents, except where the service relates to a financial instrument or deposit in respect of which the recipient is the issuer or holder and the instrument is used in a Canadian business; for a SPAC, issuance of its own common shares is an exempt 'financial service', not zero-rated, because the share is a financial instrument of a resident issuer, while placement-agent and advisory fees relating to non-resident subscriptions may qualify as zero-rated if the agency service is supplied to the non-resident investor and the exception does not apply. Export of Canadian goods by a SPAC is outside the core business model.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or approved electronic export report via the Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit evidence substantiating foreign exchange proceeds
  • Non-resident KYC and residency evidence for zero-rated financial services, including W-8 or certificate of residence, subscription agreement, and escrow trust statements

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory tax-authority API authorization exists in Canada. CRA requires every GST/HST registrant to issue tax invoices containing the BN, customer name, date, description, consideration, and tax charge (ETA s. 221 and the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations). Electronic filing of GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable revenues above CAD 1.5 million. CRA has adopted a voluntary federal e-invoicing framework using the Peppol/UBL standard, but no mandate is in force. SPAC cross-border subscription wires settle through SWIFT without prior tax clearance; CRA does not pre-approve outbound funds transfers.

Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline under the Excise Tax Act or the Income Tax Act; the 180-day figure is retained as a conservative contractual benchmark for SPAC escrow and trust repatriation arrangements rather than a CRA requirement. CRA requires export zero-rating evidence to be obtained within a reasonable time, generally no later than two years after the GST/HST return is filed; failure to substantiate an export results in reassessment to 5% GST/HST, compound arrears interest, and, where misrepresentation is involved, penalties under ETA s. 280.1 of 25% of the net tax, ETA s. 280 of the greater of CAD 100 or 25% of the amount, and criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327(1) for evasion exposing fines up to 250% of the tax attempted to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. For income-tax exposure, fraudulent export claims or inflated foreign-source revenues trigger ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax, a normal reassessment period extended indefinitely where misrepresentation is proven, and potential clawback of SPAC input tax credits and Part XIII withholding assessments on distributions to non-residents.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a floating, fully convertible fiat currency. Canada maintains no exchange-control restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions. Non-residents, including SPAC sponsors and investors, may freely convert CAD into foreign currencies, repatriate invested capital, and remit profits without prior central-bank licensing or administrative approval. Reporting obligations are primarily informational: FINTRAC requires reports for international EFTs and physical currency movements exceeding CAD 10,000; the CRA requires T1134/T1135 filings for specified foreign property and controlled foreign entities where thresholds are met. Profit remittances to non-residents are subject to withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, typically 25% or reduced by treaty, but not to exchange-control hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose export-proceeds surrender or mandatory compulsory-conversion rules. Exporters and treasury vehicles may retain export receipts in foreign-currency accounts at domestic or foreign banks without any statutory timeline or conversion ratio. All proceeds must be recorded for Canadian income-tax purposes on an accrual basis in Canadian-dollar equivalent using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate, and any unrealized FX gains or losses are recognized under GAAP. No statutory surrender or repatriation deadlines apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in conjunction with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), whose registration regime is being operationalized by the Bank of Canada, requires payment service providers, including EMIs and fintechs, to register as legal operated in Canada. Non-resident EMIs cannot solicit or service Canadian clients without an appropriate Canadian registration, FINTRAC registration as a money services business (MSB) where applicable, and compliance with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Leading cross-border fintechs such as Wise or Airwallex can be viable if they maintain a registered Canadian subsidiary and comply with correspondent-banking standards. For a regulated SPAC holding trust and escrow assets, major Schedule I banks remain the only fully commercial and legally certain clearing path.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for inbound capital: (1) Sponsor or investor executes an international wire using SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian beneficiary bank's correspondent account; (2) the payment order must include the SPAC's legal name, account number (or IBAN-style CCPA routing), and a clear purpose code or remittance advice such as 'Equity Contribution', 'Capital Injection', or 'Escrow Trust Funding'; (3) the Canadian receiving branch completes creditor validation under FINTRAC and screens the transaction in accordance with the PCMLTFA; (4) for amounts above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC EFT reports are mandatory; (5) all inbound funds are held in a segregated capital escrow account subject to the escrow agreement, and any interest credited is received accordingly; (6) the corporate treasury confirms the source-of-funds documentation and issues a creditor receipt.Outbound disbursements are cleared through: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct international credit transfers; (ii) SWIFT MT700/Mt707 for documentary commercial letters of credit used in trade settlements; (iii) SWIFT MT202 for interbank and bank-to-bank cover payments. Domestic legs use Canada's real-time payment system 'Lynx' (successor to LVTS) for high-value CAD payments and the Automated Clearing Settlement System for batch payments. All outbound disbursements must respect corporate payment mandates, dual-signature controls, relevant sanctions and export controls administered by the Department of Global Affairs Canada and OFAC, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds of CAD 10,000. Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), whose registration regime is being operationalized by the Bank of Canada, requires payment service providers, including EMIs and fintechs, to register as legal operated in Canada. For a regulated SPAC holding trust and escrow assets, major Schedule I banks remain the only fully commercial and legally certain clearing path.
