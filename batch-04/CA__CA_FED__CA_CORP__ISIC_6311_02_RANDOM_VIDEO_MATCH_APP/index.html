Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal tax administration portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/businessregistration; post-registration compliance via My Business Account (MyBA).

E-government filing portal

A Business Corporation must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RT) before carrying on taxable supplies in Canada. Foreign entities apply by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts) through BRO, by mail/fax, or through the CRA's online simplified registration portal for non-resident suppliers of digital services, attaching the foreign incorporation certificate, proof of foreign tax residence, evidence of Canadian permanent establishment or agent, and authorization of a Canadian representative. The CRA issues the BN/RT upon validation; registration becomes compulsory once the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is met, and is available voluntarily below the threshold. Non-residents carrying on business in Canada also require an RC corporate income-tax account and, where withholding obligations arise, an RZ non-resident account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box regime; foreign entity classification follows the entity's legal form under its jurisdiction of incorporation. Available elections include Income Tax Act s. 216(1) (non-resident rental-income return), s. 217 (pension benefits), and Excise Tax Act elections: s. 179 (closely related corporations), s. 172.5 (quick method), s. 186 (capital-property transfers) and s. 273 (joint ventures). Hybrid-mismatch limitation rules under Income Tax Act ss. 12.2(1.1), 18.2 and 18.21 apply automatically to cross-border hybrid arrangements (payments made on or after 1 July 2022) and do not involve elective check-the-box treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: the small-supplier registration threshold under Excise Tax Act s. 148(1), measured by worldwide taxable revenues in the preceding four consecutive quarters or CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter. For non-resident suppliers of digital services such as video-match subscriptions and in-app virtual items, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies based on specified supplies made to recipients in Canada; platform-operator obligations may apply separately to non-resident app stores such as Apple IAP. Voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted and advisable to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% GST/HST rate applies to: (i) supplies of tangible personal property exported by the supplier or its agent; and (ii) supplies of services and intangible property made to a non-resident who is not registered under the Excise Tax Act, subject to the statutory exceptions for services relating to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, personal services performed by an individual in Canada, and intangibles used primarily in Canada. For the video-chat app, revenue from foreign end users is zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V (ss. 5 and 7); revenue from Canadian end users bears 5% GST or applicable HST. The supplier must maintain contemporaneous evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and of actual provision of the service abroad.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (CAED/Customs B13A) for any tangible goods exported; not applicable to pure digital services but maintained where merchandise is dispatched
  • Commercial invoice evidencing the electronic supply, consideration, and foreign end-user destination
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill for goods, or an automated digital-service delivery log and geolocation/IP evidence for the app platform
  • Non-resident status evidence: written customer confirmation, foreign billing address, foreign tax registration data, or Apple IAP / Paymentwall country-of-account records
  • Foreign settlement proof: bank inward-remittance statements, SWIFT/ACH/PSP settlement reports showing foreign-source funds received in Canada

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital-stamping, or pre-approval e-invoicing mandate. The CRA accepts electronic invoices if they are legible, accurate, and retained in an accessible format; the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing standard is voluntary and recommended for B2B interoperability. Non-resident and domestic registrants report through the digital portal using GST/HST NETFILE or Represent a Client API access, but there is no per-invoice API clearance obligation; audit reliance is placed on transaction-level records, payment-settlement files, and server-side delivery logs.

The Excise Tax Act does not codify a 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for zero-rating; however, CRA audit practice requires contemporaneous proof of foreign settlement, and the platform's compliance SOP treats 180 days from the transaction date as the maximum evidentiary cycle for documenting foreign-source remittance before zero-rating is challenged. Income Tax Act s. 17 imputes interest where a Canadian corporation's receivables from non-residents remain outstanding for more than 12 months. CRA may reassess zero-rated supplies as taxable within the normal four-year limitation period, extended where false statements, omissions, or wilful default are established. Penalties include: s. 280(1) failure-to-comply penalty (CAD 250 plus CAD 100 per day to a 12-month maximum); s. 280(1.1) false-statement penalty (CAD 250 plus 5% of the tax that would have been payable, increased to 10% for knowing or grossly negligent misstatements); and the s. 285 civil evasion penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (50% on a subsequent violation). Criminal prosecution under s. 326 of the Excise Tax Act may result in fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to five years on indictment; directors may be jointly and severally liable under s. 323.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII on both current and capital account; no exchange-control legislation restricts profit remittance, dividend repatriation, or loan repayment. The only outbound frictions are Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, generally treaty-reduced, e.g., 5% for non-resident corporate shareholders under applicable tax treaties) and transfer-pricing documentation (Income Tax Act s.247) to prevent recharacterization of cross-border intercompany payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime; there is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export or service-proceeds under the Currency Act or Income Tax Act. Export receipts and cross-border telephony service revenues may be retained freely in CAD or foreign currency, subject only to balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Statistics Canada and annual income tax disclosure to the CRA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of clearing banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision and Money Service Business (MSB) registration by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium under a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) model. Canada has no regulated 'EMI' license class; foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, dLocal) may serve the entity only if (i) registered with FINTRAC as an MSB or Foreign MSB, (ii) they rely on a Schedule I bank sponsor for Payments Canada LVTS/ACSS clearing access, and (iii) they do not accept deposits in Canada. Legal constraints: s.11 of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and related regulations; no direct clearing account at the Bank of Canada is available to non-bank EMIs.

15.0% general federal corporate income tax rate (38.0% base less 10.0% provincial abatement less 13.0% general rate reduction under Income Tax Act s. 123; 9.0% applicable to qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations [CCPCs] on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder (Income Tax Act s. 212(2)); applicable treaty reductions may lower the rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end (s. 150(1)); final balance-of-tax due 2 months after year-end for non-CCPCs and 3 months after year-end for CCPCs eligible for the small business deduction, subject to quarterly instalments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 must be filed for all reportable non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents regardless of amount; mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation (Form T2201) is required when the aggregate value of related-party reporting transactions during the year exceeds CAD 5,000,000; mandatory Master File (Form T2200) applies when the MNE group's consolidated gross revenue in the preceding fiscal year exceeds CAD 30,000,000; contemporaneous written documentation must exist by the T2 filing due date and be submitted to CRA upon request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, economic substance is mandated through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), the statutory arm's-length recharacterization power (s. 247(2)), and the substantive jurisprudence of the Supreme Court of Canada. For a real-time video-matching and cross-border social telephony application, the Canadian-resident corporation must demonstrate genuine operational substance - local personnel making strategic decisions, Canadian server and data-management functions, in-market maintenance of Apple IAP and Paymentwall merchant reconciliation, and actual assumption of commercial risk - to prevent recharacterization as a shell conduit for non-resident principals.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act ss. 163(2), 238, 239; Criminal Code s. 380)

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax (s. 163(2)); upon conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 5 years (indictable offence); summary conviction carries a fine of 50% to 100% and up to 2 years' imprisonment; prescribed-rate interest applies to all underpayments.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced through s. 227.1 (joint and several director liability for corporate tax withholdings and penalties), s. 160 (transfers to non-arm's-length persons), and Criminal Code party-liability for directing minds; convicted principals face arrest warrants, Interpol red notices in cross-border cases, extradition exposure, and effective travel restrictions while pending prosecution.
Part XIII Non-Resident Withholding Non-Compliance (Income Tax Act ss. 212, 215, 227)

Statutory sanction: The Canadian payer remains liable for the full amount of tax that was required to be withheld (s. 215(1)), plus a 10% failure-to-remit penalty (s. 227(8)), accrued interest at the prescribed rate compounded daily, and additional administrative penalties under ss. 162(7) and 227(9); the unpaid amount is a collectible Crown debt.

Director personal liability: Every director of the corporation is jointly and severally liable for the unremitted Part XIII withholdings, interest, and penalties under s. 227.1(1), even after corporate dissolution; CRA issues Director Liability Assessments, files liens, freezes bank accounts, seizes receivables, and may examine directors personally and seek civil orders restricting disposition of assets.
Transfer Pricing Recharacterization and Shell Conduit Structure (Income Tax Act ss. 247, 245)

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust consideration to arm's-length terms under s. 247(2); a 10% penalty applies under s. 247(3) where the TP adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue and the taxpayer failed to make reasonable efforts; GAAR may disallow the entire tax benefit and impose interest; substantial TP penalties and recharacterized income are not routinely insurable.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who deliberately create hollow non-resident conduits risk personal charges as counselling or aiding tax evasion; although s. 247 penalties primarily attach to the corporation, if withheld amounts or source deductions were not remitted during the scheme, directors become personally liable under s. 227.1, and fraudulent conveyance claims may arise against personal assets.
Anti-Money Laundering and FINTRAC Compliance (Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; Criminal Code s. 462.31)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties against the entity up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for certain FINTRAC non-compliance (with corporate AMPs commonly CAD 250,000 for repeat failures); criminal money-laundering under Criminal Code s. 462.31 carries imprisonment for up to 10 years; laundering-related offence proceeds are subject to restraint and forfeiture orders; FINTRAC registration may be revoked for unregistered MSB activity.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and controlling shareholders who direct, authorize, or acquiesce in the laundering activity may be prosecuted as principals or parties under s. 21 of the Criminal Code; sanctions include personal fines, up to 10 years' imprisonment, Interpol red notices and travel bans in cross-border enforcement, immigration inadmissibility to Canada and allied states, and personal forfeiture orders against interests in the corporation.
