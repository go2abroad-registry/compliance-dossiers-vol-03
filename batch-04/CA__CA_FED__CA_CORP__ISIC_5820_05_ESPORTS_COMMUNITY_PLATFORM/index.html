Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business registration and filings administered through CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO). Federal corporate T2 returns and GST/HST returns are filed with the CRA; provincial tax registrations are separate where applicable.

E-government filing portal

A federally incorporated Business Corporation obtains its Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation through Corporations Canada or the provincial registrar, with program accounts activated via CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts) or My Business Account. A non-resident foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must apply for a BN and a GST/HST program account (RT) through BRO or Form RC1, providing foreign registration details and a Canadian representative or agent. The BN serves as the Tax Identification Number for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and customs purposes. Non-resident digital-services sellers may use the simplified GST/HST registration regime without a Canadian permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act and is taxed on worldwide income at the federal level, plus provincial/territorial corporate tax where a permanent establishment is attributed. No pass-through tax classification is available under federal income tax law; GST/HST applies at the entity level as a value-added tax requiring registration when taxable supplies exceed the small supplier threshold.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) ETA subsection 273(1) GST/HST joint election to elect nil consideration for supplies between closely related registrants; (ii) ETA subsection 156(1) closely related corporation election to treat group members as a single registrant; (iii) Income Tax Act subsection 85(1) rollover election for tax-deferred transfers to a corporation; (iv) Income Tax Act subsection 216(1) election for non-resident landlords to file a Part I return based on net rental income; and (v) the hybrid mismatch provisions under Income Tax Act section 18.6 and related rules (BEPS Action 2), which disallow deductions for certain payments in structured hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies measured cumulatively over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and in any single calendar quarter (small supplier threshold). Public service bodies have a CAD 50,000 threshold. Non-resident digital-services suppliers may have a mandatory registration obligation with no explicit threshold where significant digital supplies are made to Canadian consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, where the supplier exports the goods from Canada and delivers them outside Canada. Services are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, section 7 when performed wholly outside Canada, or section 9 when supplied in respect of real property situated outside Canada. For an esports community platform, digital matchmaking and tournament services supplied to non-resident consumers or non-resident businesses are zero-rated only where the service is performed entirely outside Canada and is not connected with Canadian real property; supplies made to non-resident registered businesses for their commercial use may qualify for zero-rating in respect of certain intangible and platform services. Supplies of digital services to Canadian resident consumers are taxable at 5% GST or the applicable HST/PST/QST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13/CAED filing through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration system)
  • Commercial Invoice (with HS classification, valuation, and seller/buyer details)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/ledger evidence of foreign-currency settlement from the non-resident purchaser
  • Contracts, platform service agreements, and digital delivery records substantiating the nature and location of services

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government clearance API, or digital stamping regime currently exists under federal GST/HST law. Electronic invoicing is permitted with the recipient's consent under ETA subsection 223(1). The CRA is participating in the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework for voluntary e-invoicing, but issuance of a qualified digital invoice remains optional; all invoicing records must be retained for six years and support GST/HST audit trails.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST and income tax within four years of the original assessment, and the limitation period is indefinitely suspended or extended where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation, committed fraud, or filed wilfully false documents. Export zero-rating claims are subject to contemporaneous documentary substantiation; failure to provide export evidence results in reassessment of the zero-rated treatment with interest and penalties. Gross negligence penalties under ETA subsection 285(1) equal 25% of the net tax or input tax credit misstated. Wilful evasion or false export declarations may additionally attract criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, with fines up to CAD 200,000 and possible imprisonment, as well as penalties under the Customs Act, retrospective clawbacks, and revocation of registrant status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully liberalized. Canada applies no exchange controls under the Currency Act; the former Exchange Control Act has been repealed. Both current-account flows (dividends, royalties, platform subscriptions, tournament fees) and capital-account flows (equity injections, intercompany loans) are freely convertible to CAD at market-determined rates. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted but is subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory; treaty-reduced to 5%/15% for dividends and typically 0%/10% for royalties under the Canada-United States treaty). Esports platform revenue must be GST/HST registered with the CRA where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement. Canada imposes no statutory deadline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation holding period for export receipts. A Canadian business corporation may retain and settle export or tournament revenue in any freely convertible currency. Reporting obligations are limited to tax disclosure: foreign-currency and other specified foreign property with an aggregate cost exceeding CAD 100,000 must be reported annually on CRA Form T1135, and foreign source income is reported on the corporate T2 return.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy is coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada. Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange control administration, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium with registration. Canada does not recognize the EU electronic-money institution (EMI) passport. A non-resident digital payment provider or EMI clearing CAD rails must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian compliance officer, and maintain a physical agent in Canada. Stripe and PayPal operate as registered payment processors and are usable as customer-facing rails, but they do not substitute for a Schedule I bank relationship for treasury, escrow, or FX hedging purposes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 250,000 for local file documentation; CAD 50 million consolidated group revenue for master file

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to Canadian entities with international business structures lacking Canadian economic substance; esports platform as operating entity generally exempt; holding companies may require Canadian directors and premises

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Income Tax Act; fines up to $1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for corporations and individuals

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability; civil penalties, restitution orders, and potential disqualification from corporate directorships
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions; penalties up to $250,000 per year for non-compliance with economic substance rules

Director personal liability: Piercing of corporate veil; directors personally liable for taxes, fines, and potential imprisonment for fraudulent misrepresentation
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to $250,000 per transaction or year; interest on underpaid taxes

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for penalties; potential criminal charges if willful
Failure to Withhold Taxes on Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the amount not remitted, plus interest; possible criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the withheld amounts and penalties; potential jail time for willful non-compliance
