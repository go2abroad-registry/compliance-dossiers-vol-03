Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; corporate tax and registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account; customs and export reporting interface: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS); sub-national Quebec authority: Revenu Québec.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian Business Corporation (federal CBCA or provincial) first obtains its Certificate of Incorporation from Corporations Canada or the competent provincial registry, then obtains the Business Number (BN) from CRA through Business Registration Online; the BN is the single TIN and carries program accounts RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll). A foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada applies for a BN via Form RC141 or RC1, attaching foreign incorporation documents, Canadian establishment or agent particulars, and board resolutions; mandatory GST/HST registration arises once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, with voluntary registration available for input-tax-credit recovery. Non-resident registrants must maintain books and records accessible to CRA; a corporation with Quebec nexus must additionally register with Revenu Québec for QST and provincial corporate filing obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident corporation taxed on worldwide income at the entity level; non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada taxed on Canadian-source income only; no pass-through or partnership look-through for a CBCA business corporation)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; CRA applies its own foreign-business-entity classification doctrine. Relevant elections for a CRO corporation include: voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier (Excise Tax Act s.240); ITA s.216 election for non-resident corporations deriving Canadian rental or timber income; ITA s.219 branch-tax election for non-resident corporations carrying on business through a Canadian permanent establishment; and the non-resident registrant's election to file GST/HST on a net-tax basis. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.2 and 18.21) apply mandatorily and do not provide elective carve-outs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully and freely convertible. Canada permits unrestricted conversion on both the current account and the capital account. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, interest payments, and management fees may be repatriated to non-resident shareholders without foreign exchange approval, subject only to corporate law solvency requirements, regulatory holdbacks, and Canadian income tax withholding obligations under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in Canada. No timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts or cross-border service revenues. Export proceeds may be retained in original foreign currency, held in foreign-currency bank accounts, or converted to CAD at prevailing commercial market rates at any Canadian domestic Schedule I bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border payment and AML/ATF oversight; Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a supplemental clearing channel only. Offshore digital EMI or cross-border financial technology platforms may be used for low-value disbursements and FX conversion, but they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under Canadian law. Any entity providing payment services to Canadian residents or processing transactions on behalf of Canadian clients must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Offshore EMIs cannot replace a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank for escrow, clinical trial contract management, or settlement of material cross-border payments.

Lack of Canadian substance or physical operational presence

Root cause: Lack of Canadian substance or physical operational presence

Defense evidence: Office lease agreement in Canada; copy of business license or municipal permit; payroll records and T4 filings for Canadian employees; minute book records of board meetings held in Canada; corporate annual return filings; and operational documents such as Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), ethics board approvals, and investigator site contracts demonstrating Canadian clinical trial management activity.
Sanctions, PEP, or politically exposed persons screening flags

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP, or politically exposed persons screening flags

Defense evidence: Shareholder and director declarations confirming absence of sanctions exposure; independent sanctions screening report from a reputable provider; PEP risk assessment with explanation of any low-risk classification; audit trail of legal counsel verification of directors' backgrounds; and confirmation from Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) registry where available.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are routed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the Canadian subsidiary's CAD-denominated bank account using SWIFT BIC and Canadian clearing code (institution + transit + account). The wire must state an accurate corporate beneficiary name and a business purpose code equivalent to 'Capital Contribution' or 'Equity Injection' / 'Intercompany Loan'. For CAD settlement, funds clear through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). Compliance verification requires board resolutions, signed subscription or loan agreement, source-of-funds bank statements, FATCA self-certification, and CRS tax residency forms. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory dollar threshold is codified in the Income Tax Act; however, CRA administrative policy requires contemporaneous TP documentation (Local File / Master File-style) where aggregate transactions with non-resident related parties exceed CAD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year. Furthermore, Form T106 – Information Return for Transactions with Non-Residents must be filed if the aggregate value of transactions with each non-resident related party exceeds CAD 1,000,000 (transactions involving goods or services) or CAD 250,000 (for services and intangibles).
