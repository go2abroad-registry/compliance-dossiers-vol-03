Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Foreign corporation obtains a statutory 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN): (1) incorporate a Canadian company under the Canada Business Corporations Act or obtain extra-provincial registration in each operating province; (2) register for the BN through the Business Registration Online portal or form RC1, indicating the GST/HST account and corporate income tax account; (3) apply for GST/HST registration by filing form RC1 or using the CRA online services, identifying the parcel consolidation hub as making taxable Canadian supplies; (4) obtain separate program accounts for payroll (RP0001) and import/export; (5) enrol in CRA 'My Business Account' and submit Canada Revenue Agency Form RC59 for representative authorisation. Non-resident corporations with a Canadian permanent establishment are subject to the same BN issuance protocol.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' entity election exists in Canadian federal tax law. Corporate classification follows legal form, with the Canadian-controlled or public corporation status determining eligibility for the small business deduction and investment tax credits. Statutory elections include: Income Tax Act subsection 216(1) election allowing non-resident recipients of Canadian-source rental income to compute net tax on a remittance basis; Income Tax Act subsection 219(1) Canadian branch return obligations for non-resident corporations; Income Tax Act section 125(3) allocation election among associated corporations for the small business deduction; and elections under the hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act sections 18.4–18.6) that apply mandatory deduction disallowances rather than elective recharacterisation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (gross annual taxable supply revenue for small supplier exception; once exceeded, the parcel consolidation hub must register and collect GST/HST on Canadian taxable supplies; non-resident suppliers without a Canadian permanent establishment face the same threshold unless they supply specified digital services, which carry separate rules)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies meet the federal 0% GST/HST rate under the Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 22 for goods exported from Canada: goods must be shipped by the supplier or the supplier's client to a destination outside Canada, or delivered to a carrier/common carrier for direct export; services are zero-rated where provided to a non-resident person and not performed principally in Canada, or relate directly to exported goods. Parcel consolidation hub services qualify only when they are supplied to a non-resident recipient and the underlying physical handling can be documented as performed in respect of goods destined for export. A written export evidence package must be retained proving the goods left Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A electronic export declaration via eManifest or the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management system)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (carrier-issued transport document showing export destination and port of exit)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX credit advice (documenting receipt of export sale proceeds from abroad)

Canada does not currently operate a real-time fiscal invoice clearance or digital stamping regime applicable at the federal level. All GST/HST registrants must electronically file periodic returns through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or a CRA-approved EDI/API service. Invoices must include the GST/HST registration number, and CRA requires electronic payment (via My Payment or bank transfer) for amounts owed. CRA cross-matches GST/HST refund claims and export zero-rating with Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) eManifest and customs data, and bulk filers may use file upload APIs. Quebec imposes an additional invoice data reporting requirement for certain provincial programs, but federal e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or block-chain monitoring is not yet law.

CRA and CBSA jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Failure to substantiate export risks reassessment of the entire GST/HST zero-rated value with a 4-year normal reassessment period and a 6-year period for misrepresentation or fraud; ETA section 287(2) imposes a gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the unremitted tax; ETA section 280 charges daily compound interest; false export declarations attract Customs Act penalties, including section 160 penalties of CAD 25–CAD 100 per infraction, potential criminal prosecution, and retrospective clawback with no limitation period for fraudulent zero-rating claims. The hub must retain export evidence, proof of freight, and payment records for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no material exchange-control restrictions on foreign direct investment, profit repatriation, dividends, loan repayments, or trade settlements. Non-resident entities may freely convert CAD and remit proceeds without advance approval. The Currency Act and Bank Act provide the legal foundation; Canada retains the right to impose emergency controls but in practice exercises no currency convertibility barriers for compliant corporate operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or prescribed timelines for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters may hold foreign currency in domestic or offshore bank accounts indefinitely. However, all cross-border transactions above CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting obligations, and corporate taxpayers must include foreign exchange gains/losses in taxable income under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT enforcement; Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority and operates under an open capital account regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border digital financial institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Wise Business, and OFX are viable for ancillary payment routing and multi-currency settlement with a medium-to-high feasibility rating. However, they are not chartered banks in Canada and cannot offer deposit insurance under the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC). They must be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and may be subject to provincial licensing. Their use is best limited to treasury optimization and last-mile trade payments, while primary capital custody remains with a Tier-1 bank.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file corporate tax return

Statutory sanction: $50 per day late filing penalty (max $5,000) plus interest on unpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes; potential criminal charges under Criminal Code for evasion
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income, penalties up to 50% of underpaid tax, and interest

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for willful non-compliance; possible imprisonment for evasion

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for tax benefits obtained; potential criminal liability for fraudulent misrepresentation
