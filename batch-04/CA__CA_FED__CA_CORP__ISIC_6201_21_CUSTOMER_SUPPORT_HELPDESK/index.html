Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Accepted for this non-regulated software-as-a-service helpdesk business. The CBCA requires a registered office at a stated physical address in Canada for service of process and the keeper of statutory books and records; a P.O. Box alone is not permitted. A virtual office or registered agent service providing a physical street address, mail forwarding, and an ISC register location satisfies the registered office requirement. The company will need to maintain cloud-based and contract records accessible in Canada for CRA and FINTRAC purposes, but no physical employees or stock need to be present at the registered office for this industry.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the CBCA; shares may be issued without par value for such consideration as the directors determine. No escrow account, capital verification, or bank blocked-account requirement applies to federal incorporations. The Corporation must, however, maintain a registered office and a minute book containing organizational records in Canada.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and structural load clearance are not federally imposed. The Corporation must comply with the local fire code and municipal fire inspection for its leased premises, including exit path adequacy and fire alarm system inspections. Provincial occupational health and safety legislation (e.g., Ontario's Occupational Health and Safety Act) governs workplace ergonomics and employee safety; the Corporation must register its workers with the relevant workers' compensation board (e.g., WSIB Ontario) and obtain an employer account within 10 days of hiring. No heavy structural load or dangerous machinery clearance applies to a standard office/helpdesk operation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada maintains IMF Article VIII status and OECD Code of Liberalisation obligations, and there are no exchange controls on acquiring, holding, or disposing of CAD or any foreign currency. Profit remittances, dividends, royalties, interest, and intercompany settlements are unrestricted at the FX regulatory level; the only material burdens are tax-driven, i.e., non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25 percent, typically reduced by treaty, e.g., Canada-United States: 5 percent on qualifying dividends, 10-15 percent on many interest and royalty items, with software and certain copyright payments potentially eligible for lower or zero rates), and CRS/FATCA reporting to the CRA. No capital-import license, no repatriation permit, and no conversion declaration is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no mandatory surrender rule, no statutory repatriation window, and no conversion ratio for export proceeds. A Canadian exporter may keep foreign receipts in any domestic or offshore account in any currency indefinitely. The only compliance triggers are: (1) financial-institution reporting to FINTRAC for international electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more, where the client need not file a parallel report but must retain supporting invoices and contracts; and (2) routine record-keeping under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act, including classification of receipts as services, infrastructure, or software for GST/HST and withholding purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) is the federal prudential regulator of Schedule I banks; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Payments Canada operates the Lynx high-value settlement system, ACH, and the Real-Time Rail program. Canada imposes no foreign-exchange-control office and no central-bank approval requirement for cross-border remittances.
