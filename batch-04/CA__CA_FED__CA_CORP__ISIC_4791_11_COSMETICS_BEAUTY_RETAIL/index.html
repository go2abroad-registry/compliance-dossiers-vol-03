Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully and freely convertible on both current-account and capital-account basis under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange controls, no prior approval for inbound portfolio or direct investment, and no restrictions on dividend distributions, management fees, royalty payments, or repatriation of paid-up capital by a federally incorporated Canada Business Corporation (CBCA corporation). Canadian withholding tax at 25% on non-resident payments, reduced by treaty, is administered under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and does not constitute a convertibility or remittance barrier. FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply to currency and virtual currency transactions, but do not impose exchange control licensing. The legal currency remains legal tender but banks are free to price and settle transactions in any currency. Profit remittance through a Schedule I bank is executed as a routine FOREX transaction using standard settlement rails and requires only ordinary banking instructions plus the applicable tax compliance attestations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed timeline for repatriating export receipts, and no Central Bank custodian mechanism for export earnings. Cosmetic and beauty retailers operating as Canadian federal corporations are free to retain foreign currency proceeds in domestic or offshore settlement accounts, subject to general record-keeping under the Income Tax Act and to FINTRAC's 'large transaction' and 'financial entity' reporting obligations for transactions over CAD 10,000. For inbound e-commerce settlement via Shopify Payments or PayPal, funds may be pooled abroad and swept to a Canadian operating account without any exchange control filing, although banks may apply internal anti-money-laundering hold periods for new client relationships.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in conjunction with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking prudential oversight; financial intelligence and AML/ATF supervision is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not operate a separate foreign exchange control administration or exchange surrender authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium for foreign-domiciled electronic money institutions (EMIs) and non-bank payment service providers. Wise, Payoneer, and similar regulated e-money platforms are usable for merchant settlement and vendor disbursements but are not licensed as banks in Canada. They generally cannot accept deposits in Canada without registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act and oversight by the Bank of Canada and FINTRAC. Offshore EMIs that are not Canadian-registered may only provide inbound remittances and outbound transfers on a pass-through processing basis; they are not viable substitutes for a payments schedule and cannot hold Canadian operational funds as a licensed deposit-taker. It is recommended to maintain a Schedule I bank account as the primary liquidity anchor and use EMIs only for settlement optimization. Shopify Payments and PayPal are treated as merchant acquiring payment rails and are compatible with Schedule I account structures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital and operational funds uses SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers through a Canadian Schedule I bank correspondent network. Common ancillary SWIFT fields should be accurately coded: complete legal name of the CBCA corporation, Toronto or registered provincial address, federal jurisdiction, and any partnership with the ultimate shareholder reference. Canadian banks do not apply exchange controls, but they must screen against Global Affairs Canada sanctions, FINTRAC reporting, and OSFI anti-money-laundering directives. For tax purposes, inbound capital contributions should be designated as paid-up share capital or shareholder loan, not as trading revenue; the bank requires an account-purpose declaration and the client must record the funds in the corporate treasury system as 'capital injection', not 'sales proceeds'. Attention is required to SWIFT purpose-of-payment fields that reference 'foreign direct investment' or 'capital subscription' to ensure accurate filing of the Government of Canada's balance-of-payments reports through the Bank of Canada's statistical reporting system. Real-time domestic clearing via Lynx (large-value transfer system) is available for CAD-denominated domestic concentration from merchant acquirers.Outbound trade settlements for procurement of cosmetics inventory, packaging components, and international logistics are cleared via standard commercial payment instruments: SWIFT MT103 for irrevocable international wire transfers; clean collection (MT400/MT210) where no documentary credit is required; SWIFT MT700 issuance of an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit for high-value bulk raw-material purchases; and MT707 amendments or MT740 authorization when necessary. For low-value recurring disbursements, the automated clearing settlement system via Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) and pre-authorized electronic funds transfers (EFT) in CAD is permitted, but international trade payments should consistently use SWIFT to preserve audit trails and correspondent-bank exchange rate certainty. Payment instruction packages should include the tax invoices, incoterms, proof of export, and customs declarations, because Canadian banks conduct post-transaction sanctions screening and enhanced due diligence on the cargo description. For retail-oriented inbound business, Shopify Payments can be used for customer refunds and supplier disbursements within Canada, while cross-border inventory settlement should be channeled through the Schedule I bank treasury desk to manage FOREX and regulatory reporting holistically.Entering into deliverable FX spot and forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/CNY) with a Schedule I bank treasury as permissible OTC hedging products, with confirmation under ISDA or Canadian Bankers Association master agreements.Non-deliverable forward contracts for specific currencies for which convertibility may be limited in the counterparty jurisdiction, executed as offshore NDFs through Canadian Schedule I bank dealing desks, with underlying exposure to Chinese renminbi and certain Asian settlement currencies.Cross-currency basis swaps and plain-vanilla currency swaps between CAD and USD or CAD and EUR, accessibly to the treasury for matching long-term sales and procurement currency exposures.Plain-vanilla FX options and collars under the same treasury mandate for managing volatility around seasonal promotional cycles, subject to board approval and prudent risk management policies.Futures and listed options on CAD/USD traded on North American exchanges (e.g., CME Group) as exchange-traded linear instruments when OTC counterparty credit limits are absent.
