Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (Partnership Information and Application for a Business Number) or by using Business Registration Online (BRO), providing the general partner's name, address, and partnership declaration. Foreign corporate general partners must supply certified legal existence documents (e.g., certificate of status). Once the BN is issued, a GST/HST program account (RM) is compulsory when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or when the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada; voluntary registration is also available. The BN is generally issued within 5 to 10 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (Partnership Information and Application for a Business Number) or by using Business Registration Online (BRO), providing the general partner's name, address, and partnership declaration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. A limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes and a separate person for GST/HST. The partnership may, however, elect a non-calendar fiscal year under subsection 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act, and non-resident partners may file a protective section 216 election to compute Canadian-source rental income on a net basis. Hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act sections 18.2 and 18.3 and MLI provisions) are mandatory and are not subject to elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (per-year worldwide taxable supplies; non-residents must include all global taxable supplies for the threshold test, and registration becomes mandatory once exceeded)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (plush mascots and stuffed animals) are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the registered supplier exports the goods directly by delivering them outside Canada within a reasonable time, proves the export through documentary evidence, and retains ownership risk and title transfer consistent with the export contract. Services supplied to non-residents (e.g., OEM manufacturing of plush dolls) may also be zero-rated when the service is performed wholly outside Canada or the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is not otherwise used within Canada, subject to statutory exceptions.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or electronic export report (CAED/XML)
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice with export description and declared value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation of foreign-currency settlement

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. E-invoicing via PDF, EDI, or custom XML is permitted; invoices must contain prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act. CRA does not pre-clear invoices, but all source books and records must be retained for six years and be accessible in Canada. Digital reporting is under consideration but not mandated as of 2026.

CRA may reassess an export zero-rating claim within four years of the filing date (indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation). Lack of sufficient export documentation reverses the zero-rating, triggering assessment of 5% GST on the supply, interest from the date the tax would have been payable, and penalties for false statements or omissions equal to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act. Wilful evasion may lead to criminal prosecution under section 327 ETA, with fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. Additionally, associated input tax credits claimed on the export are clawed back, and records must be kept for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada imposes no exchange controls, no obligation to repatriate earnings, and no restriction on non-resident profit remittances. A Canadian Limited Partnership may distribute cash to foreign partners freely, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 15% under the Canada-US treaty). Investment Canada Act screening applies to change-of-control acquisitions but not to routine capital injections. Banking operations are subject to Bank Act liquidity and OSFI capital adequacy rules, which do not constrain convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts. Exporters may hold foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts. Threshold reporting applies under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more and is declaratory only.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential oversight of Schedule I banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Non-resident electronic money institutions (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may provide operational CAD settlement rails but must be registered as money services businesses with FINTRAC to serve Canadian-domiciled corporate clients. For a non-resident-funded Limited Partnership in the plush-toy OEM trade, a FINTRAC-registered EMI is viable as a secondary disbursement rail for supplier and platform payments, but capital escrow and primary liquidity should reside at a Schedule I chartered bank. Legal constraint: the EMI must not hold the partnership's funds in a manner classified as a deposit-taking activity, which would breach the Bank Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Contributions are wired from each partner's home bank to the LP's CAD operating account at the Canadian Schedule I bank using SWIFT MT103 (direct transfer) or MT202 (cover payment). The payment narrative must cite the LP's CRA business number, the provincial registration number, and the purpose code for capital contribution (ISO 20022 code INVS for investments, or bank-specific 'GIFT' mapping for partner advances). The account officer will normally hold each credit for a 24-hour compliance review if the amount exceeds CAD 100,000; the pre-delivered source-of-wealth dossier clears the hold. For Chinese partners, routing via a Hong Kong or US correspondent bank reduces CAD clearing delays. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold for contemporaneous Local File/Master File; CRA requires reasonable documentation for any material non-arm's-length cross-border transaction. Mandatory information reporting: Form T106 is required where aggregate specified transactions with a particular non-resident related party exceed CAD 1,000,000; Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 (approximately CAD 1.1 billion)

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) and judicial substance-over-form doctrines to disregard transactions lacking economic reality. The Limited Partnership must maintain a physical presence, real employees, inventory control, and management decisions in Canada; a shell entity with artificially routed cash flows will be reassessed, and treaty benefits may be denied under limitation-on-benefits provisions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239: fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years (indictment) or 2 years (summary); civil gross negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) up to 50% of the understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid withholding taxes, interest, and penalties under ITA s. 227.1; CRA may impose travel bans, seize personal assets, and pursue continuous liability for wilful participation
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under ITA s. 245 (GAAR), denial of loss allocations, transfer pricing penalty of 10% of net adjustment, and application of Part XIII branch tax; contracts may be reclassified as shams

Director personal liability: General partner directors may be held liable for T5013 filing failures and for PAYE/CPP/QPIP remittance deficiencies; courts may pierce the veil where the partnership is a mere facade used to conceal personal wrongdoing
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under ITA s. 247(2) and penalty under ITA s. 247(3): 10% of the net adjustment, minimum CAD 5,000, plus interest and escalating penalties for repeated non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable, but if they instruct or condone the mispricing, they may be assessed for gross negligence penalties and face criminal charges for aiding tax evasion; corporate indemnity will not be available
Failure to File T5013 and International Disclosure Forms

Statutory sanction: T5013 late penalty of CAD 100/day (max CAD 2,500); T1135 penalty of CAD 25/day (min CAD 100, max CAD 2,500); T1134 penalty of CAD 25/day (max CAD 12,000 or CAD 2,400 for foreign affiliates); gross negligence penalties up to CAD 24,000 for false or incomplete foreign reporting

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under ITA s. 227.1 where they directed the failure to file or omitted material information; the due-diligence defence requires proactive confirmation of compliance by the LP's general partner and Canadian tax adviser
