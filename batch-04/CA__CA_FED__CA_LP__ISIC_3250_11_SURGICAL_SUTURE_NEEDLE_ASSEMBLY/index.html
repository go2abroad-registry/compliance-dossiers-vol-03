Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence de revenu du Canada (ARC); official portal: CRA Business Registration Online and My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) handles export clearance documentation.

E-government filing portal

Foreign persons establishing a Canadian limited partnership must apply for a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online. The partnership obtains its GST/HST account using Form RC1, Request for a Business Number - GST/HST Account; representative authorization is completed via Form RC59. The BN is a 9-digit business identifier with account-specific suffixes (e.g., RT for GST/HST). The limited partnership must file an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return. A non-resident entity without a Canadian permanent establishment may still be required to register if it carries on a taxable business in Canada through a Canadian agent or employee, or it may voluntarily register for GST/HST recovery.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign persons establishing a Canadian limited partnership must apply for a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online. The partnership obtains its GST/HST account using Form RC1, Request for a Business Number - GST/HST Account; representative authorization is completed via Form RC59. The BN is a 9-digit business identifier with account-specific suffixes (e.g., RT for GST/HST). The limited partnership must file an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return. A non-resident entity without a Canadian permanent establishment may still be required to register if it carries on a taxable business in Canada through a Canadian agent or employee, or it may voluntarily register for GST/HST recovery.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian income tax treats a limited partnership as a flow-through partnership under Income Tax Act s.96; there is no US-style check-the-box election. Statutory mechanisms include: (i) designation of partnership members for allocation purposes under s.96(2.1) through (3) and filing of Form T5013; (ii) GST/HST joint elections for selected partnerships using Form GST/HST 152; (iii) voluntary GST/HST registration election for non-resident suppliers below the CAD 30,000 threshold under ETA s.240; (iv) partnership disposition elections on Form T5013 when property is transferred; and (v) anti-hybrid rules under Budget 2021 amendments, which require self-assessment if a partnership is treated as a hybrid entity creating double non-taxation. No elective Canadian flow-through classification exists; application of Canadian partnership provisions is statutory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD - annual worldwide taxable supplies threshold under Excise Tax Act s.240; a non-resident supplier making taxable supplies in Canada (including export sales) exceeding CAD 30,000 must register for GST/HST; below the threshold registration is voluntary subject to conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are zero-rated supplies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports the goods within 60 days after physical possession is transferred to the purchaser and the goods are not acquired for use in Canada. Surgical suture assemblies falling under ISIC 3250 and HS 3006.10 qualify for zero-rating when the goods are shipped to a foreign destination by common carrier, courier, or the purchaser's own transporting vehicle. The supplier must be registered for GST/HST, must retain documentary evidence of export, and must not know or have reason to believe that the goods will be consumed or used in Canada. Direct identification of the goods with the export transaction and proper commercial invoicing in the purchaser's name are prerequisites to claiming the 0% rate.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration / Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) transmission record or Form B13A for non-CES shipments
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent contract of carriage evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange wire receipt evidencing payment from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial Invoice / Customs Invoice stating 'GST/HST zero-rated export' and indicating H.S. 3006.10 classification
  • Warehouse release note or carrier pre-advice confirming the date of export from Canada
  • Packing slip and quantity records linking the exported suture assemblies to the corresponding export declaratoin

No government-run real-time e-invoicing clearance, digital fiscal stamping, or mandatory invoice clearance API exists in Canada. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST34 via NETFILE) and electronic filing of T5013 returns through Internet File Transfer where certain volume thresholds are met. E-invoicing follows commercial EDI/Peppol practices but is not a statutory mandate. Export shipments must be reported to CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) or, for low-value transactions, using Form B13A; there is no pre-clearance approval of commercial invoices for zero-rating purposes.

CRA may reassess an invalid zero-rating claim and recover unremitted GST/HST at 5% federal rate (or applicable HST rate) plus daily compound interest. Specific penalties include: up to C$6,400 for knowingly making a false statement in a return under ETA s.280; a 25% gross-negligence penalty under ETA s.285 on additional tax assessed; a 50% false-statement penalty under Income Tax Act s.163(2); document preparation penalties under the Customs Act of up to C$1,000 per shipment or C$25,000 for more serious violations; and failure to keep records results in a C$100 monthly penalty under ETA s.277(1). Fraudulent schemes for refunds or zero-rating can result in Criminal Code s.380 prosecution and imprisonment. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day reference in this model is adopted as a contractual and audit-evidence benchmark for matching incoming wire transfers to the zero-rated export sale.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible, freely floating currency regime with no exchange control laws. The Canadian dollar trades freely on global markets; current account (trade) and capital account (investment and loan) transactions are unrestricted. Profit and capital repatriation by a Canadian limited partnership is not subject to exchange-approval requirements. The sole tax-facing hurdle is non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on 'income from Canadian sources' that is paid or credited to non-resident partners; active business income allocated to residents/non-residents may be subject to treaty qualification. There are no convertibility delays, surrender requirements, or prior licensing permissions for remitting dividends, distributions, or return of capital. Payments in USD or CAD are permitted, and settlement can be made in either currency at the bank's prevailing rate or an agreed contract rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or time-bound repatriation timelines for export proceeds in Canada. Export receipts (e.g., from sales of surgical sutures/needles) may be retained in foreign currency, converted at any commercial rate, or used directly to settle offshore supplier invoices. However, for AML/FTM and statistical purposes, cross-border electronic transfers of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the reporting financial institution; the enterprise should also maintain underlying trade documentation (commercial invoices, bills of lading) to evidence legitimate export transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking prudential matters; FINTRAC administers AML/CFT reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a full banking alternative. Canada does not license 'electronic money institutions' as deposit-taking banks; offshore EMIs can only offer payment services through federally regulated bank partners. They may operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, but cannot accept insured deposits or extend credit as banks. For a medical-device LP handling cross-border trade finance, an EMI is legally viable for auxiliary FX conversion and low-value supplier settlement but is not acceptable as the sole treasury/clearing rail. The safer structure is a Schedule I bank account supported by SWIFT MT103/LC operations; EMIs may be used for sub-account liquidity subdivision only if the underlying clearing bank is federally regulated.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity/debt injections from foreign partners must be wired to the LP's Canadian clearing account using SWIFT MT103/ISO 20022. There are no Bank of Canada conversion permits; the remittance purpose should be coded as an investment/capital contribution (e.g., bank code 'A3010/A380' or ISO purpose code 'CBLK' for capital, or 'LOAN' if debt). The LP should supply the LP agreement, subscription schedule, transfer-in letter, and beneficial ownership declaration so the bank can classify the source of funds. For amounts of C$10,000 or more, the receiving Schedule I bank auto-reports the inward transfer to FINTRAC. If the physical settlement currency is USD, the USD can remain in a USD account or be spot-converted to CAD; no convertibility filing is required. For inbound loans, prepare an arm's length promissory note and book interest expense; non-resident withholding tax may apply on interest, subject to treaty relief (e.g., Canada-US).Outbound supplier/trade payments (e.g., raw PGA polymer, needle wire, packaging materials, assembly components) should be cleared via (1) SWIFT MT103 free-form telegraphic transfers for small-to-mid value payments, with invoice numbers and contract references in the payment narrative; (2) irrevocable, confirmed commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) for bulk raw-material purchases or capital equipment imports to protect against counterparty risk; and (3) documentary collections (CAD/SWIFT MT400) where a balance-sheet LC is not warranted. CAD-denominated domestic disbursements may be cleared through the Real-Time Rail (RTR) or Lynx; USD disbursements must move over Fedwire/CHIPS via a Canadian bank's US correspondent. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% General Rate Reduction; the Limited Partnership is a flow-through vehicle not itself assessable, and each partner is taxed on the allocated share at the applicable federal and provincial combined rates, typically 26.5% to 31% for corporate partners in major provinces.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation; an LP distribution is not a dividend and does not attract Part XIII on active business income — the income flows through and is taxed under Part I at partner level; Part XIII at 25% applies to LP distributions attributable to passive Canadian-source income such as rents, royalties, and interest unless a treaty reduces the rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than five months after the LP's fiscal period end (ITA subsection 229(1)); for a calendar-year LP, the filing deadline is May 31 of the following year; non-resident partner allocations and T5013 slips must be completed and issued by the same date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 is due no later than six months after the corporate partner's taxation year-end; if the LP's T5013 is filed late, the corporate partner's due date remains unchanged, and the balance of Part I tax is due no later than three months after the corporate partner's year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Form T106 reporting are mandatory where aggregate covered transactions with non-arm's length non-residents equal or exceed CAD 1,000,000; Master File documentation is required where the multinational group reports consolidated revenues over CAD 50,000,000 and the Canadian LP participates in intercompany IP, manufacturing, or procurement flows for surgical suture assembly; contemporaneous documentation must be produced to the CRA within three months of a written request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as in offshore centres, but ITA section 245 GAAR and section 247 transfer pricing rules require real economic substance; a Limited Partnership carrying on surgical suture needle assembly must maintain a physical Canadian production facility, employ operational and technical staff, exercise entrepreneurial risk in procurement and sales, and keep full local books and records; a 'mailbox' LP or treaty-conduit arrangement with no manufacturing footprint will be recharacterized, with profits reallocated to the beneficial owner and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns (ITA s. 239)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ITA section 239(1) exposes the LP and its officers to a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years; civil penalty under ITA subsection 163(2) is 50% of any misstated amount; CRA may also impose a 10% gross negligence penalty and full restitution of unpaid tax plus compounded interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner and directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part I or Part XIII amounts under ITA section 227.1; CRA may pierce the LP veil where the general partner is a shell, execute civil seizure against personal assets, issue a travel ban certificate under ITA section 226, and obtain imprisonment of officers who authorized or participated in the fraudulent returns.
Transfer Pricing and T106 Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under ITA section 247(2), reallocate profits, and apply the 10% transaction penalty under subsection 247(3); interest on the adjustment compounds daily; failure to file Form T106 by the deadline attracts a penalty of CAD 5,000 for each calendar year plus CAD 1,000 per month up to a maximum of your total penalty exposure; the gross negligence penalty may be added where documentation is fabricated.

Director personal liability: Where the general partner or directors sign the transfer pricing disclosure knowing it to be incomplete, CRA may name them personally for the section 247(3) penalty; directors can be assessed for the full tax adjustment if the LP lacks assets, and personal travel bans under ITA section 226 remain available for unpaid assessments.
Shell Company / GAAR / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under ITA section 245(2) nullifies the tax benefit; mandatory disclosure rules under sections 237.3 and 237.4 impose penalties from CAD 5,000 to CAD 100,000 per unreported avoidance transaction; treaty benefits are denied under substance-over-form and the Limitation on Benefits article; the full Part I tax is restated with daily compound interest and the 50% gross negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: CRA may pierce the LP's limited liability shield to recover from the general partner; directors who approved the shell structure face personal civil restitution, disqualification from managing Canadian companies, asset freezes, and in aggravated cases criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code involving smuggling or fraudulent commercial dealings.
AML/CFT and Cross-Border Payment Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, the CRA and FINTRAC may impose administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 500,000 per violation, increased to CAD 1,000,000 for repeat violations; criminal proceedings under Criminal Code section 462.31 for laundering proceeds carry imprisonment of up to six months on summary conviction or up to five years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Officers or the general partner who knowingly direct, authorize, or participate in non-reporting face personal fines and imprisonment; CRA may coordinate with FINTRAC to freeze the LP's bank accounts, and directors remain personally liable for any resulting unremitted tax assessed against the partnership.
