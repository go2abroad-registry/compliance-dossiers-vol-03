Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Federal tax administration; GST/HST is administered under the Excise Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. E-15) and corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)); official access portals: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency, including 'My Business Account', GST/HST NETFILE, and Business Registration Online (BRO).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) through Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada) or use an existing provincial/territorial corporation; articles of incorporation are issued electronically. Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus CRA program accounts through Business Registration Online at canada.ca; for newly incorporated CBCA entities, the BN and RC (corporate income tax) account can be issued in the same online session due to BRO/CRA integration. Step 3: Register for the GST/HST program account (RT0001) via BRO or by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Accounts'; non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada may send RC1 by mail/fax to the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre or the International and Large Business Directorate. Step 4: Add payroll account (RP0001) and import/export account (RZ0001) as required. Step 5: File the T2 Corporation Income Tax Return (mandatory electronic filing where gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000), remit corporate instalments, and comply with Part XIII non-resident withholding obligations. Foreign-incorporated entities carrying on business in Canada must obtain a BN under s. 242 of the Excise Tax Act and may voluntarily register for GST/HST even if below the statutory threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election; a CBCA Business Corporation is per se a separate taxable corporation under ss. 89(1) and 248(1) of the Income Tax Act. Available elections most relevant to an Offshore Development Center (ODC) structure: (i) GST/HST closely-related corporation election under s. 156 of the Excise Tax Act, which deems intercompany supplies between closely related corporations not to be made for consideration, simplifying GST/HST administration; (ii) s. 150 ETA election to treat financial services as taxable (rarely relevant to an ODC); (iii) foreign affiliate designations and FAPI computations under s. 95(2) ITA, including the s. 93(1) election to deem dividends paid from exempt surplus; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under ss. 18.4 and 18.5 ITA are mandatory and do not permit elective override; (v) applicable tax treaties may permit elections or filings to reduce Canadian withholding tax on cross-border interest, royalties, or dividends.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold based on total worldwide taxable supplies (including those of associated persons) over four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-resident suppliers of 'specified supplies' (digital services and intangible property) must register under the Simplified GST/HST Registration regime and collect tax on supplies made to Canadian-resident recipients once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of software engineering, development, maintenance, and support services to a non-resident recipient qualifies for the 0% GST/HST rate under Section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided: (a) the recipient is a non-resident at the time of the supply and is not registered for GST/HST; (b) the service is not supplied in connection with real property situated in Canada; (c) the service is not performed in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed (except goods temporarily imported into Canada solely for maintenance, repair, or overhaul, which CRA administrative policy treats as zero-rated); and (d) the service is not a personal service rendered to an individual in Canada. The supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence of non-resident status, contract terms, delivery, and payment. Zero-rated supplies are 'taxable supplies', so the Canadian ODC may claim full input tax credits (ITCs) on Canadian overheads such as payroll, rent, and cloud infrastructure while charging 0% on qualifying export services. No customs declaration is required for pure services; physical exports of goods (e.g., servers, hardware) must satisfy Part V, s. 5 of Schedule VI (export of tangible personal property) and may require a CAED/export declaration through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (ERS). Note: Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day treasury benchmark aligns with standard CRA audit expectations for transfer-pricing documentation and with the deemed-interest rules of ITA s. 17 relating to non-arm's length cross-border balances.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CAED / CBSA ERS submission) — required only for physical exports of goods, not for offshore software services
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport documentation — required only for physical exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing foreign-currency receipt
  • Signed service agreement with the non-resident recipient (identifying the recipient, scope of services, and delivery terms)
  • GST/HST invoice issued to the non-resident recipient showing 0% GST/HST applied
  • Attestation or written confirmation that the recipient is a non-resident and is not registered for GST/HST
  • Delivery, acceptance, and performance evidence (offshore server logs, time tracking, project milestones, support tickets, code repository access records)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and may be exchanged via PEPPOL, EDI, or PDF. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000; mandatory electronic remittance applies to CRA payments, including GST/HST, exceeding CAD 10,000. GST/HST returns (Form GST34) are due one month after the reporting period for monthly and quarterly filers and within three months after fiscal year-end for annual filers.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST net-tax position within four years of the filing due date (ETA s. 298(1)); the limitation period is unlimited where a return or application was false or involved neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims trigger gross-negligence penalties under ETA s. 285 of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax understated or refund over-claimed. Knowing tax evasion is an offence under ETA s. 327 punishable by imprisonment of up to two years on summary conviction and up to five years on indictment, together with fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount. Parallel income-tax exposure includes the 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) and evasion penalties under ITA s. 239 (fines of 50%–200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to five years). Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable for corporate GST/HST amounts under ETA s. 323. Where export pricing to foreign affiliates is not at arm's length, ITA s. 247(3) imposes a transfer-pricing penalty equal to 10% of the pricing adjustment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no approval gates for inbound or outbound remittances, and no restriction on the repatriation of share capital, dividends, intercompany loans, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance frictions are purely fiscal, not currency-based: (i) dividends and deemed dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders attract Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, at a statutory rate of 25% generally reduced to 5% for a U.S. corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of the voting stock under the Canada-US Treaty; (ii) genuine arm's-length technical-service fees and management fees are not subject to Part XIII withholding, provided the payments are not re-characterized as royalties for copyright or software IP; (iii) royalties paid to non-residents for the use of software copyright or patents attract a 25% statutory withholding subject to treaty reduction (commonly 10% or 0% depending on treaty and instrument), which is the principal characterization risk for an Offshore Development Centre (ODC); and (iv) interest paid at arm's-length rates to non-resident lenders is generally exempt from Part XIII withholding under the 'fully exempt interest' rules where the lender is dealing at arm's length and the Canadian borrower is not using the borrowed funds to earn exempt income. No Bank of Canada or OSFI permission is required for a non-resident to establish a Canadian ODC; the only filing obligation is the Investment Canada Act notification for the establishment by a non-Canadian of a new non-sensitive business (software consultancy and computer programming services), with no pre-closing review applicable below the WTO-investor enterprise-value threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules: there is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to sell foreign currency to the central bank or an authorized dealer. A resident exporter may retain foreign currency indefinitely in Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) lawfully maintained with a Schedule I or Schedule II bank under the Bank Act. For income-tax purposes, the Canadian-resident ODC must otherwise compute income in Canadian dollars unless it validly elects under Income Tax Act section 261 to use an eligible functional currency (USD is an eligible currency for a Canadian corporation whose 'books of account' are kept in that currency), in which case FX translation follows the functional-currency rules and realized FX gains or losses are recognized as ordinary income or deductions. The only mandatory compliance overlay is reporting, not surrender: FINTRAC requires an International Electronic Funds Transfer Report (IEFTR) for every international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more transiting a Canadian financial entity; the reporting obligation is discharged by the financial intermediary but the corporate client must ensure wire narratives and beneficial-ownership data are accurate to avoid delayed clearing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), being the central bank and monetary authority for the Canadian dollar. Foreign-exchange and banking-prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46), while cross-border electronic-funds surveillance and anti-money-laundering enforcement are performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and associated Regulations. Canada maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration decree, no exchange-control law, and no requirement of central-bank approval for the opening of foreign-currency accounts or the execution of FX contracts.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a secondary, non-primary clearing rail. Canada has no statutory 'EMI' licence equivalent to the UK/EU regime; the functionally analogous category is the Money Service Business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC. Offshore digital payment platforms in scope for the mandate (Wise Business, Payoneer, and comparable B2B FX wallets) are, from a Canadian legal perspective, unregulated or partially regulated non-bank payment processors; they may offer CAD-denominated multi-currency settlement, low-cost contractor disbursements, and virtual IBANs, but none is authorized as a Schedule I or Schedule II bank and none can hold operating deposits or escrow balances with deposit-insurance coverage. Consequently, they must not be used for: (i) cash escrow for capital contributions, (ii) payroll rails for Canadian-resident employees subject to source deductions, (iii) GST/HST or payroll remittances to the Canada Revenue Agency, or (iv) the primary relationship account for Part XIII withholding. Recommended architecture: Schedule I bank as the sole primary and escrow hub; Wise B2B and Payoneer are permitted only for sub-major-bank-threshold cross-border contractor payments aggregating less than CAD 10,000 per transfer or where a broker-account structure can be reconciled through the primary operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing: foreign parent bank issues a SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary account held at the Canadian Schedule I bank, with settlement across Lynx (Payments Canada's high-value Real-Time Gross Settlement-equivalent system, ISO 20022-compliant, formerly the Large Value Transfer System) or, for smaller wires, across the Automated Clearing Settlement System. SWIFT Wire fields must be populated with precision: Field 50K / 52A must show the ordering parent as the funding party; Field 59 must show the Canadian ODC as beneficiary with the bank's transit and account number; Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state the substantive purpose — 'Share subscription proceeds — capital contribution', 'Intercompany shareholder loan advance', or 'Intercompany services settlement — software engineering fees' — matching the bank's declared account purpose and the FINTRAC beneficial-ownership profile. There is no statutory CAD purpose-code (such as a national BOP-like classification code) mandated by Canadian law; however, the bank's EDD classification typically requires selection from its internal purpose taxonomy corresponding to '01 Intercompany Funding', '02 Equity Injection', or '03 Trade / Services'. For the non-resident parent establishing a new ODC, a notification under Investment Canada Act section 12 must be filed within 30 days of establishment; no pre-closing review is required for a non-sensitive software-consultancy business below the applicable WTO-investor review threshold (C$2.084 billion enterprise value for 2024). All inbound international transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable by the Canadian bank as international EFTs; the bank will request source-of-wealth documentation for the parent when cumulative quarterly injections exceed defined de minimis risk appetite, so the corporate package in 'critical_aml_kyc_blockers_and_defense' must be maintained current at the time of each material injection.Outbound ODC flows split across four clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 international wires from the Schedule I bank for contractor payments, intercompany service fees, cloud infrastructure procurement, and software licence fees — each wire must carry a true and accurate remittance narrative (e.g., 'ODC engineering fees', 'subcontractor milestone settlement') to avoid unfreezing delays and FINTRAC mismatch alerts; (2) the Canadian ACSS / Lynx rails for domestic CAD payments, including payroll for Canadian-resident employees, GST/HST remittances to CRA, and payments to resident contractors; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary and standby) reserved for material infrastructure purchases or contractual performance guarantees where a counterparty requires a non-cancellable payment undertaking — the ODC should maintain an uncommitted LC facility at the primary bank to support such instruments; and (4) the target fintech B2B rails (Wise Business and Payoneer) for low-value contractor disbursements below the CAD 10,000 international EFT reporting band, used only as a secondary channel with monthly reconciliation to the primary operating account to prevent cash-management fragmentation. For Canada-US intercompany settlement, the Schedule I bank's USD correspondent relationship also permits cross-border ACH/file-based payments (through the U.S. Automated Clearing House network) as an alternative to SWIFT for small recurrent payments. All outbound international EFTs equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 are reported by the institution to FINTRAC; the ODC treasury function should provide the bank with an approved beneficiary list and a quarterly projected disbursement schedule to prevent velocity-based account freezes.Vanilla FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/INR) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex or account-control margin arrangement with the Canadian Schedule I bank, covering committed ODC revenue and payroll exposures typically up to 12 months.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for cross-currency exposure in non-fully-convertible or restricted-delivery currencies (e.g., INR legs against USD/CAD), documented under ISDA and booked through the Canadian bank's offshore booking entity where local deliverability is unavailable.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (cross-currency swaps / CCIRS) limited to fixed-to-floating CAD/USD principal-and-interest exchange on intercompany shareholder loans or term-debt maturities, aligned with the underlying intercompany borrowing.Vanilla FX options, including zero-cost collars and capped participation forwards, for asymmetric protection of CAD/USD cash flows where the treasury committee has approved premium or premium-free collar mandates.Multi-currency Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) and natural hedge offsetting — retention of USD receivables in an FCA against USD payables without any derivative documentation, thereby avoiding ISDA credit thresholds and margin posting for the low-risk ODC profile.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility as a secondary, non-primary clearing rail. Canada has no statutory 'EMI' licence equivalent to the UK/EU regime; the functionally analogous category is the Money Service Business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC. Offshore digital payment platforms in scope for the mandate (Wise Business, Payoneer, and comparable B2B FX wallets) are, from a Canadian legal perspective, unregulated or partially regulated non-bank payment processors; they may offer CAD-denominated multi-currency settlement, low-cost contractor disbursements, and virtual IBANs, but none is authorized as a Schedule I or Schedule II bank and none can hold operating deposits or escrow balances with deposit-insurance coverage. Consequently, they must not be used for: (i) cash escrow for capital contributions, (ii) payroll rails for Canadian-resident employees subject to source deductions, (iii) GST/HST or payroll remittances to the Canada Revenue Agency, or (iv) the primary relationship account for Part XIII withholding. Recommended architecture: Schedule I bank as the sole primary and escrow hub; Wise B2B and Payoneer are permitted only for sub-major-bank-threshold cross-border contractor payments aggregating less than CAD 10,000 per transfer or where a broker-account structure can be reconciled through the primary operating account.
