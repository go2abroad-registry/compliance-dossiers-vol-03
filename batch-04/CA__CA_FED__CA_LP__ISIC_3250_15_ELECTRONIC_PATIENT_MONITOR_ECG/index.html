Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — My Business Account digital portal, GST/HST NETFILE, and the CRA Partnership Information Return (T5013) system; customs program account ('RD') is administered jointly with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Limited Partnership under the applicable provincial Limited Partnerships Act (non-resident general partner must appoint an attorney for service in the province of registration; e.g., Form LP2A in Ontario). Step 2: Apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) in the LP's legal name using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); non-resident applicants file Form RC1A. Step 3: Add program accounts to the BN: 'RC' for GST/HST registration, 'RM' for payroll (if the LP has employees), and 'RD' for import/export transactions via CBSA. Step 4: The partnership itself is required to file an annual T5013 information return if it carries on business in Canada and has a corporate partner or a partner that is a non-resident. Step 5: Each partner independently obtains or maintains its own tax identification number for reporting its allocated share of LP income in its home jurisdiction.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — fiscally transparent under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act; the LP computes income at partnership level and allocates to partners for taxation in their own hands. The LP is nonetheless a distinct 'person' registered for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — fiscally transparent under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act; the LP computes income at partnership level and allocates to partners for taxation in their own hands. The LP is nonetheless a distinct 'person' registered for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act. A non-resident partner is taxable in Canada under Part I on its share of Canadian-source business income and may also be subject to Part XIII withholding on any interest, rent, or royalty payments sourced in Canada.

Available election forms

1) Subsection 249.1(5) election to adopt a non-calendar partnership taxation year. 2) Subsection 96(3)/(3.1) partnership agreement on income and loss allocation, binding on all partners and acceptable to CRA. 3) Section 156 Excise Tax Act election whereby a registrant partner that controls the partnership elects, jointly with the LP, to treat otherwise taxable supplies between them as made for nil consideration. 4) Subsection 18.4(4) Income Tax Act election, applicable where the Canadian LP is treated as an opaque corporation under a foreign tax system, to deem the partnership a corporation solely for Canada's hybrid mismatch regime compliance. 5) Subsection 152(4) waiver election to extend the CRA reassessment period beyond the normal four-year window.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD — GST/HST small-supplier threshold determined by taxable supplies made in Canada during the preceding four calendar quarters; a non-resident LP exceeding this threshold must register. Voluntary registration is available regardless of revenue to recover input tax credits on export-related and pre-operating expenses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of multi-parameter ECG bedside telemetry monitors and subcontracted calibration services qualify for 0% GST/HST if the goods are exported in the ordinary course of business (section 6, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act) or if services are performed on tangible personal property that is exported before the service is made available in Canada (section 7, Schedule VI). ECG monitors supplied to licensed health institutions may further qualify as zero-rated medical devices under Part II of Schedule VI. The LP must retain evidence of physical export to a foreign consignee and, for calibration services, documentation that the instruments were shipped out of Canada prior to completion of the service.

  • B13A Export Declaration filed electronically via the Canadian Export Reporting System (G7) for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 or for controlled goods
  • Commercial Invoice in English or French detailing HS 9018.19 ECG monitor product line, quantity, value, and foreign consignee
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air waybill, or highway manifest) evidencing delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire advice confirming payment from a non-resident buyer in foreign currency
  • CUSMA (or other applicable free trade agreement) Certificate of Origin if preferential customs treatment is sought at the destination port
  • Export Permit issued under the Export and Import Permits Act if the device or its embedded encryption modules appear on the Export Control List
  • Complete forwarding file, including pickup order, CBSA customs release, and foreign customs entry or delivery confirmation

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or mandatory digital stamping exists in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records if the format permits verification and data extraction in English or French. Canada participates voluntarily in the Peppol pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework for B2B transactions. GST/HST registrants must retain complete invoice and export evidence pursuant to section 286 of the Excise Tax Act; failure to maintain adequate electronic or paper records is itself an offence.

CRA may reassess within four years of the reporting period; the limitation period is extended if a waiver is filed or is unlimited in cases of fraud or misrepresentation. Where export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary proof, CRA will assess GST at 5% on the supply, disallow input tax credits associated with the invoice, and charge arrears interest. Gross negligence penalties under subsection 285.1(1) of the Excise Tax Act apply to false statements made knowingly or due to gross negligence, equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated. Failure to register or remit attracts an additional 6% penalty plus interest; failure to file the T5013 partnership information return triggers penalties of CAD 25 per partner per day to a maximum of CAD 2,500 per month. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day repatriation deadline; however, the 180-day benchmark is accepted by CRA as evidence of a genuine export sale. Unrepatriated receivables beyond this period may be recharacterized under transfer pricing rules in subsections 247(2)–247(3) as a deemed loan, taxable benefit, or deemed dividend to non-resident partners, with correlative Part XIII withholding obligations and possible adjustment penalties of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment.
