Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); online portal: My Business Account (CRA) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from CRA by using Business Registration Online (BRO) or filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). CRA issues a 9-digit BN which is then activated for the Income Tax program account (RC####) and, where applicable, the GST/HST program account (RT####). Foreign-incorporated entities must provide proof of incorporation, registered office address, Canadian agent or representative details, and, if carrying on a taxable business, complete the T2 corporate income tax registration procedures. The BN serves as the corporate TIN and as the GST/HST registration number; Canada does not maintain a separate VAT identification numbering system.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from CRA by using Business Registration Online (BRO) or filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). CRA issues a 9-digit BN which is then activated for the Income Tax program account (RC####) and, where applicable, the GST/HST program account (RT####). Foreign-incorporated entities must provide proof of incorporation, registered office address, Canadian agent or representative details, and, if carrying on a taxable business, complete the T2 corporate income tax registration procedures. The BN serves as the corporate TIN and as the GST/HST registration number; Canada does not maintain a separate VAT identification numbering system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or elective pass-through classification for business corporations. A federal corporation is subject to entity-level tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act as a resident or non-resident corporation. Limited statutory elections include the section 216(1) election for a non-resident corporation to compute Canadian rental income on a net basis and the section 98(6) partnership continuation election in eligible reorganizations. Hybrid mismatch consequences are governed by Income Tax Act sections 18.2 and 18.3, and no optional classification election is available for GST/HST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable revenues (including zero-rated supplies) over any four consecutive calendar quarters under Excise Tax Act section 148; non-residents supplying taxable goods or services in Canada are subject to the same threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, goods exported by the supplier or delivered outside Canada are zero-rated when the supplier maintains contemporaneous proof of export. Services supplied to a non-resident person, including medical device regulatory consulting services, are zero-rated if the non-resident is not registered under the GST/HST system and the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at all times the service is performed. The supplier must obtain and retain documentary evidence establishing the non-resident status of the recipient and delivery of the service to that non-resident.

  • Customs export declaration submitted to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) when the export value exceeds CAD 2,000
  • Bill of lading, air waybill or combined transport document evidencing shipment from Canada for goods
  • Commercial invoice describing the medical device regulatory consulting service or exported goods and identifying the non-resident counterparty
  • Bank wire confirmation or foreign currency receipt showing receipt of consideration from the non-resident customer
  • CBSA cargo control document, freight forwarder certificate, courier tracking record, or service completion report evidencing offshore delivery

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice stamping, or government authentication of electronic invoices. Businesses may issue electronic invoices, and CRA requires GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 10,000 to file returns electronically (GST34) in most cases. Exported supply documentation must be retained in a readable electronic or paper format, and CRA may electronically audit records using the authorized GST/HST record layout requirements.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, extended indefinitely where the exporter has misrepresented facts or committed fraud. If export zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA will assess uncollected GST or HST at the applicable rate, plus daily compound interest and penalties. Late filing penalties for GST/HST returns amount to 3% of the balance owing plus an additional 1% for each complete month, to a maximum of 12 months. False claims of export zero-rating made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence attract an additional 5% penalty on the understated tax; willful evasion can result in fines of up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment under Excise Tax Act sections 327 and 328. CRA will also claw back input tax credits that were claimed in respect of non-qualifying or unsupported supplies and may apply prosecution to repeat offenders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility as an IMF Article VIII member. There is no foreign-exchange-control legislation, no prior approval requirement for inward investment, and no restriction on the repatriation of dividends, branch profits, or capital by non-resident shareholders of a corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA). Service-export revenue from FDA 510(k) and European CE Mark consulting may be received and held in CAD, USD, or EUR without conversion mandate. The only residual frictions are non-treaty Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%; generally reduced to 5% on dividends and 0-15% on interest/royalties under an applicable tax treaty) and FINTRAC reporting by the receiving financial institution of international electronic fund transfers (EFTs) equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline, minimum conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export or service-export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts. The only compliance duty is passive: financial institutions must report international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and the corporate client must supply accurate remittance purpose narratives when requested. The Export Development Canada (EDC) framework supports exporters voluntarily but imposes no surrender directives.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act; anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with material legal constraints. A non-resident digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer) may lawfully service a Canadian corporate client, but any EMI carrying on money-services business in Canada must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA; unregistered offshore EMIs cannot clear CAD through Payments Canada and typically route through pooled omnibus accounts that do not constitute 'deposits' protected under the Bank Act or eligible for CDIC insurance. EMIs are suitable only for low-value fee intake and small vendor disbursements, not for escrow of client regulatory fees or high-value SWIFT treasury flows. A Tier-1 chartered bank account remains the legally required anchor for capital injections and multi-currency clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For share capital or shareholder loan injections, route a SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the Canadian bank's clearing account (USD via the bank's New York/Chicago correspondent and internal journal into the CAD operating account, or direct CAD through a Lynx participant). The remittance narrative must state a clean purpose descriptor: 'Share capital contribution' or 'Intercompany loan', referencing the subscription or loan agreement number and date. FINTRAC requires the receiving Canadian financial institution to report international EFTs ≥ CAD 10,000; the corporate client is not independently obligated to file, but must answer source-of-wealth requests within 48 hours. CAD funds become immediately available after clearing; no Bank of Canada approval or surrender-timeframe constraint applies. Note that a shareholder loan from a non-resident may incidentally trigger Form T106 reporting for transfer-pricing and Part XIII purposes, which is a tax-compliance matter outside the banking onboarding gate.Clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border USD/EUR vendor payments or refunds; (2) Payments Canada Lynx real-time gross settlement for same-day CAD payments; (3) commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) for equipment purchase or subcontractor payments — permissible but disproportionate for advisory-only disbursements; (4) cross-border ACH through EFT/Paynet for recurring US subscriptions, and SEPA euro transfers through the bank's European correspondent. To avoid OFAC/sanctions false positives, include exact beneficiary legal name, full address, ISO 20022 structured remittance information, and the underlying contract reference. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous TP documentation trigger: controlled non-resident transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate annual value; T106 mandatory for individual transactions > CAD 100,000.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in Canada; substance is tested under GAAR (section 245 Income Tax Act), treaty shopping abuse analysis, and CRA administrative practice requiring beneficial ownership, real management, and commercial risk assumption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False T2 Filing

Statutory sanction: Section 238 and 239 Income Tax Act: fine up to 200% of evaded tax + civil penalties (50% gross negligence penalty); additional civil liability for interest compounding daily.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid withholding amounts (s.227.1 ITA) and for any CPCA/Excise Act liabilities; criminal conviction may impose imprisonment up to 5 years in addition to restitution orders.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance Claiming Treaty Benefits

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief under s.245 GAAR; withholding tax recollected at 25%; penalty up to CAD 10,000 per false statement under s.162(6); reassessment of sham transactions.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under s.245(3): directors jointly and severally liable for taxes that would have been payable absent the GAAR; travel bans and asset freezes may occur under criminal AML proceedings.
AML – Unreported Beneficial Ownership and Wire Transparency

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and up to CAD 1,000,000 for entities; criminal fine and imprisonment up to 5 years for directors found guilty.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted personally for authorizing, permitting, or acquiescing in money-laundering activity (s.83.08 Criminal Code); personal bank accounts may be frozen via civil forfeiture orders, with mandatory reporting to FINTRAC for any cross-border transfer > CAD 10,000.
Criminal Corporate Violation – Fraudulent Certification for FDA/CE Support Work

Statutory sanction: Under the Criminal Code s.380 (fraud), indictable offence with imprisonment up to 14 years; additional penalties under the Food and Drugs Act for false declarations; possible debarment from federal procurement.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for fraudulent misrepresentations under common law; in addition to restitution to harmed parties, travel bans and extradition may proceed from Canada due to treaty obligations.
