Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); business portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign LP exporting from Canada must first determine whether it has a Canadian GST/HST registration obligation: registration is mandatory if the partnership's worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 over any four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available for zero-rated export-only businesses. Apply through Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Account), online via CRA My Business Account or by mail/fax. Submit proof of partnership formation, partner ownership and a Canadian address for service or appoint a Canadian tax representative. CRA will issue a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with an RT program account. The LP should then register excise accounts under the Excise Act, 2001 (Excise Duty Program) and, if involved in duty-paid spirits warehousing, a licensed user's licence. No separate VAT ID exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canadian federal tax law; a limited partnership is statutorily treated as a flow-through for income tax and as a separate person for GST/HST. Elections that may be made for commercial transactions include the Income Tax Act s. 85(1)/97(2) rollover on property contribution to the partnership, and non-resident partnership allocation choices. Hybrid mismatch/election rules are not elective; statutory anti-hybrid measures apply automatically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters; CAD 50,000 for charities and qualifying non-profits). Exporters of baijiu are zero-rated but the threshold still applies to total taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian LP may zero-rate baijiu sales under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 if the supply is fulfilled by shipping the goods to a non-resident recipient outside Canada, and the exporter retains proof that the goods left Canada within 60 days. The supplier need not own the means of export if goods are delivered to a common carrier/courier. Services supplied to a non-resident (e.g., logistics commissions) are zero-rated where the service is primarily performed outside Canada. CRA will require export invoices, payment from abroad, and customs export declarations.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Customs Invoice in CCI)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange credit advice
  • Commercial invoice identifying HS 2208 spirits and quantity
  • Excise Act, 2001 export duty-relief/warehouse removal document
  • Provincial liquor export permit or LCBO/board authorization, where required

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or mandatory digital e-invoicing exists at the federal GST/HST level. Suppliers must retain valid invoices with prescribed identifiers (BN/RT, date, supplier and recipient names, description, tax charged). The CRA participates in a voluntary Peppol-compliant e-invoicing framework. For exported alcohol, CBSA cargo reports (B13A/CCI) and Excise Act, 2001 duty-relief evidence serve as clearance controls; physical bottles do not require fiscal QR codes for export.

The CRA can deny zero-rating and reassess the unremitted GST/HST where export evidence is false or deficient. Normal reassessment is 4 years, but a misrepresentation of fraud removes the limitation period. Penalties under ETA s. 285.1 (gross negligence) is the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax omitted; s. 327 wilful evasion exposes the LP and management to fines up to 200% of the tax and up to 2 years' imprisonment. Prior zero-rating claims are clawed back with compound interest from the date originally assessed and records must be retained for 6 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account convertibility regime under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. There are no general foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on profit remittance by non-resident partners, and no requirement for prior central bank approval for cross-border payments. Dividends, interest, royalties, and service fees may be freely remitted in Canadian dollars or any convertible currency, subject to withholding tax and tax reporting requirements under the Income Tax Act (Canada). The Limited Partnership pass-through structure is recognized, but non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source income and the mandatory GST/HST registration for commercial import/export activities. For Chinese Baijiu export, the only practical hurdle is the excise duty and GST/HST calculation on import into the province of destination; profit remittance from the CA LP to foreign partners is generally unrestricted. The current account is fully convertible; the capital account is unrestricted for direct investment and portfolio capital, subject to national security review under the Investment Canada Act only for significant acquisitions. There is no surrender requirement, no foreign exchange rationing, and no restriction on holding foreign currency accounts in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Canadian exporters may retain export proceeds in foreign currency indefinitely, received into any Canadian-chartered bank account or a foreign bank account, and may convert to CAD at their discretion. The Bank of Canada does not monitor or require the repatriation of trade receivables; however, for GST/HST purposes, export sales of physical goods are generally zero-rated provided proper commercial evidence of export (e.g., Form A, bill of lading, customs entry, or commercial invoice) is maintained. Financial transactions of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC as Large Virtual Currency Transactions (if applicable) or Large Cash Transactions; wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to the FINTRAC Wire Transfer Report (EWT). There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the CA LP may settle USD, CNY, or CAD invoices in any agreed currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT; the Exchange Control Act regime is administered by the Department of Finance Canada with operational enforcement through the Bank of Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for inbound and outbound USD/EUR/CAD settlement through regulated Canadian MSB (Money Service Business) and EMI platforms such as Wise (registered as a Money Service Business in Canada), OFX, and Convera, provided the platform is registered with FINTRAC as a Money Service Business and complies with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canadian law does not permit a pure 'EMI' (Electronic Money Institution) in the EU/UK sense; instead, digital wallet providers and payment service providers are regulated as MSBs and must register with FINTRAC. The CA LP may use a FINTRAC-registered MSB for spot conversions and settlements up to transaction thresholds; however, for definitive cross-border trade settlements and LC operations, a Tier-1 chartered bank account is mandatory because MSBs cannot issue or advise Letters of Credit. Legal constraint: MSBs cannot accept deposits for onward lending, cannot provide treasury hedging instruments, and must not be the primary account for large-volatility Spirits export proceeds. Viability rating: High as a supplementary conversion rail, Low as the sole treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection for the CA LP is via ordinary SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the Chinese partner or the Hong Kong vehicle. Recommended protocol: (1) Use a purpose code consistent with 'Capital Investment - Limited Partnership Equity Contribution' or 'General Partner Contribution'; (2) The Canadian bank's Beneficial Ownership and Control Data (BOCD) form must list the ultimate funder; (3) For amounts above CAD 10,000, the receiving bank reports an Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) to FINTRAC; (4) The capital injection must be supported by a capital contribution notice and a partnership resolution, to rebut any characterization as debt; (5) The incoming transfer should be denominated in USD, EUR, or CAD - the Canadian bank will not accept CNY directly as an account currency, and the transfer must be converted through the Chinese bank's domestic FX system (SAFE-approved) to USD/CAD at the PRC's onshore rate; (6) If the capital relates to a future import of baijiu, the wire should reference a Proforma Invoice to avoid being classified as an operational deposit. To avoid the remittance being frozen, the LP must open a dedicated Capital Escrow Account for the first 90 days and do not intermingle trade receipts; (7) A 10% non-resident withholding tax may apply to partnership distributions to non-resident partners; therefore, inject capital as equity rather than as a loan, and file Form NR4 and Section 216 returns when required.For payment of the Chinese Baijiu supplier, the CA LP may use either: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfer directly from its Canadian bank to the supplier's bank in China (standard 3-5 business days); (ii) a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by the Canadian bank (e.g., RBC, TD) in favor of the Chinese beneficiary, usually under ICC UCP 600, with a sight draft or deferred payment tenor; (iii) a document collection (D/P or D/A) under ICC Uniform Rules for Collections (URC 522). For maximum compliance, the recommended rail is a DP (Documents against Payment) collection or a confirmed irrevocable LC issued by a Canadian Tier-1 bank with a Chinese avalising bank (e.g., Bank of China). The CA LP must include in all SWIFT MT103 instructions: (a) reference to the purchase order; (b) Incoterms; (c) commodity classification code (HS 2208.90 - Chinese Baijiu); (d) the destination port; and (e) the contract’s advance payment percentage. The outbound disbursement must be routed via the Canadian bank’s correspondent network (e.g., RBC and TD use proprietary USD clearing through their US subsidiaries); for CAD settlement, use the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); for USD/CNY dual settlement, use a correspondent in the United States (e.g., JPMorgan for RBC). The CA LP may also use a cross-border payment API from Wise or Convera for small ticket wires under USD 50,000, but LC disbursements must go through the bank's Trade Finance desk. Canadian law does not permit a pure 'EMI' (Electronic Money Institution) in the EU/UK sense; instead, digital wallet providers and payment service providers are regulated as MSBs and must register with FINTRAC. The CA LP may use a FINTRAC-registered MSB for spot conversions and settlements up to transaction thresholds; however, for definitive cross-border trade settlements and LC operations, a Tier-1 chartered bank account is mandatory because MSBs cannot issue or advise Letters of Credit. Legal constraint: MSBs cannot accept deposits for onward lending, cannot provide treasury hedging instruments, and must not be the primary account for large-volatility Spirits export proceeds. Viability rating: High as a supplementary conversion rail, Low as the sole treasury rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal corporate rate; limited partnership is pass-through, partners taxed at individual rates)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% for non-resident recipients of dividends from Canadian corporations; for LP distributions, non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII tax on their share of Canadian-source income at 25% unless reduced by treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million for goods transactions with non-residents (CAD 1 million for services)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. CRA may scrutinize transactions lacking economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties up to 200% of tax evaded, fines up to CAD 200,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; civil restitution and travel bans may apply.
Shell Company / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR may recharacterize transactions; penalty up to 50% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the avoidance scheme.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations, and imprisonment for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly failed to comply; travel bans may be imposed.
