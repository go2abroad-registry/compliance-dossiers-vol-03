Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — national tax administration (GST/HST Program, Excise Duty Program, Income Tax and Business Number registration) via My Business Account and Business Registration Online at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — customs administration, export reporting, and customs bonded warehouse licensing under the Customs Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Register the LP under provincial limited partnership legislation and complete extra-provincial registration in the operating province if the head office is outside that province. Step 2 — Apply through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus program accounts (RT — GST/HST; RM — import/export; RD — excise duty); non-resident partners must submit proof of legal creation and may be required to appoint a Canadian representative for service of process. Step 3 — If taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or registration is otherwise required/beneficial, obtain the RT account in the partnership's own name; the partnership is a 'person' for GST/HST purposes. Step 4 — For bonded trade, apply to CRA for an excise warehouse licence under the Excise Act, 2001 in the partnership's name and post prescribed security; for imported wines/spirits held for customs deferral, apply to CBSA for a customs bonded warehouse licence under the Customs Bonded Warehouses Regulations — CBSA requires the licensee to maintain a Canadian place of business or a resident agent. Step 5 — Enrol in the CBSA Single Window Initiative and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) using the BN/RM account. Step 6 — File T5013 Partnership Information Return annually via CRA's Internet File Transfer; each resident and non-resident partner reports their distributive share separately.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with full current- and capital-account convertibility. No exchange-control permit is required for an inbound equity contribution, a capital distribution, a partner loan, or a bonded-wine/spirits trade remittance. The Bank of Canada does not veto or register repatriation flows; the only compliance constraints are Part XIII withholding tax on distributions carrying interest, royalties, or management fees (treaty-reduced where applicable), FINTRAC reporting of international transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, and contractual documentary checks by the receiving Canadian bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Government of Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, conversion, or holding-period rule on export proceeds. A Canadian limited partnership may retain foreign-currency revenue in correspondent demand accounts indirectly in Canada or remit settlement to foreign suppliers without Bank of Canada consent. FINTRAC's cross-border wire-transfer reporting, large-value transaction monitoring, and the usual trade-documentation requirements of Schedule I banks are the only operational compliance duties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority; prudential bank supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and cross-border transaction reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange-control authority with prior-approval power over trade settlements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI banks do not hold Canadian bank licenses and cannot offer deposit-taking, commercial LC issuance, or bonded-duty escrow; they may operate lawfully as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and provide multi-currency IBAN wallets, SWIFT gateways, and wholesale FX conversion. Provincial licensing (e.g., Alberta, British Columbia, Québec) or exemption requirements must be verified per province; the enhanced medium-risk profile of a non-resident-owned spirits wholesale LP will typically require a Schedule I Canadian bank as the primary settlement layer, with an EMI used only as a secondary disbursement rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal 15% general corporate rate (non-CCPC) plus provincial general rate 8%–16%; the LP itself is fiscally transparent, so each partner is taxed on allocated income at their marginal or corporate rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII default statutory rate for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident. A limited partnership does not pay dividends; profit distributions to a non-resident partner are treated as income allocations, subject to Part I tax under section 2(3) of the Income Tax Act. If a non-resident corporate partner carries on business in Canada through the LP, the after-tax branch profits may also be subject to Part XIV branch tax at the 25% statutory rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is recommended for any non-arm's length cross-border transaction exceeding CAD 5,000,000 in value; a Master File is mandatory for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding CAD 1,000,000,000. Canadian case law also requires contemporaneous transfer pricing records for every non-arm's length non-resident transaction, regardless of amount, to avoid penalty relief loss.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations, but CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and statutory substance-based recharacterization. The LP must maintain a real place of management, substantive bonded-warehouse operations, and genuine partner profit-sharing arrangements; a pure paper entity or an unreasonable allocation of profits to a non-resident partner may be recharacterized under sections 103(2), 247(2) or 253 of the Income Tax Act and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Section 239 of the Income Tax Act, a conviction for tax evasion carries a fine of 50%–200% of the tax evaded and up to five years' imprisonment; CRA can also reassess without limitation period and impose gross-negligence penalties of 50% of any understatement.

Director personal liability: Directors or managing partners who knowingly participate or direct the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the evaded taxes, penalties and interest, and may face travel bans, civil restitution orders and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the partnership structure under GAAR and reattribute all income to the beneficial owner; penalties include a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the reattributed amount, plus interest from the date of the original misassessment. Excise licenses for bonded trade can be canceled by the provincial liquor authority.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the LP is a façade; directors/shareholders are jointly liable for asserted tax and excise liabilities, and may be subject to director residence restrictions and bankruptcy proceedings.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) - Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), the maximum penalty for a corporate entity is CAD 2,000,000 per administrative violation, plus potential criminal liability under the Criminal Code for laundering proceeds of crime; conviction can result in an additional fine of 10% of the laundered value.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly fail to establish a compliance program or who authorize unreported transfers face personal fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to five years; civil forfeiture of the bonded warehouse inventory may also restrict future business.
Transfer Pricing Penalty

Statutory sanction: Under Section 247(3) of the Income Tax Act, a penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment is imposed where the understatement exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue. CRA may additionally disallow deductions for customs duties and excise taxes on the adjusted price.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable only if they were grossly negligent or willfully blind in approving the related-party pricing; such liability is assessed jointly and severally under the Director's Liability provisions of the Income Tax Act and can extend to court-ordered restitution.
