Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Provincial Limited Partnership Registry through the Business and Personal Property Registration branch (e.g., ServiceOntario) in the province of formation, with federal coordination via ISED Corporations Canada for the NUANS name report and the Canada Revenue Agency for the federal Business Number, GST/HST and payroll accounts.

Governing statute

No federal incorporation statute applies to a limited partnership. The LP is created under the provincial Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.9, ss. 2–9), supplemented by the provincial Partnership Act; federal dimensions are governed by the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), ss. 96–103 (pass-through taxation), and the Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement Implementation Act for trade privileges.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (statutory Form 2 under the Limited Partnerships Act), filed digitally with the provincial registry and prefaced by an ISED Corporations Canada NUANS Name Search Report.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 40 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Canadian limited partnership; capital contributions are contractual and set out in the Limited Partnership Agreement. No federal escrow requirement applies, except that under immigration-linked EPC investment programs the contribution may be escrowed in a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank; the registered Declaration must state the aggregate limited partner contribution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the National Fire Code of Canada with annual inspection by the municipal fire marshal; structural load inspection certificate from a licensed structural engineer for racked pallet-storage systems; occupational health and safety clearance under the Canada Labour Code Part II (or the applicable provincial occupational health and safety act), including WHMIS 2015 training, first-aid kit compliance, a workplace violence and harassment policy, and documented emergency evacuation drills for all warehouse personnel.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: The General Partner, in its capacity for the Limited Partnership, (i) adopts the Limited Partnership Agreement and the Declaration of Limited Partnership and authorizes their filing with the Provincial Limited Partnership Registry; (ii) approves the Limited Partners' capital contribution subscriptions and the deposit of contributions into a segregated trust account at a Schedule I Canadian bank; (iii) fixes the registered office at the address stated in the Declaration and appoints the Registered Agent for service of process; (iv) appoints the initial General Partner, Chief Compliance Officer, and Financial Officer, each authorized to execute all statutory declarations, tax business-number registrations, customs import-export account applications, and banking resolutions necessary to complete formation and commence wholesale green coffee bean distribution.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial production licensing board applies to wholesale green coffee distribution; the relevant oversight authorities are the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) for commodity import compliance and the provincial Ministry of Economic Development, Job Creation and Trade or its local counterpart for trade facilitation.

Category: No statutory manufacturing or operating permit is required for the scope of single-origin green coffee bean container distribution; the entity maintains only a municipal business licence, a CBSA import/export account, and a warehouse occupancy permit.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), GST/HST and Business Number registration; online portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and separate GST/HST account using Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts, or via CRA's online Business Registration service. The partnership itself, not its partners, is the registrable person for GST/HST purposes. The application must be filed before the first taxable supply exceeding the small-supplier threshold or before claiming input tax credits / export refunds. Supporting documentation includes: certificate of limited partnership from the provincial registry, constituent agreement, identification of all general partners with their residential status, and details of the Canadian permanent establishment or economic activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and separate GST/HST account using Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts, or via CRA's online Business Registration service. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act, a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent: no entity-level income tax, with income allocated to partners and assessed in their hands. For GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and must file a partnership-level GST/HST return; partners do not separately elect to be the registrant. Available elections include: (1) the annual GST/HST instalment election for closely related persons under s. 156, where applicable; (2) an election under s. 150(1) of the Excise Tax Act to include zero-rated exported goods in a simplified reporting period; and (3) provincial partnership penalties and allocation elections under the applicable limited partnership statutes. No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; however, a foreign non-resident partner may claim relief under an applicable tax treaty to reduce or exempt Canadian source business income, provided the partnership does not have a permanent establishment in Canada.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (exclusive of zero-rated exports and limited supplies) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; if exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required. A registrant may seek voluntary registration when below the threshold to claim input tax credits and claim export refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier exports the goods in the course of commercial activity, satisfies the exporter status requirements, and retains evidence that the goods were physically exported from Canada as soon after delivery as reasonably possible. Specialty green coffee beans sold to non-resident buyers for direct export qualify for 0% GST/HST if the supplier has documentary proof of export and the recipient is a non-resident person. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident (other than services to be performed in or wholly performed in Canada) may also be zero-rated under section 2 of Part V of Schedule VI, provided the ordinary rules of jurisdiction apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A / CADEX electronic export declaration, or Canadian Customs coding form)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill issued by the carrier evidencing export from Canada and consignment to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank wire / documentary letter of credit settlement proof confirming receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the overseas purchaser

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing, or digital stamping at the federal level. A registrant must issue invoices containing prescribed particulars (e.g., supplier name, BN, date, description, tax charged, rate) and may issue electronic invoices if the recipient agrees and the format preserves all legally required data. CRA requires electronic invoices and records to be retained in an accessible, legible format for six years; no real-time API or clearance obligation exists under federal law. Reporting is periodic through GST/HST NETFILE or CRA My Business Account.

CRA may audit any zero-rating claim and require comprehensive supporting records. Failure to substantiate the export renders the supply taxable and triggers GST/HST assessment, plus interest under the Excise Tax Act, s. 280, and a civil penalty of at least 5% of the tax sought to be evaded under s. 281.1(1). Knowingly making a false or misleading statement, participating in an export fraud scheme, or creating fictitious documentation invokes criminal penalties under s. 239 and s. 327(3), including fines up to 250% of the amount in question, imprisonment up to five years, and joint and several liability for officers and directors. CRA may also apply the 'gross negligence' penalty under s. 285.1. Retrospective clawbacks may extend to a four-year reassessment period; misrepresentation by a partner or agent opens the period to eight years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with unrestricted current account convertibility for trade transactions and profit remittances. Capital account transactions are generally free for Canadian-resident entities; foreign-owned LPs face no FX repatriation controls but must comply with tax withholding on distributions. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to FINTRAC reporting thresholds and beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies to export receipts under Canadian law. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds or convert at prevailing market rates. All FX transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 require FINTRAC reporting; import-related FX follows identical reporting obligations without surrender mandates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
