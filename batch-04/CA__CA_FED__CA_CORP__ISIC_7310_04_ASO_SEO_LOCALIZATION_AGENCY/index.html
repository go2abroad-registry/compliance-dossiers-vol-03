Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A federal corporation must maintain a registered office in the province or territory specified in its articles and must be able to receive legal notices and maintain or provide access to required corporate records. A commercial virtual office may be used if it provides a genuine physical address, reliable document reception, access to corporate records when required, and appropriate authorization for service of process. A mere mailbox, accommodation address, or address incapable of supporting records access is unsuitable. For an ASO, SEO, and multilingual localization agency, no federal industry licence is ordinarily required; however, the corporation should maintain commercially credible substance, including service agreements, accounting records, client contracts, website disclosures, personnel or contractor records, data-protection controls, and operational banking evidence. Wise Business and Payoneer onboarding may impose stricter address, ownership, transaction-purpose, and source-of-funds requirements than federal incorporation law.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the CBCA and no escrow account requirement. The articles fix authorized capital and the first board resolution sets issued stated capital, here 100 Class A common shares at US$1.00 each. Subscription proceeds must be credited to the corporation's stated capital account and deposited into the corporate operating account; funds need not be escrowed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under IMF Article VIII for both current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating; no exchange-control permits, repatriation licensing, or prior approval is required for dividend, royalty, interest, or capital distributions. Non-resident profit remittance is subject only to statutory withholding tax (25% domestic, reducible by treaty) and to the solvency tests of the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA). Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are FINTRAC-reportable. Capital account movements from non-resident investors may trigger Investment Canada Act notification thresholds, but no Bank of Canada or OSFI pre-approval applies to an advertising and localization services agency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio of export receipts into CAD. A resident exporter may retain foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts. Mandatory trade information obligations include an export declaration to Statistics Canada / Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for goods shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 per transaction, and FINTRAC reporting of every electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more conducted through a domestic financial entity. Service-export receipts for app store optimization are classified for balance-of-payments purposes but require no conversion or escrow.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange administration agency. FX policy operates under the Bank of Canada floating-rate mandate and the federal Department of Finance / Investment Canada Act framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High viability as supplemental settlement rails; medium as primary treasury locus. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.) and Payoneer operate in Canada as registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), enabling digital onboarding and multicurrency clearing. Legal constraints include absence of CDIC deposit insurance, prohibition on chartered-bank-style deposit-taking and lending, full FINTRAC EFT reporting at CAD 10,000, and elevated counterparty scrutiny when domestic banks observe MSB-concentrated flows. Recommended structure: maintain the chartered bank account as master treasury account and use Wise B2B / Payoneer exclusively for low-value contractor disbursements and cross-border payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all inbound equity injections via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the designated Canadian-dollar or US-dollar settlement account, with purpose descriptor 'Capital Contribution / Equity Injection' and the CBCA share-resolution number referenced in field 72. The receiving bank must match the remitter against the CBCA ISC Register and obtain a funds-received confirmation; transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are FINTRAC-reportable EFT events. No Bank of Canada exchange-control approval is required. Document the subscription with an amended Articles of Incorporation, directors' resolution, and subscription agreement; monitor Investment Canada Act notification thresholds for direct acquisition of a Canadian business before closing. If the shareholder's home jurisdiction applies outward-remittance controls (e.g., PRC SAFE or Indian ODI), secure a boarding letter and complete source-of-funds verification prior to booking.Use SWIFT MT103 customer transfers for low-value recurring settlements, SWIFT MT202/COV for interbank cover transfers, and Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) or documentary collections for purchase-order-backed disbursements. Canada's high-value domestic rail is Lynx (Payments Canada) for real-time gross settlement; cross-border USD transfers clear through correspondent banks via Fedwire or CHIPS. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties exceed CAD 1 million; Master File required if revenue exceeds CAD 500 million

Economic substance scope: Canada applies general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits; no specific ESR regime, but CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial rationale

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of tax evaded plus interest; criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid corporate tax withheld (e.g., payroll, GST/HST) if they failed to exercise due diligence; civil restitution and potential travel bans
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, reassess income, impose penalties up to 50% of tax avoided, and apply GAAR

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for penalties if they knowingly participated in sham arrangements; potential criminal charges for fraud
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal liability for directors; asset forfeiture

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for willful blindness
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (if adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000); interest on reassessed tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they signed false or misleading documents; civil liability for negligence
