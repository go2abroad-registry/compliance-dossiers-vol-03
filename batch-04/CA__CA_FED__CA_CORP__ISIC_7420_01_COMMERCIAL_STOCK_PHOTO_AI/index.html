Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/business-registration) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/my-business-account). In Quebec, Revenu Quebec additionally administers the QST.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) before carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada. Procedure: (1) complete Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or register online via Business Registration Online (BRO); (2) provide entity formation documents (federal/provincial articles of incorporation, certificate of status), registered office address, Canadian permanent establishment address, and a Canadian agent/representative for service of process; (3) select the 'RT' GST/HST program account suffix; (4) when the GST/HST account is opened, begin charging GST/HST on taxable supplies made in Canada on the effective date; (5) retain the CRA-issued confirmation letter and quote the BN on all invoices. A non-resident that only makes export supplies (zero-rated) may elect to register voluntarily to claim input tax credits; mandatory registration applies once the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold is exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian 'Business Corporation' (federal/provincial, elf 8615) is a resident corporation subject to Part I Income Tax Act on worldwide income at entity level; no pass-through, no check-the-box, and no elective transparent treatment for domestic corporations. Territorial scope: worldwide taxation with foreign tax credit relief; active business income earned through a controlled foreign affiliate is deferred until repatriation under the exempt surplus regime, while foreign accrual property income (FAPI) is taxed currently. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian 'Business Corporation' (federal/provincial, elf 8615) is a resident corporation subject to Part I Income Tax Act on worldwide income at entity level; no pass-through, no check-the-box, and no elective transparent treatment for domestic corporations. Territorial scope: worldwide taxation with foreign tax credit relief; active business income earned through a controlled foreign affiliate is deferred until repatriation under the exempt surplus regime, while foreign accrual property income (FAPI) is taxed currently. A Canadian corporation cannot elect into partnership or disregarded-entity status.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Canada; hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.2, 18.3, 18.4 ITA) apply automatically. Statutory elections available: (1) s. 89(11) ITA election to be deemed not to be a Canadian-controlled private corporation; (2) s. 85(1) ITA rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of property to the corporation; (3) s. 156 Excise Tax Act election between closely related corporations (90% common share value/votes) to deem intercorporate supplies to be made for nil consideration for GST/HST; (4) s. 150(3) Excise Tax Act election for a person with de minimis financial services revenue to be deemed not to be a financial institution; (5) for foreign corporations with a Canadian permanent establishment, the Part XIV branch tax can be reduced by an applicable income tax treaty without election, except that treaty-based waivers are claimed via CRA Form NR301/NR302 in respect of withholding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the 'small supplier' threshold is exceeded if the supplier (including associated corporations) makes worldwide taxable supplies of more than CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter, or more than CAD 30,000 in aggregate in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. A non-resident is deemed to be carrying on business in Canada if it solicits orders in Canada (in person or through an agent) or maintains an inventory in Canada; non-resident suppliers of digital services and online marketplace operators are also subject to mandatory registration under the specified digital services rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (1) s. 7 zero-rates a service supplied to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and is not carrying on business in Canada, where the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time it is performed, and is not for use in Canada by the non-resident; (2) s. 10 zero-rates a supply of intangible personal property (including copyrights, digital licences, stock-photo/image licences, and generative visual asset rights) made to a non-resident non-registered person, unless the property is for use in Canada by the recipient otherwise than exclusively in the course of commercial activities; (3) exports of tangible goods are zero-rated when the supplier ships the goods to a destination outside Canada. For a Stock Photography Marketplace / Generative Visual Assets platform (ISIC 7420 / NAICS 541922), licence fees charged to non-resident licensees are zero-rated where the asset is downloaded or used outside Canada, or used in Canada exclusively in the licensee's commercial activities; supplies to Canadian-resident customers and to non-residents carrying on business in Canada are taxable at 5% GST/13-15% HST. The zero-rating claim is substantiated by evidence of the recipient's non-resident status (e.g., residential/business address, foreign registration records), the contractual place-of-use clause, and geo-IP or digital delivery logs.

  • For physical goods exports: Canadian Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A / electronic export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System), Commercial Invoice, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, Packing List
  • For services/IP exports: fully executed licence or subscription agreement identifying the non-resident licensee (legal name, jurisdiction, registered address), asset description, permitted-use scope, and territorial exclusivity clause
  • GST/HST zero-rated invoice issued with 0% rate, citing the applicable zero-rating provision (s. 7 or s. 10, Part V, Schedule VI) and the licensee's non-resident status
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or acquiring-bank foreign-currency payment confirmation evidencing receipt of the licence fee from outside Canada within 180 days of invoicing
  • Supporting operational records: geo-IP download logs, digital delivery receipts, user KYC/onboarding data, IP registration records, and commission statements proving the offshore destination of the digital asset

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), pre-clearance approval, API-reporting mandate, or digital stamping requirement exists under the Excise Tax Act. The CRA accepts paper and electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, XML, PEPPOL) if they contain the supplier's name, GST/HST registration number, date, recipient identification, description of supply, tax rate, and tax amount. E-invoices must be retained in an intelligible format for six years, and records located outside Canada must be made electronically available to the CRA on request; there is no advance digital clearance or fiscal invoice numbering regime.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within the four-year normal reassessment period (extended indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud). If a zero-rating claim fails on audit, the supplier is assessed 5% GST/13-15% HST on the full licence fee with compound interest, and any input tax credits relying on the zero-rated export stream are clawed back. Civil penalties: failure to file/remit penalty (s. 280 Excise Tax Act), gross-negligence penalty equal to 25% of the understated net tax (s. 285), and a 10% penalty for large cross-border transfer-pricing misstatements (s. 247(8) Income Tax Act) where the adjustment exceeds CAD 5,000. Criminal sanctions (s. 327 Excise Tax Act) for wilful evasion carry fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; fabricated bills of lading, depository receipts, or shell non-resident licensees trigger joint CRA-CBSA enforcement referrals, and directors/officers face personal liability for unremitted amounts under s. 323 Excise Tax Act. CRA also verifies that inward remittances in foreign currency are genuine, arm's length, and economically associated with the purported export; invoices outstanding beyond 180 days are rebuttably presumed not to support the zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating currency with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No prior-exchange-control approval is required for foreign investment, dividend remittance, interest payments, or profit repatriation; the only constraints are the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review for controlling acquisitions above statutory thresholds, Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, often reduced by treaty), and corporate law solvency tests for dividends. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for all business purposes and there are no exchange control permits restricting commercial payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion obligation exists for export proceeds or digital-service receipts. Export earnings may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency without Bank of Canada authorization; reporting is limited to FINTRAC cross-border currency and monetary instrument reports for physical movements above CAD 10,000, plus routine income tax and GST/HST declarations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange and monetary policy conduct governed by the Bank of Canada Act, with banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/ATF administration by FINTRAC.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal Part I general corporate rate; combined federal-provincial headline rate of approximately 23%-27% where the corporation maintains a permanent establishment in a province (provincial rates range from 8% in Alberta to 12% in Quebec and the Maritime provinces).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; treaty-reduced to 5% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% of the payer's voting shares and 15% for portfolio shareholders (Canada-US DTA and most OECD-based Canada tax treaties).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1M aggregate fair-market value of all non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents triggers mandatory CRA Form T106; CRA expects contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation when total cross-border intercompany transactions exceed C$5M; Country-by-Country Report (Form RC7000) mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding C$1B.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations registration exists in Canada; substance is tested through (i) central management and control residence analysis; (ii) the 'carrying on business in Canada' permanent establishment rubric under s.253 and s.255.1; (iii) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in s.245; (iv) transfer-pricing documentation obligations; (v) thin-capitalization limits in s.18(4)-(5) and the new Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses (EIFR) 30% EBITDA cap for years beginning after October 1, 2023; a stock-photo AI platform holding only IP licenses and payment-gateway contracts with no Canadian premises, employees, or management will be considered substance-less and exposed to income re-attribution and Part XIII withholding assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Wilful Corporate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163.2: 50% of the understated federal and provincial tax; criminal conviction under s.239 on indictment: fine of 100% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; Criminal Code fraud s.380 carries up to 14 years where the fraud exceeds C$1M; CRA may seize assets via s.160/non-arm's-length assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized false filings are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts under s.227.1 with no relief where gross negligence is proven; CRA can freeze personal bank accounts, issue director travel bans under s.225.2-225.6, and pursue criminal fraud charges with personal restitution obligations.
Phantom Shell Corporation / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR adjustment under s.245 re-attributing licensing revenue to the non-resident beneficial owner; GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit plus arrears interest; deemed-PE assessment under s.253; foreign-reporting penalties up to C$2,500 per unreported Form T1135 plus C$25 per day for each late form.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face false-declaration penalties under s.163.2 (minimum C$100 up to 50% of the tax attributable to the misrepresentation); participation in the shell scheme can ground Criminal Code fraud charges and civil fraudulent-conveyance claims against personal assets.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA re-determination of profits under s.247(2) on arm's-length terms; documentation penalty of C$1,000 per month up to C$24,000; s.247(3)(b) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where reasonable documentation is absent; denial of interest deductions under thin-cap rules; EIFR limitation of net interest to 30% of tax-adjusted EBITDA; daily compounded interest on all reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed inaccurate T106 filings or declarations are subject to gross-negligence penalties under s.163.2; directors who orchestrated the conduit-pricing structure may be personally assessed as participants in the tax benefit, with personal restitution liability and possible international travel prohibitions pending CRA criminal referral.
AML and MSB Payment Rail Misuse

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under PCMLTFA up to C$1,000,000 per violation for corporations and C$500,000 for individuals; failure-to-register is a criminal offense punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment; criminal money laundering under s.462.31 Criminal Code carries up to 10 years imprisonment; forfeiture of funds under s.462.38; FINTRAC suspends MSB tolerances where suspicious-transaction reporting fails on Stripe/PayPal payouts.

Director personal liability: Officers who directed, authorized, or acquiesced in AML violations are personally liable under s.73.1 PCMLTFA with fines up to C$500,000; directors may be prosecuted for money laundering as principals; travel bans can be imposed to prevent flight pending resolution of the AML and tax charges.
Part XIII Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Aggravated non-remittance penalty of 10% of the amount under s.227(9); late remittance penalty of 3% per month up to 12 months on a repeat basis; NR4 filing penalty of C$25 per day per slip up to C$2,500; s.212.2 re-characterization of disguised distributions as dividends subject to Part XIII; interest compounding daily from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors have strict joint-and-several personal liability for unremitted Part XIII withholdings under s.227.1; the deemed-trust amount is not dischargeable in personal bankruptcy; CRA may collect from personal assets of directors within 2 years of the corporate dissolution.
Dividend Stripping / Surplus Stripping

Statutory sanction: s.84.1 re-characterizes share sale proceeds as a dividend requiring Part XIII withholding at 25%; s.246 imposes tax on artificial surplus-stripping arrangements; CRA applies GAAR to eliminate the tax benefit; interest under s.161(1) runs from the original dividend date; s.212.2 deems a dividend where winding-up distributions avoid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who implemented the strip can be reassessed as principal shareholders and become personally liable for unpaid withholding tax arising from the deeming provisions; misrepresentation of fair value in the share sale exposes directors to s.163.2 penalties and criminal fraud liability.
