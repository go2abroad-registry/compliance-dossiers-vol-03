Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Primary portals: CRA My Business Account (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and Business Registration Online; GST/HST NETFILE for electronic return filing. Customs border operations (export declarations) are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Provincial sales taxes are separately administered by Revenu Quebec and provincial ministries.

E-government filing portal

A federally incorporated CBCA corporation automatically receives a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA upon Corporations Canada online incorporation. Program accounts are then added via Business Registration Online, by telephone (1-800-959-5525), or by Form RC1: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RD (import/export). A foreign parent establishing a subsidiary may pre-register with Form RC4601 (non-resident GST/HST) or RC1 submitted to CRA Summerside Tax Centre; processing takes 10-15 business days and CRA mails access codes within 5-10 days. Registration must precede Canadian payroll, GST/HST collection, or customs activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act (ITA) is mandatory, and hybrid mismatch rules (ss.18.2, 18.3, 94B ITA) are non-elective. Relevant elections: non-calendar taxation year (s.249.1 ITA); capital dividend election (s.83(2)); paid-up capital increase election (s.89(11)); s.85 asset rollover; associated-corporation allocation; GST/HST s.156 election for closely related corporations; s.167 change-of-use election; quick method or simplified input tax credit election for registrants.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold under s.148 ETA); zero-rated exports count toward this threshold. Non-resident digital service suppliers (including AI/LLM API platforms) providing specified digital supplies to Canadian recipients must register under the simplified GST/HST regime once Canadian-source taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters or current quarter (effective July 1, 2021). Registration is voluntary but recommended where all supplies are zero-rated, to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border AI/LLM API token and inference services supplied to a non-resident recipient qualify for 0% GST/HST under Schedule VI, Part V, ss.22-23 of the Excise Tax Act, provided: (i) the recipient is a non-resident person; (ii) the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada; (iii) the service is not performed in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; and (iv) the service is not consumed by an individual physically present in Canada. The zero-rating applies to both exported services and licensed intangible digital property; input tax credits on Canadian hosting, compute, office, and professional expenses remain fully recoverable with adequate export substantiation.

  • Executed master subscription agreement and order form identifying the non-resident customer and jurisdiction
  • Commercial invoice denominated in CAD expressly stating 0% GST/HST with zero-rating statutory reference
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103 credit advice, card scheme settlement statement, or USDC/stablecoin transfer receipt
  • Non-resident status certificate: certificate of residence, foreign incorporation certificate, or treaty residency attestation
  • API usage logs and token consumption records evidencing offshore service delivery
  • Where physical media or hardware is exported: Form B13A export declaration filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) within 15 days of exportation, plus bill of lading

None. Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls; e-invoicing is voluntary, with a federal PEPPOL-based e-invoicing pilot under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (2024-2025). Electronic filing of GST/HST returns via NETFILE is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5M. All books, records, invoices, and supporting export documentation must be retained for six years.

CRA may reassess within four years of the original assessment; the limitation period is unlimited for fraud, neglect, or willful default. Unsubstantiated zero-rating triggers retroactive GST/HST at 5%-15% with arrears interest and penalties: 10% under s.285.1 ETA or 50% under s.163(2) ITA for gross negligence; criminal evasion under ss.327 ETA and 239 ITA draws fines up to 200% of the tax and imprisonment. Failure to retain export records attracts a CAD 500 penalty per failure. Canada has no federal statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, but export receivables outstanding beyond one year may trigger deemed interest under s.17 ITA; a 180-day documentary repatriation covenant is therefore market standard and supports the export journal.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions and Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation are not subject to exchange control approval, conversion quotas, or central-bank permit layers. Frictions arise solely from non-FX regulatory domains: Part XIII withholding tax filings for dividends and royalties, CRS/FATCA reporting, FINTRAC large-transaction reporting for amounts equivalent to C$10,000 or more, and Canadian CFC attribution rules for passive foreign income of controlled foreign affiliates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion timelines, or minimum conversion ratios for export receipts in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency in permitted foreign-currency bank accounts, use their CAD earnings for operational liquidity, and enter into voluntary forward or swap contracts. The only obligations are contemporaneous financial recordkeeping, income tax recognition under Canadian GAAP/IFRS, and transfer-pricing compliance for intercompany export settlements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, Department of Finance Canada, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally chartered banks. Canada maintains no codified Foreign Exchange Control Act; the Canadian dollar floats freely under a market-determined exchange rate regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for a pure offshore electronic-money institution and Medium for a Canadian-registered money service business (MSB). An offshore EMI is not permitted to take deposits or hold CAD in Canada unless licensed as an authorized foreign bank branch; it may, however, be used as a payment-processor conduit for AI API subscription billing if supported by Stripe Canada as an MSB. Core treasury liquidity should reside in a Schedule I Canadian bank account, not in an off-Channel EMI wallet.
