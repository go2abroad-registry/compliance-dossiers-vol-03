Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration and collection authority; registration and filng through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA) portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership establishing a Canadian presence or carrying on business in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1, Request for a Business Number, and must separately register for a GST/HST account when making taxable supplies in Canada unless the small supplier threshold is met. Non-resident partnerships without a permanent establishment in Canada may still require a BN to file certain returns, pay GST/HST, or claim refunds; the CRA also issues a Trust Account Number (RT) for payroll or a GST/HST number (RT) for indirect tax purposes. The application can be submitted by an authorized representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Canadian limited partnership is a fiscally transparent flow-through entity for income tax purposes; income and losses are allocated to partners who are separately taxable. For GST/HST purposes, a partnership may elect to be treated as a separate registrant, but each general partner is jointly and severally liable for GST/HST obligations.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification system. Partnerships are statutorily flow-through under the Income Tax Act; no affirmative election is needed. Hybrid mismatch rules under subsections 18.2, 18.4, 18.5, and 18.6 and the foreign affiliate investment rules (s. 113.1) may restrict the deductibility of interest and other expenses arising from hybrid arrangements. GST/HST joint election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act is available for partnerships and related registrants to simplify reporting.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) per single calendar quarter over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident taxable suppliers with commercial activity in Canada may be required to register regardless of threshold when carrying on a business in Canada and making taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property (pre-packaged hotpot soup base bricks) are zero-rated for GST/HST purposes when exported by the supplier from Canada pursuant to a sale to a non-resident and delivered outside Canada, provided the supplier maintains evidence of export as required under section 30.1 and paragraph 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Additionally, a non-resident’s direct purchase and removal from Canada can satisfy zero-rating if the seller obtains proof of export within the prescribed time.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CAED, or CCI for low-value shipments)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading issued by the carrier (or air waybill / truck manifest)
  • Outward foreign freight or shipping contract showing delivery outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation of payment from the non-resident purchaser
  • Customs proof-of-export confirmation from the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) where applicable

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API clearance for electronic invoices. Registered persons must issue GST/HST invoices containing prescribed information, retain electronic books and records in an accessible format, and file GST/HST returns electronically in certain cases. The CRA’s MyBA and GST/HST NETFILE services handle electronic return submission and refund claims. No compulsory continuous transaction control (CTC) model is in effect at the federal level.

A false or unsupported zero-rating claim triggers reassessment of uncollected GST/HST at the full 5% rate, interest from the due date, and penalties of 25% of the tax owing for a knowing or grossly negligent false statement under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act; the CRA can apply an additional 100% penalty for repeated violations. For income tax, overstated export revenue or artificial arrangements may be attacked under the general anti-avoidance rule with adjustments, re-assessments, and penalties. Canada does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline for export sales, but a 180-day inward remittance cycle is the administrative expectation in commercial export substantiation; failure to provide credible payment evidence will jeopardize zero-rating and trigger clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII obligations and provides full current-account and capital-account convertibility without exchange-control permits. Profit remittances by a Canadian limited partnership to foreign partners are freely permitted, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax on certain distributions, treaty relief documentation (NR301/NR303), and standard anti-money-laundering verification. No central-bank approval, ex-ante license, or administrative conversion authorization is required for cross-border payments. The LP may hold foreign-currency operating accounts freely, but large EFTs (over CAD 10,000) trigger FINTRAC reporting by the reporting financial institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in Canada. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory timeline requiring repatriation of export proceeds into CAD. Exporters may retain receivables in foreign currency, remit them at their discretion, and consolidate treasury balances. Export transactions must, however, be recorded consistently for income tax and GST/HST purposes, must be supported by commercial documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, CI/CO), and any large currency transfers are automatically reportable to FINTRAC thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential supervision); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML surveillance). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority or exchange-control legislation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. Canada does not issue a distinct 'electronic money institution' (EMI) charter; non-bank payment service providers must apply for registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada for retail payment activities. An offshore digital EMI may not independently provide clearing in CAD unless it is registered or operates through a partnership with a regulated Schedule I Canadian bank. For the LP, an RPAA-registered Canadian fintech PSP (e.g., Payoneer, Wise Edge Canada) is viable for small-volume settlements, but primary treasury clearing through a Tier 1 bank is legally safer for SWIFT/LC trade flows. Restrictions include transaction-reporting obligations, segregation of funds, and inability of unregistered offshore EMIs to issue local settlement guarantees without entering prohibited deposit-taking activities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound capital injections into the Canadian LP, the compliant routing is a Swift MT103 / MT202 from the overseas investor bank to the LP's CAD-denominated or multi-currency account at a Schedule I bank. The following purpose/business codes may be provided in ISO 20022 format as required by receiving institutions: 'CAPT' (Capital Transfer), 'COST' (Capital Contribution), or 'DEPT' (Investment) depending on bank mapping. Supporting documents, including signed capital-call notices, certificate of LP status, and FATCA-NR301 details, must accompany the wire to satisfy FINTRAC and bank KYC.
