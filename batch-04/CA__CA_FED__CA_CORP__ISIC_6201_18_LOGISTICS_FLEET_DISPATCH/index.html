Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Federal tax administration. Registration portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (canada.ca).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or provincially to obtain articles of incorporation; 2) apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with supporting tax accounts (GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll) via BRO, Form RC1, or by fax/mail; 3) CRA issues the BN immediately for online applications; 4) non-resident entities without a Canadian permanent establishment may use the CRA simplified non-resident GST/HST registration procedure with proof of foreign incorporation, board resolutions, and an authorized representative within a recognized tax treaty jurisdiction; 5) update registration within 30 days of material changes (address, directors, business activity).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a federal/provincial business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer resident in Canada subject to Part I corporate income tax; if Canadian-controlled, it qualifies for the small business deduction (SBD) on active business income up to the CAD 500,000 federal limit; if non-resident-controlled, SBD is denied and Part XIII withholding applies to dividends, with potential branch tax exposure on unincorporated Canadian business income of the foreign parent. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a federal/provincial business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer resident in Canada subject to Part I corporate income tax; if Canadian-controlled, it qualifies for the small business deduction (SBD) on active business income up to the CAD 500,000 federal limit; if non-resident-controlled, SBD is denied and Part XIII withholding applies to dividends, with potential branch tax exposure on unincorporated Canadian business income of the foreign parent. Canada does not recognize pass-through or US-style check-the-box classification for corporations.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. Relevant elections: (i) ETA s. 156 closely related corporation election to treat intercorporate supplies as non-taxable for GST/HST; (ii) ETA s. 227 Quick Method election for simplified GST/HST accounting; (iii) ITA s. 216 election enabling non-residents to file a Canadian net-income return on rental/royalty income; (iv) ITA hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.4-18.7) deny deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements without a discretionary 'opt-out', though taxpayers may elect to apply deemed-timing adjustments prospectively; (v) Part XIII reduced treaty withholding elections via Form NR302/NR301 for cross-border service and royalty payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — annual worldwide taxable supplies made by a person trigger mandatory GST/HST registration (ETA s. 240(1)); for non-residents, registration is required only if they carry on business in Canada and earn more than CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies sourced to Canada; zero-rated supplies count toward the threshold, but exempt financial supplies do not.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

SaaS and fleet-telematics platform subscriptions supplied to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 23 of the ETA, provided the supply is not made in the course of a business carried on by the non-resident in Canada and does not relate to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada. Under ETA s. 142.1(2), a supply of a service is made outside Canada when the recipient is outside Canada and the supplier's place of supply is not in Canada, taking the supply outside the GST/HST base; the supplier may zero-rate the export and claim full input tax credits (ITCs) on related Canadian inputs. Physical logistics hardware export supplies are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 with proof of export within the statutory timeframe.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Canada Automated Export Declaration (CAED) or export reporting via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) with proof of the Canadian Export Reporting Code
  • Bill of Lading (ocean B/L, AWB, CMR waybill, or truck carrier manifest evidencing physical export for goods components)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank issuance, SWIFT MT103, or foreign currency conversion advice evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident customer
  • Signed SaaS subscription agreement / services contract identifying the non-resident customer, service description, delivery location, and jurisdiction of consumption
  • Supporting evidence of recipient's non-resident status (foreign tax residency certificate, IP geolocation logs of platform access, corporate record of the customer)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API stamping, or digital codification regime exists in Canada (as of 2025). E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable via the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Network (Peppol BIS 3.0.0) for B2B and B2G procurement. CRA requires registrants to retain books and records, including electronically generated invoices, in an electronically readable format for six years (ETA s. 286), and CRA digital auditors may request delivery of summarized transaction data upon written notice; no pre-clearance of invoices is required.

CRA may reassess and claw back zero-rated treatment within four years of the original assessment (ETA s. 298), extended to seven years for misrepresentation or neglect and unlimited for fraud; where documentation is incomplete, the supply is reclassified as taxable at the full 5% GST (plus HST where applicable), net tax is reassessed with arrears interest, and refunds are reversed. Penalties under ETA ss. 280-285 include CAD 250 per false statement, 5%-10% of net tax for repeat misrepresentations, 50% gross-negligence penalties under ITA s. 163.2 for participation in false export claims, and criminal evasion fines up to 250% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment of up to five years under ETA s. 285 and ITA s. 239; the exporter must retain the complete documentation bundle for a minimum of six years to sustain the zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada imposes no general restriction on current account payments, capital account transactions, foreign currency purchases, or dividend repatriation. Equity injections, intercompany balances, loan advances, and profit remittances may be effected freely through authorized dealers. The principal compliance hurdles are contractual and tax related: withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends and deemed interest, and the enhanced documentary due diligence obligations under the PCMLTFA imposed on Canadian banks. Capital account repatriations are not screened by the Bank of Canada but may be subject to FINTRAC large EFT reporting where a reporting threshold is met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement in Canada. Exporters are not required to convert foreign currency receipts into Canadian dollars, do not face a mandated conversion ratio, and may retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts with authorized chartered banks. No timeline for repatriation is fixed by statute or Bank of Canada regulation. The applicable controls are the general record-keeping, identification, and large transaction reporting duties under the PCMLTFA and the contractual obligations of the bank's account terms.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, together with the Department of Finance and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT obligations; OSFI supervises federally regulated financial institutions. Canada does not operate a dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration, and there is no central bank approval requirement for cross-border capital flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Limited. A foreign fintech or digital EMI is not permitted to operate as a 'bank' in Canada because deposit-taking requires federal incorporation or a bank charter under the Bank Act. Non-resident EMIs may, however, operate as money service businesses by registering with FINTRAC as an MSB and, if engaged in retail payment services, by complying with the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by the Bank of Canada. Such entities are viable only as payment settlement enablers, not as substitutes for a regulated capital escrow or operational account at a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital is effected via SWIFT MT103 from the parent investor to the Canadian operating account, with a consolidated narrative in field 70 such as 'Capital contribution for general corporate and treasury purposes'. No prior Bank of Canada or OSFI approval is required. The corporate entity must ensure the Canadian bank has a completed EFT waiver and that the wire references the company legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, and corporate account number. Intragroup financing paid as foreign currency is reported to the CRA under tax information schedules as applicable, and any single EFT equal to or above CAD 10,000 may be subject to FINTRAC large EFT reporting. Settlement occurs via the Canadian Lynx wholesale payment system for CAD-denominated finality or via correspondent clearing for USD inbound wires.Trade settlements are executed using SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for direct supplier payments, with CAD/domestic disbursements cleared through Lynx or the Real-Time Rail, USD disbursements cleared through correspondent U.S. bank networks, and high-value FX-related payments settled on a payment-versus-payment basis through CLS Bank where both counterparties are CLS qualifying. For significant commercial transactions, a documentary Commercial Letter of Credit may be issued on behalf of the Canadian entity with the bank acting as issuing or confirming bank, subject to UCP 600 rules.
