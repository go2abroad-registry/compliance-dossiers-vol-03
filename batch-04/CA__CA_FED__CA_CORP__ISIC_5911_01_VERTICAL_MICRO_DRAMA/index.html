Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - My Business Account portal and GST/HST NETFILE

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity doing business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, which serves as the statutory TIN. Apply online through Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1, requesting the GST/HST program account (RC). Registration is mandatory when taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident suppliers of digital services may use the simplified GST/HST registration. The same BN is used for corporate income tax, payroll, and GST/HST; non-resident corporations may appoint a Canadian tax representative under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-incorporated entity doing business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, which serves as the statutory TIN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections; corporate status is determined under the Canada Business Corporations Act and income tax law. Relevant domestic elections include: s. 85 rollover (tax-deferred transfer of property on incorporation/reorganization), s. 89(4) paid-up capital election, s. 95 foreign affiliate deemed-dividend election, and anti-hybrid mismatch conformity elections under ss. 18.4-18.5 and 20.5 of the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST, a registrant may elect under s. 156 to combine branches or use the quick method if the entity's taxable supplies (including those of associates) are no greater than CAD 400,000 in a fiscal year.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada in any single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); the threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of streaming and digital services based on worldwide and Canadian supply volumes, without requiring physical presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s. 145 of the Excise Tax Act to: (i) supplies of goods that are exported by the supplier (documented export to a non-resident); (ii) supplies of services performed wholly outside Canada (or in Canada directly in respect of exported movable property) to a non-resident recipient; and (iii) supplies of intangible personal property, including streaming licences, copyright, and digital content, to a non-resident where all consideration is reasonably attributable to the enjoyment of the property outside Canada. For the vertical micro-drama business, sales of distribution rights to non-resident platforms and download sublicences are zero-rated where the geographic exploitation is confined to territories outside Canada; direct supplies to Canadian subscribers through app stores are taxable at 5% GST (or HST in participating provinces).

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) record
  • Commercial invoice stating zero-rating under s. 145 of the Excise Tax Act
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill for physical media, or digital delivery logs / download reports for intangible content
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign currency bank statement evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Distribution or licence agreement with non-resident counterparty defining territorial rights
  • Evidence of non-resident status (certificate of incorporation, permanent establishment analysis, or NR tax form)

Canada has no federal real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, XML clearance, or digital stamping mandate. GST/HST registrants may provide paper or PDF invoices, although electronic invoices and PDFs are valid. Registrants whose taxable supplies exceed CAD 1 million in a fiscal year must file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account. No API-based tax authority clearance is required, but CRA can request supplier data in XML, CSV, or Excel during audits.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export beyond the four-year normal reassessment period if there is misrepresentation, negligence, fraud, or wilful default; denial of the zero-rating results in reapportioned GST/HST output tax plus interest under s. 280 of the Excise Tax Act. Gross negligence penalties of 25% of the net deficiency (ETA s. 285.1) and false-statement penalties under ITA s. 163.2 (50% of the misrepresented amount) may be imposed; criminal tax evasion can attract fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. Directors and officers remain jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST under ETA s. 323, and clawback risks are heightened in digital-export audits because CRA scrutinizes the geographic enjoyment of streaming rights and requires irrefutable non-resident evidence within the audit response period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar is free-floating with no exchange controls, no conversion licensing, and no advance approval requirements for inward or outward flows. Profit remittance is subject only to standard fiscal gates: Part I corporate income tax on earned net income of CA_CORP at combined federal/provincial rates, a 25% Part XIII gross withholding tax on dividends, royalties, and certain interest paid to non-residents, and treaty-rate reductions (US typically 5%-15%, PRC typically 5%-10%, UK typically 5%-10% depending on ownership tier and treaty residency evidence). No exchange-control choke point exists on repatriation; practical hurdles are limited to FINTRAC threshold reporting on electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, CRS/FATCA information reporting by the financial institution, and bank-level enhanced due diligence on flows from non-transparent jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion obligation exists in Canada. The Foreign Exchange Control Act regime was repealed in 1951, and neither the Bank of Canada Act nor the Currency Act imposes repatriation or conversion ratios. Export and streaming receipts (e.g., USD remittances from Apple App Store, Google Play, and Stripe) may be kept indefinitely in USD, held in a multi-currency operating account, or converted to CAD at market rates. Reporting duties are confined to FINTRAC obligations applicable to reporting financial institutions (incoming/outgoing EFTs exceeding CAD 10,000) and the CAD 10,000+ physical cross-border currency declaration; none of these restrict the exporter's retention or conversion rights.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole monetary and exchange-rate policy authority; residual foreign exchange powers rest with the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act. Canada maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange control licensing body; day-to-day enforcements of payment transparency are administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and by OSFI for bank prudential conduct.

Rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a settlement utility only; LOW-MEDIUM as a primary cash custodian. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50,000 for local file (related party transactions); CAD 50,000,000 for master file (group revenue)

Economic substance scope: Limited applicability under Canadian law; relies on General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules to address artificial arrangements lacking economic substance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: up to 2 years imprisonment and $100,000 fine; Indictable: up to 5 years imprisonment and $1,000,000 fine

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability, restitution orders, and potential disqualification from corporate directorships
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to $100,000 per violation; director fines and imprisonment for willful non-compliance

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors personally liable for unpaid taxes, penalties, and possible criminal charges
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustments to income up to 10-50% of transfer pricing adjustment; penalties of $1,000 to $100,000 per transaction

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for penalties and interest if they directed or acquiesced in the non-compliance
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to $100,000 for corporations; criminal penalties including imprisonment

Director personal liability: Personal liability for directors who fail to exercise due diligence, including fines and jail time
