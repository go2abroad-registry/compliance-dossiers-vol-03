Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Primary online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-registration-online.html; My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html; GST/HST Non-Resident Simplified Registration portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-goods-services-tax-harmonized-sales-tax/digital-economy-gst-hst.html.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity incorporated as a Canadian Business Corporation (federal CBCA or provincial act) is automatically resident in Canada for Income Tax Act purposes. The CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) as the statutory tax identifier; the BN is integrated with GST/HST, payroll, income tax, and import/export program accounts (RT, RP, RC, RM suffixes). For federal incorporations, Corporations Canada transmits corporate data to the CRA, which then issues the BN without a separate application. For provincial incorporations or non-resident applicants, the responsible officer must submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online via BRO or by mail to the applicable CRA tax centre, attaching the certificate of incorporation, corporate bylaws, foreign registration evidence, and identification for directors/beneficial owners. The recommended sequence is: (1) incorporate under the CBCA or provincial statute; (2) confirm the CRA-issued BN and open the RC income tax account; (3) apply for the RT GST/HST account where taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 single-quarter threshold, or voluntarily to claim input tax credits on Canadian startup costs; (4) open an RP payroll account when Canadian employees are hired; (5) open an RM customs account if physical goods are imported/exported; (6) for non-resident shareholders, designate a representative for service and comply with the CBCA beneficial-ownership transparency register. A non-resident vendor without Canadian incorporation or permanent establishment may instead register under the GST/HST Non-Resident Simplified Registration regime, which issues a simplified account (BN + RT program account) without requiring full Part I tax compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope; Tax_Exempt_Zone: Not_Applicable. A Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (federal general corporate rate: 15% after the 13% general rate reduction; 9% for eligible Canadian-controlled private corporation active business income up to the CAD 500,000 federal business limit; 28% basic rate net of the 10% provincial abatement before reductions). It is not a pass-through entity; shareholders are taxed only on dividends or capital gains, with Part XIII withholding on dividends paid to non-residents (generally 25%, reduced by treaty, e.g., 5% for a US parent by election). A resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations without a Canadian permanent establishment are taxable only on Canadian-source income via Part I and the 25% Part XIV branch tax, subject to treaty relief. No federal tax-exempt zone applies to AI data-labeling BPO activities; provincial tax credits and Ontario/Quebec digital-industry incentives are outside this federal dossier.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is determined by incorporation and residence, not by elective filing. Available elections relevant to a BPO corporation include: (i) ETA s. 156 election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration for GST/HST, eliminating intercompany GST/HST administration on intra-group services; (ii) ETA s. 211.1 election for a non-resident 'qualifying non-resident' to report GST/HST under the simplified digital-supplies regime (available to an offshore parent, not to the Canadian resident BPO subsidiary); (iii) ITA s. 216 election for non-residents earning Canadian rental income and ITA s. 219(2) branch-tax reinvestment election reducing Part XIV branch tax where Canadian business profits are reinvested in Canadian property; (iv) ITA s. 89(11)/s. 256 elections affecting CCPC status and associated-company aggregation for the small business deduction; (v) hybrid mismatch treatments are governed by mandatory domestic rules (ITA ss. 94.2-94.4 and s. 18(4)), not by elective mechanisms, and treaty tie-breaker/competent authority relief is the only avenue for hybrid entity residual frictions; there is no bilateral US-Canada 'check-the-box' reciprocal election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold. A supplier ceases to be a small supplier and must register for GST/HST if the value of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter, or if the aggregate for the immediately preceding four calendar quarters exceeds CAD 30,000. For non-residents supplying digital services, including remote AI-data-labeling and database services to Canadian recipients, the simplified registration regime applies once Canadian-attributable taxable revenues exceed CAD 30,000 in any 12-month period. Registration below the threshold is permitted and is commonly elected by export-oriented BPOs to claim input tax credits on Canadian computing costs, salaries, and overhead. There is no separate business-licence threshold; the GST/HST registration threshold is the sole federal VAT trigger.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility for the CA_FED scope follows ETA Schedule VI. Exported goods are zero-rated under Part V, items 1-4 where the exporter retains evidence of export within the prescribed time. Data-labeling, AI-training annotation, RLHF content moderation, and related BPO services are zero-rated under Part V, s. 7 (services supplied to a non-resident) provided that: (a) the recipient is a non-resident at the time the supply is made; (b) the recipient is not registered for GST/HST in respect of the supply; (c) the service is not rendered in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; and (d) the service is not supplied to an individual who is present in Canada. Pure digital/offshore data services delivered by remote access qualify. If the service beneficiary is a Canadian-resident customer, if the training data concerns Canadian physical assets, or if the contract is structured as onshore BPO with Canadian-subcontractor deployment, the supply is taxable at 5% GST (or 13-15% HST). The supplier must obtain evidence of non-resident status before filing the return; otherwise, zero-rating is denied and tax assessed.

  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of settlement in foreign currency from the non-resident client

None. Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API-based invoice validation, or digital stamping. GST/HST registrants may issue invoices in paper or electronic form provided the invoice contains the prescribed particulars (supplier BN, recipient, date, description, tax shown separately), and all source records must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286; SOR/91-30). Electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE or via EDI) is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million, and T2 Corporation Income Tax Returns must be filed electronically for corporations with gross revenue exceeding CAD 1 million. The federal government announced in Budget 2024 a strategic commitment to a pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework compatible with the Peppol network, but adoption is voluntary and there is no legislative mandate or clearance gateway as of the knowledge cutoff.

The CRA audits export zero-rating claims by examining the commercial contract, the non-resident status of the recipient, the place-of-supply analysis, and the exporter's records. If the export is not substantiated or the recipient is found to be resident, the CRA will reverse the zero-rating and assess the 5% GST (or 13-15% HST), disallow related input tax credits, and charge arrears interest at the prescribed rate, compounded daily. Civil penalties: a false-statement penalty under ETA s. 280(1) of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated; if the CRA establishes knowledge or gross negligence, a 50% gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 285.1(1). Criminal exposure: wilful evasion or false declarations under ETA s. 285 are punishable by fines and imprisonment on summary conviction or indictment. The normal four-year reassessment window under ETA s. 298 is extended indefinitely where the return involves misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect. Separately, foreign receivables left outstanding beyond the 180-day commercial benchmark may attract transfer-pricing adjustments under ITA s. 247 with 10% penalties for documented failure, deemed-interest income under ITA ss. 17/80.4 for non-arm's length loans, and AML/KYC scrutiny by the financial institution processing the inward remittance. Canada does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is adopted as the prudential and transfer-pricing audit benchmark.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted: no exchange-control permits, capital import/export licenses, or prior central bank approvals are required for equity injections, service exports, or dividend repatriation. Repatriation is taxed at source under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25% non-resident withholding on dividends, typically reduced to 5-15% under applicable tax treaties). An AI data-labeling BPO exporting services is treated as an ordinary exporter; USD receipts may be freely converted or retained in USD accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules: no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to credit receipts to a designated domestic account. Export invoices may be settled in USD or any freely usable currency via SWIFT MT103, Wise, or PayPal Payouts; the entity may hold foreign currency offshore or in a domestic USD sub-account without penalty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - Canada's central bank and supervisor of retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); prudential regulation of banks supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No dedicated foreign-exchange control authority exists; the former Foreign Exchange Control Board was dissolved and the Foreign Exchange Control Act repealed in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Rating: Medium-High as cross-border payment rails, Low-Medium as a primary treasury bank. Offshore EMIs (UK/FCA or EU-licensed e-money institutions) are not chartered banks in Canada: they cannot take deposits, provide Canadian-dollar clearing, offer CDIC-insured balances, or extend credit. They may process B2B payments, but providers serving Canadian clients must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act or fall within its transitional window; Wise is registered as a Canadian MSB-type payment service provider and PayPal operates through PayPal Canada Co. Legal constraint: capital escrow, FX forwards, and commercial credit must be hosted at a Schedule I chartered bank; offshore EMI balances should be capped at operating float only.
