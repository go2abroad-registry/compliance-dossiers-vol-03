Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For foreign entities: (1) assess supply nexus in Canada to determine mandatory vs. voluntary registration; (2) apply for a Business Number (BN) via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 (Non-Resident Registration); (3) provide legal entity proofs (certificate of incorporation, articles, directors, foreign address, authorized Canadian representative if applicable); (4) CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number, supplemented by a program account suffix (e.g., RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RC for corporate income tax); (5) obtain a separate import/export account through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CARM portal for customs purposes; (6) for dangerous goods forwarding, comply with Transport Canada's TDG Regulations licensing as a carrier or handler, which is a separate regulatory registration outside CRA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

There is no check-the-box regime for Canadian corporations; a Canada Business Corporation is taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Available statutory elections affecting cross-border structure include: s. 128.1(6)/(7) deemed disposition election for tax emigration; s. 216(1) election for non-residents to report Canadian-source rental/taxable income; s. 93(2) election for foreign affiliate surplus distribution characterization; s. 34(2) election for contingency reserves; and GST/HST elections for closely related corporations (Form GST20) including the direct settlement election (Form GST25). Proposed hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act s. 18.2 and s. 18.21) are expected to operate statutorily with no elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies; mandatory registration for corporations resident in Canada once threshold is exceeded; non-resident persons carrying on a business in Canada are subject to the same threshold on supplies made in Canada, with voluntary registration permitted below threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports is available under section 1 of Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act for supplies of goods exported from Canada to a non-resident purchaser, and under section 7 for international transportation services of goods to a destination outside Canada (including freight forwarding services for dangerous goods). The supplier must maintain contemporaneous documentary evidence showing that the goods were physically exported within the time limits prescribed by CRA (generally 60 days for direct shipments, or 18-month period for installment or drop-shipment scenarios) and supplied to a non-resident who does not use or consume the goods in Canada.

  • Validated Canada Customs Export Declaration (CAED) / Canadian Automated Export Declaration (G49 EDI) or ACI e-manifest for the outbound freight
  • Commercial invoice and itemized packing list
  • Bill of Lading (air or sea) issued by the carrier
  • Proof of export conveyance (terminal receipt, sailing/airway write-off, or carrier track-and-trace manifest)
  • Destination evidence showing delivery to a foreign port or foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or equivalent bank credit advice evidencing foreign currency settlement
  • Dangerous Goods Declaration (IMDG/ADR/TDG) issued by the DG forwarder and shipper for regulated hazardous chemicals

Canada does not impose a national real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime for e-invoices. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoice standard (Peppol/CIUS) is recommended for B2B and public procurement. CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST EDI for certain thresholds, requires preservation of source records for six years, and requires export declarations through the Canada Export Reporting System (CAED) or ACE e-manifest for goods. No fiscal inventory lock or API-based clearance is currently statutory for GST/HST.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export supplies if documentary proof is deficient, applying the full GST/HST rate (5% federal GST, or higher HST in participating provinces) to the consideration. Reassessments attract interest under the Excise Tax Act, and penalties under sections 280-283 can reach 25% of the net tax; deliberate misstatements or fraudulent claims may trigger the gross-negligence penalty under subsection 285(1) plus criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Criminal Code section 380, carrying fines and imprisonment. Additionally, CRA can deny refunds and apply a 18-month limitation window for export proof; failure to deliver the prescribed documents within the statutory retention period results in loss of zero-rating and immediate clawback of any refund previously credited.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control permits, no central bank approval for profit remittance, and no quantitative restrictions on dividends, royalties, interest, intercompany loans, or disposal of Canadian assets. Cross-border payments remain subject to FINTRAP electronic funds transfer reporting and Part XIII withholding tax for certain amounts payable to non-residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not require exporters to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any government body, does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio, and does not prescribe a holding timeline for export receipts. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency as long as they are lawfully transacted through a Canadian reporting institution; a FINTRAP electronic funds transfer report is required for any inbound or outbound wire of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; currency regulation operates under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Financial Administration Act, with prudential and AML/privacy supervision administered by OSFI, FINTRAP, and the Department of Finance Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low. Canada does not have a direct EMI licence in the EU/UK sense; non-bank payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act and with FINTRAP. An offshore digital EMI cannot access LVTS clearing directly and must transact through a Canadian Schedule I bank sponsor. For a high-risk dangerous-goods freight forwarder, Canadian banks generally view offshore EMI settlement accounts with suspicion and may decline to sponsor them; the viable route is to open a Schedule I bank account and use Canadian-licensed money service businesses or PSPs for ancillary FX and cross-border payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% general federal corporate income tax rate (Income Tax Act R.S.C. 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.), s. 123(1)); Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) may claim the 9% Small Business Deduction rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income (s. 125(1)); non-resident-controlled corporations are ineligible for the SBD and are taxed at the general 15% federal rate plus applicable provincial corporate rates where a permanent establishment exists (e.g., Ontario 11.5%, combined 26.5%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividend payments to non-residents (ITA s. 212(2)). Additionally, a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada through a branch is subject to the 25% Branch Tax under ITA s. 219 on after-tax branch profits left in Canada (reduced to 5% under Article X(6) of the US-Canada DTA; other treaties generally 5%-15%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than 6 months after the fiscal year-end (ITA s. 150(1)). Part I balance due is payable within 2 months of year-end (or 3 months for a CCPC meeting the definition in s. 157(2)); non-resident corporations and large corporations pay quarterly installments on estimated Canadian-source taxable income.

Deadline: · Frequency:

NR4 forms and the corresponding NR4 Summary must be filed by the last day of February following the calendar year in which the non-resident payment was made. Part XIII withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, rents and management fees must be remitted to the CRA by the 15th day of the month following the month of payment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: contemporary transfer-pricing documentation is mandatory where the aggregate gross value of specified transactions with non-arm's length non-residents exceeds this amount in a taxation year (CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R and ITA s. 247(4)); documentation must be completed before the T2 filing deadline and furnished to the CRA within 90 days of a written request. The 2024 federal budget proposes alignment with the OECD Local File (CAD 1,000,000 threshold) and Master File (consolidated group revenue exceeding CAD 200,000,000) rules; a 10% transfer-pricing penalty is imposed on net upward adjustments exceeding CAD 1,000,000 where no contemporaneous documentation exists.

Economic substance scope: The federal regime does not apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); substance is instead enforced through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), which recharacterises transactions designed solely to avoid tax; (ii) the central-management-and-control residence test; (iii) the permanent establishment degree-of-activity threshold under Article V of the applicable DTA; and (iv) the s. 18(1)(a) + s. 67 reasonable-incurrence doctrine. For an ISIC 5229 DG freight forwarder, an economic-substance file should evidence: locally employed TDG-certified staff, warehouse or terminal usage agreements in Canada, transport liability insurance placed in Canada, a Canadian-denominated bank account, and board records documenting substantive decision-making. A pure letter-box DG agent with no local operations will be collapsed as a conduit, re-attributing all Canadian-source income to the enterprise.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction on indictment: fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: fine equal to 50%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 2 years (ITA s. 239). Civil consequences: gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax plus compound interest and asset seizure under the Seized Property Management regime.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who direct, authorize or participate sit behind the corporate veil; civil assessments may be issued directly against them where the corporation is a mere facade; criminal prosecution exposes the director to personal fines, passports surrender orders, travel bans, and extradition cooperation in serious cross-border evasion cases.
