Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST registration and Business Number (BN) portal: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering through Business Registration Online or by submitting Form RC1. For a non-resident limited partnership, the registration must identify the partnership as the person registering for GST/HST and provide the partnership agreement, legal entity information of each general and limited partner, and a Canadian or foreign address for service. The CRA assigns a BN with a GST/HST account number (RT) after verifying the entity's existence and its Canadian nexus. If the partnership has no permanent establishment in Canada, the non-resident may still be required to register if it makes taxable supplies in Canada that are not zero-rated and is not a small supplier. After registration, the entity must file periodic (annual, quarterly, or monthly) GST/HST returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; the partnership itself is not a taxpayer under the Income Tax Act, and each partner is separately liable on its share of income. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and may hold its own GST/HST registration number. Foreign partners who are non-residents may be taxed in Canada only to the extent the partnership carries on a business in Canada through a permanent establishment.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under the Income Tax Act for a limited partnership; fiscal transparency is mandatory. For GST/HST, an elected partner may be designated as the representative of a partnership. Closely related Canadian-resident corporations or partnerships can elect under ETA section 273 to form a GST/HST group, effectively treating them as one registrant for GST/HST. Entities may also elect to use annual filing periods if taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 1.5 million. Financial institutions may make separate elections under the ETA (e.g., section 150) for characteristics of financial supplies. Hybrid mismatch rules do not introduce an elective classification; Canadian tax treatment of the partnership as transparent is respected for treaty purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (worldwide for non-residents) in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; below this, the entity is a small supplier and is not required to register. Registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, unless the supplies are zero-rated exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a taxable supply of goods that are exported from Canada, supplied to a non-resident for use or enjoyment outside Canada, and delivered by the supplier or the recipient by common carrier or courier to a destination outside Canada within a reasonable time (generally within 60 days). Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated if the services are performed wholly or partly outside Canada, and the recipient is not a Canadian resident who uses the services in Canada. For FPV racing drones, the sale of goods to a foreign buyer with proof of export qualifies for 0% GST/HST. The supplier must keep evidence demonstrating the goods were sold for export and were actually exported.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or approved alternative export reporting filed in the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the consignee and destination outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Canadian exporter to the foreign buyer
  • Packing list and shipping documentation evidencing physical movement of the goods across the border
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer receipt confirming the export proceeds were received from a foreign settling bank (to satisfy FX repatriation and audit trail)

Canada does not currently impose a national real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate. Electronic invoices and records are accepted if they contain all required GST/HST information (ETA sections 169 and 221, and Regulations). Registrants with taxable annual supplies above CAD 1.5 million must file electronically via GST/HST NETFILE (or EDI for large companies). The CRA uses electronic audit tools and data analytics to cross-match export records, customs data, and invoice details. No API-based mandatory pre-clearance is in force, but the federal government has announced plans to align with Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standards.

If the CRA determines that an export zero-rating claim is not substantiated, it may reassess the supply at the applicable GST/HST rate (5% or HST as applicable), add arrear interest from the filing date, and impose penalties. Under ETA section 285, a registrant who knowingly makes a false statement in a GST/HST return is liable to a penalty of 25% of the tax that would have been payable, or CAD 5,000, whichever is greater. Gross negligence penalties under Income Tax Act section 163(2) can apply to related misinformation in income tax filings. Fraudulent export zero-rating can also result in criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code; in serious cases, fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment of up to five years, as well as loss of export clearance privileges through the Canada Border Services Agency.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. No exchange-control approval is required for inbound capital injections, outbound trade disbursements, or profit remittances from a Canadian Limited Partnership. A non-resident partner may freely receive distributive profits, but Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes a 15%-25% withholding tax on such distributions (treaty rate may be reduced to 15% for US partners under the Canada-US Tax Convention). The Investment Canada Act applies only where a non-Canadian acquires control of a Canadian business above specified asset thresholds; a passive LP capital contribution without acquisition of control is not subject to review, although a notification filing may be required if the structure is deemed to acquire control of an unrelated enterprise.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts from FPV racing and hobby drones (ISIC 3240) may be retained indefinitely in USD or any foreign currency at a Canadian authorized financial institution. All international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC within 5 business days on a cross-border wire transfer report; settlement flows processed by a payments service provider are reported by the PSP as a money service business rather than by the exporter itself.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole central bank and monetary authority. Foreign-exchange transactional oversight is operationalized by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and by Statistics Canada for Balance of Payments reporting of cross-border capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an auxiliary settlement rail; Low as a primary treasury account. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) can offer virtual CAD/USD receiving accounts and rapid supplier payables in Asia, but the Canadian LP is legally required to hold its principal deposit relationship at a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II authorized deposit-taking institution because an EMI is not licensed as a bank under the Bank Act. Balances held at an EMI are not CDIC-insured, and a foreign money services business soliciting Canadian clients must register with FINTRAC and appoint a Canadian compliance officer. Accordingly, the LP may use the EMI strictly for operational cross-currency pockets and must sweep final settlement proceeds to the onshore Canadian bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident partner wires the capital contribution via SWIFT MT103 using purpose code '5150 – Direct Investment Equity' with the narrative 'LP Capital Contribution – Invoice [PPM reference]'. The Canadian receiving bank must be pre-notified so the receipt is classified as a Balance of Payments capital inflow; amounts above CAD 1,000,000 also require completion of Statistics Canada Form BPI-2 and a copy of the partnership agreement to prevent funds-holds under the PCMLTFA. If the capital is instead structured as a shareholder loan, the parties must execute a signed loan agreement and the Canadian borrower files Form T2024 with the Canada Revenue Agency where the loan exceeds CAD 25,000. Funds remitted directly into the PayPal or Stripe wallet cannot be treated as equity capital because a payments service provider is not an authorized clearing house for partnership capital contributions.Supplier payments to the Asian drone-component base settle via two primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for amounts below CAD 1,000,000 using purpose code 'Trade in goods – General merchandise', accompanied by the commercial invoice and purchase contract; and (ii) a Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700, UCP 600) for first-time or higher-value supplier contracts, where the LC pays only upon presentation of a compliant bill of lading and commercial invoice. CAD/USD conversion is executed at the bank’s onshore forward desk so the exact CNY-denominated cost line is hedged; USD proceeds from PayPal/Stripe are received into the BMO or RBC USD operating account and then allocated by the treasury function to supplier payables without third-party currency conversion.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracts (USD/CAD) with tenors up to 12 months, available from any Schedule I bank dealing desk without a derivative-eligible legal opinion for the LP.FX vanilla options on USD/CAD, including calls and puts to protect forecast USD retail revenue received through Stripe and PayPal.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) linking CAD working-capital borrowings to USD receivables where the partnership’s debt exceeds the stated leverage covenant.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) used only for synthetic hedging of non-convertible CNY exposure on the Chinese vendor cost base, transacted under the Canadian bank’s swap-dealer license and cleared through a recognized central counterparty where applicable.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as an auxiliary settlement rail; Low as a primary treasury account. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) can offer virtual CAD/USD receiving accounts and rapid supplier payables in Asia, but the Canadian LP is legally required to hold its principal deposit relationship at a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II authorized deposit-taking institution because an EMI is not licensed as a bank under the Bank Act. Balances held at an EMI are not CDIC-insured, and a foreign money services business soliciting Canadian clients must register with FINTRAC and appoint a Canadian compliance officer. Accordingly, the LP may use the EMI strictly for operational cross-currency pockets and must sweep final settlement proceeds to the onshore Canadian bank account.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate (for taxable corporate partners; the limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and pays no federal income tax at the entity level). Provincial corporate rates range from 8% (Alberta) to 16% (Nova Scotia); combined federal-provincial rates typically range from 23% to 31%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents. An LP is fiscally transparent: allocations of business income to non-resident partners are taxed under Part I (income tax) rather than as dividends; actual cash distributions of previously taxed income are generally non-taxable returns of capital, with the partner's cost base adjusted accordingly.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 information return and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation are mandatory for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year; a Master File and Country-by-Country report are required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 1 billion or more (fiscal years beginning on or after 2017).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the standalone Economic Substance Regulations used in Caribbean jurisdictions. Instead, the CRA invokes the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 of the Income Tax Act) and the transfer-pricing rules (section 247) to deny benefits where a limited partnership or its allocations lack genuine economic reality. The LP must maintain a real Canadian presence - physical books and records, a Canadian bank account, substantive general-partner decision-making, and arm's-length contracts - to preserve limited liability, prevent a deemed permanent establishment for non-resident partners, and sustain pass-through status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements (sections 163 and 239, Income Tax Act)

Statutory sanction: Civil: gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded plus full arrears and compound interest. Criminal: fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years on indictment, plus forfeiture orders.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act and section 323 of the Excise Tax Act; in criminal cases, courts may impose travel bans (passport surrender), asset freezes, and restitution orders, and CRA may issue director-liability certificates.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (section 247, Income Tax Act)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions and adjustment of income; a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 50,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue; information-return penalties under section 162 for each failure to file T106.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the pricing policy may be assessed director liability for corporate penalties; CRA may seek civil fraud remedies, freeze bank accounts, and, in serious cases, obtain court orders restricting travel pending determination of liability.
Sham / Shell Limited Partnership and GAAR (section 245, Income Tax Act)

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization denying the tax benefit; disallowance of losses where the partner's at-risk amount is negative; reinstatement of full personal liability of limited partners under provincial Limited Partnership Acts for partnership debts and obligations.

Director personal liability: Limited partners engaged in management become jointly and severally liable as general partners; directors of the general partner face personal liability for trust-fund amounts, and CRA can issue garnishment and seizure orders against personal bank accounts and real property.
Anti-Money Laundering / Sanctions Red Flags (Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA (up to CAD 500,000 per violation for entities and CAD 100,000 for individuals in first-instance assessments); criminal fines up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to five years for wilful contraventions; FINTRAC compliance orders and freezing of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed or failed to prevent misleading reporting are personally prosecutable; criminal bail conditions can include travel restrictions and passport surrender; merchant accounts at payment providers may be permanently revoked, causing loss of the primary revenue rail.
