Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) - Administering the federal Goods and Services Tax / Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) and the Income Tax Act; primary digital portal is 'My Business Account' (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services) alongside GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity intending to carry on taxable activity in Canada as a Limited Partnership must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering directly with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) through 'Register a Business' online, or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'. The partnership itself registers as a GST/HST registrant using the RC1 form, selecting the 'RT' program account type. Each Canadian-resident partner and, where required, each non-resident partner providing services in Canada must separately obtain a Business Number or use a Social Insurance Number if an individual. The 9-digit Business Number followed by the 'RT' program account identifier constitutes the GST/HST registration number. Registration must occur no later than the day immediately following the first calendar day on which the partnership is required to collect tax; retroactive registration is permitted under subsection 240(1) of the Excise Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - For income tax purposes, a Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: the partnership itself is not a taxable entity and each partner is subject to tax at the partner level on the partner's allocable share of partnership income or loss for the fiscal period. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and itself becomes a GST/HST registrant and collector when it makes taxable supplies in Canada. The territorial scope of the partnership is Canada-wide, with provincial allocations where required.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide an 'LLC-style check-the-box' elective regime similar to the United States. A Canadian Limited Partnership is statutorily classified as a partnership under the applicable provincial partnership legislation and by default is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; no separate entity classification election is permitted. The partnership may choose a non-calendar fiscal year end with CRA approval under subsection 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act. For cross-border hybrid mismatch planning, Canada applies its own domestic characterization rules; hybrid mismatch provisions in sections 18.4 and 18.6 of the Income Tax Act may deny deductions or impose inclusion adjustments where an entity or arrangement is fiscally transparent in one jurisdiction and non-transparent in another, but no elective mechanism exists to override statutory characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada during any single quarter and over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to the 'small supplier' rules in section 148 of the Excise Tax Act. Once the threshold is exceeded, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory effective the first day of the month, or at the earlier agreed date; non-residents making supplies of intangible personal property to Canadian recipients are generally deemed to be carrying on business in Canada and may have different nexus rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian Limited Partnership may zero-rate (i.e., apply a 0% GST/HST rate) supplies of tangible personal property that are exported from Canada by the supplier, provided the export is accomplished directly by the partnership through shipping to the non-resident recipient or by delivering the goods to a carrier or forwarder for export. The supply must qualify under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (with specific documentary evidence retained). Dental clear aligner products, being manufactured tangible goods, are eligible for zero-rating when actually exported. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Part V generally, unless the service is in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. In all cases, the supplier must obtain and retain satisfactory evidence, such as an export declaration, shipping manifest, or carrier certificate, within a reasonable period, normally within 60 days of billing in accordance with CRA guidance.

  • Canada Export Declaration (electronic submission via Canadian Export Reporting System - CAERS, formerly B13A)
  • Commercial Invoice / Canada Customs Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (issued by the carrier) evidencing export from Canadian port
  • CBSA Cargo Control Document (CCD) or carrier certificate of export
  • Foreign exchange record or Inward Remittance Certificate showing payment received from the non-resident buyer
  • Proof of export declaration confirmation from the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)

Canada does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for GST/HST invoices. Electronic invoicing is permissible and may be used without pre-clearance. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Interoperability Framework (based on the PEPPOL/BIS 3.0 standard) is supported on a voluntary basis for B2B transactions; there is no statutory mandate. Nevertheless, every GST/HST registrant must ensure that invoices issued contain prescribed information under section 166.5 of the Excise Tax Act, including seller's name and business number, recipient, date, description, and tax calculation; records must be retained for six years. Registered taxpayers may be required to file GST/HST returns electronically through Netfile, and certain large taxpayers must transmit data in a specified electronic format, but this is a filing control rather than a transaction-level fiscal clearance requirement.

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) actively audits export zero-rating claims and may require contemporaneous evidence of physical export. Where zero-rating is applied without adequate documentary support, CRA will reassess and collect the unpaid GST/HST at the 5% federal rate (plus applicable HST if exported from a participating province), along with interest under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act. A person who knowingly makes a false statement or omission in an export return may be liable to penalties under subsection 285(1) of $250 or 25% of the net tax that would have been payable (whichever is greater), and to gross negligence penalties under subsection 285(2) of $250 plus 50% of the amount of tax or net tax sought to be evaded. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act may result in fines and imprisonment. Directors and partners can be held jointly and severally liable for corporate/partnership GST/HST obligations in limited circumstances. If export documentation is fabricated, the CRA may also impose penalties under the Income Tax Act for fraudulent misrepresentation and extend the normal reassessment period from four to six years, or indefinitely where fraud or willful misrepresentation is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Canada imposes no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, partnership distributions, dividends, interest, and management fees payable to non-resident limited partners are freely transferable without prior regulatory approval, subject only to applicable withholding taxes under the Canadian Income Tax Act and any relevant double-tax treaty. The capital account is fully liberalized; there are no licensing, quota, or prior-approval mechanisms for cross-border fund movements. The CAD operates under a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no administered band or peg.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada maintains no statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements. Exporters of dental clear aligner sheets (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339114) may retain 100% of foreign-currency export proceeds in offshore or domestic foreign-currency accounts, without any statutory timeline for collection, conversion, or remittance. There are no FOREX control gates on export invoices, customs declarations, or bank settlements. Exporters may freely negotiate trade payment terms; however, where intercompany export receivables remain outstanding beyond customary commercial periods, arm's-length and transfer-pricing documentation must be retained to support the position for Canadian corporate tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential banking regulator and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervisory authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with material legal constraints. Non-resident fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) may operate in Canada only after registering as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and, where applicable, obtaining licensing under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which is supervised by the Bank of Canada. Foreign digital banking platforms such as Wise and Payoneer are permitted as FINTRAC-registered MSBs for ancillary payment collection, but they are not substitutes for a Tier-1 Canadian commercial bank account for capital escrow purposes. A Tier-1 bank may decline to open a settlement account for an unregistered foreign EMI, and OSFI-regulated institutions generally require a Canadian legal presence or an established correspondent banking relationship. The recommended structure is therefore a domestic Tier-1 operating account supplemented by a fintech EMI only for retail patient-payment collection through payment platforms, not for core treasury liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital-contribution routing from non-resident limited partners must use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers in CAD or USD directly to the LP's designated capital escrow or operational account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. The MT103 message must include the LP's full legal name, the general partner's name, and the corresponding capital-call notice reference. Canadian law does not impose a mandatory purpose-code framework for inbound direct investment; however, the wire details should state 'Direct Investment — LP Capital Contribution (Capital Call No. [X])' to support KYC classification and the CRA's capital-account statistical reporting (Balance of Payments programme). The supporting verification bundle must include: (i) a notarized capital-call notice issued by the general partner citing the LP agreement capital-contribution section; (ii) a signed subscription agreement from each contributing partner; (iii) the received-funds ledger synchronized to the capital account; and (iv) proof of the source of funds (bank statements and source-of-wealth documentation) for any single contribution exceeding CAD 250,000. Where the cash-equivalent contribution exceeds CAD 10,000, the receiving bank has independent FINTRAC reporting obligations; the LP should coordinate to avoid duplicate or conflicting declarations.Trade settlement outflow for raw-material imports and supplier payments is executed through two primary clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 electronic telegraphic transfers for open-account supplier terms within established credit limits; and (b) Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600 for high-value or first-time supplier relationships, issued by the LP's Tier-1 Canadian bank through SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT707 where necessary. For USD-denominated payments to US or Asian suppliers, the LP should maintain a USD clearing sub-account alongside its CAD operating account at the same bank to eliminate double conversion spreads and access US Fedwire and CHIPS same-day settlement. All outbound wires must be screened against Canadian sanctions (United Nations Act and Special Economic Measures Act), OFAC-administered sanctions, and internal restricted-party lists. Non-resident fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) may operate in Canada only after registering as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and, where applicable, obtaining licensing under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which is supervised by the Bank of Canada. Foreign digital banking platforms such as Wise and Payoneer are permitted as FINTRAC-registered MSBs for ancillary payment collection, but they are not substitutes for a Tier-1 Canadian commercial bank account for capital escrow purposes. A Tier-1 bank may decline to open a settlement account for an unregistered foreign EMI, and OSFI-regulated institutions generally require a Canadian legal presence or an established correspondent banking relationship. The recommended structure is therefore a domestic Tier-1 operating account supplemented by a fintech EMI only for retail patient-payment collection through payment platforms, not for core treasury liquidity.
