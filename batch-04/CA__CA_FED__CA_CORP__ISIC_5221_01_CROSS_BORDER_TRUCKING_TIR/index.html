Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement under the CBCA; articles may provide for unlimited authorized common shares with no-par or nominal-par value, and subscription consideration may be money, property, or past services (CBCA s.25(2)). Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange-control restrictions on capital inflows, outflows, profit remittance, dividends, or loan repatriation. All cross-border electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting, and transactions with sanctioned countries or individuals are prohibited under the United Nations Act and the Special Economic Measures Act. The only practical hurdles are AML/KYC documentation requirements and Part XIII income tax withholding on payments to non-residents, reducible under applicable tax treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds. Foreign-currency receipts may be retained, invested, or freely converted. The entity must reflect all proceeds in its books and comply with CRA transfer-pricing and tax-reporting rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; foreign exchange administration is shared with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal Part I general corporate rate on Taxable Income before provincial allocation; combined federal and provincial effective rate ranges from approximately 23% to 31% depending on the province of permanent establishment. A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) benefits from the Small Business Deduction, reducing the federal rate to 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income per year, subject to phase-out rules based on CAD 15,000,000 taxable capital and CAD 50,000,000 passive investment income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder unless reduced by an income tax treaty. Treaty rates commonly reduce withholding to 5% for a corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% of voting shares and to 15% for portfolio shareholders (Canada-US Treaty Article X; Canada-China DTA Article 10).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due within six (6) months after the fiscal year-end. Final income tax balance due within two (2) months after year-end for non-CCPCs, and within three (3) months for an eligible CCPC claiming the Small Business Deduction. Quarterly corporate income tax instalments are required when tax payable exceeds CAD 3,000 in the current or previous year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian-dollar thresholds under the 2021/2022 master-file and local-file rules: a Local File is mandatory for taxation years beginning after January 1, 2022 where the aggregate fair market value of reportable cross-border transactions between the Canadian taxpayer and each non-arm's-length non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000; a Master File is mandatory where consolidated annual group revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000,000; a Country-by-Country Report is required where group revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000 and foreign operations exceed the de minimis threshold. CRA also expects a contemporaneous transfer pricing study under IC 87-2R for any non-arm's-length cross-border transaction, and Form T106 must be filed for reportable transactions even below the CAD 1,000,000 local-file threshold unless an administrative de minimis instruction applies.
