E-government filing portal

Foreign corporations must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account. Steps: (1) Apply via Business Registration Online (BRO) or complete Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts); (2) designate a person resident in Canada or rely on a qualified non-resident representative; (3) provide corporate charter, certified copy of articles of incorporation (foreign entity), director IDs, address for service in Canada; (4) CRA issues a 9-digit BN plus program account suffix (e.g., RT0001 for GST/HST); (5) register for non-resident simplified GST/HST if supplying digital services (including SaaS) to Canadian residents and taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; (6) open a payroll account (RP) if hiring Canadian employees; not required for pure B2B SaaS without payroll. Registration is mandatory before invoicing Canadian clients subject to GST/HST.

Canada has no check-the-box regime. A Canadian federal corporation is by default a resident taxable corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible and Canada maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility; there is no exchange-control approval, no investment licensing, and no profit-repatriation permit for ISIC 6201 (PropTech SaaS). Dividends, royalties, management fees, and interest may be remitted freely, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%; reduced by treaty, e.g., 5-15% for US-dividend recipients, 10% royalties, 0-15% interest). Inward equity investment is unrestricted for non-residents and no minimum-capital or escrow requirement applies to a CBCA Business Corporation; only a post-incorporation notice under the Investment Canada Act is required. The platform's foreign-produced subscription revenue is not subject to export-surrender constraints and may be consolidated in CAD or USD accounts at Canadian banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline exists in Canada; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951. Foreign-currency receivables from overseas SaaS and tenant-screening clients may be settled via FX conversion at commercial rates, held in USD accounts, or netted offshore without reporting to the central bank, provided transactions are reflected in the CRA tax filings and are subject to FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting (threshold CAD 10,000) when cleared through a Canadian bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating concurrently with the Department of Finance Canada; FX policy is administered under the Currency Act and operates in a liberalized environment with no exchange-control board. Bank and clearing supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Canadian Payments Act administrator, and FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-as-primary / High-as-auxiliary. Canada does not issue EU-style EMI licences; non-resident digital banks (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) operate as Money Service Businesses (MSB) or payment-service providers registered with FINTRAC and, since November 2024, with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Such PSPs are legally usable for collecting subscription fees through Stripe Billing or for small-value disbursements, but they cannot maintain a Lynx settlement account, cannot hold the corporate payroll/tax float, and cannot execute deliverable FX forwards on behalf of a CBCA entity. The entity should use a Schedule I D-SIB account as the primary source-of-funds treasury and restrict EMIs to daily operational float below CAD 50,000.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (general rate); 9% federal rate for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPC) on qualified active business income up to CAD 500,000; provincial/territorial corporate rates apply in addition to the federal rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, subject to reduction under an applicable bilateral Income Tax Convention to generally 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for dividends paid to a corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% of the voting capital.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the end of the fiscal period; instalments are due on the 15th day of the second, fourth, seventh, and tenth months of the taxation year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Filing of Form T106 must occur where the aggregate value of non-arm's length transactions with a non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation must be prepared and retained when such transactions exist; Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting applies to Canadian ultimate parent entities of multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations, but the Canada Revenue Agency applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), sham doctrine, substance-over-form, and beneficial ownership analysis under treaty LOB provisions. A PropTech real-estate SaaS corporation must maintain real strategic decision-making, employees, office premises, and business purpose in Canada; a shell or conduit with passive receipts and no local substance will face re-characterization, denial of treaty relief, and Part XIII assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under ITA s. 163(2); criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239 for tax evasion: upon conviction by indictment, a fine of 50% to 200% of tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; for summary conviction, a fine up to CAD 10,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; interest and penalties compound on all arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participated in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts under ITA s. 227.1 for unremitted withholdings; criminal liability attaches to those who 'caused, authorized, acquiesced or participated' in the offence; the CRA may obtain asset freezes and garnishments, and a court may prohibit a director from leaving Canada during a serious evasion investigation.
Shell Company / Sham / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR re-characterization of the structure, denial of DTA benefits, loss disallowance, and gross-negligence penalty where false statements are made; failure to file T2 returns for two years can lead to administrative dissolution of the federal corporation under CBCA s. 209, with a fine to each director of up to CAD 5,000 and prohibition from acting as director for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed under ITA s. 227.1 for unremitted Part XIII or source deduction liabilities; CBCA s. 209 exposes directors to personal fines if the corporation is struck off for non-filing; a court can order equitable compensation where the corporate veil is pierced on grounds of sham or fraudulent intention.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may re-determine income under ITA s. 247(2); a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment may apply where the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts; foreign withholding tax under Part XIII is assessed on any deemed dividend/royalty; interest is charged on the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate or fraudulent TP documentation may be subject to gross-negligence penalties and may be personally liable for unremitted amounts; wilful participation in a false transfer-pricing scheme can lead to criminal exposure and personal civil liability for unpaid corporate taxes.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations; criminal conviction for knowingly failing to report a transaction can result in imprisonment and fines; FINTRAC may make public enforcement notices.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be held personally liable for AML penalties where they knew, caused, consented to, or negligently failed to prevent the offence; criminal sanctions include fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; CRA may use AML-derived evidence to assess unreported income and impose related tax penalties.
