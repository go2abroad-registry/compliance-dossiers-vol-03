Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Check-the-box elections are not available under the Income Tax Act. However, a non-resident corporation may elect under subsection 216(1) or 217 (non-resident withholding) for certain income streams. For GST/HST, a foreign corporation may elect to file on an annual basis or use the Quick Method or Simplified Accounting Method if selected. No hybrid mismatch election is applicable, but Part XVI.1 rules (hybrid mismatch arrangements) and the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) are relevant for treaty-based tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (worldwide taxable supply threshold over a calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; mandatory registration if exceeded). Non-resident carrying on business in Canada without a permanent establishment may still be required to register if making taxable supplies in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported over a prescribed period (section 1 of Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act), including from a licensed customs broker's export transactions. Customs brokerage services themselves are generally taxable at 5% whereas the goods are zero-rated. Services rendered to non-residents exclusively for use outside Canada may be zero-rated if they qualify as excluded supplies or meet the 'performance outside Canada' test. A customs broker must retain evidence that they made a supply of exported goods, including export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / Export Declaration via CBSA system)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Cargo Control Document / Contract of Carriage
  • Packing List (when required)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Proof of Export (e.g., marine or air waybill signed by carrier; customs confirmation)

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. GST/HST returns and registrations are filed electronically via the CRA GST/HST NETFILE system (API integration permitted). Electronic invoicing is accepted but not mandatory. Customs brokers operating through the CBSA (Canada Border Services Agency) must use the Automated Customs Declaration System (ACCD) and supports electronic data interchange (EDI) through the Single Window Initiative (SWI) for import/export declarations.

CRA and CBSA may reassess GST/HST zero-rated claims. Penalties include: GST/HST reassessment with interest, negligence penalties under subsection 285(1) Excise Tax Act (the greater of 5% of the amount and 50% of the net tax, or 10% of the tax when made knowingly); civil penalties under the Customs Act (up to CAD 25,000 per violation); criminal prosecution for fraud under the Income Tax Act or Excise Tax Act with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Retrospective clawbacks may apply for a period of 4 years (8 years in case of misrepresentation) plus compound interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted; no prior approval for dividend remittances or investment repatriation. Profit remittance is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (typically 25% statutory, reduced by treaty) and requires a Canadian withholding agent to report to the CRA. Barriers are non-exchange-control in nature, primarily AML/KYC and tax documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements exist in Canada. Exporters may hold, convert, or remit export earnings freely; if proceeds are retained abroad, Canadian tax disclosure rules may apply, but no FX timeline or ratio is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) as central bank; anti-money laundering and cross-border transaction oversight by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Offshore EMI accounts (e.g., UK or EU electronic money institutions) are not recognized as authorized deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot provide banking services to a licensed customs broker without registration as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and compliance with provincial remittance laws. Such EMIs may serve as auxiliary payment rails, but not as primary settlement accounts.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% federal general corporate income tax rate (after 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction); 9.0% applicable to active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) on the first CAD 500,000 per year.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents; reduced by income tax treaties (e.g., 15% general rate under US-Canada DTA, 5% for a US recipient corporation controlling at least 10% of Canada's voting stock).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., December 31 fiscal end = June 30 deadline). The balance of Part I tax is due within two months after fiscal year-end for non-CCPC corporations; a three-month balance-due date applies to CCPCs eligible for the small business deduction on at least some active business income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with non-resident related parties triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File); a separate T106 information return threshold is CAD 100,000 for all reportable non-arm's length transactions. The documentation duty is proportionate to the transaction's size and complexity.

Economic substance scope: Canada lacks a standalone ESR, but the CRA invokes the doctrine of substance over form and the s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR). A customs brokerage house must maintain a genuine Canadian office, licensed broker employees, administrative decision-making, and board oversight. Treaty benefits can be denied, and a shell arrangement recharacterized, where the principal purpose of the corporate structure is to obtain a tax benefit without real business activity; compliance with the customs broker license and FINTRAC reporting is strong evidence of substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to File (ITA Part XVIII)

Statutory sanction: Upon summary conviction under s.239(1) ITA: fine up to CAD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to two years. On indictment: imprisonment up to five years and a fine not exceeding 200% of the evaded tax; plus 50% gross negligence penalty under s.163(2), prescribed interest and reassessment rights without time limitation where misrepresentation is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under s.227.1 ITA; the CRA can seize assets, impose travel bans, require personal restitution, and apply for a court order to prohibit the director from leaving Canada. Fraud-related directors can face imprisonment and criminal forfeiture.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits under the Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test; the intermediary will be ignored, and Part XIII tax reassessed at 25% on all distributions. Penalties include the s.163(2) misrepresentation penalty (up to 75% of the benefit) and a fine where the arrangement was knowingly concealed.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil if the shell is a bona fide facade; directors and officers may be subject to joint tort liability, civil restitution for the amount of tax avoided, and travel restrictions pending criminal investigation; in flagrant cases, money laundering charges carry up to 14 years imprisonment.
AML / Customs Broker Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the PCMLTFA, FINTRAC can issue monetary penalties up to CAD 250,000 for a corporation and CAD 125,000 for an officer/director in a separate violation; Customs Act penalties up to CAD 50,000 per false declaration, seizure/forfeiture of goods; Criminal Code s.462 conviction for laundering can lead to a fine up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers with operational control may receive prison sentences, permanent travel bans, extradition proceedings, and orders requiring restitution to the Crown; their personal assets may be seized where the corporate veil is legally disregarded.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Section 247(3) penalty equals 10% of the net upward transfer pricing adjustment, unless the taxpayer discloses the transaction in a timely manner with adequate documentation; T106 late-filing penalty is CAD 100 per day, up to CAD 2,500; underlying tax, interest, and the 50% gross negligence penalty can also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer-pricing returns or direct the mispricing may be held accountable under CBCA fiduciary duties; personal exposure includes claw-back of performance bonuses, disqualification from corporate office, and civil damages for consequential tax liability if the corporation becomes insolvent.
