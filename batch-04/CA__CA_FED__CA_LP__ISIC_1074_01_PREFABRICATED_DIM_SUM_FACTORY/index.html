Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://bro.businessregistration.ca; CRA My Business Account portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-business/business-account.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Canadian limited partnership (LP) must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) for the partnership using CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). The BN serves as the primary tax identifier for the partnership for GST/HST and payroll, customs (import/export), and other program accounts. The registration is completed by the general partner or an authorized representative and requires the partnership agreement, identification of partners (including non-resident jurisdictions), and a Canadian contact address. The CRA will issue a BN and, if the LP is registered for GST/HST, a separate GST/HST program account (RT). The LP itself is fiscally transparent for income tax; each partner reports its share of income separately using a Canadian tax identifier (e.g., Social Insurance Number, BN for corporate partners, or a non-resident identification number). For customs purposes, the LP must also register for a CRA Import/Export (RM) account. Non-resident partners may be required to obtain a non-resident individual or corporation tax account for compliance with withholding and information reporting under the Income Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The limited partnership is treated as a fiscally transparent flow-through entity for Canadian federal income tax purposes: income, losses, deductions, and credits flow through to partners pro rata under the partnership agreement. The LP is, however, a separate person for GST/HST purposes under the Excise Tax Act and must account for GST/HST on taxable supplies made in Canada.

Available election forms

No general 'check-the-box' election exists under Canadian federal income tax law; partnership classification is determined by applicable provincial partnership statutes and is not elective. Specific statutory elections include: (i) the joint election under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act for a non-resident partner to file a Canadian income tax return on a net basis for certain Canadian-source rental income (not directly applicable to an active manufacturing operation); (ii) the GST/HST election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act permitting closely related corporations and partnerships to treat certain mutual supplies as made for nil consideration; and (iii) an election under section 150 of the Excise Tax Act to treat a qualifying partnership as a separate person from its members for GST/HST purposes (default rule is that a partnership is deemed a person). Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (excess interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) and foreign affiliate dumping) apply mandatorily; there is no elective opt-out, but taxpayers may submit prescribed filings to establish the ordinary course transaction exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in cumulative gross consideration from taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (and in the current quarter). Mandatory GST/HST registration applies once this small supplier threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. Non-resident persons supplying taxable goods or services in Canada must register regardless of volume if they do not qualify as small suppliers under the Excise Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under GST/HST applies to goods that are exported from Canada by the supplier or on behalf of the supplier, provided the supplier maintains satisfactory evidence of export. For services supplied to a non-resident, zero-rating applies if the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and the service is not supplied to a person carrying on business in Canada, and the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property in Canada, or certain other exceptions. Prefabricated frozen dim sum and dumpling products are considered goods; if sold to a foreign customer and physically shipped out of Canada, the export supply qualifies for the 0% GST/HST rate (federal component). The zero-rating claim requires documentary proof of export and may be denied if adequate evidence is not retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CERS export report or B13A Export Declaration where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading (original or certified true copy)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of foreign currency receipt, as transferable to Canadian exporters; effectively a bank certificate or SWIFT/TT confirmation showing receipt of payment from the foreign buyer)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, or digital stamping of invoices. However, registrants must issue and retain invoices in a prescribed format containing the supplier's GST/HST registration number, the date of invoice, a description of goods/services, and the tax amount. Electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) is permitted but not compulsory. GST/HST returns and remittances must be filed electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or via approved service providers for registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million. The CRA may request export substantiation documents and use data analytics to match customs and tax records.

The CRA has unrestricted authority to reassess a GST/HST return where there is misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect under subsection 298(5) of the Excise Tax Act. For export zero-rating claims, the CRA may deny the 0% rate and assess GST at 5% if the exporter fails to produce satisfactory export documentation. Civil penalties under sections 280 and 285.1 include a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the tax that would have been payable (or 5% of the net understated tax), and a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated or tax credits overstated where a false statement is made knowingly or in circumstances amounting to gross negligence. Interest on assessed amounts accrues at the prescribed rate from the date the amount should have been paid. Criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act may result in fines up to 300% of the tax sought to be evaded and/ or imprisonment up to 5 years. Retrospective clawback periods extend beyond the normal 4-year limitation where a false or misleading statement is filed, or where the exporter commits fraud, permitting the CRA to reopen and reassess export transactions indefinitely.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada imposes no exchange controls or FERA-style approval requirements on either current-account or capital-account transactions. There is no obligation to register capital inflows, loans, or repatriations; the only frictions are (i) Part XIII withholding tax on deemed dividends, management fees, royalties, and certain remittances to non-resident partners, reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty where applicable, (ii) GST/HST considerations on imported services and goods, and (iii) administrative reporting thresholds (FINTRAC large currency transaction reports at CAD 10,000 and EFT reports on wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more). Profit remittances by a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident partners are feasible without central-bank permit provided the partnership files its T5013 information return and Form T2 tax allocation; non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the permanent establishment of the LP remain taxable on a net basis with refundable Part I treatment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime. Exporters and manufacturing exporters, including a frozen-food limited partnership, are legally entitled to retain 100% of export receipts in CAD or any foreign currency, without mandatory conversion timelines or forced sale to the central bank. The applicable compliance obligations are (i) filing the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) B13A Export Declaration for goods exceeding the export reporting threshold (CAD 2,000 or 2,500 depending on destination, with special rules for controlled goods), (ii) reporting wire transfers of CAD 10,000+ to FINTRAC through the financial institution, (iii) maintaining underlying commercial invoices, bills of lading, and CFIA export certificates for food products, and (iv) applying arm's-length transfer pricing under section 247 of the Income Tax Act for intercompany export sales where the dim-sum factory sells to a foreign related distributor.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; Canada operates no separate foreign-exchange administration. Cross-border monetary and FX functions are discharged jointly by the Department of Finance Canada (Monetary Policy and Financial Sector Policy branches), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential regulation, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CTF oversight of cross-border fund movements and wire transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium for core treasury, Medium for ancillary payment origination. Canada has no statutory Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category; under the Bank Act, deposit-taking for the public requires a Schedule I or Schedule II federal bank charter or an exempt provincial credit union. Offshore E-Money Institutions (e.g., UK FCA EMIs or EU PSD2 EMIs) are legally incapable of holding the LP's Canadian payroll or escrow balances as 'deposits' and are generally not acceptable to Canadian banks as correspondent counterparties. A FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB, subclass: foreign money services business) can lawfully execute FX conversion and cross-border remittances but cannot operate the LP's settlement account. Recommended architecture: use a Schedule I bank as the master account holder and route disbursements through registered fintech platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Western Union Business Solutions) solely for vendor FX payments. Any offshore EMI that is not FINTRAC-registered presents a material compliance blocker and should be excluded from the treasury stack.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from the foreign sponsor into the Canadian LP's capital-escrow and operating accounts must execute over SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008) with a clear purpose narrative in field 70: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO CANADIAN LIMITED PARTNERSHIP FOR ISIC 1074 FROZEN FOOD FACTORY', accompanied by the LP's Canadian bank account in cleared funds at a Schedule I institution. The routing library should be: (i) CAD injections settle in real time through Lynx (Payments Canada's RTGS system) with finality at the Bank of Canada; (ii) USD injections should be routed via the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent (e.g., RBC/TD/BMO New York branches) through Fedwire or CHIPS and then cleared into the domestic USD clearing and settlement system operated by Payments Canada; (iii) no purpose-code registry exists in Canada, but the bank will populate FINTRAC EFT report fields from the MT103 narrative, and any single wire of CAD 10,000+ must be captured. The sponsor must also provide a source-of-funds certification (audited financial statements, board resolution, utilization schedule), and counsel must confirm whether the Investment Canada Act requires a notification for establishing a 'new Canadian business' or whether the WTO-related business exemption applies to the dim-sum manufacturing activity; a protective notification is recommended to eliminate any risk of post-closing penalties (maximum CAD 25,000 per violation).Trade disbursements should split across three rails: (i) supplier prepayments and raw-material purchases by direct SWIFT MT103 (with SWIFT gpi and UETR tracking) in USD or CNY, from the BMO/Scotiabank operating account, each wire carrying a compliance narrative such as 'PAYMENT FOR FROZEN DIM SUM INGREDIENTS AND PACKAGING'; (ii) documentary trade settlements via a Commercial Letter of Credit (MLC for repetitive shipments) issued by the Canadian bank under MT700, payable at sight with documents against payment, beneficiary bank in the supplier's jurisdiction, covering commercial invoice, bill of lading, CFIA-compatible phytosanitary/HACCP certificates, and insurance documents; standby credits (SBLC under MT760) for performance and advance-payment guarantees; (iii) domestic CAD operational disbursements (payroll, utilities, logistics) via Lynx and Payments Canada's retail AFT/SWIFT-equivalent EDI. All FX conversions for supplier payments should be executed through the Canadian bank's dealing desk with same-day value dates and pre-agreed cut-off times, ensuring no in-house commingling of hedging and settlement positions and complete ISDA/CSA documentation before any derivative-linked payment.Vanilla FX Forwards (deliverable CAD/USD and CAD/CNY up to 24-month tenors) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support AnnexFX Swaps (roll-over and tomorrow-next structures) for managing temporary CAD liquidity gaps between inbound capital and outbound supplier paymentsOffshore Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) solely for non-deliverable crosses (e.g., offshore CNY/CNH and certain emerging-market currencies) where an onshore deliverable forward is unavailable; CAD itself is fully deliverable, so CAD/USD NDFs are unnecessary and not recommendedVanilla FX Options (call-and-put combinations, including zero-premium collars with minimum-risk disclosure) for protecting export-receipt conversion against CAD appreciation while permitting upside participationCross-Currency Swaps (fixed-for-fixed and fixed-for-floating) to match long-term capital injections in USD against CAD-denominated factory-equipment debt and to achieve synthetic CAD fundingCurrency-settled energy and frozen-food commodity basis swaps are permissible for hedge accounting only where the notional is directly tied to documented procurement exposure, subject to OSFI non-centrally-cleared margin rules, the Canadian Derivatives Assessment, and FINTRAC reporting for any cash-settled transactions over CAD 10,000

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium for core treasury, Medium for ancillary payment origination. Canada has no statutory Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category; under the Bank Act, deposit-taking for the public requires a Schedule I or Schedule II federal bank charter or an exempt provincial credit union. Offshore E-Money Institutions (e.g., UK FCA EMIs or EU PSD2 EMIs) are legally incapable of holding the LP's Canadian payroll or escrow balances as 'deposits' and are generally not acceptable to Canadian banks as correspondent counterparties. A FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB, subclass: foreign money services business) can lawfully execute FX conversion and cross-border remittances but cannot operate the LP's settlement account. Recommended architecture: use a Schedule I bank as the master account holder and route disbursements through registered fintech platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Western Union Business Solutions) solely for vendor FX payments. Any offshore EMI that is not FINTRAC-registered presents a material compliance blocker and should be excluded from the treasury stack.
