Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-registration-online.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that carries on business in Canada or makes taxable supplies in Canada must register for a federal Business Number (BN) with the CRA, using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or the Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The entity must identify the legal corporate name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered office, directors, and Canadian agent (if applicable). The CRA will issue a BN with program accounts, including the GST/HST account (RT) and, when required, payroll and corporate income tax accounts. Non-resident corporations may also apply by mail with certified translated incorporation documents and an officer's declaration; a Canadian representative or agent is recommended. Once registered, the entity must file annual GST/HST returns and maintain a Canadian filing compliance calendar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box flow-through election. A federal business corporation is, by default, taxed at the corporate level under the Income Tax Act. Available elections include: (i) a non-resident corporation electing under subsection 216(1) to compute Part I tax on its Canadian-source taxable income from real property or timber operations on a net-taxable basis; (ii) a treaty-based election under subsection 115.2 or the Canada-U.S. Article IV tie-breaker process to establish residence; (iii) an election under subsection 150(2) to align the taxation year of a controlled foreign affiliate; and (iv) group GST/HST filing elections using Form GST9 for closely related subsidiaries. Hybrid-mismatch rules under the Multilateral Instrument and CRA administrative guidance restrict deduction-mismatch arrangements and require disclosure under section 233.6. There is no federal check-the-box election for disregarded entities; a federal corporation is always a separate taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada (non-resident suppliers are subject to the same small-supplier threshold, derived from the Excise Tax Act s. 240(1) and s. 123). Once the threshold is exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required; certain non-resident operators of digital platforms may require registration under the simplified 'specified digital services' regime without a Canadian physical presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported by the supplier or by an export facilitator from Canada to consignees outside Canada, provided the supply is made to an export customer, the goods are delivered outside Canada or to a common carrier for export 'as soon as reasonably possible' after the sale, and the supplier retains evidence of exportation. For online retail of consumer electronics, the seller must confirm that the goods physically leave Canada and are delivered to the foreign purchaser. Qualifying documentation includes a customs export declaration (or a confirmed B13A Export Declaration for non-commercial exports), carrier waybill, proof of final foreign destination, customs stamps or electronic border-crossing records, and proof of payment from abroad. Goods supplied to a foreign customer in Canada for hand-carry across the border do not automatically qualify unless border documentation establishes export. Digital or software-only supplies fall under separate place-of-supply rules; the scope here is physical devices, which are zero-rated upon export.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration (B13A / B13A-F) for commercial exports or the eExport electronic submission
  • England: Commercial invoice containing description, HS code, quantity, value, and export reason
  • Bill of lading (ocean or air waybill) or a CMR/road consignment note naming the Canadian shipper and foreign consignee
  • Proof of export tracking and final delivery (carrier report, post-office proof of acceptance, or foreign customs entry record)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or wire-transfer confirmation from a Canadian chartered bank evidencing payment in foreign currency
  • Packing list and any certificate of origin required by the destination country
  • Supplier's internal evidence of export intent (sales contract, purchase order, and correspondence with the foreign buyer)

No federal real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing regime currently applies in Canada. The CRA does, however, require electronic filing of GST/HST returns (via GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account) for registrants above the prescribed threshold, and supports a voluntary pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard (X12/UBL) under the Canadian government's Digital Interchange and Data Exchange (DIDX) initiative. There are no digital stamping, tax-warehousing, or continuous auditing mandates at the federal level; all GST/HST registrants must retain complete source documents and business records, and the CRA may perform real-time data verification through e-audit and pre-assessment analytics.

The CRA and CBSA conduct targeted audits of export-zero-rating claims by e-commerce sellers. If export substantiation is insufficient, the CRA disallows the zero rating, reassesses the GST/HST at 5.0% (or 13% HST in participating provinces), and may apply interest on the deemed net tax, penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act (5% to 10% of the tax amount plus interest), and civil liability for false statements. When claimed refunds are determined fraudulent or as a result of wilful misrepresentation, subsection 283 proceedings can yield penalties equal to the tax evaded and a jail term on summary conviction. A deliberate scheme to claim zero-rating on goods not exported constitutes tax evasion under sections 327 and 332 of the Excise Tax Act; the CRA can also share findings with Canada Border Services Agency and the RCMP for seizure and criminal proceedings. Reassessments may be issued beyond the normal four-year reassessment period where misrepresentation or fraud is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full free-market convertibility of the Canadian dollar for current-account and capital-account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; no licensing, prior approval, or repatriation deferral applies to trade payments, service fees, royalties, dividends, or capital inflows. Profit remittance of dividends to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted but attracts Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory; reduced to 15% under the Canada-US Treaty and to 5% for a treaty-qualifying corporate shareholder controlling at least 10% of voting shares). Foreign-currency denomination inter partes is permitted, though payments involving the Crown must be in CAD under the Currency Act; the corporate treasury may hold USD and other currency balances in Tier-1 bank multi-currency accounts without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline exists for export receipts. The exporter may retain 100% of proceeds in original foreign currency or convert at market rates at its sole discretion; the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate must be used for income-tax translation under s. 261 of the Income Tax Act. The only mandatory obligations are FINTRAC reporting for international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and standard large-cash transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; exchange-control policy is administered jointly by the Department of Finance Canada, FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) for AML/ATF supervision, OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) for prudential bank regulation, and Global Affairs Canada for sanctions administration. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration.

Medium-High as a supplementary collections/payout rail, Low as primary treasury infrastructure. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity-injection protocol: remit from the shareholder's bank via SWIFT MT103 with OECD balance-of-payments purpose code 'G110 - Inward Direct Investment (Equity)' or the sending bank's equivalent BOP coding; CAD value-date settlement into the corporate operational account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose a blanket filing threshold for contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation, but to obtain 'reasonable efforts' protection from the 10% transfer-pricing penalty under section 247, the taxpayer must prepare and retain OECD-compliant Master File and Local File before the T2 filing due date. For fiscal years after statutory transition, a Transfer Pricing Information Return is required for an MNE group with consolidated annual revenue of C$250 million or more. CRA may also demand local documentation for non-arm's length transactions with a related party where the aggregate transaction value exceeds a de minimis commercial threshold, typically C$1 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not implement Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance is central to Canadian tax law. CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), permanent establishment rules and transfer-pricing functional analysis to disregard or recharacterize transactions that lack genuine commercial purpose, personnel, premises, risk-taking and economic assets in Canada. A Canadian shell or mailbox entity interposed to receive treaty benefits or to channel e-commerce transactions without substantive operational activities will be treated as having no valid Canadian commercial nexus, may be denied treaty relief, and may be reassessed at arm's length with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239: on indictment, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded, or imprisonment up to five years, or both; on summary conviction, imprisonment up to two years and the same fine range. CRA may also reassess beyond the normal reassessment period and impose gross negligence penalties under section 163.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will not protect directors who direct, participate in or authorize the false filings. They can be held civilly liable for restitution of the tax, waive personal immunity and may be subjected to criminal custody, travel bans as bail conditions, asset freezes and extradition proceedings where cross-border laundering is involved.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Abuse

Statutory sanction: Section 247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment in addition to the income tax reassessment. The penalty may be avoided only if the taxpayer demonstrates reasonable efforts, including timely documentation and arm's length application of the most appropriate method. Imputed royalties or deemed dividends may also trigger Part XIII withholding tax at 25%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of false or misleading T106 disclosures or transfer-pricing studies may be jointly liable for penalties as persons who knowingly participate in false statements. They may also bear fiduciary liability for transferring value out of the Canadian entity, and in fraudulent schemes, may be subject to personal garnishment and criminal prosecution.
AML/FINTRAC Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, failure to comply may result in administrative monetary penalties imposed by FINTRAC, summary conviction fines up to C$100,000, indictment fines up to C$500,000, and imprisonment up to five years, plus public disclosure of penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly control an unregistered payment business or who direct employees to conceal cash or cryptocurrency transactions can be prosecuted individually. Criminal forfeiture may reach personal assets, and travel bans, pre-trial detention and deportation-escalating restrictions for non-citizens are common in cross-border MST/AML enforcement.
