Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Principal portals and channels include Business Registration Online, My Business Account, Represent a Client, and Corporation Internet Filing. Federal corporate incorporation is administered separately by Corporations Canada, but corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and related tax accounts are administered by the CRA.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian corporation obtains a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA and registers the applicable program accounts, including the corporate income tax account RC0001 and, where required, the GST/HST account RT0001. Registration is normally completed through CRA Business Registration Online or, where online registration is unavailable to the applicant, through the CRA business registration process or Form RC1. A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a BN and register for the relevant CRA program accounts when statutory conditions are met. The applicant should provide legal name, incorporation jurisdiction and documents, registered and business addresses, directors, authorized representative details, business activities, expected Canadian revenue, fiscal year-end, effective registration date, and banking or contact information requested by the CRA. A federal corporation must also file annual corporate income tax returns using its BN, even where no Canadian tax is payable, if it is resident in Canada or otherwise has a Canadian filing obligation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally taxed as separate legal entities under the Income Tax Act and do not have a United States-style check-the-box election. There is no general election for a Canadian corporation to be treated as a pass-through entity. Partnerships and certain other arrangements may receive flow-through treatment under statutory Canadian rules, but this is not an elective corporate classification. Canada has anti-hybrid mismatch rules that can deny deductions, restrict foreign tax relief, or include amounts in income; those rules do not create an elective hybrid entity regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, generally measured over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, is the principal small-supplier threshold. Registration can be required earlier where the entity is no longer a small supplier, makes supplies through specified arrangements, or falls within special non-resident or industry rules. The applicable GST/HST place-of-supply rules must be analyzed for vessel leasing, chartering, possession or use of vessels in Canada, and related services. HST rates apply in participating provinces and consist of the 5% federal component plus the applicable provincial component.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated where the statutory GST/HST export conditions are satisfied and the supplier retains documentary evidence that the goods were exported. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated where the service falls within the applicable exported-service rules and is not excluded, including services relating to Canadian real property, certain tangible personal property situated in Canada, or other prescribed Canadian-connected matters. Offshore vessel leasing, chartering, vessel operation, and support services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is non-resident or payment is received offshore. The supplier must separately analyze the vessel's location, delivery and redelivery points, use, customer status, contractual rights, possession or control, voyage or charter structure, and any specific zero-rating rule for qualifying ships or maritime services. A registrant may claim input tax credits for eligible GST/HST incurred in commercial activities and may seek a net tax refund where recoverable input tax credits exceed tax collected, subject to documentary and filing requirements.

  • Canadian export declaration or applicable electronic export reporting record filed through the Canada Border Services Agency or other legally applicable reporting channel
  • Commercial invoice, charterparty, lease agreement, purchase order, and detailed description of the vessel, goods, or services
  • Bill of lading, sea waybill, cargo manifest, port records, or equivalent evidence of export, delivery, or movement outside Canada
  • Evidence of foreign destination, vessel location, offshore use, or non-Canadian performance location, as applicable
  • Proof of delivery, acceptance, voyage completion, or redelivery outside Canada where relevant
  • Customer identification and evidence supporting non-resident status where zero-rating depends on the recipient's status
  • Payment records, bank statements, and contract reconciliation supporting the consideration and supply
  • Inward remittance certificate: not generally a Canadian statutory GST/HST document; payment evidence may nevertheless be retained as corroborating commercial evidence

Canada has no general federal real-time GST/HST invoice-clearance, mandatory government e-invoicing, API pre-approval, or digital tax-stamping regime. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed or commercially acceptable invoices and maintain reliable electronic records supporting tax charged, zero-rating, input tax credits, customer identity, supply description, consideration, tax rate, and tax amount. CRA records must generally be retained for six years from the end of the relevant tax year, subject to extended retention requirements where an objection, appeal, investigation, or other statutory exception applies. Electronic filing obligations may apply to corporate income tax and certain GST/HST registrants, but electronic filing is not equivalent to real-time invoice clearance.

Canada does not generally impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline or require an inward remittance certificate as a condition of GST/HST export zero-rating. CRA audit scrutiny focuses on the statutory characterization and place of supply, export or offshore-use evidence, customer status, contractual substance, vessel location and use, tax charged, and entitlement to input tax credits or refunds. If zero-rating is unsupported, CRA may assess the uncollected GST/HST, deny related input tax credits or refunds, assess arrears interest, and impose applicable filing, gross-negligence, false-statement, or other penalties under the Excise Tax Act and related legislation. Refunds paid in error may be reassessed and recovered. Deliberate tax evasion, false documentation, obstruction, or fraudulent claims may result in prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. Records should be preserved for at least six years and longer where an audit, objection, appeal, investigation, or statutory retention rule requires it.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member; the Canadian dollar is freely convertible for both current and capital transactions. No capital controls, surrender requirements, or centralization regime operate. Inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, charter-hire receipts, and outbound dividends require no Bank of Canada approval. The Investment Canada Act imposes a national-security and net-benefit review only for non-Canadian acquisitions above statutory thresholds. Profit remittance is unrestricted but subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, reduced to 5%/15% for dividends under the Canada-US Treaty and similar instruments) verified by the paying bank against treaty certificates. Payment clearance is unconditional once AML/KYC and sanctions screening are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished exchange controls in 1951. Offshore supply vessel charter revenue earned from non-residents in USD or other freely convertible currencies may be retained indefinitely in a foreign-currency account of a Schedule I chartered bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory surrender timeline, and no designation of a domestic account is required. FINTRAC Electronic Fund Transfer Reports (EFTR) are required for all international EFTs at or above CA$10,000 per transaction; underlying charter party documentation must be retained and produced upon bank or regulator request.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of authorized foreign exchange dealers and clearing banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as cross-border reporting authority. There is no dedicated foreign exchange control administration or permit authority in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not license EU-style electronic money institutions (EMIs). Foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) operate as money service businesses (MSBs) and must register with FINTRAC; they lack direct membership in the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and cannot provide definitive Canadian-dollar clearing. Acceptable only as ancillary rails for low-value operational disbursements not exceeding CA$10,000 per transaction. All material charter hire, escrow releases, and vendor settlements must clear through a Schedule I bank account to avoid MSB concentration risk and third-party settlement failure. Any unregistered offshore PSP acting for the entity is an immediate compliance blocker.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Wire transfer protocol: shareholder equity and shareholder loans should be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the receiving Schedule I bank's Nostro account quoting purpose code 'Capital Contribution' (OECD CPM 2310 for equity capital; 2399 for other capital transfers), with the corporate LEI or CRA Business Number in field 72. For USD-denominated injections, settlement clears via the bank's New York correspondent on CHIPS; CAD clears via LVTS. Verification: the bank will validate the remitter against sanctions lists, confirm beneficial ownership of the sending account, and require the share subscription or loan agreement to be filed with the relationship manager before booking. Interest on intercompany loans must be at or above the CRA prescribed rate (Section 80.4 of the Income Tax Act) and the loan documented in writing. All inbound international EFTs of CA$10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC by the bank. No Bank of Canada or OSFI prior approval is required.Operational disbursements (spares, provisions, crew wages, insurance, port fees) clear via SWIFT MT103 with corresponding purpose codes for payments for current transactions; CAD payments clear in LVTS real-time gross settlement, USD payments clear via the clearing bank's New York correspondent on CHIPS. Large capital procurements and vessel retrofits settle via documentary letters of credit (MT700) with commercial invoice, bill of lading, and certificate of inspection as presenting documents. For seller-financed vessel acquisition, an Escrow Maritime structure is recommended: a vault-escrow account at a Schedule I bank with dual-signatory release conditions (vessel mortgage discharge plus bill of sale delivery). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (for goods) or CAD 500,000 (for services) in aggregate transactions with non-residents; also required if the corporation is a 'large entity' (gross revenue > CAD 50 million)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions (e.g., under BEPS MLI) require genuine economic substance for treaty benefits; offshore vessel leasing must demonstrate operational substance (crew, management, and decision-making in Canada) to avoid recharacterization

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax evaded (gross negligence penalty) plus interest; criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239 with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate tax under s.227.1 if they fail to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for willful evasion
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under GAAR or treaty abuse provisions; reassessment of income and penalties up to 25% of the tax avoided; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement; possible injunctions and asset freezes
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment for officers

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned for AML violations if they knowingly fail to implement compliance programs; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation
