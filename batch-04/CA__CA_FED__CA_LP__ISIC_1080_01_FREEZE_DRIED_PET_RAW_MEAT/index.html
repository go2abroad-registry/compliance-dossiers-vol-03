Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online (BRO) service and CRA My Business Account portal. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs and commercial import/export program registrations. Provincial revenue authorities separately administer provincial sales taxes where applicable.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership generally obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) and the required program accounts, including GST/HST, payroll, import-export, and corporate income tax accounts where applicable. Registration is completed through CRA Business Registration Online, by telephone, or by prescribed form. A non-resident partnership should provide its legal name, partnership agreement or formation evidence, Canadian business address or authorized representative details, responsible-person information, formation jurisdiction, business activities, fiscal period, and non-resident ownership information. The partnership must register for GST/HST when required, may register voluntarily while below the small-supplier threshold, and must obtain an import-export RM account before commercial exports or imports. CRA may request certified constitutional documents, identity verification, authorization evidence, and proof of Canadian business activities. A partnership is not generally subject to Canadian corporate income tax as a separate taxable person; its income and losses are allocated to partners under the Income Tax Act. Non-resident partners may require Canadian tax identification numbers and may be subject to Canadian filing, withholding, and treaty-relief procedures.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a Canadian limited partnership. A valid Canadian partnership is generally fiscally transparent, with partnership income computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners under the Income Tax Act. Elections may apply to specific matters, including partnership fiscal-period and accounting positions, foreign-affiliate or treaty-related positions, resource expenditures, rollover transactions, and GST/HST joint-election mechanisms where statutory conditions are satisfied. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act can deny deductions, impose inclusion consequences, or otherwise neutralize arrangements involving differences in entity or instrument characterization; they do not create a general elective entity-classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding supplies of capital property and financial services, measured over a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Zero-rated supplies generally count toward the threshold. Registration may be voluntary below the threshold. HST rates are generally 13% in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia, and 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island. Provincial sales-tax registration may apply separately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied for export from Canada may generally be zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act where the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including delivery or shipment outside Canada and retention of prescribed evidence that the goods left Canada. A manufacturer of freeze-dried pet treats or meal kibble must also comply with applicable CFIA requirements, destination-country rules, animal-product controls, labeling requirements, and export-certificate requirements. Services may be zero-rated only under specific statutory place-of-supply rules, such as qualifying services performed in respect of goods that are exported, certain services supplied to non-residents, or other prescribed categories; merely invoicing a non-resident does not automatically establish zero-rating. A GST/HST registrant may claim input tax credits for eligible GST/HST incurred in commercial activities and may seek a refund or rebate only where the applicable statutory conditions and documentation are met.

  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier evidence showing shipment outside Canada
  • Packing list and inventory or production records linking the exported goods to the zero-rated invoice
  • Proof of delivery or foreign import record where reasonably available
  • Purchase order, sales contract, customer correspondence, and payment evidence

Canada has no general federal real-time e-invoicing clearance, mandatory fiscal-device stamping, or universal invoice-transaction API requirement for ordinary GST/HST taxpayers. Registrants must issue compliant invoices or other prescribed documentation, maintain electronic or paper books and records, retain source documents generally for six years, and file GST/HST returns through CRA-approved electronic channels where electronic filing is required or elected. Invoices should identify the supplier, BN or GST/HST account number where applicable, date, consideration, tax rate, tax amount or qualifying zero-rating, and sufficient description of supplies. CBSA commercial export and import data may be filed electronically through approved customs systems, including the CARM client portal and related customs interfaces.

Canada has no general foreign-exchange control regime requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days, and no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applies to this business. CRA may reassess GST/HST, deny input tax credits or export zero-rating, impose net-tax and gross-negligence penalties, assess interest, and recover improperly paid GST/HST refunds or rebates. False statements, omissions, fabricated export evidence, or obstructive conduct may trigger penalties under the Excise Tax Act and Tax Administration Act and may support criminal prosecution. Customs violations may result in seizure, administrative monetary penalties, duties and taxes, permit consequences, and prosecution under the Customs Act or related legislation. Where a supply was incorrectly treated as zero-rated, the registrant may become liable for the tax that should have been collected, applicable penalties, and arrears interest. The limitation period is generally four years for GST/HST reassessments, subject to statutory extensions or exceptions for misrepresentation, fraud, or failure to file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange-control approvals, ceiling limits, or remittance licensing requirements for inbound equity injections, outbound trade payments, debt service, or partnership profit distributions. The Canadian dollar is freely floating and fully deliverable onshore. For a fiscally transparent limited partnership, non-resident partners may repatriate distributable profits and liquidating distributions without Bank of Canada clearance; the only fiscal friction is Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents at the 25% statutory rate, ordinarily reduced to 15% for U.S. partners under the Canada-United States Tax Convention (Article X), and to lower treaty rates for partners resident in other eligible jurisdictions. Because the LP is pass-through for income tax purposes, and assuming no permanent establishment or carrying-on-business issues in Canada, no separate entity-level profit-remittance tax is imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts. Freeze-dried pet-food proceeds denominated in USD or other foreign currencies may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency operating accounts with a Canadian Schedule I bank without forced conversion to CAD. The only compulsory reporting duties are (i) FINTRAC reports on every international electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more, and (ii) income-tax conversion of foreign-currency income to CAD using Bank of Canada daily exchange rates or, where the taxpayer has made a functional-currency election under the Income Tax Act, the elective reporting currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), constituted under the Bank of Canada Act, is the central bank and operates Canada's freely floating exchange-rate framework; prudential regulation and supervision of federally incorporated banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing surveillance over banks, money services businesses, and payment service providers is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) pursuant to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control ministry.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational settlement float, Low for primary treasury functions. Canada does not grant a standalone electronic money institution (EMI) licence; offshore digital payment platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) must register as money services businesses with FINTRAC and comply with the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act framework, but they are not CDIC-insured deposit-taking institutions. Consequently, they cannot lawfully hold escrowed subscription capital, cannot issue Canadian commercial letters of credit, and exhibit heightened OFAC friction on the USD correspondent corridor. They are viable only as auxiliary rails for small-value FX conversions, vendor payouts, and Platform_Pay settlement; all capital aggregation, CFIA-linked trade payments, and hedged liquidity must reside at a full-service Schedule I bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

38% federal basic corporate income tax rate before provincial abatement; after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction, the effective federal general corporate rate is 15%; qualifying manufacturing and processing profits may be taxed at the preferential 13% federal rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under ITA s.212(2). A Canadian-resident corporation paying dividends to a non-resident partner or shareholder must withhold 25%, unless reduced by treaty, for example 5% for a US person owning at least 10% of the voting shares and 15% in most other cases. Ordinary distributions by a limited partnership are not dividends and are not subject to Part XIII dividend withholding; such allocations retain their character and are taxed under Part I or Part XIV as applicable.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 Summary and T5013 slips must be filed no later than March 31 following the calendar year in which the partnership fiscal period ends; a partnership with non-resident partners remains subject to the same annual filing obligation.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return is due no later than six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; the balance-due date is generally two months after year-end, or three months for a CCPC eligible for the small business deduction; Part XIII withholding on amounts paid to non-residents is due on the 15th day of the month following the payment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 is the CRA's de facto transactional threshold for requiring contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation covering aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions involving a Canadian partnership; the mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting threshold is C$750,000,000 consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to those of offshore financial centres. Instead, the applicable doctrines are the Income Tax Act General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s.245, the sham doctrine, and judicial anti-treaty-shopping principles. The limited partnership must maintain genuine business substance: physical premises in Canada, qualified staff, independent decision-making, functional equipment, real manufacturing activity, and contemporaneous board/partner meeting records. A partnership lacking economic substance may be disregarded or recharacterized by the CRA, and treaty benefits or domestic tax losses may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239, tax evasion is punishable by a fine of 50% to 200% of the taxes evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; civil gross negligence penalties under s.163(2) equal to 50% of understated tax or any misclaimed refund.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who authorize, participate in, or consent to false filings expose personal assets to civil restitution, enforcement orders, travel bans, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced where fraud or wilful evasion is proved.
Unremitted Withholding Taxes

Statutory sanction: Under ITA ss.227(8) and 227(9), unremitted withholdings are deemed trust property; the payor is liable for the full amount plus 10% penalties and compounding interest, and criminal prosecution may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for unremitted Part XIII and payroll withholdings, including penalties and interest; CRA director-liability assessments, asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment are available enforcement remedies.
Transfer Pricing Non-Arm's-Length Transactions

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.247, the CRA may recharacterize the transaction and compute an arm's-length adjustment; a 10% transfer-pricing penalty applies when the net adjustment is greater than the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue, plus interest and compounding penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false or incomplete transfer-pricing documentation are exposed to civil fraud claims, restitution orders, travel bans, and imprisonment where the conduct rises to wilful participation in tax evasion.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply GAAR under s.245 to deny the tax benefit, disregard the limited partnership, reallocate income to the substance owner, and impose gross negligence penalties if facts were misrepresented; treaty benefits may also be denied under general anti-abuse principles.

Director personal liability: Courts may disregard separate legal personality where the LP is used to commit fraud or abuse legislative intent; directors and controlling partners may be held liable for unpaid taxes, civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
AML and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act permits administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 for non-compliance; the Criminal Code allows restraint, seizure, and forfeiture of property linked to proceeds of crime, and sanctions violations carry criminal imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who exhibit wilful blindness are personally exposed to civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment, and the corporate veil will be pierced where the business structure is used as an instrument of laundering or sanctions evasion.
