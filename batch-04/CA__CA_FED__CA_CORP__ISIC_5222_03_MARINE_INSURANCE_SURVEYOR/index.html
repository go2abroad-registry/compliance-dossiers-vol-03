Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for GST/HST and federal corporate income tax administration; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs export control. E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owner marine survey corporation: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act or register extra-provincially to obtain a corporate registry file number; (2) apply for the CRA 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; (3) establish the RC (corporate income tax) program account and, where GST/HST registration is required or elected, the RT program account; (4) appoint a Canadian representative for service of process and submit the CRA non-resident ownership and Canadian address information; (5) no CBSA exporter identifier is required unless the surveyor itself arranges physical export of salvage or cargo.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Business Corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation under ITA s. 89(1) taxed at the entity level. Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed on Canadian-source business income only (territorial scope). No tax-exempt-zone benefits apply; zero-rated exports affect GST/HST cash flow only and do not alter income tax status.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; entity classification follows corporate law and the CRA's administrative positions for foreign hybrids (e.g., U.S. LLCs). Available elections include: (i) the ITA s. 216 election to file a net-income return for Canadian real property rental income; (ii) the s. 219 branch-tax regime applicable to non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada through a permanent establishment; (iii) the GST/HST Quick Method election for eligible small registrants; (iv) the ETA s. 156 closely-related-corporations election for nil-consideration internal supplies; and (v) the selected-listed-financial-institution special attribution method (SAM) election. Hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 15(6.1)) restrict deduction/non-inclusion structures involving hybrid instruments or disregarded entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: the CRA small-supplier threshold. A non-resident marine surveyor carrying on a business in Canada must register for GST/HST when its worldwide taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits, and registration can be mandatory without regard to the threshold where the non-resident is deemed to make taxable supplies in Canada under ETA s. 143.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of the marine hull/cargo damage inspection supply is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: (a) section 1 zero-rates the supply of tangible personal property exported by the supplier; (b) section 5 zero-rates a service performed in respect of tangible personal property that is exported by or on behalf of the supplier; (c) sections 7 and 9 zero-rate a supply of services to a non-resident recipient where the service is not in respect of Canadian real property and not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. For a survey-only engagement, the electronic delivery of the report to a non-resident underwriter is zero-rated where the inspected vessel or cargo is outside Canada at the time of inspection or is directly exported by or on behalf of the surveyor; otherwise the supply is deemed made in Canada and the 0% rate is unavailable.

  • CBSA Canada Export Declaration (B13A or Canadian Export Reporting System electronic confirmation) where the inspected cargo or vessel is physically exported
  • Bill of Lading evidencing the export movement of the surveyed goods or vessel
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-issued proof of FX receipt from the non-resident underwriter)
  • Executed marine survey engagement contract naming the non-resident recipient and specifying the 0% GST/HST rated supply
  • Final marine survey report with surveyor credentials, inspection location, and date-stamped evidence
  • Commercial invoice bearing the CRA GST/HST registration number and the non-resident billing address
  • Documentary proof of export (cargo manifest, customs transit document, or vessel clearance certificate) where the surveyed goods are exported after inspection

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice pre-authorization, or digital stamping. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 300,000 must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE for reporting periods ending on or after 1 January 2024, and must remit net tax electronically when the amount exceeds CAD 10,000. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Interoperability Framework is voluntary and Peppol-based; no CRA-operated invoice validator or government clearance code is required for zero-rated marine survey invoices.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated export claim within four years from the end of the reporting period, and without limitation where the return contains a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. On audit, the exporter must produce the complete documentary export bundle on demand; failure to sustain the 0% rate triggers reassessment at the applicable GST/HST rate with daily compound interest, a default penalty under ETA s. 280 of 5% of the unreported net tax plus 1% per full month (up to 12 months), and a civil penalty under ETA s. 285 of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax sought to be avoided for knowingly or grossly negligent false statements. Wilful evasion under ETA s. 327 is an indictable offence punishable by a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable under ETA s. 323 for unremitted amounts, penalties, and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account; there is no administered rate and no prior authorization requirement for cross-border payments. Profit remittances are unrestricted at the exchange-control level, but dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced as low as 5% for a qualifying 10%+ corporate shareholder and typically 15% in default). Foreign-currency accounts such as USD operating and sweep accounts are ordinary products at Schedule I banks. The marine surveyor's USD fee income from offshore underwriters may be retained abroad or repatriated freely without any Bank of Canada filing or exchange-control license.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable in Canada. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation exists for export receipts or cross-border services invoices. USD survey fees received from foreign hull and cargo underwriters may be credited to a foreign-currency account and converted at commercial market rates whenever treasury policy requires CAD liquidity. The only binding cross-border reporting rule is FINTRAC obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, which require reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and currency movement reports at the same threshold; these are informational AML/ATF filings, not exchange-control restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (central monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential regulator); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML/ATF intelligence). No dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration exists; exchange-rate policy is market-based under the Currency Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary disbursement rail; Low as sole treasury provider. Canada has no EU-style EMI or authorized payment institution licence, and the Bank Act reserves deposit-taking to chartered banks; an offshore digital EMI therefore cannot legally operate as the principal depository for CA_CORP. Wise Payments Canada Inc., registered as a money-services business with FINTRAC and as a payment service provider with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (in force November 2024), is viable for cross-border vendor payments and field-expense disbursements below 50,000 CAD per transaction, provided the Schedule I bank account remains the treasury anchor. Foreign unregistered EMIs must not solicit or receive funds from Canadian clients.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate controlled transactions (goods, services, intangibles, interest or fees) with all non-resident related persons triggers mandatory T106 filing; Canada does not prescribe formal Local File/Master File documents, but contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be provided when requested by CRA; Country-by-Country Reports required for consolidated groups with ≥ EUR 750 million revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general Economic Substance Regulations, but the GAAR (s. 245) may void an avoidance transaction that lacks substantive commercial purpose; the 'permanent establishment' test and the 'treaty shopping' limitations require actual business activity, board oversight, and risk-taking in Canada for a marine surveyor corporation; insurance brokers and surveyors licensed in Canada must maintain a physical office and professional staff.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty 50% of understated tax plus daily interest; CRA may seize assets through Tax Court judgment

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for corporation's unremitted source withholdings, GST/HST, and 50% of unpaid penalty amounts if they failed to exercise required diligence (s. 227.1 and s. 323); court-ordered civil restitution, asset freeze, travel-hold through departure prohibition orders, and potential imprisonment for fraud
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at arm's length price; penalty of 10% of adjusted net amount if documentation absent; CRA may impose 10% 'transfers of pricing violation' penalty and refuse treaty relief; criminal prosecution for fraudulent misrepresentation

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or participated in misrepresentation are personally liable for the penalty and additional federal tax (s. 227.1); exclusion from cross-border board appointments; travel restrictions under the Income Tax Act departure prohibition
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Application of GAAR s. 245 to re-determine the tax consequences, denial/termination of treaty benefits, amended assessments with interest and penalties up to 50% of understated tax; corporate name strike-off and revocation of T2 registration; possible criminal sanction if the shell is used for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors can be sued for restitution of distributions received from the shell; May be subject to 'piercing the corporate veil' if the corporation was interposed for a dominant fraudulent or misrepresentation purpose; Court may issue costs orders and travel bans
AML and Insurance Regulatory Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines up to CAD 250,000 for individuals and CAD 1 million for corporate entities; licence suspension or cancellation; CRA may impose Remittance-Withholding Penalties and suspend the T2 return processing; FINTRAC monetary penalties up to CAD 1.5 million for each violation; prohibition from participating in government procurement

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fines under provincial insurance statutes if they knowingly permit non-registered practice; in serious cases, personal imprisonment under AML laws up to 3 years; travel bans under the Federal Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act
