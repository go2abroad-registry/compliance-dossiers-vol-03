Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals.html) and GST/HST NETFILE (for registrant return filing).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting a commercial catering business in Canada must first incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (or a provincial act) to obtain a corporate identity. It then applies to the CRA for a 15-character Business Number (BN) using Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Related Accounts', filed online through CRA's My Business Account portal, by telephone, or by mail. Within the same application, the entity opens a GST/HST account (account code 'RT') and, where applicable, payroll ('RP') and import/export ('RM') accounts. Non-resident corporations that do not have permanent establishment in Canada may register directly for GST/HST using the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration procedure, supplying the name and address of a Canadian agent if required. The BN becomes the statutory TIN for all corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and customs interactions. Tax registrations are effective upon the legal commencement of commercial activity, and CRA will issue a letter of confirmation for the BN and accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime akin to the United States for Canadian-resident corporations. The relevant statutory elections for a commercial catering corporation include: (1) the joint election under Excise Tax Act section 156 allowing closely related corporations to treat a taxable supply as having been made for nil consideration for GST/HST purposes; (2) the capital dividend election under Income Tax Act subsection 83(2) to elect that a dividend be treated as a tax-free capital dividend to shareholders; (3) the associated corporation election under Income Tax Act subsection 256(9) in circumstances of common control; (4) the alternative fiscal year-end election under Income Tax Act subsection 249.1(1) for corporations wholly owned by non-resident airlines; and (5) where a non-resident airline's Canadian-source income is concerned, the vendor may rely on applicable treaty elections under the Canada–US tax treaty or other bilateral treaties to claim permanent establishment or business profits relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — gross revenue (excluding zero-rated supplies) from all taxable supplies made by the entity in Canada over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters. Once the CAD 30,000 small-supplier limit is surpassed, registration is mandatory. Voluntary registration below the threshold is available to claim input tax credits and reduce competitive friction for non-resident airline clients.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a food or catering supply made by an airport bonded catering kitchen can qualify for the zero-rated (0% GST/HST) 'exported goods' regime where the supplier sells tangible personal property (meals) and delivers the goods to a common carrier (the airline) for immediate export, and the goods are actually exported by the carrier as part of the international flight. The catering supplier must obtain documentary proof of export and cannot retain any further use or beneficial entitlement to the goods. In addition, a supply of a catering service performed for a non-resident airline may be zero-rated as a supply of an intangible personal property/service to a non-resident (other than a Canadian resident) provided that the service relates to goods that are being exported, and the service is not performed to goods consumed in Canada other than incidental to the export. For catering to domestic flights, zero-rating may be denied because the goods are not exported. The supplier must also ensure the invoice is issued to the non-resident airline and clearly identifies the destination; failure to retain the required evidence makes the supply taxable at 5% GST/HST.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A or CAED electronic submission)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (including International Flight Aircraft Manifest)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Settlement Evidence in CAD evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Supplier's Catering Delivery Receipt and Ramp Sign-Off Log
  • Non-Resident Airline Contract and International Flight Schedule
  • Bonded Warehouse / Bonded Kitchen Control Records (where applicable)
  • Cargo Export Document or Consignor's Export Declaration

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime, API reporting, or digital stamping of tax invoices at the federal level. GST/HST invoices may be issued electronically and CRA accepts electronic records, provided the registrant maintains books and records in Canada or electronically accessible from Canada. For federal government procurement, suppliers participating in buyAndSell/GC procurement must support Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing (Peppol BIS v3) standards, but those requirements are commercial procurement mandates, not tax clearance obligations. CRA does not pre-clear invoices.

CRA may audit export zero-rating claims and reassess within the normal four-year limitation period; the period is unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. If a catering supplier is found to have improperly claimed zero-rating on domestic flights or to have fabricated export evidence, CRA will: (i) reassess to collect unremitted GST/HST (the 5% federal rate plus provincial component, where applicable) together with compound daily interest (currently a prescribed rate set quarterly under the ETA/ITA); (ii) impose a civil penalty under Excise Tax Act subsection 280(1) equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understated net tax; (iii) impose a separate supplementary penalty of up to 50% for gross negligence under ETA subsection 285(1); and (iv) where the claim is a deliberate false statement, subject the responsible persons to criminal prosecution for tax evasion, with fines not exceeding 200% of the amount sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. CRA may also claw back input tax credits and unreasonably late registrations, and where a non-resident airline is connected to the scheme, disallow deductions, levy non-resident withholding penalties, and pursue director liability under section 323 of the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible across both current and capital account transactions. No exchange control approvals, capital repatriation permits, or mandatory profit remittance filings exist under federal law. Dividends and branch profits may be remitted freely in any currency, subject only to corporate solvency tests under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) s. 214 and to withholding tax obligations for non-resident shareholders per Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. Anti-money-laundering reporting does not constitute a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation requirement for foreign exchange receipts. Airport bonded in-flight catering revenues derived in CAD or foreign currency may be retained, hedged, or converted at the corporation's discretion. Only FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply for large cross-border electronic funds transfers (CAD 10,000+).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking oversight and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT; the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act govern legal tender and settlement finality via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS / Lynx).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for standalone offshore digital EMIs; Medium when partnered with a federally regulated Tier 1 bank. Since September 2024, payment service providers operating in Canada must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and hold funds in segregated accounts at a Canadian financial institution. Offshore EMIs not RPAA-registered cannot lawfully initiate CAD clearing, hold client funds in Canada, or route ACH payments. Practical blueprint: retain the offshore EMI only to facilitate non-resident payroll and secondary FX notional pooling; all primary liquidity clearing must flow through a regulated Canadian bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for injection of non-resident shareholder capital or intercompany loan from parent: (1) execute a CBCA-compliant directors' resolution setting out the share issuance or debt instrument; (2) route via SWIFT MT103 from the originating bank with CAD USD payment purpose code 'TRF' (transfer) and remittance information stating 'INVESTMENT – ISSUANCE OF COMMON SHARES' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN', using the Depositary and Clearing Corporation (CDS) settlement standards for any security-related transfer; (3) for amounts above CAD 10,000 the receiving bank records an EFTS report to FINTRAC; no Bank of Canada approval is required and no unofficial or unlicensed transfer corridor may be used; (4) to meet the requirement for clear segregation of the 0.10% CBCA authorized capital tax offset (if applicable) or future escrow uses, direct the wire to the Capital Escrow Account and then internally transfer to the Operational Account via the bank's internal journal entry with a documented board reference.Primary clearing rails for airline catering trade settlements: (a) for invoices and galley equipment imports, use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with gpi tracking via the correspondent banking network, quoting purpose code 'SUPPLIES' or 'TRADE'; (b) for airport concession payments and inflight duty-free procurement, use Canadian dollar ACH transactions routed through Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS), with FX conversion executed at the Tier 1 bank's desk rate; (c) for large non-recurring purchases (e.g., kitchen equipment, aircraft ovens), use a Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Canadian bank in favor of the overseas equipment manufacturer, with presentation documents including the airway bill, inspection certificate for duty-free bonded goods, and a Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) customs entry receipt confirming temporary importation or warehouse relief; (d) settle the beneficiary bank through correspondent rails with 48-hour confirmation via SWIFT GPI.
