Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported or offshore services performed outside Canada qualify for 0% GST rate provided recipient is non-resident and export conditions are satisfied.

  • Export Declaration (where required), Bill of Lading or equivalent shipping proof, Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment to non-resident

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility under IMF Article VIII and imposes no foreign exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar is fully floating. Dividends, royalties, and intercompany payments are freely remittable, subject only to domestic Part XIII withholding taxes (typically 25% treaty-adjusted), source-of-income rules, and FINTRAC AML reporting. No central bank authorization is required for profit repatriation or capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender or forced conversion ratio. Exporters may receive, hold, and settle foreign currency receipts in any manner. No statutory timeline applies to repatriation or conversion of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary and currency authority; foreign exchange administration is decentralized under the Currency Act, with prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/ATF oversight by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI / payment fintechs have Low-Medium viability as a primary treasury route for a Canadian operating entity. They are not chartered banks under the Bank Act, do not offer CDIC deposit insurance, and cannot access the Lynx high-value clearing system directly. They may provide ancillary FX or card issuance if registered as a money services business (MSB) in applicable provinces and, if carrying out electronic payment functions for Canadians, must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and comply with financial integrity requirements. Real CAD settlement is most compliant via a Tier 1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the foreign owner should be routed as SWIFT MT103 (USD/EUR) or ISO 20022 wire via the Lynx system for CAD telegraphic transfers. Use payment purpose code B01 (Direct Investment) or equivalent, include the legal entity identifier (LEI), reference the corporate board resolution approving the loan/equity injection. For reportable investments under the Investment Canada Act, mandatory post-closing notification must be submitted; for aggregate investments use the CEM2035 form. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian taxpayers with cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties exceeding CAD 1 million in gross revenue must prepare contemporaneous documentation; Master File required for transactions exceeding CAD 10 million; Local File required for transactions exceeding CAD 5 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires bona fide business purpose; arrangements lacking economic substance may be challenged by the CRA

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties: 50% gross negligence penalty

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unremitted withholdings or unpaid corporate taxes; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under GAAR to deny tax benefits; penalties up to 25% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for fraudulent conveyances or if they knowingly participated in a sham
AML/CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million per violation for corporations; criminal penalties for non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly or negligently failed to comply
