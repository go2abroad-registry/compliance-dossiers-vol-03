Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

CBCA s.20 requires every federal corporation to maintain a registered office in Canada situated in the province or territory stated in its articles. A physical street address, including the premises of a licensed or established corporate service provider/registered agent that receives documents and is capable of statutory service, satisfies the registered office requirement. A virtual office that is merely a post-office box, mail-forwarding box, or a purely online address without a physical presence or authorized agent in the stated province is not permissible. For a virtual CFO consultancy (ISIC 6920, NAICS 541219), the use of a commercial agent's physical registered-office address is accepted in practice; however, for a non-resident-controlled federal corporation, CRA may examine whether the virtual office supports factual commercial substance for treaty and economic-substance purposes, particularly where foreign directors manage operations and no Canadian employees or physical service capacity exist.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA), and GST/HST NETFILE

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated federal Business Corporation or a foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) as the single federal tax identifier. Procedure: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or register an extra-provincial corporation; (2) apply for the BN through BRO or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); (3) register for a GST/HST account under the BN if annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or on a voluntary basis; (4) register for a payroll account and import/export account where applicable; (5) obtain provincial registrations (e.g., Revenu Québec for QST and provincial corporate income tax) where a permanent establishment exists; (6) for non-resident corporations, use the CRA Non-Resident account registration procedure with Form RC1 and supporting foreign incorporation documents.

Procedure: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act or register an extra-provincial corporation; (2) apply for the BN through BRO or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); (3) register for a GST/HST account under the BN if annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or on a voluntary basis; (4) register for a payroll account and import/export account where applicable; (5) obtain provincial registrations (e.g., Revenu Québec for QST and provincial corporate income tax) where a permanent establishment exists; (6) for non-resident corporations, use the CRA Non-Resident account registration procedure with Form RC1 and supporting foreign incorporation documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The Income Tax Act (Canada) contains no general check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: ITA section 85 rollover election to transfer assets to a taxable Canadian corporation at mutually agreed amounts; ITA subsection 216(1) election for a non-resident to file on net rental income; ITA subsection 93(1) election on dividends from foreign affiliates; ITA section 18.2/18.3 hybrid mismatch and excessive interest recharacterization rules; Excise Tax Act subsection 156(1) election for nil consideration on supplies between closely related corporations; and GST/HST grouping election for closely related corporations. Foreign entity classification follows CRA administrative practice, generally respecting U.S. check-the-box classification of LLCs while applying Canadian substance-over-form and treaty override rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (small supplier threshold); registration becomes mandatory immediately when the threshold is exceeded in any calendar quarter; a higher CAD 50,000 threshold applies to charities; nil thresholds apply to taxi and ride-sharing services and certain non-resident performers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act: Part V for goods exported as commercial goods; Part VII for services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and where the service is not supplied for consumption in Canada; and Schedule VI sections 10-13 for intangible personal property (e.g., software or IP) supplied to a non-resident for use outside Canada. Fractional CFO advisory and multicurrency bookkeeping services delivered to foreign clients will generally qualify as zero-rated services under Part VII where the recipient is a non-resident non-registrant and the benefit of the service is not received in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or commercial export reporting through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading, airway waybill, or trucker's freight receipt for goods shipments
  • Commercial invoice and signed service contract detailing scope of work and place of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank transfer evidence proving receipt of payment from the non-resident client
  • Non-resident status evidence (e.g., foreign certificate of incorporation, tax residence certificate, or written confirmation that the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant)

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, API-based stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing clearance regime is currently in force. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for all registrants, and e-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 standard. Registrants must retain complete invoice and supporting records in Canada for a six-year statutory retention period.

Zero-rating claims are subject to strict CRA audit scrutiny. CRA may reassess within four years of the original assessment, or without time limit in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Refunds are recoverable with compounded daily interest under ETA section 281. Civil penalties include a false-statement penalty under ETA section 280 of up to 100% of the net tax evaded or refunded, and gross-negligence penalties under ETA section 285. Criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 for fraudulent export claims can result in fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. Convincing contemporaneous evidence of export and foreign payment receipt is essential to sustain a zero-rating claim.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a floating exchange rate regime under the Bank of Canada Act with full current account convertibility. Capital account transactions are generally open for resident corporations engaged in legitimate business activities such as service fees and dividend remittances. Profit remittance is permitted without prior approval for current account items, subject only to FINTRAC reporting thresholds of CAD 10,000 and beneficial ownership verification. Hurdles are minimal for routine multicurrency ledger activities typical of outsourced virtual CFO services.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service-based export receipts (professional advisory fees) do not require mandatory surrender to the Bank of Canada. All foreign currency receipts must be converted to CAD for Canadian tax filing purposes within statutory timelines, typically 90 days. No fixed conversion ratio applies; however, all FX transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 trigger mandatory reporting to FINTRAC via electronic funds transfer reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
