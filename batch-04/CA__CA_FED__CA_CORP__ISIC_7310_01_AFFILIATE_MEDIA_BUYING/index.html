Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal tax administration; GST/HST registration and filing through CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) portal.

E-government filing portal

Register for a Business Number (BN), the de facto TIN for corporations, via CRA's Business Registration Online or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Non-resident corporations without a place of business in Canada may file Form RC1E. For GST/HST purposes, add a GST/HST program account to the BN on the same registration or by writing to the relevant CRA tax centre. The BN is a 9-digit number followed by a two-character program identifier (e.g., RC0001). Registration must be obtained before charging GST/HST in Canada.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian resident and non-resident corporations are separate taxable entities under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no look-through treatment is available for federal income tax purposes). — Register for a Business Number (BN), the de facto TIN for corporations, via CRA's Business Registration Online or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian resident and non-resident corporations are separate taxable entities under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no look-through treatment is available for federal income tax purposes).

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box classification regime. Permanent combinations: corporations are always taxed as corporations. Elections that may be relevant include the S.216 election for non-residents receiving Canadian rental income to be taxed on net taxable income rather than gross Part XIII tax, elections respecting foreign affiliate status under the S.95 foreign affiliate regime, and specified hybrid mismatch election forms added by Canada's BEPS Action 2 implementation. No general pass-through election exists for Canadian corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total annual worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies for most persons; a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada is required to register for GST/HST once its taxable supplies sourced to Canada exceed this small supplier threshold, subject to specific exceptions (e.g., supplies of excluded property, or supplies deemed not carried on business in Canada).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods requires physical export from Canada by the supplier or on its behalf, and evidence that the goods were shipped to a destination outside Canada; supplies of services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident of Canada and not rendered primarily in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property located in Canada (or the service is performed wholly outside Canada). Digital advertising, affiliate media buying, and lead-generation services supplied to non-residents will normally be zero-rated as intangible property/services if not referable to Canadian situs property.

  • B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration (or CES/CAED equivalent with detailed goods description and value)
  • Commercial Invoice or Supplier Invoice including Canadian business number, description, quantity, value, and zero-rating indicator
  • Bill of Lading or other shipping/transport evidence showing export from Canada and destination outside the customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT/MT103 wire confirmation, or other proof of payment receipt from the foreign buyer to substantiate the export proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing in Canada. GST/HST registrants must retain and issue invoices containing prescribed particulars (BN, invoice date, description, amount, and GST/HST rate and amount). Electronic invoices are permitted if they are accessible and readable by the CRA. Mandatory electronic GST/HST return filing applies to registrants with taxable supplies in excess of CAD 1.5 million, but this is not a transaction-level clearance requirement.

CRA audits may require full due diligence on every zero-rated supply, including proof of export, non-resident status, and payment documentation. If the documentation is insufficient or the commercial substance is found to reflect a round-trip financing arrangement, the CRA may deny zero-rating, reassess GST/HST at the standard rate plus interest, and levy penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act (5%–10% of the amount if uncorrected, or a higher civil penalty of 25% for knowingly making a false statement). For corporate income tax, such arrangements may also attract transfer-price reassessments, Part XIII withholding and penalties, and general anti-avoidance rule application under the Income Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts under a market-determined free float. Capital repatriation, dividend distribution, and profit remittance to non-resident parents require no BOC or OSFI approval; the sole fiscal impediment is Part XIII withholding tax of the Income Tax Act (25% statutory, reducible to 5% or 15% under an applicable income tax treaty, e.g., the Canada-U.S. Convention, subject to limitation-on-benefits provisions). The Investment Canada Act (ICA) applies only to non-Canadian investments exceeding the WTO enterprise-value threshold (CAD 1.095 billion as of the 2024 annual adjustment for non-cultural businesses; CAD 5 million direct / CAD 50 million indirect for cultural businesses). Digital advertising media arbitrage (ISIC 7310 / NAICS 541810) is non-cultural and typically falls below all ICA filing thresholds unless foreign control of a Canadian cultural enterprise is established. No exchange-control contracts, licensing, or by-law approvals are required for inbound or outbound treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline for repatriation of export/advertising-services proceeds, nor any mandatory conversion ratio for foreign-currency receipts. Affiliate media-buying revenues may be retained indefinitely in USD, CAD, or any convertible currency. Reporting obligations remain applicable: (i) FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Report (LCTR) for cash receipts over CAD 10,000; (ii) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) for inbound/outbound wire and ACH transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period; (iii) Common Reporting Standard (CRS) and FATCA account disclosures; and (iv) CBCA annual return declarations of foreign beneficial ownership.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Foreign-exchange regulation in Canada is liberalized under the Currency Act R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52; the former Foreign Exchange Control Act has been repealed and no dedicated foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control permit authority currently exists. OSFI supervises all Schedule I banks; FINTRAC enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) at the transactional reporting layer.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High as an ancillary payout rail; Medium as sole primary banking. Wise Business (Canada) operates as a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business (MSB) through Canadian partner banks and offers low-cost multi-currency accounts with segregated client funds; Payoneer maintains a Canadian MSB license and supports publisher/affiliate payouts to 70+ countries. Legal constraints: any fintech EMI must be registered as an MSB under the PCMLTFA before client onboarding, must execute its own LCTR/EFTR reporting obligations, and cannot provide deposit insurance (CDIC) nor extend credit or overdraft facilities. Mercury (U.S.-chartered, non-branch foreign bank) is not viable as a primary operating account for a CA-FED entity; it may only serve as an upstream treasury account at the U.S. parent level, with the Canadian subsidiary settling through a Schedule I bank. Recommended architecture: RBC or TD as the primary settlement bank, with Wise Business sub-accounts for low-value publisher payouts where SWIFT per-transaction costs exceed rail economics.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: (1) Inward remittance from the U.S. parent to the RBC or TD Operating Account via SWIFT MT103, with field 23E purpose code '7020' (capital to subsidiary) and field 70 REMITTANCE INFO stating 'SUBORDINATED LOAN' or 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION'; (2) instruct the receiving bank to retain USD in a dual-currency sub-ledger, or execute a same-day spot conversion to CAD at the bank's retail-trade rate with the FX contract recorded in the treasury system; (3) onward domestic movements settled through the Lynx large-value payment system (Payments Canada successor to LVTS) or the ACSS for sub-threshold amounts; (4) for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, the bank files the FINTRAC EFTR and the entity must avoid structuring to evade reporting; (5) document the contribution in the CRA corporate register and evaluate a section 85.1 share-for-share rollover or section 86 reorganization if preferred-equity instruments are used. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under the CBCA, every business corporation must appoint an auditor at its first and each subsequent annual meeting; the audit may be dispensed with only by unanimous shareholder resolution each fiscal year for a non-distributing corporation. No small-company revenue or asset exemption exists under the CBCA. Distributing (public) corporations cannot waive the audit, and a shareholder or group holding at least 5% of the outstanding voting shares may apply to a court to require an independent auditor.
