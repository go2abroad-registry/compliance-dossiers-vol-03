Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), administered through My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online (BRO). Official access at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian or foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). The BN is the statutory TIN. Separate program accounts are required: RC for corporation income tax and RT for GST/HST. Non-resident entities without a Canadian establishment file Form RC1 (Application for Registration) with the CRA International Tax Services Office; non-resident digital services suppliers may use the simplified GST/HST registration facility. Online registration is generally immediate; paper applications take 15-30 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian federal business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Income Tax Act (ITA) subsection 2(2), resident in Canada and taxable on worldwide income. No pass-through eligibility applies to this entity form. GST/HST registration is a separate assessment nexus; a corporation is also liable for payroll, excise, and provincial allocation obligations as applicable. — A Canadian or foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian federal business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Income Tax Act (ITA) subsection 2(2), resident in Canada and taxable on worldwide income. No pass-through eligibility applies to this entity form. GST/HST registration is a separate assessment nexus; a corporation is also liable for payroll, excise, and provincial allocation obligations as applicable.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. Available elections include: the closely-related corporation election under Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 156 (deemed taxable supply between GST/HST registrants, enabling full input tax credit recovery); alternative GST/HST filing and remittance frequency elections (monthly/quarterly/annual) under ETA sections 227 and 228; and, for non-resident investors deriving Canadian rental or royalty income, the net-basis election under ITA section 216. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available for Canadian resident corporations; the CRA applies OECD-aligned transfer pricing and treaty anti-avoidance rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold, ETA section 148, applicable to the single calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive quarters; proposed increase to CAD 50,000 effective January 1, 2026). Registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded using worldwide qualifying taxable supplies, excluding financial services and certain exempt supplies; zero-rated exports count for threshold measurement only for non-resident suppliers under the July 1, 2021 digital services rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of a virtual concert admission or streaming right (characterized as a service or intangible personal property) qualifies for the 0% rate when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not in Canada at the time of the supply, provided the supply is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada and is not received by an individual present in Canada (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, sections 7 and 10). The metaverse platform must evidence non-Canadian consumption through IP geolocation, billing address, payment instrument, and session delivery logs. Canadian purchasers must be segregated and charged 5% GST or applicable HST.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Not applicable to pure intangible service exports; a B13A Export Declaration is required only if physical media, branded merchandise, or hardware accompanies the virtual concert. For the metaverse scope, provide CRA-compliant delivery evidence: server/CDN edge logs, metaverse session records, and on-chain tokenized ticket issuance metadata.
  • Bill of Lading: Not applicable for digital supply; replace with electronic delivery manifests including platform access logs and smart-contract issuance receipts evidencing transfer of digital admission rights to non-resident wallets.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: For fiat rails, retain SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank remittance confirmations; for USDC crypto rails, provide chain explorer transaction hashes, settlement layer confirmations, and off-ramp fiat statements proving receipt from non-Canadian counterparties.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime exists at the federal level. Electronic records are valid if complete and legible (ETA section 286; CRA Information Circular IC 04-10R). The Pan-Canadian B2B E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol PINT) is voluntary. The CRA may require electronic format on audit; retain all invoice, payment, and settlement data for at least six years.

CRA export audits require contemporaneous proof of non-residency. The normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years from the filing date (extended to eight years for non-resident registrants), and the corporate income tax reassessment period is four years; both are unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or failure to file. Reclassified zero-rated exports attract unremitted GST/HST, daily compound interest at the CRA prescribed quarterly rate, and gross-negligence penalties of 25% of the net tax under ETA section 285.1 and ITA section 163(2). Criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 and related Criminal Code provisions can impose fines of up to 200% of taxes evaded and imprisonment. Fraudulent export claims trigger full retrospective refund clawbacks, cancellation of the exporter's GST/HST registration, and inclusion of the exporter in the CRA high-risk audit registry.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible currency on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no general restrictions on inbound equity investment, capital injections, dividend remittances, interest payments, or liquidation proceeds for non-resident shareholders, provided the payer is not a sanctioned person or entity. Royalty, management-fee, interest and dividend outbound payments are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under Canadian domestic law and may be reduced by an applicable Canada tax treaty; no exchange-control prior approval is required for profit repatriation. However, the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) may review transfer pricing, thin capitalization, and treaty shopping if remittances are disproportionate to economic substance. Virtual-currency ticket receipts in USDC are treated as taxable dispositions when converted to CAD, and fintech settlement providers must satisfy FINTRAC client-identification and virtual-currency requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion timeline, or minimum conversion ratio for export receipts. A Canadian exporter may retain export proceeds in foreign currency bank accounts in Canada or abroad without a Bank of Canada licence. Nevertheless, the payer must report reportable international transactions to the CRA through the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) when requested, and all receipts exceeding the FINTRAC threshold must be reported as large virtual-currency or cash transaction reports where applicable. For this virtual-concert business line, revenue generated through USDC, Stripe, and other digital payment rails is not subject to export-proceeds surrender; the only compliance touchpoints are tax recognition, GST/HST where applicable, and anti-money-laundering reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); FINTRAC is the designated financial intelligence and AML/ATF supervisor. Canada does not maintain a standalone Foreign Exchange Administration, an import/export validation desk, or a general exchange-control licensing authority. Cross-border capital movements are governed by the Currency Act, the Bank Act, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), the United Nations Act and federal sanctions statutes administered by Global Affairs Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High, subject to Canadian licensing conditions. A non-Canadian fintech or electronic-money institution (EMI) cannot hold a Canadian Schedule I banking licence and cannot accept Canadian deposits unless it establishes a federally regulated bank, a provincial credit union, or obtains an authorized foreign bank branch licence under the Bank Act. For non-deposit-taking payment functionality, a non-resident EMI may operate lawfully only if it registers as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoints a Canadian compliance officer, maintains a Canadian agent or physical compliance presence, and implements transaction monitoring and sanctions screening. Cross-border EMI issuing stored-value e-money in Canada, or operating a prepaid-access product, will generally need an MSB registration; issuing securities-backed stablecoins or crypto-backed e-money may trigger securities regulation. Given the virtual-concert commercial model, a Cayman/Delaware EMI is viable only as a payment-processing intermediary paired with a Tier 1 Canadian bank for ultimate CAD settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or loan capital should be wired through the SWIFT network using an MT103 message to the Canadian Tier 1 bank's clearing code (institution number, transit number) and CAD account number. The wire should include a clear narrative in field 70 identifying 'CAPITAL INJECTION', 'SUBSCRIPTION EQUITY', or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN', together with the Canadian corporate entity name and federal incorporation number. Although Canada has no mandatory purpose-code registry for individual wires, the bank will classify the transaction internally under IMF Balance of Payments categories for direct investment/equity capital; the sender must supply a board resolution and a copy of the share registration or loan agreement to the receiving bank. For amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, the Canadian bank must preserve the record for FINTRAC purposes; large non-resident investments must be checked against the Investment Canada Act thresholds, and the beneficiary company must file the applicable notification or net-benefit application. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax rate on taxable income (net of provincial abatement; applicable to federal jurisdiction CA_FED). Combined federal-provincial rates vary by province, generally 23% to 31%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on gross dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder (unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty; e.g., 5% under the Canada-US Treaty for 10% corporate shareholders, 15% generally for portfolio dividends).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents in a taxation year triggers the T106 filing and contemporaneous documentation requirement. Canada has no separate mandatory Master File/Local File statute for mid-size groups, but CRA expects proportionate documentation; for MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 750,000,000, Country-by-Country reporting and, upon request, a Master File and Local File are required. Maintain documentation within three months of CRA request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to offshore jurisdictions; however, the CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the broad business-purpose/economic-substance test under subsection 245(3) of the Income Tax Act, disregarding transactions that lack an economic substance and whose main purpose is tax avoidance. A virtual-concert metaverse entity used solely as a tax-shield with no real activity directors, expenses, or Canadian decisions will be vulnerable to GAAR, treaty-shopping (beneficial ownership), and permanent establishment re-characterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act, s.239)

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest; on conviction for indictable offence: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years (summary offence: imprisonment up to 2 years and reduced fines).

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be prosecuted personally; penalties include full restitution of evaded taxes, interest, and punitive fines; travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years if convicted. CRA may also personally assess directors for unremitted source deductions under s.227.1.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reallocation of income under transfer-pricing rules (s.247); GAAR reassessment on a reasonable basis, with a 50% gross-negligence penalty on tax avoided; interest accrual from the original return due date.

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid taxes under the Income Tax Act, including GAAR reassessments, if they signed false returns or directed the sham structure; personal bankruptcy orders, travel restrictions, and director disqualification may follow.
AML / Money Laundering (Proceeds of Crime and Money Laundering Act)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations (CAD 500,000 for individuals) for non-compliance with AML record-keeping and reporting obligations; criminal prosecution for laundering proceeds: imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be personally subject to administrative monetary penalties if they knowingly authorise or acquiesce in AML failures; they may be named in enforcement actions and face imprisonment for money laundering offences under s.462.31 of the Criminal Code.
Transfer Pricing Penalties / T106 Non-filing

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment, disclosed or not, subject to the threshold of CAD 5,000,000 adjustment or 10% of gross revenue; T106 non-filing penalty of CAD 100/day, up to CAD 25,000 per transaction; CRA may also impose the gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the avoided tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise false transfer-pricing returns can be assessed personally for the penalty and interest; under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine, courts may impose personal liability where the corporation is a mere agent or a sham designed to evade taxes.
