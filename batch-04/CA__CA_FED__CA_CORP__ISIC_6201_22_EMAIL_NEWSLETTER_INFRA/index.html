Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); primary online portal: My Business Account, supported by the Business Registration Online service. Federal corporate incorporation is administered by Corporations Canada under Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, but tax registration and administration are performed by the CRA. Provincial and territorial tax authorities separately administer applicable provincial corporate income taxes, retail sales taxes, and other subnational taxes.

E-government filing portal

A federally incorporated corporation generally receives or registers a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with the CRA and must open the applicable CRA program accounts. The principal accounts are the corporation income tax account (RC), GST/HST account (RT), payroll deductions account (RP), and import-export account (RM), as applicable. Registration is completed through CRA Business Registration Online, My Business Account, an authorized representative, or CRA-supported paper or telephone procedures where online registration is unavailable. A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada, making taxable supplies in Canada, importing commercial goods, or otherwise having a Canadian filing obligation must obtain a BN and the relevant program accounts even if it is not incorporated in Canada. GST/HST registration requires identification of the legal entity, directors or responsible persons, business activities, Canadian business address or non-resident contact information, expected taxable revenues, effective registration date, fiscal period, and authorization information. A non-resident supplier of qualifying digital products or services to Canadian consumers may be required to register under the simplified GST/HST regime when the applicable Canadian revenue threshold is exceeded. Registration does not by itself establish Canadian corporate residence; residence depends principally on incorporation and central management and control, subject to treaty rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally subject to entity-level corporate income taxation and do not have a United States-style check-the-box election. A corporation cannot ordinarily elect to be treated as a fiscally transparent entity for Canadian federal income tax purposes. Relevant statutory elections may include section 85 rollover elections for qualifying transfers of property to a taxable Canadian corporation, section 86 share-reorganization elections, section 51 conversion transactions, section 97(2) partnership contribution elections, capital dividend elections under section 83, and elections concerning foreign affiliate and treaty-related matters where statutory conditions are met. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules under Part VI.1 of the Income Tax Act and related provisions generally deny deductions or include amounts where specified deduction or inclusion mismatches arise; they do not provide a general entity-classification election. A corporation may elect or be required to use particular GST/HST accounting methods, including the quick method where eligibility conditions are satisfied, but that is not an income-tax entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, determined under the applicable small-supplier rules, generally measured over the relevant preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter. The threshold generally applies to a single supplier and associated persons, subject to statutory aggregation rules and special rules for taxi, commercial ridesharing, charities, public institutions, non-residents, and specified digital-economy supplies. The standard federal GST rate is 5%. HST rates vary by place of supply: generally 13% in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia, and 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island. A taxable supplier may voluntarily register below the threshold, subject to the applicable minimum registration period and compliance obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada can generally be zero-rated where the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including export by the supplier or delivery to a specified non-resident recipient for export, with documentary evidence that the goods were removed from Canada within the prescribed period. Services supplied to a non-resident may generally be zero-rated where the service is not excluded by a specific statutory exception and the recipient is outside Canada under the applicable GST/HST rules. Services performed in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, certain personal services performed in Canada, and specified services connected with Canada may be taxable rather than zero-rated. Electronically supplied SaaS, email infrastructure, hosting, and newsletter services require a transaction-specific place-of-supply analysis; a non-resident customer alone does not automatically establish zero-rating. Exporters may claim input tax credits or GST/HST refunds for eligible tax paid on inputs used in making zero-rated supplies, provided registration, documentary, timing, and attribution requirements are met. Evidence should identify the customer, contractual supply, customer location, service-use facts, delivery or export facts, applicable zero-rating provision, and tax charged or not charged.

  • Canada Border Services Agency export declaration or applicable export reporting record, where required
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract or purchase order
  • Packing list and product description, where goods are exported
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier evidence showing export from Canada
  • Proof of delivery or import documentation in the destination jurisdiction, where available
  • Proof of non-resident recipient status and customer location for exported services or digital supplies
  • Foreign-currency settlement evidence, bank records, or payment records where relevant to substantiate the transaction
  • GST/HST invoices, credit notes, general ledger records, and input-tax-credit support
  • Written evidence of the contractual and operational facts supporting the applicable zero-rating provision

Canada has no general federal real-time electronic invoicing, mandatory invoice clearance, or universal digital tax-stamping regime for ordinary GST/HST transactions. Registered persons must issue invoices or other prescribed supporting documents when required, maintain books and records, calculate tax correctly, retain records generally for six years, and provide sufficient information for customers to substantiate input tax credits. CRA electronic filing and electronic payment requirements apply to designated registrants and circumstances, but they do not constitute real-time invoice clearance. CRA's GST/HST electronic filing and My Business Account systems support returns, payments, registrations, and account administration. Certain public-sector procurement arrangements or provincial programs may impose separate electronic invoicing requirements. A SaaS, bulk transactional email, or newsletter platform must analyze place-of-supply rules, customer status, billing address, recipient location, and whether the supply is a telecommunications, digital, hosting, advertising, or other service for GST/HST purposes. The supplier must retain evidence supporting tax treatment and apply provincial HST according to the place-of-supply rules.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement that export receipts be repatriated to Canada. Zero-rating may be denied or clawed back if export, customer-location, place-of-supply, or documentary conditions are not satisfied. The CRA may reassess uncollected GST/HST, deny input tax credits or refunds, assess arrears interest, and impose gross-negligence or other administrative penalties. Failure to register, file, collect, remit, maintain records, or provide required information may attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act. False statements, deliberate tax evasion, fraudulent refund claims, obstruction, or knowingly participating in false invoicing may result in additional penalties and criminal prosecution, including fines and imprisonment. Assessments and reassessments may be subject to extended or unlimited periods where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, willful blindness, or fraud is alleged, subject to the applicable statutory rules and procedural rights. Interest and penalties can materially increase the liability; voluntary disclosure may provide relief only if the CRA's statutory conditions are satisfied and acceptance is obtained.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account under Canada's IMF Article VIII status. There is no exchange control permit regime, no forced conversion, and no profit-remittance licensing requirement. Capital injections, subscription revenue, dividend remittances, royalties, and intercompany loan repayments may flow freely. The only material constraints are fiscal: Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding on outbound dividend/interest/royalty flows (statutory 25%, reducible under applicable tax treaties such as the Canada-US Treaty to typically 5%/15%), CRS self-certification obligations, and Investment Canada Act net-benefit review only where a non-Canadian acquires control of a Canadian business above prescribed WTO thresholds (greenfield treasury injections are not subject to review). No central bank approval is required for electronic wire settlement in CAD or foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts. SaaS subscription proceeds derived from the email newsletter infrastructure business may be held indefinitely in CAD, USD, or any other currency. Indirect compliance duties apply: FINTRAC requires reporting of physical cross-border currency movements exceeding CAD 10,000; Form T1135 requires disclosure of specified foreign property having a cost amount above CAD 100,000; Form T1134 identifies controlled/affiliated foreign entities; and underlying commercial documents must evidence the arm's-length nature of subscription transactions for CRS and transfer-pricing purposes. A formal 'Foreign Receivables and FX Retention Policy' should be adopted to map all proceeds to Stripe settlement reports and customer contracts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and retail payment supervisor; the prudential regulator for banking and FX-related capital rules is the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); the agency responsible for cross-border currency transaction surveillance, AML/CFT supervision, and MSB registration is the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Medium-High with strict conditions. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, or Revolut-style Money Services Business) is legally recognized in Canada not as a bank but as a money services business and cannot accept deposits or provide CDIC-insured accounts. To service CA_CORP, the provider must register as an MSB with FINTRAC, and if it offers retail payment services in Canada it must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. The EMI may serve as an efficient secondary rail for USD/EUR subscription settlement and supplier FX conversion, but it cannot replace the primary Tier 1 Schedule I bank account that receives Stripe billing payouts and funds payroll, GST/HST remittances, and Part XIII withholding obligations. Legal constraints include complete UBO disclosure, no deposit-taking, mandatory Canadian bank partnership for local clearing, sanctions-list alignment, and data-residency/record-keeping obligations under PCMLTFA.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the foreign shareholder instructs its bank to issue a SWIFT MT103 to the CA_CORP account at the Canadian Tier 1 bank; field 70 (Remittance Information) must carry the purpose 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' and field 72 may reference 'PAID-UP CAPITAL INCREASE'; the beneficiary bank's compliance desk verifies the Banking Resolution, corporate register, ISC register, and CRS self-certification. For USD funding, the shareholder must use the bank's USD correspondent clearing instructions (typically a New York correspondent account), specifying field 56A/57A intermediary and beneficiary bank BICs and charge codes OUR/BEN/SHA. CAD conversion at the bank's spot rate is unrestricted and does not require central bank approval. The corporation must record the contribution in the share capital journal with the board resolution and shareholder subscription agreement, and retain all wire records for at least five years under PCMLTFA. Investment Canada Act notification is not required for greenfield capital injection, but if the transaction acquires control of an existing Canadian business, the higher WTO enterprise-value thresholds and net-benefit review would apply.Clearing rails: (1) domestic CAD supplier and operational payments via ACSS EFT and Lynx high-value wires with GPI tracking; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium-High with strict conditions. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, or Revolut-style Money Services Business) is legally recognized in Canada not as a bank but as a money services business and cannot accept deposits or provide CDIC-insured accounts. To service CA_CORP, the provider must register as an MSB with FINTRAC, and if it offers retail payment services in Canada it must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. The EMI may serve as an efficient secondary rail for USD/EUR subscription settlement and supplier FX conversion, but it cannot replace the primary Tier 1 Schedule I bank account that receives Stripe billing payouts and funds payroll, GST/HST remittances, and Part XIII withholding obligations. Legal constraints include complete UBO disclosure, no deposit-taking, mandatory Canadian bank partnership for local clearing, sanctions-list alignment, and data-residency/record-keeping obligations under PCMLTFA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 mandatory where aggregate transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000; Country-by-Country Report required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ CAD 1,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: No separate ESR regime in Canada; CRA applies GAAR (s.245) and substance-over-form doctrine; non-resident corporations must demonstrate real economic activity (employees, assets, decision-making) to avoid permanent establishment exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of tax avoided plus interest; criminal indictment with fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions (s.227.1); civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraudulent evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment plus a penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment; documentation insufficiency can reduce penalty to 5%.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessable for penalties if they participated or acquiesced; personal liability under s.160 for asset transfers intended to defeat CRA.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: Payer liable for unwithheld amount; penalty of 10% of amount not withheld (20% for a third failure in a 5-year period) plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted amounts and penalties under s.227.1; collection actions without limitation.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, recharacterization of dividends as taxable income, disallowance of exemptions, and penalties up to 25% of the tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and advisers liable for culpable negligence; possible travel bans and extradition for cross-border fraud; corporate veil pierced for restitution.
Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code s.462.31 indictable offense with imprisonment up to 10 years; PCMLTFA administrative penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 and suspension of payment provider.

Director personal liability: Directors or officers criminally liable for directing or participating in laundering; personal asset seizure and restitution; unpaid source deductions trigger director liability.
