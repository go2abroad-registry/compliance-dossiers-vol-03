Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online (BRO) via the CRA's My Business Account and Represent a Client portals; export filing interfaced with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) system.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian Limited Partnership is a flow-through for income tax purposes, but it must separately register for GST/HST using its own Business Number (BN) with the 'RM' program account where it makes taxable supplies in Canada and exceeds the small-supplier threshold. Registration is initiated online through Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1. Non-resident partners may require a BN or a Non-Resident Account for withholding obligations; if the partnership qualifies as a non-resident registrant under the Excise Tax Act (ETA), it must appoint a resident Canadian agent for service (ETA s. 242.1) and maintain the agent's contact information on file with the CRA. Electronic registration is typically effective immediately, and the BN is issued on screen; paper filing is available for exceptional cases.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Not applicable in the manner of the US check-the-box election: a Canadian limited partnership cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation at the federal level; it is taxed on a flow-through basis under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96. Available election mechanisms include: (i) designation of a partner as the partnership's reporting 'registrant' for GST/HST purposes; (ii) the GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA s. 156 (limited utility for an LP structure); (iii) partnership profit-allocation elections contained in the limited partnership agreement affecting fiscal-period and share-of-income reporting; and (iv) hybrid-mismatch elections and adjustment rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3, which apply to cross-border structures to neutralize BEPS Action 2 outcomes. For US federal tax purposes only, the foreign LP may also make an entity-classification election on IRS Form 8832, but such election has no effect on Canadian classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD of worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or in the preceding calendar quarter where the person was not already a registrant); a non-resident person is a small supplier for this threshold only where it does not carry on business in Canada, and once business is carried on in Canada the non-resident must register if worldwide taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 threshold. Voluntary registration is permissible once a Canadian taxable presence is established. Designer blind-box toys sold for consumption in Canada attract 5% federal GST in non-HST provinces such as Alberta; where the supply is made in an HST province, the combined rate is 13% or 15%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of designer toy goods physically exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the ETA where the supplier exports the goods or delivers them to a common carrier for shipment outside Canada. The supplier must obtain documentary evidence of export within 30 days of the supply. Services performed on goods purchased for export, freight and transportation services to non-residents, and certain intangible services supplied to non-residents are also potentially zero-rated. The Canadian LP must collate commercial invoices, customs declarations, carrier receipts, and banking evidence into a single per-transaction export file to substantiate every zero-rated sale.

  • CBSA Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) or Electronic Export Report with cargo control number
  • Commercial invoice with HS classification corresponding to ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930 and stated Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill) confirming physical removal of goods from Canada
  • Proof of export dated within 30 days of the supply, retained in the GST/HST audit file for six years
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank foreign-exchange settlement confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the relevant currency

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous-transmission e-invoicing, or digital tax stamping regime exists in Canada. CRA supports voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing interoperability (PEPPOL BIS 3.0); invoices must nevertheless contain the prescribed supplier, recipient, description, and tax information required under ETA s. 235.1 and related regulations. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by non-resident registrants and by most resident registrants above prescribed revenue thresholds. Export zero-rating claims require documentary proof of physical removal from Canada, with all supporting books and records retained for six years. Export declarations for non-restricted goods must be transmitted electronically to CBSA through CAED.

CRA will deny zero-rating and assess unremitted GST/HST, interest, and penalties where export documentation is inadequate or goods are diverted into the Canadian market. Civil penalties apply under ETA s. 280 for failure to file (greater of CAD $250 and 25% of net tax) and under ETA s. 285 for false statements (greater of CAD $250 and 25% of the tax at risk); gross negligence at the income-tax level invokes ITA s. 163.2, generally 50% of the understated tax plus 50% of the false refund or credit. Criminal tax evasion proceedings may be commenced under the ETA and ITA s. 239, with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years. The normal four-year federal reassessment window extends to seven years in cases of carelessness and is unlimited for fraudulent filings, enabling retrospective clawback of zero-rating benefits and input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII for both current and capital transactions. Non-resident partners may repatriate dividends, redemption proceeds, partnership distributions and sale proceeds without Bank of Canada approval or exchange-control license. The only compliance gates are Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (e.g., dividends, interest, royalty and distribution rates under applicable tax treaties), Transfer Pricing rules, and the Investment Canada Act review for acquisitions of control of a Canadian business by non-Canadians; none of these imposes FX convertibility or remittance limits. For passive investment into a Canadian LP, no Investment Canada Act filing is required except where control of an existing Canadian business is acquired or where an above-threshold new business is established in a prescribed cultural or strategic sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or e-commerce sales receipts in Canada. Exporters may keep receivables in CAD, USD or any other currency in domestic or offshore accounts; all amounts must be recognized for income tax on an accrual basis and reported on the Canadian income tax return of the LP (to CRA via T5013/RL-15 slips, as applicable). Balance-of-payments reporting, where requested by Statistics Canada, is statistical only and does not restrict disposition of foreign currency. Bank supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act requires Canadian banks to report all cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, but this is a reporting obligation and not a surrender or conversion control.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; exchange control administration is not centralized. The Minister of Finance, the Canada Border Services Agency, the Investment Review Division and FINTRAC share relevant mandates for currency flows, trade reporting and AML/CFT. Canada does not maintain a separate foreign exchange administration body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary operational rail; Low as a primary treasury concentration account. Foreign fintech companies and offshore EMI/neobank providers (e.g., UK e-money institutions, Singapore EMIs, or Hong Kong licensed money service operators) cannot accept deposits in Canada without being authorized as banks or recognized as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Funds held with such EMIs are not protected by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation. Digital EMIs are useful for low-value cross-border vendor payments and multi-currency collections, but the Canadian LP should maintain a Schedule I bank account as the principal capital escrow and clearing hub.
