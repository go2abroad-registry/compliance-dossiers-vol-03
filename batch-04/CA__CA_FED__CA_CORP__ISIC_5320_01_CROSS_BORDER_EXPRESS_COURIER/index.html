Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) – My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online (BRO)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation must obtain a Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST account by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the Canada Revenue Agency, either by paper or online through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The application must include the corporation's legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered office or agent in Canada, particulars of directors, and a description of business activities. Where the non-resident does not have a permanent establishment in Canada, a Canadian agent or representative may be required. The GST/HST account is established upon registration; the effective date is the earlier of the date of first taxable supply in Canada or the date registration is requested.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a 'check-the-box' regime; corporate law personality determines tax status. A federally incorporated corporation is a resident of Canada for tax purposes. Elections available include the section 216 election regarding rental income from real property or timber royalties for non-residents, the family farm rollover election, and the elective registration of a person as a GST/HST registrant under subsection 240(1) of the Excise Tax Act even when not required to register. No structural entity classification elections comparable to U.S. Form 8832 are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; however, a non-resident corporation that is carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies in Canada must register even if below the small-supplier threshold in circumstances where the CRA deems registration appropriate. Voluntary registration is permitted under the Excise Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods that are exported from Canada, provided the supplier has satisfactory documentary evidence of export in its books and records. For cross-border express courier services, the supply of a transportation service to a non-resident for shipping goods to or from Canada may be zero-rated when the destination is outside Canada and the goods are not subsequently imported into Canada. A supply of service that is in respect of movable property delivered outside Canada is also zero-rated. The supplier must retain documentary evidence to show the goods were delivered to a destination outside Canada, which may include a bill of lading, customs export declaration, or written confirmation from the carrier.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or financial institution proof of foreign currency payment remitted to Canada)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List

Canada does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control mechanism. Electronic invoicing is permitted but not compulsory. Registered persons with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 10,000,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI. Taxpayers with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1,000,000 must remit amounts by electronic payment. No government real-time stamping or API-based e-invoice clearance is currently in force.

The Canada Revenue Agency closely scrutinizes zero-rated export claims. If the supplier fails to produce the required export documentation, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 5% GST/HST and the amount is assessed as unpaid tax, plus arrearage interest and penalties. False statements or omissions in documentation can attract a penalty of CAD 250 plus 5% of the amount of tax assessed under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act. Gross negligence attributable to willful disregard may result in a penalty of 50% of the tax understated, an extended reassessment period of four years (one year for ordinary reassessments), and criminal prosecution in egregious fraud cases. Retroactive clawbacks may apply to the full unpaid GST/HST for the entire audit period where documentation is missing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized capital and current account regime under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible, with no restrictions on the repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or branch profits for non-resident investors. The Income Tax Act imposes non-resident withholding tax at statutory rates (typically 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty, e.g., Canada-U.S. Treaty Article X for dividends), but the withholding tax is a fiscal measure, not a currency control barrier. Profit remittance is therefore unfettered from an FX control perspective, subject to Form T106 reporting for transactions with non-residents and to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) reporting for large virtual currency or wire transfers. There are no surrender requirements, no central bank approval gates for current account payments, and no capital account authorization requirements beyond standard economic sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada. Export receipts may remain in foreign currency; however, eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations must compute foreign exchange gains and losses on an accrual or realization basis consistently. For courier operations that carry goods on behalf of clients, the courier itself must distinguish service export revenue (zero-rated for GST/HST under s. 7 of Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act) from deemed goods exports to avoid misapplication of surrender-like reporting. All payments received from non-residents are subject to FINTRAC reporting if the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000, and any sanctions-related screening must be maintained.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI banking platforms (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Interpolitan, or Payoneer) are operationally viable for disbursements and collections in Canada only when they are authorized under Canadian payment service provider law or registered as MSBs with FINTRAC. They cannot replace a tier-1 Canadian Schedule I bank for corporate escrow, stamping, and GST/HST registration requirements. An offshore EMI that is not registered in Canada as an MSB and has no physical presence may lawfully provide payment services to a Canadian resident business on a cross-border basis, but Canadian banks may refuse to settle with the EMI, and the CRA may treat undisclosed foreign accounts or property as reportable under the Foreign Income Verification Statement (T1135). Feasibility rating for principal treasury functions: Low-to-Medium; viable only for low-value operational payouts and FX conversion, provided the EMI maintains FINTRAC MSB registration or operates as an authorized foreign money services business under provincial rules.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident director or shareholder structure with no Canadian revenue history

Root cause: Non-resident director or shareholder structure with no Canadian revenue history

Defense evidence: Certified certificate of corporate existence; constitutional documents (articles of incorporation, by-laws); register of directors and UBOs; proof of commercial substance (Canadian office lease, operating agreement, courier license under provincial/territorial motor carrier legislation); bank references from home jurisdiction; three years of audited financial statements or, for a start-up, a detailed cash-flow projection and verifiable source of initial capital; notarized identification of every director; and a completed FINTRAC-compliant corporate KYC form.
Cross-border express courier business touching international trade and delivery of goods causing Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML) red flags

Root cause: Cross-border express courier business touching international trade and delivery of goods causing Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML) red flags

Defense evidence: Contractual framework with shippers/customers (master service agreements, airway bills, customs brokerage authority); accounting of revenue vs disbursements per shipment; end-to-end transaction trail from POS/ACH origin to beneficiary bank; evidence of physical handling or subcontracting (warehouse lease, tracking data); AML/CFT risk assessment; sanctions screening logs; beneficial ownership documentation; and a written TBML defense policy explaining the economics of express courier margins and operational correlation with shipping volume.
High transaction velocity and flow-through payments to foreign carriers and customs brokers

Root cause: High transaction velocity and flow-through payments to foreign carriers and customs brokers

Defense evidence: A documented operating cash-flow model showing that funds received from customers are not laundered but are committed to trade payables to airlines, ground carriers, customs brokers, and government duties; copies of signed carrier agreements and established settlement terms; the client's internal controls for matching invoices to waybills; and month-end reconciliation statements. Provide the bank with Article 13 of the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty (if applicable) and evidence that the entity is a bona fide courier under ISIC 5320 rather than a shell invoicing entity.
Use of offshore digital EMIs and unusual FX conversion patterns

Root cause: Use of offshore digital EMIs and unusual FX conversion patterns

Defense evidence: FINTRAC MSB registration (if applicable) or a complete transactional rationalization with a Schedule I bank as the primary settlement account; documented FX hedging policy; historical conversion reports; and a letter from a licensed Canadian cross-border payment processor confirming commercial purpose. Maintain T1135 reporting if any foreign account balance exceeds CAD 100,000 in the year.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: use a Canadian Schedule I bank as the receiving institution. For each inbound wire from a non-resident shareholder, the bank requires the originator to provide (1) the precise legal name and address of the shareholder, (2) the SWIFT BIC and account number of the originating bank, (3) the beneficiary account number of the Canadian corporation, and (4) a narrative stating 'shareholder capital contribution — CBCA incorporation — non-operating funds'. Correspondent banks must not route funds through OFAC or UN-sanctioned jurisdictions. The receiving bank must complete FINTRAC International Electronic Funds Transfer (IEFT) reporting for any inbound transfer >CAD 10,000. Verification: submit the executed subscription agreement, the resolution of the board of directors accepting the capital contribution, and the CBCA annual return records to the bank's AML unit. The funds must be booked in the corporate ledger as contributed surplus or share capital to align with the CRA's paid-up capital (PUC) definitions.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for direct payments to airlines, foreign postal administrations, and customs brokers; (2) SWIFT MT202/MT202COV for bank-to-bank transfers in cover of a customer transfer; (3) Commercial LC (UCP600/ISP98) for high-value commitments with foreign consolidators or franchise partners; (4) Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for domestic CAD disbursements to Canadian carriers; and (5) card rails (Visa/Mastercard) for POS transactions relating to cash-on-delivery or customer billing.
