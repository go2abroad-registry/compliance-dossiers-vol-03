Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); portal: CRA My Business Account via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html; incorporation registry: Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada) via https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Canadian cross-dock/3PL fulfilment operation must: (1) incorporate a federal business corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or register extra-provincially with the relevant provincial registry; satisfy CBCA director-residency thresholds and maintain a registered office/address for service in Canada; (2) apply to the CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts or the CRA Business Registration Online service; (3) open program accounts on the BN in the same application, selecting RC0001 (GST/HST), RC0002 (Payroll) if the corporation employs staff, and RP0001 (Corporation Income Tax); the GST/HST registration number is the BN plus the RC0001 suffix; (4) receive the BN confirmation letter, registration effective date, and NETFILE access codes before issuing tax invoices or filing T2 corporate returns; (5) if the non-resident parent has no Canadian permanent establishment, separately document treaty/branch filing positions and consider the section 216 election for Canadian rental/property income; and (6) use the same BN to open the CBSA importer/exporter account through the Customs Self-Assessment and Single Window interface for export reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a Canadian Business Corporation is mandatorily subject to entity-level taxation under Part I of the Income Tax Act. The relevant election mechanisms for a foreign-owned Canadian fulfilment/cross-dock corporation are: (1) Income Tax Act section 216 election allowing a non-resident corporation receiving Canadian rental/fee income to file a Part I net-basis return instead of remitting gross Part XIII withholding; (2) section 85(1) rollover election permitting tax-deferred transfer of assets into the Canadian corporation; (3) Excise Tax Act section 156 election for closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration; (4) Excise Tax Act section 167 election on the sale of a business applying GST/HST relief where the purchaser acquires all or substantially all of the assets; and (5) voluntary GST/HST registration under the small-supplier rules to claim input tax credits before the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded. Hybrid mismatch rules in Income Tax Act sections 18.2 to 18.4 and the foreign affiliate rules constrain elections involving hybrid instruments; no election is available to override a hybrid mismatch adjustment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - a person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when taxable supplies (including zero-rated) made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters under the Excise Tax Act small-supplier rules; the threshold applies to residents and non-residents alike, and non-resident e-commerce sellers, online marketplace facilitators, and foreign operators using Canadian fulfilment centres are separately required to register for GST/HST without any threshold when selling taxable digital services or goods delivered in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a sale of goods is zero-rated under Excise Tax Act sections 146-147 only where (i) the goods are exported by or on behalf of the supplier, (ii) title passes to a non-resident buyer, (iii) the goods are delivered outside Canada by common carrier, courier, mail, or by the supplier taking them out of Canada, and (iv) the supplier retains contemporaneous documentary proof of export; goods diverted to Canadian consumption are fully taxable at 5% GST/HST. Services: Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act zero-rates a supply of a service made to a non-resident unless the service is rendered to an individual in Canada, relates to real property situated in Canada, or relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; therefore, a Canadian cross-dock/3PL operator's storage, handling, order-fulfilment, and kitting services performed on goods physically located in a Canadian warehouse are ordinarily taxable at 5% even if the customer is a non-resident, whereas international freight transportation services, freight-forwarding services integral to export, and services performed after the goods leave Canada may be zero-rated. To claim zero-rating on any export-related service, the operator must obtain the non-resident recipient's written status declaration, invoice zero-rated transport elements separately from taxable Canadian storage/handling elements, and maintain evidence that the underlying goods left Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / G7 or CAED EDI transmission to the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Commercial Invoice / Canada Customs Invoice showing the foreign buyer, Incoterms, export value, and Canadian exporter of record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of the goods from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT or FX settlement record proving the foreign-currency payment was received and repatriated to the Canadian entity
  • Carrier eManifest and cargo control records (ACI for air/marine, PARS/PAPS for highway) corroborating departure from the Canadian warehouse and border crossing
  • Warehouse inventory and cross-dock movement logs linking lot-level inventory to the specific export declaration

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government API clearance regime for domestic B2B e-invoicing. GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE; large registrants above prescribed revenue thresholds must remit and file certain information returns electronically. E-invoicing is voluntary, with the CRA implementing a Pan-Canadian e-invoice standard based on Peppol PINT and piloting from 2025. Export clearance is electronic through the CBSA Single Window Initiative and eManifest: exporters must transmit export data via the Canadian Export Reporting System (G7/CAED/EDI) before departure, and all books and records substantiating zero-rated exports must be retained for at least six years and produced in English or French on CRA demand.

CRA auditors apply a strict proof-of-export standard to zero-rating and GST/HST refund claims; any missing or inconsistent third-party export documentation triggers reassessment of the supply at 5% GST/HST, denial of input tax credits, interest on arrears, and clawback of refunds already paid. The normal four-year reassessment period is suspended or indefinitely extended in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Civil penalties include: (1) gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of the net tax, refund, or credit misstated; (2) false-statement penalties under the Excise Tax Act (minimum amounts plus 50% of the amount claimed in deliberate cases); and (3) fines and forfeiture under the Customs Act for incorrect export declarations. Criminal tax evasion is punishable by fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to five years per count, and directors of the Canadian corporation may be assessed personally for unremitted GST/HST under Excise Tax Act section 323 and unremitted source deductions under Income Tax Act section 227.1.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully floating exchange rate regime and maintains no exchange control legislation; the Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible on both current account and capital account. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, intercompany payments, and capital repatriation are unrestricted and require no central bank approval, subject only to: (i) Canadian withholding tax on certain payments to non-residents under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (ITA); (ii) transfer-pricing documentation under Section 247 of the ITA; and (iii) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) obligations for cross-border wires of CAD 10,000 or more. Non-resident capital injections attract Investment Canada Act reporting and, above the prescribed financial thresholds, pre-implementation review; below those thresholds, only a 30-day post-implementation report is required. Foreign-currency bank accounts at Tier-1 Canadian banks are permitted without restriction and may be designated as Canadian-dollar or United-States-dollar accounts under the Currency Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio exists under Canadian law. Exporters and cross-border e-commerce fulfillment operators may retain 100% of foreign-currency export receipts in domestic or offshore accounts and are not compelled to sell any percentage into CAD. Regulatory obligations are limited to: (i) accurate CAD-denominated recognition of foreign exchange gains or losses for income tax purposes under the ITA; and (ii) FINTRAC EFTR filings for each cross-border wire transfer at or above CAD 10,000. Inward remittances from e-commerce platform sales and overseas warehouse settlement flows are unrestricted and require only the underlying commercial documentation and a compliant payment purpose description.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential bank regulator, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as anti-money laundering supervisor, the Department of Finance Canada as currency policy coordinator, and Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada as administrator of the Investment Canada Act for foreign investment review.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High as a settlement overlay for cross-border e-commerce collections, overseas seller payouts, and multi-currency virtual account management; Medium as a replacement for a Tier-1 chartered bank operating account. Canadian access to Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer, Stripe, and Nuvei is legally valid only where the provider is registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) or Foreign Money Services Business (FMSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Unregistered offshore EMIs may not lawfully solicit or transact business with Canadian resident entities, and their use would create an enhanced-due-diligence trigger point at the primary bank. Defense evidence: Up-to-date federal or provincial corporate registry extracts; full UBO chain chart extending to all natural persons; beneficial ownership declaration signed by each attributable owner; shareholder registry and share transfer ledgers; notarized and apostilled board resolutions confirming the capital escrow and operational mandate; independent legal opinion confirming the entity layer rationale.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the foreign shareholder capital injection through SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) from the home jurisdiction into the Canadian entity's CAD or USD operational or capital escrow account held at a Tier-1 chartered bank; inbound international wires settle through the receiving bank's correspondent network into Canada's Lynx large-value payment system, with finality in central-bank settlement. The payment narrative must be explicit, for example CAPEX INJECTION and PAID-UP CAPITAL or SHAREHOLDER LOAN, and must match the underlying corporate documentation to avoid a suspicious transaction report. The receiving financial institution will file a FINTRAC EFTR where the wire equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. Where the aggregate investment exceeds the Investment Canada Act financial thresholds, file a pre-implementation notification; otherwise file the post-implementation report within 30 days. For any shareholder loan, document an arm's-length interest rate and principal-repayment schedule so that interest paid to the non-resident lender is treaty-exposed for Part XIII withholding tax; for bona fide equity injections, issue share certificates and file the required corporate registry updates to evidence the paid-up capital.Disburse trade settlements through four complementary rails: (i) Payments Canada Local ACH for domestic CAD payables such as warehouse rent, Canadian carriers, customs brokerage, and payroll; (ii) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for international supplier, overseas warehouse, and cross-border logistics disbursements; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600 for large or high-risk procurement shipments from overseas vendors, providing documentary compliance and payment security; and (iv) registered fintech EMI virtual accounts (Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer, or Stripe) for high-frequency e-commerce platform payout reconciliation. For USD-denominated outbound settlement into the United States, use Fedwire or CHIPS through the Canadian bank's US-dollar clearing account. Every outbound wire must carry a purpose code and narrative consistent with ISIC 5210 cross-dock operations, for example FREIGHT AND WAREHOUSING, CUSTOMS BROKERAGE, or ORDER FULFILLMENT SERVICES, and must be verified against the UBO and counterparty whitelist to prevent unauthorized third-party payments.Deliverable plain-vanilla foreign exchange forwards in CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP, and CAD/CNH under an IFX Master Agreement or ISDA FX/EMEA Master Agreement with the relationship Tier-1 bank; no mandatory central clearing applies where the instruments are bilateral, non-cleared, and settled by physical delivery.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for lightly traded or non-convertible trade-route currencies relevant to overseas sourcing and fulfillment, including CNY, VND, PHP, INR, and MXN, where a corresponding offshore deliverable market is unavailable; permitted for end-users with genuine commercial FX exposure.Cross-currency swaps (fixed-floating or floating-floating) to hedge long-term intercompany funding and shareholder loan principal and interest exposures across CAD and USD or other functional currencies.Vanilla foreign currency options and zero-cost collars (risk reversals) purchased from a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank to cap adverse exchange-rate movement while preserving upside on e-commerce settlement cycles.Window forwards and flex-date forwards allowing drawdown within a defined settlement window, matched to the weekly or bi-weekly payout cycle of cross-border e-commerce platforms and overseas warehouse operating expenses.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High as a settlement overlay for cross-border e-commerce collections, overseas seller payouts, and multi-currency virtual account management; Medium as a replacement for a Tier-1 chartered bank operating account. Canadian access to Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer, Stripe, and Nuvei is legally valid only where the provider is registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) or Foreign Money Services Business (FMSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Unregistered offshore EMIs may not lawfully solicit or transact business with Canadian resident entities, and their use would create an enhanced-due-diligence trigger point at the primary bank. A hybrid treasury structure is recommended: Tier-1 chartered bank for CAD operating liquidity, statutory capital escrow, and FX hedging; a registered fintech EMI for platform collections and high-volume cross-border disbursement rails.
