Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — CRA My Business Account, Business Registration Online (BRO), GST/HST NETFILE; Corporations Canada for federal incorporation.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian federal Business Corporation obtains its Business Number (BN) automatically upon federal incorporation and filing of the initial corporate income tax return; the CRA simultaneously issues the corporate tax program account (RC). Where a non-resident entity has not incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act, tax registration is effected by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or through Business Registration Online. The registrant must then open separate program accounts for GST/HST (RT), payroll deductions (RP), and corporate income tax (RC) as applicable. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are required to obtain a BN and may be required to designate a Canadian resident representative for CRA correspondence and statutory notices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. I-5). Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed on Canadian-source business income and certain Canadian-source passive income, subject to Part I tax, Part XIII withholding tax, and the Part I branch tax applicable to non-resident corporations operating in Canada. — A Canadian federal Business Corporation obtains its Business Number (BN) automatically upon federal incorporation and filing of the initial corporate income tax return; the CRA simultaneously issues the corporate tax program account (RC). Where a non-resident entity has not incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act, tax registration is effected by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or through Business Registration Online. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. I-5). Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed on Canadian-source business income and certain Canadian-source passive income, subject to Part I tax, Part XIII withholding tax, and the Part I branch tax applicable to non-resident corporations operating in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or disregarded-entity election comparable to the United States regime; entity classification is determined by CRA administrative practice, corporate law, and the Income Tax Act. Available statutory elections relevant to cross-border operations include the ITA section 216 election, under which a non-resident may elect to file a Canadian return and pay Part I tax on net Canadian rental income instead of Part XIII gross-basis withholding, and certain treaty-based elections to reduce branch tax. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Canadian anti-hybrid and anti-avoidance rules apply under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies but excluding exempt supplies) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for the small supplier threshold; non-residents with a permanent establishment or deemed carrying-on-business activity in Canada may be required to register even below this threshold. Voluntary registration is available for persons making taxable supplies in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, supplies of goods exported by the supplier are zero-rated where the goods are delivered outside Canada within the prescribed statutory period and the supplier retains proof of export. Supplies of services to non-resident persons are zero-rated under section 23 of Part V unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada, relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is supplied to an individual while the individual is physically in Canada. For a Global EOR / cross-border contractor payroll provider, zero-rating is available where the recipient is a non-resident and the employer-of-record, payroll administration, compliance, and workforce management services are not connected to Canadian real property or tangible personal property physically situated in Canada and are not direct services rendered to an individual present in Canada. The supplier must maintain contemporaneous documentation establishing the non-resident status of the recipient, the place of supply, and the nature of the services.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export report / cargo declaration, including Form B13A where applicable
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export of goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or wire/ACH bank records establishing receipt of foreign-source funds from the non-resident recipient
  • Signed service agreement or purchase order with the non-resident client
  • Non-resident status evidence, such as a foreign tax residency certificate, foreign business registration document, or sworn declaration
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supply as zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act

Canada has no federal real-time fiscal invoice clearance, API stamping, or mandatory digital seal regime for GST/HST invoices. A GST/HST registrant must issue a valid tax invoice containing prescribed information, including supplier name, CRA Business Number and GST/HST account number, invoice date, description of supply, tax status (taxable, zero-rated, exempt), and consideration paid. Electronic invoicing is permitted, including B2B EDI, XML, or PEPPOL formats; however, CRA does not pre-clear invoices. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million, and T4/T4A payroll information returns are subject to mandatory electronic filing. Quebec maintains a separate provincial electronic invoicing and centralized registration environment that is distinct from the federal GST/HST system.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims and overstated input tax credit refunds are subject to CRA reassessment, cancellation of refunds, denial of input tax credits, and retroactive clawback of previously allowed non-resident refunds. Statutory penalties apply for false statements and omissions in GST/HST filings, including the greater of a fixed amount and a percentage of understated net tax, and gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the amount assessed; interest is compounded daily. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act may result in fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment, and directors or officers may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid GST/HST amounts where they authorized or acquiesced in the false claim.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with unrestricted convertibility for both current account transactions and capital movements. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without prior approval or surrender obligations. No mandatory surrender of export proceeds is required; foreign currency earnings may be retained in offshore accounts or converted at market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds offshore or convert to CAD at prevailing market rates. All foreign exchange transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC. Certain goods exports may require permits under the Export and Import Permit Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
