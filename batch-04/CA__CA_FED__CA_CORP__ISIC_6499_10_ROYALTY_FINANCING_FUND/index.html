Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — International and Large Business Directorate; federal corporate registry: Corporations Canada via the Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada Online Filing Centre; taxpayer portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html).

E-government filing portal

1) A newly incorporated federal Business Corporation receives its statutory Business Number (BN) automatically from CRA through the Corporations Canada online filing process. 2) An existing foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada must apply for a BN by submitting Form RC1 (domestic) or Form RC59 (non-resident) to the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre, or by telephoning the Business Registration service at 1-800-959-5525. 3) Program account suffixes are activated as needed: RC (Part I corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll). 4) A non-resident financing vehicle with no physical presence must authorize a fiscal representative or agent using Form RC4593. 5) Registration triggers a T2 Corporation Income Tax Return filing obligation within six months after fiscal year-end, and GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable Canadian-source supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian-resident Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) subject to worldwide taxation on its income; headline federal general corporate rate is 15% (38% statutory rate less 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction); combined federal-provincial rates typically range from 23% (Alberta) to 27% (British Columbia), with Ontario at 26.5%. No check-the-box pass-through treatment is available to a Canadian domestic corporation; CRA classifies entities by legal form and commercial substance, applying ITA s. 94.9 to foreign hybrid entities, while the foreign affiliate exempt-surplus regime (ITA ss. 113(1)(a) and 5907) provides the only 'territorial' carve-out for active foreign business earnings.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election for domestic corporations, but the following statutory elections are relevant to the ISIC_6499 royalty financing fund: (1) capital dividend election under ITA s. 83(2) using Form T2054 to distribute the Capital Dividend Account tax-free; (2) associated-corporation election/allocation under ITA s. 256 for small business deduction purposes (T2 Schedule 46) if the fund is a Canadian-controlled private corporation; (3) branch tax election under ITA s. 219 and the s. 216 net rental income election if the fund holds Canadian real-property collateral or a permanent establishment; (4) fiscally-transparent-entity election under the Canada-US Tax Convention Article IV(7), allowing a US LLC member to be recognized as a resident of the treaty jurisdiction through US Form 8832 check-the-box filing plus a CRA non-resident treaty submission; (5) hybrid mismatch positioning is constrained by ITA s. 94.9 and the draft hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.2 and 18.3), which deny deduction of double-dip financing payments — no elective bifurcation is permitted; (6) GST/HST elections: closely related corporation election (Form RC4017) and divisional election (Form RC4027) eliminate GST on intra-group supplies, and listed financial institutions may elect the s. 225.2 simplified net-tax method.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the ETA s. 123(1) small-supplier threshold measured by worldwide taxable supplies from Canada in the preceding four calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies and returns must be filed electronically through My Business Account. Non-residents that make 'specified supplies' (e.g., software, intangibles used in Canada, telecommunication services) are deemed under ETA s. 143(1.1) to carry on business in Canada and must register upon reaching the same threshold; the fund's pure financial services to non-resident digital-brand borrowers are not specified supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (a) tangible goods exported by the supplier as soon as reasonably possible, and generally within 60 days of delivery under ETA s. 138 (Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1); (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not a GST/HST registrant carrying on business in Canada, where the service is not performed wholly or substantially in Canada for Canadian consumption (Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7); (c) transfers of intangible personal property, licences, and platform IP to non-residents (Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 10 and 10.1); and (d) management, administrative, and advisory services rendered to non-resident digital-brand borrowers — a 0% rate that permits full input tax credit recovery on inputs attributable to those supplies. The fund's core financing return (interest, revenue-share, royalty) is exempt rather than zero-rated, so ITC apportionment under ETA s. 141.01 must segregate zero-rated management fee revenue from exempt finance revenue to maximize recoverability.

  • Commercial Invoice and packing list identifying the non-resident purchaser and the 0% consideration

Canada imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, digital stamp, or blockchain-based e-invoicing mandate as of 2025. ETA s. 286 requires retention of books and records for six years, and invoices must be produced in a readable electronic format on CRA demand. Canada is building the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing interoperability standard (Peppol BIS 3.0 / UBL 2.1) on a voluntary pilot basis; mandatory nationwide e-invoicing is not expected before 2027. The fund's sponsor bank must report cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more under FINTRAC rules, and CRA cross-references these records in export-proceeds and GST audit files.

Canada has no statutory 180-day FX repatriation ceiling; the 180-day benchmark is the CRA documentary standard for export-proceeds evidence and audit-file sufficiency. A false zero-rating claim triggers ETA s. 280 (false statement / gross negligence) penalties of CAD 250 or 25% of the overstated net tax, whichever is greater, plus daily arrears interest at CRA prescribed rates (currently 9% per annum). Criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 exposes the fund to fines of up to 250% of the net tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. On the income tax side, ITA s. 163(2) imposes a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax, and ITA s. 152(4)(c) removes the reassessment limitation period for fraud or misrepresentation, permitting retrospective clawback of every input tax credit and export rebate claimed within the six-year record-keeping window or longer. Failure to document repatriated foreign-exchange proceeds within the 180-day benchmark reverses zero-rating, recovers previously allowed ITCs, and compounds interest from the original filing date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. The Canadian dollar is a freely floating fiat currency with no exchange control legislation (no Foreign Exchange Control Act). Profit remittance, dividends, and royalty payments may flow freely without prior approval, subject only to reporting thresholds under the Income Tax Act and anti-money laundering rules. No capital controls or restrictions on repatriation exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters are free to hold, convert, or repatriate foreign currency earnings without any surrender obligation. Certain reporting under the Customs Act and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) may apply for large cross-border movements of currency or monetary instruments (threshold CAD 10,000).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Limited-Medium. Canada does not have a dedicated EMI licence; fintechs operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). As of 2024, the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) imposes a new supervisory framework for payment service providers. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) can offer CAD-denominated accounts to Canadian corporations, but they do not provide the legal status of a Canadian bank account. Key constraints: (1) such EMIs cannot lend or provide credit; (2) balances are not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC); (3) obligation to comply with Canadian MSB registration and FINTRAC reporting; and (4) the provision of FX services by non-resident EMIs requires care to avoid triggering unauthorized currency exchange business restrictions under provincial laws.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire for USD from the US) into the designated Capital Escrow Account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank. The wire must reference the correct Transaction Purpose Code (e.g., 'CF' for conversion of branch/head office funds, or '71002' for direct investment in non-resident enterprises), but Canada does not mandate a numeric code for bank supervisory purposes; the beneficiary account name should match exactly the corporate legal name and the payment memo must cite the funding/capital injection resolution and the appropriate securities deminimus exemption (if applicable). For funds exceeding CAD 10,000, the sending institution must report to FINTRAC, and the receiving Canadian bank may issue a Large Cash/Incoming Wire information request (FINTRAC reports are mandatory).
