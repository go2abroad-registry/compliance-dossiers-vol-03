Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online (BRO); CRA My Business Account (MyBA); GST/HST NETFILE portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated federal or provincial business corporation, or a foreign corporation entering the Canadian market, must apply for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or paper Form RC1, providing the certificate of incorporation, registered office address, directors and business activities. The CRA opens the corporate income tax account (RC) automatically and issues a GST/HST account (RT) when the registrant exceeds the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or applies voluntarily; the BN/RT is the statutory Canadian TIN and VAT identifier. A non-resident registrant without a Canadian establishment may be required, under section 241 of the Excise Tax Act, to appoint a Canadian agent/resident representative or provide security to ensure GST/HST compliance and service of CRA documents. The BN/RT must be quoted on all invoices, customs declarations, platform merchant agreements and CRA filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists in Canadian federal law; a Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer and a 'person' for Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act purposes, taxed at entity level with no flow-through to shareholders. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) ITA s. 216(1) election for non-resident recipients of Canadian rental/period income to file a Canadian return and pay graduated rates instead of flat 25% Part XIII withholding; (ii) ETA s. 156 election to treat taxable supplies between a Canadian corporation and its closely related non-resident parent or affiliate as non-supplies for GST/HST; (iii) GST/HST simplified-accounting, annual-filing, and combined federal-provincial account elections for eligible registrants; (iv) waivers under ITA s. 152(4) to extend the reassessment period where audit finality is needed; and (v) automatic, non-elective hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2-18.8, which disallow deductions for hybrid mismatch payments lacking corresponding inclusions and offer no elective carve-out.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII status (effective March 25, 1952) and maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating, fully deliverable currency with no surrender, conversion, or repatriation approvals. Profit remittance hurdles are purely tax-driven, not FX-driven: non-resident dividends and royalties are generally subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 5-15% under the Canada-US, Canada-UK, and Canada-EU member treaties), and cross-border licensing payments must satisfy CRA transfer-pricing (s.247) and anti-hybrid (s.18.4? Actually s. 18.4 is hybrid mismatch rules) documentation. No prior Central Bank approval is required for cross-border payments, but international wire transfers are queued by Canadian banks' AML compliance and FINTRAC reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or services receipts. Exporters/distributors of digital content may retain USD/EUR/other foreign currency indefinitely in Canadian or offshore accounts. Reporting obligations instead apply: (i) Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting — direct investment/receipts surveys for transactions above CAD 10,000 thresholds; (ii) FINTRAC cross-border currency and monetary instrument reports for movements over CAD 10,000; and (iii) GST/HST zero-rating documentation for exported digital services (HS 88? Actually HST exports zero-rated under s.1 Part V Schedule VI) must be maintained to support zero-rated treatment.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary and financial system authority; prudential regulation of banks and authorized foreign bank branches is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange administration (no equivalent of a SAFE-style control bureau); currency administration is limited to the Currency Act (legal tender), the Bank Act (bank powers), and Statistics Canada/Bank of Canada balance-of-payments reporting.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate (38% basic rate less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction); provinces levy additional allocable rates of 10%–16%, resulting in combined rates of approximately 25%–31%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or deemed paid to non-residents, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty (commonly 5% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% of voting shares, otherwise 15%; additional reductions under comprehensive DTAs).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed where the aggregate value of reportable cross-border transactions with a non-resident exceeds CAD 25,000. Contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation (Local File/Master File and a Transfer Pricing Memorandum) is expected where aggregated relevant transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000, or where the taxpayer could otherwise reasonably be expected to be subject to CRA transfer-pricing review. Failure to prepare contemporaneous documentation shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer and exposes the corporation to the s.247(3) penalty.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation regime (unlike offshore financial centres); instead, economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), through s.247(2) which permits the CRA to recharacterise cross-border transactions lacking substance, and through treaty Limitation on Benefits / beneficial-ownership clauses. A Canadian corporate entity that is a bare shell, with no employees, no premises, and no participation in the webtoon business, will be denied treaty benefits, may have intercompany payments reclassified as dividends or capital, and may trigger GAAR reassessments and penalties of up to 50% of the tax sought to be avoided.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: On indictment under s.239(1) Income Tax Act: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; on summary conviction: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; in addition, gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2), plus arrears interest compounded daily and mandatory officer director penalty assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed or acquiesced in concealment are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax shortfall, penalties, and interest; courts may issue restitution orders under s.738 Criminal Code; travel bans are routinely imposed pending prosecution; conviction results in permanent criminal record, disqualification from directorship, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Sham Arrangements Stripping Canadian Profits

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s.245 nullifying the avoidance transaction; s.247(2) recharacterisation of cross-border amounts; transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment where reasonable efforts were absent; denial of treaty withholding-tax relief; interest and penalties flowing from reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors signing avoidance transaction documents may be assessed under s.160 for property transferred for inadequate consideration; CRA can pierce the corporate veil where the corporation is a façade; criminal charges under s.239 for counselling false returns; foreign travel bans and extradition cooperation apply.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Dividends, Royalties, and Interest

Statutory sanction: Payer corporation is liable for the full unremitted Part XIII tax, plus arrears interest, plus a failure-to-remit penalty of 10% of the amount remitted late; any deferred or avoided Part XIII tax is due immediately upon discovery; treaty relief is permanently denied for the payment.

Director personal liability: Section 227.1 provides strict personal liability of directors for unremitted Part XIII withholding, equal to the entire unremitted amount plus interest and penalties, irrespective of intent; the CRA can issue a director liability assessment and enforce against personal assets; criminal prosecution for intentional non-remittance carries up to 5 years imprisonment and travel restrictions.
Failure to File International Information Returns and Transfer-Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Non-penal sanctions: s.162(7) penalty of CAD 25 per day (minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per return) for T1135; T106 penalty CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500; for large corporations, CAD 100 per day up to CAD 25,000 per return under s.162(8); loss of automatic SBD and loss-carryforward claims; transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of any upward adjustment; the taxpayer loses the burden-of-proof protection and must justify every cross-border transaction with accompanying documents.

Director personal liability: Section 162(7) penalties are imposed on the corporation, but directors who knowingly permit continued non-compliance are exposed to negligence claims by shareholders, CRA director liability under s.227.1 where unremitted amounts underlie the returns, and criminal prosecution under s.239 for failure to provide accurate information; travel bans are possible where CRA suspects flight risk.
AML and Financial Reporting Red Flags on Digital Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 and registration revocation for regulated payment processing entities; the earned income will be fully reassessed as undeclared Canadian-source business income with gross negligence penalty (50%) and potential anti-money-laundering prosecution; violations may be referred to FINTRAC and the RCMP.

Director personal liability: Directors with control over financial accounts can be personally prosecuted under the Criminal Code for laundering proceeds of crime, punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years; civil fraud liability and disgorgement of corporate profits; mandatory travel restrictions and asset freezes under ancillary orders.
