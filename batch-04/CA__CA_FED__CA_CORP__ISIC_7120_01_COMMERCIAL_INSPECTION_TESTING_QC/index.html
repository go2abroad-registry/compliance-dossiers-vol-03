Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC), direct tax and federal GST/HST administration; portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and GST/HST NETFILE; corporate registration registry: Corporations Canada (ISED).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or newly incorporated CBCA corporation must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) as its statutory tax identifier. The CRA issues the BN automatically upon CBCA incorporation via the Corporations Canada integration; where no Canadian incorporation exists, the non-resident must submit Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts', by mail or through a representative authorized in My Business Account, specifying the program accounts required (RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RC for corporate income tax). The BN application must disclose the entity type, registered office or care-of address in Canada, non-resident director/agent details, anticipated taxable revenue, permanent establishment information, and expected first reporting period. Once the RT account is opened, the registrant must file periodic GST/HST returns by NETFILE and may claim input tax credits; the BN remains valid indefinitely and must be quoted on all tax invoices, customs declarations, and supplier remittances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer an elective check-the-box regime; a federal business corporation is mandatorily taxable at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA). Key statutory elections and mechanisms available: (1) subsection 216(1) ITA election permitting a non-resident to file a net-basis Section 216 return for Canadian rental or real-property income; (2) Section 216.1 ITA election, implemented through subsection 241.1(1) ETA, enabling a non-resident without a permanent establishment in Canada to become a voluntarily registered non-resident registrant for GST/HST; (3) Part XIV Section 219 ITA election for a Canadian corporation to be treated as a Non-Resident-Owned Investment Corporation (NROIC), permitting remission of Part XIV tax in lieu of ordinary Part I tax on non-resident investment income; (4) Section 89(2) joint election deeming a corporation to be a Canadian corporation for NROIC purposes; and (5) hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 12.9, 18.4, 94.1 and 95.2 ITA (BEPS Action 2), which impose mandatory recharacterization rather than taxpayer-favorable elections, although protective joint elections may be available where dual-resident status is present. A single-member foreign parent cannot elect to disregard the Canadian subsidiary: the corporation remains a resident taxpayer on all worldwide income unless central management and control is displaced to a treaty jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: a person is a small supplier if the total value of its taxable supplies (including supplies of associates) in Canada does not exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, mandatory registration applies. Non-residents not carrying on business in Canada (no permanent establishment and no solicitation) are not compelled to register, but may elect under ETA s. 241.1 to become registrants; non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register and charge GST/HST once the threshold is surpassed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated under Section 6, Schedule VI ETA when goods are exported from Canada by the supplier in accordance with the Export of Goods Regulations and no further processing or transformation occurs in Canada after export. A supply of services is zero-rated under Section 7, Schedule VI ETA when supplied to a non-resident person, unless the service is a personal service rendered in Canada to a Canadian resident, relates to real property situated in Canada, or relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the tax becomes payable. Consequently, on-site factory pre-shipment inspection and quality-control services performed on goods physically present in Canada, including such services rendered to a foreign buyer, will generally attract GST/HST; zero-rating may be claimed only where the service is provided entirely outside Canada or the documentary record demonstrates that the tangible personal property was not situated in Canada at the time of performance and the client is a non-resident acquiring the service for consumption wholly outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or G13, or the electronic Canadian Export Reporting System confirmation showing the export permit and exit port)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill naming the non-resident consignee, the Canadian place of departure, and the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement/credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the seller's account within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice showing the 0% GST/HST rate applied and the statutory zero-rating reason code
  • Signed factory inspection/QC service contract, detailed test reports, and proof of client identity establishing the non-resident beneficiary and the export nexus

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or compulsory government-side transaction authorization exists at the federal level. A registrant must issue a valid GST/HST invoice containing the supplier BN, invoice date, description, taxable amount, and tax shown separately (or tax-inclusive pricing with a clear disclosure); electronic invoices are permissible in PDF, XML, EDI or CRA-accepted API formats provided they are legible and accessible. Books and records substantiating zero-rated exports, customs declarations, and FX receipts must be retained for six years in English or French. CRA auditors may invoke digital-audit procedures to require machine-readable records; however, this is an audit-right, not a clearance condition.

The CRA conducts post-refund audit verification of zero-rated export claims; any failure to substantiate export movement results in reassessment of uncollected GST/HST plus interest. Knowingly making a false or negligent statement in a return, refund application, or supporting invoice incurs a Section 280 ETA penalty equal to the greater of CAD 5,000 and 25% of the tax sought. Where export documentation is deliberately fabricated, criminal proceedings under Section 239 ITA may impose fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to two years. CRA may additionally claw back previously credited input tax credits, disallow refunds, impose a 10% repeated-failure penalty, and hold directors or senior officers personally liable for corporate amounts remittable under the ETA. The standard reassessment window is four years from the date of the notice of assessment, but fraud cases carry no limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible with free-floating exchange rates under an open current-account and capital-account regime; no exchange-control licensing, pre-approval, or repatriation permit is required for equity injection, service fees, or dividends. Profit remittance for a CBCA commercial inspection agency is subject only to: (i) the CBCA solvency test (CBCA s.42) for any dividend declaration, and (ii) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, reduced to 5%-15% under an applicable income tax treaty upon submission of CRA form NR301/NR302 or equivalent substantiation). Inward direct investment is reportable for statistical purposes when cumulative cross-border transactions exceed C$1,000,000 per 12 months.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio on export receipts. Inspection and QC service fees earned in foreign currency may be retained offshore or converted freely. Statutory obligations are limited to: (i) CBSA B13A export declaration for goods-based service shipments exceeding C$2,000 (where ancillary goods move), (ii) FINTRAC reporting of each cross-border financial transfer of C$10,000 or more, and (iii) Statistics Canada International Transactions reporting for receipts above C$1,000,000 annually. A Schedule I bank will apply its own incoming-wire verification but cannot legally require conversion of USD, EUR, or CNY proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; Canada maintains no dedicated Foreign Exchange Control Administration because the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951. FX and prudential oversight of resident banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), with statutory reporting channeled to Statistics Canada and FINTRAC.

Legal constraints: (i) registration as an MSB under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act is mandatory; (ii) the Bank of Canada Retail Payment Activities Act registry applies to payment service providers as of the 2024-2025 rollout; (iii) EMIs are not members of Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the Canadian clearing system, so settlement of LCs, standby LCs, and commercial FX hedges must remain at a Schedule I bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% federal Part I general corporate income tax rate; combined federal-provincial effective statutory rate ranges from approximately 23.0% (Alberta) to 31.0% (Prince Edward Island) depending on permanent establishment allocation, with Ontario approximately 26.5% and Quebec approximately 26.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or deemed credited by a resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; no domestic exemption for inter-corporate dividends unless DTA relief is invoked

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after each fiscal year-end; balance of tax payable due within 2 months after year-end (3 months where the corporation is a CCPC eligible for the Small Business Deduction); quarterly instalment payments due on the last day of each month in the next taxation year; GST/HST returns must be filed and remitted by the end of the month following the reporting period (1 month for monthly/quarterly, 3 months for annual)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous section 247 transfer-pricing documentation becomes mandatory for penalty protection where (i) the taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000 AND (ii) aggregate fair market value of controlled transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 5,000,000 (CAD 1,000,000 for loans, advances, and share transactions); CRA Master File and Local File must be prepared by the T2 filing deadline; Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain an Economic Substance Regulation regime analogous to UAE/DIFC; instead, substance is policed through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), sham doctrine and substance-over-form jurisprudence, transfer-pricing economic-substance comparability tests, anti-surplus-stripping rules (sections 84, 212.1, 212.3), the Excess Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (section 18.2, limiting net interest deductions to 30.0% of EBITDA), and the Global Minimum Tax Act imposing a 15.0% OECD Pillar Two top-up on large MNEs for fiscal years beginning on or after 31 December 2023

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239(1): summary conviction fine of 50.0%-200.0% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; indictment fine of 50.0%-200.0% plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil reassessment with the 6-year limitation period for gross negligence; gross negligence penalty under section 163(2) of 50.0% of understated tax; ongoing CRA criminal prosecution costs and publication of convictions

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and advisors who direct, assent to, or participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable; liability extends to unpaid source deductions and GST/HST under section 227.1; veil-piercing via section 160 assessments against assets transferred to non-arm's-length persons; Minister may issue a section 226 departure-denial certificate restricting international travel where the taxpayer plans to leave Canada to avoid payment; criminal conviction creates a permanent record, bars re-entry into Canada for non-resident directors, and risks personal bankruptcy without discharge of tax liabilities
Shell Company / Artificial Avoidance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under section 245 denies the full tax benefit plus a 15.0% penalty on the denied benefit; transfer-pricing adjustments recompute grossed-up arm's-length amounts and impose 10.0% penalties on documentation deficiencies; anti-surplus-stripping rules (sections 84, 212.1, 212.3) recharacterize distributions as dividends; EIFEL section 18.2 disallows excess interest deductions above the 30.0% EBITDA cap, producing sizable reassessments and interest

Director personal liability: Directors who execute sham agreements or false T106/T1134 filings are personally exposed to section 160(1) assessments attaching to undervalue transfers to related entities; directors face personal liability for corporate tax debts where they authorized non-arm's-length stripping that rendered the corporation insolvent; CRA may obtain personal travel bans, freeze personal bank accounts, and pursue director liability assessments jointly and severally
AML / Money Laundering (Cash and Wire Surcharges)

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per contravention for entities (CAD 2,000,000 for repeat violations); Criminal Code section 462.331 money laundering offense punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years; asset forfeiture under Part XII.2 of the Criminal Code; inclusion in FINTRAC public enforcement notices damaging bank licensing of the corporation

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and compliance officers are personally subject to FINTRAC enforcement actions and administrative monetary penalties; senior managers who knowingly structure transactions face personal criminal liability; the corporate veil does not shield directors where they control disbursement of tainted funds; personal travel restrictions and director prohibition orders may be imposed, and judicial restitution orders can attach personal assets
