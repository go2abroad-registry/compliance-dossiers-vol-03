Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), including the Charities Directorate; digital portal: CRA My Business Account (MyBA) and Represent a Client (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html)

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian entity: complete Federal/Provincial incorporation (Corporations Canada), then obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The federal/provincial corporation is a taxpayer separate from its shareholders under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA). A registered private charitable foundation is exempt from Part I income tax under ITA s.149(1)(f), but remains subject to Part V tax on non-qualified investments and to revocation tax under ITA s.188(1); it must satisfy the annual disbursement quota of 3.5% of average investment assets (post-2023 rules) and issue official donation receipts under ITA s.110.1/s.118.1. Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada are taxed only on Canadian-source income (Territorial_Scope). Canada has no pass-through classification for corporations; trusts may elect passive partner status but this is outside the corporate form. No federal Tax_Exempt_Zone applies.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification flows from legal form and the ITA. Relevant elections include: (i) ITA s.85 corporate rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property to a corporation (with the 2019+ required joint election on Form T2057/T2058); (ii) ITA s.150(3) non-calendar taxation year election for wholly-owned Canadian subsidiaries; (iii) ITA s.216(1) election by a non-resident on Canadian rental/royalty income; (iv) trust-related elections under ITA s.104(4) and s.108(1) (24-month deemed disposition at death) for dual-status structures; (v) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s.18.2, s.18.21, s.20.35, s.20.36) apply mandatorily without election, neutralizing cash basis/hybrid entity mismatches and imported mismatches with non-resident affiliates, subject to Part XVIII reporting (Form RC700 for reportable transactions).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold under ETA s.148): an entity must register for GST/HST once worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters; registered charities are subject to the same threshold but may voluntarily register and claim the 50% (federal) public service bodies' rebate on GST/HST paid.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST/HST) applies to supplies made by a registrant (including a registered private foundation) where: (i) goods are exported from Canada by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for export (Schedule VI, Part V, s.1 ETA); and (ii) services are supplied to non-resident persons and are not rendered in connection with real or tangible personal property situated in Canada (Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 ETA). Grant distributions by a private foundation are generally gratuitous gifts and fall outside the GST/HST net; where a grant constitutes consideration for an administrative or management service, the place-of-supply rules in ETA ss.142/143 determine Canadian nexus. Claim refunds or input tax credits via Form GST189 (General Application for Rebate of GST/HST) or on the GST/HST NETFILE return (Form GST34) within the four-year limitation period (ETA s.225(2) and s.252(2)).

  • Customs Export Declaration filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) - Form G13
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (ocean or air transport documentation evidencing physical export)
  • Commercial Invoice (seller/supplier invoice describing goods, value and foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank statement or foreign-currency payment confirmation from the non-resident purchaser
  • Packing list and freight forwarder's certificate of export (where applicable)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or digital tax stamp regime exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing program (Peppol-based, launched 2023-2025). Registrants must nonetheless issue invoices containing prescribed particulars (ETA Information Returns Regulations, s.3), retain all books and records for six years (ETA s.286 / ITA s.230), and make electronic records available to CRA in auditable format upon demand.

CRA may reassess on audit where export documentation is deficient, reversing the zero-rating and clawing back input tax credits with interest under ETA ss.280-281. Specific exposures: (i) gross negligence penalty equal to 25% of the amount assessed (ETA s.285); (ii) false statements/omissions penalty up to 25% of the tax sought to be avoided (ETA s.327.2 and s.238 ITA); (iii) criminal prosecution for knowing evasion or fraudulent export claims (ETA s.327.2(3); ITA s.239(1)) with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment; (iv) for a private foundation, revocation of charitable registration under ITA s.168 triggers a revocation tax of 100% of the foundation's assets (ITA s.188(1)), loss of the GST/HST charity rebate, and a five-year retroactive clawback of official donation receipts. Although Canada imposes no fixed statutory forex repatriation deadline for export proceeds, the 180-day benchmark is the standard documentary turnover accepted by CRA and export financing banks; longer deferral invites transfer-pricing scrutiny under ITA s.247.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both the capital account and the current account, with no exchange-control statute in force (the former Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951). There are no general restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distributions, or cross-border grant transfers by a registered charitable foundation. A federally registered charity is exempt from Part I tax under paragraph 149(1)(f) of the Income Tax Act provided it is resident in Canada, constituted exclusively for charitable purposes, and expends its income in accordance with CRA disbursement-quota rules. The only practical hurdles are Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-resident non-qualified payees (statutory 25%, subject to treaty relief) and FINTRAC threshold reporting for wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. The Investment Canada Act review regime is generally inapplicable to a passive endowment vehicle unless the Canadian business acquired by a non-resident reaches the C$1.293 billion (2024) enterprise-value threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. Exporters, research institutions, and charitable foundations earning foreign-currency licensing royalties, legacy receipts, or research-grant income may retain funds in Canadian or foreign-currency accounts at a Schedule I bank, convert at the prevailing floating market rate, or employ them directly. Obligations are limited to record-keeping, FINTRAC electronic funds-transfer reporting for wires equal to or above CAD 10,000, cross-border currency declaration of CAD 10,000 or more in physical cash at the border, and sanctions screening against the OSFI/Department of Finance consolidated lists and UN Security Council measures.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), the federal central bank and monetary authority, with shared foreign-exchange administration responsibilities exercised jointly with the Department of Finance Canada (currency-policy), OSFI (Bank Act prudential supervision), and FINTRAC (AML/CFT supervision under the PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs (UK/EU e-money institutions and US fintech platforms) are not authorized to accept Canadian-deposit liabilities or maintain Lynx-access accounts unless licensed as banks under the Bank Act. They may lawfully operate only as FINTRAC-registered money service businesses (MSBs) or as payment service providers registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada, and therefore are viable solely as supplementary grant-payment rails. Endowment principal must reside with a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank; no offshore EMI can provide the custodial integrity, CRA reporting interface, or settlement access required for a private charitable foundation's statutory treasury functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should arrive as SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers to the foundation's CAD or USD account at a Tier-1 Canadian bank, followed by local clearing through Payments Canada's Lynx (real-time gross settlement, minimum CAD 25,000) or the Real-Time Rail for smaller amounts. The remitter must state a proper ISO 20022 purpose code for philanthropic contributions (e.g., 'GIFT' or, when available, 'CHAR' — charitable grant) and in the free-text field 70 include the exact wording 'Endowment capital contribution to CA-FED registered charity [CRA Business Number] — no goods or services rendered in return'. The receiving bank screens the transfer and both counterparties against OFSI/UN sanctions lists, applies the PCMLTFA currency-transaction-report threshold of CAD 10,000 / USD 10,000, validates the foundation's charitable-registration status with CRA, and verifies that no personal account is used as the conduit.Outbound disbursements use four distinct rails: (i) domestic CAD grants — Lynx wire for values above CAD 25,000 or Real-Time Rail for instant sub-CAD 25,000 credits; (ii) USD grants — SWIFT MT103 transmitted through the Canadian bank's US correspondent (RBC Royal Bank USA for RBC, TD Bank N.A. for TD) with Fedwire/CHIPS settlement to the beneficiary's US account; (iii) EUR/GBP/CHF grants — SWIFT MT103 to the bank's London/Frankfurt/New York correspondent, clearing through SEPA/Target2/CHAPS to the final beneficiary; (iv) commercial letters of credit (UCP-600) issued under MT700/MT707 for scientific-equipment procurement, with the complete application and grant documentation retained for CRA expenditure review. Each outward wire's field-70 narrative must carry the grant agreement reference number, and any transfer to an entity absent from the CRA qualified-donee list is automatically blocked by the bank's screening engine.Outright deliverable FX forwards contractually binding on both parties, with tenors of 1-24 months matched to the foundation's annual grant and disbursement cycle.FX spot transactions for immediate conversion of USD, EUR, GBP receipts or capital transfers into CAD at the prevailing floating market rate.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) converting foreign-currency endowment investment principal into CAD at a fixed swap rate, subject to prior board approval and the foundation's Statement of Investment Policy and Procedures.FX plain-vanilla call and put options on liquid G10 pairs, exercised only where the foundation's SIPP expressly authorizes option-based hedging for endowment protection.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to genuinely non-convertible currencies (CNY, KRW, INR, etc.), permitted only under an ISDA 2002 master agreement and an executed special-risk disclosure with the dealer's capital-markets desk; prohibited for freely convertible currencies.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs (UK/EU e-money institutions and US fintech platforms) are not authorized to accept Canadian-deposit liabilities or maintain Lynx-access accounts unless licensed as banks under the Bank Act. They may lawfully operate only as FINTRAC-registered money service businesses (MSBs) or as payment service providers registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada, and therefore are viable solely as supplementary grant-payment rails. Endowment principal must reside with a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank; no offshore EMI can provide the custodial integrity, CRA reporting interface, or settlement access required for a private charitable foundation's statutory treasury functions.
