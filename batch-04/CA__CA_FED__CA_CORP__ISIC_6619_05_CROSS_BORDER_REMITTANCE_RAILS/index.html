Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A federal corporation must maintain a registered office in Canada under the Canada Business Corporations Act. A commercial registered-office service may be acceptable for statutory notices if it satisfies the Act and filing requirements, but it is not sufficient by itself for a cross-border remittance or money-services operation. The business must maintain reliable books and records, a Canadian compliance function, a designated compliance officer, customer and transaction records, sanctions and AML controls, complaint handling, operational oversight and access to records. A virtual office is high risk where it is the only Canadian presence, where the company operates cash pickup or agent networks, or where a bank, FINTRAC, provincial regulator or law-enforcement body requires operational verification. Registration with FINTRAC as a money services business is mandatory before carrying on prescribed remittance or foreign-exchange activities; FINTRAC registration is not a general operating licence. Provincial or territorial registration, consumer-protection compliance, payment-service requirements and Quebec-specific licensing may also apply depending on the service model and locations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods from Canada under the Excise Tax Act. Cross-border services qualify for zero-rating where performed outside Canada for non-resident recipients or under specific export criteria.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII with a free-floating exchange rate. Current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted; profit, dividend, and capital remittances to non-residents require no Bank of Canada approval, being subject only to Part XIII withholding tax on outbound dividends (statutory 25%; treaty rates generally 5%-15% under applicable DTTs) and to standard corporate solvency and creditor-protection tests. For the target consumer remittance agency (ISIC 6619 / NAICS 522390), the operative convertibility constraint is regulatory rather than FX-based: FINTRAC MSB registration must be active before operations commence, retail payment activities require RPAA registration with the Bank of Canada, and provincial MSB statutes (e.g., British Columbia, Quebec, Alberta, Manitoba) impose layered licensing obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable - Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio. The CAD floats freely; export and service receipts may be held in any currency, retained offshore, or converted at market rates without central bank approval. FINTRAC obligations are purely reporting duties (cross-border funds transfers of 10,000 CAD or more within any 24-hour window, large cash transaction reports, and suspicious transaction reports) and do not constitute surrender, conversion, or repatriation mandates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - central monetary authority and retail payment supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) - prudential regulator of Schedule I banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) - AML/ATF supervisor registering Money Services Businesses (MSBs); Payments Canada - operator of Lynx and Real-Time Rail clearing systems; Global Affairs Canada and the Minister of Finance - administration of sanctions and economic measures. No separate foreign-exchange control administration exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High, with material legal constraints. A non-resident fintech or Electronic Money Institution (EMI) may lawfully deploy cross-border money transmission rails into Canada only after: (i) FINTRAC MSB registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), including the Foreign MSB registration track where no Canadian place of business exists; (ii) RPAA registration with the Bank of Canada where retail payment functions are performed for Canadian end-users; and (iii) securing a Schedule I bank sponsor or a Payments Canada direct-member clearing agent for CAD clearing, because a foreign EMI cannot obtain Payments Canada membership directly. The Local_Cash_Pickup_Clearing rail therefore requires either a licensed Canadian payout-agent network or an agent-of-record arrangement through an OSFI-regulated trust company or bank. A home-state EMI license is legally insufficient in Canada; the sponsor bank will require the FINTRAC MSB registration number, a named Canadian compliance officer, evidence of segregated client funds, and typically a compliance-trust or float segregation structure. Practical de-risking constraints are decisive: where the Big Six decline, mid-tier sponsors (Canadian Western Bank, Laurentian Bank, ATB Financial) and major credit unions (Vancity, DUCA) offer viable alternatives.

Correspondent and local cash-pickup agent network opacity

Regulatory reporting and suspicious-transaction filing readiness

Root cause: Regulatory reporting and suspicious-transaction filing readiness

Defense evidence: Demonstrated regulatory reporting architecture: large cash transaction reports (LCTR) over 10,000 CAD, cross-border electronic funds transfer reports (EFTR) over 10,000 CAD per 24-hour window, terrorist property reports, suspicious transaction reports (STR) to FINTRAC, records retention system satisfying the 5-year statutory period, and signed service commitments with a deployed transaction-monitoring platform.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol: (1) the foreign parent or shareholder originates a SWIFT MT103 in CAD or USD through its home-country bank, referencing the Canadian entity name and FINTRAC MSB number in the remittance information field; (2) the receiving Schedule I bank credits the CAD Operational Account or segregated Capital Escrow Account after FX conversion of any USD inflow at prevailing market spot; (3) for statistical reporting, the bank tags the receipt under Statistics Canada's Canadian Balance of Payments (CBP) classification as direct investment - equity injection into a Canadian business enterprise; (4) the verification file comprises the board resolution approving share issuance and allotment, executed share subscription agreement, cash contribution schedule, inward remittance advice, and source-of-funds evidence; (5) funds become available via Payments Canada Lynx same-day settlement or the bank's internal book transfer, with escrowed capital released to operations only upon satisfaction of enhanced due diligence conditions; (6) no foreign-investment approval is required under the Investment Canada Act unless the transaction exceeds the WTO-investor review threshold, which a procurement-type remittance agency does not typically trigger.Three-rail disbursement architecture: (1) Domestic CAD payout rail - Payments Canada Lynx for high-value settlement to local cash-pickup clearing agents and Real-Time Rail for instant low-value agent funding, with agent accounts held at OSFI-regulated institutions to receive clearing credit; (2) International payout rail - SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers, preferably gpi-tracked for end-to-end status transparency, and MT202 COV interbank funding transfers to correspondent payout banks for offshore beneficiary settlement; (3) Trade-financing rail - commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP600) and standby letters of credit issued by the sponsor bank where merchandise-import settlement accompanies remittance flows. Every outbound cross-border funds transfer of 10,000 CAD or more within a 24-hour window is reported to FINTRAC as an EFTR; cash-pickup funding withdrawals in currency over 10,000 CAD trigger LCTR. The agency must maintain segregated client float funds under trust-style segregation and pre-fund agent liquidity pools ahead of payout windows to prevent intraday overdrafts.Deliverable CAD/USD and CAD/major-currency outright and window FX forwards with the Schedule I sponsor bank, with optional early termination featuresFX options, zero-premium collars, and participation structures for corridor liquidity protectionCAD/USD cross-currency swaps for rebalancing the settlement liquidity pool under a 1992/2002 ISDA Master Agreement or the 2011 Canadian Master AgreementFX swaps (swap-points trades) for short-term bridging between CAD and USD intraday clearing poolsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible in Canada but commercially redundant for CAD itself, which has a deep deliverable onshore forward market; NDF overlay is used only for soft-currency corridors in the remittance source network, settled in USD
