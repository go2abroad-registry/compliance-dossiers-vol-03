Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign limited partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and, where applicable, a GST/HST account and import/export account. The application is initiated via BRO or through Form RC1 filed by mail/fax. The BN (also used as the federal TIN) is typically issued within five business days for online applications. A non-resident LP must provide its limited partnership agreement, evidence of extra-provincial or federal registration, a Canadian address for service, and Form RC59 (authorized representative) if using an agent. GST/HST registration is mandatory within 30 days after the entity exceeds the CAD 30,000 worldwide taxable-supply threshold, with separate CBSA account setup required for physical customs transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box classification election exists under Canadian federal income tax. A limited partnership is statutorily transparent under Income Tax Act section 96; hybrid mismatch outcomes are constrained by ITA ss.18.2 and 18.3 implementing BEPS Action 2. Elections available include section 97(2) for tax-deferred contribution of partnership property and section 96(2.1) fiscal-period elections. For GST/HST purposes no entity-classification election is offered; the partnership is treated as a separate registrant person under the Excise Tax Act.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies during a calendar quarter or four consecutive calendar quarters; registration mandatory within 30 days after threshold is exceeded. Non-resident suppliers of imported physical goods to Canada are subject to the same basic threshold for GST/HST, with no separate low-value import GST registration regime for mail/parcel imported goods.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating of organic matcha powder applies under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act when a registrant supplier exports the goods from Canada in a continuous journey to a destination outside Canada, or delivers the goods to a common carrier for export. The purchaser must be a non-resident or the goods must be exported by the supplier; commercial invoices, freight forwarder instructions, and proof of ongoing voyage must demonstrate that title passed outside Canada. Any supply incidental to the matcha trade, such as offshore logistive services, may be zero-rated if the service is performed outside Canada and relates to real property situated outside Canada or tangible personal property outside Canada.

Canada does not mandate real-time electronic clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping for GST/HST. CRA requires electronic return filing and maintains data-validation protocols through the Business Number registry. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed supplier and purchaser identification, tax registration number, supply description, tax amounts, and zero-rating statements. All customs import/export entries require electronic transmission to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via eManifest/ECAP; there is no mandatory government pre-clearance e-invoice API at the federal tax level.

      
CRA may reassess export zero-rating positions beyond the normal four-year period in cases of misrepresentation or fraud attributable to the taxpayer. False or incomplete export records attract penalties under Excise Tax Act sections 280 and 285.1, including a gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the understated net tax or overclaimed ITC, plus interest. Customs Act penalties for false export declarations may reach CAD 2,000 per violation with goods forfeiture. Criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 can result in fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to five years on indictment, together with retrospective clawback of refunds, interest charges, and denial of future input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada imposes no exchange-control permits, no central-bank pre-approval for trade settlements, capital injections, or profit remittances, and no restrictions on the holding of foreign currency by a Canadian Limited Partnership. The binding constraints are fiscal rather than monetary: Part XIII withholding tax may apply to treaty-protected passive payments to non-resident partners, and inbound investments that result in control of a Canadian business may trigger notification or review under the Investment Canada Act. Partnership distributions to non-resident partners are freely remittable, but the LP must file a T5013 partnership information return and allocate Canadian-source income to each partner for CRA and treaty administration purposes.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime; the LP is under no obligation to repatriate, convert, or time-limit foreign-currency receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at any Schedule I bank, used directly to settle offshore supplier invoices, or converted to CAD at the LP's discretion. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply only to international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, which are reported by the banking institution, not by the LP as a non-MSB customer.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating together with the Department of Finance Canada for foreign-exchange policy and with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision; transactional FX and anti-money-laundering surveillance is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Pass-through partnership opacity and lack of separate legal personality triggering enhanced due diligence
        
Root cause: Pass-through partnership opacity and lack of separate legal personality triggering enhanced due diligence

        
Defense evidence: Certificate of Limited Partnership, complete LP agreement, CRA business number and GST/HST registration, T5013 partnership information return history, schedule of all partners with pro-rata capital percentages, and a signed undertaking to notify the bank within five business days of any transfer of partnership interests.

      

      
      

        Non-resident beneficial ownership or foreign general-partner control
        
Root cause: Non-resident beneficial ownership or foreign general-partner control

        
Defense evidence: Notarized partnership agreement, beneficial-ownership register (25% and 10% threshold schedules), passport copies with apostille, proof of residential address, bank reference letters from each partner's home institution, source-of-wealth declaration, and, where applicable, a Canadian federal or provincial Investment Canada Act notification receipt.

      

      
      

        High-frequency cross-border commodity payments to Japanese or Asian matcha suppliers
        
Root cause: High-frequency cross-border commodity payments to Japanese or Asian matcha suppliers

        
Defense evidence: Executed supply and purchase contracts, commercial invoices, packing lists, airway bills or bills of lading, Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) B3 customs entries, CFIA import documentation, supplier bank-account verification, and a counterparty due-diligence file containing sanctions screening and adverse-media results for each supplier.

      

      
      

        Unusual third-party disbursements or commission payments outside the documented supplier chain
        
Root cause: Unusual third-party disbursements or commission payments outside the documented supplier chain

        
Defense evidence: Signed distribution or agency agreements, commission calculation schedules, commercial substance evidence, name-screening and watchlist checks on the third party, and retained records of goods received or services rendered to rebut any inference of money laundering or trade-based fraud.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Partner capital contributions must be received via SWIFT MT103 from each partner's home bank into the LP's designated Operational Account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must cite the purpose as 'PARTNERSHIP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - CA_LP ORGANIC MATCHA POWDER TRADE'; Field 59 must carry the exact registered legal name of the LP, and the originating bank must be a recognized correspondent of the Canadian beneficiary bank. International CAD wires are cleared and settled through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) via the Bank of Canada, while USD wires may be cleared through the US correspondent network (for example, RBC via BNY Mellon or TD via Bank of America) to avoid double conversion. No exchange-control license or central-bank approval is required; the LP must maintain a capital-account ledger documenting each partner's contribution and liaise with the bank to satisfy FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer recordkeeping for all inbound transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.Supplier payments for matcha powder will be executed through (i) SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfers for open-account purchase invoices below USD 100,000, with standard value dating of T+1/T+2; (ii) SWIFT MT700 irrevocable documentary letters of credit, typically 90-day usance, issued by the Canadian bank for large or recurring shipments; and (iii) LVTS or Real-Time Rail clearing for any domestic Canadian supplier payments. For JPY-denominated disbursements to Japanese suppliers, the Canadian bank will clear via its JPY correspondent (for example, MUFG Bank or Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation), and the remittance instruction must include the supplier-provided purpose code under Japan's Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act (FEFTA), such as code '293' for payment of imported goods. Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
15% Federal general corporate rate; not assessed on the Limited Partnership itself because the LP is fiscally transparent, but applied to Canadian-resident corporate partners on their allocated share of Canadian-source business income

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% Part XIII on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident; for the LP, ordinary profit allocations are not dividends, but a non-resident limited partner is taxable in Canada under Part I on its share of Canadian-source business income and may be required to file a T1 or T2 return; deemed-dividend treatment under s.214(3) can apply to excessive management fees and certain payments

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate threshold for filing Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) covering cross-border procurement, loans, royalties, and management fees; CRA may request contemporaneous Local File and Master File style documentation when the potential adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000; taxpayers with gross revenue of CAD 15 million or less in the year are excused from full documentation under Information Circular 87-2R but must still make reasonable efforts to establish arm's length terms

      
Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a separate Economic Substance Regulation styled on BEPS Action 5 for ordinary trading LPs; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s.245 recharacterises transactions lacking economic substance that defeat the object and spirit of the Income Tax Act; in a Matcha trading structure, CRA will examine whether the LP has a real Canadian office, personnel of substance, inventory-risk oversight, and a general partner with substantive decision-making authority; a non-resident limited partner that manages the LP risks losing limited liability, creating a foreign permanent establishment for the partnership, and exposing profits to full Canadian taxation without treaty shelter

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Under s.239 of the Income Tax Act, fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment; gross negligence penalties under s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax payable; CRA may register a Federal Court certificate of arrears and seize bank accounts and inventory

        
Director personal liability: General partners and directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST; criminal conviction for tax evasion carries personal restitution orders, asset freezing, and a travel ban through CBSA exit-control measures while serious criminal proceedings are pending

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under s.245(2) applying GAAR, denial of treaty benefits, 25% Part XIII gross-up on deemed dividends, and a s.163.2 penalty of up to CAD 100,000 for participation in an abusive avoidance transaction; the CRA also applies foreign accrual property income rules if the non-resident affiliate is a controlled foreign affiliate

        
Director personal liability: Directors who certified the shell structure may be subject to civil restitution for lost Crown revenue and penalties assessed under the corporate general partner; travel bans and seizure of personal assets can follow a finding that the director knowingly promoted the sham arrangement

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Compliance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: Under s.247(3), a penalty of 10% of the transfer pricing adjustment, minimum CAD 1,000, unless the taxpayer made reasonable efforts to determine arm's length terms; additional tax reassessment and prescribed-rate arrears interest apply from the filing deadline; documentation must be contemporaneous to avoid penalty

        
Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate partner are personally liable under s.227.1 for any unremitted amounts of the underlying tax adjustment; CRA may pursue the directors' personal assets for the corporate penalty if the corporation has been assessed and remains unpaid

      

      
      

        AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 500,000 for a corporate entity and up to CAD 100,000 for individuals; the Criminal Code imposes imprisonment up to 10 years for laundering proceeds of crime; smuggling prosecutions under the Customs Act carry fines of up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate, or fail to report suspicious Matcha transactions, face criminal prosecution, personal asset forfeiture, and travel restrictions pending extradition or money-laundering proceedings; regulatorily imposed compliance orders may include a ban on holding director positions

      

      
      

        Non-Resident Withholding Failure (Part XIII)
        
Statutory sanction: Under s.215(2)(b), the LP and its withholding agent are jointly liable for the full 25% of the gross amount paid, plus interest and penalties; the withheld amount is not deductible to the payer under s.214(2); late T1062/NR4 filing adds per-slip fines of CAD 25 per day, maximum CAD 2,500

        
Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally assessed under s.227.1 for the unremitted Part XIII amount if the partnership fails to remit, and CRA may issue a garnishment order against the director's Canadian bank accounts; persistent non-remittance triggers criminal prosecution and restrictions on leaving Canada
