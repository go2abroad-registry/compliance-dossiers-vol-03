Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca, GST/HST NETFILE, and CRA My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) as its single statutory tax identifier by filing Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts') or by using CRA's Business Registration Online. The entity may concurrently open program accounts for GST/HST, payroll, and corporate income tax. Registration is mandatory if the entity (including branches or agents) makes or expects to make taxable supplies in Canada exceeding CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; otherwise registration is voluntary. A Canadian address or an authorized representative is generally required for CRA correspondence and service of notices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime; entity classification follows incorporation law. A Canadian federal corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act. Relevant elections include the section 216 Income Tax Act election for non-resident rental income and the optional joint election by a GST/HST registrant and a non-resident agent for agency supplies. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act may apply to payments connected with hybrid instruments, but no elective classification is available for Canadian-incorporated entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies during any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; since July 1, 2021, non-resident e-commerce sellers and marketplace facilitators must also register and collect GST/HST when sales to Canadian recipients exceed the same threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V when the supplier obtains documentary proof of export, with the goods generally required to be exported as soon as reasonably possible after shipment. Services supplied to non-residents, including digital design services and fulfilment fees for print-on-demand merchandise ordered by offshore customers, may also be zero-rated when not rendered to any person present in Canada. The zero-rating claim requires a clear paper trail linking the Canadian supplier, the foreign purchaser, the payment remittance, and the foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency ACI/eManifest or Form B13A where required)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing foreign destination and consignee
  • Commercial Invoice/Customs Invoice with HS tariff classification and declared value
  • Proof of payment and inward remittance from the non-resident purchaser
  • Carrier tracking and delivery proof (delivery confirmation by mail/courier to foreign address)
  • POD production and shipping records showing the goods left Canada

Canada has no general mandatory e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance requirement. GST/HST invoices are governed by Excise Tax Act section 169 and Schedules; simplified invoices are permitted for supplies under CAD 150. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million; NETFILE and XML/API filing via CRA is accepted. No digital stamping or continuous transaction control is presently imposed; however, selected listed marketplace operators are statutory collection and remittance agents for non-resident print-on-demand sellers.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST if documentary evidence is insufficient, imposing uncollected tax, interest, and penalties including 6% gross negligence penalties under Excise Tax Act sections 280-285, plus ministerial criminal penalties for false statements. For exports, the CRA has a four-year normal reassessment period, extended indefinitely for fraud or intentional misrepresentation; a clawback can recapture input tax credits claimed on related inputs and apply a 20% false-statement penalty. In high-risk export audits, CRA requires the supplier to link each shipment to payment and foreign bank records, and non-compliance can lead to cancellation of registration or imposition of security.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unencumbered currency convertibility on the current account, capital account, and profit-repatriation account. There is no exchange-control licensing, no convertibility certificate, and no limitation on dividend distributions or withdrawal of capital for a federally incorporated CBCA corporation. The only structural hurdles are: (i) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes a 25% withholding tax on dividends and certain interest paid to non-resident shareholders, reducible under an applicable income tax treaty; (ii) the solvency test set out in section 42 of the Canada Business Corporations Act must be satisfied before reducing capital or declaring a dividend; and (iii) cross-border currency movements equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 must be declared/reported to FINTRAC. CAD/USD and any other major currency conversion is free at bank, credit union, or registered money-services business rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. An online retailer may keep export receipts in foreign currency indefinitely, hold balances in USD/CAD settlement accounts at a Schedule I bank, or convert as required at spot rates. The residual duties are statistical and AML-oriented: reporting export values to Statistics Canada in the Merchandise Trade and Balance of Payments surveys, and filing a FINTRAC Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report where CAD 10,000 or more enters or leaves Canada. No surrender certificate or central-bank clearance is required for returning proceeds from e-commerce registries such as Shopify, Stripe, or PayPal.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the foreign-exchange and cross-border transfer control perimeter is administered jointly by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally chartered banks and by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium — permissible as auxiliary payment and FX-conversion rails, never as the primary treasury hub. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, PayPal Business) are not OSFI-licensed banks, do not qualify for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protection, and must be registered with FINTRAC as Foreign Money Services Businesses (FMSBs) before serving Canadian customers. A CBCA corporation should open and maintain a Schedule I operating account as the master account; the EMI may be used to receive USD settlements from US print-on-demand channels and to return CAD proceeds to the Canadian account through an identifiable correspondent-bank leg. Legal constraints: an FMSB cannot accept deposits intended for lending or hold funds as a bank in Canada; any credit balance is an unsecured contractual claim. For this Low_Risk_Standard POD profile, the EMI is viable as a velocity settlement layer, but working capital and statutory distributions must clear exclusively through the Canadian banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound equity: the foreign parent or shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 wire to the CBCA corporation's USD or CAD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Field 50K must disclose the actual ordering shareholder; Field 52A/56A should name the correspondent bank (e.g., a New York clearer for USD); Field 70 must read 'Share Capital Contribution under the Canada Business Corporations Act'. The receiving bank verifies the CBCA articles, directors' resolution, and share subscription agreement before posting value; domestic clearing and settlement finality is achieved same-day through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for amounts above CAD 25,000 or by the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for smaller credits. Proceeds are recorded as share capital/contributed surplus and reflected on the CBCA share register; a non-resident contribution is also screened against the Investment Canada Act notification thresholds and the Canadian tax reporting framework. No exchange-control permission is required, though the bank may require a standard FX conversion ticket at spot or forward when USD receipt must be converted for CAD disbursement.Outbound trade payments to print-suppliers, freight-forwarders, logistics and customs intermediaries are cleared through: (i) SWIFT MT103 international telegraphic transfers for non-resident beneficiaries, with Field 70 containing the invoice/reference number; (ii) LVTS/ACSS credit transfers for Canadian domestic payees; and (iii) cross-border ACH/EFTA transactions for US-based POD fulfilment and shipping partners. Commercial letters of credit are permissible under UCP 600 for high-value committed print purchases: the Schedule I issuing bank advises/confirms the LC to the supplier's bank, with payment at sight or after presentation of transport documents. Effectively the merchant uses a card-acquiring settlement intake (Stripe/PayPal) and a separate vendor-payment disbursement rail; treasury daily sweeps collect settlement balances into the operating account under a standard sweeping instruction. All outbound disbursements must trace to an approved supplier master list so that the bank's sanctions-filtered, AML-monitored payment factory remains unblocked.Outright OTC foreign-exchange forwards on CAD/USD and other liquid currencies under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Schedule I bank counterparty, tenors up to 12 monthsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) used only for illiquid/non-convertible currency legs arising from the overseas supply chain, cash-settled in CAD/USDCross-currency swaps (CCS) for matched funding of the USD operational account against committed revenue streams over a fixed tenorExchange-traded and OTC foreign-exchange options used strictly to cap adverse USD procurement cost spikes
