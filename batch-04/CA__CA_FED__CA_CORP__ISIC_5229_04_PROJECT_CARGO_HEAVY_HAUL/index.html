Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the CBCA; the corporation may incorporate with zero authorized paid-up capital. Shares may be issued for cash, property, or past services; no escrow or capital deposit account is mandated by the federal regulator.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and occupancy permit from the applicable municipal fire marshal for the head office; structural load clearance for any project cargo warehousing or staging yard leased by the corporation; workplace safety compliance under the Canada Labour Code, Part II (if federally regulated employer) or provincial Workers' Compensation Board coverage (e.g., WSIB in Ontario) for all employees; first aid, hazardous materials handling (TDG/ADR training), and forklift/heavy equipment operator certification where staging operations are performed.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the CBCA; the corporation may incorporate with zero authorized paid-up capital. Shares may be issued for cash, property, or past services; no escrow or capital deposit account is mandated by the federal regulator.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign corporation must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (Form RC1 / RC1A) by providing legal name, articles of incorporation, Canadian agent or place of business, and corporate status documents. The BN with Program Code 'RT' constitutes the GST/HST registration number; Program Code 'RM' is used for an Import/Export (Customs) account. Registration is mandatory once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; voluntary registration is recommended from day one for a project-cargo logistics carrier with milestone billing, as it permits input tax credits and clean zero-rated export treatment of outbound heavy-haul services.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and applies no exchange-control licensing or central bank approval for CAD conversion. Funds may be freely repatriated as dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, or capital reductions, subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%; treaty-reduced, including 5% or 15% under the Canada-US tax convention depending on ownership and residency). Profit remittance documentation generally requires audited or reviewed financial statements, tax residency certificates, and treaty benefit forms where applicable. No FX conversion license or prior monetary authority authorization is required for non-resident shareholders or project cargo operators.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for converting foreign currency receipts into CAD. Exporters may retain export earnings offshore or onshore in any currency. Compliance documentation is required by Export Development Canada (EDC) and federal customs reporting (B13 export declaration) for financing or statistical purposes, but there is no central-bank surrender obligation. FINTRAC large-transaction reporting and record-keeping duties may apply if export payments involve cash or certain equivalents above statutory thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential banking supervision by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF transaction reporting by Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Ancillary multi-currency settlement — Medium; primary CAD treasury — Low. A non-resident offshore EMI or e-money institution cannot lawfully accept deposit liabilities in Canada unless authorized as a bank or authorized foreign bank under the Bank Act. It may register with FINTRAC as a money service business (MSB) for transmission services, but it cannot provide CDIC-insured CAD accounts, direct Payments Canada clearing, or a complete treasury foundation for federal corporate tax/payroll. The viable structure is a Schedule I bank as the primary CAD operator, with a regulated foreign EMI used only for OECD WH/European/US disbursement nodes and lower-value project cargo operating expenses in EUR/USD/GBP.
