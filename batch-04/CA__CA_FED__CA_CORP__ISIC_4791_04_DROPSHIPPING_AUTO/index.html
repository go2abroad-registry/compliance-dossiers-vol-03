Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Registration and filing portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/business.html; corporate account administration through CRA My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

1. Determine residency: a corporation incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act is Canadian-resident and must file T2 returns. 2. Apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) through BRO, selecting program accounts: Corporate Income Tax (RC), GST/HST (RT), Payroll (RP) if employees, and Import/Export (RM) with CBSA if goods cross the border. 3. Register for GST/HST once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in a single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive quarters; voluntary registration is available to recover input tax credits. 4. For physical goods exports, activate a CBSA Export Account and file export declarations (Form B13A/e-manifest) via the Canadian Export Reporting System. 5. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must register through BRO or use Form RC8 to obtain a BN and file a T2; a treaty-based waiver of filing is available under s. 150(2.1) ITA. 6. Maintain CRA My Business Account credentials and appoint a Canadian resident representative where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to Part I tax on worldwide income; no pass-through election (e.g., US check-the-box or S-corporation) exists. A corporation controlled by non-residents is a non-CCPC and is denied the small business deduction; income tax is levied at the federal general corporate rate (15% federal plus provincial rates) and Part IV tax applies to certain dividend receipts. — 1. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to Part I tax on worldwide income; no pass-through election (e.g., US check-the-box or S-corporation) exists. A corporation controlled by non-residents is a non-CCPC and is denied the small business deduction; income tax is levied at the federal general corporate rate (15% federal plus provincial rates) and Part IV tax applies to certain dividend receipts.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box mechanism. Available elections include: (i) subsections 216(1)/217 ITA elections permitting a non-resident to file returns on net Canadian rental or employment income instead of Part XIII withholding; (ii) subsection 273 ETA joint venture election designating one GST/HST registrant to account for tax on the venture's supplies; (iii) Quick Method of Accounting election for GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000; (iv) election to treat a dropshipper and its non-resident customer as having made separate supplies under section 179 ETA; and (v) fiscal-year and instalment-basis elections under subsection 249.1 ITA. Hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.4-18.6 ITA) apply mechanistically without elective reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters ('small supplier' threshold). Non-resident distribution platform operators supplying goods to Canadian consumers through an electronic marketplace are deemed suppliers and must register once the same CAD 30,000 threshold is met in respect of Canadian-market sales; marketplace/digital-service rules effective July 1, 2021.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a supply of goods that are exported by the supplier under the contract of sale is zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI, ETA, provided credible export evidence exists before the return claiming the zero-rating is filed or within a CRA-accepted extension period. Services: a supply of services to a non-resident is zero-rated under s. 7, Part V, Schedule VI unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or is performed wholly in Canada for a Canadian-resident person; automated dropshipping support services (platform, payment, logistics arrangement) are generally zero-rated when supplied to non-residents not registered for GST/HST. The section 179 ETA drop-shipment rules operate to make both the domestic transfer of goods to a registered non-resident customer and the dropshipper's markup zero-rated where goods are forwarded directly from a Canadian supplier to the foreign end-customer.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A or e-manifest B13C) filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill issued by the common carrier, evidencing physical movement to a foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice with exporter/importer legal names, HS classification, quantity, and contract value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment gateway settlement statement (Stripe/PayPal) evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds
  • Supporting carrier logs, freight-forwarder certificates, and warehouse outbound manifests demonstrating the goods left Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax stamping, or digital invoice clearance mandate exists in Canada. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE (mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5M). Tax invoices must meet the s. 221/223 ETA requirements (supplier BN, date, description, tax amounts); e-invoicing using XML/EDI is voluntary. CRA requires 6-year retention of sales, purchase, and e-commerce platform records.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export as taxable where documentation is deficient, clawing back input tax credits with interest. Penalties: ETA s. 280(1) - 25% of the tax on knowingly false statements; ETA s. 281 - amounts up to 100% of evaded tax for culpable conduct; ETA s. 285 - failures to collect or remit, minimum CAD 250; ITA s. 163(2) - 50% gross-negligence penalty on understated income; criminal liability under ETA s. 327 (up to five years imprisonment and fines), and under the same provision for documentary forgery. CRA's International and Large Business Directorate may also impose director liability under ETA s. 323 for unremitted GST/HST. No statutory Canadian FX-repatriation deadline exists; CRA administratively examines prolonged foreign-currency retention as evidence of domestic consumption, and the 180-day benchmark governs audit due diligence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible foreign exchange regime for both current and capital accounts. Corporate profit remittances are unrestricted subject to tax compliance and beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion timeline applies to export receipts. Repatriation occurs freely; however, all FX transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 require mandatory reporting to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border electronic money issuance by fintech entities is subject to stringent licensing under Canada's Payment Services Act. Offshore EMIs require integration with regulated Canadian banking rails and full compliance with FINTRAC anti-money laundering standards.
