Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) — GST/HST administration under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act; official taxpayer portals: My Business Account (canada.ca/cra-my-business-account) and Business Registration Online (canada.ca/business-registration); export clearance and customs reporting are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Canadian Limited Partnership (LP) exporting ISIC 3250 medical imaging hardware: (1) determine whether the LP carries on business in Canada (ETA s. 123) and whether it meets the small-supplier test (ETA s. 148); (2) apply for a federal Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1, identifying the entity as a Limited Partnership; a non-resident LP must appoint a Canadian representative (Form RC59) and furnish the LP certificate and partnership agreement; (3) register for the GST/HST RT program account (BN + RT suffix) with the CRA; registration is mandatory where worldwide taxable supplies exceeded CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, with zero-rated export supplies counted in that test; (4) where only exports are made, use Form RC402 for non-resident registration and claim zero-rated treatment; (5) upon issuance of the BN/RT account, the LP receives its statutory GST/VAT identification number and commences GST/HST filing obligations on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis as assigned.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; a Canadian LP is statutorily fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act, and each partner is taxed on its allocable partnership income. Available elections and filings for an exporting LP include: (i) ETA s. 156 election for closely related persons (limited scope for partnerships); (ii) ETA s. 273 joint venture election for GST/HST filings; (iii) ITA s. 249.1(4) fiscal-period election for the partnership taxation year; and (iv) prescribed elections and reporting under the hybrid-mismatch provisions of the Income Tax Act (ss. 12.7 and 18.4) to align Canadian treatment where the LP is fiscally transparent in one jurisdiction but opaque in another under a structured cross-border arrangement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a taxable supplier (including a partnership) must register for GST/HST where its total annual worldwide consideration from taxable supplies exceeds CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148); voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, and zero-rated export sales count as taxable supplies for this test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (i) tangible personal property (color Doppler ultrasound console units) exported by the LP or delivered to a common carrier for export as soon after the supply as is reasonably possible (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1); (ii) goods supplied for export under Part V, s. 39; (iii) manufacturing and processing services performed on tangible personal property destined for export (Part V, s. 11); and (iv) services (including software updates and remote diagnostics) supplied to a non-resident not registered under the ETA, provided the service is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada (Part V, s. 7). The LP must be a GST/HST registrant, record the zero-rated supplies on its GST/HST return, and maintain written proof of export evidencing both physical departure from Canada and transfer of beneficial ownership to the foreign consignee.

  • CBSA Cargo Export Declaration / Canadian Export Reporting (Form B13A as applicable) cleared at the port of exit
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Truck Waybill with Canadian exit port and foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice reflecting incoterms, consignee, and product description
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / Letter of Credit drawing evidencing foreign-currency receipt
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin
  • Freight-forwarder or third-party logistics proof of export (customs stamp, container tracking, or exit confirmation)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or invoice pre-authorization. Registrants must issue GST/HST invoices meeting the content requirements of ETA ss. 169-170 and may adopt EDI, PDF, or Peppol-based e-invoicing voluntarily. CRA requires that electronic invoicing and supporting records be complete, accurate, and available for inspection; for export zero-rating claims, the invoice must contain export-specific data fields (consignee, incoterms, export declaration reference) to pass audit.

CRA audits cross-match export zero-rating claims against CBSA export declarations, freight-forwarder records, and inward remittances. Failure to substantiate an export results in reassessment of the supply as taxable at 5% GST, with ETA ss. 280-282 interest and penalties, and denial of affected input tax credits. Civil penalties include the false-statement/omission penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated (ETA s. 285). Criminal prosecution for evasion under ETA s. 327 may yield fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years. The standard reassessment period is four years (ETA s. 298(1)); it extends to seven years for neglect or carelessness (ETA s. 298(2)) and is unlimited for misrepresentation or fraud, allowing retrospective clawbacks of refunds, credits, and zero-rating benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange-control approvals for profit remittance, capital repatriation, loan repayment, or shareholder distributions. Cross-border remittances are subject to standard AML/ATF reporting, including FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports at CAD 10,000 or more. Non-resident recipients may be subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on dividends, interest, rents, and royalties paid by the Canadian limited partnership, but no Bank of Canada or central-bank conversion licence is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export receipts. A Canadian exporting entity may invoice, receive, and retain proceeds in any freely usable foreign currency. Export documentation and customs reporting under the Customs Act remain applicable, and physical currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 must be declared upon crossing the border, but no central-bank conversion obligation applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control administration. Currency movement and payment transparency are overseen by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability is Medium with substantial legal constraints. Canadian-domiciled fintech EMI-style platforms that are registered as money service businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and operate through a regulated Canadian schedule-I/Schedule-II bank partner may support payment aggregation and cross-border collections. Offshore or non-Canadian electronic money institutions without a Canadian MSB registration generally cannot offer CAD settlement accounts to a Canadian-resident entity and may create regulatory risk under the Retail Payment Activities Act once fully in force. Such channels should be used only for ancillary payment verification and not as the primary treasury clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or capital contributions from non-resident limited partners should be settled via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer in CAD or USD to the Canadian LP’s named operational or capital account at a tier-1 Canadian bank. The payment narrative must reference the LP capital contribution number and the underlying limited partnership agreement. Accepted purpose coding includes corporate investment, shareholder loan, or capital contribution; banks may apply ISO 20022 purpose codes such as CORP, INVE, or CAPC under the receiving institution’s interpretation. Verification requires (i) executed capital-call certificates, (ii) LP agreement extract, (iii) source-of-funds documentation from the remitting bank, (iv) confirmatory board resolution from the general partner, and (v) FINTRAC EFT report where the transaction is at least CAD 10,000. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal Part I general corporate rate (ITA s. 123(1)); 13% preferential rate for qualifying Canadian manufacturing and processing (M&P) profits; 9% federal effective rate on the first C$500,000 of active business income of a CCPC. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 is due on the last day of the fifth month after the partnership's fiscal period end (e.g., a 31 December 2024 year-end is due 31 May 2025). The partners' own T2 returns (if corporate) are due 6 months after each corporation's tax year-end; part-year and NR4 filing rules apply for non-resident partners.
