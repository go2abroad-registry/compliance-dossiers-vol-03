Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Number registration and GST/HST administration via Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

The limited partnership (LP) must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering through CRA's Business Registration Online or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The LP then applies to open a GST/HST account within the BN registration, supplying details of the partnership, partners, and Canadian agent or representative if non-resident. If taxable supplies in Canada exceed C$30,000 in the current or previous four calendar quarters, a GST/HST registration is mandatory. Registration can be voluntary for entities below the threshold. A non-resident LP must also complete Form RC372 for non-resident account registration. Once registered, the LP uses the BN followed by the GST/HST program account identifier (e.g., RT0001) for filings and remittances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The LP is a flow-through entity under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; no check-the-box election exists in Canada. However, a partnership may make elections for GST/HST purposes, such as the election under subsection 177(1.1) of the Excise Tax Act to treat the partnership as a separate person. For foreign entities claiming transparent treatment, a formal request for a determination under section 107 of the Income Tax Act may be filed with the CRA to confirm classification as a partnership or corporation. Non-resident partners may also elect a deemed year-end under subsection 150(2) or use a Canadian fiscal representative under section 275 of the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST compliance.

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V (sections 1 and 5) of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, the supply of goods (craft beer) whereby the registrant exports the goods, or delivers them to a common carrier for export, to a non-resident purchaser, qualifies for zero-rating (0% GST/HST). The registrant must retain documentary evidence that the goods were physically exported from Canada within a reasonable time (typically 60 days of invoicing but not statutorily fixed) and that the purchaser is non-resident for GST/HST purposes, keeping the beer outside Canada for all times prior to the export. The zero-rating also applies to supplies of intangible property or services that are performed exclusively or primarily for export or for use by non-residents, but craft beer distribution is a tangible goods supply. The registrant must retain all documentation to support the export claim, including export declarations, commercial invoices, and transport documents.

  • Canada Customs / Commercial Invoice (stating the goods are for export)
  • Export Declaration / B13A or electronic Export Report via the Canadian Online Export System (COES)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement outside Canada
  • Packing List
  • Proof of the purchaser's non-resident status (e.g., GST/HST registration status, residency certificate)
  • Evidence of payment / SWIFT wire confirmation showing foreign exchange repatriation

No national continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists in Canada. The CRA requires GST/HST returns to be filed electronically (NETFILE via approved software) for registrants with annual taxable supplies above C$1,500,000, and mandates electronic remittance for certain types of payments. Invoices must contain prescribed information under Section 223 of the Excise Tax Act and may be issued in paper or digital form, but no government pre-clearance or digital stamping is required. The CRA does maintain a live online portal (My Business Account) to submit returns, track assessments, and make payments.

No statutory Canadian deadline requires overnight repatriation of export proceeds; however, under the Income Tax Act, export revenue is converted to Canadian dollars and must be recognized on the appropriate taxation year. The 180-day period is a reasonable conventional window used for documentary substantiation; delays beyond this may invite CRA audit. If a registrant claims zero-rating without sufficient export documentation, CRA reassesses the supply as taxable at the applicable GST/HST rate. The Excise Tax Act provides a 25% gross negligence penalty (section 285.1) for false statements or omissions, plus unpaid tax and arrears interest under section 280. In cases of deliberate tax evasion or fraud, criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code (sections 380, 384) applies with fines up to C$10,000 or five years imprisonment. CRA may also claw back refunds for a period of four years (seven years in cases of misrepresentation or neglect, or 10 years for a willful fraud) with full interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and a de facto open capital account. No general exchange-control statute restricts the conversion or remittance of dividends, interest, royalties, or capital proceeds. Profit remittance hurdles are not FX-related but tax-driven: a Canadian limited partnership is a pass-through entity for Canadian tax purposes, so income allocated to non-resident partners is subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25% on amounts deemed distributions, subject to income tax treaty reduction). The absence of legal currency controls is complemented by anti-money-laundering reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and administrative review under the Investment Canada Act for transactions above applicable thresholds or involving national-security sensitivities. For cross-border payments exceeding CAD 10,000, FINTRAC electronic reporting and Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Reporting (post-CIFR) thresholds may apply. There is no material restriction on channeling equity or loan proceeds into Canada from non-resident partners, provided the transferor is identifiable and source-of-funds documentation is available to the receiving deposit-taking institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio exists for Canadian export receipts. Exporters are free to retain proceeds in any currency, hold them offshore, and decide independently on conversion to Canadian dollars. The only relevant reporting obligations are (i) EDI/export declarations under the Customs Act for goods exported, (ii) mandatory reporting to FINTRAC of cross-border currency movements greater than CAD 10,000, and (iii) business reporting to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) on export documentation. For bookkeeping and tax purposes, exporters purchasing or disposing of foreign currency on a settlement basis must adopt the bank’s published exchange rate for determining Canadian-dollar revenue; this is an accounting and tax-measurement rule, not an FX-control rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium and strictly supplementary. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorized to carry on a Canadian deposit-taking business, as only Schedule I/II banks and provincial cooperative credit unions are permitted to accept deposits under the Bank Act and relevant provincial statutes. A non-resident EMI may, however, operate in Canada as a money service business (MSB) or foreign MSB if registered with FINTRAC, and may provide payment-correspondent services, foreign-exchange execution, and cheap cross-border account-to-account remittances. These platforms are viable for disbursing trade payments or hedging amounts below significant treasury thresholds, but they cannot substitute for a domestic Schedule I bank account for statutory capital escrow, provincial liquor licensing, or receiving wholesale settlement from Canadian licensed distributors. Legal constraints include the need for a FINTRAC money service business registration, compliance with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) from 2024 for payment service providers, and the requirement to maintain all customer relationships within FATF-compliant KYC standards. Banks may reject a fintech-founder’s channel solely if it becomes the principal treasury relationship without an underlying Canadian deposit-taking institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The LP’s initial equity and capital contributions from non-resident partners should be sent via SWIFT MT103 from a commercial bank in the partner’s jurisdiction. The wire must include the full legal name of the LP, the LP’s operational account IBAN (if applicable) or Canadian bank account number, and a purpose code that maps either to the Canadian Bankers Association’s BOP/ECB purposes or IMF BPM6 'Equity and investment fund shares' classification. A practical purpose code is 'EQUITIES – Capital Contribution' or 'K299 – Capital Contributions' under SWIFT’s BOP category; if the capital is recorded as a loan, use 'L556 – Related-Party Loan'. The receiving Schedule I bank will request a bank validation letter confirming the beneficiary account details and may require the underlying limited partnership agreement to support the equity classification. All inbound wires above CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC wire-transfer reporting but do not require central-bank approval. To avoid false stop-identifiers, the source bank should clear the funds directly between regulated deposit-taking institutions; no crypto corridor should be used. If a partner contributes physical currency, it must be declared on a Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Report (CCMIR) with the CBSA/FINTRAC.Trade settlements to foreign breweries or distributors should be cleared through (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct corporate-to-corporate cross-border payments in USD or CAD; (ii) a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) issued under UCP 600 using SWIFT MT700/MT760 for high-value, documentary sale transactions; and (iii) the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or Real-Time Rail (RTR) via Payments Canada for same-day CAD-denominated domestic interbank settlements. For small recurring royalty or freight payments, an automated clearing house (ACH)/direct debit relationship may be established with the Canadian bank, subject to online banking mandates and applicable wire thresholds. Export documents should align with URC 522 for documentary collections to avoid discrepancies. To reduce foreign-currency friction, the LP may maintain a multi-currency account (USD, EUR) alongside the CAD operational account, allowing pre-confirmed FX conversions and eliminating dual conversion layers. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder, subject to treaty reduction. Because a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent, distributions of Canadian-source partnership income to non-resident partners are generally not dividends for WHT purposes; instead, Part XIII withholding at 25% applies to the non-resident partner's share of the partnership's Canadian-source income, allocation of certain rents, royalties, and management fees. Reductions under the relevant income tax treaty may apply.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a Canadian partnership with a December 31 fiscal year-end, the T5013 Information Return must be filed no later than five months after the fiscal year-end; a T2 corporate partner return is due six months after the corporation's tax year-end. Installment payments of corporate income tax are generally required quarterly, with the first installment due on the last day of the first month of the corporation's tax year for corporations in the prior year's installment base.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory CAD threshold requiring Local File or Master File preparation; the CRA applies a reasonable efforts standard. For large taxpayers with CAD 50 million or more in gross revenue or assets, contemporaneous documentation (Local File/Master File equivalent) must be prepared before the filing due date and provided within three months of a CRA request, failing which penalties apply.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute applicable to all entities, but the CRA can recharacterize or deny treaty benefits where transactions lack commercial rationality or where a foreign affiliate is not carrying on real business activity. For a limited partnership distribution entity, the partnership must maintain a Canadian permanent establishment, real business purpose, payroll, premises, and decision-making presence in Canada to avoid the CRA applying the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) or treaty shopping provisions under the Multilateral Instrument.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act, subsection 238(1): on summary conviction, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to two years; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 300% of the tax evaded and/or up to five years' imprisonment; gross negligence penalties under subsection 163(2) of 50% of the understatement may also apply.

Director personal liability: Limited partners who participate in the management or control of the LP lose limited liability and become liable for the partnership's obligations; directors and officers of a corporate general partner can be personally assessed for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under sections 227.1 and 323 of the Income Tax Act and section 320 of the Excise Tax Act respectively, and may be subject to travel bans under the Income Tax Act and criminal prosecution if implicated.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard the partnership and reallocate the income to the non-resident participant under section 247(2) transfer-pricing adjustments, apply a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the net adjustment, and deny treaty relief under the principal purpose test (PPT) in Canada's tax treaties and the MLI; the withholding tax at 25% can be assessed on the deemed payments.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for the LP's unpaid amounts only if they consented to the misallocation or participated in a sham; in flagrant cases they may be personally assessed for penalties and face prosecution for tax evasion if they signed or approved the false filings.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for entities, and criminal liability; distribution of alcohol without a provincial liquor authority licence attracts separate provincial fines and licence revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly cause or assist in money laundering are criminally liable under the Criminal Code, with penalties of up to 10 years' imprisonment; personal civil liability to creditors and the Crown can follow misuse of corporate funds or fraud.
Failure to File or Remit Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Failure to remit withheld taxes attracts a penalty of 10% of the amount not remitted plus interest; non-filing penalties of CAD 25 per day to CAD 2,500; if a demand for return is ignored, the CRA can assess the amount, and offences under subsection 238(1) can lead to fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding, GST/HST, and source deductions if they were directors at the time the failure arose and did not exercise due diligence; the CRA may issue a director's liability assessment and enforce personal assets, travel bans, and criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Subsection 247(2) adjustment reflecting arm's-length prices; subsection 247(3) penalty of 10% of the gross adjustment where the taxpayer failed to make reasonable efforts to use arm's-length prices or failed to provide documentation within three months of request; judicial doctrine of sham may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or prepare false transfer-pricing documentation face gross negligence penalties and possible criminal prosecution for tax evasion; because the LP is not a separate corporate taxpayer, the corporate general partner's directors may be personally liable for the resulting unpaid tax under the director liability provisions.
