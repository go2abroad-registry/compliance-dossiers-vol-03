Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Portal: CRA My Business Account, Business Registration Online, and GST/HST NETFILE/File Transfer API; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs export declarations.

E-government filing portal

A federally incorporated CBCA corporation is issued a 9-digit Business Number (BN) by CRA through the incorporation data exchange or by filing Form RC1/using Business Registration Online. The BN with program-account suffixes constitutes the statutory tax identifier: RC identifies the corporate income tax account and RT identifies the GST/HST account. A foreign non-resident vendor that is not incorporated in Canada and that is or will be carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada should file Form RC1 to obtain a BN and a non-resident GST/HST program account; no statutory operating licence is required for royalty-free stock-music licensing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Canadian business corporation is a separate tax person under the Income Tax Act; it is not a pass-through entity. Resident corporations pay Part I corporate income tax on worldwide income, while non-resident corporations are taxable on Canadian-source income and are also subject to Part XIII withholding and branch tax on Canadian business profits where applicable.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box entity-classification election. A CBCA corporation cannot elect to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity. Relevant statutory elections include: section 216(1) election for a non-resident earning Canadian rental or royalty income to file a Part I net-basis return instead of Part XIII gross withholding; section 219 branch-tax elections; GST/HST Quick Method and annual-filing elections for qualifying small suppliers; and optional simplified GST/HST registration for non-resident vendors. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.4 and 18.5 of the Income Tax Act are mandatory and do not provide elective avoidance mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A person is a small supplier and is not required to register for GST/HST until the total worldwide taxable revenues from taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; upon exceeding this threshold the person must register and collect GST/HST. A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada or soliciting customers in Canada must also register once the threshold is exceeded, and may register voluntarily before reaching the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate supplies of goods exported from Canada under Schedule VI, Part I of the Excise Tax Act, and supplies of services or intangible personal property made to non-resident recipients under Schedule VI, Part V, provided the supply is not for use, consumption, or enjoyment in Canada and does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. For royalty-free stock-music licences and subscriptions, digital delivery of the right to use copyrighted music to a non-resident customer generally qualifies for zero-rating if the supplier retains evidence that the customer is non-resident, the transaction is not connected with Canadian property or use, and the customer is not present in Canada at the time the service is rendered. Electronic download or streaming does not by itself prevent zero-rating.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration, CAED) and carrier Bill of Lading/Airway Bill where licensed music is delivered on physical media to a non-resident consignee; not applicable to pure online download or streaming supply.
  • Commercial invoice or licensing order generated by the marketplace platform identifying the customer, country of residence, date, licence type, and zero-rating basis.
  • Non-residency documentation: customer account profile, foreign postal address, Stripe/PayPal payout and country-code records, IP-geolocation evidence, or an executed W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E declaration where the customer claims entitlement to non-resident treatment.
  • Payment receipts and foreign-currency settlement statements from Stripe or PayPal evidencing receipt from abroad, serving as the functional equivalent of an inward remittance certificate (Canada does not issue a formal inward remittance certificate).
  • Zero-rated export reconciliation workbook cross-referencing each zero-rated sale to the retained non-residency and payment evidence.

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for GST/HST. CRA accepts electronic invoices and requires no transaction-by-transaction government pre-approval. A GST/HST registrant must issue invoices bearing the registrant's GST/HST registration number, supplier name, date, description, and total amount. CRA supports return filing through GST/HST Internet File Transfer and API channels, and pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing is voluntary, not a clearance mandate.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year reassessment period, or beyond it in cases of misrepresentation or fraud, and reverse export zero-rating where the recipient is shown to be a Canadian resident or the supply was for use in Canada. Temporary or permanent zero-rating clawback plus arrears interest may be assessed. Civil penalties under section 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act include the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax understated for knowingly or negligently making a false or misleading statement. Failure-to-register, failure-to-file, and failure-to-remit penalties also apply. Intentional fraudulent export zero-rating can expose the taxpayer and its directors to tax-evasion penalties, fines (which may be up to 200% of the evaded tax), and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current account and capital account items are fully convertible. The Canadian dollar (CAD) operates under a free-floating exchange rate regime. There are no general restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation for non-residents, subject to applicable withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) and anti-money laundering reporting requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or repatriation of export proceeds. There is no statutory timeline or required conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain revenues in foreign currency accounts; however, all financial transactions are subject to FINTRAC recordkeeping and reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Central Bank); Canada does not maintain a dedicated exchange control authority. Foreign exchange administration is governed by the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act, with transaction compliance monitored by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are legally permitted to provide payment services in Canada but must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act and register with the Bank of Canada if they perform clearing, settlement, or payment processing. These platforms are viable for merchant collection and sub-account settlement, but are not valid substitutes for a licensed Canadian chartered bank account for substantive treasury operations, capital escrow, or trade finance instruments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loans must enter Canada via a SWIFT MT103 remittance from the investing entity's bank to the Canadian corporation's operational account. The wire must include a meaningful purpose code, for example 'Equity Contribution' or 'Intercompany Loan', and must reference the Canadian company's legal entity identifier (LEI) if registered. Canadian banks require source-of-funds documentation for each inbound transfer exceeding CAD 10,000 under FINTRAC rules. No exchange control approval is required for inbound capital injection, but the recipient must retain all records for potential filing of Form T1135 (Foreign Income Verification Statement) if applicable.Outbound trade settlements and supplier payments are processed over SWIFT MT103 (for international wires), via commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued by a Canadian chartered bank, or through domestic EFT (for CAD payments within Canada). U.S.-dollar disbursements are typically routed through correspondent banking relationships over Fedwire or CHIPS, while CAD disbursements settle on Payments Canada's Lynx system. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods transactions; CAD 500,000 for services; CAD 250,000 for interest/royalties (per transaction or aggregate with same non-arm's length party). Master File and Local File required for entities with gross revenue exceeding CAD 50 million or cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions (e.g., under BEPS MLI) require genuine economic substance. A shell company without real business activity may be denied treaty benefits and face reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, plus civil penalties and interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s.227.1) if they failed to remit withholdings; travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under GAAR or MLI; reassessment of income; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for fraudulent misrepresentation; court orders for restitution; travel bans if criminal charges filed
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals, CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to implement AML compliance programs; personal liability for penalties if they directed or acquiesced
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income; penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (if adjustment exceeds CAD 50,000); interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in transfer pricing mispricing; personal liability for unpaid taxes if they failed to withhold or remit
