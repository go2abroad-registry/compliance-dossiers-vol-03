UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: The principal corporate beneficial-ownership regime is the Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44, sections 2.1, 21.1 and related provisions, together with the Canada Business Corporations Regulations, 2001, SOR/2001-512, including the individuals-with-significant-control record requirements. The principal AML/CFT legislation is the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, S.C. 2000, c. 17, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Regulations, SOR/2002-184. FINTRAC guidance applies to reporting entities. Canada does not maintain a single universally public federal UBO register for all corporations; corporate records are maintained by the corporation and may be produced to Corporations Canada or law-enforcement authorities in accordance with statute. Federal corporations must file annual returns and prescribed information with Corporations Canada, but ISC information is not generally open to unrestricted public search.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A federal corporation must maintain a registered office in the province or territory specified in its articles under the Canada Business Corporations Act. A commercial virtual office may be acceptable if it is a genuine physical address, receives legal notices, permits access to required corporate records, and is not merely a post-office box. It does not replace the operational premises required for a hotpot restaurant. The restaurant must separately satisfy provincial, territorial, and municipal zoning, food-premises, public-health, fire, building, liquor, employment, signage, and business-licensing requirements. Banks, payment processors, tax authorities, and municipal inspectors may reject a purely virtual operating address.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); federal tax registration and account administration are available through CRA online services, including Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account. Provincial and territorial tax authorities administer certain separate taxes and registrations.

E-government filing portal

Incorporate federally or provincially and obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Register the corporation for a corporation income tax (RC) program account and, where required, a goods and services tax/harmonized sales tax (GST/HST) account through BRO or the CRA. A non-resident corporation may apply for a BN and required program accounts using the CRA’s applicable non-resident registration process. Provide legal name, incorporation details and jurisdiction, registered and mailing addresses, directors, business activities, contact information, and requested effective dates. Registration may require supporting incorporation documents and authorized-representative information. Obtain separate provincial or territorial tax registrations where applicable. A BN or account registration does not itself establish tax residence or determine whether a non-resident is carrying on business in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian corporation is generally a separate taxpayer under the federal Income Tax Act; Canada has no general U.S.-style check-the-box election for a corporation to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity. Available elections are specific statutory elections and do not change the corporation’s general entity classification. Cross-border hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose income inclusions in specified circumstances; they are not an elective entity-classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies), measured under the small-supplier rules over a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. The test generally includes relevant associated persons. A person ceases to qualify at the statutory point when the threshold is exceeded. Special rules apply to non-residents, taxi and ride-sharing operators, and other specified persons; non-residents carrying on business in Canada may be required to register even if otherwise below the small-supplier threshold. The 5% figure is the federal GST rate; HST rates are higher in participating provinces, and separate provincial taxes may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exported goods may be zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met, including applicable requirements concerning delivery or shipment outside Canada and the purchaser’s status. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under specified provisions, subject to exclusions and conditions; services related to Canadian real property, certain tangible personal property, and specified personal services are examples of potentially excluded categories. Restaurant meals supplied and consumed in Canada are not zero-rated merely because the customer is foreign or pays from abroad. A registrant may generally claim input tax credits for eligible GST/HST paid on inputs attributable to zero-rated supplies, subject to ordinary documentation and limitation rules. Confirm the applicable Excise Tax Act provision and province-specific treatment for each transaction.

  • Export declaration and proof of export or shipment, where required under customs law and relevant to the zero-rating provision
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, contract, and records identifying the goods, purchaser, destination, and supply date
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record, or other carrier documentation supporting delivery or shipment outside Canada
  • Accounting records and GST/HST invoices or receipts supporting the reported supply and any input tax credit claim
  • Inward remittance evidence where relevant to substantiating payment; Canada does not impose a general inward-remittance certificate requirement for GST/HST zero-rating

Canada has no general federal real-time e-invoice clearance, mandatory API submission, or digital-stamping system for ordinary restaurant transactions. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices or receipts containing prescribed information when required, retain adequate books and records, and file returns electronically where legally required or elected. CRA electronic filing and payment services are available. Provincial requirements, payment-platform rules, and sector-specific obligations may apply separately.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST returns, deny unsupported zero-rating or input tax credits, and assess tax, interest, and applicable penalties. A registrant must retain records and substantiate that statutory zero-rating conditions were met. False statements or omissions may attract gross-negligence or other penalties under the Excise Tax Act; tax evasion or false documentation may also lead to prosecution. Reassessments are subject to statutory time limits and exceptions, including circumstances involving misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. Provincial tax authorities may independently reassess applicable provincial taxes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Non-resident investors may repatriate capital, dividends, interest, and branch profits without exchange-control approval, subject to corporate income-tax compliance, treaty eligibility, and Canada's withholding tax regime under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. Domestic banks and regulated foreign-exchange dealers execute conversions freely, but all cash and monetary instrument movements of C$10,000 or more cross-border are reportable to FINTRAC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency or convert through licensed banks. Reporting obligations arise solely under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for cross-border currency movements exceeding C$10,000, and under Statistics Canada / Balance of Payments reporting thresholds for trade-payment data.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering and cross-border reporting by Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not operate a centralized foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditional feasibility at Low-to-Medium reliability. Offshore digital banks and electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Canada; they may operate only as money-services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and, in Quebec, with the AMF. They can provide payment processing and multi-currency wallets, but cannot hold Canadian corporate deposits as if they were banks. If an overseas parent uses an EMI to pre-fund a Canadian operating account, payments must settle through a Schedule I Canadian bank, and the offshore EMI must provide full KYC, source-of-funds, and AML transaction-monitoring records acceptable to the Canadian correspondent bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

In-bound equity injections must be wired through a regulated Canadian Schedule I bank using the SWIFT MT103 high-value transfer protocol. Beneficiary bank instructions should use purpose codes consistent with Canada's international-transactions reporting categories, typically 'Direct investment – equity injection', 'Intercompany long-term loan', or 'Capital contribution to subsidiary', and the beneficiary field must include the legal entity name and the 9-digit CRA business number. Supporting verification includes a notarized board resolution approving the investment, the foreign parent's audited financial statements, and a deemed-value analysis of fully-paid share consideration. Transfers must originate from the identified corporate account of record; any cash movement into Canada above C$10,000 CAD is FINTRAC-reportable.Valid settlement rails are (i) SWIFT MT103/MT101 for dispatches to suppliers and fee payees; (ii) irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by a Canadian bank to the condiment-equipment supplier's bank, with Incoterms 2020 contract support; and (iii) documentary sight collections where the supplier ships under invoice and title documents. Payments are cleared over the Canada Lynx high-value payment system for CAD, SWIFT for USD/EUR/CNY, and the Automated Clearing Settlement System for batch domestic payments.
