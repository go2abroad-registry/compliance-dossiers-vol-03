Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; My Business Account: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html

E-government filing portal

Foreign-incorporated entities obtain a federal Business Number (BN) either by incorporating under the Canada Business Corporations Act and registering through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or by submitting Form RC1 as a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada. The BN is the statutory TIN; GST/HST program registration is added to the same BN as a program account with an 'RT' suffix. The CRA does not issue a separate VAT certificate beyond the GST/HST program account confirmation. No industry-specific statutory license is required for ISIC 6201 payroll automation, but the taxpayer must register for payroll deductions if it has Canadian employees or engages in Canadian payroll remittance services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election; legal-form classification governs and Canadian federal corporations are taxed at the entity level. Relevant statutory elections available include: subsection 85(1) rollover election for tax-deferred transfers to a corporation, subsection 216(1) election enabling non-residents to file a Canadian tax return on certain Canadian-source income, and subsection 261(3) functional-currency election for qualifying corporations maintaining records in a foreign currency. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.4 and 18.5 are mandatory, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters or CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter triggers mandatory GST/HST registration; non-residents carrying on business in Canada may also be required to register under a substantial-presence test applicable to suppliers of software and payroll services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada under a contract of sale, delivered to a common carrier for export, or otherwise proven to have been exported qualify for 0% GST/HST under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Services and intangible personal property, including cloud-based payroll automation software, qualify under Schedule VII when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and where the supply is not primarily for use or enjoyment in Canada. The supplier must maintain reasonable evidence demonstrating the non-Canadian destination or use.

  • Commercial invoice with export terms and full description of services or goods
  • Canada Export Declaration filed with CBSA via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or legacy B13A declaration for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export movement
  • Foreign exchange/remittance confirmation, including inward remittance certificate or bank wire record, matching the invoice payment
  • GST/HST zero-rated invoice bearing the 0% or export zero-rating notation

No national e-invoicing clearance or digital tax stamping regime exists. The CRA imposes prescribed invoice content requirements for GST/HST purposes and accepts electronic invoices. Registrants are required to file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA-approved channels where applicable thresholds are met. The CRA provides developer APIs for business-system integration, including payroll and tax return transmissions, but performs no real-time fiscal clearance or stamping.

The CRA may audit export zero-rating claims and will deny the claim where the documentation bundle is incomplete or contradictory. Reassessment triggers collection of unpaid GST/HST, interest from the due date, and civil penalties for false statements or negligence, typically in the range of 25%–50% of the overstated credit. The normal four-year reassessment period is inapplicable where misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default is shown, permitting retrospective clawbacks. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating can result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: Canada has no general foreign exchange control regime on current or capital account transactions since the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951. CAD and USD flow freely; profit repatriation by dividend, interest, or management fee is unrestricted for a federal corporation subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax and transfer-pricing documentation under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act. No exchange approval, no repatriation quota, and no investment registration requirement exist for ordinary software export or payroll SaaS service revenue. The only residual checks are CBSA declarations for physical currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more and FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for cross-border wires of CAD 10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandated conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for foreign sales receipts. Export proceeds may remain freely in CAD or foreign currency. Relevant obligations are limited to: (i) cross-border currency report under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for CAD 10,000+ physical movements; (ii) FINTRAC EFT reporting by the bank for CAD 10,000+ wires; and (iii) sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act for dealings with restricted jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium (conditional). Canada does not recognize offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) charters; a foreign EMI cannot take deposits or operate as a bank without a Schedule I Bank Act charter. The compliant model is: (i) FINTRAC registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) for FX dealing and money remittance; (ii) holding pooled client funds in segregated trust accounts at a Schedule I Canadian bank; (iii) using a Canadian payment processor for ACH/Eft debits via Payments Canada. Legal constraints include mandatory MSB registration before operations, no CDIC deposit insurance on pooled trust accounts, transaction reporting thresholds, and full PCMLTFA compliance. Pure offshore EMI onboarding without a Canadian MSB registration or bank partner is not a viable treasury route.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate for the active business income of a general corporation after the General Rate Reduction; the statutory basic rate of 38% is reduced by the 10% provincial abatement to 28%, and by the 13% General Rate Reduction to arrive at 15%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada, unless the rate is reduced under an applicable tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within six months after the end of the corporation's fiscal period. The balance-due day is generally two months after the fiscal period-end for non-CCPC corporations and three months after fiscal period-end for a CCPC eligible for the SBD.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Legal Name) Enter the corporation's legal name as registered under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or the applicable provincial corporations statute. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate fair market value of transactions with a single non-resident non-arm's length party in a fiscal period triggers the expectation of contemporaneous Local File documentation; a Master File is additionally expected for multinational groups having consolidated group revenue above CAD 750 million (the CbC reporting threshold).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose separate Economic Substance Regulations as seen in offshore jurisdictions; however, the CRA applies substantive economic-activity tests through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), the treaty Limitation on Benefits/Principal Purpose Test (PPT), and the foreign accrual property income (FAPI) rules. A Canadian payroll-SaaS entity must maintain real employees, premises, decision-making authority, and operational control over its payroll automation services to avoid recharacterization of intercompany fees.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought and imprisonment of up to 5 years. Civil penalties include a 10% gross-negligence penalty under subsection 163.2(10) for false statements or omissions, in addition to arrears interest and compounded monthly penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participated in the evasion may be personally assessed for unremitted source deductions, GST/HST, and income tax from evasions under directors' liability provisions; in egregious cases, they face criminal imprisonment, travel bans, and civil restitution including the piercing of the corporate veil to satisfy CRA judgments, both federally and under provincial equivalent statutes.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to deny the tax benefit, recharacterize the payment, and impose a 25% penalty on the tax benefit avoided (reduced by reliance on bona fide written opinions in certain cases). FAPI and deemed-dividend rules may also retroactively reattribute the income to the Canadian shareholder.

Director personal liability: A director who signed the T2 return or the structuring documents can be liable for a 50% civil penalty for false or misleading statements, and if the structure is deemed a scheme to evade tax, the director may be prosecuted and face up to 5 years imprisonment and a travel ban preventing departure from Canada.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 250,000 for individuals and CAD 500,000 for entities; wilful non-reporting may lead to criminal fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, plus suspension or revocation of the money-services registration and freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly or recklessly permit AML violations are individually liable for penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment; travel bans may be imposed by the CRA if the MSB is also a tax debtor, and the director may be subject to forfeiture of personal assets and prohibition on acting as a director.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The CRA has the authority to reprice the transaction under subsection 247(2). A taxpayer that does not make reasonable efforts to determine and document an arm's-length price is liable for a transfer-pricing penalty equal to 10% of the mispricing adjustment, on top of the adjusted tax and interest. Also, a 100% gross-negligence penalty may apply where the mispricing is attributable to deliberate false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the mispricing scheme may be personally liable through shareholder oppression remedies, and if the scheme is found to be an intentional tax evasion, directors may be imprisoned for up to 5 years and banned from crossing the Canadian border; the corporate veil will likely be pierced to recover the TP penalty and interest from the directors' personal assets.
