Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Canadian limited partnerships; capital contributions are contractual and may be cash or in-kind. Escrow account rule: initial subscription proceeds are held in a Canadian trust/escrow account and released to the limited partnership only upon issuance of the Certificate of Limited Partnership; non-resident partner contributions require enhanced AML/KYC verification and, where non-resident control is triggered, Investment Canada Act scrutiny.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), CRA My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/businesses.html).

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian limited partnership, the statutory tax identifier is the federal Business Number (BN). The partnership must first be registered under provincial limited partnership law. The authorized general partner then applies to the CRA using Form RC1 or BRO, supplying the partnership's legal name, provincial registration number, business address and a Canadian contact agent. Once the partnership carries on a business in Canada and its taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold, it must open a GST/HST program account under the BN. A non-resident partner does not separately register the LP; the LP itself obtains the BN and GST/HST account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a check-the-box election. A limited partnership is per se fiscally transparent for income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of partnership income and loss. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) partnership taxation year alignment under ITA s. 96(1); (ii) cost carry-over election on capital property contributions to the partnership under ITA s. 97(2); and (iii) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.7 which apply automatically and cannot be elected out. No election can alter the LP's transparency for income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada in the current calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident limited partnerships carrying on business in Canada must register once this threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI (Part V) to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of goods is zero-rated where the goods are exported from Canada in a continuous journey directly out of Canada or transferred to a common carrier and documented as for export before the GST/HST return is filed. A supply of services to a non-resident qualifies as zero-rated where the non-resident is not present in Canada and the service is not connected to real property/performance present in Canada, subject to the ETA's conditions. For sterilized nitrile examination gloves, the zero-rating claim must be supported by competent proof of physical export and actual payment sourced from the foreign buyer.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System automated report)
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order identifying the foreign buyer
  • Full set of original or electronic Bill of Lading (or airway bill/waybill) evidencing outbound shipment from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Canadian bank credit advice confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Carrier/cargo control documentation or CERS export confirmation showing goods left Canada

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping obligation at the federal level. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) by registrants with total annual taxable revenue above CAD 1.5 million, and all export declarations must be transmitted electronically to the CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

CRA audits zero-rated export claims and may reassess within the normal 4-year period (unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is made). An unsupported zero-rating claim results in clawback of the unremitted GST/HST at 5% (or applicable HST rate) plus interest and penalties. Penalties can include the 5% failure-to-file and penalty-the-greater-of provisions, a false statement penalty of up to 50% of the assessed tax, and criminal fines/imprisonment for fraud under the Criminal Code. The exporter must retain the documentation bundle for six years for potential CRA/CBSA review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII, with a free-floating Canadian dollar (CAD) and no exchange control regime requiring central bank approval for inward or outward remittances. Profit remittance and partnership distributions to foreign limited partners are unrestricted, but are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) at a 25% statutory rate for distributions characterized as partnership income or dividends, which may be reduced under an applicable tax treaty (e.g., the Canada-US Treaty). No friction is imposed on capital injections, loan repayments, liquidation proceeds, or royalties. The only mandatory reporting overlay is FINTRAC's Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) report for qualifying cross-border wire transfers at or above C$10,000, plus OFAC/OSFI sanctions screening by the clearing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. Exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in any convertible currency in any jurisdiction. A CA_LP manufacturing sterile examination nitrile gloves may lawfully maintain USD, EUR, or CAD settlement accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank, convert freely at negotiated commercial rates, and use export receipts to fund supplier prepayments, inventory build, and CAPEX without Bank of Canada involvement. Compliance is limited to FINTRAC EFT reporting for wire transfers exceeding C$10,000, accurate bookkeeping of export revenue for CRA purposes, and verification that counterparties are not on the OFAC Consolidated Sanctions List or the Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI and neobank platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex are legally treated in Canada as unregistered or registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). They are not 'banks' as defined under the Bank Act, cannot accept deposits in the legal sense, are not members of CDIC, and cannot provide Lynx or SWIFT direct membership. Such platforms may be used as auxiliary collection or disbursement rails for non-Canadian counterparties, remote marketplaces, or low-value supplier payments, but they cannot serve as the primary repository of the LP's capital and must be supplemented by a full banking relationship with a Schedule I bank to satisfy underwriters, trade financiers, and OSFI-regulated counterparty credibility requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions are routed by foreign limited partners via SWIFT MT103 to the CA_LP's CAD or USD operational account at the Canadian Schedule I bank. There is no Bank of Canada prior-approval or purpose-code licensing requirement; the receiving bank classifies the credit for balance-of-payments statistics as a direct investment capital contribution. The MT103 remittance information field must state 'Capital Contribution to CA_LP' and reference the specific capital call number. Verification documentation includes the executed capital call notice, source-of-funds confirmation, partner identity pack, FATCA/CRS self-certifications, and a general partner resolution. For contributions of C$10,000 or greater, the bank files a FINTRAC EFT report; large-value CAD transfers settle irrevocably through Lynx (Payments Canada high-value clearing system) in central-bank settlement balances, while USD transfers clear through the SWIFT correspondent network with final settlement in the United States.Outbound trade disbursements are settled through three complementary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for recurring open-account supplier payments, equipment deposits, freight forwarders, and sterilization service fees; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 under UCP600 for bulk purchases of nitrile latex and high-value dipping machinery, with negotiation, acceptance, or deferred payment at a designated confirming bank; and (iii) Canadian domestic electronic payments settled through Payments Canada ACSS or Lynx for local CAD vendors. FX conversion on outbound USD, EUR, or Asian currency payments occurs at the clearing bank's commercially negotiated rate or under an executed forward contract. All outbound EFTs above C$10,000 are reported to FINTRAC, and the partnership retains underlying trade documents (purchase orders, pro-forma invoices, packing lists, certificates of origin, and shipping records) to evidence the commercial purpose of each disbursement.Deliverable foreign exchange forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/CNH) executed under an ISDA master agreement with a licensed Schedule I Canadian bank for firm commitment hedging of known receivables and payablesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or thin-market currencies (e.g., THB, VND, IDR, INR) where the Canadian counterparty relies on published offshore fixing rates and settles in CAD or USD, available to the extent the bank confirms no onshore deliverable alternative existsCross-currency swaps (CCIRS) for medium-term risk management of CAD/USD or CAD/EUR principal and interest exposures arising from partner loans, trade finance facilities, or capital lease obligationsPlain vanilla OTC European-style FX options purchased from an authorized Canadian banking counterparty to cap adverse CAD/USD or CAD/EUR moves without removing favorable residual upsideNatural hedges through multi-currency current accounts (CAD, USD, EUR) at the primary Schedule I bank, enabling receipt-to-payment matching and inter-currency netting of export invoice proceeds against import payables
