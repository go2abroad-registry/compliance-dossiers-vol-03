Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST Registration Portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses.html)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities without a Canadian permanent establishment must apply for a CRA business number (BN) and GST/HST account using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the CRA's online registration portal. For non-residents carrying on business in Canada, registration is generally required once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, unless the entity is already registered. The application requires proof of incorporation, foreign tax identification number, and details of the Canadian representative or agent. The CRA assigns a 9-digit BN, and a separate GST/HST account number (RT) is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a check-the-box regime comparable to the US; corporate entities are taxed at the entity level. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST election under section 191.1 of the Excise Tax Act to treat related corporations as a single registrant, (ii) simplified GST/HST accounting election for small suppliers, and (iii) election to use net tax for certain exported goods and services under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act. Publicly traded or large corporate groups may elect to file consolidated income tax returns? No corporate consolidation election exists federally, but provincial allocations via the Taxation Agreement are mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada over any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, measured quarterly; for non-resident businesses, registration is required if the threshold is exceeded unless the supplies are exempt or zero-rated, in which case registration may be voluntary but is often mandatory to claim refunds and facilitate zero-rating documentation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods (continuous export under section 12(b)(ii) or 15 of the Excise Tax Act) and to certain services rendered to non-residents (e.g., services directly related to exported goods, such as stevedoring and loading of goods for export, qualify as zero-rated where the service is performed in respect of tangible property destined for export). The supplier must obtain documentary evidence of export, including proof that ownership of goods passed to a non-resident or that the goods were exported for use outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or CAED - Canadian Automated Export Declaration, as applicable)
  • Bill of Lading / waybill evidencing export from Canadian port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or commercial invoice and payment evidence confirming settlement of the transaction

Canada does not mandate continuous transaction controls (CTC) or real-time fiscal clearance by tax authorities. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed minimum information (supplier name, registration number, date, description, tax amount or rate) and retain all records for six years. The CRA requires electronic records to be in an accessible, readable format and supports direct electronic filing via the GST/HST NETFILE or the CRA's online portal. Digital stamping or clearance is not currently required, but taxpayers must maintain robust electronic audit trails.

Under the Excise Tax Act, false or fraudulent zero-rating claims can trigger reassessment, interest, and penalties up to the greater of CAD 250 or 5% of the net tax declared, and gross negligence penalties of 10% of unreported tax. Additionally, CRA may impose penalties for misrepresentation (s. 285.1) and an enhanced penalty of up to 50% of tax evaded for wilful attempts to evade GST/HST. Fraudulent export claims may result in retroactive clawback of refunds, loss of registration, and criminal prosecution. Document retention of six years is mandatory for export substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There is no exchange-control regime requiring central bank permits for foreign-currency payments or profit repatriation. A Canadian federal corporation may remit dividends, interest, management fees, and intercompany balances in CAD or convertible foreign currency without Bank of Canada authorization, subject to: (i) corporate solvency tests and declaration requirements under the Canada Business Corporations Act; (ii) Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents, subject to applicable tax treaty relief; (iii) transfer-pricing documentation for cross-border intercompany charges; and (iv) Investment Canada Act notification for the establishment or acquisition of control of a Canadian business by a non-Canadian, plus a possible national-security review for port-related transport infrastructure. No profit remittance licence or FX allocation is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or statutory repatriation timelines for export proceeds. Stevedoring revenue denominated in USD, EUR, or other currencies may be held in foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada or abroad, provided the books are maintained in CAD and foreign currency balances are translated at appropriate period-end rates. There is no required conversion ratio. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the reporting financial institution; trade and service transactions must be classified for Canadian balance-of-payments statistics.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange control policy and balance-of-payments statistics are administered in cooperation with Department of Finance Canada and Statistics Canada, with bank prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering transaction reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rate Low for the primary operating treasury and Conditional Medium only for ancillary low-value collections. Offshore electronic-money institutions are not licensed depositary institutions in Canada; a foreign EMI operating without a Money Services Business (MSB) registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act would be non-compliant, and retail payment services require authorization under the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by the Bank of Canada. Large bulk-port stevedoring cash management should use Schedule I banks, which provide Lynx high-value clearing, USD correspondent relationships, multi-currency accounts, escrow capacity, and SWIFT trade services. Fintech EMIs are not acceptable for capital injections, Port Authority clearing, vessel disbursement accounts, or commercial letters of credit.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity in an offshore or intermediate holding-company structure

Defense evidence: Current federal incorporation certificate, good-standing certificates for each corporate layer, updated corporate registers, ultimate individual beneficial owners with share percentages, notarized or apostilled organizational chart, and a written explanation of the ownership rationale for the Canadian stevedoring entity.
Large cross-border capital contribution from a non-resident parent

Root cause: Large cross-border capital contribution from a non-resident parent

Defense evidence: Shareholder resolution or board minutes approving the capital injection, official bank source-of-funds wire reference (SWIFT MT103), funded shareholder loan or subscription agreement, audited parent financial statements or accountant's confirmation, funds-flow diagram, and evidence that the capital is recorded as paid-in capital or subordinated debt in the Canadian entity's books.
Politically exposed persons or government and port-authority relationships in the concession

Root cause: Politically exposed persons or government and port-authority relationships in the concession

Defense evidence: PEP declarations for all directors, shareholders, and senior officers; organizational chart demonstrating separateness from government entities; arms'-length port concession or terminal lease agreement; legal counsel opinion confirming no improper influence; and enhanced monitoring notice filed with the bank's compliance officer.
Sanctions and maritime cargo risk from foreign vessel owners, charterers, or freight forwarders

Root cause: Sanctions and maritime cargo risk from foreign vessel owners, charterers, or freight forwarders

Defense evidence: Sanctions screening policy and audit trail, vessel name and IMO number for each ship call, list of charter parties and sub-charterers, cargo manifests and bills of lading for bulk cargo, Incoterms, export and import permits, certificates of origin, and confirmation that no sanctioned entity or embargoed destination is serviced by the terminal.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loans must be routed through the Canadian bank's SWIFT MT103/MT202 correspondent network using the bank's balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment in Canada, specifically equity or intercompany debt. The payment narrative must identify the Canadian federal corporation as beneficiary, the non-resident parent as originator, and the economic purpose such as subscribed capital, paid-in surplus, or subordinated loan. Funds are credited to the capital escrow or operating account after clearing and verification. FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting applies to each transfer equal to or greater than CAD 10,000. The bank must receive board resolutions, share subscription or contribution agreement, register of shareholders, updated federal corporate profile, and source-of-funds evidence. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services transactions; CAD 5 million for other transactions (e.g., goods, intangibles) with non-residents. Master File and Local File required if total gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like UAE, but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act. Transactions lacking economic substance and solely for tax avoidance may be recharacterized. For stevedoring, a permanent establishment in Canada is required to earn Canadian-source income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s. 227.1) if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraud.
