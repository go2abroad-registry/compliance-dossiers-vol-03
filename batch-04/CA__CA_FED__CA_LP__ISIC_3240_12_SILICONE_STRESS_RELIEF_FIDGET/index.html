Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online filing via CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and the Canada Business Registry; GST/HST program account ('RT') is administered exclusively by CRA at the federal level.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain a CRA Business Number (BN, a 9-digit statutory identifier) by filing Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts', either electronically through My Business Account or by paper submission to the CRA Business Registration Directorate; a GST/HST registration number is issued as a BN followed by the 'RT' account suffix on the same application. A non-resident partnership may file using a Canadian representative or an authorized agent, must provide an address for service or a prescribed non-resident address in Canada, and may be required by CRA to post security or a bond under Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 266 to secure GST/HST liabilities; registration must be completed before the partnership first makes any taxable supply in Canada once its worldwide taxable revenues exceed the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — for federal income tax purposes the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (Canada) sections 96(1) and 103, so taxable income is allocated to partners and no entity-level federal income tax is imposed; however, under ETA section 240(1) the partnership is itself a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes and must maintain its own BN/RT program account. Provincial optionality applies: in HST-participating provinces the partnership also administers the provincial component through the same federal account, while Quebec separately requires registration for QST (Revenu Québec, Form LM-2). — Foreign entities obtain a CRA Business Number (BN, a 9-digit statutory identifier) by filing Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts', either electronically through My Business Account or by paper submission to the CRA Business Registration Directorate; a GST/HST registration number is issued as a BN followed by the 'RT' account suffix on the same application. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — for federal income tax purposes the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (Canada) sections 96(1) and 103, so taxable income is allocated to partners and no entity-level federal income tax is imposed; however, under ETA section 240(1) the partnership is itself a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes and must maintain its own BN/RT program account. Provincial optionality applies: in HST-participating provinces the partnership also administers the provincial component through the same federal account, while Quebec separately requires registration for QST (Revenu Québec, Form LM-2).

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by legal form and statute. Available statutory elections relevant to a Canadian Limited Partnership include: (i) the joint election under ITA section 96(1.1) to treat a co-ownership arrangement as a non-partnership for income tax, thereby avoiding deemed partnership status; (ii) the ETA section 156 election to supply on a nil-consideration basis between closely related corporations/partnerships for GST/HST purposes; (iii) partnership allocation elections filed on Form T5013 Schedule 14 and prescribed allocation rules under ITA sections 96(5)–96(6); and (iv) elections relating to the treatment of a non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source income, including deferred recognition under article IV-A of the Canada-US tax treaty where applicable. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are governed by ITA sections 18.4 through 18.7 and are mandatory, not elective, with no unilateral taxpayer election available to override those provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small supplier threshold under ETA section 148(2); a partnership engaged in commercial activity in Canada must register once the value of its worldwide taxable supplies (including taxable supplies made by associated persons) exceeds CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, registration is mandatory and the supplier must collect and remit GST/HST on all subsequent taxable supplies made in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible personal property (the manufactured food-grade silicone tactile sensory fidgets) is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 where the supplier satisfies the statutory export test: the goods must be physically exported after the supply is made and the supplier must maintain satisfactory evidence of export within a reasonable time. The supply is zero-rated if made to a recipient who exports the goods, or if the supplier itself exports the goods, and the conditions of ETA section 40(1) are met, which require the supplier to obtain and retain evidence prescribed by the Regulations — including the Customs Export Declaration, bill of lading, and foreign remittance records. Services supplied in respect of exported tangible personal property, or performed wholly outside Canada, may qualify for zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 where the recipient is a non-resident or the services relate to the exported goods. The exporter must retain the statutory documentation for at least six years for CRA audit purposes, and zero-rated export treatment is only confirmed where the goods leave Canada and the supporting evidence shows a clear foreign destination with no diversion or return to the Canadian market.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Canada Customs Coding Form B13-A, or electronic filing via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), showing the goods leaving Canada for a foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading or transport document — ocean bill of lading, air waybill, rail waybill, or truck/pilot-car manifest evidencing carriage to the export destination, or a bonded courier/postal export manifest for low-value consignments
  • Foreign Remittance / Payment Proof — bank statement or SWIFT TT credit advice, or platform payment statement (for Platform_Pay rail) evidencing the receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List — identifying the ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930 manufactured goods, HS heading 9503 or applicable tariff classification, quantity, unit value, destination address, and shipping marks
  • Sales Contract or Purchase Order — prescribed export transaction evidence under the ETA Regulations, retained together with freight forwarder certificates of export when a forwarder handled the shipment

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or government digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. Suppliers must issue a GST/HST invoice containing the prescribed particulars under ETA section 223(1) and applicable Regulations (supplier BN, date, recipient, description, tax rate, amount of tax, and a statement that tax was paid); electronic invoices are legally recognized if the prescribed information is retained and readable. The federal government announced in Budget 2024 a voluntary national e-invoicing framework aligned with the PEPPOL technical standard to be piloted by the Canada Revenue Agency and Shared Services Canada, but no mandatory API clearance or pre-approval regime applies to standard export transactions.

CRA may reverse a claimed zero-rating and reassess GST/HST payable within the normal four-year reassessment window under ETA section 298, extendable to ten years where misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default is established; any reversed export zero-rating triggers repayment of unreported net tax plus daily compounding interest under ETA section 280. A false or careless statement in a GST/HST return carries a penalty under ETA section 285.1 equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated, and gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax liability may be imposed where the taxpayer knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence claimed export zero-rating without real exports. Customs misdeclarations or failure to meet export reporting duties attract CBSA administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) of up to CAD 25,000 per violation under the Customs Act, with higher amounts for second or subsequent violations, and fraudulent export schemes are prosecutable under ETA section 328 and the Criminal Code with fines and imprisonment; CRA also applies a comprehensive cross-border audit program targeting false export claims and may impose a penalty of 5% of the value of tax withheld on the failure to file an information return, in addition to the denial of input tax credits connected to the clawed-back export supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible. Canada accepted IMF Article VIII in 1952; CAD is freely transferable for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control licenses, no capital-approval processes, no FX allocation quotas, and no balance-of-payments restrictions. Profit-remittance hurdles are purely tax-driven: (i) a non-resident limited partner's distributive share is Part I taxable if it carries on business in Canada through the LP; (ii) remittances characterized as interest, dividends, royalties, or management/administration fees are subject to Part XIII withholding at the statutory 25% (reducible by treaty, e.g., Canada-US to 5%-15% on dividends and 0%-10% on interest/royalties under the relevant treaty provisions); and (iii) capital returns must be documented against the LP's capital account to avoid Part XIII recharacterization. There are no separate federal or provincial FX repatriation permits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory surrender deadline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no custody obligation for export proceeds in CAD or USD. Exporters may retain export receivables in offshore accounts indefinitely; FINTRAC record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting apply at threshold values (≥ CAD 10,000 for EFTs/cash and equivalent instruments), but no repatriation or conversion mandate exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and retail-payment-system supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) is the prudential supervisor of all Schedule I banks. FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. Canada has no dedicated foreign exchange control board; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and cross-border fund movements are governed only by banking, AML, trade-sanction, and reporting statutes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium - ancillary only. Canada does not recognize the 'EMI' licence concept. A foreign digital bank (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) may operate only as a retail payment service provider registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (mandatory since November 1, 2024) and cannot take deposits under Bank Act s. 522. Therefore a foreign EMI cannot hold the LP's escrowed capital or settlement funds in its own Canadian name, but it is acceptable for fee-optimized platform payouts and modest cross-border TT flows if it maintains a segregated clearing account at an OSFI-regulated bank and provides full audit trail to the LP's primary bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate. The CA_LP is fiscally transparent; corporate partners are assessed Part I tax on allocated income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; reduced by DTA to 5%-15% depending on ownership level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of non-arm's-length inventory, services, intangible, and financial transactions with any single non-resident related party; mandatory contemporaneous local file and, for MNE groups above EUR 750 million consolidated revenue, Country-by-Country Report filing.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime akin to the UAE but enforces s.245 GAAR, treaty anti-abuse and beneficial-ownership doctrines, and s.96 partnership validity; CRA disregards incidental, low-substance LPs or flow-through conduits where the arrangement lacks commercial reality, and may recharacterise income and impose transfer pricing penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239, conviction for evasion attracts a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; s.163(2) imposes a civil gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax. CRA will also assess original tax plus compound interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners risk personal civil liability under s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions; criminal convictions may result in forfeiture orders, imprisonment, and disqualification. CRA may obtain a court order restricting travel or seizing personal assets for deliberate tax debts.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard the partnership under sham or GAAR principles, deny treaty benefits, recharacterise the transactions on an arm's-length basis, and apply a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000). Part XIII tax and arrears interest apply to recharacterised distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors/general partners who knowingly cause the sham are jointly and severally liable for the tax avoided; courts may lift the corporate veil, issue compliance orders, impose travel bans, and in aggravated cases convict participants under Criminal Code s.380 fraud with up to 14 years' imprisonment.
Anti-Money Laundering and Unreported Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Under the PCMLTFA and Criminal Code, FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation; failing to report a foreign transfer or suspicious transaction is punishable by fine up to CAD 500,000 and/or 5 years' imprisonment on indictment; CRA adds tax penalties and interest for unreported foreign income.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised or neglected AML compliance are subject to director and officer liability; the court may issue restitution orders, freezing injunctions, travel bans, and, where fraud is shown, imprisonment of up to 10 years.
