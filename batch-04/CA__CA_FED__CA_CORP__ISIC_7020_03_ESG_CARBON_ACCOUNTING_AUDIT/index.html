Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; domestic customs: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a Business Number (BN) by filing CRA Form RC246 (Business Number Registration for Non-Residents), accompanied by certified copies of the foreign incorporation charter, articles of incorporation/continuation, directors' information, and a completed Form RC366 (Basic Information for Non-Resident Corporations) or equivalent. To register under the GST/HST system, the entity must separately file Form GST494 (Non-Resident Application for GST/HST Registration) as part of the BN program-account activation; the CRA then issues a unique nine-digit BN with an RT program account suffix (e.g., 123456789RT0001). A representative for service of process in Canada is required, and the acquisition of federal corporate heimat requires filing through Corporations Canada prior to CRA registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box classification under domestic law. A federal business corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to entity-level income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act. No pass-through election is available for corporations. Relevant statutory elections include voluntary GST/HST registration (under s.240 Excise Tax Act), election for a closely related corporation to be treated as a separate branch for GST/HST purposes (s.s. 28-29 of Parts VI/VII regulations), and section 150(2) ITA taxation-year elections. Hybrid mismatch rules are found in ss.18.3-18.5 and s.112 of the Income Tax Act and operate as disproportion-avoidance measures; they do not permit corporate classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, applied to persons carrying on commercial activity in Canada; voluntary registration is available below the threshold, with immediate obligation to charge GST/HST with certain related tax credit eligibility.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act applies to (a) tangible goods supplied to a non-resident or delivered by the supplier or consignee to an export shipper or freight carrier for direct export outside Canada, and (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and performed exclusively outside Canada, or where the service relates to real or tangible property located outside Canada, subject to the intangible-personal-property and financial-service exclusions. For ESG carbon auditing, offset brokerage, and carbon-accounting services, eligibility requires the service recipient to be a non-resident and the service to be performed outside Canada (or the service to be for a foreign-located property); remitted brokerage commissions may constitute taxable supplies unless the recipient is non-resident and the use or enjoyment occurs outside Canada under s.25.5 or s.5.1 (ETC).

  • Bill of Lading (ocean bill of lading, air waybill, truck waybill, rail waybill, or courier proof of export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank wire, SWIFT MT103 confirmation, or foreign exchange settlement receipt evidencing payment in CAD or foreign currency)

No real-time fiscal clearance, VAT fiscal stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate is currently in force at the federal level. GST/HST registrants must issue electronic or paper sales invoices containing the supplier's BN and tax registration number, date of supply, buyer and supplier name, and explicit GST/HST amounts. CRA accepts standard EDI/XML formats (e.g., PEPPOL BIS) and requires documentary records to be kept in Canada or an accessible electronic form. A federal e-invoicing roadmap consultation is in development, but CRA does not require API-based fiscal approval at the time of filing.

CRA auditing practice accepts contemporaneous export and payment documentation within a 180-day administrative window as the evidentiary benchmark for export zero-rating; there is no separate statutory currency-repatriation cutoff under the Excise Tax Act, but delayed settlement exceeding 180 days exposes the claim to heightened evidentiary inquiry. Where zero-rating is denied, CRA will assess uncollected GST/HST at the applicable federal rate plus interest compounding under s.281 of the Excise Tax Act, impose false-statement penalties under ss.285-288 ETA (generally 5% of the net tax amount or CAD 5,000, whichever is greater), and, in cases of gross negligence or fraudulent intent, apply civil penalties for wilful disregard under s.327.1 ETA and refer to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada for criminal charges under s.327(2) ETA, carrying fines up to CAD 50,000 or double the tax evaded, plus imprisonment for up to five years. Retrospective clawbacks normally cover a 4-year reassessment window, or 8 years involving negligence or fraudulent misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange rate regime. Canada maintains no exchange controls; both capital account and current account transactions are freely permitted and profit remittance of dividends, interest, royalties, and service fees is unrestricted, subject only to withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) and applicable tax treaty relief. Non-resident shareholders and service recipients may convert CAD to foreign currency without prior central bank approval. The Bank of Canada does not ration foreign exchange and does not impose reporting thresholds on commercial wire transfers above standard FINTRAC cross-border reporting requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and imposes no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline for export receipts. Exporters, including providers of cross-border ESG carbon auditing and carbon offset brokerage services, may retain earnings in foreign currency, convert them freely, or deploy them for working capital without repatriation deadlines. Compliance obligations remain limited to income tax reporting of foreign source earnings and FINTRAC reporting of incoming or outgoing cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more per transaction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating in conjunction with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering supervision is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW to MEDIUM for a Canadian-domiciled federal corporation. Canada does not issue European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences; cross-border fintech providers must register as Payment Service Providers (PSPs) under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), administered by the Bank of Canada, and must establish a Canadian contact presence and compliance program. Registered PSPs are not permitted to take deposits, are ineligible for CDIC deposit insurance, and can serve only as complementary settlement utilities rather than primary corporate bank substitutes. Core treasury operations should therefore be anchored with Schedule I banks to ensure regulatory confidence, in-bound capital settlement, and access to credit facilities.

15% general federal corporate income tax rate (38% basic rate less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction); Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) are taxed at a reduced federal rate of 9% on active business income up to CAD 500,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder (ITA s.212(2)), subject to treaty reduction (e.g., Canada-U.S. treaty: 5% for a beneficial owner that is a company holding at least 10% of the voting shares; otherwise 15%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 year-end return is due June 30 of the following year); the final tax balance must be paid within two months of year-end (three months for eligible CCPCs) regardless of the six-month filing deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer-pricing books and records under ITA s.247(4) and Income Tax Regulations s.5801 apply where the taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000; the T106 Information Return for Non-Arm's-Length Transactions with Non-Residents must be filed when the aggregate value of specified transactions (including loans) with non-arm's-length non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000; CRA expects full OECD-conformant Master File and Local File documentation for multinational enterprises with material intercompany transactions; and a Country-by-Country Report is required where the ultimate parent Canadian entity reports consolidated group revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a dedicated UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime; substance is nevertheless enforced through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, ITA s.245), which denies tax benefits arising from avoidance transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose; (ii) the statutory requirement that a corporation maintain real management and control in Canada for tax-residency purposes; (iii) OECD Multilateral Instrument and treaty anti-abuse rules, including the Principal Purpose Test; and (iv) CRA audits of foreign affiliate and permanent establishment structures, which examine personnel, premises, risk-bearing functions, and decision-making. A Canadian ESG advisory or carbon-offset entity cannot rely on a nominal mailbox office or exclusive delegation of functions to a non-resident affiliate to support treaty relief or profit attribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False or Deceptive Statements (ITA s.239; Criminal Code s.380)

Statutory sanction: On indictment, a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; on summary conviction, a fine of up to CAD 10,000 and imprisonment for up to two years; a civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under s.163(2); mandatory prescribed-rate compound daily interest; and waiver of normal reassessment limitation periods where misrepresentation is due to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, participate in, or acquiesce to the evasion are parties to the offence and face imprisonment, personal restitution orders, and travel restrictions through court-ordered surrender of passports; CRA may also use s.160(2) to recover corporate tax from directors where corporate property has been transferred to them; the corporate veil is pierced where the company is found to be a mere instrument of fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Artificial Profit Stripping (ITA s.247 and GAAR s.245)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to the arm's-length price or profit allocation, with additional tax, compounded daily prescribed interest, and, where the taxpayer failed to make reasonable efforts to establish arm's-length terms, a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment in excess of CAD 50,000; GAAR denial of the entire tax benefit plus interest; and separate penalties for failure to file T106 returns or CbC reports, including per-day penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false T106 declarations or knowingly architect profit-stripping structures may be personally assessed under s.227.1 for unremitted amounts and under s.160 for benefits received; directors who transfer corporate assets to avoid assessed tax become jointly and severally liable for the tax up to the transferred value; criminal exposure arises where false records are supplied to CRA auditors.
