Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Federal Treasury Board; filing and registration via the CRA 'My Business Account' web portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first complete any required federal or provincial extra-provincial registration, then apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), online through 'My Business Account' or by mail. The BN integrates the income-tax account, GST/HST account, payroll account and customs import/export account. For a Canadian REIT, the trust deed and trustee identity must be supplied; if a director or trustee is not a Canadian resident, Form RC497 or equivalent agent-for-service documentation is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – a Canadian REIT is a statutory trust or corporation taxed at the entity level, but an eligible REIT (100 or more beneficiaries, income predominantly from real estate and rents, annual REIT election) is taxed under the Specified Investment Flow-Through Trust ('SIFT') rules with flow-through treatment for distributions to unitholders. The SIFT trust pays a refundable entity-level tax on non-flow-through income; public-scheme REITs otherwise retained income is taxed at entity level and distributions are deductible/flow-through. — A foreign entity must first complete any required federal or provincial extra-provincial registration, then apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), online through 'My Business Account' or by mail. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a Canadian REIT is a statutory trust or corporation taxed at the entity level, but an eligible REIT (100 or more beneficiaries, income predominantly from real estate and rents, annual REIT election) is taxed under the Specified Investment Flow-Through Trust ('SIFT') rules with flow-through treatment for distributions to unitholders. The SIFT trust pays a refundable entity-level tax on non-flow-through income; public-scheme REITs otherwise retained income is taxed at entity level and distributions are deductible/flow-through.

Available election forms

Canada does not use a US-style check-the-box election. Entity classification is statutory under the Income Tax Act (trust, corporation or partnership). A REIT may make the annual REIT election under ITA s.122.1; a trust may elect under s.104(5.8) regarding dispositions; hybrid mismatch countermeasures apply through the deeming rules in ITA s.94 (non-resident trusts) and s.18.4 (deductibility hybrid mismatch), plus the SIFT-partnership anti-deferral rules in ITA s.210.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – small-supplier threshold over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST registration; however, a REIT that makes taxable supplies of commercial real property substantially all of which is in the course of a business is required to register regardless of threshold where it makes significant leasing supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero rating applies on export of goods when the goods are delivered by the supplier to a common carrier or mailed/shipped directly to a destination outside Canada and the supplier obtains proof of export. For services (e.g., offshore real estate asset management, property management or REIT advisory services supplied to non-residents), zero rating applies if the service is supplied to a non-resident and the service is performed wholly or mainly outside Canada, or is in respect of real estate situated outside Canada. Leasing income from Canadian land is exempt not zero-rated; residential rents are exempt and commercial rents are taxable.

  • CBSA B13 Canadian Customs Export Declaration (or AES/ECA export reporting acknowledgement)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill with proof of delivery outside Canada (Carrier Certificate)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-issued foreign-exchange repatriation proof showing the export consideration was received in Canada)
  • Service contract or offshore property-management agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and the offshore site of performance

Canada has no mandatory real-time tax clearance or digital stamping regime. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and the CRA accepts electronic books and records provided they are accurate and legible; if a REIT uses an API/EDI invoicing system it must retain the full digital source data. Federal digital services tax (enacted July 2023, deferred in force until pillar-one implementation) applies to large digital-service businesses, but is not an e-invoicing clearance system.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims under the GST/HST Memorandum and may reassess within four years (or longer for misrepresentation); fraudulent zero-rating triggers penalties of 25% of net tax, wilful-damage penalties up to 250% of the false claim, interest on unreclaimed tax, retrospective clawback of input tax credits, Customs Act penalties up to CAD 100,000 or 200% of duty for false statements, and imprisonment up to five years for knowingly making false statements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully liberalized current and capital account convertibility under a free-floating exchange rate regime. The former Exchange Control Order was repealed in 1951, and Canada has accepted all Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement, meaning no restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions and no discriminatory currency arrangements. For a Federal REIT, profit and dividend remittances to non-resident unitholders are not subject to any exchange-control approval, repatriation license, pre-funding declaration, or Central Bank authorization. The only outbound financial impediments are ordinary fiscal in nature: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on distributions (statutory 25%, generally treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% under the Canada-US treaty for qualifying REIT distributions, and 15% under many other Canadian treaties); (ii) the requirement to file T3 Trust Returns and T5/T3 slips substantiating the distribution character; and (iii) the REIT's obligation under its trust indenture to maintain distributable cash calculations per Canadian GAAP and audited financial statements before remitting. The Canadian dollar is a heavily traded, fully convertible G10 currency; bank spreads, not regulatory barriers, determine net conversion economics.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, surrender obligation, or repatriation deadline on export receipts, trade proceeds, rental income collected from cross-border tenants, or capital inflows. All CAD/USD and multicurrency conversion decisions are commercially discretionary and executed at interbank or negotiated bank rates, subject only to standard documentation requirements for the underlying transaction. This is consistent with the absence of any foreign exchange control law in the Canadian federal statute book since 1951.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange-related oversight is jointly exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT transaction monitoring, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for cross-border currency movement reporting under the Cross-border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI and virtual account providers (e.g., foreign-incorporated non-bank payment firms) cannot operate as primary treasury rails in Canada without registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and in several provinces a separate money transmitter license. A Federal REIT may use a reputable foreign EMI for pre-onboarding liquidity testing or limited disbursement pilots, but legally mandated trust-asset segregation, investor distribution traceability, and CAD clearing efficiency require all operating and escrow funds to settle at a Schedule I domestic bank with access to the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS)/Real-Time Rail (RTR). FINTRAC registration, nomination of a Canadian agent for service, monthly transaction reporting, and compliance program obligations render pure offshore EMI treasury structurally unviable for a licensed, income-generating REIT.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for foreign investment into the REIT's Canadian capital escrow: (i) the originator bank transmits a SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary being the REIT's trust account at a Canadian Schedule I bank; (ii) MT103 field 50K/Ordering Customer must match the subscribed entity name; field 59/Account is the REIT's segregated operating or escrow account; and field 72/Bank-to-Bank Information must contain the reference 'REIT Capital Subscription – [Contract ID]' to permit straight-through processing; (iii) the purpose code used must align with Statistics Canada's Balance of Payments reporting framework — for direct investment into Canadian real estate use code 'Direct Investment in Canadian Enterprise' (BOP code DIE), and for portfolio equity use 'Portfolio Investment – Canadian Equity Securities'; (iv) any physical import or export of currency or monetary instruments equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to the CBSA on Form E677 (Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report); (v) FINTRAC EFT reporting applies to every electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more received in the course of the REIT's business; (vi) the bank will request a Client Risk Disclosure confirming the originating jurisdiction and investor KYC pack before crediting the escrow; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI and virtual account providers (e.g., foreign-incorporated non-bank payment firms) cannot operate as primary treasury rails in Canada without registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and in several provinces a separate money transmitter license. A Federal REIT may use a reputable foreign EMI for pre-onboarding liquidity testing or limited disbursement pilots, but legally mandated trust-asset segregation, investor distribution traceability, and CAD clearing efficiency require all operating and escrow funds to settle at a Schedule I domestic bank with access to the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS)/Real-Time Rail (RTR). FINTRAC registration, nomination of a Canadian agent for service, monthly transaction reporting, and compliance program obligations render pure offshore EMI treasury structurally unviable for a licensed, income-generating REIT.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate (basic 38% less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction); provincial and territorial rates add approximately 8% to 16%, resulting in a combined headline range of roughly 23% to 31%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to dividends paid or credited to non-residents. The same statutory rate applies to distributions of Canadian real-estate trust income paid to non-resident unitholders. Separately, a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada may be liable for 25% Part XIV branch tax on Canadian after-tax business earnings repatriated or deemed repatriated, subject to treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return is due no later than six months after the corporation’s taxation year-end. The balance-due date for payment of remaining tax is generally two months after year-end, or three months for certain Canadian-controlled private corporations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA requires a complete, contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation package, including a Canadian-specific master/local file, where the taxpayer’s gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and the aggregate value of non-arm’s-length cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 5 million in the taxation year. Taxpayers below these thresholds still must retain records sufficient to verify that each cross-border transaction was priced on arm’s-length terms.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but economic substance is enforced through section 247 transfer-pricing rules, section 245 GAAR, treaty abuse rules, and permanent establishment analysis. A corporate REIT must maintain real Canadian offices, active management of Canadian property assets, board meetings, complete corporate records, and local personnel. A letter-box entity with off-shore decision-making and no Canadian activities is exposed to GAAR recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Incomplete Filing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to two years; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% and imprisonment up to five years. A gross-negligence penalty under section 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax may also apply, plus interest and loss of the normal reassessment limitation period.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or participate in the false return may be charged as principals or co-conspirators. They can be assessed jointly and severally for unremitted withholdings, and the court may order civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment in serious criminal tax cases.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may assess a penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment. Inadequate documentation shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer, and the full adjustment plus prescribed-rate interest remains payable. Failure to file T106 also gives rise to daily information-return penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false transfer-pricing documentation or direct the diversion of value to related entities may be named in criminal or civil fraud actions. Corporate veil protection can be pierced where the director treats corporate assets as personal property or strips the Canadian entity of the ability to pay assessed tax.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under the GAAR to deny the tax benefit and impose full Part I and Part XIII taxes plus interest. Treaty relief is denied under the Principal Purpose Test or limitation-on-benefits provisions. If the arrangement is criminal, section 239 evasion penalties and imprisonment apply, and forfeiture of the asset may be ordered.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil if the corporation is a mere instrumentality, particularly for fraud or inequitable conduct. Directors may be ordered to restore diverted funds, pay damages for civil conspiracy, and may be subject to travel bans or freezing orders while criminal or regulatory investigations proceed.
Unremitted Dividend / Interest / Royalty / Rental Withholding

Statutory sanction: CRA may assess the payor for the full withholding tax amount, interest, and penalties for failure to deduct and remit under section 227. Penalties can be substantial and are not dischargeable by dissolving the corporation. The non-resident recipient remains separately liable for Canadian tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholdings under section 227.1. CRA can issue a director’s liability assessment and pursue civil execution against personal assets; directors who knowingly caused the non-payment may also face criminal prosecution and, in appropriate cases, travel bans and imprisonment.
Money Laundering / Asset Title Abuse

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code section 462.31 prohibits laundering proceeds of crime and carries a maximum imprisonment term of ten years on indictment. FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties may reach CAD 1 million per violation for corporate entities. Civil forfeiture of affected property is possible.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in, authorize, or benefit from the laundering scheme are personally exposed to criminal liability. Courts may grant restraining orders, freeze personal assets, impose travel bans, and order restitution or forfeiture of any property obtained through the unlawful activity.
