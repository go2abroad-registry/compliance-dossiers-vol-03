Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA); federal incorporation via Corporations Canada under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA).

E-government filing portal

A federal corporation automatically receives a 9-digit Business Number (BN) upon filing Articles of Incorporation through Corporations Canada. A foreign entity not incorporated in Canada must obtain a BN by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) through BRO, supplying certified proof of legal existence, registered office, directors, and where relevant, treaty residency. CRA activates on the single BN the program accounts: RP (corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RZ (import/export). Online registration is typically effective immediately; a Canadian representative may be appointed for service of process when the corporation has no Canadian presence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope - a CBCA or non-resident corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act; a resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxed only on Canadian-source income and capital gains. Canada does not recognise US-style check-the-box pass-through elections; fiscally transparent foreign entities (e.g., US LLCs) are classified by CRA administrative guidelines by reference to legal form and liability shield.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists. Income Tax Act elections relevant to a towage/salvage corporation include the s. 216 election for non-residents to file on net rental/timber income and capital cost allowance elections; hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2/18.3) apply mandatorily without an operative opt-out election. Under the Excise Tax Act: s. 156 election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as taxable for GST/HST purposes, and s. 273 joint venture election allowing a designated operator to account for GST/HST on behalf of the venture.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (aggregate worldwide taxable supplies of the person and associates in the current and preceding four calendar quarters). A non-resident carrying on a commercial activity in Canada must register once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is available, including for non-residents making only zero-rated supplies, to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V when transferred in Canada and exported by the supplier or recipient as soon after the supply as is reasonable (CRA generally applies a 60-day benchmark), with export documentation retained. Services to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or specified personal/cultural services. For offshore towage and salvage: services rendered to a non-resident in respect of a vessel situated in Canadian territorial waters remain taxable at 5% (plus HST where applicable) because the vessel is tangible personal property situated in Canada; services performed on the high seas or otherwise outside Canadian waters, or supplies of international marine transport, are eligible for 0% where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is consumed outside Canada. The registrant must obtain a signed non-residency certificate and retain contracts, invoices, and voyage logs to claim full input tax credits and net refunds.

  • Bill of Lading / ocean waybill evidencing carriage outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation evidencing foreign-currency settlement
  • Commercial invoice and executed towage/salvage contract
  • IMO port log / vessel positioning report demonstrating place of service performance

Canada has no real-time tax clearance, fiscal stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime at the federal level. Electronic invoices and records are accepted, but registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE), and large corporations must remit electronically. CRA may require machine-readable records and uses audit-match analytics to verify export zero-rating documentation.

The ETA imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure in this dossier is a documentary benchmark. Zero-rating disallowances are assessed within the normal four-year window (ETA s. 298(1)), which is unlimited for misrepresentation or fraud. Clawback risks include reassessment of the uncollected 5% GST (plus applicable provincial component), denial of input tax credits, daily compound interest under ETA s. 280, gross-negligence penalties of CAD 250 or 50% of the evaded amount (ETA s. 285), criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA s. 327, and director liability under ETA s. 323.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. The Canadian dollar trades freely on a floating exchange-rate basis; there are no surrender requirements, allocation controls, import-export licensing for foreign exchange, or mandatory approval for capital inflows and outflows. Profit remittances from Canadian subsidiaries to foreign shareholders face no exchange-control bottleneck; the only material constraint is tax compliance, particularly Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends and branch remittances (25% statutory, treaty-reduced where applicable), the requirement that distributions be funded from after-tax retained earnings, and enhanced FINTRAC monitoring of large-value cross-border payments. Bermuda/offshore corporate structures should note that funds repatriated through Canada require transparent documentation of source and beneficial ownership to satisfy bank AML/KYC reviews.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation exists in Canada requiring export proceeds to be surrendered, converted, or repatriated within a prescribed timeline. Exporters in the offshore towage and salvage sector may maintain foreign-currency accounts and convert receipts at their commercial discretion. However, for Canadian tax purposes, foreign exchange gains and losses are recognized under section 39 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), and all cross-border receipts exceeding CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds. Banking mandates may impose contractual conversion arrangements for treasury simplicity, but the legal framework imposes no mandatory conversion ratio or timing rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank regulation and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision. Canada has no separate foreign exchange administration or exchange control authority; the Bank of Canada administers foreign currency reserves and monetary policy while the Department of Finance oversees economic and financial policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary payment flows, Low for primary capital escrow and salvage settlement infrastructure. Non-resident fintech digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut Business may operate in Canada only as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and, where providing payment services to Canadian clients, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) overseen by the Bank of Canada effective for registration from November 2024. These entities cannot hold a deposit-taking banking license, cannot provide Canadian-dollar bank accounts, and generally rely on partnerships with Schedule I banks for settlement. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections from the ultimate foreign parent to the Canadian corporation should be executed exclusively as SWIFT MT103 customer-credit transfers through a Canadian Schedule I operating account, with the Canadian bank receiving BIC and the parent's remitting bank acting as the instructing financial institution. The wire must include a clear corporate reference such as 'Capital Contribution pursuant to Board Resolution dated [date]' in field 70, and should be linked to a downloaded board resolution and share subscription agreement held in the account file. For statistical and tax reporting, inbound equity should be classified using Statistics Canada's Balance of Payments framework as 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital', and the Canadian corporation should issue common shares or contribute paid-in surplus to match the wire. Verification steps include: (i) pre-notification SWIFT MT103 to the receiving relationship manager; (ii) AFX-FATCA self-certification confirming non-US beneficial ownership if applicable; (iii) FINTRAC Large-Cross-Border Transaction reporting by the bank if funds originate from outside Canada; and (iv) reconciliation of the deposited amount against the escrow statement and the Canadian Revenue Agency Schedule 50/51 foreign reporting thresholds. No CAD conversion is required because Canada imposes no surrender rule, but the bank may require a purpose-of-settlement letter for liquidity reporting.Outbound treasury settlements for towage and salvage operations should be routed through SWIFT-standard rails: (i) equipment bunker fuel payments, subcontractor towage fees, and port disbursements via MT103 with SWIFT UETR; (ii) interbank transfers to consortium members and lenders via MT202/MT202COV; (iii) import and export trade finance using commercial letters of credit (LC) opened as MT700, with MT798 advice and MT799 for amendments, for major capital acquisitions; and (iv) domestic CAD disbursements using Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) or Canadian-dollar Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day irrevocable settlement. For higher-risk maritime payments, banks may require invoice, bill of lading, AIS position, and disbursement account agreements to be uploaded through the bank's trade portal. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary payment flows, Low for primary capital escrow and salvage settlement infrastructure. Non-resident fintech digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut Business may operate in Canada only as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and, where providing payment services to Canadian clients, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) overseen by the Bank of Canada effective for registration from November 2024. These entities cannot hold a deposit-taking banking license, cannot provide Canadian-dollar bank accounts, and generally rely on partnerships with Schedule I banks for settlement. For an offshore towage and salvage corporate treasury, fintech EMIs are acceptable for low-value supplier disbursements, employee expense wires, and FX execution, but they do not substitute for regulated Canadian bank accounts for capital escrow, SWIFT-based trade settlements, or commercial standby letters of credit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 reportable transactions threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length transactions with non-residents per taxation year. Country-by-Country Reporting under Form RC5208 applies when the multinational group has consolidated revenue equal to or above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone offshore Economic Substance Regulation regime; instead, commercial substance is tested through common law 'sham' doctrine, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 ITA), and permanent establishment / central management and control analysis. A towage and salvage entity must prove actual decision-making, asset ownership, crew administration and operational dispatch in Canada; otherwise profits may be reallocated to the offshore operator and treaty protections may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Part I Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239(1): criminal fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to five years; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; CRA may freeze bank accounts and arrest vessels

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholdings; courts may pierce the corporate veil, order civil restitution, impose travel bans, and direct imprisonment for participating in or directing evasive schemes
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty under ITA s.247(3): 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, or 10% of the gross adjustment where no documentation exists, plus reassessment, compounded interest, and disallowance of deductions; CRA may also impose additional administrative exposure for non-filing of T106

Director personal liability: Directors are not strictly liable for s.247 penalty solely for documentation failure; however, if the documentation gap is part of a sham or treaty-shopping arrangement, the corporate veil may be pierced and personal civil restitution or criminal liability may follow
GAAR / Treaty Abuse / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s.245 ITA, denial of treaty benefits, reinstatement of 25% Part XIII withholding tax on recharacterised amounts, plus s.163 penalties, interest, and restrictive assessments under the anti-avoidance provisions

Director personal liability: Directors who arrange the conduit structure and cause funds to be distributed outside Canada while liabilities remain unpaid face personal liability under s.227.1; travel restrictions, asset freezes, and imprisonment may apply where concealment or fraudulent conveyance is proven
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance (Medium-Risk Enhanced)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 per violation, compliance orders, licence revocation under federal marine shipping rules, and possible criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally fined up to C$500,000 for repeated due-diligence failures; in cases of knowingly laundering proceeds, imprisonment up to five years, personal restitution orders, asset freeze mandates, and travel bans are enforceable
