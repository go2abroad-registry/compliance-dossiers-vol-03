Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Federal Government of Canada; CRA My Business Account portal available at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Accounts) to the CRA. The partnership may register directly with a foreign address and an authorized Canadian representative. The statutory identifier for indirect-tax purposes is the BN plus the GST/HST program account. Because a limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax, each partner reports its share of income; the partnership itself files T5013 information returns where required.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; a Canadian limited partnership is treated as a flow-through aggregate for income tax, with no entity-level federal income tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; a Canadian limited partnership is treated as a flow-through aggregate for income tax, with no entity-level federal income tax. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is separately treated as a registrant for taxable supplies made through the partnership.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box election. The partnership may elect to be GST/HST registered even if it is a small supplier, may choose a non-calendar fiscal period in accordance with the Income Tax Act, and may make certain elections regarding partnership allocations and returns. Hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act (sections 12.7 and 18.4 and related provisions) may deny unintended deduction or inclusion outcomes for cross-border hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (including worldwide taxable supplies by the person and associated persons) over any trailing twelve consecutive months; a non-resident supplier whose taxable supplies into Canada exceed this threshold must register, although zero-rated exporters may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property exported by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, including hard-enamel lapel pins shipped from Canada to non-resident customers. Certain services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated if not for use in Canada. The exporter must maintain contemporaneous evidence that the goods left Canada and that the supply was made to a non-resident.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export documentation where an export declaration is required, or carrier shipment records where low-risk goods are exported by courier or postal service
  • Commercial invoice stating the supplier, purchaser, terms of sale, description of goods, and foreign destination
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier manifest, or postal record showing Canadian origin and foreign delivery
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent bank settlement record confirming receipt of foreign payment

No federal real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. Electronic invoices are permissible if they meet the information requirements of the Excise Tax Act. Tax filing is electronic via GST/HST NETFILE and CRA APIs, but invoices do not require pre-clearance, continuous transaction controls, or government-approved digital stamping.

CRA may scrutinize export documentation and, if zero-rating is not substantiated, reassess the supply as taxable GST/HST. The exporter would be liable for the tax, arrears interest, and civil penalties, and any input tax credits or refunds claimed in connection with the supply may be clawed back. False or fraudulent zero-rating claims may also lead to gross-negligence penalties and, in serious cases, criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act. While Canadian federal law does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is applied here as the internal documentary and treasury control standard for export substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is a fully convertible, freely floating currency and Canada adheres to Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange controls, no central-bank approval for conversions, and no capital account restrictions on capital contributions, loan repayments, or profit distributions. Current-account and capital-account transfers may be executed in any currency without quantitative limits. Profit remittance to non-resident limited partners is unrestricted once Canadian tax obligations are satisfied: the LP must file T5013 partnership returns, remit Part I tax on Canadian-source business income allocated to non-resident partners, and no additional Part XIII withholding applies to distributions of taxed partnership income. Interest, management fees, or royalties paid to non-resident parties would attract Part XIII withholding at treaty-reduced rates. Practical remittance hurdle: the clearing bank will require tax-compliance evidence (T5013, NR4 where applicable, CRA clearance statements) before processing recurring cross-border distributions to partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds under Canadian law. The LP may retain export receipts indefinitely in CAD or foreign-currency bank accounts. The Currency Act designates CAD as legal tender for domestic settlement but does not mandate conversion of foreign-currency receivables. The only statutory obligations are transactional: all international wire transfers of CAD $10,000 or more, and all large cash transactions of CAD $10,000 or more, must be reported to FINTRAC by the financial institution; the LP itself must maintain export transaction records for 7 years under the Income Tax Act and PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority under the Bank of Canada Act; prudential bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF regulatory oversight by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange control administration; foreign exchange transactions are unrestricted.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium — viable only as an overlay, not as the primary settlement account. Canada does not recognize the EEA-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence; foreign digital banks (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, PayPal) operating in Canada must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, cannot take deposits under the Bank Act, and are not CDIC-insured. Etsy Direct Pay and Stripe may settle directly to a Schedule I bank account or to an MSB-issued account. Recommended architecture: retain RBC or TD as the liquidity hub; use an MSB/EMI overlay solely for high-volume micro-disbursements, supplier top-ups, and low-value FX conversions; sweep MSB balances to the Tier-1 bank daily. Legal constraint: MSBs are subject to FINTRAC KYC, reporting, and holding-account rules; funds held with an MSB are uninsured corporate funds and may create segregation risk upon insolvency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No exchange-control approval is required on the Canadian side for inbound wires. Protocol: the non-resident partner instructs its remitting bank to send a SWIFT MT103 to the LP's CAD operating account, inserting in field 70 the exact remittance reference: 'CALLED CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - LIMITED PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT DATED [DATE] - [LEGAL NAME OF PARTNER]'. To prevent OFAC/FINTRAC flags, the LP must pre-submit to its Canadian bank a Source-of-Funds for Capital Call letter, an executed capital-contribution schedule, and identity/source-of-funds documentation for cumulative transfers exceeding CAD $50,000. All incoming international wires of CAD $10,000 or more are automatically reported to FINTRAC by the receiving bank. Etsy Direct Pay settles in CAD via direct deposit to the LP's Canadian operating account, and Stripe settles to the same CAD or USD account; both must be reconciled monthly against the marketplace sales ledger. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (LPs are fiscally transparent; entity-level corporate tax does not apply, income is allocated to partners and taxed at partner level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; treaties commonly reduce to 15% (portfolio) or 5% (10%+ voting/controlled); the rate applies to cash dividends, deemed dividends, and stock dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000 annual aggregate value of reportable cross-border non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents (Form T106 requirement); contemporaneous documentation must be prepared and available by the filing deadline. CbC report threshold: parent revenue ≥ CA$750 million. No separate Master File/Local File filing threshold; documentation is required whenever the CA$1M threshold is exceeded.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone ESR code; however, CRA assesses commercial substance under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), the underlying 'bona fide' and 'sham' doctrines, and the place-of-effective-management concept. The LP must carry on real economic activities (production, order fulfillment, stock management) and maintain substantive records to withstand GAAR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Willful Evasion)

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded (minimum CA$5,000) and imprisonment up to 5 years; plus civil reassessment and gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors who assisted or connived are jointly and severally liable under s.160 for tax and penalties; criminal convictions trigger travel bans and extradition agreements; imprisonment applies to the accused personally.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the OECD BEPS Principal Purpose Test; GAAR reassessment and penalty of 25% of the tax avoided; registry strike-off under federal AND provincial law.

Director personal liability: CRA can pierce the corporate veil to hold promoters and general partners personally liable; bans on serving as director; civil restitution plus criminal sanctions for fraudulent incorporation.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment to Canadian taxable income plus a 10% penalty on the net understatement, with a CA$5,000 minimum penalty, under ICTA s.247(3); statutory interest and compounding penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP documentation may face criminal liability; CRA may seek civil recovery against directors who caused the mispricing to strip profits from the Canadian entity.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Tax (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: Arrears of withholding tax plus interest; penalty under s.227(9): 10% of the unremitted amount (20% if deliberate); the partnership's assets are subject to legal seizure (negotiable securities, bank account garnishment).

Director personal liability: Directors and partners are personally liable under s.227.1 for unremitted amounts if they did not exercise due diligence; individuals may face travel bans for substantial arrears and director disqualification.
AML / Money-Laundering Compliance (Payment Rails)

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act penalties: administrative penalties up to CA$2 million for non-compliance; criminal forfeiture of derived proceeds and charges under the Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors/officers who facilitated money laundering face individual criminal liability, extradition risk, and permanent bans on FINTRAC-regulated activities.
