Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-registration-online

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada, first obtain a prescribed corporate existence record from Corporations Canada (if federally incorporated) or the relevant provincial registry (if provincially incorporated), then register with CRA by filing Form RC1 directly or Form RC465 (Business Number Registration for Non-Resident Corporations). A Business Number (BN) with applicable Program Accounts (GST/HST, Payroll, Corporation Income Tax) is issued through CRA BRO or by written application through an authorised representative. The BN is the statutory TIN; the GST/HST Program Account is the VAT-style identification used for all GST/HST filings and invoicing. Before invoicing Canadian customers, the entity must be registered and must notify CRA if carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada that are not incident to a foreign enterprise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian-incorporated business corporation is always a separate taxpayer from its shareholders; there is no check-the-box pass-through classification. Non-resident corporations are also taxed at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act but only on Canadian-source employment income, Canadian-source business income, and gains on dispositions of taxable Canadian property. A branch of a non-resident corporation is not a separate legal entity but is treated as a Canadian permanent establishment for tax purposes. Federal territorial scope: resident corporations are subject to worldwide tax; non-resident corporations are subject to Canadian tax only on Canadian-source income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Canadian corporations are not eligible for pass-through classification. Available statutory elections relevant to classification include election as to the fiscal period (s. 249.1 of the Income Tax Act), the s. 216(1) election for non-resident owners of Canadian rental land, and the s. 219 election for non-resident corporations in respect of Canadian branch tax. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are not electable: Canada applies the anti-hybrid rules of ss. 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act and denies deduction matching on a mechanical basis, with no elective override. Treaty residence is determined by applicable tie-breaker provisions and competent authority procedures rather than by taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or cumulative over four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident corporation making taxable supplies in Canada must register once it ceases to be a small supplier (threshold CAD 30,000). Voluntary registration is available where the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada even below threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: the supply must be made by way of sale and the supplier must export the goods as part of the supply in accordance with an export contract originating in Canada; the goods must be delivered to a destination outside Canada, or removed from Canada within 60 days, and evidence of export must be in the carrier's or exporter's records. Services: offshore and digital services, including API data access, web scraping, and proxy gateway services supplied to a non-resident, may be zero-rated under Section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act, provided the service is not performed wholly in Canada and is not for use in relation to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property ordinarily situated in Canada. The recipient must be a non-resident who is neither registered under the GST/HST nor acquiring the service for use in Canada in a commercial activity. Zero-rating is denied where the service is supplied in respect of Canadian-based underlying data or to a Canadian operator or if the supply is made through a Canadian permanent establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A) or approved carrier electronic export declaration
  • Commercial invoice containing description, value, seller and buyer details, terms of sale and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing goods departed from Canada and consigned to a non-resident
  • Packing list and container tracking or rail/truck carrier proof
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/proof of payment evidence for export proceeds
  • Non-resident customer contract, purchase order and non-resident status record, including registered or exempt status under GST/HST
  • Company records substantiating the offshore delivery of digital API services or scraping-output data, including server logs, API access records and downstream use attestations

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance framework such as the Latin American CFDI model. No e-invoicing clearance API or mandatory digital stamping exists at federal level. GST/HST invoices may be issued in electronic form if all prescribed invoice data is retained and readable; recipients and suppliers must preserve records for six years. Mandatory electronic GST/HST filing applies to most registrants above the prescribed threshold and to all entities using CRA partner systems, but this is not an invoice-level clearance requirement. A registered entity must include on each supply invoice its GST/HST registration number and the tax status and amount of GST/HST charged or zero-rated.

CRA may reassess an export zero-rated supply within the normal reassessment period, or at any time where misrepresentation, negligence, wilful default or fraud is established. Where export evidence is missing or insufficient, the supplier becomes liable for the uncollected GST/HST on the full consideration, plus interest, and will be denied input tax credits underpinning the export. Penalties include: 5% of any understated net tax plus 5% per month on unreported amounts (ETA s. 280), 25% + penalty on gross negligence penalties (ETA s. 285.1), and 40% penalty for failure to retain mandatory records (ITA s. 162(11),(11.5)). Criminal prosecution is possible for knowingly issuing false or fraudulent zero-rated invoices or for tax evasion, resulting in fines up to CAD 200,000 or 200% of the tax evaded, and imprisonment up to five years. CRA also scrutinises delayed foreign exchange repatriation; although the Excise Tax Act does not codify a fixed 180-day deadline, a prolonged non-repatriation of export proceeds will be treated as a shareholder benefit, loan or foreign affiliate receivable under applicable Income Tax Act provisions, and may trigger imputed interest, dividend recharacterisation and transfer-pricing adjustments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD, ISO 4217) is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions; there are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits by a Canadian corporation to its non-resident shareholders. The Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1, 5th Supp.) Part XIII imposes a statutory withholding tax of 25% on dividends paid to non-residents, treaty-reduced (e.g., 5% under the Canada-United States and Canada-United Kingdom treaties for a corporate shareholder beneficially owning at least 10% of the voting shares). In-bound non-resident equity injections trigger review only under the Investment Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 28, 1st Supp.) when enterprise-value thresholds are met (WTO-investor review threshold adjusted annually, approximately CAD 1.35 billion for 2025; lower thresholds apply to state-owned enterprises and cultural businesses). No currency convertibility hurdles exist for the receipt of USD, USDC-converted CAD, or the settlement of export invoices; cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. A Canadian exporter of services (including API gateway and data-pipeline services) may hold USD or other foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts without surrendering receipts to the Bank of Canada. The only obligations are (i) FINTRAC reporting for electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, (ii) accurate conversion of foreign-currency revenue into CAD for income tax filings using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or the CRA published annual average rate, and (iii) normal commercial record-keeping for invoices and payment evidence. There is no requirement to convert export receipts within a prescribed period or to maintain any percentage of proceeds in CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), in cooperation with the Department of Finance Canada and the Minister of Finance, who manage the Exchange Fund Account. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises Schedule I banks for prudential conduct, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/CTF registration and reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains a fully floating exchange-rate regime with no forex-control statute currently in force.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for use as the primary operating bank, MEDIUM-HIGH only as an ancillary collection and FX-conversion rail. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK Financial Conduct Authority-authorised, European Economic Area licensed, or Bahrain CBB-licensed electronic money institutions) are legally constrained in Canada: a non-bank entity cannot accept deposits or clear CAD through Lynx or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) without obtaining a bank charter under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) or a provincial credit-union licence. An offshore EMI may provide foreign-currency payment services into Canada only if it registers with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the PCMLTFA, maintains a Canadian agent, and implements a full Canadian AML program. For this CA_CORP entity, the recommended treasury architecture is: Stripe (authorised merchant acquirer) for global card and USDC receipt conversion into CAD, with settlement directly to a Schedule I bank account; an offshore EMI such as Wise or Airwallex may be used marginally for cross-border vendor disbursements in USD/EUR, but must not become the sole clearance channel because of FINTRAC MSB filing obligations, volatility in payment-rail compliance, and the medium-risk enhanced AML classification of the data-scraping business.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal Part I general corporate rate (base rate 38%, less 10% provincial abatement, less 13% General Rate Reduction); provincial corporate income tax of 8% to 16% applies where a permanent establishment exists

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited by a resident corporation to a non-resident; treaty reductions apply, including 5% under the Canada-USA Convention for a 10%+ corporate beneficial owner and 15% in all other cases, with similar 10% to 15% rates under the Canada-UK, Canada-Germany, and Canada-Japan treaties

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed registration threshold in CAD; section 247(4) requires the taxpayer to prepare contemporaneous documentation and to produce all relevant records within 90 days of a CRA written demand. The transfer-pricing penalty under subsection 247(3) is 10% of the net adjustment where the aggregate adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation. Substance is assessed through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), the common-law sham doctrine, and the OECD Multilateral Instrument limitation-on-benefits rules, which re-characterize conduit financing and treaty-shopping structures; a no-staff, no-office non-resident holding corporation will be disregarded for Part XIII purposes and the actual beneficial owner's residence will govern the withholding tax rate

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud (Income Tax Act section 239)

Statutory sanction: Gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the federal tax understated; criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to 5 years for indictable offences and up to 2 years for summary conviction, fines without limit, plus interest on all unpaid amounts

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or acquiesced in the misstatement are jointly and severally liable for the penalty and the taxes under section 160; the CRA may pursue personal assets, freeze bank accounts, and obtain a travel-ban order under section 192 pending collection
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure (section 247)

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; the CRA may substitute the arm's-length price and charge interest from the first assessment year

Director personal liability: A 90-day documentation demand failure combined with omission of the T106 constitutes wilful participation in misrepresentation, exposing directors to a culpable-conduct penalty under subsection 163.2 of CAD 100,000 or more, plus personal restitution where the director knowingly signed false particulars
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse / Hybrid Mismatch

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule or the treaty principal-purpose test denies the reduced withholding rate; Part XIII tax is reassessed at 25% on gross amounts; hybrid-mismatch rule disallows the Canadian deduction; administrative penalty up to CAD 100,000 applies for misrepresentation of the treaty position

Director personal liability: Directors who signed a return claiming the treaty rate may be personally assessed under subsection 163.2 for making false statements with penalties up to CAD 100,000; persistent conduit behaviour attracts the same personal restitution and potential travel sanction as tax evasion
