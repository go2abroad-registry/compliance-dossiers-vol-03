Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian-resident federal corporations are taxable persons under the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) and are subject to entity-level federal and provincial income tax; no pass-through or check-the-box eligibility exists for a CBCA business corporation. The 'Canadian-controlled private corporation' (CCPC) status may apply, entitling the corporation to the small business deduction on active business income up to CAD $500,000 (federal effective rate approximately 9% before provincial abatement). Territorial_Scope = worldwide taxation on residence with foreign tax credits under ITA s. 126; qualifying active business income of foreign affiliates is exempt through the s. 113 participation exemption, while passive income remains subject to the foreign accrual property income (FAPI) regime. Canada has no federally designated tax-exempt zones or special economic zones applicable to SaaS.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election or flow-through entity classification is available for CBCA corporations. Available elections relevant to a B2B SaaS exporter: (i) functional-currency election under ITA s. 261 (CAD, USD, GBP or EUR); (ii) capital dividend account election under ITA s. 83(2); (iii) deemed dividend election on winding-up under ITA s. 88; (iv) ITA s. 216 and s. 217 elections for non-resident passive/employment income; (v) small-supplier voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(3); (vi) ETA s. 167 election on transfer of business assets. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed at the federal level through ITA ss. 18.2/18.20 (deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes, excessive interest) and ss. 17.2/18.4 for branch/permanent establishment mismatches; these rules are mandatory and not subject to elective override.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime. Canada imposes no exchange controls under the Currency Act and no administrative approval requirements on current-account payments (SaaS licence fees, subscription disbursements) or capital-account flows (equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation). Profit remittances to non-residents are unrestricted, subject only to Part XIII withholding tax (treaty-reducible, typically 25% statutory/15% under the Canada-US treaty where applicable) and FINTRAC reporting for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. No exchange licence, central-bank permit, or advance repatriation notice is required for a federal Business Corporation operating enterprise SaaS treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist under Canadian federal law. There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation window for export receipts generated from SaaS/software services (ISIC 6201/NAICS 511210). Exporters may hold USD or other foreign-currency balances in local or offshore accounts at their discretion; the only binding obligations are transfer-pricing documentation under subsection 247 of the Income Tax Act and FINTRAC large-currency-transaction reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate after 10% provincial tax abatement and 13% general rate reduction applied to the 38% basic federal rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on gross dividends paid or deemed paid to non-residents, including dividends from undistributed surplus and deemed dividends from beneficial arrangements such as upstream loans to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the corporation's taxation year-end, both for resident and non-resident corporations with a permanent establishment in Canada. The final balance for federal corporate income tax is due within 2 months after year-end for general and non-resident corporations, and within 3 months for eligible CCPCs claiming the small business deduction.
