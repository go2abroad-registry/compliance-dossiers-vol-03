Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership with Canadian taxable activities must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Registration may be completed by the foreign general partner or a designated agent using Form RC1, or via the CRA's online registration service where non-resident eligibility permits. For GST/HST specifically, non-residents use Form RC460 and file by mail/fax. The CRA issues a 15-character BN in the format 123456789RC; the 'RC' account number is the GST/HST registration identifier. The GST/HST registration becomes effective immediately upon issuance, and the registrant must begin charging GST/HST on taxable supplies made in Canada unless the small-supplier threshold is met.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes; income, gains, deductions, and losses flow through to partners. However, for Excise Tax Act (GST/HST) purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and must register in its own capacity if not a small supplier.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Available elections relevant to a foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership include: (i) with respect to rental income from real property, a non-resident partner may file an election under section 216 of the Income Tax Act; (ii) a partnership may designate a 'responsible partner' or tax matters partner for administrative filings; (iii) the partnership and GST/HST registrant partners may jointly elect under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to have a single partner report and remit GST/HST on behalf of the partnership; and (iv) a qualified registrant may elect to use the Simplified Method or Net Tax Calculation Method if eligible. Hybrid-mismatch election mechanisms are not available under Canadian federal tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters, computed on worldwide taxable supplies (including Canada and foreign supplies) for GST/HST small-supplier determination; once exceeded, full GST/HST registration is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exported goods if the goods are sold, shipped, and documented for export to an address outside Canada, including through common carrier, mail, overseas container, or air freight, and the registrant retains evidence that the goods left Canada. For services related to exported robotic interactive AI pet toys, zero-rating applies when the service is supplied to a non-resident person and the service is not performed in Canada, or the service is performed in Canada for non-resident consumers of licensed intellectual property in accordance with Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. The supplier must have full GST/HST registration status and should not hold any intention to recover the goods or services in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (submitted through CERS or equivalent customs system)
  • Bill of Lading or comparable transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Canadian financial institution confirming receipt of foreign funds for the exported goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reflecting the HS/ES classification of the robotic interactive AI pet toy
  • Proof of foreign consignee location and sales contract terms

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, CRA API clearance, or digital stamping for federal GST/HST. Registrants are required to issue invoices containing prescribed disclosure elements (vendor BN, date of invoice, name and address, description of supply, GST/HST charged or deemed charged) and to retain them for six years. Electronic invoicing in EDI, XML, or PDF is valid provided the invoice is accurate and legible. No CRA pre-approval or fiscal stamping is required for ordinary B2B exports.

Where the CRA determines that zero-rating was claimed without genuine export or that documentation was fabricated or misrepresentative, the registrant is retroactively reassessed for the GST/HST not collected, plus daily compound interest from the original reporting date. Penalties may be assessed under the civil enforcement regime of Part IX of the Excise Tax Act, including gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax at issue, and false statements or omission can expose the responsible partner to criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act. CRA can also deny or suspend future zero-rating privileges and require enhanced security. Exporters must preserve all export and foreign-currency-receipt records for six years for audit verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada complies with IMF Article VIII, imposes no exchange-control permits, no quantitative limits, and no profit-remittance approvals. Non-resident partners of a Canadian limited partnership are nonetheless exposed to Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source income allocations and distributions (25% domestic rate, treaty-reducible), administered through T5013 partnership schedules and NR4 returns; capital contributions and their return are non-taxable remittances. The functional remittance hurdle is documentary: banks require a stated underlying purpose, FINTRAC reporting applies to cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, and capital injections above CAD 50,000 trigger source-of-funds and source-of-wealth diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timelines and no mandatory conversion ratios. Exporters may lawfully retain sale proceeds in CAD or any foreign currency, maintain foreign-currency accounts domestically or offshore, and repatriate funds freely. For the CA_LP, the binding obligations are evidentiary: retain commercial invoices, CBSA export/import declarations, bills of lading, ISED radio-equipment certifications, and UL/IEC 62115 toy-safety certifications to support underlying trade; FINTRAC and the LP's bank will screen all cross-border transfers, and no Bank of Canada foreign-exchange position reporting applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of Schedule I banks and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT surveillance; Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign digital EMIs and neo-banks (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Airwallex) are useful ancillary corridors for international supplier payouts, multi-currency wallets, and e-commerce receivable sweeps, but they cannot replace a Schedule I chartered bank in Canada: they are not licensed to accept deposits or clear CAD through RITS/ACSS under the Bank Act, and those acting as payment service providers in Canada may require registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Consequently, EMIs must not hold the LP's capital escrow, issue commercial letters of credit, or serve as the primary treasury and clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are to be remitted by SWIFT MT103 into the segregated Capital Escrow Account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, with Field 70 carrying a purpose text such as '/INTEND/CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — CA_LP' and, where required by the remitting bank, a standard equity-injection reason code. The GP must file a capital-call notice, the updated register of partners, and the partnership agreement before the bank credits the escrow ledger. Incoming EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC; amounts exceeding CAD 50,000 trigger a source-of-funds and source-of-wealth questionnaire. Escrow balances are released only on a GP drawdown certificate and are reconciled to the T5013 capital schedule. Stripe credit-card settlements arrive daily in CAD to the operational account as ordinary operating receipts under Stripe's automated payout rail.Supplier settlements outside Canada are effected through (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wires for routine invoices, denominated in USD or CNH with invoice-specific remittance references, converted on the bank's best-execution electronic FX platform; or (ii) an irrevocable documentary Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Canadian bank via SWIFT MT700 for new or large supplier contracts (conventionally US$100,000 and above), payable at sight against presentation of the commercial invoice, full set of clean bills of lading or airway bills, and inspection certificates.
