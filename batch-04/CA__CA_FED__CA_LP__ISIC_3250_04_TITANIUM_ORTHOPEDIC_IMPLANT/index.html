Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal income tax and the Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) jointly administers import/export client records and customs clearance through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform. Registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA 'My Business Account' at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-number-registration.html.

E-government filing portal

1) Confirm the limited partnership (LP) carries on business in Canada at a permanent establishment; a non-resident LP exporting manufactured goods through a Canadian plant requires extra-provincial registration in the province of the plant and a federal Business Number (BN), the Canada-wide tax identification number. 2) File Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' via BRO (or by fax/mail to the CRA Tax Services Office), marking the entity type as 'Partnership' and completing Schedule C with the names, addresses, CRA-issued BNs or social insurance numbers of every general partner. 3) The CRA issues a nine-digit BN; the LP activates program accounts on the same BN as needed: RT (GST/HST), RD (excise levy if applicable), and RM (CBSA import/export program account) for customs export declarations. 4) A non-resident corporate general partner that derives Canadian-source income must separately register for an RZ corporation income tax account and obtain a certificate of compliance before repatriating remittances. 5) The LP must file an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return and T5013 Summary no later than six months after fiscal year-end even if the partnership's net income is nil. 6) For GST/HST, registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration may be elected earlier to recover input tax credits on plant construction, equipment and titanium raw-material purchases.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no IRS-style 'check-the-box' election; classification follows statutory form. The private limited partnership is automatically opaque? No - automatically transparent for federal income tax (and provincial income tax in harmonized provinces), with each partner paying tax on its allocation of income or loss; the LP itself files only informational returns. A publicly traded or 'specified investment flow-through (SIFT)' partnership is taxed at entity level. Relevant elections include: (i) Income Tax Act section 97(2) joint election allowing a partner to contribute capital property to the LP on a tax-deferred rollover basis; (ii) elections governing partnership fiscal-period recognition and allocation of income, losses and investment tax credits among partners; (iii) voluntary early GST/HST registration under the Excise Tax Act notwithstanding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; (iv) ETA joint election to treat a subsidiary as a 'qualifying subsidiary', simplifying GST/HST inter-corporate supply treatment; and (v) for a non-resident registrant not carrying on business in Canada, ETA section 179 election to deem supplies made outside Canada, removing Canadian GST/HST collection duties. The Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) and the hybrid mismatch rules operate as mandatory disallowances; no elective relief exists other than the EIFEL group-ratio election allowing a taxpayer to substitute the consolidated group's debt-to-equity ratio.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in gross revenue from worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports but excluding exempt supplies, financial services and goodwill) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a partnership associated with other registrants must aggregate associated revenues. Once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory; the threshold is not prorated for part-years.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of titanium hip-joint prosthetics by the Canadian LP is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, sections 1 and 17 of the Excise Tax Act when the goods are delivered to a non-resident customer or consigned to a destination outside Canada by common carrier in the normal course of export trade. GST/HST is charged at 0%, the LP recovers all embedded input tax credits, and no tax is collectible from the foreign buyer. Services performed on the implants outside Canada, and supplies of intangible property such as 3D-print design data to non-residents for use wholly abroad, qualify for zero-rating under separate Part V provisions. Deliveries made inside Canada to Canadian hospitals or distributors (e.g., seed implants or consignment stock) are taxable at the applicable 5% GST / 13% HST rate and cannot be zero-rated. Canada prescribes no statutory FX-repatriation clock; the 180-day settlement benchmark in this dossier is the compliance standard applied by the internal audit model, and all export books and records must be retained for six years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Automated Export Declaration transmitted through the CARM portal showing the export declaration identifier)
  • Bill of Lading or other through-carrier waybill evidencing physical shipment from Canada to a named foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice evidencing foreign-currency settlement of the export sale)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) for GST/HST. Invoicing is privately regulated; taxable suppliers must issue sales invoices containing prescribed information but may issue them in paper or electronic form. The federal government is implementing a voluntary, PEPPOL-aligned e-invoicing framework for business-to-government procurement, and the CRA accepts structured electronic records (XML, EDI and PDF/A) and provides machine-readable APIs for GST/HST returns and BN validation. No ministerial pre-approval of invoices is required. For exports, CBSA mandates electronic filing of the Automated Export Declaration through the CARM portal; postal export documentation may remain paper-based below the export reporting threshold.

Zero-rating success depends entirely on contemporaneous documentary substantiation retained for six years; reconstructed 'export folders' prepared after audit notice are rejected. The CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period under ETA section 298(3), and the limitation period is unlimited for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud. Reassessment reverses the 0% rate, collects the unremitted GST (plus any provincial HST component), compounds daily interest at the prescribed rate, and imposes civil penalties under ETA sections 280 and 285.1: 25% of the understated net tax for false statements or omissions, and no less than CAD 100 and 25% of the additional tax for gross negligence. Fraudulent export-invoice schemes also expose the LP and its general partner to criminal referral for tax evasion, revocation of registration privileges, transfer-pricing and GAAR adjustments, and restitution orders; clawback of input tax credits is automatic where the corresponding supply is recharacterized as taxable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; the Canadian dollar trades in a freely floating regime with no approval, rationing, or surrender constraints. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and no statutory machinery currently exists to block inward or outward transfers of funds, securities, or trade payments. Profit and capital distribution remittances by a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident limited partners are unrestricted from an FX and banking standpoint; however, a statutory 25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax applies to amounts paid or credited to non-resident partners under sections 212 and 215 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), subject to treaty reduction (commonly 10-15% under the Canada-US treaty), and requires Form NR4/NR301/NR302 reporting as well as remittance of the withholding within 15 days of the payment. The bank will treat the remittance as routine only after a valid CRA withholding authorization and the partner's valid foreign tax-residency certificate are on file.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirements apply. Canada imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no foreign-exchange retention cap on export receipts. Export proceeds may be held indefinitely in USD, EUR, or other foreign-currency accounts in Canada or abroad. Compliance obligations are limited to: (i) FINTRAC international EFT reporting for any incoming or outgoing transfer of CAD 10,000 or more (report rendered by the bank within 24 hours); (ii) CBSA Form B13A export declaration under the Customs Act for all exported goods exceeding CAD 2,000 in value, or for controlled, regulated, or permit-required goods (including medical devices) regardless of value, to be filed within 15 days after export; and (iii) transfer-pricing documentation under Income Tax Act section 247 confirming that export prices for titanium orthopedic implants reflect arm's-length consideration.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Department of Finance Canada; foreign exchange control is concurrently administered through OSFI (prudential supervision of Schedule I banks), FINTRAC (AML/EFT reporting), and the Canada Border Services Agency (export declaration enforcement). No separate foreign exchange administration or exchange-control approval body exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viability rating: LOW-TO-MEDIUM as a peripheral collection/payment channel, never as the primary treasury rail. Canadian-regulated fintechs such as Wise Canada Ltd., Wealthsimple, and Koho operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and may execute cross-border payments; they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, accept deposits insured by CDIC, provide capital-call escrow custody, or offer FX forwards in the LP's name. Offshore or unregulated EMIs are not legally viable for the Canadian LP: soliciting Canadian clients or maintaining a cross-border relationship without FINTRAC MSB registration breaches the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and will instantly become a due-diligence red flag for any Schedule I bank. A federally chartered Schedule I bank remains mandatory for statutory capital escrow, trade finance, and sanctioned-counterparty screening.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the foreign investor remits via SWIFT MT103 through its home-bank correspondent with value settling in CAD or USD to the LP's designated Schedule I operating account. Canada publishes no statutory purpose-code table, so the bank relies on the 20-character remittance-information field; the LP must instruct the remitter to enter CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [LP FULL NAME] - SUBSCRIPTION AGREEMENT DATED [DATE] to prevent purpose-code mismatch. International transfers of CAD 10,000 or more automatically generate a FINTRAC EFT report by the receiving institution within 24 hours. For statistical balance-of-payments classification, the bank codes the credit under IMF BPM6 direct-investment equity and the relationship manager must obtain a signed declaration of purpose before funds are credited. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: LOW-TO-MEDIUM as a peripheral collection/payment channel, never as the primary treasury rail. Canadian-regulated fintechs such as Wise Canada Ltd., Wealthsimple, and Koho operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and may execute cross-border payments; they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, accept deposits insured by CDIC, provide capital-call escrow custody, or offer FX forwards in the LP's name. Offshore or unregulated EMIs are not legally viable for the Canadian LP: soliciting Canadian clients or maintaining a cross-border relationship without FINTRAC MSB registration breaches the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and will instantly become a due-diligence red flag for any Schedule I bank. A federally chartered Schedule I bank remains mandatory for statutory capital escrow, trade finance, and sanctioned-counterparty screening.
