Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); official portal: Canada.ca (My Business Account for Businesses and Business Registration Online, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs export reporting.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishes a federal Business Number (BN) by registering with the CRA via Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The BN is the single statutory TIN and serves as the GST/HST account identifier when an RT program account is added. The foreign corporation must provide its certified articles/incorporation certificate (or foreign equivalent), registered office address, Canadian representative details if applicable, directors' information, and a statement of expected taxable activity. For venture capital GP management, the principal activity is classified under NAICS 525910 and the entity must indicate that supplies will consist of management/advisory services. If the entity is not resident in Canada and is not a small supplier, GST/HST registration under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) is mandatory before making taxable supplies in Canada; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits (ITCs). The CRA may require non-resident registrants to post acceptable security (s. 240(2) ETA) and to appoint a Canadian representative if the non-resident does not have a Canadian presence or does not meet residency requirements. Registration is completed through the same BN account; no separate provincial TIN is required at the federal level, although provinces administer their own corporate registry and provincial income tax accounts where a permanent establishment exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian-resident corporations are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA) on worldwide income; a federally incorporated business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer. If Canadian-controlled and private, it may qualify as a CCPC eligible for the small business deduction (federal 9% rate on active business income up to CAD 500,000, subject to phase-down to CAD 200,000 in tax years beginning after 2024). The federal general corporate rate is 15%; provincial/territorial corporate income tax applies where a permanent establishment exists. A non-resident corporation is taxed only on Canadian-source business income and is subject to Part XIII withholding on certain passive payments (e.g., management fees, interest, dividends) unless a tax treaty reduces the rate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Canadian-resident corporations are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA) on worldwide income; a federally incorporated business corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a separate taxpayer. If Canadian-controlled and private, it may qualify as a CCPC eligible for the small business deduction (federal 9% rate on active business income up to CAD 500,000, subject to phase-down to CAD 200,000 in tax years beginning after 2024). The federal general corporate rate is 15%; provincial/territorial corporate income tax applies where a permanent establishment exists. A non-resident corporation is taxed only on Canadian-source business income and is subject to Part XIII withholding on certain passive payments (e.g., management fees, interest, dividends) unless a tax treaty reduces the rate. For GST/HST purposes, the entity is a 'person' and, if registered, a 'registrant'; financial services (e.g., securities issuance, carried interest, loan origination) are exempt supplies under Schedule V, Part VII ETA, while management fees are generally taxable supplies.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows statutory corporate law and the ITA. Available elections relevant to a VC GP: (i) GST/HST closely related corporation election (ETA s. 156) to treat taxable supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration; (ii) GST/HST joint venture election (ETA s. 273) permitting a venturer to account for GST/HST on behalf of a joint venture; (iii) ITA s. 261 functional currency election allowing tax computations in a qualifying functional currency; (iv) ITA s. 85 corporate rollover and amalgamation elections for fund restructuring; (v) ETA s. 211.1 election for qualifying special purpose entities. Hybrid mismatch and excessive interest/financing expense limitations (EIFEL) are mandatory under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.21 for taxation years commencing after September 30, 2023, and are not elective; they restrict the deduction of excessive interest and financing expenses for multinational groups, including Canadian members of a foreign-controlled VC fund group.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 148(1) measured on worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) made in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once the threshold is exceeded in a calendar quarter, registration becomes mandatory effective the first day of the following calendar quarter (ETA s. 240(1)); non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada are subject to the same threshold and, once registered, must charge GST/HST on taxable supplies unless the supply is zero-rated or exempt. Voluntary registration is available immediately to claim ITCs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI ETA if the registrant supplies goods that are exported directly or deemed exported and proof of export is retained. Services supplied to a non-resident person (including cross-border management, advisory, and administrative services rendered by a VC GP) are zero-rated under s. 7 of Part V, Schedule VI ETA unless the non-resident recipient is registered for GST/HST at the time the service is supplied, or the service relates to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Supplies of financial services (e.g., lending, securities issuance, investment fund units, carried interest) are generally exempt under Schedule V, Part VII ETA, not zero-rated, and ITCs on related inputs are not recoverable except to the extent of the registrant's taxable (including zero-rated) commercial activity. To claim zero-rating on management services to a non-resident fund, the GP must demonstrate the non-resident status of the recipient, the absence of Canadian situs of the service, and that the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant. Zero-rated suppliers are eligible for input tax credit refunds of embedded GST/HST because they are not exempt supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency, or an electronic export declaration processed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CESR) with proof of acceptance
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export of goods and delivery outside Canada within the statutory timeframe
  • Commercial invoice and underlying supply contract/agreement detailing the goods or services supplied
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign bank credit advice evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency from the non-resident party
  • Evidence of the non-resident purchaser's status (foreign incorporation certificate, residency declaration, confirmation that the recipient is not registered for GST/HST)
  • Supporting records demonstrating the place of performance for services (e.g., engagement letters, time records, invoices showing services rendered to the non-resident fund)

No mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API stamping, or digital stamping is in force at the federal GST/HST level. The CRA accepts electronic invoices and records. A voluntary Pan-Canadian Electronic Invoice Standard based on Peppol BIS 3.0 has been adopted for interoperable B2B e-invoicing; Quebec has enacted a phased mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting regime under the QST (large enterprises by September 1, 2026; SMEs by 2027), which may affect Canadian GP entities with a Quebec presence. For the federal GST/HST, there is no statutory requirement for pre-clearance of individual invoices.

The CRA rigorously audits export zero-rating claims, particularly services supplied to non-residents and management fees rendered by VC GPs, and will deny zero-rating where documentation is inadequate or the supply is connected with Canadian property. Penalties include: (i) false statements penalty under ETA s. 285 equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax that would have been payable (50% where the misstatement is made knowingly); (ii) daily compound interest under ETA s. 280 on unremitted or overclaimed amounts; (iii) evasion penalties under ETA s. 327 ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment; (iv) gross negligence penalties under ITA s. 163(2) for income tax understatements (10% or 50% of the understated tax); and (v) retrospective clawback of ITCs and zero-rating adjustments for up to 4 years from the date of assessment (ETA s. 298(1)), extended to 7 years or indefinitely in the case of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud (ETA s. 298(2)-(3)). Export proceeds should be repatriated within 180 days of shipment or billing as a prudent evidentiary benchmark; the ITA does not impose a statutory repatriation deadline, but unrepatriated or artificially held receivables may attract transfer pricing adjustments, deemed benefits, or CRA challenge of the underlying commercial substance.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has a floating exchange-rate regime and no foreign exchange controls. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts. Non-resident LPs may lawfully subscribe to a venture capital GP fund, send capital call remittances, receive carried interest or distributions, and exit in CAD or foreign currency without Bank of Canada approval. The only material friction is tax withholding under Income Tax Act Part XIII, typically 25% on dividends, interest, or royalties, reduced under applicable tax treaties, not currency convertibility. Canadian banks require no exchange-control permit but will perform FINTRAC-mandated CDD and international EFT reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. Exporters are not required to surrender foreign currency or to repatriate export receipts within a fixed period. Foreign-currency receipts may be held in domestic or offshore accounts at market rates. For regulated financial institutions, incoming and outgoing international electronic transfers of C$10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange control and AML administration is shared with FINTRAC and OSFI under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and the Bank of Canada Act.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate under ITA s. 123(1.1), calculated as the 38% basic rate minus the 10% provincial abatement and the 13% General Rate Reduction; combined federal-provincial headline rate in Ontario is 26.5% (15% federal + 11.5% provincial) for 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; the balance of tax payable is due two months after year-end (three months for an eligible CCPC); instalment payments are required where the corporation's current-year or prior-year Part I tax payable exceeds CAD 3,000, with monthly instalment due dates for large corporations and quarterly instalments for smaller CCPCs.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the T2 filing deadline, i.e., six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; non-resident filers must observe the same deadline without extension.
