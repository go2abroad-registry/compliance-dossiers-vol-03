Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); official business portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html) and CRA's GST/HST NETFILE; federal corporate registration is administered by Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada); customs export reporting is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Customs Act.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada (including leasing real property such as data centre rack space or earning Canadian-source revenue) must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online via the 'Register a Business' service or by writing to the CRA Business Window. A federal or provincial business corporation formed under the CBCA first obtains a corporation registry number from Corporations Canada or the provincial registry, after which the CRA automatically assigns the BN unless a non-resident corporation opts to register directly. In addition to the BN (RT program account for GST/HST), the applicant must open a corporate income tax account (RC) and may request a payroll account (RP) if it has Canadian employees. Supporting documentation for non-residents includes: certified copy of the certificate of incorporation, register of directors, name and address of the agent for service in Canada, Form RC1A (for persons without Canadian residency), and a completed Form T2SC (Schedule 91 for non-resident corporations) on filing. CRA generally issues program accounts within 5-10 business days, but non-resident registrations are subject to anti-abuse verification under s. 240 of the Excise Tax Act and ITA s. 253 and may require 6-12 weeks when a fiscal representative is mandated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: A Canadian business corporation (CA_CORP) is resident in Canada and taxed on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA), while a non-resident corporation is taxable only on Canadian-source business income, on capital gains from taxable Canadian property, and on Part XIII withholding on certain passive income (ITA s. 2(3), s. 115, s. 253). Canada has no entity pass-through regime for business corporations; a CBCA corporation is a separate taxpayer. There is no check-the-box classification, and an incorporated entity is not eligible for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections for cross-border data centre colocation structures include: (1) ITA s. 216 / s. 216.1 election - a non-resident landlord may elect to file a Part I return on net rental income (rather than the 25% Part XIII gross withholding on rents) if rental amounts constitute income from real property in Canada; the CA_CORP as lessee must withhold Part XIII on lease payments to a non-resident lessor absent a valid s. 216 election. (2) ITA s. 219 - election and branch tax on the Canadian branch's taxable income of a non-resident corporation (Part XIV). (3) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 273 - joint election for closely related corporations to treat otherwise taxable intercompany supplies of services or real property as being at zero consideration (zero-rated) for GST/HST. (4) ETA s. 167 - joint election to treat a transfer of a business or real property as a supply not subject to tax. (5) ETA s. 156 - quick method election for small registrants to simplify input tax credit calculation. (6) Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2 to 18.6) apply automatically; no elective relief exists, and an informational Schedule 88 must be filed where hybrid instruments or structures exist.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold). A non-resident person that makes taxable supplies in Canada (including supplies of real property or services in respect of Canadian real property) must register for GST/HST once its worldwide taxable supplies, including those of associated persons, exceed CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or over the preceding four calendar quarters (ETA s. 240, s. 148). Registration is mandatory regardless of threshold where the person is a non-resident carrying on a business in Canada and supplying services relating to Canadian real property; voluntary registration may be elected to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For a Canadian business corporation providing data centre colocation, zero-rating eligibility is narrowly limited. Under Part V, Schedule VI to the ETA: (i) a supply of goods exported from Canada is zero-rated under s. 20-22 provided the exporter retains proof of physical export within 60 days; (ii) a supply of a service to a non-resident person who is not present in Canada at the time the service is performed is zero-rated under s. 7 only where the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and not in respect of goods situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Because colocation rack space leases, power, and cooling services are performed at and relate to Canadian real property, the core colocation supply is standard-rated (GST/HST) even if the recipient is a non-resident; Budget 2021 amendments (effective July 1, 2021) codified that data centre services supplied in Canada are deemed to be made in Canada and taxable. Only separable and genuinely remote services (e.g., fully offshore remote monitoring software, management services performed entirely outside Canada that do not relate to the customer's Canadian premises) may qualify for s. 7 zero-rating. For any zero-rated export claim, the exporter must maintain: a signed non-resident declaration, the recipient's foreign business address, proof the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant, the supply contract, and payment records. Over-claimed input tax credits relating to zero-rated supplies are recoverable by the CRA.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A Export Declaration or the CBSA electronic Export Reporting System (CAED/G7) record showing HS classification, value, destination, exporter BN and port of exit)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (ocean, air, rail or highway carrier document indicating Canadian port/place of exit and consignee details)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or foreign exchange confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency)
  • Commercial Invoice / GST/HST sales invoice (showing supplier BN, non-resident recipient name and address, supply description, place-of-supply analysis, and tax status)
  • Signed non-resident declaration (confirmed non-resident status and, where applicable, non-registration status under ETA s. 240)
  • Data centre colocation lease or master services agreement (evidencing contractual terms, Canadian premises address, and any separately priced out-of-Canada services)

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. Electronic invoicing is permissible under ETA ss. 223-223.1 and is accepted in EDI, XML, or PDF formats provided the invoice contains the supplier's BN, date, description of supply, GST/HST registration status, rate applied, and tax charged. All GST/HST registrants are required to file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or CRA web services API) and to remit electronically where annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million. CRA's 'My Business Account' portal and the 'Represent a Client' API enable machine-readable return submission and audit data extraction; there is no CRA pre-clearance of individual invoices or mandates for CRA-approved invoicing software.

Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline in the ETA or ITA; the 180-day benchmark reflects CRA audit tolerance for export receivables, and longer outstanding intercompany receivables risk recharacterization as a loan, dividend, or benefit under ITA ss. 15(2), 80.4, and 247, attracting transfer-pricing adjustments. For income tax, the CRA may reassess within 3 years (4 years for the ETA) of the original assessment (ITA s. 152(4); ETA ss. 298-299), extended to 7 years (ETA) or 10 years (ITA) where misrepresentation or fraud exists. Incorrectly zero-rated exports trigger: (a) reassessment to collect the unremitted GST/HST (5% federal, or HST up to 15%); (b) arrears interest at prescribed rates compounded daily (ETA s. 280, ITA s. 161); (c) civil penalties under ETA s. 285.1 of 25% of the net tax at issue for false statements and up to 50% of the tax avoided for gross negligence (ITA s. 163(2)); (d) criminal penalties under ETA s. 327.5 and ITA s. 239 - fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; and (e) denial of input tax credits on the underlying inputs, plus recovery of any refund or rebate previously paid. The general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) may also apply to artificial schemes alleging zero-rating of services actually performed at Canadian data centre premises.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, interest, costs, or invested capital, and no requirement for prior regulatory approval for cross-border remittances. The only statutory hurdle is compliance with tax withholdings, primarily Part XIII withholding tax on certain payments to non-residents, and FINTRAC reporting for wire transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000. The data center colocation leasing model is eligible for unrestricted inbound equity and outbound operational payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion timeline on export proceeds. Corporate exporters may retain export receipts in foreign currency or convert to Canadian dollars at their sole discretion. There are no statutory ratios for forced conversion. Export transactions remain subject only to FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting thresholds for cross-border transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange oversight is shared with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for deposit-taking entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary Canadian-dollar treasury operations. Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) providers are largely unsuitable as the principal operating bank in Canada because only entities licensed under the Bank Act or authorized as foreign bank branches may accept deposit liabilities. Non-bank fintech payment providers can offer limited cross-currency disbursement services but must register with FINTRAC under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). For the colocation leasing model, a foreign EMI may serve as a supplementary channel for non-Canadian-dollar vendor payments, but it cannot clear CAD-denominated tenant collections through Payments Canada without a sponsoring Schedule I bank arrangement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan capital must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 with clear narrative fields; the transaction should be classified for Canadian Balance of Payments purposes as a direct investment capital contribution. The remitting bank and receiving bank must include the corporate legal name and invoice/contract reference in field 70. FINTRAC reportable information is captured by the Canadian receiving institution for all inbound cross-border electronic transfers equal to or above CAD 10,000. Funds are cleared through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for Canadian-dollar credit to the operational account at the Canadian Schedule I bank. Ordinary corporate verification is limited to confirming the capital source is not derived from criminal activity, there is no reason to suspect sanctioned involvement, and tax forms (e.g., T2 and Part XIII returns) are properly filed once income accrues.International vendor disbursements should be executed over SWIFT MT103 for direct wire settlements, particularly for high-dollar purchases of server hardware, cooling systems, and electrical infrastructure. For capital-intensive equipment imports, an irrevocable confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 provides bank-to-bank settlement certainty and favorable documentary controls. Domestic Canadian-dollar payments for utilities, landlord leases, and staffing are efficiently cleared via Payments Canada's LVTS for urgent same-day transfers or through Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batched electronic payments. Tenant collections should be wired via SWIFT MT103 or inward LVTS payment, while smaller monthly colocation invoices may be collected through direct debit (PAD) under Canadian Payments Association rules.Outright deliverable FX forwards on USD/CAD and other G10 currency pairs through Canadian Schedule I banks.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for aligning long-term foreign currency lease obligations with capital and operational payment streams.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currencies where local convertibility is restricted; USD/CAD hedging is normally executed through deliverable forwards.FX swap transactions using standardized ISDA and Canadian Financial Institutions (CSDA) master documentation, subject to credit counterparty limits and OSFI-compliant collateral arrangements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 (aggregate FMV of non-arm's length cross-border transactions per taxation year) — triggers contemporaneous documentation under ITA s. 247(4), including Local File content; Master File aligned with OECD TP Guidelines for groups with total revenue above C$100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to UAE/BVI; the functional equivalents are the GAAR (ITA s. 245), which denies tax benefits lacking a bona fide business purpose, the CBCA significant-control register, treaty anti-abuse provisions (MLI PPT), and the requirement that a treaty resident maintain real substantive presence (place of effective management).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 163(2) 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated tax plus backdated interest; criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1): fine of 50%–200% of the tax evaded, and imprisonment up to 5 years; GST/HST penalties under Excise Tax Act s. 285

Director personal liability: Corporate veil pierced for unremitted source deductions under ITA s. 227.1 and for GST/HST under ETA s. 323; directors are jointly and severally liable with the corporation; a tax-evasion conviction triggers discharge-from-bankruptcy bar, asset seizure, garnishment, and extradition-based travel restrictions
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization (s. 245); denial of DTA benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test; Part XIII reassessment at the full 25% statutory rate; 50% penalty if gross negligence is established; fees for offshore arrangements under s. 237.1 (avoidance transactions)

Director personal liability: A director who signs or certifies a false treaty-residency certificate or misleading T106 is personally exposed to s. 239(1) prosecution; CBCA significant-control register failure triggers fines up to C$200,000 and 6 months imprisonment
Transfer Pricing Mispricing (Intercompany Colocation Leasing)

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(2) adjustment, reducing deductions or recharacterizing transactions; 10% TP penalty under s. 247(3) when the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue and proof of 'reasonable efforts' is absent; interest at prescribed rate; failure to file T106 incurs per-form penalties

Director personal liability: No direct statutory TP penalty on directors, but wilful participation in fraudulent TP documentation constitutes a criminal offence under s. 239(1); directors of the Canadian payer remain s. 227.1 liable for the unremitted Part XIII WHT if the mispricing is structured as a dividend or royalty substitute
