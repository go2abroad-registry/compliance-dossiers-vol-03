Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Central registration and digital filing portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html and My Business Account at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html. Provincial tax administration is separate for Quebec (Revenu Quebec) and provincial PST regimes.

E-government filing portal

Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus required program accounts. Process for a foreign-owned or domestic federal business corporation: (1) Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) via Corporations Canada, or provincially, obtaining the incorporation number; (2) Apply for the BN through Business Registration Online (immediate issuance) or paper Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'; the BN is the single CRA identifier for all tax accounts; (3) Enrol program accounts: RC (Part I corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll, if employees are engaged), and RZ (import/export account if the corporation clears own shipments); (4) GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters; the effective registration date is the earlier of the first taxable sale in Canada and the date the small-supplier threshold is crossed (Excise Tax Act s. 242); (5) A non-resident vendor without a Canadian corporation may directly register under the simplified non-resident GST/HST digital-economy regime using Form RC1 or BRO, with no permanent establishment required; (6) No federal trade licence is a precondition for online retailing of jewellery and watches, so no additional licensing clearance is needed before BN issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections available to a federal business corporation include: (i) ITA s. 85(1) rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of eligible property to the corporation; (ii) ITA s. 97(2) joint election for partnership rollovers; (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election to treat taxable supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration; (iv) ITA s. 261 functional-currency election where the corporation keeps records in a qualifying foreign currency other than Canadian dollars; (v) ITA s. 256 associated-corporations allocation election for the CAD 500,000 small business deduction limit; (vi) ETA s. 243.1 election to fix a non-calendar GST/HST reporting period; (vii) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, 266-269) operate compulsorily and cannot be overridden by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A Canadian-incorporated online retailer is a small supplier exempt from GST/HST registration and collection until total taxable supplies (excluding financial services) exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Once crossed, registration is compulsory. Non-resident e-commerce vendors supplying cross-border goods, digital services or platform-mediated supplies to Canadian consumers are subject to the same CAD 30,000 threshold under the July 2021 digital-economy rules and, upon crossing it, must register under the simplified non-resident GST/HST regime even without a permanent establishment in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated under Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V. Tangible personal property (jewellery, watches) sold online to non-residents is zero-rated where the goods are supplied by way of sale and exported as part of the supply, with export occurring as soon as is reasonably possible after possession transfers, normally evidenced by carrier outbound movement. Services supplied to non-residents (e.g., SaaS, online advertising, offshore fulfilment-logistics management) are zero-rated when not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of performance. Intra-Canada supplies of finished jewellery/watches are taxable at the applicable GST/HST rate; only investment-grade precious-metal bullion (refined gold wafers per ETA Schedule VI Part VII) receives a narrower zero-rating, which is not relied upon for fashion jewellery.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (eManifest / Form B13A) required for shipments of CAD 2,000 or more; for lower-value e-commerce parcels, carrier-generated export manifests and tracking records satisfy the export-evidence requirement.
  • Commercial Invoice, packing list, and transport document (ocean bill of lading, air waybill, courier waybill, or Canada Post epacket manifest) naming the non-resident consignee and the foreign destination, plus proof of delivery (Delivered status or destination-customs out-turn report).
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT/SEPA credit advice, bank foreign-exchange contract, or payment-processor settlement report (Stripe/PayPal) showing receipt of funds in CAD or foreign currency from the non-resident buyer; a formal Inward Remittance Certificate can be issued by a Canadian chartered bank on request for foreign-currency export transactions.

No real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API, digital stamping or continuous transaction control mandate is in force in Canada. Statutory e-filing requirements: GST/HST registrants with annual taxable revenue above CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE); corporate income tax (T2) returns must be filed electronically when gross revenue exceeds CAD 1 million. The Pan-Canadian E-invoicing framework (PDX) was launched on 1 April 2024 using PEPPOL BIS 3.0; participation is voluntary and access points/interoperable exchanges are used. Electronic invoices and underlying source records (serial numbers for fine timepieces, provenance certificates for diamond-set jewellery) must be retained for six years and produced on audit.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export within the normal four-year period, extended to seven years for misrepresentation attributable to carelessness, neglect or wilful default, and without time limit where fraud is alleged. Failed substantiation triggers reassessment of the sale at the domestic GST/HST rate (5% to 15%), disallowance of related input tax credits, compounded daily interest (ETA s. 280), and a 25% civil penalty (minimum CAD 250) for false or grossly negligent statements (ETA s. 285). Late filing attracts a 1% initial penalty plus 0.25% per month up to 12 months (ETA s. 281). False export documentation may invoke CBSA penalties up to CAD 25,000 per offence and seizure of goods. Criminal evasion under ETA s. 327 is indictable, with fines up to 200% of the amount evaded and imprisonment up to five years; equivalent income-tax fraud exposure (ITA s. 239) also applies where export revenue is under-reported. Records substantiating export must be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. No exchange-control approval is required for investment capital, trade settlement, loan repayment, or profit remittance. Dividend repatriation is subject solely to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, reduced to 5% or 15% under the Canada-U.S. and other tax treaties) and to solvency tests under corporate law. Commercial banking practice remains the sole gatekeeper through KYC, source-of-funds verification, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Export proceeds are freely retainable in any currency with no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. Exporters must, however, include receivables in taxable business income under accrual accounting rules and provide documentary support where requested by CRA. International EFT receipts exceeding CAD $10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC within prescribed timeframes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and lender of last resort; prudential FX supervision by Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT surveillance and cross-border EFT reporting by FINTRAC; no dedicated foreign-exchange control board exists. Canada applies a free-floating exchange-rate regime under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act, with no centralized FX licensing or registration requirement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High viability as a secondary disbursement rail. Canadian-registered fintech providers (e.g., Wise Payments Canada Inc.) operating with FINTRAC Money Service Business authorization may offer cross-border settlement in multiple currencies but cannot replace a tier-1 operating account due to regulatory constraints: no escrow facility, no credit lines, and transaction limits. Unregistered offshore EMIs are Low viability and legally prohibited from providing payment services in Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which mandates registration with the Bank of Canada before operating. Using unregistered foreign EMIs also creates FINTRAC non-compliance risk for the entity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal Part I general corporate income tax rate, before provincial/territorial top-ups ranging from 0% to 16%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident recipient, imposed under subsection 212(2) of the Income Tax Act on the gross dividend amount.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed within 6 months after the taxation year-end (Income Tax Act subsection 150(1)). Balance-due payment deadline is 2 months after year-end, or 3 months for a CCPC entitled to the Small Business Deduction for the year. Interest accrues on late payments from the balance-due date at CRA prescribed daily compound rates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes no standalone monetary trigger for contemporaneous Local File or Master File documentation; however, the transfer-pricing penalty under subsection 247(3) of 10% of the adjustment applies where the aggregate value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 5,000,000. Form T106 must be filed for reportable transactions regardless of amount, and Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million (approximately CAD 1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; substance is tested through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, subsection 245(3) of the Income Tax Act), the artificial transaction doctrine, and common-law sham and agency principles. Transactions lacking bona fide business purpose are recharacterized, associated corporation rules allocate a single CAD 500,000 small business limit among related entities, and 'dummy corporation' conduits are collapsed or denied deductions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) equals 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or acquiesce in the evasion are treated as parties to the offence and face imprisonment and shared monetary fines; CRA pierces the veil using the section 160 transfer rule and section 227.1 director liability, while courts may freeze personal assets and impose travel restrictions during criminal proceedings.
Shell Company and Artificial Avoidance Transactions

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment denies the tax benefit and recharacterizes the arrangement; transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the adjustment applies when transactions exceed CAD 5,000,000; associated corporation rules disallow the small business deduction; section 160 assessment recovers the fair market value shortfall from the non-arm's length transferee.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when the foreign entity is found to be a pure conduit or agent, making individual directors and shareholders liable for the full corporate tax arrears and penalties; directors also face criminal exposure for conspiracy to defraud the CRA, with civil restitution, asset freezing, and travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA / FINTRAC) Breaches

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act up to CAD 500,000 per violation for corporations, plus daily accrual for continued non-compliance; laundering proceeds of crime under Criminal Code section 462.31 is an indictable offence carrying up to 10 years imprisonment, asset forfeiture, and confiscation of the e-commerce business proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize, or participate in the laundering are criminally liable as parties; they face up to 10 years imprisonment, personal forfeiture orders, civil confiscation, and court-imposed travel bans or interim seizure orders to secure attendance and recovery.
GST/HST and Source Deduction Trust Fund Misappropriation

Statutory sanction: Penalty under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act of 5% of the net GST/HST payable plus 1% per month up to 12 months; prescribed daily compound interest on all arrears; CRA may garnish receivables, freeze bank accounts, and register liens; director assessments recover the full trust fund amounts.

Director personal liability: Subsection 227.1 of the Income Tax Act and subsection 323(1) of the Excise Tax Act impose strict joint and several liability on directors for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST even without fraud; directors are personally pursued via civil garnishment, asset seizure, and court-imposed travel bans while trust-fund amounts remain unpaid.
