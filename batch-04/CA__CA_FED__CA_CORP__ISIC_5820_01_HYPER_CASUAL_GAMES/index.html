Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); secured web portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/canada-revenue-agency-online-services.html); Business Registration Online (BRO) for real-time Business Number and program account creation; non-resident filing through the CRA Non-Resident Tax program or a Canadian tax representative.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation that will carry on business in Canada or remit GST/HST must first obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Where the entity is incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act or a provincial equivalent, the BN is issued at incorporation or extra-provincial registration. For a foreign entity with no Canadian incorporation, the standard procedure is to file Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number and certain accounts', together with certified constitutional documents, foreign registration certificates, and completed authorization if a representative will file. The BN is then activated for separate program accounts: GST/HST, corporate income tax, import/export, and payroll. A non-resident supplier of digital services or intangible property may register for the simplified GST/HST reporting regime, using the same RC1 or the dedicated non-resident GST/HST registration portal, and must retain all support in English or French acceptable to the CRA.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian-resident business corporation is taxed as a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on its worldwide taxable income. There is no federal C-corporation/S-corporation-style pass-through status for a business corporation. A corporation that is neither resident nor carrying on business in Canada remains subject to federal tax only in respect of proceeds of disposition of taxable Canadian property and resource income earned in Canada. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian-resident business corporation is taxed as a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on its worldwide taxable income. There is no federal C-corporation/S-corporation-style pass-through status for a business corporation. A corporation that is neither resident nor carrying on business in Canada remains subject to federal tax only in respect of proceeds of disposition of taxable Canadian property and resource income earned in Canada. A Canadian-controlled private corporation may access the small business deduction, while a private corporation may be subject to the refundable dividend tax on hand rules.

Available election forms

No Canadian equivalent of a US check-the-box election exists; corporate classification follows domestic corporate law and Income Tax Act article, with no elective transparency for business corporations. Statutory elections relevant to hybrid mismatch and intercompany arrangements include the offsetting adjustment election under ITA ss. 18.2(6) and 18.3(3) to neutralize double-counting relief when hybrid mismatch rules have denied a deduction, and elections regarding deemed residence of a corporation under the Canada-US tax treaty. Useful tax elections also include fiscal period election under s. 249.1 for a non-calendar year end, accelerated capital cost allowance elections where permitted, section 216 elections for non-residents with rental income from Canadian real estate, and the non-arm's length transfer pricing documentation election? No general entity-classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada in any single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). Non-resident suppliers of digital services and intangible property to Canadian customers are required to register for GST/HST once the same CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded; platform operators may be deemed to make the supply. A registrant is then required to collect and remit 5% federal GST, plus provincial HST component where applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies where the supply of exported goods, services, or intangible personal property is made in Canada and the destination/place-of-supply rules deem the supply to be outside Canada. Hyper-casual gaming software licensed or sold to non-resident publishers and app-store operators is zero-rated where the software is to be used wholly outside Canada and the Canadian supplier has obtained the non-resident recipient's name, address, country of residence, and GST/HST registration status (if any). For services such as ad inventory mediation, performance marketing, and backend game support supplied to a non-resident advertiser or platform, zero-rating is available if the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant and the supply is not in respect of Canadian real estate or goods situated in Canada. For physical promotional merchandise exported from Canada, zero-rating requires export to be completed by the supplier within a reasonable time and documentary proof of export. The supplier must obtain the underlying transactional evidence before filing the net tax remittance in which the zero-rated supply is reported.

  • Customs Export Declaration / B13 Canada Customs Coding Form filed with the Canada Border Services Agency where goods are exported
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and complete product/game service description
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing foreign destination and export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/FX credit advice evidencing settlement in foreign currency from the non-resident counterparty
  • Non-resident status evidence and GST/HST registration status of the recipient, including contracts, purchase orders, and end-user licence agreements
  • Export shipment records, customs broker acknowledgements, and internal shipping/dispatch logs retained in the CRA books-and-records file

No real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-clearance API, or digital stamping mandate exists at the federal level in Canada. CRA does not operate a continuous transaction control regime. Electronic invoices are acceptable if they preserve integrity, authenticity, and readability, and all books and records required by ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230 must be retained for at least six years. Voluntary e-invoicing networks based on Peppol or EDI are permitted; CRA may request invoice-level data during audits but does not require pre-clearance or fiscal validation before issuance.

CRA may audit zero-rating claims by testing place-of-supply, non-resident status, and the documentary trail for consideration received. Where a supply is found not to be zero-rated, the supplier is assessed for uncollected GST/HST, plus daily compound arrears interest from the date the return was due. The supplier may also lose previously claimed input tax credits or refunds. Civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act apply to false statements and omissions; a gross negligence scenario can attract a penalty, while conviction for wilful evasion can give rise to fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and, in serious cases, imprisonment. CRA's normal reassessment period is four years, but misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default removes the limitation period and permits retrospective clawback without time limit. Export zero-rating claims should be retainable for six years from the end of the reporting period, with augmented records when cross-border technology transactions are involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized, free-floating FX regime with no exchange-control licensing, no capital-account approval gates, and no current-account restrictions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible into all major reserve currencies; foreign-currency deposit accounts (notably USD) are permissible both domestically and offshore. Profit remittance in the form of dividends and royalties faces no FX obstacle; the only frictional costs are Canadian Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, typically reduced to 5% for dividends and 0-10% for royalties under an applicable income tax treaty such as the Canada-US Treaty), Part I corporate tax clearance, and CRA informational filings (T5, T106, T1134/T1135). Directors and treasury managers should document the tax-residency of the ultimate beneficial owner of each receiving entity to ensure treaty-dependent withholding relief is correctly applied and that no deemed-dividend treatment arises on subordinated shareholder loans.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds or other foreign-currency receipts, including ad-network payouts received from AdMob and AppLovin. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency receivables offshore indefinitely; however, all worldwide income of a Canadian tax resident must be reported in CAD annually at the Bank of Canada noon exchange rate. If aggregate specified foreign property (including foreign bank accounts, foreign securities, and qualifying foreign receivables) exceeds CAD 100,000 in the year, Form T1135 must be filed, and controlled foreign affiliate holdings may trigger T1134 filings. No CAD conversion is compelled even when funds are later repatriated to a domestic operating account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with foreign-exchange administration exercised through the Minister of Finance, the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; prudential supervision of banking counterparties rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/CFT supervision is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — admissible strictly as an ancillary collection/disbursement rail, never as the primary treasury account. Offshore digital EMIs are not chartered banks, are not prudentially regulated by OSFI, and carry no CDIC deposit insurance. A Canadian entity may use such providers for fast receipt of ad-network settlements, but the entity remains responsible for its own Part XIII / GST/HST / income-tax obligations and must keep the EMI float immaterial (recommended cap: CAD 50,000 aggregate, swept to a Schedule I bank within 24 hours). Any EMI that solicits Canadian business must itself be FINTRAC-registered as a money service business (MSB) and must meet Canadian AML obligations; the corporate treasury team should nonetheless treat EMI virtual-IBAN transactions as pass-through rather than revenue evidence, reconciling each EMI credit to the underlying AdMob/AppLovin payout statement. Legal constraint: a Canadian Business Corporation cannot use an unregistered foreign MSB as its registered bank, and auditors may require list of bank accounts to include EMI custody balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injections from a non-resident parent or financing shareholder: transmit via SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001 if initiated via the bank's file-based channel) with remittance-information field coded as 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION – CAPITAL INJECTION – [Legal Entity Name]', and pre-notify the relationship manager with the name and jurisdiction of the remitter, the economic purpose, and the source-of-funds bundle. The bank routing must direct funds to a separately designated Capital Escrow / Share-Proceeds account so that operating cash is not commingled with contributed capital. All injections must be reconciled to the CBCA-required board resolution and the shareholder register; proceeds should be booked as stated capital (share premium if applicable), not as loans, unless a formal subordinated loan agreement is executed to avoid deemed-dividend treatment under Part XIII and to preserve the reserve-deficiency analysis for CRA. The Investment Canada Act must be assessed: for WTO-source non-cultural investors, notifiable review triggers on enterprise-value thresholds (CDN 1.045B+ for 2021 review thresholds, indexed and periodically adjusted); for cultural businesses such as broadcast or film, lower thresholds apply — although ISIC 5820 software publishing is generally non-cultural, the full game value chain should be validated by counsel before closing. Foreign reporting forms T1134/T1135 must be monitored where the parent or foreign shareholder creates specified foreign property in excess of CAD 100,000. Treasury should retain for each injection: subscription agreement, board minutes, remitter bank confirmation, foreign tax clearance if available, and a funds-flow diagram.Primary rail for cross-border trade payments is SWIFT MT103 via the bank's secure file-upload or API channel, using the entity's standard credit line and pre-agreed purpose/beneficiary coding; recurring USD vendor and royalty payments should be consolidated into daily batches using pain.001 payment-initiation messages, with each payment carrying the underlying contract reference and tax withholding indicator. Domestic Canadian disbursements (contractors, hosting, payroll, HST remittances) settle over the Canadian EFT/ACH and LVTS wire systems; commercial letters of credit are not an appropriate rail for purchasing services or licensing intangibles in the hyper-casual games vertical, but may be used only where a major licensor or ad-tech partner demands documentary assurance — in such cases a standby LC or bank guarantee is the more standard alternative. For royalty and license-tuition outflows, the disbursing bank must receive (i) a valid non-resident withholding certificate or treaty-reduction documentation, (ii) the licensing agreement, and (iii) the royalty calculation workbook, so that bank-side OFAC, sanctions, and SOF checks are cleared before release. AdMob and AppLovin collection handling is reversed: the entity should instruct the ad networks to ACH/SWIFT to the Schedule I operating account in USD with immediate same-day wash into CAD where the functional-currency cap is applied, or to the dual-currency USD account that is swept periodically under a board-approved FX hedge policy.Outright deliverable FX forwards (up to 12-month terms, rollable) executed on bank master dealing terms for CAD/USD and CAD/EUR revenue sweepsFX swaps (spot against future or same-day swap/rollover legs) for managing USD/CAD liquidity timing without changing the structural positionCross-currency interest rate swaps (USD fixed/float vs CAD fixed/float) for medium-term balance-sheet hedging where the Cayman or Canadian holdco carries USD-denominated intercompany debtVanilla FX options (call/put) and zero-cost collars on CAD/USD for hedging probabilistic ad-network revenue, subject to a board-approved hedging policyNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) solely for restricted or non-convertible emerging-market contractor currencies (e.g., certain LATAM/SEA/CE non-fully-convertible settlements); CAD itself is freely deliverable so NDFs on CAD/USD are not necessary or standardSpot execution with bilateral credit lines and limit/stop orders via the bank's dealing desk; no algorithmic or high-frequency FX trading, and no crypto-backed hedging instruments permitted under the treasury mandate

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium — admissible strictly as an ancillary collection/disbursement rail, never as the primary treasury account. Offshore digital EMIs are not chartered banks, are not prudentially regulated by OSFI, and carry no CDIC deposit insurance. A Canadian entity may use such providers for fast receipt of ad-network settlements, but the entity remains responsible for its own Part XIII / GST/HST / income-tax obligations and must keep the EMI float immaterial (recommended cap: CAD 50,000 aggregate, swept to a Schedule I bank within 24 hours). Any EMI that solicits Canadian business must itself be FINTRAC-registered as a money service business (MSB) and must meet Canadian AML obligations; the corporate treasury team should nonetheless treat EMI virtual-IBAN transactions as pass-through rather than revenue evidence, reconciling each EMI credit to the underlying AdMob/AppLovin payout statement. Legal constraint: a Canadian Business Corporation cannot use an unregistered foreign MSB as its registered bank, and auditors may require list of bank accounts to include EMI custody balances.
