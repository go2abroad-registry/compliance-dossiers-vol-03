Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and Federal Corporate Income Tax; CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

Foreign-entity branch or Canadian-incorporated corporation must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) using CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or paper Form RC1, providing registered name, Canadian place of business or resident agent, director information, and intended business activities; after BN issuance, the entity must open a GST/HST program account (RT) with the CRA if annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD $30,000 or where the non-resident is carrying on business in Canada and has Canadian-source taxable supplies; CRA will then issue a separate GST/HST account number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a Canadian Business Corporation is taxed as a separate taxpayer under federal Part I and applicable provincial corporate income tax; non-resident corporations are subject to Canadian tax only on Canadian-source income, but a Canadian-incorporated entity is resident in Canada and taxed on worldwide income). — Foreign-entity branch or Canadian-incorporated corporation must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) using CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or paper Form RC1, providing registered name, Canadian place of business or resident agent, director information, and intended business activities; after BN issuance, the entity must open a GST/HST program account (RT) with the CRA if annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD $30,000 or where the non-resident is carrying on business in Canada and has Canadian-source taxable supplies; CRA will then issue a separate GST/HST account number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Canadian Business Corporation is taxed as a separate taxpayer under federal Part I and applicable provincial corporate income tax; non-resident corporations are subject to Canadian tax only on Canadian-source income, but a Canadian-incorporated entity is resident in Canada and taxed on worldwide income).

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box entity election exists in Canada. Relevant statutory elections for a Canadian corporation include GST/HST annual filing election, GST/HST grouping election for closely related corporations and non-residents, election for nil consideration between registrants, section 216 Income Tax Act election for non-resident rental income, and subsection 85(1) rollover election. For GST/HST, registrants may also elect to use the quick method, simplified method, or annual filing threshold methods where eligible.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) over the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters; a lower or mandatory registration rule applies to non-residents carrying on a business in Canada and making taxable supplies in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Part V and Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. Goods qualify when exported directly and substantively removed from Canada. Services qualify where supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and not carrying on a business in Canada, or where the service is performed and delivered wholly outside Canada. Multilingual translation and media dubbing services supplied to foreign clients generally qualify as zero-rated export services if no Canadian nexus is present and documentary proof of the offshore supply is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Export Declaration - B13A), Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No national real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory electronic invoice clearance mandate; GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or EDI where annual gross revenue exceeds CAD $1.5 million; electronic records and e-invoicing are acceptable evidence and must be retained for six years. Canadian provinces are gradually moving toward Peppol e-invoicing but not as a fiscal clearance requirement.

CRA can reassess within the normal 4-year reassessment period (or longer where misrepresentation is proven), deny zero-rating, and claw back unremitted GST/HST plus arrears interest. Gross negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act may be the greater of CAD $250 and 25% of the assessed deficiency, and making false or misleading statements may lead to criminal liability and director liability. Similarly, failed substantiation can result in denial of input tax credit refunds and imposition of culpable-conduct penalties.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely floating and fully convertible in both the current and capital accounts under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. There are no convertibility restrictions, exchange-control permits, surrender requirements, or repatriation authorizations for a Canadian federal business corporation. Dividend distributions, royalty payments, management fees, and cross-border service revenue may be remitted to foreign shareholders without Bank of Canada approval, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutorily 25%, commonly reduced under Canada's income tax treaties to 5-15% for dividends and 0-10% for royalties) and the remitting bank's standard sanctions and source-of-funds screening. Revenue recognition and tax reporting require conversion of foreign-currency amounts to Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or an acceptable alternative rate defined in the Income Tax Act, as administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). No profit-remittance hurdle, capitalization threshold, or prior-approval regime applies to a treasury operation executed through a Schedule I bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export or cross-border service proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio to CAD. Localization-service receivables denominated in USD, EUR, or other currencies may be held indefinitely in a bank-operated foreign-currency account or repatriated at the corporation's discretion. For CRA reporting purposes, however, revenue must be translated into Canadian dollars at the spot rate applicable to each transaction or at a consistently applied year-end rate under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act, and the corporation's accounting policy must document the chosen translation methodology to withstand audit scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting as the monetary and financial-system authority, in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada for federal financial-sector policy. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control ministry. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises anti-money-laundering compliance, Global Affairs Canada administers economic sanctions and trade controls, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates the chartered Schedule I banks and their treasury dealing rooms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise B2B and Payoneer are regulated in Canada as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); they are not chartered banks. Feasibility rating for the localization agency: High as supplementary disbursement rails for low-value, high-volume contractor payouts and multicurrency collections; Medium as a primary treasury operating account because MSB/EMI platforms are not CDIC-insured deposit-taking institutions, do not issue commercial documentary letters of credit, do not participate in SWIFT correspondent banking as direct members, and do not provide operating credit or treasury dealing-room lines. Legal constraints: (i) onboarding requires full FINTRAC KYC forms and verification of the corporation's existence from Corporations Canada; (ii) funds held with Wise or Payoneer represent uninsured contractual claims; (iii) Canadian Schedule I banks may apply enhanced monitoring to onward transfers routed to such EMIs, requiring the corporation to present evidence of the MSB's registration and the underlying transactional contracts; (iv) electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting obligations routed through the MSB or the originating bank as applicable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital contributions are routed by SWIFT wire to the Schedule I operating account with a purpose description identifying the shareholder, the share-subscription or subordinated-loan nature of the inflow, and the jurisdiction of origin; no Bank of Canada authorization or exchange-control filing is required under Canadian law. The receiving bank screens all inbound SWIFT messages against OSFI, OFSI, and UN sanctions lists and may require a certified board resolution authorizing the share issuance and the account-opening deposit. Foreign-currency funds may be converted at the bank's dealing rate or credited to a parallel USD/EUR multicurrency account. Cash components exceeding CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC large-cash reporting, and cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are captured by the bank's EFT reporting regime. Share subscriptions must be fully paid in money or property within the constraints of the Canada Business Corporations Act, and the consideration is credited to stated capital for CRA and provincial capital-account purposes, supported by a share register and directors' resolution.The localization agency's primary outbound clearing rails comprise: (i) SWIFT MT103 for high-value one-off payments to rights holders, studio counterparties, and overseas production partners; (ii) commercial letters of credit and documentary collections under UCP 600 for larger procurement of media equipment or inventory requiring bank-payment security; (iii) the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and Lynx, operated under Bank of Canada supervision, together with Payments Canada ACSS for domestic CAD payables including CRA instalments and vendor payments; (iv) Interac e-Transfer and payroll direct deposit for Canadian contractors and employees; and (v) Wise B2B and Payoneer batch APIs for pooled low-value international contractor settlements. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general business rate after the General Rate Reduction; provincial/territorial corporate rates of 8% to 16% apply separately, producing a combined headline range of approximately 23% to 31%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents, reduced by tax treaty: e.g., 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% for a US corporate shareholder beneficially owning at least 10% of the voting stock under the Canada-US DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate reportable transactions with any single non-resident non-arm's-length party in a taxation year (triggering Form T106 and contemporaneous documentation). Country-by-country reporting threshold: group consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million (approximately CAD 1.1 billion) for the ultimate parent's CRA filing.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to offshore regimes; substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245 ITA), the treaty-shopping limitations introduced by the Multilateral Instrument (principal-purpose test), and the judicial sham doctrine. In the localization context, the CRA will treat a Canadian letterbox corporation without employees, premises, or risk-bearing functions as lacking economic substance and may re-attribute income to the substantive non-resident principal, denying treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: s. 239(1) ITA: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded; imprisonment up to 2 years on summary conviction or 5 years on indictment; CRA may reassess beyond the normal limitation periods where misrepresentation is shown.

Director personal liability: Aiding or abetting evasion renders directors criminally liable as parties; civil joint and several liability for the corporate tax, interest, and penalties; conviction generally supports travel bans, asset seizure, and removal-related restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and T106 Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where reasonable efforts are absent; per-week penalties for late T106 filings; CRA may adjust income under the arm's-length standard for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are not shielded where the non-arm's-length transfer was designed to strip Canadian income; s. 160 transferee liability may attach to assets moved out of the corporation, and culpable directors face s. 227.1 liability for unremitted withholding embedded in the arrangements.
Unremitted Payroll / Source-Deduction Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of unremitted source deductions plus interest and penalties of up to 20% of the amount not remitted under the ITA and CPP/EI Acts; payroll audit exposure for the full remuneration paid.

Director personal liability: s. 227.1 ITA imposes strict personal liability on directors for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST, even where the corporation is insolvent or dissolved; CRA collects through garnishment of personal bank accounts and travel/exit controls.
Shell Company Abuse / GAAR and Sham Structures

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245 ITA) re-characterizes the arrangement and disallows the tax benefit; where false statements or omissions are made knowingly, s. 163(2) imposes a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated tax; the MLI principal-purpose test may additionally deny treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil where the entity is a mere facade; directors face s. 160 reassessments for assets transferred to non-arm's-length persons, civil restitution orders, and potential conspiracy charges under the Criminal Code.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for entities; any bank relationship may be terminated and the corporation placed on FINTRAC's public non-compliance list.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally prosecutable for laundering proceeds under s. 462.31, Criminal Code, carrying imprisonment of up to 10 years; convictions trigger asset freezes, travel restrictions, and de facto exclusion from regulated markets.
