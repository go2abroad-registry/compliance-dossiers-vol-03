Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — head office Ottawa; federal tax administration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) at canada.ca/revenue-agency. Provincial exceptions: Revenu Québec (QST and QC corporate income tax) and Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration (ATRA policy; corporate income tax administered by Government of Alberta) require parallel registration if nexus exists.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or provincial analogue; obtain articles of incorporation and Corporations Canada file number. Step 2: CRA receives incorporation data electronically via BRO and automatically issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) to the registered office; if not auto-issued, file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Step 3: Open CRA program accounts under the same BN: RC (corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) — required before making the first taxable supply; the EMI must register for GST/HST immediately because its zero-rated export supplies (financial services to non-residents) are taxable supplies and will exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. Step 4 (non-resident foreign entity establishing a Canadian branch/PE): file Form RC1 with the CRA International Tax Services Office (Surrey Tax Centre); provide certificate of corporate status, constitutional documents, and appoint a Canadian resident representative for service of process; obtain BN and RC/RT accounts. Step 5: Activate MyBA; complete Form GST1 confirmation of GST/HST registration; register for FINTRAC MSB (PCMLTFA) as the tax-adjacent licensing prerequisite for the electronic money institution (EMI) license; if operating in Quebec or Alberta, register separately with Revenu Québec (QST/AT1) and ATRA as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a federal/provincial Business Corporation under CA_CORP is a separate taxable person resident in Canada; taxed on worldwide income under Part I of the Income Tax Act with foreign tax credit relief; no pass-through or flow-through eligibility; Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status determines the CAD 500,000 small business deduction (9% federal rate on active business income) but foreign-controlled or publicly owned EMIs are taxed at the 15% net general federal rate; no territorial election; no tax-exempt-zone status.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; all corporations are irrefutably entity-level taxpayers. Available elections relevant to an EMI: (i) GST/HST section 156 election for closely related corporations (deems related corporations a single person); (ii) ITA subsection 85(1) rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of assets into the corporation; (iii) ITA subsection 93(1) election to treat foreign affiliate dividends out of FAPI as non-taxable; (iv) GST/HST input-tax-credit allocation election between reasonable determination and the mandatory Special Attribution Method (SAM) for selected listed financial institutions (SLFIs); (v) hybrid-mismatch elections under ITA subsections 18.2(8) and 18.21(6) to neutralize deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; (vi) treaty-based Form 9006 waiver for withholding-tax relief under Canada's tax conventions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates an open capital and current account with no general foreign-exchange control law. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible. Profit remittances by non-residents are permitted without FX approval, but may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25%, reduced under applicable tax treaties). Banks require standard tax residency declarations and, in the case of an EMI, proof that the remitted amount is after-tax surplus derived from licensed money-service activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency. The Export and Import Permits Act only restricts controlled goods and strategic technology and does not require repatriation or conversion. FINTRAC reporting requirements apply to large cross-border transactions, but no timeline exists for converting export proceeds to CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential banking supervision under the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/ATF supervision under the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and payment-clearing oversight through Payments Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. A foreign fintech EMI cannot lawfully issue virtual IBANs or provide retail payment services to Canadian end-users without a Canadian money-services-business (MSB) registration and compliance with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and PCMLTFA. Offshore EMI licences are not automatically recognized in Canada. A partnership with a Canadian Schedule I bank or a provincially licensed MSB is required for IBAN issuance and wallet reserve management; otherwise, the EMI would be operating as an unregistered payment service provider and may be subject to FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection is executed using a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank through a Canadian correspondent, with the beneficiary account being the EMI's capital escrow account at a Schedule I bank. The payment narrative must explicitly state 'Paid-in capital / share subscription' and reference the relevant board resolution and share issuance. The receiving bank will verify the corporate authorization documents and may require a travel-rule-compliant originator declaration. For transactions exceeding CAD 10,000,000, FINTRAC large-transaction reporting and a pre-advice to the bank's global transaction services desk are prudent.Outbound trade settlements are effected via SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) for direct payments, MT202 for bank-to-bank cover, and documentary letters of credit under UCP600. Canadian-dollar disbursements settle through Payments Canada ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) or LVTS (Large Value Transfer System); USD and other currencies are cleared through the issuing bank's New York correspondent via Fedwire or through the European clearing system for SEPA. All outbound payments require the EMI to embed a child transaction identifier and a purpose code in the free-format remittance field to maintain end-to-end auditability for the OFSI and FINTRAC.Outright FX forwards (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD, GBP/CAD) with a licensed Canadian bank or a registered swap dealerNon-deliverable forwards (NDF) for currencies subject to convertibility restrictionsCross-currency swaps for medium-term treasury rebalancingVanilla FX options (collar, knockout) for hedging known disbursement streamsFX swaps for day-to-day liquidity management without speculative exposure
