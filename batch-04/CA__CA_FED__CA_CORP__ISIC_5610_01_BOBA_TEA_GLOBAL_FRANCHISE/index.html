Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement under the CBCA and no escrow or deposit requirement at incorporation. The incorporators may subscribe for shares without immediate capital contribution; consideration for issued shares is determined by the board under section 49 CBCA. A bank-account opening deposit is routinely nominal. Escrow rules may only become relevant in a later public securities offering under provincial securities law, not in a private corporate formation.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire marshal inspection and fire code compliance certificate; approved commercial building occupancy and structural load assessment from the municipal building department; provincial occupational health and safety clearance under the applicable Occupational Health and Safety Act, including machinery guarding, WHMIS training, first aid/CPR coverage, and workplace inspection records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital and current account convertibility. There are no foreign exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, royalty payments, or capital repatriation. Transactions are subject exclusively to general AML/KYC, sanctions enforcement, and FINTRAC reporting obligations. Profit remittance is effected through any Canadian Schedule I bank via ordinary wire transfer; withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act (Canada) apply to cross-border payments to non-resident payees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Exporters are free to hold, convert, or remit export proceeds in any currency. All export documentation must comply with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) reporting rules, and any wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or above require FINTRAC reporting by the financial institution. No repatriation or conversion obligation is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Department of Finance Canada (Foreign Exchange Administration is not a separate statutory authority; Canada operates without an exchange control regulator)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Canadian law does not recognize a separate 'Electronic Money Institution' licensing regime. Fintech payment service providers must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and must operate either as authorized foreign bank branches or as non-deposit-taking payment entities. They cannot accept client deposits or operate clearing settlement accounts with the Bank of Canada. For franchise royalty aggregation and cross-border liquidity, a Schedule I chartered bank remains the core treasury rail; fintechs may be used for ancillary payment collection but require separate contractual due diligence and cannot provide guaranteed clearing finality.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident beneficial owners or directors with tax residence in high-risk jurisdictions

Root cause: Non-resident beneficial owners or directors with tax residence in high-risk jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Certified passports, provincial or federal corporate registry extract, proof of address dated within 90 days, certificate of incumbency, FBI/RCMP police clearance letters, tax residence certificates, and a detailed organizational chart evidencing commercial rationale for non-resident control.
Royalty payments to a related offshore master franchise or trademark holding entity

Root cause: Royalty payments to a related offshore master franchise or trademark holding entity

Defense evidence: Executed trademark license and franchise agreements, royalty valuation report prepared by an independent appraiser, evidence of actual arm's-length consideration under OECD transfer pricing standards, and audited financial statements of the receiving entity.
High-volume cross-border wire transfers to supplier jurisdictions in Asia-Pacific for syrup or ingredient procurement

Root cause: High-volume cross-border wire transfers to supplier jurisdictions in Asia-Pacific for syrup or ingredient procurement

Defense evidence: Purchase orders, bills of lading, commercial invoices, import declarations filed with CBSA, direct match of goods description to franchise operating documents, and supplier KYC packs including business licences and banking references.
Capital injection structure involving shareholder loans or subscriptions for shares in a newly formed Canadian business corporation

Root cause: Capital injection structure involving shareholder loans or subscriptions for shares in a newly formed Canadian business corporation

Defense evidence: Subscription agreement, share certificate ledger, bank-stamped proof of funds (source of wealth), sworn declaration of lawful source of wealth, and a certified copy of the shareholder resolution approving the injection.
Franchise fee clustering disproportionate to business scale at launch

Root cause: Franchise fee clustering disproportionate to business scale at launch

Defense evidence: Pro forma cash flow model, signed franchise agreements opening multiple locations, security deposits schedule, construction and equipment supplier quotations, and evidence of commercial counterparties performing due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through a Canadian Schedule I chartered bank. The remitter must specify purpose code 'S0101' (capital transfer) or equivalent under the Canadian Balance of Payments reporting convention, and include the bank institution number, transit number, and CAD account number. The corporate registry legal name and Common CUSIP / corporate identifier must match the bank's KYC file. For share subscription proceeds, the fidelity of the payment reference must include 'share capital contribution, corporate resolution dated [date]'. Contributions exceeding CAD 10,000 will automatically trigger FINTRAC reporting and are subject to proof of legitimate source of wealth for non-resident shareholders.Outbound settlement for franchise trade disbursements shall be conducted over SWIFT MT103 wires, Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) issued by the Canadian bank through its correspondent network, or documentary collection (D/P) where trade credit is not required. For recurring syrup and packaging imports from overseas franchise suppliers, the recommended rail is a standby L/C with a revolving purchase line, mitigation of carriage risk through INCOTERMS 2020 rules, and full document checking covering commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and certificate of origin. All payments to related-party suppliers must include transfer pricing documentation and, where applicable, withholding tax remittance to the Canada Revenue Agency. CAD settlement is via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Real-Time Rail for domestic legs; cross-border US-linked disbursements may be cleared through the Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS) or Fedwire via the Canadian bank's US correspondent.Outright FX Forwards (maturity up to 2 years) with Canadian chartered banks or authorized foreign bank branches in CanadaNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for emerging market currencies where capital controls exist (e.g., Chinese renminbi; limited to trade-related underlying exposure)Cross-Currency Swaps (for medium-term funding where principal and interest are exchanged, permitted with Schedule I banks under ISDA master agreements)Liquidity-optimized FX swaps for day-to-day treasury management of royalty receipts in multiple currenciesOTC plain-vanilla options (limited to hedging trade payables/receivables, not for speculative purposes)
