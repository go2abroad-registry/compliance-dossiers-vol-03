Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: CRA My Business Account at canada.ca and GST/HST NETFILE

E-government filing portal

Register the Canadian limited partnership under the applicable provincial Limited Partnership Act, then apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or CRA Business Registration Online. Select the entity type 'Partnership' and open a GST/HST program account (RT) in the name of the LP; if export reporting is required, add an export/import program account. Provide the LP's extra-provincial registration certificate, general partner identifiers, and the non-resident limited partners' foreign tax identification numbers. CRA issues a unique 9-digit BN plus program account numbers; this BN is the statutory Canadian TIN for income tax, GST/HST and customs purposes. The LP must file Form T5013 partnership information return annually even though it is fiscally transparent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. The Canadian LP is fiscally transparent by default; no election permits entity-level income taxation. For GST/HST the LP is treated as a separate person and may voluntarily register as a small supplier; it may also elect to file through a specified appointing representative. Anti-hybrid rules in the Income Tax Act apply automatically; no election is available to avoid the deemed treatment of certain partnership hybrid mismatch payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada in the preceding four calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); zero-rated export supplies count toward the threshold but the export supply itself is zero-rated, and a below-threshold LP may register voluntarily.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of raw cotton bales is zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act when the goods are exported from Canada in the course of the supply, the goods are delivered to a destination outside Canada, and the supplier retains satisfactory evidence of export. The Canadian LP acting as principal or as disclosed agent for a non-resident principal may claim 0% GST/HST on the export sale or on the zero-rated brokerage service supplied to a non-resident recipient, provided the service is not an excepted service related to Canadian real estate or tangible personal property. The LP must have a valid GST/HST registration number and must substantiate the export within its records before claiming input tax credits or refunds.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent air waybill showing port of exit and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice / Canada Customs Invoice describing the raw cotton bales, value, goods description, and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank remittance advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Container/booking records, terminal receipts, or shipping company documentation evidencing physical movement out of Canada
  • Export contract/purchase order confirming the non-resident customer and the export terms

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-blessed continuous controls for e-invoicing in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing the prescribed particulars under the Excise Tax Act and retain books and records, making them available to CRA on request. Returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or certified software; Pan-Canadian or Peppol e-invoicing is voluntary.

CRA may reassess a claimed zero-rated export as taxable at 5% GST if the supporting export and currency-receipt evidence is missing or conflicting, and may apply arrears interest from the date the GST was originally due. A false statement or omission on a GST/HST return can draw a penalty under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act, and gross negligence or wilful evasion can result in enhanced penalties and criminal prosecution. If the 180-day inward remittance/evidence deadline is not met, the zero-rating may be clawed back, input tax credits disallowed, and the exporter may be liable for unpaid GST, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible on both current and capital account, fully floating, and Canada satisfies IMF Article VIII convertibility obligations. No capital controls, no prior central-bank authorization for inbound or outbound transfers, and no repatriation licence or registration procedure exist. A limited partnership brokerage distributing commission income to non-resident partners is subject only to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (default 25%; generally 15% under the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention where a US partner files Form NR302 for a reduced rate and provides the beneficial-ownership declaration). Capital contributions are not subject to withholding; however, any remittance of proceeds must be supported by the underlying trade documentation to satisfy the bank's purpose-of-payment requirement under FINTRAC regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio into CAD, and no deadline for repatriation of export proceeds. The LP may retain the full foreign-currency balance in domestic or non-resident accounts. Receipts must be recorded in the CRA partnership financial statements on an accrual basis, and all foreign-currency gains and losses are computed under subsection 39(1) of the Income Tax Act. No surrender certificate or customs-linked settlement proof is demanded beyond the ordinary GST/HST zero-rating export evidence (commercial invoice, bill of lading, and proof of export within the prescribed claim period).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating with the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act and the Financial Administration Act; prudential regulation of all federally incorporated banks and foreign bank branches is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML transaction surveillance is conducted by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); tax-related foreign-payment administration is administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-to-low for core treasury custody. Canada has no European-style EMI licence; non-bank payment providers operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, in force November 2024). Wise Canada, Payoneer, and Stripe are viable for small-value supplier payouts and commission receipts (practical C$50,000-per-transaction threshold), but they cannot open statutory trust or escrow accounts, issue documentary letters of credit, provide CDIC-insured deposit custody, or host LC settlement flows. Without a Bank Act Schedule I licence, such entities cannot take demand-deposit liabilities; therefore the primary settlement rail must remain a Schedule I bank. If the LP itself deals in foreign currency for clients, a registered MSB designation with FINTRAC and an RPAA licence are required before any fintech rails are used.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partner contributions enter through SWIFT MT103 with an ISO 20022 purpose code such as CAPT (capital transfer) or EGCP (equity participation), naming the LP's registered partnership identity and CA-FED jurisdiction in the remittance information. For CAD-denominated contributions, the receiving Schedule I bank clears funds through Payments Canada Lynx (the LVTS successor) high-value real-time rail with immediate finality. All inbound electronic funds transfers above C$10,000 are subject to FINTRAC EFT reporting; the bank will hold the account until a capital-contribution schedule, partnership-agreement excerpt, and general-partner resolution are placed on file. A capital contribution is not taxable to the partnership under CRA folio S4-F6-C1, must be allocated to the contributing partner's adjusted cost base, and carries no Part XIII withholding exposure unless subsequently distributed as income to a non-resident.International cotton-settlement and buyer-payment operations are executed over three complementary rails: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT199 for flat commission disbursements, refunds, and operational expenses, with correspondent clearing in USD through CHIPS/Fedwire and CAD through Lynx; (2) documentary Letters of Credit issued or confirmed by a Schedule I bank trade desk using SWIFT MT700/MT707 series, subject to UCP 600, where the LP is beneficiary or arranging bank and the cotton-bale export is evidenced by presentation of the original bill of lading, certificate of origin, weight/packing list, commercial invoice, and phytosanitary certificate; and (3) documentary collections under URC 522 using SWIFT MT798/MQ messaging for works against payment or acceptance. The LP must maintain a separate trade account margined at 20-100% of each LC face amount depending on the facility agreement, must preserve all disbursement invoices for CRA foreign-transaction reporting where any transaction exceeds C$10,000, and should execute USD conversion on the bank's trading desk with an agreed all-in spread of 10-30 basis points at wholesale volumes.OTC CAD/USD deliverable forwards under ISDA 2013 documentation, standard tenors of 1 to 12 months, exempt from mandatory clearing and initial-margin requirements as a non-financial counterparty below the OSFI margin-rule threshold, provided the notional is directly tied to cotton-trade invoicing.CAD/USD FX swaps (spot-start and forward-start principal exchanges) for rolling inventory turnover and working-capital bridging between inbound commission receipts and outbound supplier payments.Cross-currency basis swaps for long-horizon cotton purchase agreements with foreign producers or buyers, tenors up to 24 months, documented under ISDA/CSA.Exchange-traded CAD/USD futures and options on CME and the Montreal Exchange (Bourse de Montréal), preferred for transparent price discovery and elimination of bilateral counterparty credit risk.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible under Canadian law and OSFI guidance, but are economically unnecessary because CAD is a fully deliverable G10 currency; the LP should contract in deliverable forwards unless a counterparty jurisdiction cannot effect physical CAD delivery, in which case an NDF with a Canadian Schedule I counterparty is the fallback.
