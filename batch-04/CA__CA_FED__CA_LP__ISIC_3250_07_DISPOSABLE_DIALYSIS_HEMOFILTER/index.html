Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Provincial limited partnership registry (e.g., Companies and Personal Property Security Branch, Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery, Province of Ontario); federal tax and GST registration administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Business Registration Online portal.

Governing statute

No federal limited partnership statute exists in Canada; formation is governed by the provincial Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16, ss. 2-5, 8) and extra-provincial registration rules, with federal recognition under the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), s. 96, and the Excise Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. E-15 (GST/HST).

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (provincial form under the LP Act) plus CRA RC1 Business Registration; the provincial registry issues a Certificate of Limited Partnership and the CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number with program accounts.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Canadian limited partnership; contributions may be made in cash, property, or services as agreed in the LP Agreement, and no escrow account is mandated by federal law, though the general partner may establish a project escrow account at the request of lenders.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire marshal certificate confirming NFPA 101 life-safety compliance for cleanroom and sterilization areas; structural load inspection and stamped engineer certificate for ISO Class 7/8 cleanrooms; provincial workers' compensation registration (WSIB equivalent); electrical safety authorization (ESA/CSA) for controlled medical equipment; and an occupational hygiene clearance for ethylene oxide and solvent exposure with ventilation permits.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Canadian limited partnership; contributions may be made in cash, property, or services as agreed in the LP Agreement, and no escrow account is mandated by federal law, though the general partner may establish a project escrow account at the request of lenders.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; CBSA customs interface through Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership: (1) form the LP under applicable provincial partnership legislation; (2) apply for a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or by filing RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; (3) activate the GST/HST program account (RT) under the same BN once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold or upon voluntary registration; (4) use the BN to obtain CBSA importer/exporter account codes; (5) cite the BN and RT account on export declarations, GST/HST returns, and all CRA filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists for limited partnerships. A Canadian LP is fiscally transparent by statute; income, losses, and relevant attributes flow through to partners. Hybrid mismatch interest deduction-disallowance rules under ss. 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act apply but do not permit entity reclassification. SIFT rules may tax a commercially traded fund-like partnership at entity level only in narrowly defined public-market situations; a private Canadian LP remains pass-through.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies per calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters for persons carrying on business in Canada, including non-residents; non-resident vendors making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register regardless of threshold under specified circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under s. 6 of the Excise Tax Act applies to tangible personal property (e.g., disposable dialysis hemofilters) supplied in Canada that is exported by the supplier as part of the supply. The LP must hold documentary evidence that the goods were sent from Canada to a destination outside Canada. Services performed in Canada but supplied to a non-resident and enjoyed wholly outside Canada may qualify under s. 7. The supply must not be made to a Canadian-resident individual for personal use, and the goods must physically leave Canada within a reasonable time.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic CBSA CERS export report or B13A where permitted)
  • Bill of Lading / Waybill (ocean, air, or highway transport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank evidence of foreign receivable credited to Canada
  • Commercial invoice showing the zero-rated export supply and foreign destination
  • Carrier certificate or freight forwarder receipt confirming the goods left Canada

No federal real-time digital stamping or government pre-clearance fiscal invoice regime is currently in force for GST/HST. Invoices must contain the prescribed information under s. 223 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA); records must be retained for six years. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for most registrants, but does not operate a mandatory per-invoice clearance or API stamping process.

CRA auditors require strict proof of export before accepting zero-rating. If the documentation is incomplete, false, or misleading, CRA can reassess the supply as taxable at the applicable GST/HST rate, charge arrears interest, deny input tax credits on related inputs, and invoke penalties under s. 285.1 of the ETA (minimum C$500 and up to 50% of the tax net at issue for false statements or omissions). Knowingly making false statements is an indictable offence under s. 327 ETA, punishable by fines up to C$100,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years. The normal reassessment period is four years, but may be extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Profit remittances from a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident partners may be made through distributions or capital withdrawals without exchange-control approval, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, Canadian income tax reporting via T5013 schedules, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) verification obligations. Inward direct investments may require notification under the Investment Canada Act if the transaction meets prescribed threshold criteria.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation exists to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency receipts into CAD. Export transactions require mandatory reporting to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System if the value exceeds CAD 2,000; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, held in Canadian-chartered bank accounts, or repatriated freely.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated financial institutions; the Minister of Finance and Department of Finance administer the Financial Administration Act and the Currency Act for foreign exchange policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs may operate in Canada only if licensed or registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and authorized to clear through Canadian payment rails. A non-resident EMI without a Canadian registration is not viable as a primary liquidity rail because it cannot access the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). Use as a supplementary payment gateway is possible but limited by CAD settlement timelines and AML/KYC duplication.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal basic corporate income tax rate, after provincial abatement; the limited partnership itself is a flow-through and is not subject to Part I federal income tax – corporate partners pay this rate on allocated Canadian-source income)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian corporation. An LP is fiscally transparent, so distributions to non-resident partners are generally not dividends; however, Part XIII may apply to interest, royalties, or management fee payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions with a non-arm's-length non-resident; corporations in a MNE group with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000 must also prepare a Country-by-Country report. CRA expects contemporaneous documentation for all controlled transactions, irrespective of size.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) denies tax benefits from artificial transactions lacking economic substance. Treaty benefits are denied where the main purpose is treaty shopping, and courts apply substance-over-form in assessing sham or conduit arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Part XIII Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the unwithheld amount, arrears interest, and a penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount (increased to 20% for gross negligence), per s.227(8) and s.227(10) of the Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholdings under s.227.1; CRA may file a Federal Court certificate, impose travel bans, and attach personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (s.247(3)), plus tax reassessment and arrears interest. The penalty is not waived if documentation was not completed before the filing due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false transfer pricing documentation may be subject to s.239 penalties for false statements, including fines and imprisonment.
Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax plus interest; criminal conviction: fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and up to 5 years' imprisonment (s.239).

Director personal liability: Directors participating in the evasion are liable as parties to the offence (s.239(2)) and face personal fines, imprisonment, travel bans, and civil restitution.
Economic Substance Failure / GAAR

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule denies the tax benefit and applies a 50% penalty (s.245 ITA); CRA may also reassess the LP's entire profit allocation as business income of the non-resident partner.
