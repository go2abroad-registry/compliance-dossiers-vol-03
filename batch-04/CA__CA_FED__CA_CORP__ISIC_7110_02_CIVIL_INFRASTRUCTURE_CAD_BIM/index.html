Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the CBCA permits incorporation with nominal share capital. No escrow or capital deposit account is mandated; subscriber funds may be held in the corporation's own bank or trust account, and issued shares may be fully or partly unpaid until called by the board.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: municipal/regional fire marshal inspection certificate per the National Fire Code and provincial Fire Code before initial occupancy. Structural load: landlord's building occupancy certificate and stamped structural reports for any raised-floor, server-cabinet, or mezzanine loads. Occupational: provincial workplace safety inspection (OHS/safety plan, first-aid station, fire evacuation signage, and office ergonomic assessment) and workers' compensation coverage where the province mandates payroll enrolment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Ottawa; taxpayer portal: My Business Account (Mon dossier d'entreprise) at www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; online entity registration: Business Registration Online (BRO); legal basis: Income Tax Act (Canada), Excise Tax Act, Canada Business Corporations Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: On incorporation under the CBCA, CRA automatically issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and the corporate income tax program account (RC). Step 2: A non-resident or foreign-owned Canadian corporation that will carry on activity in Canada or supply taxable services must add program accounts via BRO or Form RC391: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RM (import/export). Step 3: For the offshore CAD/BIM export model, the entity should register for GST/HST (voluntarily if below the CAD 30,000 threshold) so that input tax credits on Canadian software, rent, and subcontract costs may be recovered; the non-resident client's tax residence must be evidenced by a certificate of residency or a contractual representation clause. Step 4: No minimum capital is required, but the CBCA requires a registered Canadian office address and, for CBCA incorporation, that at least 25% of the directors be resident Canadians (subject to statutory exceptions).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident business corporations are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source business and property income; standard CBCA corporations cannot elect pass-through treatment, though a Canadian-controlled private corporation may claim the small business deduction and associated-company aggregation rules apply).

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant elections include: (i) s. 249.1 fiscal-period election permitting a non-calendar taxation year; (ii) s. 256.1 loss-restriction election to crystallize losses before an acquisition of control; (iii) GST/HST closely related corporation election under s. 156 ETA to zero-rate qualifying inter-corporate supplies; (iv) s. 216 election for non-resident rental income; (v) s. 219(2) deemed-year-end election when a non-resident corporation ceases to carry on business in Canada; (vi) amalgamation and continuation elections under s. 87 ITA; and (vii) hybrid-mismatch characterization rules under ss. 18.4 to 18.6 ITA, including the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) applicable to taxpayers whose group net book equity exceeds CAD 20 million.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (the s. 148 Excise Tax Act 'small supplier' threshold measured over the preceding four calendar quarters; a non-resident without a permanent establishment in Canada becomes a mandatory registrant once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000; below the threshold registration is optional but recommended to claim input tax credits; certain non-resident suppliers of digital and remote services are subject to simplified mandatory registration).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The offshore BIM/CAD civil-infrastructure service qualifies for 0% GST/HST as a supply of a service to a non-resident under s. 9 of Part V, Schedule VI, ETA, provided the service is not in connection with real property situated in Canada, is not in connection with tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, and is not for consumption, use, or enjoyment in Canada. Because the commercial model is ISIC 7110 CAD/BIM drafting for foreign civil-infrastructure assets, the zero rate applies to the full consideration if the design situs lies outside Canada; any work product referencing Canadian real property (e.g., a Canadian highway or utility network) or Canadian onshore goods will be taxable at 5%. Services may also be zero-rated when performed for a non-resident even if some contractor costs are incurred in Canada, subject to the 'made available in Canada' exception. Supplies of exported goods (if any drawings are physically delivered on media) fall under s. 1, Part V, Schedule VI on proof of export.

  • Customs export declaration (Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) transmission via CBSA where the transaction involves physical goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (carrier-issued transport document evidencing physical export of any goods-based deliverables)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign-currency sale proceeds from the non-resident buyer)
  • Commercial invoice and executed service agreement (identifying the non-resident recipient, offshore real-property situs, and scope of BIM/CAD deliverables)
  • Certificate of non-resident status or tax residence certificate of the foreign client (supporting the zero-rated service supply under Part V of Schedule VI)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, electronic stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime exists at the federal level. The Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing network is adopted on an opt-in basis. GST/HST invoices must be issued on paper or electronically and must contain the prescribed particulars under s. 223 ETA (supplier name and BN, date of invoice, recipient name, description, tax status, and rate). Registrants with annual revenue above CAD 1.5 million must file electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI; reporting periods are generally annual (under CAD 1.5 million), quarterly (under CAD 5 million), or monthly (CAD 5 million or more). Non-resident simplified registrants file through Form GST494.

CRA may reassess zero-rating claims within the normal four-year reassessment period (and indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud exists), clawing back input tax credits and assessing 5% GST/HST (or 13%-15% HST where applicable) on previously zero-rated revenue, plus daily compound interest under s. 280. Penalties include: failure to register (greater of CAD 100 and 1% of collectible net tax), failure to file (5% of net tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months), false statements or omissions (the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understated net tax), and gross negligence penalties under s. 285.1; criminal tax evasion under s. 327 carries fines and imprisonment. The supplier bears the evidentiary burden to demonstrate export or offshore supply; deficient documentation results in denial of input tax credits and retrospective clawback assessed against the corporation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized convertible currency system with no exchange controls on current or capital account items. There is no approval requirement for converting CAD, maintaining foreign-currency bank accounts, paying dividends/management fees to non-residents, or repatriating invested capital. The only binding restrictions arise from Canadian sanctions regulations, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and the bank's internal AML/CFT risk policies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or to any designated bank, and no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline exists. CAD/BIM service exporters may invoice in CAD, USD, EUR, or other freely convertible currencies, may retain foreign-currency receipts in Canadian foreign-currency accounts, and may convert or concentrate those proceeds according to treasury policy. FINTRAC record-keeping and large-transaction reporting thresholds still apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no standalone foreign-exchange control agency exists in Canada. Prudential regulation of clearing banks is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money-laundering supervision to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High for operational payment flows, but not a full substitute for a regulated Canadian bank account. Wise and similar FINTRAC-registered money-services businesses may be used for small-value cross-border wires, vendor disbursements, and international receivable collections. Legal constraints include inability to offer CDIC-insured CAD deposit accounts, no capital-escrow or project-finance holding role, and no ability to issue documentary credit instruments or banker's guarantees. A Schedule I commercial bank remains mandatory for capital injections, liquidity concentration, and trade-finance instruments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Canada does not maintain a central foreign-exchange purpose-code registry; however, Canadian banks require the payment to be coded in the purpose-of-transfer field as 'Capital subscription / paid-up capital', 'Shareholder loan', or 'Payment for exported engineering services'. The transfer is screened against Canadian sanctions and FINTRAC large-transaction-reporting thresholds. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For entities with gross revenue > CAD 50 million, a Master File and Local File are required if the aggregate value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 5 million (goods) or CAD 1 million (services, interest, royalties). For all entities, Form T1062 must be filed if transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1 million (goods) or CAD 50,000 (other).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime, but the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse rules to deny benefits where transactions lack commercial substance. A Canadian corporation must demonstrate real business activity (e.g., employees, office, decision-making) to maintain tax residency and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: 50% gross negligence penalty on understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate tax (s. 227.1) if they fail to withhold/remit source deductions; travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) denies tax benefits; treaty abuse provisions may deny treaty relief; CRA may reassess and impose penalties of 50% of the benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for negligence if they knowingly participate in a sham arrangement; civil restitution and potential criminal charges for conspiracy to defraud.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment for officers.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for willful non-compliance; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation.
