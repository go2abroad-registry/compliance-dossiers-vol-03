Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Limited Partnerships, ServiceOntario (Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery); Corporations Canada for federal name and trademark registrations; Canada Revenue Agency for federal tax registration.

Governing statute

No federal Companies Act governs limited partnership formation. The limited partnership is formed under the Limited Partnerships Act of the chosen province, e.g., Ontario R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16, ss. 2(1), 3(1), 6 and 9. Federal jurisdiction status is established through the Canada Revenue Agency Business Number and, where applicable, interprovincial registration. The Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44 is not used for LP formation.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); official portal: www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; e-services via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account (RP account) through the CRA. The general partner, acting for the partnership, applies online via Business Registration Online, by phone, or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for Business Number), Form SS-4? not applicable. Required details include the partnership agreement, all general partners or managing partners, legal name, operating address, mailing address, principal business activity, and estimated taxable supplies. CRA issues a 9-digit BN followed by an RP account identifier. Non-resident partnerships must also appoint a Canadian fiscal representative or recognise service address for CRA correspondence; a power of attorney (Form RC59) is required. There is no fee for registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No federal 'check-the-box' election exists. Limited partnerships are inherently fiscally transparent under the federal Income Tax Act: income, gains, losses and credits flow through to partners according to the partnership agreement. For GST/HST, the partnership is a separate 'person' for statutory registration purposes. Standard elections available: (a) Income Tax Act s. 97(2) election to transfer partnership property on a tax-deferred rollover basis; (b) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related corporations (not generally applicable to LPs); (c) optional joint election under ETA s. 169(4) for exempt supplies between registrants. Hybrid mismatch and foreign affiliate anti-avoidance rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4, 18.5, 94, 95) apply automatically; no affirmative hybrid classification election is available to alter Canadian tax character.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies made in Canada (and, for small-supplier status, worldwide). A person making taxable supplies other than exclusively zero-rated supplies must register if cumulative taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident limited partnership carrying on commercial activity in Canada is required to register even below the threshold if it solicits orders in Canada, has a permanent establishment in Canada, or supplies digital/distance services under the deemed supply regime effective July 1, 2021.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property exported by the vendor (or delivered outside Canada to a non-resident for use outside Canada), services rendered to non-residents (except certain situations), transportation services to or from Canada, and qualifying ancillary services. For ISIC 3240 manufactured Montessori wooden blocks, the export of the completed goods from Canada to a foreign buyer ordinarily qualifies as a zero-rated supply under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 20 if the exporter has reasonable evidence of export in its books of account.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada has no federal real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate; no continuous transaction controls, no digital stamping, and no mandatory invoice validation by CRA before issuance. Vendors must issue invoices containing prescribed information under Excise Tax Act s. 169(4) and the Input Tax Credit Information (GST/HST) Regulations. CRA accepts electronic invoices provided they are legible, complete, and retained in a CRA-compatible electronic format. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by most registrants using GST/HST NETFILE (XML/API gateway or CRA-approved software). Electronic records must be retained for at least six years.

CRA may reassess GST/HST beyond the normal four-year statutory period (up to seven years) for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Penalties for knowingly or negligently making false statements or participating in fraudulent schemes, including fictitious export claims, include fines of up to 250% of the amount of tax evaded and imprisonment on summary conviction or indictment. Where zero-rated export documentation is insufficient or the goods are subsequently returned or diverted to Canada, CRA may claw back input tax credits previously claimed, impose interest on unpaid tax, and issue penalties under ETA ss. 280–285. Systems for documentary validation are heavily scrutinised; exporters must retain the standard export documentation bundle, including customs export declarations, bills of lading, commercial invoices, freight documentation, and proof of sales proceeds returning, for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible hard currency. Both current-account transactions (goods, services, royalties) and capital-account transactions (foreign direct investment, portfolio flows, intercompany loans) are free from exchange-control licensing or approval. Profit remittances and non-resident partnership distributions are unrestricted, provided the payer deducts Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% (reducible under an applicable tax treaty) on income of a Canadian-resident limited partnership allocated to non-resident partners; the LP must also comply with FATCA and CRS reporting and maintain Form NR4/NR6 filing discipline. No practical convertibility hurdles exist beyond standard AML/KYC bank due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory requirements to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency into CAD within any prescribed timeline. Exporters and Limited Partnerships may retain export receipts in interest-bearing foreign-currency bank accounts at authorized Canadian financial institutions under the Bank Act. No mandatory conversion ratio applies; repatriation timing is a treasury decision, subject only to FINTRAC reporting thresholds (CTR at CAD 10,000) and the entity's normal tax accounting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act, in coordination with the Federal Minister of Finance and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); no standalone foreign-exchange control authority exists as Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital account regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High only for Canadian-regulated fintechs or payment-service providers registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, hold a provincial licence (e.g., AMF Québec). Wholly offshore digital EMIs that lack Canadian MSB registration or partnership with a licensed custodian are not lawful for holding client funds of a Canadian-resident LP; they are exposed to correspondent-bank de-risking and will trigger enhanced scrutiny for routes receiving Amazon Pay and Stripe payouts. A restricted trust company licence or a sponsored MSB arrangement is preferred over an unregulated offshore EMI.
