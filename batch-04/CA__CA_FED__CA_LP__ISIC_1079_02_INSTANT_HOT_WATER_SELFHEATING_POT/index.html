Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Canadian limited partnership. The Declaration of Limited Partnership must state each limited partner’s agreed contribution, and contributions may be cash or property under the partnership agreement. No regulatory escrow account is mandated for formation, but financial institutions must perform beneficial-ownership verification under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act before deposits are treated as paid-up capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the National Fire Code and provincial Fire Code; electrical system inspection under the Canadian Electrical Code; structural load inspection and certification by a professional engineer; workplace clearance under the provincial Occupational Health and Safety Act, including WHMIS training, hazardous dust control, thermal burn hazard mitigation, and emergency evacuation plans.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account electronic services.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity establishing a Canadian Limited Partnership obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST account (RT-prefix) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or the RC1/RC2 paper forms. The application must include the LP registration certificate, partnership agreement, foreign incorporation certificate, and identification of non-resident partners. The BN and GST/HST account are issued upon CRA registration review and take effect from the first taxable supply made in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under the Income Tax Act (Canada) a Limited Partnership is not a taxpayer; income and losses are allocated to resident and non-resident partners, while GST/HST is administered at the partnership level as a separate 'person'.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (i) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident partners to file Part I tax returns instead of Part XIII withholding; (ii) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(7); (iii) ITA s. 97(2) election for transfer of partnership property at fair market value; and (iv) Excise Tax Act s. 273 close-connection election for GST/HST on inter-entity supplies. Canada has no check-the-box regime: classification follows statutory legal form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies made in Canada before the small-supplier exception ceases (ETA s. 148); mandatory GST/HST registration once the threshold is crossed or when a non-resident carries on a 'business in Canada' through a permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act: goods exported commercially from Canada (s. 5) and services supplied to non-residents that meet the offshore-service conditions (s. 10–23). The supplier must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence of export, be a GST/HST registrant, and make the supply as part of a commercial activity to claim input tax credits.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed with CBSA via CAED/G7
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued by the third-party carrier
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list evidencing the export sale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire confirmation from the foreign buyer
  • Freight documentation and insurance certificates where separately charged

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate currently applies in Canada. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE. For exports, the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED/G7) is used to transmit the B13A declaration to the Canada Border Services Agency for cargo valued over CAD 2,000; digital invoice data is retained by the registrant rather than cleared by the tax authority.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims and may reassess within four years, extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation. Fraudulent claims expose the registrant to Excise Tax Act s. 326.1 penalties up to CAD 25,000 and/or 200% of tax evaded, interest, retrospective clawback of input tax credits, deregistration, and prosecution under the Criminal Code for tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada applies a free-floating exchange-rate regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without central-bank pre-approval. Profit remittances, distribution payments to limited partners, shareholder loans, and capital repatriation from a Canadian Limited Partnership are legally unobstructed. Practical compliance gates are limited to: (i) Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII or treaty relief documentation if a non-resident partner receives distributions; (ii) FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, and related Canadian trade controls. No exchange-rate conversion certificate or balance-of-payments authorization is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained offshore, held in foreign-currency accounts, or converted at commercial rates. The Bank of Canada does not require repatriation within any statutory timeline. For statistical purposes, exporters may be surveyed by Statistics Canada; financial institutions must record cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC. Export of self-heating prepared-food products also requires a CFIA health/export certificate and compliance with Canadian Food Inspection Agency facility registration, but no foreign-exchange approval attaches to those export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; cross-border financial intelligence and reporting are administered by FINTRAC; banking and payment system supervision is coordinated with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). There is no separate foreign-exchange-control or surrender-monitoring authority in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as banking-as-a-service wallets can support cross-border settlement, supplier payments, and foreign-currency pooling. Canadian law, however, prohibits unauthorized deposit-taking under the Bank Act; a non-bank EMI cannot offer a true deposit or overnight lending product. A foreign EMI operating in Canada must register as a money-service business with FINTRAC, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act once licensed, and maintain a Canadian agent for AML supervision. Such platforms are acceptable as supplementary payment corridors but are not a substitute for a regulated Canadian Schedule I bank operating account or for issuable trade finance instruments such as commercial letters of credit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions are routed as SWIFT MT103 priority payments from the contributing partner's bank to the Canadian Limited Partnership's operational or escrow account. The beneficiary account must encode the Canadian financial-institution number and branch transit number, for example a 3-digit institution code and 5-digit transit number, followed by the account number. The beneficiary name must match the partnership certificate. The remitter name and address must exactly mirror the remitting account holder. In SWIFT field 70, the narrative should read: 'Capital contribution to Canadian limited partnership; no proceeds of crime; for account of [entity name].' Canadian receiving banks automatically report cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC; no exchange-control permit is needed. For contributions exceeding CAD 50,000, a source-of-funds questionnaire and confirmation of the contributor's banking due-diligence file will be required. The transaction must be booked to the partnership capital account for tax-allocation and Part XIII withholding analysis on future distributions.Outbound trade disbursements are cleared over three rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers to foreign suppliers, with full supplier beneficiary details and invoice reference in field 70; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 for medium-value or compliance-sensitive purchase orders; (iii) Canadian-dollar domestic settlement through Payments Canada LVTS for local vendor payments, using the receiving bank's institution and transit number. U.S.-dollar disbursements are cleared through Canadian banks' U.S. correspondent relationships over Fedwire or CHIPS. Documentary collections and bankers' acceptances are available for larger transactions.
