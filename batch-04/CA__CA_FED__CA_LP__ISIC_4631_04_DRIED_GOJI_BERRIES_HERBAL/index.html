Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and My Business Account portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or Canadian limited partnership must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering through the CRA's 'Register a business' online service or by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and certain program accounts). Required information includes entity legal name, partnership agreement, Canadian resident representative or agent information, and contact details. If expected GST/HST taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, the applicant simultaneously opens a GST/HST program account (type RT or RC). The CRA assigns the BN immediately for online applications, with program account confirmation issued by mail. Registration should be done before making taxable supplies in Canada and before filing tax returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is not a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act; income is allocated to partners under the partnership agreement and reported on each partner's return, with Form T5013 issued. No check-the-box election exists in Canada. Relevant statutory elections include (i) the partnership fiscal-period election under subsection 249.1(4) to adopt a non-calendar year-end, (ii) the subsection 216(1) election for non-resident partners with Canadian rental or timber income to pay tax on a net basis, and (iii) the GST/HST 'Quick Method' election, although generally unavailable to wholesalers. Hybrid mismatch elections are not specifically available in Canadian domestic legislation, but Canadian partnerships with foreign partners may be affected by hybrid mismatch rules under the OECD model.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over four consecutive calendar quarters; below this threshold the partnership is a 'small supplier' and is generally not required to register, except for non-resident entities that may be required to register if they carry on business in Canada and make taxable supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals exported from Canada are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Zero-rating applies when the supplier (i) sells goods to a non-resident customer and sends the goods directly to an address outside Canada, (ii) delivers goods to a common carrier for export, or (iii) exports goods as a commercial shipment evidenced by customs documentation. The exporter must retain proof of export, generally within two years, and the goods must not be consumed or resold in Canada before export. For goods that are not physically removed from Canada, zero-rating may still apply if the supply is an 'exported supply' under the Direct Export Rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency export report) when the goods are exported in a commercial manner, including a Canada Export Decree or eManifest, with the exporter's Registration Number (BN/RT)
  • Bill of Lading (or waybill, warehouse receipt, or similar shipping document) evidencing the transport from Canada to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire confirmation and proof of foreign currency conversion showing receipt of payment from the non-resident purchaser

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping is required at the federal level. Canada has adopted the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing network as a voluntary standard. CRA accepts paper or electronic invoices, provided they contain prescribed GST/HST information, and audits will examine electronic records when retained. There is no requirement for CRA pre-clearance of invoices or digital stamping.

CRA and CBSA can jointly audit export transactions to verify that goods were physically removed from Canada and that all documentation supports zero-rating. Failure to maintain adequate evidence within the statutory period results in the reassessment of GST/HST at 5% (plus applicable provincial HST) on the export sale, with interest compounding. Where a misrepresentation consists of negligence or willful misconduct, subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act authorizes a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the net tax amount understated. In cases of fraudulent export claims, criminal prosecution can be pursued under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code of Canada, with fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. CRA can reclaim refunds and audit records for up to four years, or seven years in cases of fraud or misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility and formally adheres to IMF Article VIII. There is no exchange-control licensing, no prior central-bank approval for remittances, and no restriction on the repatriation of profits, dividends, capital, or partnership distributions. The Canadian dollar trades under a free float. The only material offset is fiscal rather than FX-related: non-resident partners receiving Canadian-source partnership income are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25%, subject to reduction under an income tax treaty), which the Canadian paying or distributing entity must deduct and remit to the Canada Revenue Agency. This withholding obligation does not require FX release or conversion approval but demands an effective compliance workflow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for bringing export proceeds onshore, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export receipts. Export proceeds may be received in CAD, USD, EUR, CNY, or any freely convertible currency and may be retained indefinitely in domestic foreign-currency accounts or offshore treasury centres. The only applicable reporting obligation is under FINTRAC: every international electronic funds transfer (EFT) of CAD $10,000 or more must be included in bank submissions to FINTRAC, and banks may request a stated purpose code and supporting trade documents for cross-border settlement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority and ultimate prudential overseer; no dedicated foreign exchange control administration exists in Canada. Foreign exchange supervision is dispersed among the Department of Finance Canada (exchange policy), OSFI (prudential conduct of banks), FINTRAC (AML/EFT reporting), and Payments Canada (clearing infrastructure).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium viability as a primary treasury rail and highly constrained as a substitute for a Schedule I bank account. Canada does not recognise the EU-style 'Electronic Money Institution' licence. Deposit-taking, settlement in Canadian dollars, and provision of payment accounts are the exclusive domain of banks authorised under the Bank Act. A fintech/EMI acting for the CA_LP without a FINTRAC MSB (Money Services Business) registration, or without a licence under the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by the Bank of Canada, cannot lawfully clear CAD-denominated transactions through Payments Canada rails. Offshore EMIs may be used only as an auxiliary collection corridor, and all net funds must be routed to a regulated Schedule I bank account. Practical viability improves if the EMI operates as a registered Foreign Money Services Business with FINTRAC and maintains a sponsor-bank relationship with a Canadian clearing bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory document-filing threshold; CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File equivalent) for all intercompany transactions, and penalty de minimis is an adjustment exceeding the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue; Country-by-Country reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ CAD 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada applies GAAR (s. 245) and sham doctrine; CRA will disregard transactions lacking commercial substance. For a Limited Partnership, limited partners must not participate in management (losing limited liability and creating Canadian permanent establishment risk); physical warehouse, purchase orders, invoicing, and inventory risk must sit in Canada to prevent recharacterization of the LP as a conduit

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (s. 163(2)); criminal prosecution under s. 239 ITA: fines between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years on summary conviction or 5 years on indictment

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted Part XIII withholdings, GST/HST, and payroll deductions; a participating limited partner loses limited-liability protection and becomes personally exposed to criminal fines, civil restitution, and cross-border arrest/extraditable warrants
Shell Company / Sham Conduit

Statutory sanction: CRA recharacterizes the structure under sham doctrine or GAAR: full tax reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, 50% gross-negligence penalty, interest at prescribed rate plus 4%; possible Part I tax and 25% Part XIV branch tax on deemed Canadian profits

Director personal liability: General partner and any controlling limited partner are personally liable for the reassessed tax; directors face civil restitution and potential criminal charges under s. 239 ITA, with travel/extradition exposure under cross-border enforcement
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA transfer-pricing adjustment plus 10% penalty on the adjustment amount (where adjustment exceeds lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue); interest compounding daily at prescribed rate plus 4%; documentation penalty for failure to provide contemporaneous records

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for transfer-pricing adjustments, but will be liable for unremitted withholdings on adjusted payments (s. 227.1); egregious mis-invoicing may be prosecuted as tax evasion with director imprisonment
