Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the CBCA; shares may be issued for such consideration as the directors determine (s. 25) and may be paid up in instalments or received in property or past services; no escrow account or capital deposit is required at federal incorporation stage.

Funding & unlock path: The corporation must obtain a fire safety inspection certificate and building occupancy clearance from the applicable provincial or municipal fire marshal for the registered office premises; structural load inspections apply only where safes or heavy record-storage systems are installed. Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the CBCA; shares may be issued for such consideration as the directors determine (s. 25) and may be paid up in instalments or received in property or past services; no escrow account or capital deposit is required at federal incorporation stage.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
BE IT RESOLVED THAT: (1) the Corporation ratifies its Articles of Incorporation and the Certificate of Incorporation issued under the Canada Business Corporations Act; (2) the subscription for common shares by the initial subscribers is accepted, and shares are issued as fully paid for the consideration approved by the Board; (3) the registered office is established at the address recorded with Corporations Canada, to be changed only by subsequent directors' resolution; (4) the following officers are appointed: President, Secretary, and Treasurer, each with customary execution authority; (5) banking arrangements are opened at a Canadian chartered bank, with dual-signature authority for disbursements; and (6) the proposed by-laws are adopted, and management is authorized to complete all post-incorporation registrations with the Canada Revenue Agency.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA–ARC); primary portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); real-time registration via Business Registration Online (BRO); customs export administration by Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Customs Act and Excise Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

Corporations obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) before activating program accounts. Procedure: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act via Corporations Canada (or provincially) and obtain articles; resident-director rule observed (at least 25% of directors resident Canadians, CBCA s. 105(3)); (2) apply for BN plus program accounts RP (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RC (payroll) using CRA Form RC1 for Canadian corporations, CRA Form RC4607 for non-resident corporations not incorporated in Canada, or BRO electronic registration; (3) register extra-provincially in each province of carrying-on-business; (4) file T2 corporate income tax return within six months of fiscal year-end (non-residents: six-month statutory period with instalments on Canadian-source income). A non-resident corporation making taxable supplies in Canada that is not a small supplier must obtain GST/HST registration before commencing commercial activity (ETA s. 240).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: Public or private Business Corporations are separate taxpayers under Part I of the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Public or private Business Corporations are separate taxpayers under Part I of the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)). Federal general corporate rate: 38% less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction = 15%; Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) benefit from the small business deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of qualifying active business income (federal 9% SBD rate). No pass-through, check-the-box, or LLC-style flow-through election is available at the federal level. Territorial scope: resident corporations taxed on worldwide income (ITA s. 2(1)); non-resident corporations taxed on Canadian-source employment, business income, and gains from taxable Canadian property (ITA ss. 2(3), 115) and subject to Part XIII withholding (25%, treaty-reduced) on passive payments. An affiliated ESOP trust is itself taxed as an inter vivos trust under ITA s. 104 at the top marginal rate; employer corporate deductions for stock-option benefits arise under ITA s. 7(1)(a).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada. Statutory elections available: (i) ITA s. 85(1) rollover election on qualifying transfers of eligible property to a corporation; (ii) ITA s. 83(2) capital-dividend election from the Capital Dividend Account; (iii) ITA s. 256 associated-corporation elections affecting the CAD 500,000 small business deduction limit; (iv) ITA s. 219(2) branch tax election for non-resident corporations; (v) ITA s. 216 election (non-resident elects net-basis Part I taxation of Canadian rental income); (vi) ITA s. 216.1 election (non-resident service providers); (vii) ITA s. 7(8) election to treat employee stock-option benefits as a cash-settlement payment; (viii) ETA s. 150 election for employer reimbursement of employee expenses; (ix) ETA s. 211.1 close-connection election for supplies among related GST/HST registrants; (x) ETA s. 156 election for deemed taxable supplies between closely related corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated financial services) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter, per the small supplier rule (ETA s. 148(1); ss. 240-241). A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada that exceeds the threshold must register for GST/HST; an ESOP-trust administrator whose operations include 'financial services' as defined in ETA s. 123(1) may additionally qualify as a 'selected listed financial institution' and be subject to special attribution and recapture rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(i) Goods exported from Canada: zero-rated under s. 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA where the supplier possesses contemporaneous evidence that the goods were shipped and exported 'as soon as is reasonably possible' after delivery (CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4.4.1). (ii) Services supplied to a non-resident: zero-rated under s. 7 of Part V of Schedule V to the ETA unless the service is rendered entirely to a person in Canada, relates to real property situated in Canada, relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or constitutes a 'financial service' (ETA s. 123(1)); financial services are exempt, not zero-rated, regardless of recipient residence. For an ISIC 6430 ESOP-trust corporate administrator: plan administration, recordkeeping, custody coordination, payroll-equity transfer processing, and compliance services supplied to a non-resident plan sponsor may qualify for zero-rating, but arranging for and executing securities transfers (a financial service) remains exempt, and input tax credits do not attach to the exempt component.

  • CBSA Commercial Export Declaration (Form B13A) or CAED/CARM electronic export declaration for reportable commercial goods
  • Commercial invoice / Canada Customs Invoice (CCI) containing export shipment particulars and zero-rating notation
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued by the carrying carrier evidencing physical export from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103/foreign-currency deposit advice evidencing conversion and remittance of export proceeds
  • Packing list, freight/carriage evidence, and insurance cover notes as contemporaneous corroborative documentation

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime applies to federal GST/HST. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1 million (GST/HST NETFILE and My Business Account remittance). Every registrant must issue a tax invoice in prescribed form (ETA s. 223 and s. 242) containing supplier name and BN, recipient name, transaction date, consideration, and GST/HST rate charged; zero-rated export invoices require a notation of zero-rating or the applicable schedule provision. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is voluntary for B2B/B2G electronic exchange; Quebec imposes separate e-invoicing and QST reporting rules but not federal GST/HST clearance.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period; the period becomes unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default is established (ITA s. 152(4); ETA s. 298). Where zero-rating documentation is deficient, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST/HST, and previously allowed refunds, input tax credits, and rebates are clawed back with statutory interest (ETA s. 280) plus a 25% gross-negligence penalty (ETA s. 280(1)). Fraudulent claims are criminally prosecutable under ETA s. 327 (fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment). For ESOP-trust corporate administrators, exempt financial-service components attract no input tax credits, and mixed-supply allocations on administration and equity-transfer fees are an active CRA audit target. Canada imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure represents CRA administrative audit preference for documented receipt of foreign consideration and standard banking documentary practice rather than a legal mandate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirements for foreign direct investment, and no statutory restrictions on the repatriation of investment capital, dividends, interest, or trust distributions by a non-resident beneficiary. Profit remittance from a Canadian resident ESOP trust is generally unrestricted; however, payments to non-resident beneficiaries may be subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty. Dividends or distributions sourced from the trust must be properly characterized (dividend, interest, or trust distribution) to determine the correct withholding and reporting treatment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank or to convert foreign currency receipts into Canadian dollars. An ESOP trust collecting cross-border dividends, subscription proceeds, or intercompany settlements may retain funds in any eligible currency in an approved foreign currency account. No mandatory conversion ratio applies. Any requirement would arise solely from contractual covenants with the commercial bank, not from Canadian law. Cross-border electronic transfers of C$10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC, and the Commercial Bank must comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) as monetary authority; OSFI as banking supervisor; Foreign Exchange Administration is not a separate body because Canada operates a liberalized, no-approval capital movement regime under the IMF Articles of Agreement, Article VIII. FINTRAC administers AML/ATF measures for cross-border transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for reliance on offshore digital EMIs as the primary banking rail. An offshore EMI cannot hold Canadian-dollar customer funds as a bank unless licensed under the Bank Act (Schedule I or II). It may operate in Canada only as a foreign money services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC, subject to limited payment-processing capabilities. Such firms cannot provide trade finance, commercial letters of credit, or custody-grade capital escrow. They are acceptable only for ancillary payment collection if integrated with a regulated federally chartered Canadian bank, and even then risk is elevated because beneficiary-name matching and IN/OUT transfer rules may create friction for payroll and equity settlements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Source-of-funds ambiguity due to an inbound capital transfer for share subscription routed through an intermediary jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Provide a valid government-issued passport for each director and settlor, a tax residency certificate, a police clearance certificate where requested, a jurisdiction-specific sanctions and PEP search clearance, an independent legal counsel opinion confirming no sanction nexus, and a written undertaking that no additional powers of attorney will be executed without prior bank notification.

Root cause: Ongoing transaction profile inconsistency because the ESOP trust engages in payroll disbursements and equity transfers that are not clearly linked to the stated commercial purpose (administration of employee share incentive plans).

Capital escrow account structure is misused as a general operating current account, triggering retrospective transaction monitoring alarms.

Root cause: Capital escrow account structure is misused as a general operating current account, triggering retrospective transaction monitoring alarms.

Defense evidence: Execute a bank-facility agreement segregating the Capital Escrow Account from the Operational Account. Provide the banking purpose statement, the trust deed clause restricting use of escrow funds to equity acquisitions, the board resolution defining withdrawal signatories and dual-approval limits, and a written compliance plan confirming that dividends, interest, and distributions will flow only through the Operational Account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Canadian ESOP trust from the parent company or non-resident shareholder should be routed exclusively via the SWIFT network using an MT103 or MT202 COVER message, with the beneficiary bank being a Schedule I Canadian bank. The wire must clearly state a compliant purpose code on the cover page and in the narrative, typically 'Capital Subscription and Contribution' or 'CORP Treasury Funding — Employee Share Trust Capital Injection', referencing the trust deed and the underlying ESOP agreement. For amounts exceeding C$10,000, the receiving bank will trigger FINTRAC EFT reporting; the sending intermediary bank must preserve the full name, address, account number, and tax identification of the originator. No Bank of Canada permission or exchange-control license is required, but the commercial bank will run sanctions, PEP, and negative-news screening on every participant and counterparty. Clean funds must be evidenced with a board resolution, signed subscription agreement, and prior audit report where the source is a corporate treasury.For equity purchase settlements, trust distributions, and participant payouts, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct beneficiary-credited wires, supported by Fedwire/CHIPS connectivity where the receiving institution is in the United States. For larger or documentary transactions, the trust may instruct a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700 series) issued by the Canadian bank and confirmed by a foreign correspondent, especially when acquiring cross-listed securities or settling international M&A-related transfers. Payroll disbursements to employees and contractor beneficiaries should be processed through the Operational Account using ACH/CAD-Payments Canada (LYNX or the older Large Value Transfer System) for domestic items and MT103 for cross-border items. All outgoing payments must include correct Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) if applicable, tax withholding certificates where distributions are subject to Part XIII, and an internal payment-clearing policy that separates equity-transfer funds from operational cash and verifies beneficiary-name match before release.FX Forward Contracts (Vanilla Deliverable Forwards) permissible for hedging known-dated USD/CAD employee stock subscription proceedsFX Options (European or American style) permissible for protecting capital inflows against adverse Canadian-dollar appreciationCross-Currency Swaps permissible for long-duration hedging of foreign-currency borrowing or foreign-currency-denominated trust liabilitiesPrime Brokerage FX Lines permissible for fully collateralized positions held with a Schedule I Canadian bankNDF (Non-Deliverable Forward) permissible only where the underlying currency is not deliverable in Canada; for major currencies such as USD/EUR/GBP, NDFs are not necessary and Canadian prudential guidance discourages their use unless exceptional market dislocation exists

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for reliance on offshore digital EMIs as the primary banking rail. An offshore EMI cannot hold Canadian-dollar customer funds as a bank unless licensed under the Bank Act (Schedule I or II). It may operate in Canada only as a foreign money services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC, subject to limited payment-processing capabilities. Such firms cannot provide trade finance, commercial letters of credit, or custody-grade capital escrow. They are acceptable only for ancillary payment collection if integrated with a regulated federally chartered Canadian bank, and even then risk is elevated because beneficiary-name matching and IN/OUT transfer rules may create friction for payroll and equity settlements.
