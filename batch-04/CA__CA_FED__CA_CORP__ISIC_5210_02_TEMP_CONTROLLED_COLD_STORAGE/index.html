Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — national tax administration for the Minister of National Revenue, operating under the Income Tax Act, R.S.C., 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.), and Part IX of the Excise Tax Act, R.S.C., 1985, c.E-15. Primary registration and compliance portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) via canada.ca/business-registration-online; My Business Account (MABA) via canada.ca/my-business-account; and the Non-Resident Business Registration service for foreign corporations, together with CBSA customs accounts (eManifest/CAED) handled through the Canada Border Services Agency portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine nexus — a foreign corporation carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) under the CRA one-stop registration system. Step 2: Submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or the CRA Non-Resident Registration channel; a foreign entity without a Canadian registered office must provide certified corporate documents (articles of incorporation, continuous disclosure), appoint a resident agent for service, and, if applicable, file extra-provincial registration in the province of operations. Step 3: CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (e.g., 123456789) with program-account suffixes controlling the tax accounts: RC for federal corporate income tax, RP for GST/HST, RM for payroll, RD for import/export, and RT for trust accounts. Step 4: GST/HST registration (ETA subsection 240(1)) is triggered automatically when the threshold is exceeded or elected voluntarily; the VAT/GST identifier is the BN followed by 'RP' (e.g., 123456789RP0001). Step 5: The BN must appear on all invoices, customs export declarations, and CRA filings; changes to directors, registered address, business activity, or corporate structure must be reported to CRA within 30 days. Step 6: Non-resident registrants claiming input tax credits for zero-rated exports must register and may be required to provide security under the ETA where the CRA deems collection at risk; the BN is the single statutory TIN for federal tax purposes in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian federal or provincial business corporation (Inc., Corp., Ltd.) is a separate taxable 'person' under the Income Tax Act and is taxed at the entity level under Part I on worldwide income if resident in Canada (incorporated in Canada or centrally managed and controlled in Canada). A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income and is also subject to Part XIV branch tax of 25% on after-tax profits remitted abroad, subject to income tax treaty reduction (e.g., US-Canada treaty generally 15% branch tax). Canada has no check-the-box pass-through election for corporations; entity-level taxation is mandatory, with corporate losses locked inside the entity except in carefully structured amalgamations, winding-up rules, or loss-transfer mechanisms.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election for entity classification. The CRA classifies foreign legal entities according to the International Tax Audit Manual (ITAM) principles, generally respecting the juridical form under the home jurisdiction. Statutory election mechanisms include: (i) subsection 216(1) election for non-residents receiving Canadian real-property rental income to compute taxable income on a net basis rather than gross Part XIII withholding; (ii) subsection 219(2) election for a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada to pay Part XIV branch tax directly and operate under a deemed permanent establishment basis; (iii) deemed-resident and deeming provisions in section 250, including the treaty-based deemed non-resident election available to corporations managed and controlled in a treaty country; (iv) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) enabling simplified tax remittance for smaller registrants; (v) GST/HST annual filing election (Form GST20); (vi) simplified input tax credit election for qualifying non-resident registrants (Form GST34); and (vii) hybrid mismatch rules enacted under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act (effective for 2022+) which disallow duplicate deductions where the same payment receives a distinct tax treatment in Canada and a foreign jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small supplier threshold under ETA section 148. A person (including a non-resident corporation) becomes a registrant when the total value of worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports and financial services) made in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in the previous four consecutive calendar quarters, or where the thresholds projected for the current quarter and the immediately preceding three quarters are exceeded. Registration is mandatory immediately upon crossing the threshold. For a temperature-controlled cold storage operator, warehousing and ancillary logistics services supplied to Canadian customers are taxable supplies counting toward the threshold; storage services in respect of exported goods may be zero-rated and are excluded from the small-supplier computation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, a supply of goods that is exported from Canada is zero-rated provided the supplier obtains documentary evidence satisfactory to the CRA showing that the goods were exported (or delivered to a common carrier, customs broker, freight forwarder, or export warehouse for export) within 60 days after the supply, and that the goods did not undergo substantial transformation within Canada before departure. Additionally, ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 12 zero-rates a supply of a service of storing or keeping tangible personal property in a storage facility where the property is, or is to be, exported, subject to the same documentary evidence. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under ETA Schedule VII where the service is performed for a non-resident and is not in respect of or performed in connection with real property situated in Canada — an important limitation because a supply of warehousing services in respect of Canadian-located cold storage may be a supply in respect of immovable property in Canada, precluding Schedule VII zero-rating; however, the Schedule VI export-goods storage provision remains available where the goods are exported within statutory timelines. For a refrigerated logistics warehousing operation, zero-rating applies to storage and handling charges for perishable pharmaceuticals that leave Canada for the US or other foreign destinations, provided title and risk pass to the non-resident customer prior to export and the complete evidentiary bundle is assembled.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration transmitted through the CBSA Canadian Export Declaration portal (G7/CAED or B13A form where required), evidencing the exporter identity, HS classification, quantity, value, and shipment date
  • Commercial invoice issued by the cold storage operator or the non-resident principal, describing the goods, terms of sale (Incoterms), and the agreement that title and risk pass outside Canada
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill) issued by the carrier, or a CMR / truck bill of lading for surface freight, showing the port/point of exit from Canada and the foreign destination
  • CBSA cargo release records (A8A/A8B) and eManifest transmission logs confirming border clearance of the export shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank foreign-exchange receipt (SWIFT MT103, bank remittance advice, FX contract) proving receipt of payment from the non-resident customer in foreign currency and documenting repatriation of the export proceeds to the exporter's Canadian or foreign bank account
  • Warehouse custody log, cold-chain temperature chart, inventory movement record, and access-control data tying the specific goods to the export transaction
  • Non-resident customer purchase order and executed export contract establishing the agreed shipment terms, storage duration, and destination
  • Power of attorney or export-authorization agreement where the cold storage operator files the export declaration on behalf of the foreign owner

Canada does not currently mandate continuous transaction controls (CTC), real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or a national PEPPOL/electronic-invoicing clearance regime. However, mandatory requirements include: (i) every GST/HST registrant must issue an invoice containing the prescribed information under section 3 of the GST/HST Regulations, namely the supplier's name and Business Number (BN), date of supply, description of the goods/services, total consideration, and the GST/HST amount charged (or an indication that the price includes tax); (ii) electronic invoices are acceptable in any format (PDF, EDI, XML, JSON, ERP e-invoice) provided the required data fields are preserved and the records are legible; (iii) registrants maintaining electronic books and records must retain them in an electronically readable format and must produce them to the CRA on demand (ETA section 286); (iv) the CRA's electronic filing and remittance obligations apply through My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE for prescribed returns; and (v) if the cold storage business has a branch in Quebec, Revenu Québec's digital record-keeping standards apply, but no federal real-time clearance stamp is required. Best practice is to implement an ERP/EDI architecture capable of emitting GST/HST-compliant electronic invoices and preserving complete audit trails for export zero-rating claims.

CRA applies a risk-based audit program to zero-rated exporters, focusing on documentary sufficiency, export timing, and the commercial reality of the supply. The statutory reassessment window under the Excise Tax Act is four years from the date of the notice of assessment, extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default, or where a taxpayer files a waiver. If a zero-rating claim fails substantiation, the CRA recharacterizes the supply as taxable at the applicable GST/HST rate (5% federal, or 13%/15% in HST provinces) and reassesses the tax from the original reporting period with compounding daily arrears interest prescribed by the Minister. Penalties under ETA section 285 for false statements or omissions equal the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the net tax assessed; ETA section 280 imposes penalties for failure to register; and ETA section 286 penalizes failure to keep adequate books and records. Criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 is available for willful evasion, exposing the operator to fines up to CAD 20,000 per count and imprisonment for up to five years. Retrospective clawbacks and penalties are generally limited to four years unless the CRA establishes gross negligence, in which case the full 50% gross-negligence penalty (ETA section 285, analogous to ITA section 163.2) applies. Although Canada has no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST/HST zero-rating, the 180-day benchmark serves as the documentary and internal-control standard for evidencing completion of the export sale; the CRA expects export within 60 days of the supply and penalty exposure expands when the repatriated proceeds cannot be reconciled with CBSA export declarations. Mitigation requires retention of the complete customs and FX bundle for a minimum of seven years, reconciliation of zero-rated sales against CBSA data, and contemporaneous cold-chain documentation supporting each export claim.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII obligations. Canada imposes no exchange-control restrictions and treats current and capital account transactions alike: FDI equity injections, intercompany loans, trade payments, branch remittances, and liquidation proceeds are freely transferable without Bank of Canada or Finance Canada approval. Profit-remittance hurdles are limited to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends (statutory 25%, generally reduced to 5%-15% for treaty-eligible non-resident shareholders), FATCA/CRS self-certification, and standard documentary compliance by the paying Canadian bank. No remittance license or prior approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. Exporters may retain CAD or foreign-currency collections in domestic or offshore accounts and use them freely for trade settlement. The only obligations are statistical: Canadian banks report business ITRS transactions above CAD 10,000 to Statistics Canada, and FINTRAC requires cross-border EFT and large-cash reports above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT transaction supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); cross-border balance-of-payments reporting administered by Statistics Canada under the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000 (aggregate fair market value of all non-arm's-length non-resident intercompany transactions per taxation year) triggers the mandatory preparation and maintenance of a comprehensive transfer pricing documentation package, including local file and master file evidence; CbCR is separately required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: The CRA applies substance-over-form and may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s.245 of the Income Tax Act to recharacterize transactions lacking genuine commercial substance; for cold storage logistics, cognizable substance is evidenced by owned or leased refrigerated infrastructure, powered racking systems, temperature-control staff, and assumption of commercial inventory risk; OECD Pillar Two substance-based income exclusion also recognizes payroll and tangible-asset returns.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax (100% on second occurrence); criminal prosecution under ITA s.239(1) for evading tax: fine from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, or both fine and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in the evasion is jointly and severally liable for the penalties and restitution; conviction can trigger civil asset forfeiture, travel bans as a condition of bail, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Deficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to an arm's-length consideration; transfer-pricing penalty under ITA s.247(3) of 10% of the net upward adjustment; interest and consequential penalties on the additional Part I tax assessed.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced only where a director expressly directed or acquiesced in the false transfer-pricing documents with intent to evade; directorial liability then extends to civil restitution, monetary penalties, and, if fraud is proven, imprisonment.
Unlicensed Pharmaceutical Cold-Storage Operation

Statutory sanction: Food and Drugs Act and Food and Drug Regulations enforcement: medical device/drug embargo and confiscation, STOP-use orders, fines of up to CAD 200,000 per offence (summary conviction), and imprisonment for repeat or indicted violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who cause or permit the unauthorised activity can be personally charged; they face administrative monetary penalties, fiduciary liability for contaminated goods, a ban on operating regulated facilities, and criminal imprisonment.
Failure to Withhold, Remit and Report Part XIII Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: ITA s.227(8) failure-to-remit penalty of 10% of the amount (20% if the failure is wilful); interest accrues daily at the CRA prescribed rate; penalties for missing NR4 information returns start at CAD 100 per slip and can escalate to CAD 7,500 per slip; criminal conviction may result in fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for outstanding Part XIII withholdings and related penalties even after the corporation is dissolved; CRA may pursue civil recovery (garnishment and asset seizure), and wilful default can lead to travel restrictions and imprisonment.
