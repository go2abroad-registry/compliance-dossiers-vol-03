Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and Corporate Income Tax Registration via the CRA Business Registration portal (BRO) at canada.ca

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or supplying specified digital services to Canadian consumers must first obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by completing the online 'Register a Business Number' process or by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'. The foreign corporation must provide its legal name, registered office address, country of incorporation, corporate charter, director information, and, if required, a Canadian agent for service of process. After obtaining the BN, the entity may add program accounts including the GST/HST account (RT) and corporate income tax account (RC). For non-resident suppliers of digital products or services, registration for the simplified GST/HST regime is mandatory once revenue from specified digital supplies exceeds the CAD 30,000 threshold; registration can be completed remotely through the CRA's non-resident GST/HST registration channel. The GST/HST registration number serves as the statutory VAT identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election; all business corporations are taxed at the entity level. Available elections include the associated-corporations small business deduction allocation election, the GST/HST quick method election for small suppliers, the election by closely-related GST/HST registrants to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration, and elections pertaining to hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18(6) and 12(9). Treaty-based permanent establishment determinations may be elected under the dual-resident tie-break rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold); for non-resident suppliers of specified digital services, CAD 30,000 in global annual revenues from specified digital supplies to Canadian consumers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports, including digital magazine subscriptions and associated advertising services supplied to non-resident customers, is available under the Excise Tax Act where the supplier retains evidence that the recipient is a non-resident of Canada, the supply is delivered electronically outside Canada, the service is performed outside Canada, and the supply does not relate to real property situated in Canada. Goods exports are zero-rated if the supplier exports them in accordance with the Customs Act and holds the prescribed documentation. Digital downloads and electronically delivered periodicals are zero-rated when supplied to non-residents for use entirely outside Canada; testimonials, delivery IP logs, payment processor records and customer residency declarations may be used to substantiate export status.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada has no mandatory real-time electronic fiscal clearance or government-authorised digital stamping system. The CRA accepts electronic invoices and GST/HST returns filed electronically through NETFILE, GST/HST Internet File Transfer, or other approved EDI channels. Invoices must contain prescribed information including supplier name, BN/GST/HST registration number, date of invoice, description and tax amount. Provincial electronic record and e-invoicing mandates (e.g., Quebec's electronic invoicing project) are emerging separately; at the federal level no mandatory digital stamping or pre-clearance API filing is currently required.

CRA audit teams scrutinise export zero-rating claims to prevent fraudulent GST/HST recovery. A false zero-rating claim, misstatement of residency, artificial evidence, or failure to retain prescribed documentation results in reassessment, denial of refund, interest on unpaid tax, and penalties under the Excise Tax Act (including penalties up to 25% of the amounts claimed) as well as potential gross-negligence penalties and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act for wilful evasion. Retrospective clawbacks may extend the normal reassessment period to 10 years where gross negligence or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible and freely floating currency. The current and capital accounts are entirely open, with no exchange-control permits, surrender requirements, or prior approvals for convertibility. Non-resident shareholders may remit dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees abroad after deducting Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (generally 25%, reduced to 5–15% under Canada's income tax conventions). Profit repatriation is thus subject to tax compliance rather than exchange-administered approval. All foreign-currency transactions must be reflected in the company's financial statements under IFRS or Canadian ASPE as adopted for Canadian tax purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or conversion timeline on export proceeds. A Canadian exporter may retain, convert, or invest all export revenues in any freely convertible currency. Compliance obligations are limited to ordinary corporate tax reporting, transfer-pricing documentation for non-arm's-length exports (Form T106), and export trade-reporting obligations through Statistics Canada (e.g., the B13 export declaration / Canadian Export Reporting System).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential banking regulation delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering authority is the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration, and exchange-control requirements under the Currency Act and former Foreign Exchange Control Regulations have been abolished.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Offshore fintech EMI/digital-bank providers cannot be used as the primary treasury or settlement banking layer because carrying on deposit-taking activities in Canada requires authorization under the Bank Act and prudential regulation by OSFI. A non-Canadian EMI has no passporting rights into Canada; however, it may lawfully serve as an ancillary payment-instrument or payout rail for isolated transactions. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act and Bank of Canada retail-payment supervision framework, any payment-service provider facilitated for Canadian users must register with the Bank of Canada and comply with associated operational-risk requirements. For AdSense, Stripe, and sponsored-media remittances, the Canadian corporation should nonetheless maintain a fully regulated Schedule I bank account to avoid legal ambiguity and fragmented liquidity.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Beneficial ownership obscured by a non-resident corporate chain, trust structure, or nominee director

Defense evidence: A current Corporations Canada extract, a certificate or register of individuals with significant control (Form 3006/3007), a signed beneficial-ownership attestation, a complete chain of incorporation and ownership documents, notarized certified copies of shareholders' agreements and share registers, and an organizational chart identifying every intermediate holding entity and ultimate beneficial owner.
High-volume digital advertising and sponsored-media pay-in from Stripe, AdSense, ad networks, and affiliate platforms

Root cause: High-volume digital advertising and sponsored-media pay-in from Stripe, AdSense, ad networks, and affiliate platforms

Defense evidence: Publisher agreements, ad-network revenue and payout statements, platform verification reports, a complete list of merchant accounts, bank statements showing commercial traffic from the payment rails, written explanation of the content monetization model, website traffic analytics, and invoices or contracts issued to advertising sponsors.
Non-resident directors and shareholders with no Canadian credit, address, or business-trail

Root cause: Non-resident directors and shareholders with no Canadian credit, address, or business-trail

Defense evidence: Notarized passports, foreign government-issued photo identification, proof of residential address (utility bill or bank reference), prior banking references from reputable financial institutions, foreign tax-identification numbers, curriculum vitae or industry background, documented source of wealth, and an explanation of the non-resident connection to the Canadian publishing business.
Sanctions-list, political-exposure, or adverse-media name-match on the company or connected persons

Root cause: Sanctions-list, political-exposure, or adverse-media name-match on the company or connected persons

Defense evidence: Independent sanctions and adverse-media screening results, a legal opinion on the screening match, sworn declarations by the subject explaining the false positive, proof of birth certificate and resulting change-of-name documents, Canadian police or relevant foreign police reference, and detailed documentary proof of the legitimate commercial source of the inbound capital.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A compliant inbound capital injection by a non-resident parent or investor is executed through SWIFT MT103 (or, for intra-bank movements, MT202) to the Canadian Schedule I bank's correspondent accounts. The remittance instruction should identify the Canadian corporate beneficiary, the bank account number, and the purpose as 'paid-up share capital' or 'shareholder loan'. The corporate data must be reconciled with the Corporations Canada registration and the company's audited/accounting paid-up-capital records. A report of the share allotment must be filed with Corporations Canada (Form 3003); the transaction must be reported to Statistics Canada under the Balance of Payments / Foreign Direct Investment reporting framework where the threshold applies. Investment Canada Act review thresholds must be assessed because digital-magazine publishing may fall within 'cultural businesses', which carry a lower review threshold on non-resident control acquisitions.Trade settlements to foreign publishers, authors, production vendors, and technology suppliers are cleared via SWIFT MT103 direct-credit instructions, or via a confirmed/unconfirmed commercial letter of credit issued on SWIFT MT700 where the beneficiary requires payment protection. Canadian-dollar disbursements are cleared domestically through Payments Canada's Lynx / Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); US-dollar payments are cleared via the correspondent banks' Fedwire or CHIPS networks; euro or other-currency payments are routed through global correspondent rails. Dividends and interest remitted to non-resident shareholders must be sent by MT103 with the appropriate Part XIII withholding deducted, reported, and remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency under the applicable NR4 information-return framework.Deliverable over-the-counter foreign-exchange forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) with Schedule I Canadian chartered banks or their dealer subsidiaries.Over-the-counter cross-currency swaps and FX options with OSFI-regulated counterparties or registered Canadian derivatives dealers.Listed foreign-exchange futures and options on the Bourse de Montréal (TMX Group) for hedging CAD positions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible for Canadian counterparties, but are irrelevant and rarely executable for CAD crosses because CAD is a fully deliverable and freely convertible currency; NDF use is strictly limited to restricted or offshore currencies in the treasury's currency portfolio.
