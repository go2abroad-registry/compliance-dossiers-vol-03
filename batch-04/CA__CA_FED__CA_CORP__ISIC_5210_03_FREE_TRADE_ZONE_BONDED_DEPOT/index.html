Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST portal, Business Registration Online, and My Business Account

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that carries on business in Canada or makes taxable supplies in Canada must register for a Business Number (BN), GST/HST account, payroll, and import/export accounts through the CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO). The foreign entity may appoint a resident agent or use a non-resident registration process. The CRA assigns a 9-digit BN plus a 2-digit program account identifier (e.g., RT for GST/HST). Proof of legal existence (certificate of incorporation or foreign registration), board resolution, and Canadian address or resident agent details are required. Registration must occur before the first taxable supply or import; for non-residents, voluntary registration is permitted to claim input tax credits or zero-rate exports.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box partnership classification exists under Canadian federal tax law. A Canadian business corporation is treated as a separate taxable entity subject to federal income tax under the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST purposes, an election may be available for closely related corporations to treat supplies as made for nominal consideration (section 156 election); a foreign entity may elect to use the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration where all supplies are zero-rated; and certain joint venture elections and GST/HST branch accounting elections are available. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under the Income Tax Act to deny deductions in cross-border arrangements, but no entity classification election analogous to the U.S. check-the-box is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies, unless the supply is zero-rated; non-residents making zero-rated supplies may register voluntarily. The threshold applies to taxable supplies made in Canada in a calendar quarter and the preceding four calendar quarters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods exported from Canada provided the exporter has documentary proof satisfying CRA that the goods were exported as part of a commercial activity, including exports by non-residents and supplies of services performed outside Canada or in respect of exported goods. For goods, the supplier must transfer ownership or possession, and the goods must be delivered outside Canada or to a common carrier for export within a reasonable time. Zero-rating for a bonded warehouse depot applies when the goods are shipped from the depot for export to a foreign destination, or when a non-resident recipient in Canada receives goods that will be exported.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or Canada Customs Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or other acceptable payment evidence
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • POD or Delivery Confirmation to a Foreign Destination
  • Customs Bonded Warehouse Transfer or Removal Record

Real-time fiscal clearance is not generally mandated for GST/HST. Electronic invoicing and digital records must be retained. The CRA operates an electronic data interchange (EDI) and the GST/HST electronic filing mandate for registrants with annual revenues above CAD 1 million. Customs and border clearance uses the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) single window via the Canadian Integrated Import Declaration System (CIIDS). Free trade zone bonded depots operate under CBSA's Customs Bonded Warehouse program; goods may be admitted without payment of duty and GST/HST until removed for consumption, and digital records must support the duty-deferral process. No digital stamping or fiscal blackbox requirement applies.

CRA can deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST plus interest if export substantiation is insufficient. Penalties for false statements in GST/HST returns may be the greater of CAD 250 and 5%-10% of the amount understated, plus fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment for fraudulent export claims. The CBSA may also impose administrative monetary penalties for customs export violations and seize goods or demand duty/tax relief clawbacks for goods removed from a bonded warehouse without proper accounting. The income tax Act gross-negligence penalties can apply at 50% of the understated tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalised, free-float currency regime. The CAD is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; there are no exchange-control regulations, no central-bank approval requirements for remittances, no restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, loan-repayment or disinvestment proceeds, and no obligation to liquidate or convert foreign-currency balances. Indirect, non-FX constraints include: (1) Investment Canada Act pre-implementation review for non-Canadian acquisitions above annually adjusted monetary thresholds — an FDI screening mechanism, not a convertibility control; (2) Part XIII Canadian withholding tax at 25% statutory, treaty-reduced, on outbound passive income — a tax matter; (3) FINTRAC mandatory reporting of EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more — a transparency obligation, not authorisation gating. A bonded warehouse depot (ISIC 5210 / NAICS 493110) may therefore maintain CAD, USD and multicurrency accounts, sweep liquidity cross-border, book onshore or offshore FX hedges, and elect CAD or USD functional currency reporting under section 261 of the Income Tax Act without exchange-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds, re-export receipts, or bonded-depot transactional inflows. Receipts may be retained in CAD, USD, or any other freely convertible currency, held offshore or repatriated at will. Residual compliance obligations are limited to: (1) accurate CBSA export or deemed-export declarations (B13A or CARM cargo-file records) for goods leaving the bonded depot; (2) Statistics Canada trade-data reporting derived from those declarations; (3) FINTRAC EFT reporting for individual transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (4) discharge of the customs security bond by proving export or consumption within the bonded-warehouse time limit permitted by the CBSA Minister; (5) documentary evidence of re-export retained for tax and trade-compliance audits. No central-bank escrow or forced-sale mechanism applies to bonded depot USD earnings.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary institution issuing the Canadian dollar (CAD) and overseeing settlement-system risk of the Lynx RTGS payment system; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — prudential supervisor of Schedule I chartered banks and trade-finance operations; FINTRAC — Canada's financial intelligence unit receiving cross-border electronic funds transfer (EFT) reports; Global Affairs Canada — administration of sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — customs territory control and bonded warehouse licensing under section 91 of the Customs Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-TO-MEDIUM as primary treasury rail, MEDIUM as ancillary payment rail only. Canada regulates retail payment activity providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021 c 23), with Bank of Canada registration requirements effective September 2024, and money-services businesses (MSBs) must register with FINTRAC. Global digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Visa B2B Connect) may legitimately offer payment initiation, FX conversion, and cross-border transfers, but they are prohibited from taking deposits without a Schedule I/II bank licence under the Bank Act (SC 1991 c 46) and their balances are not CDIC-insured. Critical legal constraints for a bonded depot: (1) CBSA customs security deposits and duty monies must be held at a licensed Canadian financial institution — an EMI ledger cannot serve as customs-bond collateral; (2) CAD settlement finality requires Payments Canada membership (Lynx or ACSS) or an approved sponsor-bank clearing arrangement, which EMIs lack; (3) client-funds trust obligations for GST/HST and customs-related flows are more safely discharged through a bank trust or escrow account; (4) FINTRAC EFT reporting at CAD 10,000 must be contractually assigned to the EMI sponsor, creating ambiguous compliance risk. Recommended architecture: use EMIs for small-value supplier FX, payroll reimbursements, and deferred payment split-routing, while keeping core operating balances, customs security, capital escrow, and trade-finance instruments at a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

PRE-NOTIFICATION: Submit a Capital Call Remittance Memo to the account relationship manager and the bank's foreign correspondent desk two-to-three business days before wiring, identifying the remitter, amount, expected value date, and economic purpose (equity subscription or shareholder loan for bonded-depot working capital). WIRE FORMAT: Use SWIFT MT103 with field 50K (ordering customer) exactly matching the remitter name held at the originating bank; field 57A/C (beneficiary bank) set to the Canadian Schedule I bank's BIC with the client's account in field 59 using Canadian institution-transit-account routing (no IBAN in Canada); field 70 remittance narrative: 'Equity contribution per subscription agreement dated [date] — CA FED entity, ISIC 5210 bonded depot'. Canada imposes no government-mandated purpose code, but the narrative must match the source-of-funds form. INTERMEDIARY COVER: For USD, route via the Canadian bank's U.S. clearing subsidiary (RBC via City National Bank and RBC Bank (Georgia), BMO via Harris Bank N.A., TD via TD Bank N.A.) and instruct MT202 COVER for the USD settlement leg; for CAD, route directly to the bank's Lynx RTGS settlement account at the Bank of Canada. VERIFICATION AND REPORTING: FINTRAC EFT reporting at CAD 10,000 is triggered; the bank's compliance desk will reconcile the instruction against the onboarding UBO file, and any inconsistent ordering name causes a three-to-five day hold; for injections above CAD 50,000, provide the Source of Wealth declaration prior to credit confirmation. DOMESTIC LOW-VALUE: Use ACSS pre-authorized debit and EFT credit batches for recurring GST/HST remittances, landlord payments, and supplier disbursements below CAD 100,000. CROSS-BORDER SUPPLIER SETTLEMENTS: Execute SWIFT MT103 to overseas beneficiary banks, with MT202 COVER giving clearing instructions for the foreign-currency leg; for USD, the Canadian bank pre-funds through Fedwire via its U.S. correspondent; for EUR, GBP, or CNH, settle through the bank's correspondent network in the relevant clearing jurisdiction. TRADE FINANCE / BONDED-DEPOT GOODS: For goods purchased for re-export under customs control, utilise documentary collections (D/P or D/A) and commercial letters of credit issued by SWIFT MT700 under UCP600 and URR725, with presentation documents comprising commercial invoice, transport document, packing list, and CBSA transit or temporary-import confirmation; MT707 amendments and MT752 authorisations flow through the same trade finance desk. DUTY AND TAX DISBURSEMENTS: Remit customs duties and GST/HST through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal, funding the account via the bank's government-payment file formats (BAI/EDT), and reconcile the CARM statement balance monthly to avoid penalties. FX LEG INTEGRATION: When suppliers invoice in USD while the depot collects CAD, execute spot or forward FX with the same banking group so the foreign-currency and local-currency legs settle simultaneously inside the bank's nostro accounts, eliminating Herstatt-style settlement risk and avoiding ad-hoc, unfavourably priced same-day conversions.Deliverable outright FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP, CAD/CNH) — executable with licensed Canadian banks and Export Development Canada counterparties without regulatory pre-approval; documented under an ISDA 2002 master or a bank-specific FX master agreement.FX swaps (spot sale plus forward re-purchase) — permitted and standard practice for managing nightly Canadian-dollar funding behind USD inventory purchases in a bonded-depot cash cycle.Cross-currency swaps (principal and interest exchange in CAD against USD or EUR, tenors above 12 months) — permitted under Canadian derivatives rules, subject to trade reporting to CSA-authorised OTC derivatives trade repositories.Vanilla European-style FX options (call/put on CAD/USD, CAD/EUR) — permitted with freely remittable premiums; exotic barrier or digital structures are admissible upon bank derivatives-desk authorisation and ISDA CSA collateral posting.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for non-convertible or limited-convertibility currencies (CNY, INR, BRL, KRW) — bookable through the bank's emerging-market desk with offshore benchmark fixings; NDFs are not customary on CAD/USD because the Canadian dollar is fully deliverable.Interest-rate swaps hedging floating-rate operating debt — permitted under Canadian derivatives law and recommended where leveraged working-capital facilities fund the duty-free inventory holding period.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-TO-MEDIUM as primary treasury rail, MEDIUM as ancillary payment rail only. Canada regulates retail payment activity providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021 c 23), with Bank of Canada registration requirements effective September 2024, and money-services businesses (MSBs) must register with FINTRAC. Global digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Visa B2B Connect) may legitimately offer payment initiation, FX conversion, and cross-border transfers, but they are prohibited from taking deposits without a Schedule I/II bank licence under the Bank Act (SC 1991 c 46) and their balances are not CDIC-insured. Critical legal constraints for a bonded depot: (1) CBSA customs security deposits and duty monies must be held at a licensed Canadian financial institution — an EMI ledger cannot serve as customs-bond collateral; (2) CAD settlement finality requires Payments Canada membership (Lynx or ACSS) or an approved sponsor-bank clearing arrangement, which EMIs lack; (3) client-funds trust obligations for GST/HST and customs-related flows are more safely discharged through a bank trust or escrow account; (4) FINTRAC EFT reporting at CAD 10,000 must be contractually assigned to the EMI sponsor, creating ambiguous compliance risk. Recommended architecture: use EMIs for small-value supplier FX, payroll reimbursements, and deferred payment split-routing, while keeping core operating balances, customs security, capital escrow, and trade-finance instruments at a Schedule I bank.
