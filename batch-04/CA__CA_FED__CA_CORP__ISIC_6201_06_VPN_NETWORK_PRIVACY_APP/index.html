Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; no escrow account or capital deposit required. Consideration for issued shares is fixed by the board of directors under CBCA s.25, and shares may be issued for money, property, or past services.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to a virtual operations model without physical premises. Where a leased office or data center is established: (1) obtain municipal/local fire marshal inspection and fire safety certification; (2) secure structural load and electrical compliance clearance from a licensed engineer where required by municipal building codes; (3) register with the applicable provincial workers' compensation board (e.g., WSIB Ontario or WorkSafeBC) for employee coverage; and (4) comply with occupational health and safety obligations under the Canada Labour Code Part II (for federally regulated employers) or the applicable provincial OHS statute.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – 9-digit Business Number (BN) program; GST/HST accounts, corporate income tax accounts, and customs/export accounts are administered through CRA My Business Account and the GST/HST NET filing portal; CBSA administers border/customs reporting.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (or applying online through CRA My Business Account) and opening applicable program accounts. GST/HST registration is mandatory once taxable worldwide supplies equal or exceed CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter. Voluntary GST/HST registration is permitted for non-residents engaged in commercial activity in Canada. The non-resident applicant must identify its Canadian representative or comply with CRA's simplified non-resident registration requirements, including posting acceptable security when CRA deems collection at risk. For VPN/network software SaaS, the foreign supplier of digital services to Canadian consumers is deemed to make a supply in Canada under ETA s.143.1 and therefore requires a GST/HST account if the threshold is met. Corporate income tax registration (RC account) is required only if the entity is resident in Canada or carries on business in Canada and earns Canadian-source income; non-residents should also consider a Part XIII withholding tax account.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – A Canadian Business Corporation (federal or provincial) is a separate taxpayer. A Canadian-resident corporation is liable to Part I corporate income tax on worldwide net income; a non-resident corporation is taxed on taxable Canadian income from a Canadian business and on certain Canadian-source passive income, with a branch tax under Part XIV for Canadian business profits. For GST/HST, the corporation is a taxable person (registrant) rather than a pass-through; no look-through treatment exists for Canadian corporate entities. Territorial scope for non-residents is source-based; residence for Canadian corporations is determined by incorporation, central management and control, and de facto seat rules. — A foreign-incorporated entity must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (or applying online through CRA My Business Account) and opening applicable program accounts. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – A Canadian Business Corporation (federal or provincial) is a separate taxpayer. A Canadian-resident corporation is liable to Part I corporate income tax on worldwide net income; a non-resident corporation is taxed on taxable Canadian income from a Canadian business and on certain Canadian-source passive income, with a branch tax under Part XIV for Canadian business profits. For GST/HST, the corporation is a taxable person (registrant) rather than a pass-through; no look-through treatment exists for Canadian corporate entities. Territorial scope for non-residents is source-based; residence for Canadian corporations is determined by incorporation, central management and control, and de facto seat rules.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) GST/HST group registration election under ETA s.240 to treat eligible related registrants, including non-resident members, as a single filer; (ii) ETA quick method of accounting election and simplified annual/quarterly filing elections; (iii) Income Tax Act s.216 election allowing a non-resident to file Part I net-basis tax return for Canadian rental/disposition income rather than Part XIII withholding; (iv) optional s.219 deemed-dividend / branch tax election timing; (v) Canadian move-in/move-out deemed disposition elections under ITA s.128.1. Hybrid mismatch adjustment rules in ITA ss.18.2, 18.3, 12.2 and 15.2 operate without elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a single calendar quarter crossing CAD 30,000 also triggers mandatory registration. Non-resident digital-services providers who solicit customers in Canada or supply software/intangible property to Canadian recipients must aggregate sufficient worldwide consideration for the same threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on an export of goods or on certain supplies of intangible personal property and services to non-residents under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. For goods, the vendor must have evidence that the goods are exported as soon as reasonably possible and are not consumed/used in Canada. For VPN network security software and digital encryption services supplied to a non-resident customer, the supply may be zero-rated as an intangible personal property supply or service supplied to a non-resident, provided it is not for use principally in Canada and the recipient is not present in Canada when the supply is made; software leases and hosted services are similarly treated when the copy/access is acquired for export use. The vendor must keep records showing the identity of the recipient, foreign destination, contractual terms, and technical delivery (e.g., server/IP logs and subscription records) sufficient to demonstrate that the benefit is received abroad. Zero-rating is not available where the supply is a qualifying property/service supplied in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada.

  • Customs/export declaration: B13A Canadian Export Declaration via national CAED electronic system or through a licensed customs broker
  • Commercial invoice: exporter/importer identities, H.S. tariff classification, quantity, unit value, total consideration, currency, destination, and Incoterms
  • Transportation document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or postal/courier manifest demonstrating physical export
  • Bank settlement evidence: Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT wire confirmation, foreign-bank payment receipt, or card-network settlement statement
  • Digital-service supply file: executed SaaS/license agreement, IP-assignment evidence, access credentials for offshore use, and server/geolocation logs for VPN software delivered outside Canada

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, certification, or pre-approval API for commercial invoices. GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically through CRA's GST/HST NET / GST/HST NETFILE channels and maintain all prescribed invoice disclosures under ETA s.3 (taxable supply, GST/HST registration number, total and tax amounts). Electronic services may submit machine-readable XML returns via GST/HST NET, but there is no transaction-level validation. Larger registrants (annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1 million) are subject to mandatory electronic filing but not to continuous clearance.

CRA may deny the zero-rating and reassess the GST/HST, with daily compounding interest, if contemporaneous export documentation is incomplete or missing. Deliberate false claims trigger the ETA gross-negligence penalty (25% of the net tax understated) and, on criminal conviction, s.285 penalties including fines up to CAD 20,000 and imprisonment of up to two years for tax evasion; additional penalties apply to false statements under s.285.1 and to electronically filed returns. Reassessments for ordinary filing are time-barred after four years, but the limitation period does not apply where the taxpayer has misrepresented facts, been fraudulent, or failed to file. CRA may also issue a non-monetary serious-violations or audit referral to the CBSA, revoke a non-resident registrant's simplified registration, and require security for any subsequent refund claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. A Canadian corporation may generally receive, hold, convert, and remit foreign currency through Canadian financial institutions without a general central-bank approval. Principal legal and operational constraints arise from bank anti-money-laundering and sanctions controls, tax compliance, source-of-funds verification, payment-service rules, beneficial-ownership identification, and contractual restrictions imposed by banks or payment processors. Dividend, interest, royalty, management-fee, and other payments to non-residents may trigger Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, generally at 25% before an applicable treaty reduction. Dividends require legally available corporate surplus and proper directors' authorization. Intercompany loans, service fees, royalties, and other related-party payments should be supported by written agreements, transfer-pricing analysis, arm's-length pricing, invoice evidence, and required tax reporting. Capital contributions generally do not attract Canadian withholding tax, but repayment characterization, thin-capitalization rules, shareholder-benefit rules, and general anti-avoidance principles must be reviewed. Foreign-currency transactions must be recorded for Canadian tax and financial-reporting purposes using a consistent and supportable exchange-rate methodology.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a Canadian bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may normally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the corporation's discretion. The corporation should retain contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery evidence, customer remittance records, bank confirmations, and accounting records sufficient to substantiate revenue, foreign taxes, GST/HST treatment, transfer-pricing positions, and the commercial purpose of each receipt. Banks and payment institutions may impose contractual settlement, reserve, chargeback, sanctions-screening, or enhanced-review requirements. Export controls, sanctions, controlled-goods rules, privacy requirements, and destination-specific restrictions may apply to encryption software, technology, customers, or jurisdictions even though no general FX surrender rule applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls, mandatory export-proceeds surrender, or routine capital-account approval. Relevant supporting authorities include the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), and the Investment Canada Act authorities where applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection and foreign-currency settlement, subject to provider approval. Canada does not generally treat an offshore electronic-money institution as a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking bank. Offshore providers may be unable to provide Canadian-dollar deposit accounts, local clearing, safeguarded client-money arrangements, or payment services to Canadian residents without complying with applicable Canadian payment, money-services, consumer-protection, sanctions, privacy, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Stripe and Paymentwall onboarding remains contract-based and may require Canadian incorporation evidence, a Canadian operating account, beneficial-owner verification, website and product review, refund and chargeback policies, customer terms, data-flow information, sanctions screening, and evidence that VPN or encryption functionality is lawful in target markets. Funds should not be commingled with customer funds unless the provider is legally authorized to hold them in the relevant capacity. The corporation should maintain a Canadian bank account as the primary treasury and reserve account rather than relying exclusively on an offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through a Canadian bank account by SWIFT or an equivalent regulated correspondent channel using the exact legal name of the Canadian corporation as beneficiary. Use a clear payment narrative such as 'share subscription proceeds' or 'shareholder loan advance,' and do not describe an equity contribution as revenue. Provide the bank with the subscription or loan agreement, board resolution, investor identity and ownership information, source-of-funds evidence, remitter-bank details, and expected amount and currency. Canada generally has no central-bank capital-registration number or universal FX purpose-code regime. The bank may nevertheless require an internal purpose classification, beneficial-owner declaration, sanctions screening, and confirmation of whether funds are equity, debt, reimbursement, or intercompany settlement. Reconcile the receipt to the general ledger and maintain capital-account or shareholder-loan records. For related-party debt, document interest, maturity, repayment terms, thin-capitalization analysis, transfer pricing, and withholding-tax treatment. For outbound remittances, confirm the legal basis, board approval, invoice or agreement, tax withholding position, and sufficient corporate funds before release.Use SWIFT customer credit transfers, commonly represented by MT103 messaging or its ISO 20022 successor equivalent, for vendor, contractor, cloud-service, advertising, and other commercial payments. Confirm beneficiary legal name, account number or IBAN where applicable, BIC, intermediary-bank details, payment purpose, invoice number, currency, sanctions screening, and fee allocation. Use commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, or standby letters of credit for material goods or higher-risk counterparties; the issuing bank will require credit support, approved limits, underlying commercial documents, and compliance with applicable ICC rules such as UCP 600 where the LC so provides. For software and SaaS payments, retain contracts, invoices, service-delivery evidence, tax forms, transfer-pricing support for related parties, and evidence of any Canadian withholding-tax review. Domestic Canadian payments may use Payments Canada rails, including wire transfers through Lynx for eligible high-value payments and EFT or other approved bank channels for routine disbursements. Payment processors should settle into a controlled Canadian operating account with separate reconciliation of gross receipts, refunds, reserves, fees, taxes, and chargebacks.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with a Canadian bank or other appropriately regulated dealer, supported by a documented underlying exposure, treasury policy, counterparty limits, confirmation procedures, and settlement instructions.Non-deliverable forwards where commercially appropriate and legally offered by the counterparty, subject to dealer eligibility, ISDA or equivalent documentation, Canadian derivatives rules, transaction reporting, margin or collateral requirements, and accounting designation requirements.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps for recurring or longer-term funding exposures, subject to credit approval, master netting documentation, collateral terms, valuation controls, liquidity stress testing, and applicable Canadian derivatives reporting and business-conduct requirements.Foreign-currency options, collars, and structured hedges may be used only after documented management approval, suitability analysis, premium and worst-case-loss assessment, and confirmation that the product is not speculative or inconsistent with the corporation's risk mandate.Natural hedging through matching foreign-currency receipts with foreign-currency operating expenses, supplier payments, or debt service is generally the lowest-complexity method and should be documented in the treasury policy.Hedges should be limited to identifiable commercial, financing, or forecast exposures; recorded under the corporation's accounting policy; monitored for counterparty, settlement, liquidity, basis, rollover, and model risk; and supported by reconciled confirmations. Canada does not generally impose a blanket prohibition on corporate FX derivatives, but dealer onboarding, derivatives legislation, reporting, tax, and accounting requirements remain applicable.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for payment collection and foreign-currency settlement, subject to provider approval. Canada does not generally treat an offshore electronic-money institution as a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking bank. Offshore providers may be unable to provide Canadian-dollar deposit accounts, local clearing, safeguarded client-money arrangements, or payment services to Canadian residents without complying with applicable Canadian payment, money-services, consumer-protection, sanctions, privacy, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Stripe and Paymentwall onboarding remains contract-based and may require Canadian incorporation evidence, a Canadian operating account, beneficial-owner verification, website and product review, refund and chargeback policies, customer terms, data-flow information, sanctions screening, and evidence that VPN or encryption functionality is lawful in target markets. Funds should not be commingled with customer funds unless the provider is legally authorized to hold them in the relevant capacity. The corporation should maintain a Canadian bank account as the primary treasury and reserve account rather than relying exclusively on an offshore EMI.
