Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), administered federally through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and Corporations Canada for federal incorporation and program account registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) using the BRO online service or by submitting RC1 forms. The BN serves as the universal TIN/VAT identifier for income tax, GST/HST, and Customs. Registration is required for GST/HST where taxable supplies exceed the threshold or where the entity is deemed to carry on business in Canada. For federal incorporation, additional filing through Corporations Canada is required before operating. The procedure includes obtaining a Canadian address for service or appointing an agent for service under section 19 of the Canada Business Corporations Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election; classification follows the legal form under the Income Tax Act. Available statutory elections include: (i) GST/HST Quick Method under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act, (ii) joint election for zero-rated supply transportation services, (iii) section 216(1) election for non-resident taxpayers receiving Canadian rental and resource income on a net basis, and (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under Part XVI.3 of the Income Tax Act that operate as mandatory anti-avoidance overrides, not elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters, with mandatory registration carve-ins for certain non-resident suppliers carrying on business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (including smart-home IoT hardware) are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act where the supplier maintains proof of export within prescribed timeframes (generally within 60 days after delivery if freight arranged, and within two years for contractually exported goods). Offshore services rendered to non-residents may be zero-rated under Parts V and VII where the service is primarily for consumption outside Canada and is not tethered to Canadian real property or goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA export declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System or authorized portal)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill indicating foreign named consignee and port of exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-funded FX conversion and payment receipt evidencing actual repatriation of sale proceeds to Canada)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, Continuous Transaction Controls, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Electronic invoicing is permitted, including PEPPOL-based exchange, provided the invoice retains the mandatory statutory data fields required by CRA for input tax credit substantiation (ETA sections 169 and 170). No live pre-clearance API or governmental invoice stamping applies.

CRA auditor scrutiny applies to zero-rating claims through post-assessment verification, documentary completeness tests, and shipping-lifecycle analysis. False or unsupported export zero-rating triggers reassessment of the tax with disability of input tax credits, arrears interest at 6% annual rate, and penalties up to 25-50% of the tax amount for fraud under ETA s.285. Criminal referral may follow under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act, with retrospective clawbacks extending the normal four-year reassessment period by three years for gross negligence or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada maintains no general exchange control legislation under the Currency Act; profit remittance is unrestricted, but dividends, interest, rent, and royalties paid to non-residents are subject to Part XIII income tax withholding, with rates reduced under applicable income tax treaties. Proceeds from the disposition of real property or business assets may be repatriated after tax clearance where required under the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in domestic or foreign bank accounts; there is no mandatory conversion timeline or ratio. For tax and customs reporting, values must be translated into Canadian dollars under the Income Tax Act and the Customs Act using an acceptable exchange rate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; exchange rate and foreign exchange policy oversight is coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada; prudential bank supervision is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering supervision is vested in FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore EMIs are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot provide CDIC-insured accounts. They are viable as ancillary payment facilitators or e-money wallets for low-balance transactions, but must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and are subject to Retail Payment Activities Act oversight when the regime is in force. Primary treasury and clearing balances should be held at a Schedule I bank; reliance on offshore EMIs for capital or trade settlement is not advised.
