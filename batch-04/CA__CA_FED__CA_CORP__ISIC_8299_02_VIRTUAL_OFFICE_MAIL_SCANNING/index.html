Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. No exchange control laws, no central bank approval for profit remittance, dividend distribution, royalty payments, or capital repatriation. Non-resident dividend and interest remittances are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, reducible under Canada's tax treaties; e.g., 15% dividend rate under most treaties, 0-10% for interest). Statute of limitations: CRA assessments for 3-4 years. There are no restrictions on holding foreign currency accounts within Canadian banks, but CAD-denominated settlement is generally mandated for domestic revenue.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no surrender, conversion ratio, or statutory timeline for export proceeds. Exporters may freely retain proceeds in foreign currency. However, FINTRAC reporting obligations apply: any cross-border electronic fund transfer (EFT) of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by financial intermediaries. For trade-in-services (CMRA/mail scanning), no export declaration is required for digital services, but the CMS (Canadian Merchant Services) or acquiring bank will classify the merchant under MCC 7399 (Business Services - Not Elsewhere Classified) or 4829 (Money Transfer), and may require separate FINTRAC MSB registration if the entity transmits funds on behalf of clients.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — exclusive monetary authority and FX policy advisor. OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) prudentially regulates banks. FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), mandates cross-border wire reporting, and supervises money service businesses (MSBs). No dedicated foreign exchange control authority exists; Canada maintains a fully liberalized exchange-control-free regime.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal Part I rate after 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction; provincial corporate rates of 8%-16% produce a combined federal-provincial rate of 23%-31%; Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) claiming the small business deduction pay 9% federal on the first C$500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to non-residents of Canada under s.212(2) of the Income Tax Act.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due within 6 months after fiscal year-end; final tax instalment due within 2 months after year-end, extended to 3 months for a CCPC claiming the small business deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Section 247(4) documentation due diligence applies to non-arm's-length cross-border transactions where the aggregate fair market value exceeds C$1 million in a fiscal year (goods/services) or loans/advances outstanding exceed C$1 million; Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) is mandatory at these thresholds. OECD Master File and Local File documentation is expected at the same thresholds; Country-by-Country Report (Form RC4463) applies to MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least C$1.12 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies OECD substance principles through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245), treaty residence rules and the OECD Multilateral Instrument. A CMRA/virtual-office entity must maintain local premises, staff, genuine customer contracts, board minutes executed in Canada, and operational decision-making in Canada to sustain its legal form, deductions and DTA benefits. Arrangements lacking economic substance (e.g., circular loans, conduit IP holding, mailbox payments) can be recharacterized; GAAR adds a 25% penalty on the tax benefit plus interest, and treaty benefits may be denied under the principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False or Incomplete Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty (s.163(2)) equal to the greater of C$100 and 50% of the understated tax; criminal tax evasion (s.239) on indictment carries a fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; forfeiture of proceeds of crime under the Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: CRA may pierce the corporate veil for fraud or sham and reassess the director/shareholder personally under s.160 (transfers to non-arm's-length persons) and prosecute the controlling officer under s.239; travel bans, asset freezing orders and imprisonment up to 5 years apply.
Transfer Pricing / Offshore Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: s.247(2) recharacterization to arm's length amounts; s.247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment (minimum C$10,000, uncapped); T106 late filing penalties of C$100/day (maximum C$25,000); interest at prescribed rate plus 4%.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for TP penalties, but knowingly signing false or misleading T106/T2 declarations invokes s.239 criminal exposure; civil restitution may be pursued through CRA director assessments for any unremitted source deductions attached to fictional payroll arrangements.
AML / Shell-Company Facilitation (CMRA Complicity)

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code s.462.31 (laundering proceeds of crime): imprisonment up to 10 years; PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties up to C$2 million for corporations; CRA may disgorge the service fees and impose GST/HST penalties for wilful facilitation; conviction leads to forfeiture of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors participating in the address-rental scheme are treated as aiders and abettors: personal liability for victim restitution, freezing orders, travel bans pending trial, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is disregarded where the CMRA is a sham used solely to conceal unlawful beneficiaries.
Payroll & GST/HST Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: Failure to remit source deductions: 10% penalty under s.227(9) plus daily interest; GST/HST failure-to-remit penalties under s.280 of the Excise Tax Act plus interest under s.282; repeated failures attract penalties up to 20% and possible CRA suspension of GST accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted payroll deductions and GST/HST under s.227.1 of the Income Tax Act and s.323 of the Excise Tax Act, regardless of corporate dissolution or bankruptcy, unless a strict due-diligence defence is proven; the liability survives personal bankruptcy and may be enforced by seizure of personal assets.
