Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada – Business Registration Online portal and GST/HST NET suite.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Canadian Business Corporation must first complete incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or applicable provincial statute. The CRA Business Number (BN) is then obtained through Business Registration Online or Form RC1, with the entity receiving a 9-digit BN plus program account identifiers. For GST/HST, the registrant account identifier is RT and, where applicable, import/export program account RM. Registration for GST/HST is mandatory once the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and is no longer a small supplier; otherwise voluntary registration may be elected. The BN application must be supported by the certificate of incorporation, director information, registered office address, and, for non-resident directors/managers, a completed Form RC471 (or equivalent) for appointed Canadian agent authority where required by CRA. For the subject payment gateway business, statutory licensing under FINTRAC (Anti-Money Laundering), provincial money-services laws, and payment card network rules must be separately obtained; these do not replace tax registration but are verified by CRA in audits. Payroll (RP), corporate income tax, and excise accounts should be opened at the same time as the RT account to centralize compliance under the BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not recognise a US-style check-the-box classification for federal income tax purposes. Canadian-resident corporations are automatically taxable at the entity level on worldwide net income. Available elections relevant to tax identity include: (i) GST/HST election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act permitting closely related registrants to treat certain intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) elections on qualifying amalgamations, winding-up, and section 85 asset transfers to achieve tax-deferred rollover treatment; (iii) the late-filing election mechanisms under subsections 85(6)/(7) or equivalent provisions for qualifying reorganisations; and (iv) elections regarding partnership allocations and fiscal period timing under the Income Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act apply to deny inappropriate deduction/no-inclusion outcomes arising from differential entity classification in foreign jurisdictions. There is no elective flow-through entity status for ordinary Canadian corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada in the current calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a person exceeding this small-supplier threshold must register for GST/HST. Non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same small-supplier threshold, while non-residents making supplies through digital/e-commerce channels may have a narrower domestic nexus test under place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property that are exported by the supplier, delivered or shipped outside Canada, and supported by documentary proof of exportation. For services, a supply of a service to a non-resident person may be zero-rated if the service is supplied to a non-resident and is not received or consumed in Canada, subject to exclusions for services relating to real property located in Canada, tangible personal property delivered in Canada, services personally performed in Canada, or supplies to a non-resident who is a consumer in Canada. A cross-border payment gateway must determine whether its fees are exempt financial services or taxable zero-rated services: exempt financial services cannot be zero-rated, while separately itemised technical administration, reporting, operational support, or software-related services may be zero-rated as exported services if the conditions for supply to a non-resident are met. If the gateway supplies services to Canadian merchants or services otherwise connected with Canadian payment flows, GST/HST at the applicable rate applies. Claimants should obtain a CRA ruling or advance GST/HST ruling where the service classification is not clear.

Canada does not currently mandate a continuous-transaction-control e-invoicing clearance model or require digital stamping for GST/HST at the federal level. GST/HST registrants are required to file returns electronically using CRA's GST/HST NET or GST/HST TELEFILE when mandated by revenue thresholds. Invoices must contain prescribed information sufficient to support net tax reporting, input tax credits, and zero-rating claims; electronic records may be retained in machine-readable form. Real-time fiscal clearance, government API pre-authorisation, and fiscal printer requirements are not currently part of the federal GST/HST regime. For the payment gateway business, the acquirer/platform must also comply with the CRA's record-keeping rules for electronic merchant records and keep all transaction-level data, including interchange details, settlement reports, and client remittance documentation, available for audit.

CRA may audit zero-rating claims and require documentary evidence that property was physically exported or services were genuinely provided to a non-resident and not consumed in Canada. In the absence of adequate proof, CRA will assess GST/HST at the full standard rate on the invoiced transaction fees, disallow any related input tax credits, apply compound daily arrears interest, and reassess outside the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established. Civil penalties can include false-statement penalties and gross-negligence penalties for knowingly participating in a fraudulent export scheme; criminal prosecution may result in fines, imprisonment, and surrogate liability for officers, directors, and agencies of the non-resident. Retrospective clawbacks are available to CRA for fraud, including denial of refunds and recapture of GST/HST previously refunded on forged or circular export documentation. A payment gateway's fund repatriation, FX settlement, and cross-border documentation trail must therefore be complete, time-stamped, and independently verifiable to mitigate clawback and penalty exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely traded and there are no exchange controls or surrender requirements on export receipts. Dividend remittances and capital repatriation by non-resident shareholders are permitted, subject to source withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act, arm's-length transfer pricing documentation, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds. No central bank licences are needed for profit remittance, but banks may require validation of the underlying commercial substance and a Certificate of Compliance for any tax withholding remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency or convert through any licensed FX dealer at commercial rates. Documentary evidence of export proceeds is required under CBSA statistical export declarations and for transfer pricing or tax administration purposes; however, the currency composition and timing of repatriation are unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) is the monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/ATF; exchange controls are governed by the Currency Act, the Financial Administration Act, and the Bank of Canada Act. There is no separate foreign exchange administration requiring pre-approval for ordinary cross-border transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Canada does not have a dedicated EU-style EMI licence. A fintech PSP must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and for payment gateway operations must partner with a Schedule I bank to access the national payment rails. Direct membership in Payments Canada's Lynx or the Automated Clearing Settlement System is restricted to regulated financial institutions; accordingly, offshore EMIs are not viable as direct clearing members without an authorised banking partner. Feasibility rating: Medium-Low; a Canadian MSB can open operational accounts at Tier-1 banks, but cross-border digital-only EMIs face higher onboarding refusal rates due to licensing gaps.

Funds are routed to the Canadian corporate bank account through the Payments Canada Large Value Transfer System for CAD or via the correspondent bank's US clearing network for USD. T2 Corporation Income Tax Return (including audited/reviewed financial statements, general index of financial information, and reconciliation schedules) – annual federal statutory filing Due within 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; for a company using a 31 December fiscal year-end, the deadline is 30 June of the following calendar year. $0 USD Late-filing penalty equal to 5% of the unpaid tax balance due, plus 1% per full month of delay (maximum 12 months); on repeated late filing within a 3-year cycle, the penalty rises to 10% plus 2% per month (capped at 20 months); prescribed-rate interest is compounded daily on unpaid amounts; the payment-gateway's acquiring bank settlement holds and financial statements may be withheld by CRA under s. 152 if returns are persistently late.
