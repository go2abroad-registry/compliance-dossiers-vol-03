Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

BC Registries and Online Services (Corporate Registry), Ministry of Finance, Province of British Columbia — OneStop Business Registry / BC Corporate Online; counter service at 940 Blanshard Street, Victoria, BC.

Governing statute

Partnership Act, RSBC 1996, c. 348, Part 5 (ss. 52-64) — limited partnerships; Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c. 57 (applicable only to the corporate general partner); Customs Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1) and Excise Tax Act / GST-HST framework as compliance references only.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form LP-1 Declaration — Application for Registration of a Limited Partnership, filed under s. 52(1) of the Partnership Act, resulting in a Certificate of Limited Partnership Registration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None prescribed by statute. The Declaration/LPA must state each limited partner's capital contribution, which must be actually made and, except on dissolution, not withdrawn (ss. 54-55 Partnership Act). No escrow is mandatory; for client due-diligence, hold contributed capital in a Schedule I Canadian bank account titled to the LP.

Funding & unlock path: BC Fire Code (B.C. Reg. 213/2007) fire safety plan approved by the local fire department; annual inspection of fire extinguishers, egress, and emergency lighting; building occupancy permit with structural load classification verified by the municipal building official; WorkSafeBC Clearance Letter demonstrating Workers' Compensation coverage in good standing; PIPEDA-compliant data handling for client supply-chain security audits.

Paid-in capital rule: None prescribed by statute. The Declaration/LPA must state each limited partner's capital contribution, which must be actually made and, except on dissolution, not withdrawn (ss. 54-55 Partnership Act). No escrow is mandatory; for client due-diligence, hold contributed capital in a Schedule I Canadian bank account titled to the LP.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sectoral licensing agency applies to management consultancy in BC. Reference authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — Trusted Trader / Partners in Protection (AEO-equivalent) directorate — relevant only if the LP later applies for its own SAFE certification; no Customs Act licence is required because the LP does not transact customs business on behalf of clients.

Category: None required for the AEO audit-advisory service itself. Should the LP later file customs declarations for clients, it must obtain a CBSA Customs Broker Licence under s. 9 of the Customs Act and a trade-services bond; optional professional credentials: CITT Logistics Professional, CSCP, or CUSP.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal (canada.ca) for federal GST/HST, customs, payroll and partnership registration; BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch - BC Taxpayer Portal for provincial sales tax (PST).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: the BC Limited Partnership obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1, with the general partner identified as the authorized signatory. Step 2: a GST/HST program account (RT) is opened on the same BN once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, or voluntarily earlier to recover input tax credits on start-up costs. Step 3: if audit test equipment or temporary samples cross the border, open a CRA customs account (RM) and obtain a CBSA importer/exporter code. Step 4: BC PST registration is not required because ISIC 7020 management consulting and security auditing services are PST-exempt professional services; registration via the BC Taxpayer Portal is needed only if the LP also sells PST-taxable goods. Step 5: the LP files an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return, allocates income/loss to partners under ITA s.96, and issues T5013 slips; non-resident partners must register for a non-resident tax account and are subject to Part XIII withholding on certain Canadian-source payments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96 and the BC Partnership Act; there is no entity-level income tax. Income/loss allocation follows the partnership agreement, subject to ITA s.103 anti-avoidance rules. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income with a 25% Part XIII gross-withholding mechanism for passive or management-fee flows. For GST/HST, the LP is a separate registrant and person, but remains transparent for income tax; BC income tax is assessed at the partner level on each partner's allocable BC-source share.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Key elections available: ITA s.97(2) rollover on transfer of property into the LP; ITA s.98(3)/(4) distribution and wind-up rollover elections; ITA s.216(1) election permitting a non-resident partner with Canadian rental income to file a net-basis Part I return instead of Part XIII gross withholding; ITA s.217 election for non-resident partners receiving Canadian pension or retirement amounts; ETA s.240(2) voluntary GST/HST registration before exceeding the CAD 30,000 threshold; ETA s.260 election to treat supplies between closely related registrants as made for nil consideration. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss.18.4 and 603-612 apply to cross-border hybrid instruments and hybrid entities, including a BC LP that is treated as a taxable corporation by the foreign partner's jurisdiction; partners should document ITA s.126 foreign tax credits and adjusted-cost-base elections to prevent double taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the GST/HST small-supplier threshold, measured on the worldwide taxable supplies of the LP and all associated persons over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current quarter; registration is mandatory within 29 days of exceeding the threshold. The BC PST has no applicable threshold for services because the supply of professional consulting and auditing services is not PST-taxable; PST registration applies only if the LP separately sells tangible goods or enumerated taxable services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of services to a non-resident is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act where the recipient is not a Canadian-resident individual and is not a GST/HST registrant, the recipient is outside Canada at the time the service is performed, and the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada nor tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance (unless the property is for direct use in a commercial activity outside Canada). Remote AEO certification and supply-chain security audits delivered to non-resident shippers, carriers, brokers, and manufacturers qualify; on-site work in Canada on foreign-owned goods or systems requires a case-specific review of the in-transit processing exceptions in ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss.7(c)-(d). Contemporaneous substantiation must evidence the non-residency of the recipient, the place of performance, and the receipt of consideration. The BC PST does not apply because the services remain PST-exempt professional services regardless of the customer's residence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CAED) filed via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System - required where audit test equipment or samples temporarily leave Canada; not required for pure cross-border consulting services
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing movement of any goods from Canada to a foreign consignee
  • Signed service contract / statement of work naming the non-resident recipient, the place of performance, and the fee schedule
  • Commercial invoice referencing the zero-rating citation - ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.7
  • Certificate of Non-Residency issued by the foreign tax authority (e.g., IRS Form 6166, HM Revenue & Customs certificate, or foreign corporate registry extract such as Companies House)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank advice showing receipt of consideration from a foreign bank account
  • AEO audit report, deliverable time-stamps, and IP-delivery logs demonstrating performance for a person outside Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory government API, and no digital stamping regime exists in Canada for GST/HST. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million; all registrants must retain electronic invoices, ledgers, and source documents in legible format and provide them to the CRA on demand under ETA s.286. E-invoicing through the Peppol network (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is voluntary and permitted, and the BC Government recognizes Peppol for vendor invoicing, but no government tokenization or clearance-before-issuance requirement applies.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated export claim within 4 years of the normal reassessment date under ETA s.298(3), extended to 10 years where misrepresentation arises from neglect, carelessness, or willful default. Where export documentation fails, the CRA recharacterizes the supply as taxable at 5% GST on the full consideration, compounds daily prescribed interest (currently approximately 7%-9% per annum for corporate and non-corporate taxpayers), and may apply: (i) a 6% late-filing penalty plus 1% per month up to 12% under ETA s.280; (ii) a 25% gross-negligence penalty under ETA s.285 on the tax understated; (iii) on summary conviction for evasion, a fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or 12 months imprisonment, and on indictment a fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or 5 years imprisonment. Where related-party non-resident payments are recharacterized as management fees, Part XIII withholding of 25% applies on the gross amount under ITA s.212(1)(b), and a general partner may be held personally liable for unremitted GST/HST under ETA s.323(1). CBSA penalties for AEO/PIP program non-compliance under the Customs Act s.109.1 range from CAD 500 to CAD 7,500 per day, and the CRA may claw back all input tax credits previously claimed on inputs that supported the failed zero-rated supply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Foreign Exchange Act. Both current account and capital account transactions are unrestricted; profit remittances, dividends, management fees, and consulting payments to foreign partners or investors may be transferred abroad without regulatory approval, surrender requirements, or convertibility hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to service-based operations such as AEO certification and supply chain auditing. No mandatory surrender of export proceeds or conversion ratios applies; all inbound cross-border receipts must be reported to the Bank of Canada via prescribed T1 forms where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Deadline: · Frequency:

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Partnership Name and Address Legal name and registered office address of the LP. Provide the Canadian address of the agent or principal place of business; non-resident partners must be identified separately. Late filing penalty: 5% of the amount of tax payable (if any) plus 1% per full month for up to 12 months. For information returns (T5013), a penalty of $25 per day per partner, up to $2,500, for failure to file. Box 3 - Fiscal Year-End Date of the partnership's fiscal period end. Must align with the majority of partners' tax year; non-resident partners may have different year-ends, but the LP must use a single fiscal year. Late filing penalty: 5% of the amount of tax payable (if any) plus 1% per full month for up to 12 months. For information returns (T5013), a penalty of $25 per day per partner, up to $2,500, for failure to file. Additionally, a penalty of $100 per day for each day the return is late, up to $2,500, for partnerships with gross revenue over $1 million. Repeated failures may result in higher penalties and criminal prosecution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (gross value of cross-border non-arm's length transactions) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation under CRA's TP rules (Form T7).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to offshore jurisdictions. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act denies tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance. For an LP, the CRA may re-characterize a transaction if the partnership is a conduit without real business purpose. The AEO audit services must demonstrate substantive operations, including employees, premises, and decision-making in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act, s. 238, 239)

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest. Criminal prosecution: fine up to $200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for unpaid withholding taxes (s. 227.1) and for penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion. Travel bans may be imposed under the Income Tax Act for outstanding amounts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to reallocate income to the Canadian partners, impose a 10% penalty on the tax benefit, and deny treaty benefits. The LP may be deemed a 'carrying on business' in Canada, triggering Part XIV branch tax (25%) on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be assessed for the LP's tax liabilities if they signed false returns or directed the sham structure. Travel bans under the Income Tax Act (s. 226) may be issued for non-payment.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance (Proceeds of Crime and Terrorist Financing Act)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for entities per violation; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly failed to implement AML policies. Travel bans may be imposed under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act for serious non-compliance.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Income Tax Act, s. 247)

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of income to arm's length amount, plus a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 1 million). Interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be liable for the penalty if they were grossly negligent in supervising TP compliance. No travel ban specifically, but civil restitution may be enforced.
