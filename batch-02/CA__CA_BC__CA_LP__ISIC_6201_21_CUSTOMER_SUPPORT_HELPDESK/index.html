Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A BC limited partnership must have a current registered office and records office in British Columbia. The registered office may be a law firm or licensed corporate-services provider's physical address, which functions as a compliant virtual office for service of process and regulatory notices. However, a pure virtual office with no employees, no premises, and no substantive decision-making in Canada will not establish sufficient commercial substance for Canadian income-tax treaty access or for Canada Revenue Agency purposes. For an ISIC 6201 helpdesk/SaaS cloud business, the existence of a Canadian registered agent plus remote customer-support personnel outside Canada is acceptable for registration but should be supplemented with real local substance—e.g., a contracted BC office, local management, and Canadian payroll—before claiming treaty benefits.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal income tax, GST/HST registration, and Business Number (BN) issuance, administered through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service and the My Business Account (MaBA) portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance separately administers the Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through the eTaxBC portal and BC Registry Services handles extraprovincial limited partnership registration under BC's Partnership Act.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 — GST/HST small-supplier test: the LP must register when worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated export services, exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 240). STEP 2 — Obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN): a non-resident limited partnership registers via CRA BRO online (where the general partner is a Canadian resident or has a Canadian agent) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) by mail or fax; CRA requires the partnership agreement, the general partner's certificate of status/existence from its home jurisdiction, and the BC principal place of business address. STEP 3 — Add the GST/HST account to the BN (suffix 'RT0001'); the partnership, not its partners, is the registered 'person' for GST/HST purposes. STEP 4 — Register for BC PST with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC using the BN; PST registration is mandatory when the LP has a BC nexus (a place of business in BC, solicitation of sales in BC via employees or agents, or delivery of taxable software/cloud services to BC customers); there is no small-supplier monetary exemption, and a non-resident LP without a BC address must appoint a resident tax representative for service of process. STEP 5 — File an Extraprovincial Limited Partnership registration with BC Registry Services. STEP 6 — Open a CRA payroll account (BN suffix 'RP') if the LP employs helpdesk or engineering staff in BC. Timeline: online BRO registration is immediate; paper Form RC1 processing takes 20–40 business days; GST/HST registration may be backdated to the date the LP commenced carrying on taxable activity in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act s. 96, a BC limited partnership is a transparent flow-through entity: the LP computes income or loss at the partnership level, files a T5013 Partnership Information Return, and each partner includes its allocable share of Canadian-source business income. The partnership itself pays no federal or BC income tax. For GST/HST, however, the ETA s. 240 treats the partnership as a distinct 'person' that must register, collect, and remit tax in its own name. Territorial scope: partnership income is allocated to the BC permanent establishment under the Income Tax Regulations s. 402(4) formula, and each partner's residency and applicable tax treaty determine ultimate taxation of the allocated export-service income.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; the transparent classification of an LP is statutorily fixed. Available elections relevant to this entity: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 to adopt a non-calendar taxation year in limited circumstances; (ii) ETA s. 156 election for supplies between closely related registrants to be made for nil consideration, which can eliminate GST/HST on intercompany platform or subprocessing charges where a 90% control relationship exists between the LP and a corporate affiliate; (iii) ETA s. 273 joint election among co-venturers or partners to designate a representative person as the supplier for GST/HST purposes; and (iv) ITA s. 216/217 elections available to non-resident partners for certain rental or royalty income streams, which have limited application to helpdesk service revenue. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2–18.6 and related provisions) apply mandatorily and cannot be elected into or out of; these rules may defer or deny deductions where a transparent LP partner (for example, a US LLC) is party to a hybrid arrangement, and the LP's income allocation to such a partner is scrutinized against the arrangement's tax-characterization mismatches.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange controls, no quantitative restrictions on capital movements, and no restrictions on profit, dividend, interest, royalty, management fee, or service-fee remittances to non-residents. Profit and income remittances from a British Columbia limited partnership are, however, subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, typically 25% absent treaty reduction. Inbound capital contributions are generally liberalized, except that large non-resident acquisitions may require notification or review under the Investment Canada Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists for export proceeds in Canada. There is no mandatory timeline, conversion ratio, or obligation to convert foreign currency receipts into Canadian dollars. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency in authorized Canadian bank accounts or abroad, subject to Canadian income and foreign-asset reporting obligations, including Form T1135 for specified foreign property exceeding CAD 100,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), as the nation's central monetary authority; no separate foreign exchange administration exists in Canada. Banking prudential regulation is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while AML/CFT supervision is conducted by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) seeking to act as principal depositaries; MODERATE as supplementary payment processors. Canada does not recognize the European EMI licence; deposit-taking by unlicensed entities is prohibited under the Bank Act. Offshore EMIs can only operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and even then they cannot offer CDIC-insured Canadian deposit accounts or native Canadian clearing. Stripe Billing and PayPal remain the strongest cross-border commercial rails for customer support SaaS receipts, but they should complement, not replace, a Canadian chartered bank operating account and capital escrow account.

Use SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent ISO 20022 wire) from the non-resident partner's financial institution to the British Columbia limited partnership's Canadian clearing account.
