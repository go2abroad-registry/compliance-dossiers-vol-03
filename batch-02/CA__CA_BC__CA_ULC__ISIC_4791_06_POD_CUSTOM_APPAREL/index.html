Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administered by the BC Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) — portal: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst

        
E-government filing portal
A BC-incorporated ULC automatically receives its CRA Business Number (BN) through the BC Corporate Registry electronic data transfer upon incorporation; the company then activates the RT (GST/HST) and RC (corporate income tax) program accounts online via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). A foreign entity without Canadian incorporation must file Form RC1 with CRA or use the non-resident registration path in BRO to obtain a BN and GST/HST account, providing proof of foreign registration, a Canadian representative for service of documents, and the date of commencement of commercial activity. GST/HST registration is effective from the start of commercial activity or the requested effective date and is mandatory once the registrant ceases to be a small supplier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), a BC ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation and is subject to entity-level Part I income tax; no Canadian check-the-box or pass-through election exists. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the ULC is not a per se corporation under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i)(B), so it is eligible for check-the-box treatment: its default U.S. classification is a disregarded entity (pass-through) unless the ULC files Form 8832 to elect corporation status. This cross-border hybrid profile engages Canada's hybrid mismatch measures (Income Tax Act s. 18.4/18.6, reinforced by the OECD Multilateral Instrument) to deny deductions for certain hybrid outcomes. For GST/HST, the ULC may voluntarily register below the threshold to recover input tax credits and may make an ETA s. 156 election to treat closely related registrants as a single registrant; no entity-classification election exists for GST/HST purposes.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 in consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada during the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (or in the current calendar quarter if it exceeds the threshold), being the GST/HST small supplier threshold under ETA s. 123(1) and s. 247. BC PST registration is required from the first taxable retail sale made in British Columbia; the province has no small-supplier exemption for PST collection obligations on goods sold into BC.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate the supply of tangible personal property (POD custom apparel) under ETA Schedule VI Part V when the goods are exported as part of the supply and delivered to a non-resident customer outside Canada, e.g., international shipment by common carrier to a foreign destination. Supplies of services to non-residents (e.g., design or offshore services integral to the POD transaction) may be zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part IV. The registrant must demonstrate that the goods left Canada 'as or soon after' the supply and retain contemporaneous documentary evidence. Supplies delivered within Canada to Canadian consumers are taxable at 5% GST plus 7% BC PST.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or CRA-approved electronic invoicing system exists for GST/HST. A GST/HST registrant must issue ordinary tax invoices containing prescribed data (Business Number/RT program account, invoice date, supplier and purchaser identity, description, consideration, GST rate and amount) for taxable supplies exceeding CAD 150. Electronic invoices and payment-rail records (Stripe, PayPal settlement statements) constitute valid substantiation, and books and records must generally be retained for six years. For online marketplace POD sales (e.g., Etsy, Amazon), the GST/HST e-commerce framework may deem the marketplace operator to be the supplier responsible for collecting GST/HST on supplies made by non-resident sellers, which alters the invoicing/remittance flow for pipeline sellers.

      
CRA may reassess improperly zero-rated claims at 5% GST (plus PST if applicable) together with daily compound statutory interest, generally within a four-year reassessment window or without time limit where misrepresentation is found. False or unsupported export claims attract a 10% false-statement penalty under ETA s. 280, escalating to a 25% gross-negligence penalty where the claim is made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence. Willful evasion is prosecutable under ETA s. 285, with fines up to CAD 20,000 and/or imprisonment of up to two years. Refunds attributable to fraudulent export zero-rating may be clawed back with interest, and the exporter is exposed to subsequent detailed GST/HST audit review.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Full convertibility exists on both the current account and capital account. The Canadian dollar floats freely, and there are no exchange-control licences, quotas, or prior approvals for inbound investment, profit repatriation, trade payments, or intercompany payments. A British Columbia Unlimited Liability Company (CA_ULC) may receive equity injections, shareholder loans, and trade settlements in CAD or any foreign currency without Bank of Canada approval. Profit remittance to a non-resident shareholder is legally unrestricted; however, dividends and similar distributions paid to non-residents are subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax at 25%, generally reduced under an applicable tax treaty (e.g., 15% for portfolio US shareholders or 5% for certain qualifying US corporate shareholders). The ULC's potential US tax pass-through classification does not alter Canadian convertibility and does not create regulatory exchange barriers.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no required conversion ratio. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receivables and maintain foreign-denominated operating accounts. For a POD custom apparel retail operation, export proceeds from US customers may be held in USD and used directly for supplier payments. The only relevant obligations are bank-level reporting of international electronic fund transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, and tax disclosure/income inclusion under the Income Tax Act.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; Canada has abolished dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration. Prudential regulation of banks is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and cross-border funds movement reporting is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium-High as a secondary disbursement rail, but Low as a substitute for a schedule-I bank account. Cross-border digital EMIs are not chartered banks in Canada and cannot accept deposits protected by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation. To operate in Canada, an EMI must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business or Foreign Money Services Business and must comply with Canadian AML/KYC obligations. For the CA_ULC, Stripe/PayPal are commercially viable for low-value POD customer receipts and supplier settlements, but the corporate treasury backbone should remain a Canadian bank account for CDIC coverage, creditor protection, and unlimited-liability governance. Fintech EMIs should therefore be limited to collection and/or payout-side acceleration, with periodic sweeps into the Canadian operational account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Unlimited liability company with no paid-in share capital, a US pass-through tax election, and no established Canadian banking relationship.
        
Root cause: Unlimited liability company with no paid-in share capital, a US pass-through tax election, and no established Canadian banking relationship.

        
Defense evidence: Provide a BC Corporate Registry certificate of incorporation, notice of articles, certificate of good standing, corporate minute book with subscription records, board resolutions authorizing account opening, a source-of-funds declaration for the initial capital contribution, and a legal opinion from Canadian counsel confirming the lawful nature of the ULC and the absence of MSB activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound equity capital from a non-resident parent or shareholder should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the ULC's designated CAD or USD clearing account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The wire instruction should clearly identify the remitter, the ULC's full legal name, its account number, and a payment narrative stating 'share capital contribution / paid-up capital'. A director's resolution authorizing the share issuance and account activation should be held in the ULC minute book. If the bank requires a purpose categorization, the remitter should choose the capital-payment category rather than a goods/services code. Because incoming international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting, supporting documentation must include a source-of-funds letter, the shareholder resolution, and the incorporation certificate. If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, a written loan agreement with commercial terms must be maintained, and the wire narrative should read 'shareholder loan' to avoid misclassification as equity.Outbound trade settlements for POD raw materials, printing suppliers, freight forwarders, and fulfillment vendors should use SWIFT MT103 for single-receiver cross-border payments, including the ULC's name and GST/HST business number in the remitter field. For high-value or recurring garment production purchases from overseas manufacturers, the ULC may open an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (LC) at RBC, BMO, or Scotiabank; the LC should be issued on a sight-payment basis with a full set of clean-on-board transport documents, commercial invoices, packing lists, and certificates of origin. Larger-scale treasury runs may use MT101/MT202 batch-clearing protocols.
