Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Head Office, Ottawa; online portal: My Business Account (MyBA) and Business Registration Online (BRO). For British Columbia provincial sales tax, competent authority is the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (eTaxBC).

E-government filing portal

A British Columbia limited partnership (LP) must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Registration is completed online via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1. Indicate 'Partnership' as the legal entity type and list each partner's identification (Individual Tax Number, Social Insurance Number, or BN for corporations). The CRA issues a 9-digit BN with program identifiers (e.g., 'RT' for GST/HST, 'RC' for payroll). If the LP makes taxable sales in BC, register separately for PST via eTaxBC under the BC Taxpayer Registration. Non-resident partners without a Canadian tax presence must apply for an Individual Tax Number (ITN) or, if corporations, a corporation tax BN, and may be required to file Canadian income tax returns under section 216 or Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. The LP must be registered for GST/HST before claiming input tax credits on exported services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Canadian law. A limited partnership is statutorily classified as a flow-through entity for income tax purposes (Income Tax Act sections 96–103) and as a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes (Excise Tax Act section 271). Available statutory elections include: (i) subsection 216(1) election for non-resident partners to pay tax on net Canadian-source income; (ii) section 156 election for closely related corporations to treat supplies as made for nil consideration; (iii) anti-hybrid elections under Income Tax Act section 18.7 and the Multilateral Convention; and (iv) an election to treat a partnership as a 'registrant' under the Excise Tax Act for foreign suppliers of digital services. No election can convert the LP into a taxable entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies); a person whose taxable supplies exceed this threshold must register for GST/HST. For a BC LP exporting services, zero-rated export revenue counts toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Data labeling, AI training, and BPO services exported to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST qualify for zero-rating under section 22 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the recipient is outside Canada, the supply is not an excepted service (e.g., services performed in Canada for a non-resident in respect of Canadian land or tangible personal property, or supplied while the non-resident is in Canada), and the supplier maintains evidence of the export nature of the transaction.

  • Service contract / work order identifying the non-resident recipient and scope of data labeling services
  • Commercial invoice stating 'GST/HST zero-rated – Export Service' and citing section 22 of Schedule VI
  • Evidence of payment via international funds transfer (e.g., SWIFT/IBAN) demonstrating receipt of consideration from abroad
  • Place-of-supply analysis or supporting records showing the recipient is a non-resident and not a GST/HST registrant
  • If tangible deliverables are included: Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A), bill of lading / airway bill, and inward remittance certificate

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping exists federally or in BC. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information under section 221 of the Excise Tax Act and CRA Information Circular IC 4023; electronic invoices are accepted. BC PST requires sellers to issue receipts but does not impose electronic clearance obligations.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years (unlimited for misrepresentation, fraud, or if tax was evaded). Claiming zero-rating without substantiation results in assessment at 5% GST/HST plus arrears interest. Penalties include: gross negligence penalty under section 285 (up to 50% of the tax amount); false statement penalty under section 280 ($100 plus 50% of the underestimated tax); and tax evasion penalties under section 327 (up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment). Input tax credits improperly claimed are subject to clawback, and the entitlement to zero-rating may be retroactively revoked, making all prior invoices taxable at 5%.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalised and freely convertible currency regime for both current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a G10, fully deliverable currency; there are no exchange-control permits, no prior approval gates for equity injections, and no mandatory conversion or surrender schedule. Convertibility risk is therefore non-existent at the sovereign level; the operative constraints are fiscal. A British Columbia Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: business income is allocated to partners under Part I of the Income Tax Act, and the LP itself is not subject to entity-level tax. Remittance hurdles arise principally from: (i) Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, reduced by treaties) on amounts paid or credited to non-resident partners for interest, rents, royalties, and management or administration fees; (ii) treaty permanent-establishment exposure of non-resident partners carrying on the Canadian data-annotation business through the LP, requiring Canadian Part I returns on allocable income; (iii) thin-capitalisation limits where foreign partners substitute shareholder debt for equity; and (iv) the Investment Canada Act notification / net-benefit review where a foreign acquisition of control of the Canadian business exceeds statutory monetary thresholds. Pure profit distributions derived from LP business income are not subject to Part XIII withholding, but competent counsel should document all cross-border service fees and royalty payments to avoid recharacterisation as payments subject to withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no fixed repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters of services (including AI data-labelling, RLHF curation, and computer-vision annotation delivered cross-border) may retain USD or other foreign currency in offshore or onshore foreign-denominated accounts indefinitely. In practice: (i) the BC LP must measure and report income in CAD for financial statements and income tax filings, using the Bank of Canada published exchange rate as required by the Income Tax Act; (ii) international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC Large International EFT reporting and any associated Suspicious Transaction Report obligations; (iii) tier-1 Canadian banks apply AML/ATF expectations that export revenues flow through the corporate settlement account with documented underlying invoices, but no bank or regulator may compel conversion of USD into CAD. The LP may freely fund CAD operating accounts from USD receipts at negotiated corporate spot rates, pre-contracted forwards, or a bank-managed sweep facility.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); monetary and systemic liquidity authority. Prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Foreign exchange policy: Department of Finance Canada (no stand-alone FX administration exists). Anti-money laundering / counter-terrorist financing supervisory authority: Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium as a secondary mass-payout rail; Low as a primary treasury solution. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) holding an EEA or UK e-money licence cannot lawfully take deposits in Canada: the deposit-taking business is statutorily reserved for entities authorised under the Bank Act (banks or federal trust companies), and an incoming foreign EMI would require an OSFI-authorised branch under the foreign-bank entry regime. Such EMIs may nevertheless operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) in Canada through licensed subsidiaries (e.g., Wise Canada Ltd., PayPal Canada Co.), authorising solely payment-remittance services, not deposit-taking or CAD settlement-account custody. The BC LP may therefore run a compliant hybrid structure: (i) primary treasury and settlement accounts at RBC or TD; (ii) Wise Business / PayPal Business portals funded from the primary account to execute low-value USD/CAD mass payouts to individual data annotators in 40+ countries; (iii) documented client service agreements and annotator statements of work retained to evidence payment purpose; and (iv) daily or weekly sweeping of the MSB float back to the tier-1 bank to minimise counterparty and audit risk. Legal constraints: the MSB must be FINTRAC-registered; the LP must not hold out the EMI balance as an insured deposit; and any accumulated balance at the EMI is uninsured and increases exposure under Ontario/BC consumer-protection and Canadian privacy law only insofar as the MSB handles personal data subject to PIPEDA.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity funding into the BC LP is executed through standard commercial-bank wire protocols: a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's bank via the Canadian bank's US or foreign correspondent, settling either directly in USD to the LP's USD settlement account or in CAD after a treasury-desk conversion. No Canadian FX permit, declaration, or surrender is required; the Bank of Canada has no approval gate. The remitter should use payment narrative such as 'Capital contribution / additional paid-in capital to limited partnership operating account' and must reference the LP's legal name, account number, and SWIFT BIC to ensure straight-through processing. The receiving bank triggers FINTRAC Large International EFT reporting at CAD 10,000 and requires the LP to provide: (i) evidence that the investor appears on the partnership register; (ii) source-of-funds documentation consistent with the KYC file; (iii) the capital-contribution resolution of the general partner; and (iv) an instruction letter identifying the beneficiary account and intended use. For verification, the relationship manager provides the inward-remittance customer-reference report and a Lynx/ACSS credit advice; same-day CAD or USD value depends on wire cut-off times (Fedwire/CHIPS for USD legs and Lynx for CAD legs). Capital is not locked; there is no minimum stay requirement, and repatriation of contributed capital is unrestricted except for bank standard due-diligence and Part XIII captive rules where the contribution is recharacterised as debt.Outbound disbursements clear through four primary rails: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 wires for vendor invoices, overseas contractor payroll, partner distributions, and intercompany settlements — CAD legs clear through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value system (SWIFT-compatible, operating around the clock with final settlement at the Bank of Canada); USD legs clear via the correspondent bank's Fedwire/CHIPS network; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the tier-1 Canadian bank (optionally with Export Development Canada guarantees) for procurement of GPU workstations, data-centre equipment, and enterprise software from US/Asia suppliers, payable at sight or by deferred payment against compliant documents, documents-checked under UCP 600 and ISP98 for standby LC backstops; (3) EFT/ACSS batch settlement (Payments Canada Automated Clearing Settlement System) for recurring domestic CAD payroll to Canadian employees and contractors; and (4) Wise/PayPal mass-payout aggregator APIs funded from the primary Canadian account for low-value cross-border annotator remittances in multiple currencies. All outbound transactions above CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC international-EFT reporting and must pass OFAC/OSFI sanctions screening at both Canadian and correspondent banks. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) holding an EEA or UK e-money licence cannot lawfully take deposits in Canada: the deposit-taking business is statutorily reserved for entities authorised under the Bank Act (banks or federal trust companies), and an incoming foreign EMI would require an OSFI-authorised branch under the foreign-bank entry regime. Such EMIs may nevertheless operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) in Canada through licensed subsidiaries (e.g., Wise Canada Ltd., PayPal Canada Co.), authorising solely payment-remittance services, not deposit-taking or CAD settlement-account custody. The BC LP may therefore run a compliant hybrid structure: (i) primary treasury and settlement accounts at RBC or TD; (ii) Wise Business / PayPal Business portals funded from the primary account to execute low-value USD/CAD mass payouts to individual data annotators in 40+ countries; (iii) documented client service agreements and annotator statements of work retained to evidence payment purpose; and (iv) daily or weekly sweeping of the MSB float back to the tier-1 bank to minimise counterparty and audit risk. Legal constraints: the MSB must be FINTRAC-registered; the LP must not hold out the EMI balance as an insured deposit; and any accumulated balance at the EMI is uninsured and increases exposure under Ontario/BC consumer-protection and Canadian privacy law only insofar as the MSB handles personal data subject to PIPEDA.
