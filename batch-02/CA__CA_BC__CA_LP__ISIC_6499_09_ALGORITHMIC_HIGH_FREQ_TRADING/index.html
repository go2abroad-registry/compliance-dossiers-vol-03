Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal income tax, GST/HST, and Business Number (BN) administration; portal: 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/business-account.html) and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topic/registering-your-business/register.html). Provincial: British Columbia Ministry of Finance - BC PST, employer health tax, and provincial corporate/partnership income tax; portal: eTaxBC (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the limited partnership with the BC Registrar of Companies to obtain the provincial registration or extra-provincial limited partnership registration number. Step 2: Procure a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO), supplying the LP's full legal name, BC registered address, principal business address, fiscal year-end, and business activity (NAICS 2022 523130 / ISIC v4 6499). The 9-digit BN functions as Canada's statutory TIN for partnerships. Step 3: Select the required CRA program accounts, typically GST/HST (RT) and payroll (RP); a corporate income tax (RC) account is not opened for the LP itself if it is a pure pass-through except where SIFT tax attaches. Step 4: Non-resident limited partners who have Canadian-source allocated income must obtain an Individual Tax Number (ITN) if they do not hold a Social Insurance Number, or authenticate their foreign TIN for T5013/T5013SUM allocation. The LP files the T5013 information return using the BN. There is no separate Canadian statutory VAT identification number; the BN-GST/HST account number is the operative VAT identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under ITA ss. 96-103 and the BC Partnership Act, the LP is fiscally transparent for federal and BC income tax: the partnership computes income/loss and allocates it to partners who bear tax at their own rates. The LP files T5013 information returns and does not pay federal income tax, unless it is a publicly traded specified investment flow-through (SIFT) partnership subject to entity-level tax under ITA s. 197. Non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income allocable to the partnership's Canadian establishment, subject to treaty relief. For GST/HST, the LP is a separate 'person' and will constitute a financial institution under ETA Part IX if its principal business is trading of financial instruments.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; classification follows partnership law and tax jurisprudence. Available elections: (i) non-calendar fiscal-period election for partnership year-end; (ii) election under ITA s. 216(1) enabling non-resident partners to file on a net basis for Canadian rental income rather than Part XIII gross-basis withholding; (iii) GST/HST elections under ETA ss. 150 and 156 for closely related corporations and qualifying partnerships; (iv) election to zero-rate a supply of a financial service to a non-resident under Schedule VI, Part VII; and (v) hybrid mismatch elections/allocations under ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5 where foreign hybrid entity arrangements are present. All elections are effected through prescribed CRA forms or return disclosures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small supplier threshold calculated on worldwide taxable supplies over the four preceding calendar quarters plus the current quarter, excluding zero-rated supplies, supplies of financial services, and capital property dispositions. Registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded or where a non-resident carries on a commercial activity in Canada and makes taxable supplies. Because the LP's trading revenues are principally exempt or zero-rated financial services, mandatory registration may not be triggered by trading turnover alone; fee-for-service market-making revenue that is a taxable supply must be monitored separately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V to the ETA where the supplier exports or delivers them outside Canada as a supply in the course of a commercial activity. For financial services of an algorithmic HFT LP, zero-rating is available under Schedule VI, Part VII where the recipient is a non-resident and either (i) the service is a prescribed financial service under Regulation 11 to the ETA, (ii) the underlying financial instrument is not a Canadian financial instrument and the service is not for use in Canada, or (iii) the service is performed wholly outside Canada. Proprietary trading for the LP's own account is not a 'supply' to any person and cannot be zero-rated; GST embedded in Canadian trading infrastructure, exchange access fees, co-location, and data charges is generally irrecoverable unless it falls within a specified ITC attribution method applicable to selected listed financial institutions.

  • CBSA Canada Export Declaration and eManifest/AAMS export report where tangible goods are exported
  • Commercial invoice and written supply agreement identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Evidence of non-resident status, including certificate of residence, foreign TIN, or sworn declaration
  • Bank payment confirmation or SWIFT deposit slip evidencing receipt of consideration from outside Canada
  • T5013 and GST/HST return schedules documenting allocation, reporting, and substantiation of the zero-rated claim

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system, API transaction stamping, or digital fiscal stamp regime. CRA requires invoices from GST/HST registrants to contain statutory content: supplier name, BN, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description, tax rate, and tax payable. All GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST EDI, with CRA offering API-based return transmission for large filers. A voluntary pan-Canadian Peppol-standard e-invoicing framework is emerging. BC PST registrants must file through eTaxBC and retain full sales/audit trails, but there is no transaction-level pre-clearance.

CRA may reassess zero-rating claims within the normal four-year period, extended to ten years where misrepresentation or neglect is shown. An unsupported export claim is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST and assessed with interest from the return filing date. Culpable conduct attracts penalties under ETA s. 285 (minimum CAD 100 or 25% of the tax) and ITA s. 163(2) for income tax understatement of up to 50% of the tax. Zero-rating of financial services is invalid where the service relates to Canadian-risk financial instruments or is used by the non-resident in Canada; such claims trigger immediate clawback of refundable input tax credits. Canadian law does not prescribe a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied administratively to substantiate economic return and treaty consistency. Persistent failure to repatriate consideration may also attract transfer-pricing challenge under ITA s. 247 and general anti-avoidance rule review under ITA s. 245, with penalties of up to 25% of the avoided tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. CAD is freely floating; no distinction is made between current and capital account transactions, and no exchange control approval, license, or quota applies to inbound or outbound capital flows. Profit and capital repatriation is unrestricted in FX terms. The only material frictions are tax-related: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced) on distributions to non-resident limited partners characterized as income of a Canadian business, and potential Part XIV branch tax if the LP is deemed to carry on business through a permanent establishment in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds, services income, or proprietary trading gains. Foreign currency receipts may be retained indefinitely in CAD or foreign-denominated accounts. The sole compliance overlay is FINTRAC reporting: international electronic funds transfers (EFT) of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the financial institution; the LP must maintain underlying trade and funding records for at least five years under PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Foreign exchange administration is distributed: FINTRAC (AML/EFT surveillance), Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions), and Department of Finance Global Affairs Canada (sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act). Canada has no dedicated foreign exchange control agency; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility for primary clearing, Medium for ancillary payments only. The EU EMI passport does not extend to Canada; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut) must register as Money Services Businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC and cannot access Lynx (Bank of Canada high-value payment system) or CDSX securities settlement rails. Unregistered EMIs are subject to FINTRAC compliance orders and Canadian banks will reject their downstream clearing. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

SWIFT MT103 remittance from the funding LP to the designated CAD/USD operating account at a Schedule I bank. Beneficiary field (59) must exactly match the registered BC Limited Partnership legal name and address; field 50K remitter must equal the funding partner's name; field 70 must reference 'LP Capital Contribution per Subscription Agreement dated [date]'. No Bank of Canada or exchange-control approval is required. For inbound wires of CAD 10,000 or more, the receiving bank files a FINTRAC EFT report; the bank will release funds to the trading account only after receiving the signed subscription agreement, LP certificate/register, and source-of-funds documentation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate rate (15% Federal + 12% British Columbia). The BC limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and is not assessed under Part I at the entity level; the income is allocated and taxed at the partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding rate applies to taxable Canadian-source dividends paid to non-residents by a Canadian corporation. LP profit distributions are not dividends; they are partnership capital or income allocations. Active business income allocated to a non-resident partner is taxed under Part I on net income, and no additional Part XIII withholding applies at distribution. Passive amounts flowing through the LP, such as interest, rents, royalties, or management fees paid to non-residents, remain subject to 25% Part XIII withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year partnership fiscal period, filed on or before March 31 following the fiscal period end. For a non-calendar fiscal period, filed within five months after the fiscal period end. T5013 slips must be provided to partners by the applicable CRA deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within six months after the corporation's taxation year-end. For a December 31 year-end corporate partner, the T2 is due on June 30 of the following year. Final tax payment is generally due within two months after year-end for a non-CCPC and three months after year-end for a CCPC.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within one month after the anniversary of the limited partnership's registration date in British Columbia.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000. Canadian transfer-pricing documentation and Form T106 are generally required where the aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions between the taxpayer and non-residents exceeds C$1,000,000 in a taxation year. Country-by-country reporting obligations apply to MNE groups with consolidated group revenue above approximately C$900,000,000, aligned to the OECD threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the Income Tax Act s.245 general anti-avoidance rule and treaty-shopping limitations impose a de facto economic-substance standard for BC LPs. The LP must demonstrate real BC presence: physical office, employees, board or partner decision-making, trading infrastructure, bank accounts, books and records, and genuine economic risk. A shell LP with only a registered agent and conduit non-resident partners may be treated as a sham, and treaty benefits can be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Falsified Partnership Allocations

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239 criminal liability: fines of at least 50% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Civil sanctions include s.163(2) gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax, interest, reassessment of all open years, seizure of bank accounts and receivables under s.225, and asset freezing.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and controlling general partners who authorize or acquiesce in false filings may be personally prosecuted. A director of a corporate general partner faces s.227.1 director liability for unremitted withholding or Part XIII taxes and can be pursued for personal assets. Criminal indictment may trigger travel restrictions and cross-border arrest warrants.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Undocumented Intercompany Fees

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the transaction to the arm's-length amount under s.247(2). If the transfer price falls outside the arm's-length range and contemporaneous documentation is absent, s.247(7) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment, plus interest. Gross negligence penalties and denial of deductible expenses may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner can be held personally liable only where they cause or participate in fraudulent misrepresentations or abandon the corporate form. Where the LP is used as a façade, piercing the corporate veil exposes directors to joint and several liability for assessed taxes, penalties, and interest.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA can reach C$100,000 for individuals and C$500,000 per violation for entities; BC registry false or omitted beneficial ownership disclosures trigger further penalties and possible dissolution. Criminal Code s.462.31 money-laundering sanctions include imprisonment for up to 14 years and criminal forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be personally named in FINTRAC or BC Registry enforcement actions for consent or negligence. Courts can issue asset-freezing orders and travel restrictions, and criminally convicted principals face long-term imprisonment and post-release financial monitoring.
Artificial Shell / Sham / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR applies under s.245 to deny the tax benefit, with a 25% penalty on the denied tax benefit under s.245(5), plus interest. The CRA may recharacterize the entire transaction, deny DTA treaty benefits, and disregard the limited partnership for tax purposes. If criminal intent is proven, s.239 evasion provisions apply.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the LP is a mere façade. General partners and directors of the corporate general partner may be held jointly and severally liable for the LP's unpaid taxes, interest, and penalties. CRA collection powers extend to personal trusts, offshore accounts, and third-party payments. Directors may also face travel bans and surrender of passports after criminal charges are laid.
