Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a British Columbia limited partnership; capital contributions, whether in cash, property, or services, are fixed solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow deposit, capital attestation, or paid-up capital verification is required at registration. Commercial lenders and the franchise supply chain model may, however, contractually require an initial contributed capital commitment (commonly CAD $500,000) and a registered records office at the general partner's principal address.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire-marshal approval and pre-occupancy fire-safety inspection under the BC Fire Code; building department occupancy and structural-load certification for storage racks, mezzanine and process equipment; and WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety clearance under the Workers Compensation Act, R.S.B.C. 1996, c. 492, comprising employer registration, a written hazard-prevention and HACCP food-safety program, sanitation and hygiene documentation, and proof of commercial general liability coverage before production commences.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a British Columbia limited partnership; capital contributions, whether in cash, property, or services, are fixed solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow deposit, capital attestation, or paid-up capital verification is required at registration. Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. Provincial: British Columbia Ministry of Finance — BC PST registration via eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

The BC LP is fiscally transparent for income tax but a deemed person for GST/HST (ETA s. 272.1). A foreign-owned LP must: (1) register the limited partnership with the BC Corporate Registry via OneStop to obtain a provincial LP number; (2) apply to CRA for a Business Number (BN) through the BRO portal or Form RC1, selecting 'Partnership' as entity type and appointing a Canadian resident representative for service; (3) cause each non-resident partner to obtain a Canadian tax identifier (corporate partners obtain a BN; individual partners obtain an Individual Tax Number via Form T1261); (4) open GST/HST (RT) and BC PST (BC) program accounts under the same BN, plus a payroll (RP) account where Canadian employees are hired; (5) register for GST/HST immediately where worldwide taxable supplies equal or exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters, and register for BC PST before making the first taxable retail sale in BC (special-seller nexus applies to out-of-province sellers exceeding CAD 10,000 of annual BC sales); (6) maintain Canadian books and records, including a Canadian address for service if the LP is a non-resident registrant.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the BC LP is not subject to entity-level Part I income tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada); income and losses are computed at the partnership level and allocated to each partner (ITA s. 96). Non-resident partners remain taxable in Canada on Canadian-source business income allocated from the LP on a net basis (ITA ss. 2(3), 115(1)(a) and 96) and may be exposed to Part XIII withholding on other Canadian-source streams. Territorial scope: Canada taxes the LP's Canadian-source income; no tax-exempt zone treatment is available. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership itself is a registrant separate and distinct from its partners.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election and no elective hybrid-mismatch regime. Applicable elections for this structure: (i) ITA s. 216 election allowing a non-resident partner to file a net-basis Part I return for Canadian rental/forestry income instead of Part XIII withholding; (ii) ITA s. 249.1(9) election for a non-calendar partnership fiscal period; (iii) ETA s. 240(2) voluntary GST/HST registration for a small-supplier partnership; (iv) ETA s. 156 'closely related corporation' elections are unavailable because the LP is not a corporation. Hybrid mismatch rules implementing BEPS Action 2 operate by statutory deduction denial rather than taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); BC PST: no general threshold for in-province vendors, while out-of-province and e-commerce sellers trigger a vendor registration obligation upon exceeding CAD 10,000 of annual BC sales (special-seller nexus rules).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies qualify for 0% GST under ETA Schedule VI Part V: (i) s. 1 — goods physically exported from Canada by the supplier or a common carrier for the non-resident purchaser, generally within 60 days of the supply; (ii) s. 7 — services rendered to a non-resident where the service is performed for and used wholly outside Canada (e.g., international franchise consulting); (iii) s. 10 — supplies of intangible personal property (franchise rights, trademarks, recipes, know-how) to a non-resident franchisee unless the intangible relates to real property situated in Canada, goods situated in Canada at the time of supply, or is for use primarily in Canada. Export syrups and ingredients shipped to foreign franchisees qualify for zero-rating only when physically exported; BC PST applies to goods consumed in BC and is not charged on goods the LP delivers outside BC or to a common carrier for direct export.

  • Commercial invoice (supplier's invoice identifying the non-resident buyer and expressly stating that GST/HST applies at 0%)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign franchisee
  • Canada Border Services Agency export declaration (eManifest / CADEX) where goods are controlled, permit-required, or exceed the CAD 2,000 non-reportable export threshold
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire advice evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration into the LP's Canadian account
  • Franchise agreement, royalty statements and interparty correspondence demonstrating that the intangible property rights are exploited and used outside Canada

No mandatory real-time tax clearance, digital stamping, or CRA API pre-approval for invoices in Canada. Electronic invoices are valid and CRA accepts scanned, EDI and Peppol documents where they satisfy books-and-records requirements (ETA s. 286; ITA s. 230). Peppol-based e-invoicing operates voluntarily in Canada. The mandatory substantive element is issuance of a compliant tax invoice (ETA s. 223) showing the registrant's BN, transaction date, vendor/purchaser identity and GST amount, with records retained for six years.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years of the reporting-period due date (ETA s. 298(1)); the limitation period is indefinitely extended where a partnership or partner has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud (ETA s. 298(2); ITA s. 152(4)). Erroneous export zero-rating is clawed back at the full 5% GST plus statutory interest, and related input tax credits are denied. Civil penalties equal the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax sought to be evaded for knowing or grossly negligent false statements (ETA s. 285), with a parallel 50% gross-negligence penalty at the partner level under ITA s. 163(2); criminal prosecution for tax evasion is available under ETA s. 327. The substantiation burden rests on the LP: zero-rating is automatically recaptured where goods are not exported within 60 days of the supply or where the complete export evidence bundle is not maintained.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are open; there are no exchange controls, convertibility quotas, or central bank approval requirements for capital inflows or outflows. Profit remittance is generally unrestricted for a British Columbia limited partnership, subject solely to tax compliance: non-resident partners are taxable on their share of Canadian-source income, payments such as franchise royalties to non-resident licensors are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of up to 25% (treaty-reduced where applicable), transfer-pricing rules must be respected, and the Investment Canada Act may require notification or review for foreign investments that acquire control of a Canadian business. The Bank of Canada does not block or delay repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for banking export receipts. Exporters and franchise supply-chain entities may retain foreign-currency proceeds in foreign or domestic accounts. However, every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the financial intermediary, cross-border payments must be accurately described on Customs Canada Border Services Agency declarations, and balance-of-payments surveys under the Statistics Act may require reporting of franchising, royalties, and goods trade with non-residents.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank and monetary authority responsible for currency issuance, monetary policy, and the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). Prudential supervision of federally regulated banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). AML/CTF supervision is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Economic sanctions policy and administration are led by Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act. There is no dedicated Canadian foreign-exchange-control authority requiring transaction-level licensing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary bank; Medium as a supplementary payment rail. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as banks in Canada and cannot hold deposits in the same manner as Schedule I banks. A foreign EMI must, before operating in Canada, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act and register as a payment service provider with the Bank of Canada, and must also register as a money services business with FINTRAC if it engages in funds transfer, FX dealing, or remittance activities. Funds held at an unregistered or offshore EMI are not CDIC-insured and such EMIs do not have direct access to LVTS clearing finality. For franchise royalty collection from online channels and cross-border FX execution, a licensed EMI may be used as a non-banking collection conduit, but all treasury buffers and statutory capital should remain with a Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or limited-partner capital should be routed by SWIFT MT103 to the BC LP's Canadian bank account. The remittance should name the exact legal payee as registered in the BC Limited Partnership Declaration, state the beneficiary account number and Canadian transit/CC code, and include in field 70 a clear narrative such as 'Capital contribution to BC limited partnership'. Canada does not operate a formal external-account purpose-code regime; the narrative must nonetheless be transactionally accurate and sufficient for the receiving bank's FINTRAC obligations. International EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC by the financial institution, so remitters should not fragment payments. Inbound funds settle through the Canadian correspondent and LVTS or Real-Time Rail; no Bank of Canada approval is required.Franchise royalties and profit distributions to non-residents should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers in USD or CAD via a Canadian Schedule I bank. For bulk syrup and packaging imports from suppliers, commercial letters of credit under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700/MT710) are the preferred clearing rail, with CAD domestic payments executed through ACSS or LVTS. For USD outbound payments, the bank will route through its U.S. correspondent into Fedwire or CHIPS. Non-resident royalty payments must be made under a binding license agreement, with Part XIII withholding tax remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency and NR4/NR4A reporting completed where required. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary bank; Medium as a supplementary payment rail. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as banks in Canada and cannot hold deposits in the same manner as Schedule I banks. A foreign EMI must, before operating in Canada, comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act and register as a payment service provider with the Bank of Canada, and must also register as a money services business with FINTRAC if it engages in funds transfer, FX dealing, or remittance activities. Funds held at an unregistered or offshore EMI are not CDIC-insured and such EMIs do not have direct access to LVTS clearing finality. For franchise royalty collection from online channels and cross-border FX execution, a licensed EMI may be used as a non-banking collection conduit, but all treasury buffers and statutory capital should remain with a Canadian bank.
