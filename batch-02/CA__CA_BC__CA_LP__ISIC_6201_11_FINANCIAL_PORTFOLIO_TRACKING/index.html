Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax under ITA s. 96; net income and loss are allocated to partners regardless of actual distributions, and no entity-level income tax is imposed on the LP itself. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is a distinct notional 'person' that must register, invoice, collect, and remit tax. For BC PST, the LP is a vendor/seller. A non-resident LP is a domestic partnership for Canadian tax unless treaty or statutory rules otherwise apply; Canada taxes the worldwide income of resident partners and Canadian-source income of non-resident partners on a territorial carve-out basis. No tax-exempt zone applies in BC.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the legal form under the BC Partnership Act and the federal Income Tax Act. Available statutory elections and planning mechanisms: (1) ITA s. 249.1 election to adopt a non-calendar partnership fiscal period where eligibility conditions are met; (2) Excise Tax Act s. 273 election to appoint a Canadian tax representative for a non-resident LP; (3) ETA s. 172.1 election for simplified input tax credit calculation; (4) partnership agreement allocation binding through ITA s. 96(1); and (5) for hybrid mismatch exposure under ITA ss. 18.6 and 20.2, no domestic prophylactic election exists, so CRA Advance Income Tax Ruling is the only certainty mechanism; a US Form 8832 check-the-box election may independently characterize the Canadian LP for US federal purposes and must be managed to avoid unintended hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST small-supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 of aggregate worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters or the current quarter (Excise Tax Act s. 148); a non-resident LP crossing CAD 30,000 in global sales turnover must register for GST/HST. There is no PST registration threshold in BC: any vendor making taxable sales of goods, taxable software, or specified taxable services to BC consumers must hold a BC PST vendor account from the first sale; pure cloud/SaaS access is generally not subject to PST unless supplied as prewritten taxable software.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of the financial-portfolio-tracking cloud software (SaaS) is zero-rated under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI Part V s. 7 (services supplied to a non-resident) and CRA GST/HST Info Sheet GI-078 if: (i) the recipient is a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and receives the service outside Canada; (ii) the service is not performed in Canada in respect of Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or for an individual present in Canada for personal consumption; (iii) the supply is not an exempt financial service, so the contract must sever pure software licensing/cloud access from any portfolio-management advisory function that could constitute a financial service under ETA Schedule V s. 1; and (iv) the invoice names the non-resident customer and states the zero-rated export status with no GST charged. The exporter effectively obtains the refund by reporting zero-rated supplies on the GST/HST return and recovering related input tax credits; no separate VAT refund application is required.

  • Customs export declaration (Form B13A filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System) when tangible goods are exported; not applicable to pure SaaS or cloud services
  • Signed cloud service or licence agreement evidencing the non-resident recipient, place of supply, and absence of GST
  • Commercial invoice marked 'Zero-Rated Export / ETA Schedule VI Part V' with no GST charged
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103 confirmation, or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in CAD within the normal business cycle
  • Annual T5013 partner allocation schedule and GST/HST return documentation preserving the complete export audit trail

Canada currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control (CTC) API, and no digital stamping requirement. CRA mandates that electronic records be retained in readable, auditable form and accepts PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoice exchange on a voluntary basis. The Pan-Canadian Trilateral e-Invoice Framework (2024-2026) is under consultation and not yet mandatory. BC PST rules require vendor point-of-sale and e-commerce sales records for audit, but no BC government fiscal clearance API exists.

CRA may reassess zero-rating claims within 4 years of the normal assessment date, extended to 6 years for misrepresentation and unlimited for fraud or wilful omission. Penalties under ETA s. 285 include CAD 100 or 50% of the net tax understated; ETA s. 280 interest accrues daily at prescribed rates. Gross negligence penalties can reach 50% of the tax, and culpable conduct extends the assessment window and supports retrospective clawback of input tax credits. Criminal conviction under ETA s. 290 carries fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years. BC PST audits may separately disallow exempt software status and reassess PST for up to 4 years of unreported taxable software sales; interest at BC prescribed rates compounds on any clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account regime. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951, and no residual exchange controls, prior approval regimes, or surrender obligations exist. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating, fully deliverable, and clears on CLS Bank. Inward capital contributions, outward dividend distributions, royalty payments, and service fees may be remitted without Bank of Canada authorization. Profit remittance from the British Columbia Limited Partnership to non-resident partners is subject only to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (treaty-reducible, e.g., 15% under the Canada-US treaty, 10-15% under most DTAAs), not to any FX gate. For the ISIC 6201 subscription SaaS, cross-border subscription revenue collected via Stripe and Apple IAP is freely convertible and may be retained in CAD, USD, or other currencies at Canadian banks without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory repatriation timelines, and no required conversion ratios. Software exports (ISIC 6201) are treated as services under the EIPA/CBSA goods framework and are not subject to customs surrender. The CA-LP may lawfully hold 100% of foreign subscription revenue in domestic or offshore currency accounts. The only reporting obligations are FINTRAC-driven: financial intermediaries must report large cash transactions over CAD 10,000 and international electronic funds transfers over CAD 10,000; the LP itself is not a reporting entity unless it becomes a money services business.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and custodian of official international reserves; banking supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated banks. Canada has no dedicated foreign exchange control administration; the Department of Finance Canada administers the Investment Canada Act (ICA) for inward investment screening, while FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for financial compliance. No entity-level FX licensing is required for corporate treasury operations.
