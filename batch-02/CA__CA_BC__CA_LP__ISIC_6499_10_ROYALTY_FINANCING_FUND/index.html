Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

BC Registry Services – Corporate Registry, Ministry of Finance, Victoria, British Columbia; filings submitted through the BC Registry Online portal.

Governing statute

British Columbia Limited Partnership Act, RSBC 1996, c. 294, as amended, particularly ss. 2–9 (formation and registration), ss. 12–20 (limited partners, contributions and withdrawal), s. 23 (records), and ss. 45–52 (duties of general partner and dissolution); where the general partner is a BC corporation, Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c. 57, ss. 127 and 254–259.

Formation / constitutional filings

LP Form 1 – Application for Registration of a Limited Partnership (under the Limited Partnership Act), leading to issuance of the Certificate of Limited Partnership by the Registrar.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a British Columbia limited partnership. Capital contributions are fixed by the partnership agreement; limited partners' withdrawal of capital is restricted under s. 18 of the Limited Partnership Act to preserve limited liability. No escrow account or capital deposit duty is imposed by the BC Corporate Registry.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal taxation; electronic portal: CRA My Business Account (www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance through the OneStop BC portal (www.onestop.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Non-resident and domestic limited partnerships apply for a federal Business Number (BN) using the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts.' CRA issues a 9-digit BN and a GST/HST program account bearing the two-letter identifier 'RT.' The BC Registrar of Companies first registers the limited partnership under the Partnership Act (BC); Form RC1 must attach the BC certificate of registration, the partnership agreement, and, for non-resident partners, Form RC464A (Non-Resident Partnership Information Return). Because the LP is fiscally transparent, no corporate income-tax program account (RC) is opened for the partnership itself; the LP files an annual T5013 Information Return and T5013 slips, while each partner reports its allocable share under its own TIN/BN. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada reach the CAD 30,000 threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent under s.96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada). Partnership income, deductions, and tax credits are allocated to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is a separate 'person' registrant under the Excise Tax Act (Canada).

Available election forms

No Canadian equivalent of a check-the-box election exists; transparency is mandatory for income tax purposes. Available elections include: (i) a s.249(8) ITA election permitting a non-calendar fiscal period; (ii) a GST/HST Quick Method election for small suppliers with worldwide taxable supplies under CAD 400,000; (iii) Canada–US Treaty Article IV(6) relief for fiscally transparent entities, enabling US partners to claim treaty benefits on allocated shares; and (iv) a US check-the-box election via Form 8832 to treat the BC LP as an association or corporation for US federal tax purposes, which may create hybrid-mismatch exposure constrained by ITA ss.18.2–18.4 (OECD BEPS Action 2 implementation).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or total supplies exceeding CAD 30,000 over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. For non-resident suppliers, the threshold is tested against taxable supplies made in Canada; registration is mandatory once exceeded and voluntary below the threshold to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (1) exports of tangible personal property in respect of which title is transferred and goods are physically exported from Canada by the supplier; (2) services supplied to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST and who is outside Canada when receiving the supply (ETA Sch. VI Pt V s.7), excluding supplies in respect of Canadian real property or goods used in Canada; and (3) financial services supplied to non-residents (including revenue-interest participation financing and royalty funding) under ETA Sch. VI Pt VII, subject to prescribed exceptions for payment-card issuance and payment-clearing services. Royalty and financing streams generated from non-resident digital-brand counterparties must satisfy the non-resident-ordinarily-resident and non-use-in-Canada conditions to attract the 0% rate.

  • Customs export declaration via CBSA: B13A (paper) or Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED)/electronic declaration for controlled or regulated goods
  • Commercial invoice or fully executed royalty-financing term sheet identifying the non-resident payor, revenue-interest royalty rate, and waterfall calculation
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CBSA-approved carrier manifest evidencing physical export of any goods collateral
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT statements evidencing the receipt of export consideration in Canadian dollars from the non-resident payor
  • Non-resident status evidence: certificate of foreign registration, Forms W-8BEN-E/W-8IMY, tax-residency certificates, and beneficial-ownership records

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, transaction-control APIs, or digital stamping. CRA accepts paper and PDF invoices and maintains a voluntary Pan-Canadian Trust Framework pilot for PEPPOL BIS e-invoicing (business-to-government). Registrants must issue GST/HST invoices containing the BN (RT program account), the GST amount charged or a zero-rating notation, and the supplier's name. Tax records must be kept in machine-readable format for six years under ETA s.286(1); GST/HST returns are filed electronically using XML or CRA-supported EDI channels.

CRA and CBSA audit export zero-rating through document-to-payment reconstruction. Failure to evidence export delivery and receipt of consideration from a non-resident within 180 days of invoice date results in reclassification to a taxable supply, reassessment of net GST at 5%, denial of input tax credits, and statutory interest. The CRA additionally asserts penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including: failure-to-register and failure-to-collect penalties equal to 100% of the tax at issue; general non-compliance penalties up to CAD 100,000 per offence (ETA s.281.1); false-statement penalties of 25% of tax avoided where zero-rating is unsupported; and, on criminal summary conviction, fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to two years (ETA s.327.2). The four-year reassessment period is extended to seven years for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud (ITA s.152(4)). BC PST enforcement applies separate clawback and 25% penalty exposure under the Provincial Sales Tax Act (BC).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full currency convertibility under IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no exchange controls, no advance approvals on current or capital account transactions, and no restrictions on converting CAD into foreign currency. Capital contributions, loan repayments, royalties, dividends, and branch profits may be freely remitted; however, Part XIII withholding tax on non-resident passive income and transfer-pricing documentation requirements apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain receipts in CAD or foreign currency accounts without government intervention. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply to cross-border electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, and authorized dealers must report balance-of-payments data to Statistics Canada / Bank of Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); BC Financial Services Authority supervises provincial financial services providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for Canadian-registered payment service providers and OSFI-regulated fintech platforms; Low for offshore digital EMIs without a Canadian presence, as the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires PSP registration and major banks generally require domestic settlement accounts and local addresses for corporate clients.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000 for non-service transactions; CAD 1,000,000 for services

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions; however, general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. For treaty benefits, the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under BEPS may be invoked.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fines up to 200% of tax evaded, up to CAD 200,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the evasion.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided, and potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties and reassessed taxes if they directed the arrangement.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for individuals, CAD 10 million for entities, and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for fines if they knowingly failed to implement AML controls.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, plus interest on the additional tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing arrangements.
