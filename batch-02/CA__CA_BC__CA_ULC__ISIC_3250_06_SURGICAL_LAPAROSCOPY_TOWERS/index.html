Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration and Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html); complementary authorities: British Columbia Ministry of Finance (BC PST administration through eTaxBC) and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export controls and customs reporting.

E-government filing portal

A BC-incorporated unlimited liability corporation (ULC) automatically receives the federal Business Number (BN-15, e.g., 123456789 RC0001) through the integrated BC Corporate Registry/CRA notification feed; the ULC then activates program accounts on the BRO portal, including the GST/HST account (RT), corporation income tax account (RC) and payroll account (RP) where applicable. A foreign entity not incorporated in Canada that carries on business in Canada must apply for a BN-15 by filing Form RC391 (Application for a CRA Business Number for a Non-Resident Business) or by using the BRO portal; the GST/HST account should be registered before taxable supplies are made and becomes mandatory when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters, with late-registration penalties available to the CRA. BC PST registration is a separate obligation performed through the BC Ministry of Finance eTaxBC portal, requiring a distinct BC PST vendor number once taxable sales in BC occur.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Canada imposes entity-level Part I income tax on the BC ULC and does not treat it as fiscally transparent domestically; however, the ULC's unlimited-liability share capital makes it an eligible entity for a U.S. federal check-the-box election, permitting the U.S. owner to treat it as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. tax purposes while it remains a taxable Canadian corporation. The resulting hybrid status is constrained by Canada's anti-hybrid mismatch rules (BEPS Action 2) and by Part XIII dividend/withholding taxation on distributions to non-resident shareholders.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (1) the U.S. check-the-box election under IRC § 7701 and Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3, filed by the U.S. shareholder, converting the ULC into a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. tax purposes only; (2) the GST/HST group registration election under ETA section 156, allowing related Canadian corporations to file as a single registrant and apply intra-group zero-rating; (3) the GST/HST Quick Method of Accounting election for small registrants; (4) the non-resident treaty withholding election through CRA Form NR301/RFD where a U.S. shareholder claims the 5%/15% Canada-U.S. Convention rate on ULC distributions; and (5) reliance on the Canada-U.S. Fifth Protocol fiscally transparent entity provisions (Article IV(7)) to flow treaty benefits through the ULC. Canada itself has no domestic check-the-box regime, and hybrid mismatch rules may deny or defer deductions where tax outcomes diverge.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold on worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies, but excluding supplies of financial services and certain capital real property) measured over four consecutive calendar quarters; registration is mandatory once exceeded and is optional below the threshold. BC PST: there is no comparable general small-supplier dollar threshold; any vendor making taxable sales in BC must register for PST unless a statutory exemption applies, including non-resident/remote vendors with economic nexus (e.g., taxable software, telecommunications or cloud services sold into BC).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 where the ULC sells tangible personal property and either (i) the ULC exports the goods directly by common carrier, courier or mail to a foreign address under a contract of sale, or (ii) the non-resident purchaser removes the goods from Canada within the CRA-prescribed period, generally within 60 days after title and risk pass, without consuming, using or supplying the goods in Canada. The exporter must hold credible proof of export (carrier waybills, bills of lading, CERS export reports, customs gate-out evidence) in its records before the 0% rate is applied; failure to obtain such evidence within the prescribed time results in reassessment at 5% GST. Related offshore services (installation, training, software updates and maintenance performed for non-resident buyers of laparoscopy towers) are generally zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 unless the services relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada or are supplied to an individual present in Canada. BC PST provides a corresponding export exemption for goods delivered outside BC by the vendor's carrier. If the surgical laparoscopy tower qualifies as a listed medical device under Schedule VI, Part II, separate zero-rating may apply to supplies to Canadian public hospitals, but export classification generally relies on Part V and HS heading 9018.90.

None — Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no mandatory API stamping and no government-issued digital stamp for GST/HST invoices. Invoices must nevertheless contain the prescribed information under ETA section 223 and the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations (supplier name and BN, recipient name or business identifier, date of invoice, description of supply, consideration and tax amount) to support input tax credits. Electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, XML or PEPPOL) are accepted if they are legible, complete, unalterable and retained in the taxpayer's books and records, generally for six years from the end of the relevant reporting period.

CRA GST/HST auditors rigorously verify export substantiation and may recharacterize zero-rated exports as taxable supplies at 5% GST when proof of export is missing or deficient, adding prescribed compound interest and penalties; false or grossly negligent statements attract civil penalties under the ETA and the Income Tax Act, typically equal to 50% of the tax evaded or credit improperly claimed. Criminal tax evasion prosecution under ITA section 239 carries fines of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years. CBSA administrative monetary penalties (AMPS) of up to CAD 25,000 per export misdeclaration, seizure of goods and loss of future zero-rating relief may also apply where export declarations are inaccurate. If exported goods are diverted back into Canada after zero-rating, the exporter is liable for the GST that would have been payable plus interest, and the CRA may set aside the normal four-year reassessment limitation where misrepresentation, neglect or fraud is established. Note that the 180-day FX repatriation benchmark in this dossier is an internal treasury control standard, not a Canadian statutory deadline; however, maintaining the repatriation reconciliation file substantially strengthens audit defence. Record-retention requirements extend generally to six years from the end of the relevant reporting period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account for OECD and non-OECD counterparties. There are no surrender, conversion, or repatriation restrictions under federal law, and the Currency Act confirms the Canadian dollar as legal tender. BC corporate law (Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c 57) does not impede repatriation of dividends or repayment of shareholder loans, subject to corporate solvency tests and tax compliance under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)). For a BC unlimited liability company (ULC), profit remittance is straightforward, but cross-border payments must be executed through a regulated bank or money services business and reported to FINTRAC for transactions above CAD 10,000. There is no capital control or stamp duty affecting ULC escrow accounts. The only practical hurdle is banking counterparty credit approval and enhanced due diligence for the manufacturing business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Export proceeds for manufactured endoscopy equipment must be retained in the entity's operational account in any permitted currency, though settlement in CAD, USD, EUR, or CNY is typical. Canada has no timeline for bringing export receipts onshore; however, reporting under the Customs Act (export declarations) and CBSA Form A/Export Reporting must be completed. If the BC ULC receives USD proceeds, it may hold USD in a USD-correspondent account or convert at commercial rates. There is no mandatory conversion ratio whatsoever.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML enforcement; the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for trade compliance. No central exchange-control authority exists; the Bank of Canada does not administer FX approvals. Transactions in Canadian dollars are freely routed through chartered banks under the Bank Act regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Low for the primary operating account of a physical-goods exporter, because OSFI-regulated banks are the safest clearing rails for trade LC issuance and commercial wire corridors. Non-regulated or lightly regulated payment fintechs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) may serve as auxiliary settlement wallets for low-value receivables, but the BC ULC manufacturing entity must maintain a domestic bank account for statutory director meetings, escrow, payroll, and tax payments. OSFI provides no deposit insurance for non-bank EMIs. Legal constraint: any entity that 'deals with Canadians' proceeds by way of money remittance must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC. For cross-border disbursements under USD 100,000, a licensed EMI chartered in the EEA or UK may be used if it complies with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and maintains transparent correspondent ties with a Canadian Schedule I bank. A 'remote only' EMI with no physical Canadian presence should not be used for the capital injection escrow account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial owner is a non-Canadian corporation or high-net-worth individual with opaque structure.

Root cause: Beneficial owner is a non-Canadian corporation or high-net-worth individual with opaque structure.

Defense evidence: Provide: Certificate of Incorporation of the ULC (BC), Register of Directors, Register of Members (or equivalent beneficial ownership registry), a corporate organization chart, passports of all directors and any shareholder holding more than 10%, a financial reference letter from the foreign parent bank, and audited/non-audited financial statements of the ultimate shareholder for the past two years. If the ULC is owned by a non-resident U.S. LLC or trust, include a W-8BEN-E/W-8BEN, a copy of the trust deed or LP agreement, and a legal opinion confirming no shell status.
High-value capital injection from a jurisdiction considered to have stricter tax transparency or higher AML risk.

Root cause: High-value capital injection from a jurisdiction considered to have stricter tax transparency or higher AML risk.

Defense evidence: Bank statements for the source of funds (last 6 months), contractual loan/share-subscription agreements, proof of corporate economic substance (operating lease, employment contracts, vendor invoices), source of wealth affidavit for individuals, and a Canadian legal opinion confirming that funds are derived from legitimate operational or investment activities. FINTRAC threshold reporting (CAD 10,000+ cash or wire) must be expected; include an ESR (Enhanced Due Diligence) questionnaire if the jurisdiction is a 'high-risk' third country.
Cross-border trade in medical devices with government (public hospital) clients.

Root cause: Cross-border trade in medical devices with government (public hospital) clients.

Defense evidence: Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL), ISO 13485 or equivalent quality certificate, a pro-forma or actual contract with the hospital, export licenses (for controlled surgical goods), and proof of no sanctions-list presence against the end-beneficiary. Banks may require a declaration of no Ontario/BC provincial lien and compliance with Canadian organ donation / medical device import rules.
Use of a personal or third-party processor as the originator of wire funds.

Root cause: Use of a personal or third-party processor as the originator of wire funds.

Defense evidence: Provide a direct ownership chain, loan agreement, or invoice that links the processor to the ULC; include bank confirmations of the processor's authorization, and a written undertaking against money laundering. Most Schedule I banks will refuse personal processors outright; thus a same-name corporate remittance from the parent account is the only acceptable route.

Inbound capital injection is executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202/MT202COV for cover) from the foreign parent to the ULC's 'Capital Escrow Account' at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Purpose code should be 'Capital injection – equity subscription under BC Business Corporations Act Section 54 (sharess) or 'Shareholder loan' when the ULC issues promissory notes. Use UBO/UFI end-to-end reference field 70 with the corporate identifier (CA_ULC) and a short narrative 'Share capital contribution - ULC'. For Canada, wire must not exceed CAD 100,000 without FINTRAC incidental report? (Actually FINTRAC report is mandatory at CAD 10,000; file LCTR for cash over 10k or large virtual currency transactions). Provide supporting board resolution, subscription agreement, and a CIBC/BMO/RBC compliance officer communication. The bank must treat the escrow as a 'special purpose account' with anti-money-laundering verification of the originator (KYC) before funds are credited. No exchange control permission or prior certificate is required, but the bank may request a 'Form 1042' from the CRA to confirm withholding obligations on interest, if a shareholder loan is used.Clearing rails for outbound payments from the BC ULC manufacturing entity include: (1) SWIFT MT103 for operational trade settlements, using terms like CAD/USD, purpose codes 20000 / 20500 for manufactured goods; (2) Commercial letters of credit (LC) issued by Canadian banks (e.g., RBC LC in USD/CNH) for the export of surgical laparoscopy towers, with ICC UCP600 terms and Incoterms 2020; (3) CAD-dollar direct clearing via Lynx (previously Large Value Transfer System) for domestic BC suppliers; (4) high-volume USD clearing through the U.S. federal wire to the Correspondent bank (e.g., BMO Harris, RBC Bank). For disbursement to a Chinese vendor or an offshore assembly facility, a telex transfer (TT) wire with MT103 cover via a CNY correspondent in Bank of China (Toronto Branch) may be used, provided that the Federal EMIR and Canadian regulations allow. Use purpose code T200 for exports of medical devices and T150 for royalties. Confirm no OFAC / CBSA sanctions violations in the beneficiary path.Outright FX forward (foreign exchange forward) with a Canadian Schedule I bank, non-deliverable forward (NDF) for CAD/CNY if deliverable CNY is not allowed, and cross-currency swap (CAD/USD) for multi-year capital commitments. For BC corporations, forward contracts must meet the 'hedging exception' under the CRA's Commodity Futures rules; no speculative trading is permitted without registration under the Derivatives Act (BC) or IIROC.Currency options (vanilla call/put) are permissible but require a bank trading line and are not appropriate for treasury conservatism.For commodity-linked raw materials (e.g., stainless steel), a commodity swap or futures hedge is allowed if processed through a regulated dealer.Non-deliverable forwards for emerging market currencies (e.g., INR, BRL) are permissible for trade settlement, but the bank must be an eligible swap counterparty under the Credit Support Annex.Interest rate swap (inverse) may be used to convert floating-rate capital injection debt to fixed; permitted under the federal Interest Act and BC law.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating is Low for the primary operating account of a physical-goods exporter, because OSFI-regulated banks are the safest clearing rails for trade LC issuance and commercial wire corridors. Non-regulated or lightly regulated payment fintechs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) may serve as auxiliary settlement wallets for low-value receivables, but the BC ULC manufacturing entity must maintain a domestic bank account for statutory director meetings, escrow, payroll, and tax payments. OSFI provides no deposit insurance for non-bank EMIs. Legal constraint: any entity that 'deals with Canadians' proceeds by way of money remittance must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC. For cross-border disbursements under USD 100,000, a licensed EMI chartered in the EEA or UK may be used if it complies with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and maintains transparent correspondent ties with a Canadian Schedule I bank. A 'remote only' EMI with no physical Canadian presence should not be used for the capital injection escrow account.
