Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal income tax, GST/HST, and payroll administration; CRA 'My Business Account' portal (canada.ca). British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) via eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

For a BC-incorporated ULC: (1) obtain BC incorporation documents under the Business Corporations Act; (2) register with CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with applicable program accounts, including GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM); (3) register for BC PST through eTaxBC once taxable sales in BC commence; (4) file certificate of incorporation, articles, registered office address, and director/officer particulars; (5) confirm whether the TikTok Shop marketplace is the deemed collector, which may adjust direct registration duties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the ULC is a corporation resident in Canada under Income Tax Act Part I and can be subject to full CRA assessment); Pass_Through_Eligible applies only for US federal income tax purposes via the check-the-box election, with no domestic Canadian pass-through status.

Available election forms

US check-the-box election (Form 8832) to treat the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for US tax while retaining Canadian corporate tax status; GST/HST election under ETA s. 156 (form GST 341) between closely related corporations to deem taxable supplies made for nil consideration; voluntary registration election for non-resident persons under ETA ss. 240/241; joint elections available under the hybrid-mismatch rules of ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 to neutralize deduction/non-inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of total taxable supplies (worldwide) in any four consecutive calendar quarters, after which registration is mandatory; BC PST: no general monetary small-supplier exemption, registration is required once taxable sales are made in BC, and marketplace facilitators (e.g., TikTok Shop) must register once BC-sourced sales exceed CAD 10,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V where the supplier exports the goods under commercial export conditions and the goods are exported as soon as practicable; services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VII where performed wholly outside Canada or directly in respect of exported goods; domestic TikTok Shop retail deliveries to Canadian consumers remain taxable at the combined 12% BC rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA e-Export / Form B13A Export Report)
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier export documentation evidencing final foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Packing list, transport records, and export evidence required under ETA s. 169(1)(a)

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime applies in Canada; e-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Digital Interchange (Peppol framework); GST/HST invoices and BC PST receipts must contain prescribed particulars, including supplier legal name, Business Number, date, supply description, and separately stated GST at 5% and PST at 7%.

CRA may reassess beyond the standard four-year period for misrepresentation or fraud, recapture refunds and input tax credits claimed on export zero-rating, reclassify the supply as taxable at 12% with retroactive collection, impose the 25% penalty under ETA s. 285(1) for false statements or omissions, add interest and gross-negligence penalties, assert director liability, and pursue criminal prosecution for wilful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the Canadian dollar for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no exchange control legislation, no requirement for central bank licensing or approval for foreign exchange operations, and no limitation on profit remittances, dividends, or loan repayments by a British Columbia ULC to non-resident shareholders. The only operative constraints are tax-related: Part XIII withholding tax on dividends and interest paid to non-residents, transfer-pricing rules under section 247 of the Income Tax Act, and GST/HST compliance under the Excise Tax Act. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC by financial institutions, which is a reporting obligation, not a convertibility restriction. The CAD is a freely floating G10 currency settling on CLS for spot, forward, and swap transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export receipts. The BC ULC may hold TikTok Shop payout proceeds in USD or other foreign currencies in foreign-currency bank accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks, may convert at any time at arm's length market rates, or may apply such proceeds directly to offshore trade disbursements. The applicable obligations are limited to FINTRAC EFT reporting thresholds, recognition of income in CAD for CRA purposes at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate, and section 121 GST/HST characterization of imported and exported goods.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting alongside the Department of Finance and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). The Bank of Canada administers the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime for non-bank payment service providers, while foreign exchange oversight and monetary policy are vested in the Bank of Canada under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a supplementary settlement and vendor-disbursement rail, but NOT a complete substitute for a Schedule I bank account. Wise (Wise Payments Limited), Airwallex, and Payoneer may lawfully be used for cross-border collection and payout routing subject to four legal constraints: (i) they must be registered with FINTRAC as money services businesses where they conduct foreign exchange dealing or remittance activity in Canada; (ii) they must be registered as payment service providers with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), the enforcement phase of which commenced in 2024; (iii) foreign EMI licenses (e.g., FCA UK or MAS Singapore) do not extend to Canada without a separate RPAA registration; and (iv) the BC ULC must maintain a conventional Canadian bank account to satisfy statutory audit-trail expectations, CRA banking-reporting norms, and GST/HST nexus.
