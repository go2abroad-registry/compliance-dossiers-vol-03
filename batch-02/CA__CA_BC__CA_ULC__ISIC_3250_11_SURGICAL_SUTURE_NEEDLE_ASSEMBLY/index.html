Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident owned Canadian ULC carrying on business in British Columbia must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and applicable program accounts directly from the Canada Revenue Agency. Step 1: incorporate or register the ULC under British Columbia's Business Corporations Act and obtain BC incorporation documents, as well as any provincial registration number under the BC Corporate Online registry. Step 2: use CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1, Request for a Business Number, to obtain the 15-digit BN; register for the GST/HST account if the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and surpasses the small supplier threshold, and register for the BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) account through the BC eTaxBC portal. Step 3: if the ULC will have Canadian-resident employees, register a CRA payroll account. Step 4: file all applicable T2 corporate income tax returns and BC provincial returns using the BN. The ULC is a deemed resident corporation for Canadian Income Tax Act purposes when incorporated in Canada after April 26, 1965, and is required to file annual T2 returns unless a specific exemption applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (U.S. check-the-box classification; domestic Canadian default remains Entity_Level_Taxation because a Canadian ULC is a taxable corporation under the Income Tax Act and cannot be treated as a partnership under Canadian federal tax law)

Available election forms

Canada does not provide an equivalent to the U.S. check-the-box election. For U.S. federal tax purposes, a wholly owned Canadian ULC may be treated as a disregarded entity or corporation under Treasury Regulation Section 301.7701-3 only through U.S. Form 8832 or default classification rules. Under Canadian domestic law, the unlimited liability corporation remains an entity-level taxpayer. Available Canadian statutory elections relevant to tax restructuring include elections under Income Tax Act sections 85, 86 and 87 for qualifying corporate reorganizations and rollovers, the GST/HST closely related corporation election under Excise Tax Act section 156, and certain joint elections for nil consideration among closely related corporations. Canada has enacted hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.2 and 18.21 of the Income Tax Act, but these operate as disallowance rules and do not provide elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies during any 12-month period (small supplier threshold); PST: no general monetary threshold, registration is required before carrying on PST-taxable business activity in British Columbia

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian ULC may claim the 0% GST/HST rate under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act for supplies of goods exported from Canada, provided the goods are delivered by the supplier or a common carrier to a point outside Canada for use or consumption outside Canada and the supplier maintains evidence that the goods were actually exported in commercial quantities. Services that are performed on tangible personal property exported from Canada and any supplies of services to non-residents in respect of goods exported may also be zero-rated under prescribed circumstances. The supply of surgical suture needles and assembled surgical consumables destined for foreign customers qualifies for zero-rating only when title passes outside Canada and documentary proof of export is retained. Purely economic or booking evidence is insufficient without physical export evidence.

  • Canada Border Services Agency Export Declaration (B13A or electronic Canadian Export Reporting System equivalent)
  • Commercial invoice describing the surgical suture needle assembly, HS/ISIC classification, quantity, and value
  • Bill of lading or equivalent international transport document evidencing departure from Canada
  • Packing list
  • Foreign customs entry or destination-country import documentation evidencing receipt by the non-resident purchaser
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank transfer receipt, or foreign exchange confirmation evidencing settlement of the export sale
  • Contract or purchase order establishing the export sales relationship and payment terms

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for GST/HST. GST/HST invoices must contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act, including supplier and recipient identification, a description of the supply, consideration, and tax charged. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0 is voluntary. Export declarations must be transmitted electronically to the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System or an approved service provider. BC PST returns and registrations are processed through the eTaxBC portal, and CRA accepts electronic books and records for audit purposes.

The CRA and CBSA may audit export zero-rating claims through detailed transactional matching of export declarations, shipping records, bill of lading dates, customs entry evidence, and incoming foreign currency remittances. Where zero-rating was claimed without valid export evidence, the CRA reassesses the supply at 5% GST plus applicable BC PST and charges statutory interest from the original filing date. Taxpayers claiming false zero-rated export treatment are subject to gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the tax understated under the Excise Tax Act, fines up to CAD 25,000 for knowingly making false or misleading statements, imprisonment for tax evasion, and director liability for corporate penalties. A fraudulent export scheme may also trigger separate CBSA administrative monetary penalties under the Customs Act and referral to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital and current account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely against global currencies, and there are no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirements for profit remittances, and no limits on dividend distributions from a British Columbia ULC to a foreign parent or shareholder, provided the entity is solvent, articles/managing director resolutions permit, and all applicable tax withholding (Part XIII under the Income Tax Act) and thin capitalization rules are satisfied. No sizable remittance licensing hurdle exists other than standard tax clearance and financial institution compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation obligation for export receipts. A BC ULC may retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts at domestic banks as part of its treasury operations. For statistical reporting, cross-border transactions above CAD 10,000 must be reported to the Bank of Canada via the Statistics Act, but there is no surrender or forced conversion. Medical device exporters should maintain transaction-level documentation for Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and CBSA export declarations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); no separate foreign exchange administration in Canada, though the Canada Border Services Agency, FINTRAC, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) collectively oversee cross-border financial transactions, AML/ATF compliance, and prudential foreign currency operations. The Foreign Exchange and Trade Statistics Reporting System is managed by Statistics Canada under the Banking Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium with restricted scope. A foreign fintech or electronic money institution (EMI) cannot carry on retail banking or payment services in Canada without registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and, where applicable, as a Canadian deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act. For a BC ULC surgical suture manufacturer, an offshore EMI may be used for collection, but it is not permitted to serve as a primary corporate treasury vault for capital escrow or statutory licensing funds. Domestic Schedule I banks remain the recommended and fully compliant clearing backbone.
