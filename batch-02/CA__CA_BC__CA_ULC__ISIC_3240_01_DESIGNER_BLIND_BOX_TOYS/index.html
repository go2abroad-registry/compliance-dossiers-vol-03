Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under IMF Article VIII, with no FX-approval requirement for ordinary trade dividends, royalties, or capital repatriation. Legal hurdles are limited to: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced), tax clearance for deemed Canadian real-estate interests, CRA information reporting for reportable transactions, and Investment Canada Act review for non-residents acquiring control of Canadian businesses. Profit remittance is unrestricted upon board declaration, provided tax reserves are funded.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline. Exporters may retain foreign currency in USD or EUR accounts as long as books and records are maintained in Canadian dollars under ASPE/IFRS; no forced conversion is imposed. Cross-border payment and receipt records must be preserved for CRA and FINTRAC audit, and Export/Import Reporting thresholds apply to physical designer-toy shipments.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) as the monetary authority; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the financial intelligence unit; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the federal bank regulator; Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for withholding tax and remittance compliance. There is no dedicated FX-control administrator because Canada operates a free-floating currency regime with no general exchange-control legislation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low for an offshore digital EMI as a primary treasury rail. Legal constraints: any EMI offering Canadian-dollar electronic payments or e-transfers must hold Money Services Business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC, appoint a compliant AML/ATF officer, and maintain an agent relationship with a licensed Schedule I bank. Privacy law (PIPEDA) restricts personal account data export to non-Canadian processors, and offshore EMIs cannot serve as the main operating account for Canadian GST/HST, payroll, or CRA remittances. Shopify Payments and Stripe remain the primary merchant facade, with settlement into a Canadian Schedule I operating account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal (15%) and British Columbia provincial (12%) general corporate income tax rate; reduced rates apply under the Canadian-controlled private corporation small business deduction, subject to ownership and activity criteria

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation (including a BC ULC) to a non-resident shareholder, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty; Canadian withholding tax applies to deemed dividends on liquidation or wind-up
