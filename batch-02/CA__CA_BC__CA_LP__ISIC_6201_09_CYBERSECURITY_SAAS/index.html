Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the BC Limited Partnership Act for a Limited Partnership; partners may subscribe for units of nominal amount (e.g., CAD 1.00 per unit) as set in the partnership agreement. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca; BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

1) Confirm GST/HST small-supplier status: the BC LP must register once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters. 2) Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 with the BC Limited Partnership Declaration, Partnership Agreement, and partner register; the partnership (not the partners) holds the BN for GST/HST. 3) Open the GST/HST account under the BN; a non-resident general partner may file Form RC59 to appoint a resident representative for CRA correspondence. 4) Register for BC PST through eTaxBC using the same BN; the PST account is separate and is required before the first taxable BC supply unless the supply is an export. 5) For Stripe and wire payment rails, ensure invoice templates disclose the BN and PST registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — BC LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s.96; income is allocated annually to resident and non-resident partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. For GST/HST, the LP itself is a separate 'person' (ETA s.123) and registers in its own right, notwithstanding partner-level pass-through.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. Relevant statutory mechanisms applicable to a BC LP: (i) ETA s.177(2) partnership/member GST/HST election to deem specified supplies made for nil consideration; (ii) ETA s.156 election for closely related corporations where a corporate partner is involved; (iii) ETA s.74/77 quick-method and annual-filing elections; (iv) Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.6–18.65) apply mandatorily to cross-border payments through hybrid entities and cannot be displaced by elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); non-resident suppliers count Canadian-source supplies and digital services supplied to Canadian residents. BC PST: no general exemption threshold; PST registration is mandatory once the LP makes a first taxable supply of SaaS/digitally delivered software in BC unless the supply is exported to a non-resident (combined BC in-province rate = 7% PST + 5% GST = 12%).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cybersecurity SaaS and penetration-testing services (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541512) qualify for 0% GST zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI Part V s.10.1 (supply of intangible personal property to a non-resident not for use in Canada) and s.7 (services supplied to a non-resident). Conditions: (i) the recipient is a non-resident of Canada; (ii) the SaaS/pen-testing service is not for consumption or use in Canada; (iii) contemporaneous documentary evidence of the offshore supply is retained. A fully zero-rated exporter may claim full Input Tax Credits (ITCs) on Canadian inputs, including cloud hosting, wages, and subcontractor costs, through its GST/HST net-filing return.

  • Signed Master Services Agreement / Statement of Work / Order Form naming the non-resident customer and governing jurisdiction
  • Zero-rated export invoice (no GST/PST charged) with an explicit zero-rating reference to ETA Sch. VI Part V
  • Payment evidence: SWIFT, wire transfer confirmation, or Stripe payout report matched to the non-resident remitter
  • Proof of non-resident status: certificate of incorporation or tax residency certificate (e.g., W-8BEN-E) of the customer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping in Canada. Federal e-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network (OpenInvoice). The LP must retain full disaggregated invoices for Stripe and wire transactions; CRA requires a complete audit trail but not prior fiscal authorization of individual invoices, and there is no CRA-mandated invoice clearing API.

CRA general reassessment window is 4 years (ETA s.298(1)); it is extended to 10 years or unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, willful default, or fraud exists (ETA s.298(5)). Clawback mechanics: CRA may recharacterize the export as a taxable Canadian supply, retroactively assessing 5% GST plus 7% BC PST with compound daily interest. Penalties include ETA s.285 false-statement and gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of the avoided net tax, ETA s.280 failure-to-file penalties of up to 4% of unremitted net tax, prosecution under ETA s.327 for wilful evasion, and BC PST false-statement penalties under the Provincial Sales Tax Act. Zero-rating claims lacking the substantiation bundle are presumed taxable upon audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has unrestricted capital and current account convertibility, having accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the Currency Act contains no exchange-control provisions and no approvals, licenses, or registration gates are imposed on inward foreign investment into a British Columbia Limited Partnership, whether as equity capital contributions, partner loans, or intercompany advances. Profit distributions and capital returns may be remitted in any freely convertible currency without central-bank authorization. The operative constraints are tax-driven rather than FX-driven: a non-resident partner must file a Canadian Part I income tax return and pay tax on its share of Canadian-source partnership income before remitting; payments of interest, rents, royalties, or management fees to non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding at 25%, reducible under applicable tax treaties (e.g., to 15% for Canadian dividends under the Canada-US treaty, and potentially 0% for arm's-length interest under treaty provisions or Section 212(1)(b)). Canada's large-value settlement system (Lynx) operates in Canadian dollars; foreign-currency clearing is effected through correspondent banks in the United States, the United Kingdom, and other clearing jurisdictions, with no conversion of export receipts or capital inflows required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation applies to export proceeds in Canada. The BC Limited Partnership may retain all SaaS subscription and licensing revenues in Canadian or foreign currencies, indefinitely, whether in a domestic account or an offshore bank. The sole reporting obligation is on Canadian residents: each Canadian-resident partner (and the LP itself, if it holds foreign property in the partnership until allocated) must file Form T1135 (Foreign Income Verification Statement) where aggregate specified foreign property — including foreign bank deposits, foreign receivables, and offshore account balances attributable to the partner — exceeds CAD 100,000 at any time in the tax year. Non-resident partners have no Canadian obligation to report foreign accounts unless they also file a Part I return. All international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more executed by a Canadian financial entity must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the sole monetary and currency authority; Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration. Deposit-taking institutions are prudentially supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision falls to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); the national payment and clearing infrastructure is operated by Payments Canada under the Canadian Payments Act and the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar) are highly viable as auxiliary multi-currency collection and disbursement rails, particularly for fast US/EU/APAC settlement of SaaS subscription invoices and contractor payments. They are not viable as the sole or primary treasury account because they are not Canadian chartered banks: (i) balances are not covered by CDIC deposit insurance except in narrow pass-through arrangements with Canadian clearing partners; (ii) the EMI itself must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC unless it benefits from a statutory exclusion, and foreign EMIs lacking Canadian registration cannot lawfully onboard a Canadian-domiciled LP; (iii) funds held at the EMI are typically safeguarded in pooled omnibus accounts at partner banks, creating counterparty and priority risk on the EMI's insolvency; (iv) commercial credit, treasury sweeps, and interbank dealing lines are absent. A prudent structure is a Big Six operational account for core cash management plus one FINTRAC-registered EMI for low-balance cross-border payables and receivables, with daily sweeps back to the primary domestic account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Structure: inbound funds are accepted as either equity capital contributions or shareholder/partner loans; no exchange-control or central-bank authorization is required, and the Bank of Canada does not prescribe a national purpose-code classification. Wire protocol: SWIFT MT103 from the funding partner's bank to the LP's Canadian Bank, quoting the LP's full legal name and account number, the receiving bank's SWIFT/BIC and Canadian institution number plus transit, and a beneficiary reference line containing the phrase 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'PARTNER LOAN' plus the LP designation (e.g., 'CA_LP_CAP_2025_###'). Although Canada operates without a centralized purpose-code regime, the wire reference should map to an internal ISO 20022 justification category (e.g., INVE for equity investment, INTR for intercompany loan, or TRAN for general transfer) so the receiving bank's OFAC, FINTRAC, and correspondent-bank screening can clear the payment. Verification and documentation at booking: executed BC Limited Partnership Agreement; partner subscription schedule or capital account ledger entries signed by the general partner; board resolution of the general partner authorizing the contribution; source-of-funds file for the contributing partner; any promissory note or loan agreement for debt injections, with arm's-length interest terms, repayment schedule, and subordination provisions to avoid equity recharacterization; and, for contributions exceeding CAD 100,000 attributable to specified foreign property, a T1135 filing among the Canadian-resident partners. Funds received in foreign currency are credited as CAD at the bank's spot rate unless a segregated foreign-currency account is used; the LP should instruct the bank to maintain the contribution currency to avoid unnecessary conversion spread.Clearing rails operate as follows: domestic CAD disbursements (payroll, contractor payment, cloud hosting, software licences, marketing) settle through Payments Canada's Lynx system for large-value high-priority wires and through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batched electronic credits; cross-border supplier and reseller payments in USD, EUR, GBP, and other currencies are executed via SWIFT MT103, with USD clearing performed over the bank's US correspondent network (generally Chase, Citi, or BofA for Canadian banks); card-settlement receivables from Stripe and other PSPs enter the operating account as CAD/USD batch settlements cleared through Visa/Mastercard networks and the PSP's acquiring bank. Commercial letters of credit are permissible and recommended for hardware-resale or enterprise-distribution arrangements where overseas resellers require title/availability guarantees, open via the trade-finance desks of BMO or Scotiabank as irrevocable, confirmed LCs at sight; documentary collections and accounts-receivable financing can be layered over the same desk. For SaaS recurring revenue, SWIFT MT103 and local ACH equivalents (SEPA in EUR, CHIPS/Fedwire in USD) constitute the standard rails. The treasury function should implement: dual-approval HSM or token-based payment authorization, positive-pay anti-fraud controls on cheque and ACH, monthly re-forecasting of FX working capital requirements, and value-date optimization by scheduling wire cut-offs before the bank's 14:00–15:00 Eastern time deadlines for same-day settlement.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards in CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/GBP for maturities up to 12 months, executed under the bank's master FX terms, with no exchange-control restriction or regulatory product ban.FX swaps (simultaneous spot sale and forward repurchase, or two-date forward exchanges) used to bridge temporary currency mismatches between capital inflows and disbursement cycles.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) to synthetically convert floating-rate CAD or USD debt into fixed-rate funding in the target disbursement currency, transacted under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a CLE credit support annex.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) as permitted hedge tools when the LP's SaaS revenue mix exposes it to illiquid or non-convertible emerging-market currencies (e.g., BRL, INR, NGN, EGP); settlement is made in CAD as the reference in the deliverable leg.Vanilla European FX options (puts and calls) purchased for asymmetric hedging of a large contract pipeline or acquisition contingent consideration; a written hedge policy is required to evidence commercial purpose.Exchange-traded micro FX futures and options on CME for cost-effective transparent hedging of USD/CAD exposures, requiring a derivatives account classification and IIROC-approved investment dealer onboarding.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar) are highly viable as auxiliary multi-currency collection and disbursement rails, particularly for fast US/EU/APAC settlement of SaaS subscription invoices and contractor payments. They are not viable as the sole or primary treasury account because they are not Canadian chartered banks: (i) balances are not covered by CDIC deposit insurance except in narrow pass-through arrangements with Canadian clearing partners; (ii) the EMI itself must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC unless it benefits from a statutory exclusion, and foreign EMIs lacking Canadian registration cannot lawfully onboard a Canadian-domiciled LP; (iii) funds held at the EMI are typically safeguarded in pooled omnibus accounts at partner banks, creating counterparty and priority risk on the EMI's insolvency; (iv) commercial credit, treasury sweeps, and interbank dealing lines are absent. A prudent structure is a Big Six operational account for core cash management plus one FINTRAC-registered EMI for low-balance cross-border payables and receivables, with daily sweeps back to the primary domestic account.

The T2 return is due within six months after the end of the fiscal period (e.g., a December 31 year-end requires filing by June 30 of the following calendar year). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Income Tax Act and CRA Transfer Pricing Memorandum TPM-09, a complete contemporaneous Transfer Pricing (TP) documentation set (Master File and Local File) is required for transactions with non-arm's length non-residents where either the aggregate fair market value of all such transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 (for goods and services) or the aggregate face value of all loans and advances among connected parties exceeds CAD 1,000,000; for a Canadian-controlled private corporation, further documentation requirements are triggered where revenues exceed CAD 50 million or assets exceed CAD 20 million; the CRA will require a full Master File where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 1 billion. For a BC LP cybersecurity SaaS business, intercompany charges for shared software development, cloud services, and management fees are caught by these thresholds even if the LP itself has no active intercompany transactions; the LP must prepare a Transfer Pricing Policy Document, a Local File, and, if it is part of a multinational group, a Master File and country-by-country reporting (CbCR) if the group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 1 billion (equivalent to EUR 750 million). This is applicable to the BC LP; if the partnership has no controlled transactions, the documentation requirement is still triggered by any transactions with non-resident partners or related corporations exceeding CAD 1,000,000.
