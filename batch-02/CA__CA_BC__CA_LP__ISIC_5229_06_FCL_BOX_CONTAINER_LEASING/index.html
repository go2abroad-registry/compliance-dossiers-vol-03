Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; the LP is fiscally transparent for federal income tax and must file Form T5013 information return, with income allocated to partners. Available elections include a section 216 election for non-resident partners to file on Canadian-source business income, GST/HST Quick Method and annual filing elections for qualifying registrants, and the hybrid-mismatch restriction provisions in the Income Tax Act apply to cross-border deductible payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: GST/HST small-supplier threshold (taxable supplies in Canada in any calendar quarter exceeding CAD 30,000, or aggregate small-person status exhausted); BC PST registration is required upon commencement of a taxable lease in BC with no small-supplier exemption for non-exempt leasing activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may apply 0% GST to a taxable lease of intermodal ISO shipping containers if the supply constitutes an export supply under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act: the recipient must be a non-resident, the container must be exported from Canada as part of the supply, and the lessor must retain proof of export. International freight services, including demurrage, handling and container repositioning ancillary to international carriage, are zero-rated as export services. BC PST does not apply where the container is sold or leased for delivery/use outside British Columbia and valid export documentation is preserved.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or CARM/EDI export report)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/multimodal through bill) evidencing export of the container
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer record evidencing repatriation of lease payments
  • Commercial invoice and intermodal lease agreement/container interchange receipt
  • Container number and cargo movement manifest or forwarder's proof of export

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping under Canadian law; CRA accepts electronic GST/HST invoices and mandates electronic filing via CRA-approved transmission/API for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million. BC PST returns and remittances are filed electronically via eTaxBC; e-invoicing (PEPPOL) is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework.

CRA may reassess zero-rated supplies for up to 10 years in cases of misrepresentation, with daily compound interest on the deficient GST/HST. A supplier that fails to substantiate export may be charged the full 5% GST, assessed a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax, and be subject to penalties under the Excise Tax Act and, in fraud cases, criminal fines and imprisonment. BC PST may be reassessed for four years (no limitation in fraud cases), with interest and up to 25% civil penalties for uncollected/undermitted PST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating, fully convertible currency under IMF Article VIII obligations. Both current account and capital account transactions are unrestricted for residents and non-residents. Profit remittances, dividends, and partnership distributions to foreign LP partners require no regulatory approval or foreign exchange license. Canadian withholding tax under Income Tax Act Part XIII, and any applicable tax treaty relief, is the sole tax compliance gate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, settlement timeline, or declaration requirement on export receipts. Container leasing fees and intermodal equipment rental income from foreign lessees may be retained in the original currency, converted at commercial bank discretion, or remitted abroad without Bank of Canada approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; exchange control and cross-border trade/investment administration is functionally distributed among Finance Canada, Global Affairs Canada, and the Canada Border Services Agency. Canada has no dedicated exchange control ministry or foreign exchange administration bureau.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail. Canadian law does not recognize 'Electronic Money Institution' or 'EMI' as a licensed banking category. Non-bank payment service providers (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Stripe, Airwallex) are eligible for registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and may support ancillary collection/last-mile payout, but they cannot legally open corporate bank accounts, hold clearing settlement accounts, or replace a Tier-1 chartered bank for treasury operations. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the BC limited partnership entity level because the LP is fiscally transparent; each partner is taxable. If the same business were carried on by a taxable Canadian corporation, the combined federal (15%) and British Columbia (12%) general corporate rate would be 27%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax applies to taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. A BC limited partnership does not pay dividends; it allocates partnership income. Distributions of business profits to non-resident limited partners are not legally 'dividends' but may be taxed in Canada under Part I or Part XIV depending on the partner's Canadian-source permanent establishment.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 Partnership Information Return must be filed within five months after the end of the partnership fiscal period. For a BC LP with a December 31 fiscal end, the filing deadline is May 31 of the following calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 (Canadian dollars) aggregate fair market value of reportable transactions between the BC LP and non-arm's-length non-residents. Above this threshold, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, including Local File and Master File equivalent schedules, must be prepared and filed upon CRA request within a reasonable time.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations in the style of certain offshore centres, but the Income Tax Act, GAAR (general anti-avoidance rule), and treaty limitation-on-benefits provisions require genuine economic substance. A BC LP engaged in cross-border ISO container leasing must demonstrate real management presence, business functions, container assets physically placed in Canada or leased in Canadian commerce, and a valid commercial purpose. A shell LP with no employees, no books and records, and no Canadian operational activity may be disregarded, and treaty withholding reductions can be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus daily interest, and imprisonment for up to two years. Culpable individuals are also barred from certain directorships and may face national prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a corporate general partner who directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the evasion are personally liable for withholding and source-deduction recoveries; the Crown may freeze bank accounts, register liens, issue travel bans, and enforce civil restitution by piercing the corporate veil.
Shell Company and Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the partnership as a sham, recharacterize transactions under GAAR, reassess the income in the hands of the true beneficiary, impose 25% Part XIII withholding tax plus penalties, and charge interest from the date of the original non-payment.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the limited liability of the limited partners where they participate in management or where the partnership is a facade. Directors can be held personally liable for taxes and civil damages, subjected to asset seizure, and convicted of fraud leading to travel bans and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA will make upward transfer pricing adjustments, deny deductions on excessive amounts, and impose a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment amount where the adjustment exceeds C$5,000,000 or the gross misstatement threshold is met; interest and provincial penalties also apply.

Director personal liability: Senior directors or executives who sign false certificates or knowingly cause inaccurate records may be jointly and severally liable for penalties under section 247(3); CRA can pursue civil recovery against personal assets, and egregious conduct supports criminal charges with prison terms.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for corporations and/or C$100,000 plus imprisonment for individuals under FINTRAC enforcement; tax treaties also allow denial of benefits where beneficial ownership is hidden. The partnership may be dissolved by the BC Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who knowingly participate in money laundering or beneficial-ownership concealment face personal criminal liability, fines, civil forfeiture of assets, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to five years.
Late or False Filing and Unauthorized Non-Resident Loss Allocation

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties under section 162(7.1), false-statement penalties under section 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax, plus full interest. Where the arrangement lacks substance, transactions will be recharacterized under GAAR and the relevant tax benefits withdrawn.

Director personal liability: The general partner, its directors, and any controlling limited partner who knowingly helped make false statements will be assessed personally for taxes, penalties, and restitution; the CRA may freeze personal bank accounts and commence criminal prosecution, leading to travel restrictions and imprisonment.
