Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and corporate income tax administration via 'My Business Account' portal (canada.ca/cra-business); BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administered by BC Ministry of Finance — Consumer Taxation Branch (gov.bc.ca/pst).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity forming a BC limited partnership must first register the LP with BC Registry (Corporate Online), then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 or CRA online registration. The partnership registers for GST/HST (RT account) if taxable supplies exceed $30,000 CAD or if voluntary registration is elected; each partner (general and limited) must obtain/have a separate BN for income tax purposes. Non-resident partners must appoint a representative for service of notices (Form RC372) and may need an individual BN for remittance of Part XIII withholding taxes. For PST, the LP must register as a 'PST collector' with BC Consumer Taxation Branch before making taxable sales or providing taxable software services in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and provincial income tax; the LP does not pay tax, rather each partner includes their allocable share of income/loss in computing taxable income. For GST/HST purposes the partnership itself is a separate registrant (each LP registers one GST/HST account for partnership activities). For substantively Canadian-source software services and licensing with non-resident partners, 'is Canadian partnership' rules and Part I.3 tax on partnership status may require careful treaty tie-breaker review.

Available election forms

Check-the-box not available in Canada. For GST/HST, the LP may elect to treat its members as co-owners (s.273 joint election is only for closely related corporations, not LP partners). For BC PST, no partnership-election exists beyond normal collector registration. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s.18.4/18.6 and BEPS Action 2 may deny deductions on certain excessive payments flow-through of partnerships; partners may make a joint election under S.96(2) to compute income in different manner from accounting, and S.110.1 capital contribution elections are available. Where the LP has only non-resident partners and derives no Canadian-source active business income, it may be deemed not a partnership under S.96(11) unless elections are made; a 'Canadian partnership' designation is possible via Form T5013.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: $30,000 CAD in worldwide taxable supplies over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). BC PST: No general threshold — once the LP makes taxable sales of software or taxable services in BC, PST registration and collection is mandatory regardless of revenue level, with voluntary registration permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP can zero-rate exported payroll SaaS services under ETA Sch. VI, Part V, s.7 (services supplied to a non-resident) if the service is not received in Canada by a resident consumer and is not an advisory service in respect of domestic real estate, or under Sch. VI, Part V, s.23 (services performed wholly outside Canada). For B2B cross-border software licensing, zero-rating applies when the supply is made to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST, unless the service is rendered to a Canadian resident or to a non-resident who constitutes a 'person present in Canada'. Digital services must meet the 'in respect of real property in Canada' carve-outs; software hosting and payroll processing outsourced to Canadian data centres may reduce zero-rating eligibility, in which case a place-of-supply rule (based on customer's address, as prescribed in ETA s.143.2 and SOR/2010-117) will govern.

  • Commercial invoice stating 0% GST/HST and citing ETA Sch. VI section number
  • Bill of Lading or carrier record (only if any hardware/media exported; not required for pure digital delivery)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 funding evidence showing foreign currency received from offshore payer, or bank statement entry showing non-resident source remittance, retained per CRA six-year records requirement
  • IP/service delivery log showing server access from foreign jurisdiction and customer due-diligence record (KYC) confirming non-resident status
  • Completed RC4606-1E or Form GST66 if the non-resident customer provides reciprocal non-resident status (not mandatory but recommended for audit evidence)

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing stamping regime. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing specified content (registrant BN, transaction date, tax status and rate) and retain electronic records. Payments Canada API-based rail exists for payroll direct debit/credit transfers, and the CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns and T5013 partnership returns for most LPs. No government-side digital stamp or pre-clearance is mandated; however, ISO 20022 XML messaging is required for SWIFT wire, and CRA accepts EDI/e-invoice file formats for B2B invoices if agreed between parties.

CRA audit teams apply high scrutiny to zero-rated cross-border software and payroll services; if the LP cannot substantiate the non-resident, non-in-Canada-receipt nature of the supply, CRA reassesses the invoices at 5% GST, plus interest (prescribed rates compounded daily) and penalties under ETA s.280 (6% of assessed tax) and s.285 (knowingly/under circumstances of gross negligence: greater of $100 or 25% of net tax). False zero-rating claims for BC PST on software could trigger PST reassessment at 7% plus 10% penalty (BC Act s.23.2). The CRA also cites specific anti-avoidance rules (ss.274 and 276) allowing reassessment of non-resident transaction recharacterization; clawback extends retrospectively up to 7 years for wilful misrepresentation, and Canadian criminal exposure under ITA s.239 for fraudulent GST refund claims carries fines up to 200% of tax and imprisonment. For LP pass-through misreporting, CRA may also assess partners personally via T5013 audit and impose Part I.3 partnership transparency penalties, plus intra-provincial PST audit through BC Consumer Taxation Branch with 5-year lookback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible, freely floating currency with no exchange-control legislation and no capital or current account restrictions. A BC limited partnership may receive foreign capital contributions, convert CAD to any currency, and remit profits, royalties, and partnership distributions without central bank approval. Remittance friction is limited to: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on distributions and certain cross-border payments, generally 25% statutory and frequently reduced to 0–15% under an applicable tax treaty; (ii) FINTRAC International Electronic Funds Transfer (IETR) reporting for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) the general requirement to file T5013 partnership information returns; and (iv) the Investment Canada Act notification threshold if a non-resident acquires control of a Canadian business, which does not restrict ordinary treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canadian law imposes no surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds, SaaS subscription revenues, or payroll-service royalties. The LP may hold CAD, USD, or other foreign currency balances indefinitely, provided that the balances are recorded in its financial statements and worldwide income is reported to the Canada Revenue Agency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking regulation, Payments Canada for clearing and settlement (Lynx RTGS, ACSS, US Bulk Exchange), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money laundering oversight under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility as an ancillary rail; Low as a primary treasury relationship. FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (e.g., Wise Payments Canada Ltd., Airwallex Canada Inc., Payoneer Canada ULC) and payment facilitators (Stripe, PayPal) can provide fast, low-cost cross-border payroll disbursement, FX conversion, and API-driven liquidity, but they are not Schedule I banks under the Bank Act: they are not CDIC members, client funds held with them are not CDIC-insured, and they cannot clear CAD directly through Lynx or ACSS without a licensed bank agent. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign partners inject capital via SWIFT MT103 into the LP's CAD operational account, with the payment reference 'Capital Contribution to BC Limited Partnership — [legal name as registered]'. USD or EUR injections route through the receiving bank's correspondent network (USD via Fedwire/CHIPS to the bank's US correspondent, EUR via TARGET2), with explicit 'for further credit to [BC LP account]' instructions. Domestic CAD settlement for finality uses Lynx (Payments Canada RTGS) for high-value transfers and ACSS for standard next-day retail credits. FINTRAC IETR reporting applies to every international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; the LP must not structure transfers to avoid reporting thresholds. Banks verify that the sender name, account number, and purpose code match the KYC profile established at onboarding, and will reject transfers referencing unapproved counterparties or ambiguous purposes.Cross-border trade and payroll disbursements are primarily executed by SWIFT MT103 payment orders, supported by MT202 COV for bank-to-bank forwarding when a correspondent is interposed. Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) are permissible but rare for SaaS payroll and would apply only to large implementation or technology services contracts. For Canada and US payroll settlement, the LP will use ACSS Direct Deposit (CAD) and NACHA ACH (USD) through the bank's US partner for recurring small-value payments, and Lynx (CAD) or Fedwire (USD) for single high-value disbursements. Each outbound wire above CAD 10,000 is reportable to FINTRAC as an IETR. All disbursements must match the bank-approved counterparty list; the corporate invoice must reference NAICS 541214, and FX conversion is booked at the bank's treasury desk rate on the value date.Outright spot and forward FX contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) with tenors up to 24 months under the bank's treasury lineRolling and time-option forwards for payroll liquidity exposure with uncertain settlement datesFX swaps (principal exchanged at maturity) for rebalancing USD and CAD liquidityCross-currency interest rate swaps for medium-term hedging of USD or EUR working capital debtEuropean-style vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars to cap the CAD cost of payroll disbursements in convertible exotic currenciesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for payroll settlement currencies with limited convertibility (e.g., NGN, EGP, INR), permitted only as a designated hedge through an authorized derivatives dealerProhibited instruments: speculative OTC derivatives without an underlying payroll or trade exposure, cryptocurrency-denominated forwards, and products offered by unlicensed dealers under the BC Securities Act

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility as an ancillary rail; Low as a primary treasury relationship. FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (e.g., Wise Payments Canada Ltd., Airwallex Canada Inc., Payoneer Canada ULC) and payment facilitators (Stripe, PayPal) can provide fast, low-cost cross-border payroll disbursement, FX conversion, and API-driven liquidity, but they are not Schedule I banks under the Bank Act: they are not CDIC members, client funds held with them are not CDIC-insured, and they cannot clear CAD directly through Lynx or ACSS without a licensed bank agent. Legal constraint: if the LP chooses an EMI, the EMI must hold a valid FINTRAC MSB registration, maintain segregated client trust accounts, and the LP must retain a Schedule I bank account for regulatory clearing and statutory trust compliance. For this payroll SaaS platform, EMI rails are acceptable only for outbound micro-disbursements and salary settlements, not for treasury custody of client funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services; CAD 5 million for goods and other transactions with non-residents

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles. Partnerships must demonstrate genuine economic activity, including real offices, employees, and decision-making, to avoid recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under the Criminal Code.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the partnership structure under GAAR, reallocate income to the partners, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit. The partnership may be struck off the register for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Partners may be personally liable for taxes on reallocated income, plus interest and penalties. Directors of corporate general partners may face personal liability for unpaid corporate taxes.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals. Criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent AML violations. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000) if documentation is inadequate, and apply interest on reassessments.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate partners may be liable for penalties if they participated in the transfer pricing mispricing. Personal liability may extend to unpaid taxes and penalties.
