Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration, accessed through Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account; BC Ministry of Finance - Consumer Taxation Branch for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST), accessed through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

The BC ULC obtains a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST registrant account through CRA Business Registration Online using Form RC1, specifying the GST/HST account and corporation type. A non-resident officer/director may complete registration; the BN is issued immediately. Separately, BC PST vendor registration is filed with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC using the BN; registration is required for any person making PST-taxable sales in BC or collecting PST on temporary entry, storage, or use in BC. The ULC should state the medical-device export business scope and retain a copy of the certificate of incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Domestically, a BC ULC is a corporation taxable under the Income Tax Act on worldwide income and is a GST/HST registrable person. For foreign tax and financial reporting, a single-owner BC ULC may be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership under U.S. check-the-box rules; however Canada does not incorporate that foreign classification, and the ULC remains the obligor for Canadian taxes and GST/HST.

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists in Canada. The ULC may elect under U.S. Internal Revenue Code section 7701 using Form 8832 to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes. Canadian hybrid mismatch provisions in the Income Tax Act, sections 18.4 and 18.5, restrict deduction and non-inclusion outcomes. GST/HST registration is mandatory where taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; there is no voluntary Canadian corporate income tax election to be treated as a partnership for Canadian tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies for GST/HST; BC PST registration is required immediately for businesses selling taxable goods or taxable services in BC, with no equivalent small-supplier threshold for PST collection, though consumers or registered BC businesses may be required to self-assess certain non-resident B2B supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of the manufactured color Doppler ultrasound console is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the ULC exports tangible personal property outside Canada by delivering it to a carrier for shipment to a foreign address, or by transferring ownership outside Canada for consideration in the course of commercial activity. The supply ceases to be zero-rated if the goods are redelivered into Canada or used in Canada. Related offshore services, such as installation or repair performed entirely outside Canada, are generally zero-rated to non-residents if not part of a supply made in Canada.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CAED or CERS export report) filed with Canada Border Services Agency for direct exports exceeding CAD 2,000, with carrier or agent confirmation
  • Customs Export Declaration and commercial invoice identifying ISIC 3250 manufacturing nature, HS classification (e.g., HS 9018.12 or 9018.13), 'Made in Canada' statement, UPC or model, serial number, export value, and currency
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier proof of exit from Canada showing the port of export and foreign destination of the ultrasound console
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation, or letter of credit settlement showing receipt of foreign currency from the overseas buyer for the invoiced value
  • Internal records including purchase order or sales contract, packing list, signed delivery receipt from the overseas consignee or freight forwarder, and proof of no diversion back into Canada

No federal or BC law mandates real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or pre-clearance e-invoicing for GST/HST or PST. The CRA accepts electronic invoices provided they are legible and contain prescribed GST/HST information. Export invoices should include the ULC BN, date, description of the color Doppler ultrasound console, HS code, quantity, price, and foreign buyer. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; PST returns via eTaxBC. There is no national digital stamping system.

CRA and BC Ministry of Finance may deny zero-rating if export evidence is incomplete, and may reassess GST/HST, PST, interest, and penalties during the normal reassessment period that is generally four years, or six years for GST/HST in many circumstances, with an unlimited period for misrepresentation or fraud. Gross-negligence and knowingly false statements trigger Excise Tax Act penalties of 25% with a minimum of CAD 100, or higher, plus BC PST penalties up to 25% of the tax evaded for wilful evasion; criminal liability under the Excise Tax Act may result in fines and imprisonment. Input tax credits for export-related inputs may be denied or clawed back with adjusted interest if the supporting export bundle is not retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible currency regime for both current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible without exchange control restriction under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and Canada's commitments under the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, service fee, or profit remittance to non-residents; such payments are subject solely to Part XIII withholding taxes under the Income Tax Act (default 25% for non-treaty jurisdictions; 5%/15% under applicable treaties, including the Canada–US Income Tax Convention) and to FATCA/CRS reporting requirements. No Bank of Canada or government approval is required for conversion or remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts in Canada. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency, maintain foreign-currency bank accounts, or convert proceeds into CAD. All export proceeds must be accounted for in Canadian dollars for income tax purposes under the functional-curency rules of the Income Tax Act, with realized/realized gains or losses reported on the T2 corporate return. Where applicable, export volumes and proceeds must be reported to Statistics Canada under the Canadian International Merchandise Trade and Balance of Payments reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the functional central monetary authority; foreign exchange administration is vested in the Ministry of Finance and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Currency Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no separately designated Foreign Exchange Administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-moderate overall feasibility. Foreign fintech and electronic money institution (EMI) providers are not directly licensed or chartered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and cannot offer deposit-taking or settlement accounts in Canada without a Schedule I, II, or III bank licence. A foreign EMI may operate only as a money services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, or as a payment service provider through a regulated bank partner. Legal constraints include mandatory FINTRAC registration, record-keeping and transaction-reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000), a physical anti-money-laundering compliance program in Canada, and prohibition on holding consumer or corporate deposits unless licensed as a bank. For cross-border medical-device trade settlement, a foreign EMI can be used as a payment corridor adjunct, but the primary treasury account and clearing relationship must reside with a Canadian Schedule I bank to ensure reliable SWIFT routing, letter-of-credit issuance, and liquidity concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity injections to the BC ULC must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for the CAD (or foreign-currency) leg, with the remittance field specifying 'Capital Contribution / Equity Injection' and including the ULC's full legal name and CRA business number. The underlying purpose code should align with the commercial bank's corporate remittance code for foreign direct investment (FDI) (in many banks, purpose code 3800 or the bank-specific equity-injection flag). The bank will require a board resolution authorizing the capital contribution, an updated share subscription agreement, evidence of the corporate authorization in the BC corporate registry, and a source-of-funds declaration from the funding entity. Canada does not require Bank of Canada approval for capital inflows; however, the Canada Investment Act may require notification or review for the acquisition of control of a Canadian business by a non-Canadian investor where enterprise value thresholds are met; equity injections for general corporate financing that do not change control are not subject to Investment Canada Act review. For a ULC with US shareholders, the bank must also confirm the US Person status and obtain IRS Forms W-8/W-9 to comply with FATCA and CRS.Outbound trade settlements for medical-device exports shall use SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for open-account and TT-wire payments, and SWIFT MT700/MT710 for documentary letters of credit issued by the purchaser's bank. All outbound payments must be matched to underlying invoices and transport documents, use appropriate purpose codes (e.g., 2011 for goods trade, 2021 for short-term trade credit, or 2051 for financial lease of equipment), and remain above FINTRAC record-keeping thresholds. The Canadian clearing backbone comprises Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR), the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS), and the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); same-day high-value USD settlements are typically executed through the bank's US correspondent network (e.g., RBC via RBC Bank/US, TD via TD Bank US, BMO Harris, or CIBC via CIBC Bank Corp). For export LCs, the ULC's bank will advise or confirm the LC and negotiate compliant documents (strict compliance under UCP 600, ISBP 745 and URC 522 for collections).
