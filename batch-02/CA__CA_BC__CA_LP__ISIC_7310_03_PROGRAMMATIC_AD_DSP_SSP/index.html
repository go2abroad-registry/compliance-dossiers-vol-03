Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial authorities: BC Registries and Online Services (Limited Partnership registration under the BC Partnership Act) and BC Ministry of Finance Provincial Tax Administration via eTaxBC (BC PST).

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Register the Limited Partnership under British Columbia law at the BC Corporate Registry via OneStop Business Registry to obtain the BC LP registration number and legal existence. STEP 2: Apply to the CRA for the federal Business Number (BN) and the GST/HST program account (RT) using Form RC1 or the joint federal-provincial business registration service; the Canadian-resident general partner signs the application, and non-resident partners are identified through the CRA non-resident registration process. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - a BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96(1); the LP itself pays no federal Part I income tax and no BC corporate income tax. Income, gains, and losses are allocated to partners in their agreed proportions. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are taxable in Canada on their share of Canadian-source partnership business income (ITA ss. 2(3) and 96(4)), subject to treaty relief. For GST/HST, the LP is deemed a separate person (Excise Tax Act s. 272.1(2)) and must register once its worldwide taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold. Territorial scope: only income attributable to the Canadian business conducted through the LP is subject to Canadian taxation; the passive foreign income of non-resident partners is generally outside Canadian taxing jurisdiction. The entity-level SIFT Partnership rules are inapplicable because the LP is privately held and not publicly traded.

Available election forms

Canada has no elective check-the-box regime; partnership classification is fixed by statute. The following elections are available to a BC LP engaged in AdTech export services: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration before the CAD $30,000 threshold under ETA s. 240(2) to recover input tax credits; (ii) election to file GST/HST separately from the calendar year or to file annually for qualifying registrants; (iii) election for a non-calendar partnership fiscal period under ITA s. 249.1(7) where all partners are non-residents; (iv) allocation of income by the partners' agreed proportions in the limited partnership agreement (ITA s. 96(1)); (v) no hybrid mismatch election exists - proposed ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 (hybrid mismatch rules) are not yet in force, and a U.S. partner's own check-the-box election classifying the Canadian LP as opaque may trigger recharacterization by the CRA under the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) or under the Canada-US Tax Treaty business-profits article.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 - GST/HST small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1), measured on the worldwide taxable supplies of the LP and its associated persons in the current calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; crossing this threshold triggers mandatory GST/HST registration, collection, and remittance. BC PST: no registration threshold; a seller must register via eTaxBC before making any taxable sale in BC, including the licensing of standardized commercial software used in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Programmatic AdTech DSP/SSP services qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate as a supply of a service to a non-resident person under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, provided that the service is not rendered to an individual physically present in Canada at the time of performance, does not relate to real property situated in Canada, and does not relate to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance unless that property is exported. The non-resident status of the counterparty must be evidenced by its ordinary business address, tax residence documentation, and payment origin; the computer servers executing programmatic bids may be located globally, but the benefit, use, and consumption of the service must rest with the non-resident recipient outside Canada. Mixed-purpose contracts covering both Canadian and foreign campaign delivery must clearly allocate the Canadian portion to taxable supplies and the foreign portion to zero-rated supplies.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (eManifest/GCA export reporting) for any physical goods export component, if applicable
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill) or equivalent electronic proof of export for goods movements
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident counterparty
  • Executed digital services agreement identifying the non-resident recipient's legal name, registered address, and country of tax residence
  • Commercial invoice expressly citing ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 with the notation GST/HST: 0% zero-rated export of services
  • Server and IP routing logs or platform delivery reports demonstrating that the advertising service is consumed outside Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, state-run API validation, or digital stamping for GST/HST invoices. Electronic invoicing is permitted and voluntarily standardized through the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing (Peppol) framework. Every registrant must issue a GST/HST invoice containing the supplier's legal name and Business Number, date of issue, description of the supply, and either the GST/HST charged or a clear statement that the supply is zero-rated; invoices may be issued and stored electronically. All accounting records and export substantiation must be retained in readable format for six years (ETA s. 286) and produced to the CRA upon demand.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated export supply within four years of the assessment date, extended to seven years where misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud is established. Failure to substantiate the non-resident recipient or the foreign consumption of the service results in full reversal of the zero-rating and reassessment of 5% GST on the gross consideration, plus prescribed interest (currently above 9% per annum for corporate arrears). Statutory penalties include: ETA s. 280(1) failure-to-collect penalty of 10% of the tax not collected, ETA s. 280(1.1) failure-to-file penalty of 5% of the net tax plus 1% per full month of delinquency to a maximum of 12 months, and ETA s. 285 knowing or grossly negligent false statement penalty of 25% of the understated net tax. The CRA cross-matches the commercial contract, invoice, bank remittance records, and platform IP logs; discrepancies such as Canadian billing addresses, Canadian campaign geo-targeting, or domestic consumption of the service will rebut the zero-rating claim and trigger a full clawback. Intentional fraudulent export schemes may also attract the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) and criminal exposure under the Criminal Code. For non-resident partners, any reassessed GST/HST liability of the partnership is jointly and severally enforceable against each partner under ETA s. 272.1(3).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible free-floating currency. Canada abolished formal exchange controls in the 1950s and imposes no balance-of-payments restrictions, no investment-approval requirement on inward capital, and no restriction on the repatriation of capital, dividends, or partnership distributions. Both current-account items (exported advertising-technology services, software licensing, global bid-stream royalties) and capital-account items (limited-partner contributions, shareholder loans, return of capital) may be denominated and settled in CAD or any freely convertible foreign currency. The practical profit-remittance friction is exclusively tax-driven for the BC Limited Partnership (LP): Canadian-source business income must be allocated on a flow-through basis to each partner via T5013 schedules and, where a partner is a non-resident, the non-resident must either file a Canadian Part I tax return or rely on treaty-relief forms (NR301/NR302), because the partnership is not subject to Part XIII withholding on business-income distributions per se but must comply with the CRA administrative withholding and reporting regime for non-resident partners. GST/HST on exported advertising services is normally zero-rated with input-credit eligibility, removing VAT as a cash-flow hurdle. No exchange-control license or central-bank permission is required for any transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime. There is no requirement for an exporter of services to convert, repatriate, escrow, or deposit foreign-currency receivables into CAD with the Bank of Canada, and no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory timeline applies to export receipts. The BC LP may freely retain USD, EUR, and other currencies in foreign-currency operating accounts at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The only relevant obligations are: (i) FINTRAC reporting for cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more (or foreign-currency equivalent) under the PCMLTFA; (ii) accurate invoicing and source-of-funds documentation supporting the commercial purpose of each receivable from foreign ad exchanges and DSP/SSP counterparties; and (iii) documentation of foreign-currency gains or losses for income-tax purposes where the LP adopts a Canadian-dollar functional currency or elects to report in USD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and operator of the high-value settlement system (Lynx, formerly LVTS). Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange control ministry; prudential regulation of banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and transaction-level monitoring is undertaken by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Payment service providers, including fintech clearing agents, must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) for activity initiated after the applicable registration deadlines.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection corridor, LOW as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) licensed under, for example, the UK FCA EMI regime or EU electronic-money directives are not recognized by OSFI as Canadian deposit-taking institutions and cannot provide a Canadian bank account, a CAD IBAN-equivalent clearing number, or direct membership in Payments Canada's Lynx, ACSS, or Real-Time Rail (RTR) settlement systems. A BC LP may lawfully use an offshore EMI only to collect USD/EUR/GBP receivables from foreign advertisers and publishers provided: (i) the EMI is properly registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC if it solicits Canadian clients; (ii) the LP itself remains the regulated PSP for Canadian payment-taking (RPAA registration); and (iii) all EMI balances are swept daily to the LP's Schedule I Canadian bank account to avoid unregistered foreign-money-services exposure and to preserve CAD clearing capability. As a primary rail, an offshore EMI fails because it cannot originate CAD ACH debits, cannot clear Canadian payroll, and creates FINTRAC double-reporting risk.

Root cause: Sanctioned/high-risk counterparty leakage: programmatic inventory may include traffic from or payments to entities in OFAC/DFATD/UN-sanctioned jurisdictions or state-sponsored media, and the bank requires demonstrable screening.

Defense evidence: Written jurisdiction-screening and geographic-blocking policy; automated OFAC/DFATD screening reports of the supply-chain counterparty list produced quarterly; contractual representations from SSP/DSP partners excluding sanctioned inventory; evidencing of IP-address blocklists; a remediation protocol for any flagged hit; and a copy of the LP's voluntary undertaking to terminate any counterparty appearing on a sanctions list within 24 hours.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Limited-partner capital contributions should be wired by SWIFT MT103 with the remittance narrative (Field 70) stating 'LIMITED PARTNER CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT DATED [date] - NON-RESIDENT INVESTMENT' and, if applicable, the corresponding purpose/function code used by the sending institution. The receiving Schedule I bank will request: the capital-call notice issued by the general partner, a certified excerpt of the LPA establishing the contribution obligation, identity/source-of-funds documentation for the remitting partner, and, for contributions exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank will file a FINTRAC Large Cash or International Electronic Funds Transfer Report (IEFTR). No Bank of Canada or government approval is required because Canada does not operate a capital-control regime; the contribution should be booked as an increase in each partner's capital account and excluded from income. If the contribution is in USD, it may be credited to the LP's USD account and converted to CAD on a spot basis; an election under the Income Tax Act may be made to offset foreign-currency gains on the capital contribution, but the LP's reporting currency and hedge relationship must be documented.The LP should structure settlement rails in three layers: (i) domestic CAD disbursements to Canadian publishers and vendors via Payments Canada ACSS/EFT or the Lynx high-value wire system for amounts above the EFT cap; (ii) USD disbursements to US-based publishers via the Schedule I bank's US correspondent network using Fedwire or same-bank ACH, or SWIFT MT103 for foreign USD payees; and (iii) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for non-USD international payees, using the ISO 20022 pain.001/MT103 formats to include full remittance mapping (invoice number, campaign ID, publisher ID). For physical-media and out-of-home programmatic follow-ons requiring documentary trade settlement, commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 are available; however, standard programmatic RTB ad settlement is executed on net-30 or net-60 credit terms through ACH/SWIFT rather than LCs. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection corridor, LOW as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) licensed under, for example, the UK FCA EMI regime or EU electronic-money directives are not recognized by OSFI as Canadian deposit-taking institutions and cannot provide a Canadian bank account, a CAD IBAN-equivalent clearing number, or direct membership in Payments Canada's Lynx, ACSS, or Real-Time Rail (RTR) settlement systems. A BC LP may lawfully use an offshore EMI only to collect USD/EUR/GBP receivables from foreign advertisers and publishers provided: (i) the EMI is properly registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC if it solicits Canadian clients; (ii) the LP itself remains the regulated PSP for Canadian payment-taking (RPAA registration); and (iii) all EMI balances are swept daily to the LP's Schedule I Canadian bank account to avoid unregistered foreign-money-services exposure and to preserve CAD clearing capability. As a primary rail, an offshore EMI fails because it cannot originate CAD ACH debits, cannot clear Canadian payroll, and creates FINTRAC double-reporting risk.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate rate (Federal 15% + British Columbia 12%); 11% combined small business rate for qualifying CCPC partners (Federal 9% + BC 2%). The BC Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96; tax is imposed at the partner level on allocated income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by the fifth month after the partnership fiscal year-end; a partnership with a corporate partner must adopt a calendar fiscal year, making the deadline May 31.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed within six months of the corporation's fiscal year-end; balance of tax due within two months (three months for a CCPC eligible for the small business deduction).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual NR4 return and partner statements due the last day of March following the calendar year; Part XIII withholding remitted to the CRA by the 15th day of the month following the month in which the amount was paid or credited.
