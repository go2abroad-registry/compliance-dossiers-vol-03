Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and income tax administration; BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch – Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and Employer Health Tax. Portals: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/revenue-agency) and BC eTaxBC (gov.bc.ca/taxes).

E-government filing portal

Register a Business Number (BN) with the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the BN serves as the single statutory identifier for GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts. A non-resident limited partnership without a Canadian permanent establishment must register for GST/HST if it makes taxable supplies in Canada and exceeds the small supplier threshold; use Form RC171 or BRO. For BC PST, register with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch before making taxable sales into British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box system; a limited partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96, with each partner computing income separately. Available elections include: partnership fiscal period election (ITA s.249.1), allocation of income among partners by partnership agreement, s.216 election for non-resident partners, GST/HST intra-group election (Excise Tax Act s.156), and joint election to treat a partnership as a separate supply-maker for GST/HST in limited cases. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s.18.4, s.18.5) constrain deduction/non-inclusion mismatches for cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters (small supplier threshold, ETA s.148); BC PST: registration required before selling taxable goods/services into BC where remote-seller BC taxable turnover exceeds CAD 10,000 per calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of digital voice streaming services applies where the supply is made to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and the service or intangible personal property (IPP) is supplied for use outside Canada (ETA s.7 and s.10; Sch. VI Part V). App subscriptions sold to foreign end users through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing qualify if the platform remits to the Canadian partnership for offshore user consumption and the partnership retains evidence of non-resident recipient status.

  • Commercial Invoice / Order Confirmation (zero-rated supply indicator)
  • Terms of Service and End User Licensing Agreement (offshore supply and delivery terms)
  • Payment Gateway Settlement Reports (Apple IAP / Google Play Billing) tracing foreign-user receipts
  • Subscriber geolocation and IP-address records evidencing non-resident consumption
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank statements evidencing FX repatriation to the Canadian partnership
  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) only where physical media, servers, or equipment cross the border
  • Non-resident recipient declaration (optional but recommended for audit substantiation)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API tax reporting, or digital stamping applies in Canada. The CRA accepts electronic invoices and records if legible and retained for audit; pan-Canadian e-invoicing via the Peppol network is voluntary. GST/HST returns (GST 34) are filed periodically without transaction-level clearance.

The CRA may re-assess any zero-rated export claim within the normal assessment period (4 years, extended to 7 years for misrepresentation), claw back uncollected GST/HST with compounded daily interest, and apply penalties under ETA s.280 (10% of net tax) and s.285 (25% of tax sought to be evaded for false statements or omissions of gross negligence, minimum CAD 100). Willful evasion may attract criminal prosecution under ETA s.327, fines up to 200% of the evaded tax, and imprisonment; partners may be jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized, free-floating currency regime with no exchange controls. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible on both current and capital account, and there are no authorization hurdles for non-resident Limited Partners contributing capital to a BC Limited Partnership. Profit remittance of a non-resident partner's Canada-source partnership income is legally unrestricted, but is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate or a reduced rate under an applicable income tax treaty (e.g., Article IV of the Canada-US Convention); this is an income tax compliance matter, not an FX-control restriction. The practical convertibility hurdles are banking-related rather than regulatory: source-of-funds attestation, FINTRAC large transaction reporting, sanctions screening, and the Income Tax Act s.96 carry-allocation mechanics for a tax-transparent LP. No approval is needed for CAD convertibility to USD or any freely traded currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada abolished statutory exchange controls through the 1952 repeal of the Exchange Control Act and has never reimposed them. Under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, the Income Tax Act, and the Currency Exchange (Foreign Exchange) Regulations, there is no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds, cross-border service revenue, or App Store disbursements. Export receipts and digital platform proceeds may be retained indefinitely in CAD or foreign currency accounts at authorized financial institutions, subject only to statistical balance-of-payments reporting by the banking system, FINTRAC's CAD 10,000 large transaction aggregation rules, and ordinary suspicious-transaction monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (monetary authority and currency administration under the Currency Act), with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of federally chartered banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the cross-border transaction reporting and AML/CTF supervisor. British Columbia's provincial treasury and the BC Financial Services Authority govern provincial trust and credit union matters; there is no dedicated Canadian exchange-control board.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions must be routed through the SWIFT network by an MT103 payment (or Fedwire for USD clearing through the bank's US correspondent) using an ISO 20022 Extended Purpose Code. For equity-like partnership capital, use 'EQUI' (Equity Securities) or 'INVS' (Investment and Securities) with the remittance narrative 'LIMITED PARTNERSHIP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — CA_LP — LP AGREEMENT SCHEDULE [x]'. For shareholder or intercompany debt funding, use 'CFIN' (Corporate Finance) or 'LOAN', supported by an executed loan agreement and board/partner resolution. Canadian receiving banks require (1) a documented underlying investment contract, (2) the LP's and remitter's tax identification numbers, (3) FINTRAC large EFT reporting at the CAD 10,000 / 24-hour aggregation threshold, (4) beneficial-ownership certification of both remitter and LP, (5) full sanctions screening against the OSFI consolidated list, United Nations, and OFAC lists, and (6) a source-of-funds letter for any contribution above CAD 100,000. The contribution must not be split into sub-threshold tranches; such structuring constitutes an independent offence under the PCMLTFA. CAD funds clear immediately via Lynx/ACSS after conversion by the bank's FX desk; USD funds are held in the LP's separate USD clearing account until converted or deployed.The treasury clearing architecture is: (i) Domestic CAD vendor and payroll payments via ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) EFT or Lynx high-value wire; (ii) Cross-border service, content, and software disbursements via SWIFT MT103 with ISO purpose codes 'SERV' (Services) or 'SUPP' (Supplier Payments) against synced invoice and purchase-order packages; (iii) Goods-based trade settlements through irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600 (ICC Publication 600), issued by the bank's trade desk with a practical minimum threshold of CAD 50,000; (iv) Apple App Store and Google Play inflow is received through platform banking rails (ACH in USD or SWIFT to the LP's USD/CAD accounts) and subsequently redeployed to content providers, marketing agencies, and cloud infrastructure vendors; and (v) all non-resident disbursements of royalties, interest, or partnership income allocations are executed only after Part XIII withholding at 25% or the applicable treaty rate is applied, documented through CRA Forms NR301/NR302. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal (15%) and British Columbia provincial (12%) general corporate income tax rate applicable to corporate partners; the British Columbia limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. Canadian limited partnership distributions that represent allocations of active business income are not statutory dividends; they are taxed under Part I on the allocated income and are not subject to Part XIII withholding at distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 per non-resident related party per taxation year triggers the expectation of contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation in the Local File/Master File format aligned with CRA guidelines; CbC reporting is required for large multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime, but CRA aggressively applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act. A BC limited partnership that lacks real commercial activity, employees, and business purpose may be disregarded as artificial, with treaty benefits denied and re-allocation of income to partners. The limited partnership must therefore demonstrate substantive operations, local decision-making, and genuine risk-bearing commensurate with its role in the voice streaming application.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filings

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) is 50% of the amount of the understated tax, plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner can be held personally liable for unremitted corporate taxes, payroll withholdings, and GST/QST amounts. Criminal conviction may trigger personal fines, restitution orders, travel restrictions, and incarceration for the directing mind; Canadian authorities may also issue asset freezes and cross-border enforcement orders.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to arm's length amounts, with a transfer-pricing penalty under section 247(4) of 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue, whichever is less; interest and late penalties run from the year of the return.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in a sham pricing arrangement may be assessed under related anti-avoidance provisions, exposed to gross-negligence penalties, and may face disqualification as directors in Canada; criminal liability is possible if the mispricing forms part of a deliberate tax evasion scheme.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangement (GAAR / Economic Substance Failure)

Statutory sanction: CRA will invoke GAAR to disregard the arrangement, re-determine the partnership's income, and impose federal and BC provincial tax on the economic beneficiary. The transaction may also be assessed as a sham, attracting penalties of 50% of the understated tax, plus interest; the DTA benefit is denied, resulting in full 25% Part XIII withholding on any payments made to non-residents.

Director personal liability: The general partner's directors and controlling partners may be jointly liable for the resulting provincial and federal taxes on the basis of authority over partnership assets. Directors who arrange shell structures may face civil restitution, travel bans on active enforcement proceedings, and criminal charges under money laundering statutes when the arrangement is linked to cross-border fund diversion.
Anti-Money Laundering (PCMLTFA) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties for a first-time non-compliance violation can reach CAD 1,000,000 for an entity, and higher penalties may apply for repeat or intentional breaches; individuals may face fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for obstructing enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner are personally responsible for establishing a compliant AML program. Knowingly facilitating laundering of proceeds through in-app payments or virtual rewards can lead to criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code, banking-law enforcement actions, personal fines, asset forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition from and to foreign states.
