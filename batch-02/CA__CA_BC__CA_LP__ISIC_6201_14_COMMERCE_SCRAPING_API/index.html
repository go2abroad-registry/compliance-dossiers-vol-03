Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC at https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned limited partnership doing business in Canada must first obtain a CRA Business Number (BN). Where the limited partnership has significant Canadian business activity, it must register online through Business Registration Online or by completing Form RC1, Request for a Business Number. For GST/HST purposes, registration is mandatory once the partnership carries on a taxable commercial activity in Canada and worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available. The BN serves as the GST/HST account number when an RT account is opened. Separately, a vendor making taxable retail sales of goods or services in British Columbia must register for PST with the BC Ministry of Finance using the Consumer Taxation Branch registration procedures, obtaining a BC PST account identifier. Non-resident partners do not require separate individual TINs for federal partnership income tax because the LP is a flow-through entity; the partnership files the required federal partnership information return with CRA. Foreign entities may need to appoint a Canadian representative or provide proof of a permanent establishment if any, but a BC limited partnership itself is recognized as a domestic limited partnership. For PST, if the LP has no physical presence in BC but makes taxable sales to BC consumers, registration as an extra-provincial vendor is still required unless an exemption applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), a limited partnership is per se a flow-through entity and cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation absent a formal reorganization or continuation. Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime. For GST/HST, the Excise Tax Act treats a partnership as a separate person; unless a partnership and its members jointly elect under ETA subsection 273(1) to have the partnership deemed not to be a separate person, the partnership generally must register and account for GST/HST in its own BN. Hybrid mismatch election and adjustment rules under sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act require the taxpayer to make a prescribed form election (RC6160 for hybrid mismatch arrangement reporting) where a mismatch arises from a payment under an instrument or through a disregarded branch. Provincial income tax in BC is administered under the federal Income Tax Act and no separate provincial election is available for LP classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration threshold: CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies made in Canada over any single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; the threshold is measured on worldwide taxable supplies by the partnership unless the partnership is a non-resident carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada. There is no generally applicable monetary exemption for BC PST; every seller making taxable retail sales in British Columbia must be registered before conducting taxable sales, subject to narrow exemptions for occasional or non-resident sellers under the Provincial Sales Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: a registrant may zero-rate tangible personal property exported from Canada if the exporter has proof that the goods were delivered outside Canada within a prescribed period and the sale is in the ordinary course of business. For services: data processing, web-scraping, and similar digitally delivered services supplied to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the recipient is not resident in Canada and the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. A supply made to a non-resident who is not registered under Division V of the ETA is generally zero-rated as long as the service is for use outside Canada. A certificate from the non-resident establishing non-resident status must be obtained. BC PST generally has no export exemption because PST is a consumption tax imposed on use of taxable goods and services in British Columbia; services delivered through API subscriptions to non-residents outside BC are normally not subject to PST if the vendor can show the service is received outside BC.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed electronically with the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods exported from Canada where required; export permit or licence where applicable
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document proving export movement of goods out of Canada
  • Commercial invoice or contract clearly describing the goods or services sold, export destination, consideration, and parties
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation showing export sale proceeds being repatriated to the exporting entity or credited to a Canadian account

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate. Invoices must be issued for taxable supplies and retained, but there is no mandatory government pre-approval of invoice numbering or electronic stamping. CRA accepts electronic records provided they are complete, accurate, and accessible in an auditable format. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE or through approved third-party software; BC PST returns must be filed electronically through eTaxBC. API-based automated invoice exchange with fiscal authorities is not currently mandatory in either the federal GST/HST regime or the BC PST regime.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess the undeclared GST/HST on the full gross sale amount, with interest and penalties, if the exporter fails to substantiate export eligibility with contemporaneous records. Negligent or deliberate false claims for zero-rating constitute tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act. Penalties include a federal tax evasion fine of between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment for up to five years, and generally 5% plus 1% per month late-filing or failure-to-file penalties. For BC PST, false deductions or failure to collect/remit PST may result in a penalty of up to 10% of the tax owing plus 2% per month interest. A retrospective clawback period may cover the entire period from the date of the erroneous zero-rating claim, subject to the normal reassessment period of four years, extended to ten years in cases of carelessness, neglect, or misrepresentation attributable to fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; the Canadian dollar is freely floating, and there are no general exchange controls, surrender requirements, or approval gates on capital movements. For a BC limited partnership (LP), profit and capital remittances to foreign partners are unrestricted at the FX level; however, non-resident partners are subject to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on allocated taxable income (generally 25%, treaty-reduced, with the LP remitting on behalf of partners via NR4/Form NR601 filings), while return of contributed capital is tax-free in normal circumstances. Inbound foreign investment into the LP may trigger Investment Canada Act notification or national security review, though the current transactional threshold for WTO investors is materially above typical LP capital contributions (approximately CAD 1.4 billion in 2025). No significant hurdles exist for opening CAD/USD accounts, funding via foreign capital, or servicing foreign clients in the data/API industry, provided the LP maintains active business substance and complies with PIPEDA and applicable data protection rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no retention timelines for export receipts, whether from services, software subscriptions, or digital assets. Mandatory compliance obligations nevertheless apply: (i) international electronic funds transfers (EFTs) of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA; (ii) cross-border import/export of currency or monetary instruments of C$10,000 or more requires reporting to FINTRAC/CBSA; (iii) service export revenue from web-scraping API gateways must be included in Statistics Canada trade-in-services surveys when corporate thresholds are met; and (iv) virtual currency (USDC) transactions involving value transfers are reportable as virtual currency transactions at the C$10,000 EFT threshold, and the LP must maintain a wire/ETP trail demonstrating the source and beneficial ownership of all export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange administration, AML/CTF surveillance, and cross-border transaction reporting enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and its regulations, including the Enforcement of the Special Economic Measures Act and related UN and autonomous sanctions administered by Global Affairs Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low for offshore digital EMIs. Canada does not issue an EMI licence per se; a cross-border payment institution must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, for BC-based operations, obtain a BC money services business licence under the Money Services Businesses Act (British Columbia). Offshore EMI licences (e.g., UK FCA EMI or Lithuanian EMI) are not automatically recognized for soliciting Canadian business and may be used only as technical payment intermediaries, not as local deposit-holders. Domestic fintech rails (e.g., Stripe, Wise Business, Stripe Treasury/BaaS partner banks) are operationally viable for card processing and cross-border payouts, but underwriting is materially stricter for data-scraping-derived API revenue, and USDC settlement requires a separate licensed virtual asset custodian or exchange counterparty, with the LP ultimately retaining a Schedule I bank account for fiat treasury clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for non-resident partner capital contributions into the BC LP: (i) inbound SWIFT MT103 wire to the LP's CAD/USD operational account or the designated legal counsel trust (escrow) account for initial closing; (ii) wire instructions include the LP legal name, account details, and a purpose code mapping to Direct Investment - Equity Capital contribution under the Canadian International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) / IMF Balance of Payments classification, with the remitter noting 'LP Capital Contribution - [Partner Name]' in the reference field; (iii) for payments C$10,000 or above, the receiving Schedule I bank automatically files the FINTRAC international EFT report; (iv) source-of-funds verification packs (bank statements, tax returns, or liquidation statements for corporate partners) are retained with the closing binder; (v) capital account ledger entries must match the BC LP agreement's capital contribution schedule, and the CRA's partnership information return (T5013) must record the same amounts; (vi) where the capital exceeds the Investment Canada Act notification threshold (not applicable at typical API-gateway capital levels), an ICA filing is made. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low for offshore digital EMIs. Canada does not issue an EMI licence per se; a cross-border payment institution must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, for BC-based operations, obtain a BC money services business licence under the Money Services Businesses Act (British Columbia). Offshore EMI licences (e.g., UK FCA EMI or Lithuanian EMI) are not automatically recognized for soliciting Canadian business and may be used only as technical payment intermediaries, not as local deposit-holders. Domestic fintech rails (e.g., Stripe, Wise Business, Stripe Treasury/BaaS partner banks) are operationally viable for card processing and cross-border payouts, but underwriting is materially stricter for data-scraping-derived API revenue, and USDC settlement requires a separate licensed virtual asset custodian or exchange counterparty, with the LP ultimately retaining a Schedule I bank account for fiat treasury clearing.
