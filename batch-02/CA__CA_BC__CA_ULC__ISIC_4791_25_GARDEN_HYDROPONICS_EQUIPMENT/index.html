Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

BC Registry, Corporate Registry Division, Ministry of Finance (Service BC), via the Corporate Online (CORES) electronic filing portal.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (British Columbia), S.B.C. 2002, c. 57 (BCBCA), ss. 10 (incorporation), 20 (notice of articles), 21 (authorized share structure), 22 (no par value shares), 51 (unlimited liability company), 133 (registered office), 141 (directors), and 167 (restoration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation Application (Form 1) and Notice of Articles (Form 2), filed electronically under the BCBCA and recorded by the Registrar of Companies.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 42 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account requirement applies to a British Columbia unlimited liability company; the authorized share structure, including one or more classes of unlimited common shares without par value, is set by the directors in the Notice of Articles, and issued shares may be subscribed for and paid in cash at the directors' established issue price.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and inspection clearance from the local fire authority or municipality under the BC Fire Code (e.g., fire extinguisher placement, exit signage, occupancy load for the warehouse and office). Structural load inspection by a registered professional engineer is required where mezzanine racking or heavy irrigation-tank shelving is installed, confirming compliance with the BC Building Code. WorkSafeBC occupational-environment clearance includes registration as a BC employer with WorkSafeBC, a qualifying workplace orientation and health-and-safety inspection, and, where applicable, compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety Regulation (OHSR) including electrical safety measures for any in-house testing of products and manual handling procedures for warehouse employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account requirement applies to a British Columbia unlimited liability company; the authorized share structure, including one or more classes of unlimited common shares without par value, is set by the directors in the Notice of Articles, and issued shares may be subscribed for and paid in cash at the directors' established issue price.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

Technical Safety BC (Electrical Safety Branch) in coordination with accredited certification bodies such as CSA Group or Intertek for electrical product certification; no statutory industrial manufacturing licence applies to online retail and warehouse distribution.

Category: Municipal Business Licence (Warehousing & Electronic Commerce) plus Electrical Product Certification for hydroponic grow lights, pumps, and other electrically powered equipment supplied to Canadian end consumers.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), the federal tax administration for corporate income tax and GST/HST; principal digital portal: CRA My Business Account / Business Registration Online (bilingual at canada.ca, GST/HST NETFILE). BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned BC ULC: (1) incorporate under the BC Business Corporations Act using BC Corporate Online and obtain the BC incorporation number; (2) register with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1; the BN is the statutory TIN hosting program accounts (RC = corporate income tax, RT = GST/HST); (3) add the GST/HST account using Form RC160 or BRO, observing that non-resident directors, members, and foreign addresses are accepted, but a Canadian representative may be required (Form RC59); (4) apply for BC PST registration as a 'seller' through eTaxBC once taxable sales into BC exceed CAD 10,000 in the prior 12 months; (5) confirm the RC account is active for T2 corporate return filings. No minimum paid-in capital is required for a BC ULC.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - The BC ULC is pass-through eligible for U.S. federal tax purposes (check-the-box classification), yet for Canadian tax purposes it remains an entity-level taxable 'corporation' under the Income Tax Act, subject to Part I corporate income tax on worldwide income. — For a foreign-owned BC ULC: (1) incorporate under the BC Business Corporations Act using BC Corporate Online and obtain the BC incorporation number; (2) register with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1; the BN is the statutory TIN hosting program accounts (RC = corporate income tax, RT = GST/HST); (3) add the GST/HST account using Form RC160 or BRO, observing that non-resident directors, members, and foreign addresses are accepted, but a Canadian representative may be required (Form RC59); (4) apply for BC PST registration as a 'seller' through eTaxBC once taxable sales into BC exceed CAD 10,000 in the prior 12 months; (5) confirm the RC account is active for T2 corporate return filings. No minimum paid-in capital is required for a BC ULC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The BC ULC is pass-through eligible for U.S. federal tax purposes (check-the-box classification), yet for Canadian tax purposes it remains an entity-level taxable 'corporation' under the Income Tax Act, subject to Part I corporate income tax on worldwide income.

Available election forms

(1) U.S. check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3(c) (Form 8832) to classify the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. tax while preserving Canadian corporate status; (2) election/relief under Article IV(6) of the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention so that payments by the ULC are treated as payments by a resident of Canada for treaty purposes; (3) GST/HST joint election under ETA s. 156 (Form GST25) allowing inter-corporate supplies among closely related corporations to be made for nil consideration; (4) availability of limited elections under Canada's hybrid-mismatch regime (ITA Part I, s. 95, and associated regulations) to align deduction timing and avoid double-counting for hybrid structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold), including non-resident e-commerce vendors; BC PST: CAD 10,000 of taxable retail sales into British Columbia in the prior 12 months for out-of-province sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may apply the 0% rate to exported tangible personal property under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 where tangible personal property is sold and shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, with proof of export retained. Services supplied to non-residents qualify under Schedule VII, Part V; intangible property supplied to non-residents qualifies under Schedule VIII, Part V, subject to carve-outs for Canadian real property or tangible personal property. Zero-rated exporters may claim refunds of input tax credits through the normal GST/HST return; export evidence must be retained for six years.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) / Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) confirmation
  • Commercial Invoice reflecting HS/NAICS classification of garden hydroponics equipment
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to a destination outside Canada
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/POS settlement statement evidencing foreign-currency collection
  • CUSMA / U.S. entry summary documentation for sales into the United States

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based continuous transaction controls exist in Canada. GST/HST returns are filed periodically (annual/quarterly/monthly) through CRA GST/HST NETFILE (mandatory e-filing for registrants with CAD 1M+ revenue); PST returns are remitted via eTaxBC. The Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing framework is voluntary B2B infrastructure, not a tax clearance mechanism; no XML clearance, fiscal invoice stamping, or blockchain-based fiscal validation is required.

CRA subjects export zero-rating claims to rigorous audit: denial of zero-rating, reassessment at the full GST rate (with any applicable BC PST/use tax exposure), interest, false-statement penalties under ETA s. 285(1) (greater of CAD 100 and 25% of net tax), gross-negligence penalties under ETA s. 285.1 (greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax), suspension or revocation of GST/HST registration, retrospective clawback of refunds and input tax credits, and prosecution for evasion under ETA s. 327(1) for fraudulent export schemes or 'carousel' transactions; reassessment periods are extended beyond four years where misrepresentation involves neglect or wilful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts, consistent with IMF Article VIII. There is no prior approval, license, or quantitative limit for dividend, interest, royalty, management fee, or intercompany remittances by a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation. Outbound payments to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), default 25%, treaty-reduced as applicable (e.g., 5% to 15% under the Canada-United States tax treaty for a ULC held by US residents). Inbound equity injections require no exchange-control permit and are not taxable. Financial institutions automatically report international electronic fund transfers exceeding C$10,000 to FINTRAC; Statistics Canada may require a direct-investment reporting return for an inbound investor where the Canadian enterprise's total assets or gross revenues exceed C$25 million in the reporting year or on written request.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export earnings from the online retail hydroponics business may be retained in CAD, USD, or foreign-currency accounts with a Schedule I bank, or held in PayPal/Stripe merchant balances, without surrender to the central bank. For income tax purposes, foreign-currency receipts must be converted into Canadian dollars at the Bank of Canada noon exchange rate on the income-recognition date. Exporting physical goods requires Customs Self-Assessment or Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) declarations to the Canada Border Services Agency at the border. Banks must report wire transfers of export proceeds of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC; FX gains and losses are taxable under section 39 of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) for monetary policy and Retail Payment Activities Act registration; Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border funds transfer reporting and AML/CFT supervision; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of federally chartered banks. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control authority; the Foreign Exchange Control Board was abolished in 1951 and no exchange-control licensing regime exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW as a primary banking solution; MEDIUM as an ancillary rails for foreign-currency payables and expedited disbursements. Canada has no EU-style EMI licence. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Revolut are classified as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and must register with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). If the EMI holds pre-funded balances for payment services, it will also require registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada once the payment-activity regime is fully enforced. A foreign EMI is prohibited from representing itself as a Canadian bank and cannot offer Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) insured chequing accounts. CAD clearing must be performed through a Schedule I correspondent bank. A newly incorporated ULC should use an EMI only to pay overseas suppliers in emerging currencies or to receive small disbursements, not as the treasury of record.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the ULC's Schedule I bank account in CAD or USD. The beneficiary field must identify the ULC legal name, account number, bank SWIFT/BIC code (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC or TD-CATICATT for TD), institution number, and branch/transit number. The remittance purpose field must state clearly: 'Equity contribution - subscription for ULC shares'. No Canadian withholding tax applies to equity contributions; however, if the shareholder contribution is structured as a shareholder loan, the bank should receive a copy of the promissory note and an arm's-length interest rate must be documented to avoid Part XIII withholding tax and recharacterization. The ULC must update its register of shareholders and beneficial-ownership schedule after every capital event. For inward transfers of C$10,000 or more, the bank files an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC. If the ULC's assets or annual revenues exceed C$25 million, an inward direct-investment report to Statistics Canada must be filed.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for vendor wires, with the purpose field referencing the commercial invoice and the nature of goods as 'hydroponics equipment and garden supplies'. High-value USD payments clear through the bank's US correspondent network, e.g., RBC Bank (Georgia), N.A., BMO Harris Bank, N.A., or TD Bank, N.A., using Fedwire or CHIPS. For new overseas suppliers requiring guarantee of payment, the Schedule I bank may issue an irrevocable documentary Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP 600, available at sight; Standby Letters of Credit under ISP98 may support performance guarantees. CAD payments between Canadian counterparties clear through Payments Canada Lynx for same-day high-value wires and ACSS for batched EFT.
