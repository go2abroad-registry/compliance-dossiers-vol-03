Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a British Columbia limited partnership; capital is fixed solely by the partnership agreement. No escrow account is required by statute. A limited partner may withdraw a capital contribution only if authorized by the partnership agreement and provided creditor claims are not prejudiced (Partnership Act s. 50(3)).

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable for a pure licensing office beyond BC Building Code occupancy compliance and a standard municipal fire inspection. If any film vault or warehouse storage is added, obtain fire safety certification under the BC Fire Code, submit a fire safety plan, complete structural load review for storage racks, and obtain a WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety clearance including egress inspection.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada); CRA My Business Account portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; administered in conjunction with the Canada Border Services Agency for export controls

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated limited partnership seeking to carry on film licensing activity in British Columbia must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing CRA Form RC1, either as a registered partnership or as a non-resident person making taxable supplies in Canada. The BN is a 9-digit account identifier to which program accounts such as the GST/HST RT account are attached. Non-resident partnerships without a permanent establishment may register directly through the CRA International Tax Services Office under the Non-Resident Registration procedure. For British Columbia, the partnership must also file a statement of registration with the BC Corporate Registry under the Partnership Act (RSBC 1996, c. 348) to obtain a BC Incorporation Number; the CRA BN application should be filed within 30 days of commencing commercial activity, and the RT program account becomes effective upon CRA issuance, generally retroactive to the earlier of the date of first taxable supply or the date of application.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - A BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)); the partnership is not a taxpayer and each member includes its share of partnership income or loss in its own return. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - A BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)); the partnership is not a taxpayer and each member includes its share of partnership income or loss in its own return. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate registrable person under ETA s. 240(1) and is the correct registrant for the RT account, notwithstanding income-tax transparency.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. Available statutory elections include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) to align the partnership's taxation year; (ii) ETA s. 272.1 election for a partnership to be treated as a registrant (or as a member of a selected listed financial institution group in bank-oriented structures); (iii) ETA Schedule VII s. 23 joint election between the film licensor and a non-resident licensee to confirm zero-rating of an intangible-property supply; (iv) hybrid-mismatch election documentation under ITA ss. 18.4 - 18.6 where a foreign member is subject to divergent entity classification; and (v) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident film-earning individuals or corporations to pay tax at reduced rates on Canadian-source film income, where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies of goods and services in Canada over any consecutive 12-month period (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148(1)); non-resident suppliers not carrying on business in Canada must nonetheless register if they make taxable supplies of intangible property (including film licences) to Canadian recipients, irrespective of the 30,000 threshold, where the supply is not a zero-rated export.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of a film copyright licence (intangible personal property) under ETA Schedule VII, Part II, s. 23 where the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and the right is acquired for use wholly outside Canada. For physical export of film masters, zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 5 if the goods are exported as soon as is reasonable after delivery and the supplier maintains proper export evidence. The supplier must demonstrate that no part of the licence relates to exhibition or use in Canada, that the licensee is a non-resident, and that any royalties or lump-sum consideration are attributable to foreign use. Digital delivery through cloud platforms is zero-rated only if the licensee's access and exploitation are contractually and factually confined to foreign territories.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA B13A Export Declaration or CAED-generated export report for physical film masters; not required for pure intangible licensing
  • Bill of Lading or House Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement (for physical media) or an electronic delivery log paired with the licence grant (for digital delivery)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire transfer advice matching the licence consideration and clearly identifying the non-resident remitter
  • Executed worldwide copyright licence agreement designating the licensee, territory, term, and limitation of use to non-Canadian markets
  • CRA records confirming the BN and GST/HST registration status, plus the foreign jurisdiction's tax residency evidence for the licensee

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API clearance regime is enacted at the federal or British Columbia level. CRA accepts electronic invoices as valid supporting records provided the supplier retains legible digital reproductions meeting ETA ss. 286(1) and 286.1(2) record-keeping requirements. Voluntary use of Pan-Canadian e-invoice standards (UN/CEFACT or XRechnung-style formats) is permitted but not compulsory; CRA audits may require export of invoice files to the CRA portal in an accepted structured format.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal 4-year reassessment period, extended to 10 years where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established, and may claw back the uncollected GST together with interest under ETA s. 280. A false statement penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded applies under ETA s. 280.1, and wilful evasion is subject to summary conviction fines of up to CAD 20,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 6 months (ETA ss. 284 - 285). Exports are referred to the CRA Export Verification Program and the CBSA Border Export Controls; failure to remit foreign currency proceeds to Canada within 180 days will be treated as inconsistent with an export supply, triggering reversal of the zero-rating, restitution of GST at 5% on the gross license fee, plus arrears interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an Article VIII IMF member, so the Canadian dollar is freely convertible. Current account payments, including film licensing royalties, distribution advances, profit remittances, and payment for services, are immediately accessible through the banking system without central-bank approval or exchange-control permits. Capital account transactions, including capital contributions to a British Columbia limited partnership by non-resident limited partners and subsequent distributions, are also freely convertible; however, inbound acquisitions requiring Investment Canada Act review and the 25% Part XIII withholding tax on certain non-resident payments must be addressed. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to tax compliance: royalties paid to non-residents are normally subject to the 25% statutory withholding tax, commonly reduced by treaty, and the Canadian paying entity must withhold, remit, and report under Part XIII. Practically, high-risk jurisdictions may require enhanced economic-substance documentation and sanctions screening, but there is no statutory convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements in Canada, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline by which export receipts must be converted into Canadian dollars. A BC limited partnership receiving foreign-currency funds for motion picture licensing may retain those amounts in the original currency at a Canadian chartered bank or convert them when commercially advantageous. For tax and compliance purposes, transactions and account balances must be recorded with Canadian-dollar equivalents under standard accounting rules, and FINTRAC electronic-fund-transfer reporting applies to individual outgoing or incoming cross-border transactions of C$10,000 or more, but no surrender or forced conversion is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority. Canada does not operate a foreign exchange control or approval regime; day-to-day FX supervision is distributed among OSFI for prudential bank supervision and FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering and electronic-fund-transfer reporting. The Department of Finance administers the Investment Canada Act, which may screen certain large capital investments but does not require foreign exchange authorization. FINTRAC requires reporting of cross-border electronic fund transfers and large virtual currency and cash transactions, but does not approve specific FX payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for the primary treasury account; Medium only as a collection-side ancillary account. Canadian federally regulated banks and authorized foreign bank branches are the only institutions that may accept deposits in Canada, so an offshore digital EMI cannot legally act as the main deposit-taking bank for the BC limited partnership. An offshore EMI may be used for collection of small licensing fees if it is not holding Canadian deposits, if it is properly registered as a money services business with FINTRAC when serving Canadian customers, and if its counterparty bank supports the flows. The EMI route is unsuitable for capital escrow, high-value SWIFT settlements, documentary letters of credit, and Part XIII withholding remittance because most EMIs lack Payments Canada membership, cannot provide settlement finality through the Large Value Transfer System, and create opacity around beneficial ownership and source of funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the BC limited partnership must be routed through a Canadian Schedule I bank using SWIFT MT103 or equivalent high-value wire with the receiving account identified as capital contribution. The remittance instruction should include ISO 20022 external purpose category 'INVC' or the narrative 'limited partnership capital contribution pursuant to partnership agreement' if a detailed code menu is unavailable. The bank will require police registration or entity authorization if the payor is a corporate general partner, and Canadian banks expect proof that the contribution is not a loan or proceeds of crime. For wires of C$10,000 or more, FINTRAC receives an electronic-fund-transfer report on the outgoing side of the payor bank and the incoming Canadian bank must also comply with Part 1 of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. The LP's bank file should contain the partnership agreement, BC registration extract, source-of-funds evidence, and a signed undertaking that no contribution is made on behalf of an undisclosed third party.Outbound trade disbursements for film licensing, sub-distribution fees, production advances, and tax remittances should be executed on SWIFT MT103 through the Canadian bank's international payment platform. For USD and CAD settlements, the bank will use correspondent bank accounts in the United States and Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System for CAD. For CDN-dollar tax remittances to the CRA, the Treasury client should maintain a dedicated operational account with an electronic payment channel. Commercial letters of credit may be used when the BC limited partnership posted a distribution minimum guarantee as collateral or when the licensing counterparty requires bank settlement undertakings; however, documentary credits are not the standard rail for pure copyright-royalty transactions. For regular licensing receipts and disbursements, an MT103 with GPI tracking is appropriate, preserving the message in the bank's file for audit and proof of payment. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal (15%) + British Columbia general corporate (12%) on allocable income of a corporate partner; the limited partnership itself is flow-through.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. A true limited partnership distribution is not a dividend; the LP allocates income to partners, so the relevant withholding is on allocated taxable income and on specific Part XIII payments (royalties, interest, management fees) made through the partnership.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is expected under s.247(4) for any material cross-border related-party transaction; the practical penalty-protection threshold is C$1,000,000 in aggregate annual related-party goods/services/intangibles transactions (with a lower de-minimis expectation for small partnerships).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under s.245 of the Income Tax Act can disregard arrangements lacking business purpose, and CRA applies substance-over-form for sham transactions. An independent film licensing LP must demonstrate real Canadian management, partnering substance, and arm's-length royalty rates; a non-resident-controlled LP with no Canadian footprint is vulnerable to GAAR and treaty-benefit denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – false declarations or wilful under-reporting

Statutory sanction: Under s.239(1) of the Income Tax Act, an evader is liable on summary conviction to a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, and/or imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; on indictment, to a fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) is 50% of the misreported amount.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner may be assessed personally under s.227.1 for unremitted Part XIII withholdings and can be party to a s.239 prosecution if they directed a false filing. A conviction may lead to a passport surrender/travel restriction as part of a release/bail condition, with a permanent criminal record and restitution orders.
Transfer Pricing and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing reassessment plus interest; s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds the applicable threshold and no reasonable effort was made); additionally, a CRA information request can trigger late or incomplete documentation penalties under s.162(7) and exceptional audit costs.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately participate in covering up a BC limited partnership's transfer-pricing documents while managing the general partner may face civil liability as de facto directors of the partnership responsibilities; a director's clean corporate veil does not override fraudulent mispricing where the participant signs certificates of accuracy or underlies financial statements.
Shell company / economic substance deficiency

Statutory sanction: CRA may treat the arrangement as a sham and apply the GAAR under ITA s.245; all treaty benefits can be denied, Part XIII tax becomes chargeable on gross distributions, and failure to file a Canadian return may be prosecuted. The BC Registrar may also dissolve the LP for not filing annual reports, causing loss of legal existence.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner's directors are personally responsible for tax withholdings only if they fail to exercise diligence; for the limited partners, participating in the business or management of the LP removes the liability shield under BC Partnership Act R.S.B.C. 1996, c.348, creating unlimited joint liability for partnership debts, including tax debts.
AML / FINTRAC and economic sanctions

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) imposes monetary penalties for reporting failure: administrative fines up to C$500,000 for corporations, and the Department of Finance Canada can freeze funds under economic sanctions. In a criminal money-laundering prosecution, imprisonment up to 10 years is possible under the Criminal Code s.462.31.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be personally prosecuted if they knowingly or recklessly fail to implement an AML compliance program; directors of the general partner can face criminal liability for the LP's omission and be barred from serving as directors; a travel ban may follow when charged as part of the bail conditions.
