Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of CAD on both current and capital account. Inbound capital contributions to a British Columbia limited partnership (BC LP) require no exchange-control authorization; profit and capital remittance to non-resident partners is likewise unrestricted under Canadian FX law. The only frictions are tax compliance: a non-resident partner is subject to Part XIII withholding on non-business amounts paid or credited (generally 25%, reduced by applicable income tax treaty) and to Part I income tax on Canada-source business income allocated through the LP, unless a Section 216 election or treaty relief is claimed. Repatriation of contributed capital is not taxable. Under the Investment Canada Act, a greenfield BC LP capital injection is exempt from notification, but acquisition of control of an existing Canadian business above the prescribed WTO-investor threshold requires an Innovation, Science and Economic Development (ISED) filing. FATCA/CRS self-certification (Form W-8BEN-E or equivalent) and a Canadian NR301 may be required by the receiving bank. No prior approval from the Bank of Canada or the Minister of Finance is needed for any remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Export earnings and cross-border service receipts (e.g., Scope 1-3 carbon audit fees or offset brokerage commissions from US/offshore clients) may be retained in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency and may be held in foreign-currency bank accounts with Schedule I banks or cleared via SWIFT. The only obligations are informational: FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting (EFTR) applies to international wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and large-cash transaction reports apply to cash receipts above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and fiscal agent for the Exchange Fund Account; prudential bank supervision is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); deposit-taking is authorized solely under the federal Bank Act. Canada maintains no foreign-exchange-control statute and the Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely without any requirement for central-bank approval, licensing, or registration for current or capital account payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for small-value payment disbursement and operational FX; Low for primary treasury custody. Offshore electronic money institutions (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, or similar) are not authorized to take deposits in Canada: deposit-taking is reserved for banks and credit unions under the Bank Act and provincial financial institution statutes. These entities may lawfully provide payment services only if they are registered as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, where applicable, hold a provincial MSB licence in British Columbia. A BC LP may use an EMI for batches below CAD 25,000 (vendor invoices, auditor reimbursements, or registry payments), but the EMIs funds held in an EMI pool account are not deposit-insured and expose the LP to credit risk. The compliance blueprint requires that a minimum of 80% of LP liquidity sit in a Schedule I bank or a BC federal/provincial credit union account, with all principal capital contributions routed via SWIFT MT103 rather than an EMI.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the LP level (fiscally transparent pass-through); a general corporate taxpayer in BC pays 27% combined (federal 15% + BC 12%). Each LP partner is taxed at their own federal/provincial personal or corporate rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII dividend withholding rate; for LP distributions to non-resident partners, the advance Part XIII withholding on allocated Canadian-source business income is 15%, creditable against the partner's Part I liability. Treaty-reduced rates may apply: 5% for corporate partners holding at least 10% voting rights under Canada-US, Canada-UK and Canada-China treaties; 15% in most other cases.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No de minimis statutory threshold exists in Canada. The CRA requires contemporaneous documentation for all controlled cross-border transactions under ITA s.247, regardless of size, to support 'reasonable efforts' to determine arm's-length prices. The OECD-aligned Country-by-Country Report applies to groups with consolidated annual revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more. Local and Master File documentation is expected administratively for significant intercompany transactions (practical threshold is any controlled transaction exceeding CAD 1,000,000, although not codified).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations; however, the GAAR (ITA s.245) applies substance-over-form to deny tax benefits of avoidance transactions. For LPs, CRA will scrutinize whether the partnership carries on real business activity in BC (employees, office, commercial contracts, capital at risk). Treaty benefits require the partner to be the beneficial owner, and the anti-treaty-shopping provisions (including LOB clauses in Canada's DTAs) will deny relief for shell or conduit structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Gross Negligence

Statutory sanction: Civil: gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax, plus arrears interest. Criminal: ITA s.239 — fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; additional conviction for fraud under Criminal Code s.380 (over CAD 5,000) carries imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally and unlimitedly liable for the LP's tax debts under the BC Partnership Act; CRA may apply s.226 for a travel ban where serious evasion is established; directors may be subject to criminal prosecution and civil restitution for funds misappropriated.
Part XIII / Non-Resident Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: The LP is jointly and severally liable for the unreported 25% Part XIII tax plus daily prescribed interest and a 10% penalty on unremitted amounts (ITA s.227(10)); gross negligence increases the penalty to 50% under s.163(2). Failure to file NR4 slips triggers s.162(7) penalties of CAD 10/day per slip to a CAD 100 maximum per slip.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted withholding under ITA s.227.1; CRA assesses them directly and may seize personal assets; the liability is not dischargeable in bankruptcy or CCAA restructuring.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and reapplication of the 25% Part XIII tax; GAAR re-characterization under ITA s.245 with denial of the tax benefit plus a penalty of 25% of the benefit (50% for avoidance transactions subject to the post-2024 GAAR amendments); additional interest accrues from the original filing due date.

Director personal liability: CRA may assess the controlling partners personally on the basis that the LP is a sham; directors face enhanced penalties and reputational disqualification; criminal sanctions are available for wilful participation in a sham arrangement under ITA s.239.
Money Laundering / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the PCMLTFA, administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation/partnership and CAD 500,000 for an individual; criminal penalties for non-compliance with reporting or registration obligations include fines and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly direct, authorize or participate in AML non-compliance are subject to personal fines and imprisonment; FINTRAC may issue a compliance order requiring suspension of the LP's operations until remediation.
Greenwashing / Unsubstantiated ESG Claims

Statutory sanction: Under the Competition Act s.74.01(1.1), administrative monetary penalties up to the greater of CAD 10,000,000 and three times the benefit derived from the representation (or 3% of the person's worldwide gross revenue, whichever is greater); CRA may also disallow fees derived from fraudulent verification work as non-arm's-length income.

Director personal liability: Directors and professional auditors face suspension or revocation of professional credentials under BC accounting/auditing oversight bodies; criminal fraud charges under Criminal Code s.380 (imprisonment up to 14 years) may apply where knowingly false reports are used to deceive clients or regulators.
