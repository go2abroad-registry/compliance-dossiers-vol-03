Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

CAD 30,000 - ETA small-supplier annual threshold (worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters, excluding supply of financial services and capital assets of the business). Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may apply 0% GST under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 of the Excise Tax Act to royalties/licence fees for video-game copyright supplied to non-resident, non-GST-registered recipients (Valve/Steam, PlayStation/Sony Interactive Entertainment) where the intangible property is exploited wholly outside Canada (use-enjoyment test; markets outside Canada). Royalties attributable to Canadian end-user downloads are taxable at 5% GST and will normally be collected and remitted by the platform operator under the digital-economy deemed-supply rules, avoiding double collection. Offshore publishing, localization, or IT-support services to non-residents may qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 or Part VII where the recipient is a non-resident and no service is performed in Canada for Canadian enjoyment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13 or ACI/eManifest) - required ONLY for physical game media/disc exports; not applicable to digital downloads transferred via Steam/PlayStation networks
  • Valve Steam publisher payout reports and Sony PlayStation Store royalty statements showing title, contract period, territorial sales, and net remittable amounts
  • Executed publishing and licence agreements with Valve Corporation, Warner Bros./PlayStation, or other non-resident platform operators delineating territorial rights and royalty basis
  • Commercial invoices or digital delivery memos evidencing the electronic transfer of the game code/IP to foreign licensees
  • Inward remittance and wire-transfer records (bank statements, FX conversion reports) proving non-resident payment and CAD settlement
  • GST/HST returns and supporting working papers reconciling the zero-rated portion declared on each period's return (CRA GST/HST Memorandum Series 8-3 documentation standards)

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, digital stamping or CRA API pre-approval exists at federal or BC level. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records (PDF, EDI, JSON/XML, PEPPOL/Factur-X where voluntarily implemented); the 6-year retention rule under ETA s. 286(1) applies. Invoices issued by the LP must show the BN/RT account, date, supplier and recipient identity, and GST charged; Steam and PlayStation payout statements constitute acceptable underlying records for audit.

CRA audits apply the 'place of use' and 'principal purpose' tests; any retail sale downloaded by a Canadian consumer that was improperly zero-rated triggers reassessment of 5% GST plus 7% BC PST on the Canadian portion. Penalties: gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 285 (up to 25% of net tax) and ITA s. 163(2) (up to 50% of the tax), compounded daily interest under ETA s. 280, and removal of the normal 4-year reassessment window under ETA s. 298(7) where misrepresentation is established. Deliberate fraud exposes partners to joint and several liability under ETA ss. 272.1/323 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under ETA s. 327 (fines of 50%-200% of evaded tax plus up to 5 years imprisonment on indictment), plus possible Steam/PlayStation platform de-listing and reputational harm.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction: the Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts, with no exchange-control approvals, surrender obligations, or repatriation restrictions for trade settlements, capital injections, dividends, interest, or partnership profit distributions. For a BC limited partnership, a non-resident partner's share of income may be freely remitted, subject to statutory Part XIII withholding tax of 25% under the Income Tax Act, treaty-reduced where applicable (e.g., Canada-US Treaty: 0-15% on interest and royalties, 5-15% on dividends). The Investment Canada Act imposes a net-benefit review only on specified acquisitions of control of a Canadian business, not on passive LP interests. The principal operational frictions are reporting and verification: cross-border currency movements exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to CBSA/FINTRAC; electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 are recorded by financial institutions; and FINTRAC's customer due diligence (full-CDR) framework requires identification and verification of every individual or entity holding 25% or more of the partnership before account opening (PCMLTFA Regulations, SOR/2002-184, as amended).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada repealed its foreign-exchange surrender regime under the Exchange Control Act in 1951. Exporters and digital-service publishers (ISIC 5820 / NAICS 511210) may retain 100% of export proceeds in CAD or foreign-currency accounts with no statutory conversion ratio, holding period, or surrender deadline. Steam (Valve Corp.) and PlayStation Store (Sony Interactive Entertainment) USD payouts may be held indefinitely in a US-dollar subsidiary ledger at a Schedule I bank or remitted to offshore accounts without Bank of Canada authorization. For GST/HST purposes, exports of digital game licences qualify as zero-rated supplies under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, Section 9, provided the partnership retains platform payout statements as export evidence; the platform operators remain responsible for GST/HST registration and collection on sales to Canadian consumers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-control administrative authority rests with the Minister of Finance, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - usable as a secondary operational rail but not a substitute for a Schedule I bank. Wise (Wise Payments Ltd., FINTRAC-registered MSB with local partner banking) and Payoneer (Payoneer Inc., FINTRAC MSB) can receive USD payout rails from Valve and Sony, convert to CAD, and support Steam ACH and PlayStation B2B disbursements; both are acceptable for vendor payments and freelance royalties. Legal constraints: (a) neither is an OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institution, so balances are not CDIC-insured and must be swept to the primary bank; (b) offshore EU/UK EMI e-money licences do not confer membership in Payments Canada's ACSS or Lynx clearing systems, preventing same-day domestic CAD clearing and SWIFT domestic branch routing; (c) Schedule I banks will require disclosure of all payment-processor accounts as linked entities at onboarding; and (d) the partnership's GST/HST and export-evidence trail is more robust with a Canadian bank settlement record. Do not hold capital escrow or fiduciary trust balances through EMIs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all partner capital calls via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV where intermediated) into the LP's CAD or USD account at the Schedule I bank, using: Field 50K for the ordering partner, Field 59 for the LP beneficiary with legal name and BC registration number, and Field 70 remittance narrative: 'CAPITAL CALL NO. [x] per LP Agreement dated [yyyy-mm-dd]; BC LP Registration No. [number]; CRA BN [number]; UBO [name]'. Use ISO 20022 External Purpose Code 'INTC' (intercompany payment) or 'INVE' (investment) as mapped by the receiving bank's online purpose-code menu; never use gift, salary, or personal-transfer codes.
