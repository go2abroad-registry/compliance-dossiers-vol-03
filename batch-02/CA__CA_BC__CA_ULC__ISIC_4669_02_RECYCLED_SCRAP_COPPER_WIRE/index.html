Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – Federal tax administration and GST/HST registration; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch – BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration and administration

E-government filing portal

Resident and non-resident entities must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or by using the Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The BN is the statutory TIN. A GST/HST program account is created under the same BN by indicating the registrant's taxable supply activity. For non-residents carrying on business in Canada, GST/HST registration is mandatory if they make taxable supplies in Canada and are not a small supplier. For BC PST, a separate registration is filed with the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, before making any taxable retail sales in British Columbia. Foreign entities may appoint an authorized representative through CRA Authorized Representative functionality.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation under the Canadian Income Tax Act (a BC ULC is a resident corporation subject to federal Part I and provincial Part A corporate income tax). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation under the Canadian Income Tax Act (a BC ULC is a resident corporation subject to federal Part I and provincial Part A corporate income tax). Pass_Through_Eligible for U.S. federal income tax purposes through an IRS check-the-box election, but this election is disregarded for Canadian tax characterization.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a domestic check-the-box election; a BC ULC is conclusively a taxable corporation for Canadian purposes. U.S. owners may file IRS Form 8832 to elect disregarded entity or partnership status for U.S. tax. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act Part XVI.1, sections 18.4–18.6 and 247.01–247.13) apply to deny double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes for hybrid instruments and entities. Statutory elections available for Canadian corporate tax include group loss-transfer elections (Section 256.1), amalgamation rollovers (Section 87), and winding-up elections (Section 88), but none alter the ULC's opaque corporate classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CA$30,000 in total world-wide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies for small-supplier threshold) in any single calendar quarter, or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters, triggers mandatory registration. BC PST: no small-supplier exemption; any person making taxable sales or leases in British Columbia must register and collect PST before the first such transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of tangible personal property, including recycled scrap copper wire, under Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, section 1, provided the supplier sells the goods to a purchaser and ships or delivers them directly out of Canada pursuant to the contract of sale, or supplies the goods by way of sale to a non-resident purchaser and causes them to be exported within a reasonable time. The reseller must retain evidence that the goods were physically delivered outside Canada, such as proof that risk of loss passed to a non-resident buyer at a Canadian clearance point under Incoterms such as FOB, FCA, or CIF. Recycled copper scrap qualifies as zero-rated only if the goods are tangible personal property and all applicable anti-dumping/export-control documentation is in order. Services, including freight, customs brokerage, and commission agency, are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, sections 7 and 22 only when performed for a non-resident and in connection with tangible personal property exported from Canada, provided the services are not part of a supply of a service to a resident Canadian.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A) or automated export report submitted to Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) where the shipment meets threshold or control-list reporting requirements
  • Commercial Invoice reflecting the export sale, Incoterms, and declared value of the scrap copper wire
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill of Lading evidencing physical carriage out of Canada
  • Packing List and weight/grading certificate for the scrap copper wire
  • Non-negotiable copy of the freight contract or carrier waybill showing final foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the non-resident purchaser
  • Contract of Sale / Purchase Order identifying the non-resident counterparty and payment terms
  • CBSA customs export confirmation or foreign customs entry showing overseas clearance

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing mandate as of the current reporting period. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information under Excise Tax Act section 3 of Schedule V; electronic invoices are accepted with the same information content. PST vendors must maintain invoices evidencing PST charged and are required to file/remit PST electronically where the annual PST collection exceeds C$1,200; GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE for registrants above the filing threshold. No blockchain or government clearance API is currently legislated for standard B2B export invoicing.

CRA may deny zero-rating if the exporter cannot provide contemporaneous documentary proof of export and payment. Upon denial, GST is reassessed at 5% on the gross sale consideration, plus daily compound arrears interest under the Income Tax Act rules applicable to GST. The Excise Tax Act imposes penalties for false statements, omissions, and wilful evasion: a 25% penalty on any understated net tax may apply, and for gross negligence, a penalty of up to 50% of the amount of GST sought to be evaded is possible under section 285.1. Where evasion is established, criminal prosecution may result in fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment. BC PST is also unaffected by export zero-rating; PST registration obligations continue unless the scrap copper is proven to be shipped outside BC, and PST audit penalties apply for failure to produce exemption certificates. CRA audit scrutiny is elevated for scrap-metal exporters under the CBSA lookback program, and clawback assessments may be issued for prior tax years with no statute-barred protection where misrepresentation is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada adheres to IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no licensing, quota, or approval requirement on non-resident capital flows. A British Columbia ULC may receive foreign equity injections, pay trade obligations, and remit dividends, interest, redemption proceeds, or liquidation distributions to non-resident shareholders without central bank consent. The only practical frictions are documentary and fiscal: FINTRAC-mandated source-of-funds verification; portfolio or direct-investment notifications under the Investment Canada Act (if thresholds are met, which is unlikely for this trading scope); and Canadian withholding/reporting obligations under Part XIII, T106, and the Canada-foreign income tax treaty network for outbound payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada maintains no export-proceeds surrender requirement: there is no statutory repatriation deadline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to bring export receivables into an onshore account. The BC ULC may retain USD or other export proceeds in a foreign-currency account at a Canadian Schedule I bank or with an offshore institution. For tax and GST/HST purposes, export receipts must be translated into CAD using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate; balance-of-payments statistics collected under the Corporation Returns Act are informational and do not condition the use of funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary and clearing authority, operating in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada, which manages the Exchange Fund Account under the Currency Act. There is no specialized foreign-exchange control administration; cross-border payment and conversion activity is supervised prudentially by OSFI and monitored for AML/CTF compliance by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LIMITED as a primary rail. Canada does not maintain an EU-style electronic money institution passport; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Business, Revolut B2B) may operate only as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) through bank-partner custody arrangements. They are acceptable as a supplementary channel for small-value SWIFT TT disbursements, vendor payments, and transparent USD/CAD conversion, but they cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, provide CDIC-insured balances, or support high-value trade escrow. For the ISIC 4669 recycled scrap copper model, the bank-grade trade finance and clearing rails must remain with a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II institution, while the EMI is confined to ancillary fast payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

CAD credits settle same-day via Payments Canada's Lynx high-value clearing system (successor to LVTS); USD funds clear through the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent (e.g., Fedwire or internal USD book transfer) and may remain in a USD feeder account pending trade disbursement. The bank will require pre-funding documentation: executed capital contribution resolution, source-of-funds evidence, CDD clearance, and the opening balance sheet for the escrow or operating account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (services) / CAD 2,000,000 (goods)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) but requires substantive economic presence for treaty benefits and applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to transactions lacking commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution and travel bans may apply.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess and deny treaty benefits; penalties under GAAR up to 25% of the tax benefit; potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they directed or authorized the sham arrangement; asset freezes and director bans may be imposed.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; possible criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines and imprisonment if they knowingly fail to comply with AML obligations; regulatory bans from serving as directors may be imposed.
