Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — national tax administration; registered through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) portals; GST/HST program account administered under the Excise Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership is required to file a GST/HST registration application to obtain a 15-digit Business Number (BN) with an 'RT' program account for GST/HST. Obtain a Request for Business Number Registration (Form RC1) or apply online through CRA's Business Registration Online; provide the LP's certificate/registration document from home jurisdiction, all partners' names and addresses, Canadian resident agent/resident representative details if required, and the date of first taxable activity in Canada. CRA issues the BN immediately or within a proscribed review period; a partnership that is a small supplier below C$30,000 may be exempt unless it makes specified supplies of digital services.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign limited partnership is required to file a GST/HST registration application to obtain a 15-digit Business Number (BN) with an 'RT' program account for GST/HST. Obtain a Request for Business Number Registration (Form RC1) or apply online through CRA's Business Registration Online; provide the LP's certificate/registration document from home jurisdiction, all partners' names and addresses, Canadian resident agent/resident representative details if required, and the date of first taxable activity in Canada. CRA issues the BN immediately or within a proscribed review period; a partnership that is a small supplier below C$30,000 may be exempt unless it makes specified supplies of digital services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate an IRC-7701-style check-the-box regime. A Canadian limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent and is not subject to entity-level Canadian income tax. For GST/HST, the partnership is a separate 'person' and may register, and a partner may be designated as its representative. Elections available include the GST/HST joint venture election under s. 273 of the Excise Tax Act and the partnership income allocation election under s. 96(3) of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST small supplier threshold; C$30,000 of taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four quarters in respect of Canadian supplies). Non-resident suppliers of digital/e-services must register once Canadian-sourced specified supplies exceed C$30,000; BC PST registration is required for non-resident vendors making taxable sales in BC with no fixed place of business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exports of goods where the goods are exported as part of a supply made in Canada and delivered to a non-Canadian destination (Schedule VI Part V, Excise Tax Act). For services, including the medical-clinic EMR SaaS subscription, a supply of a service is generally zero-rated when made to a non-resident person who is not registered under Part IX of the ETA and when the service is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property and no direct benefit of the service is enjoyed in Canada. The supplier must obtain evidence of the non-resident status of the customer and maintain records establishing the place of supply and use or consumption outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A or approved equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading or other transport document evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-exchange / bank transfer record evidencing receipt of settled proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice / zero-rated tax invoice with CRA Business Number
  • For digital and SaaS exports: definitive time-stamped electronic service agreement, IP access/usage records, server access logs showing performance or delivery outside Canada, and Stripe_Billing / bank transfer payment reconciliation

None. Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, pre-clearance APIs, digital stamping or clearance platforms for GST/HST. Registered suppliers must issue an invoice or other prescribed documentation (BN, name, date, taxes payable, total) no later than the day the supply is made; electronic invoices are accepted if retained in legible, readable format. CRA may require records and supporting export substantiation to be made available for audit.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST return for exports within the normal four-year assessment period; the period is not limited where the return was filed on the basis of neglect, carelessness, fraud or misrepresentation. If zero-rating is denied because the supply is found to benefit a Canadian resident or to constitute a taxable Canadian supply, the supplier is liable for uncollected GST/HST plus interest and penalties. Penalties under sections 280-285 of the Excise Tax Act include failure-to-register penalties, repeated-failure penalties, false-statement penalties of 5% or 10% under ss. 285(1)/(2), and criminal tax evasion penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment for responsible directors or officers. BC PST audits may also claw back uncollected PST with interest and penalties under the Consumer Taxation Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There is no exchange control approval, registration, or license required for inbound foreign direct investment, LP capital contributions, or outbound profit remittances from British Columbia. The material friction points are (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25% on distributions to non-resident limited partners, reducible to the applicable treaty rate (e.g., 15% under the Canada-U.S. Treaty for U.S. partners), (ii) CRA reporting obligations including NR4 information returns and the T1134 partnership aggregate-investment schedule where applicable, and (iii) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for any inbound or outbound transaction of CAD 10,000 or more. Profit repatriation is otherwise unimpeded, provided distributions are documented as partnership draws rather than employment remuneration to avoid BC payroll registration and supplementary reporting obligations under the Income Tax Act and the BC Employment Standards Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Foreign-currency receipts from EMR SaaS subscription exports may be retained in any freely convertible currency or swept to a CAD operating account at the bank's discretion. The compliance layer is limited to (i) FINTRAC cross-border currency declarations for physical cash flows exceeding CAD 10,000, (ii) Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, and (iii) CRA country-by-country and partnership reporting thresholds triggered only if the LP's total assets, gross revenue, or foreign partner holdings exceed the specified limits.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Currency Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52, with prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Foreign exchange controls, where they exist for sanctions and economic measures, are administered by the Minister of Foreign Affairs pursuant to the United Nations Act and the Special Economic Measures Act; the Bank of Canada executes official intervention and sets the policy interest rate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium with strict legal limits. Canada does not maintain a standalone electronic money institution (EMI) banking class; non-bank digital payment providers (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Nuvei) are regulated as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and must be FINTRAC-registered. An offshore-domiciled EMI may lawfully serve as a payment-acquisition engine for merchant settlement of subscription fees, but it cannot function as the LP's primary treasury depository because healthcare-SaaS counterparties and privacy regulators (BC Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner) expect patient-adjacent commercial funds to sit in an insured, OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank account. Legal constraint also arises under PIPEDA and BC PIPA where domestic healthcare data and associated payment metadata must remain within Canadian jurisdiction unless the transfer is contractually protected. Recommendation: employ EMIs solely for PCI-compliant card acquiring and foreign-currency concentration, while all reserved capital, operating liquidity, payroll, and tax settlements remain in Schedule I banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound LP capital contributions should be settled via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident partner's foreign bank, cleared through a U.S. correspondent bank (e.g., JPMorgan Chase or BNY Mellon) for USD credits, or directly in CAD via the correspondent network into the Canadian Schedule I bank's clearing account, followed by settlement on Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system. The MT103 field 70 (Remittance Information) must explicitly state 'LP Capital Contribution - [BC Registry identification] - [schedule reference]' and be supported by an executed capital call delivery order. For transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, the reporting institution must submit an Electronic Funds Transfer Report to FINTRAC; for CAD 100,000 or more, the bank will ordinarily require a source-of-wealth questionnaire, underlying subscription contracts, and the partnership's audited or reviewed pro forma financial statements. While Canada does not impose purpose codes equivalent to SAFE-style regimes, the bank's economic-purpose review will map the entry to its internal 'capital subscription / partner contribution' classification, and the funds must be recorded in the LP's capital schedule and reconciled against the eventual NR4 withholding liability for non-resident partners.Outbound disbursements for the EMR SaaS operation should be segregated across two rails. Domestic operating expenses, payroll, and Canadian vendor payments use Payments Canada's ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) batch rail for low-value recurring items and Lynx RTGS for high-value same-day obligations. Cross-border supplier or distribution payments use SWIFT MT103 (customer credit) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) correspondent wires, ideally through the Canadian bank's own foreign branch or U.S. dollar clearing relationship (e.g., RBC Bank Georgia, TD Bank U.S., BMO Harris) to eliminate double foreign-exchange spread. Commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) are structurally unsuitable for recurring SaaS licensing fees; an Standby Letter of Credit (SBLC) provides a viable performance-guarantee instrument only where a clinic enterprise purchaser requires an availability assurance, and in such cases the SBLC must be issued by the Canadian Schedule I bank and advised through the clinic's bank. All treasury outflows should originate from the BC LP's own designated account - never from an EMI transit wallet - to preserve the audit trail, reconcile to the partnership's cash-flow statement, and support the bank's quarterly AML monitoring narrative.CAD/USD plain-vanilla outright forward contracts offered by Schedule I or Schedule II Canadian bank dealers, hedged against the EMR SaaS invoice rollover cycle and subscription revenue conversion.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) on CAD against USD or EUR invoice denominations - permissible but economically redundant given full currency convertibility; only advisable where a non-resident partner lacks a Canadian banking credit relationship to support a deliverable forward.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) permissible for medium-term USD/CAD liability management, provided the LP demonstrates genuine foreign-currency exposure from cross-border accounts payable and receivable rather than speculative intent; documentation must satisfy OSFI derivative dealer suitability requirements.Transactional FX swaps (tom-next rollovers) and market orders executed through OSFI-regulated counterparties for intra-quarter treasury liquidity smoothing, subject to the partnership's internal derivative authority resolution and the general partner's fiduciary sign-off.
