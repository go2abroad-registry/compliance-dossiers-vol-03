Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) via My Business Account portal; provincial registration through BC Corporate Registry (British Columbia Registry Services / OneStopBC)

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership (LP) formed under British Columbia law: (1) Register the LP with BC Corporate Registry, obtaining a registration number and statutory LP filing; (2) apply to CRA for a nine-digit Business Number (BN) associated with a partnership account; (3) open a GST/HST account (RT) if the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier, or if voluntary registration is elected; (4) where applicable, obtain an Import/Export (RM) account for customs purposes; (5) for PST collection in British Columbia, register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance under the PST Registration / BC eTax system. Foreign entities using a Canadian LP should also obtain a CRA program account for payroll (RP) only if they have Canadian employees. A non-resident LP may apply for a BN and GST/HST registration online through the CRA 'Non-Resident Registration' form or by sending Form RC4601 Canada Revenue Agency Business Number Registration. CRA generally permits a non-resident LP to elect to register for GST/HST even below the threshold, and registration makes quarterly or annual filing and GST/HST return obligations apply. The tax identity is evidenced by the BN and CRA authorization profile for the partnership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. A BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and provincial corporate income tax, and is not an eligible Canadian corporation for the deemed dividend staging in subsection 55(2) ITA. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership itself can be recognized as a person and a registrant separately from its partners, but no election exists to convert the partnership into a taxable entity. The LP may elect for GST/HST to be registered under section 240(1) of the Excise Tax Act if a small supplier. BC PST registration is mandatory once making taxable retail sales into BC; no purchase exemption election is available to a fully non-resident LP without a permanent establishment in BC for PST purposes. Where hybrid mismatch rules apply, Canadian partnerships that are not themselves taxpayer are within the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) hybrid entity framework, but Canada does not provide a statutory domestic check-the-box election under the Income Tax Act; instead, the Canadian domestic fictions of the partnership as a 'person' only apply for GST/HST, customs, and procedural tax collection rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST mandatory registration threshold: CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters, except for small suppliers; PST has no general small-supplier exemption for persons making taxable sales into BC, and registration is required before making taxable sales unless an exclusion applies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, the BC LP may zero-rate a supply as an export only if the supply qualifies under Schedule VI, Part I for goods (goods exported by or on behalf of the supplier, or supplied by way of sale to a recipient who is a non-resident and the goods are exported in a continuous journey) or under Schedule VI, Part V for services. For the production and distribution of vertical micro-drama streaming app content, the intellectual property and digital service is zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident recipient outside Canada, or to a Canadian recipient who is not a consumer of the supply while the service is for a foreign market, and the supply is not made for use in Canada. The supply of rights to stream short-form video content to a subscriber located outside Canada is zero-rated as a supply of an intangible personal right provided the purchaser is a non-resident and does not use the rights in Canada. For goods shipped out of Canada, direct or indirect export rules require proof that the goods were delivered to a carrier, forwarded via a customs export declaration, or exported within 60 days where subsection 23(1.1) applies; CRA requires records establishing that the supplier made the supply to the foreign recipient and that consideration was provided.

  • CBSA export declaration (Canada's Export Declaration, Form B13A / eManifest declaration, when required) showing the LP as exporter and the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice
  • Contract / licence / distribution agreement for the vertical micro-drama production and streaming rights
  • Bill of lading or air waybill / electronic freight records if physical media or goods are exported
  • Inward remittance certificate / foreign bank transfer records proving collection of proceeds from outside Canada
  • Settlement statements and app store sales reports (Apple App Store Connect, Google Play Console, Stripe payout reports) identifying the non-resident payor and the foreign territory
  • Any GST/HST declaration required by CRA demonstrating that the goods/services were zero-rated under Schedule VI

Canada does not mandate a national e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime. CRA requires GST/HST registrants to issue a tax invoice or sales invoice containing prescribed particulars, including supplier name and GST/HST registration number, transaction date, description, and tax calculation. There is no CRA system for electronic stamping or pre-clearance of invoices. PDF/electronic invoices are valid. CRA participates in the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and is enabling digital business-to-government integration through API-enabled 'My Business Account' and Represent a Client, but no statutory requirement exists for continuous transaction controls. For exports, CBSA eManifest and the Canadian Export Reporting System apply, requiring electronic export declarations (AES-style through the Commercial Export Reporting system) for controlled or regulated goods and for exports above prescribed thresholds. The LP must retain evidence in the format required by CRA GST/HST Memorandum 7-1; no digital stamping is required.

CRA may deny zero-rating where documentary evidence is insufficient, resulting in retrospective reassessments, GST/HST collected at the 5% rate, plus arrears from the earliest reporting period, and penalties. For fraud, misstatement, or wilful evasion, the Excise Tax Act authorizes penalties equal to between 5% and 25% of the net tax that was attempted to be evaded, with gross negligence penalties potentially reaching 50% of the federal GST portion, plus interest compounded daily and a 10-year limitation period for reassessment in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect/licence. The BC Ministry of Finance and CRA may jointly audit export transactions and impose provincial penalties under the Provincial Sales Tax Act if the export exemption is claimed without qualifying documentary proof. Criminal or quasi-criminal charges under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act or section 380 of the Income Tax Act can also arise from a false export-credit or false-export scheme; such charges expose the LP, its general partner, and controlling individuals to substantial fines, imprisonment, forfeiture of assets, and denial of a business number. In the high-risk vertical micro-drama export context, CRA expects the LP to maintain continuous, auditable records of the digital supply chain, including IP licensing, sales reports, and cross-border watertight payment rails.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no controls on current or capital account transactions, no restrictions on foreign direct investment, and no impediments to profit, dividend, or royalty remittances to non-residents. Capital and revenue transactions may be freely settled through authorized financial institutions, subject only to standard anti-money laundering, tax reporting, and sanctions screening obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender or conversion requirements on export proceeds. All export receipts, including global streaming revenue from vertical micro-dramas, may be held in foreign currency or converted to CAD at the exporter's discretion. No mandatory timeline applies for repatriation; retained foreign balances are permitted but are subject to Canadian tax transparency and Controlled Foreign Corporation rules where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank); no standalone foreign exchange administration; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) oversees federally regulated banks and financial institutions, while FINTRAC administers AML/CFT requirements

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Low for unlicensed foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs). A foreign EMI may only provide payment services to Canadian residents after obtaining registration as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, if acting as a retail payment service provider, registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. Foreign EMIs cannot accept deposits, lend, or hold settlement funds in Canada absent a recognized bank or deposit-taking institution license. For a BC Limited Partnership operating a short-drama streaming app, reliance on a foreign EMI is not a complete substitute for a Canadian banking relationship, especially for merchant settlement, treasury concentration, and tax-compliant outward remittances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require inbound investor funds to be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's Canadian dollar operating account at a tier-1 bank, with the beneficiary name matching the registered LP legal name and account number. The remittance must include a clear purposive classification, typically 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'DIRECTOR LOAN' under the Harmonized System of reporting labels used by OSFI; for statutory filings, use the Bank of Canada's balance-of-payments code 'E223' (private foreign equity investment in Canadian partnerships) or 'E268' (private foreign long-term loan) as applicable. The bank will verify the origin of funds, and any cash transaction over CAD 10,000 automatically triggers a FINTRAC large cash transaction report. Non-resident capital injections are unrestricted, but the LP should document the contributed capital in annual reporting to the BC Corporate Registry and maintain an updated reading of the Capital Account.For settlement of production costs, licensing fees, and global distribution disbursements, the standard clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 priority payments for direct wire transfers to foreign licensors, distributors, and production vendors; (b) commercial letters of credit (sight and usance) for large contract milestones with a required documentary package (contract, invoice, customs declaration, and proof of delivery of the master recording); and (c) Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system for high-value intrabank CAD transactions. In-app purchase revenues from Apple, Google Play, and Stripe are first settled to the relevant merchant account, then swept via ACH or automated clearing house to the operating account; the bank then executes outbound wires to the content acquisition network. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory audit is mandated under the BC Partnership Act, the federal Income Tax Act, or the T5013 regime for a limited partnership. A mandatory audit is triggered only when: (i) the partnership agreement or bank credit covenants require it; (ii) the LP qualifies as a large partnership (50 or more active partners) and CRA demands audited statements under subsection 233.6 of the Income Tax Act; or (iii) CRA issues an audit demand under section 231.7. The general partner may elect a CSRE 2400 review engagement in lieu of a full audit where no external covenant applies.
