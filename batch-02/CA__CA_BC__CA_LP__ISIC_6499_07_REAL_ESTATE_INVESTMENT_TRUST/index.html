Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A BC limited partnership must maintain a registered office at a physical street address in British Columbia where process may be served during normal business hours. A virtual office offering a physical street address, staffed reception, and mail-forwarding services is generally accepted by the BC Registry, but a post-office box alone is not. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

British Columbia's Partnership Act prescribes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership. Capital contributions are fixed by the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be cash, property, or services, with no statutorily mandated escrow account. Full or scheduled contributions must be supported by documentary evidence to establish capital accounts and tax basis, and a limited partner may incur statutory liability under the Partnership Act if contributions are withdrawn while the partnership is insolvent.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; national portal canada.ca/taxes; registrant self-service interface: CRA My Business Account (BizApp); GST/HST filing and BN administration administered centrally by CRA (Vancouver Tax Centre, British Columbia).

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnership obtains a nine-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; the BN is the statutory TIN for all federal tax programs. For GST/HST, file RC1 recording the RT account; registration is mandatory if either (i) taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, or (ii) the LP acquires Canadian commercial real property and cannot rely on the small-supplier exemption (non-resident deemed carrying on business per ETA s.2). The LP must also register for an RM (import/export) account and RC account if applicable. For income tax, the LP files Form T5013 (Partnership Information Return) with schedules allocating income to partners, and is not subject to entity-level filing unless it becomes a SIFT partnership; non-resident partners should obtain a Canadian BN for withholding and return purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - Canadian LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s.96; no entity-level federal tax on ordinary rental income unless the LP is characterized as a SIFT partnership (Part III.1 tax at 49.68% of non-REIT income). For an ISIC 6499 REIT business, the REIT exemption under ITA s.122.1(1) preserves pass-through treatment provided the trust/LP is a qualifying REIT (mainly earning rent from Canadian real property, no non-portfolio income, and distributed cash flow meets payout tests). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Canadian LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s.96; no entity-level federal tax on ordinary rental income unless the LP is characterized as a SIFT partnership (Part III.1 tax at 49.68% of non-REIT income). For an ISIC 6499 REIT business, the REIT exemption under ITA s.122.1(1) preserves pass-through treatment provided the trust/LP is a qualifying REIT (mainly earning rent from Canadian real property, no non-portfolio income, and distributed cash flow meets payout tests). Non-resident partners remain subject to 25% Part XIII withholding on allocated Canadian-source rental income (treaty-reduced), with a s.216 net-basis election available.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' exists in Canada. Key elections available for the LP: (1) ITA s.97(2) rollover for property contributed to the partnership; (2) ITA s.85 rollover for incorporated partners; (3) ITA s.216 election allowing non-resident partners to pay Part I tax on net rental income instead of gross Part XIII withholding; (4) ITA s.132.2 mutual fund trust and s.122.1 REIT SIFT exemption elections; (5) GST/HST elections under ETA s.156 (quick method), s.150/151 (closely related corporations/partnerships), s.191/191.1 (new residential rental rebate assignment in REIT context), and s.273 (joint election for rebates); (6) hybrid-mismatch restrictions under ITA s.18.4/18.5 (OECD BEPS Action 2) must be assessed before electing into any cross-border financing structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold under ETA s.240); mandatory registration immediately upon exceeding the threshold; a non-resident LP earning rental income from Canadian real property is deemed to carry on a business in Canada and must register regardless of the threshold where the real-property supply is commercial, or elect to register to claim input tax credits on acquisition costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated (0% GST) under ETA Sch. VI Part V; certain services supplied to non-residents (advisory, professional, financial, and similar services) are zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI Part V s.7 where the service is performed for a non-resident and the supply is not received/consumed in Canada. For a REIT LP, property-management, leasing, and maintenance services performed on Canadian land remain taxable 5% GST (and PST if applicable) because the Real Property Rule (ETA s.23) overrides the export zero-rating; only genuinely offshore services (e.g., investment-management services to a non-resident fund, international financing) can claim zero-rating. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day metric is a common commercial and documentary benchmark for the exporter of record, and CRA typically requires proof of foreign consideration within the taxation period at hand.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (electronic Form B13A filed with Canada Border Services Agency)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the LP as exporter/shipper
  • Commercial invoice and executed sale/purchase contract evidencing the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or FX conversion confirmation (wire/ACH bank reference) proving repatriation of sale proceeds to the Canadian LP account
  • Certificate of Non-Resident Status (NR301) or treaty residency certificate for the non-resident service recipient where zero-rated services are claimed

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandated continuous controls, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. Federally, GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million must file electronically (GST34 web/XML/EDI via BizApp); voluntary e-invoicing is aligned with the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PEPPOL format) but is optional. All invoices must contain the registrant's BN and be retained for six years; the CRA may demand immediate electronic access to records held outside Canada.

CRA may reassess within four years of the assessment date (unlimited in the event of misrepresentation or fraud), retroactively clawing back zero-rating if export evidence fails. Penalties: gross-negligence penalty under ETA s.285.1 equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the understated net tax or inflated rebate; late/incorrect filing penalties equal to 1% of unremitted amounts plus 25% for repeat failures; daily compound interest at prescribed CRA rates; criminal prosecution for tax fraud or willful evasion (ETA s.327/IT s.239). For a limited partnership, the general partner is jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST obligations; limited partners are generally shielded absent personal misconduct or distributions in fraud of creditors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. No foreign-exchange control authorization is required for capital contributions, debt repayments, partnership distributions, or repatriation of sale proceeds. The operative regulatory filters for a British Columbia REIT LP are: (i) Investment Canada Act review of controlling acquisitions of Canadian businesses and national-security assessments; (ii) the federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act for residential real estate, unless an exemption applies; (iii) BC foreign-buyer, speculation-vacancy, and property-transfer tax compliance; (iv) tax compliance for non-resident limited partners, including Part I tax on Canadian-source partnership income, T5013 partnership information reporting, and Part XIII withholding on specified payments such as interest, rents, royalties, and management fees, subject to treaty relief; and (v) FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for transactions of C$10,000 or more. None of these filters converts CAD convertibility into a licence-and-approval regime; capital and current account flows are freely convertible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no forced conversion ratio. Export receipts and other foreign-currency receipts may be retained and converted freely. A BC REIT LP must nevertheless include foreign-source income in its tax returns, report qualifying cross-border electronic transfers of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and comply with sanctions screening and CRS/FATCA reporting; no conversion of export proceeds to CAD is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (Canada's central bank); no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration exists in Canada. Banking and payments supervision is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and Payments Canada; anti-money-laundering and large cross-border transfer reporting is administered by FINTRAC; economic sanctions are administered by Global Affairs Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognise offshore e-money institutions (EMIs) as deposit-taking institutions. An offshore EMI may operate in Canada only through a FINTRAC-registered money-services business (MSB) and must comply with applicable provincial MSB registration and, where applicable, the Retail Payment Activities Act registration and supervision framework administered by the Bank of Canada. For a BC REIT LP, offshore EMI rails are not acceptable for investor escrow, capital calls, or core REIT distribution operations; they may be used only for non-core FX execution or incidental small-value disbursements, and even then the EMI must demonstrate FINTRAC MSB registration, clearing through a Canadian Schedule I bank, and full transactional transparency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be made to a Canadian Schedule I bank account through the SWIFT/MT103 channel for cross-border wire transfers, or through Payments Canada's Lynx large-value system for domestic Canadian-dollar transfers. The beneficiary account name must exactly match the registered BC limited partnership. The wire must name the remitter as the subscribing limited partner and include a clear purpose narrative, for example: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION FOR [PROPERTY/PORTFOLIO] — [INVESTOR NAME]'. Although Canada has no external-purpose-code plateau, the bank will require a purpose descriptor and may require the capital-call notice, subscription agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and a general-partner resolution to pass AML and sanctions screening. For non-resident investors, the bank will collect tax-residency information under FATCA/CRS and confirm that the injection does not fund a prohibited residential-property purchase; an Investment Canada Act filing or exemption analysis may be required if the contribution finances acquisition of a Canadian business above the prescribed threshold. All electronic transfers of C$10,000 or more must be recorded and, where required, reported to FINTRAC.Outbound disbursements in Canadian dollars are cleared via Payments Canada's Lynx for large-value payments or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for ACH/EFT and payroll-type payments. Cross-border foreign-currency payments are executed through SWIFT MT103 for supplier, contractor, and lender payments. For trade settlements, construction contracts, and equipment purchases, a commercial letter of credit is issued through a Canadian bank using SWIFT MT700/MT707, is governed by UCP600, and settles through the confirming or nominated bank; standby letters of credit are issued under ISP98. The REIT LP should maintain separate CAD operating and USD settlement accounts, dual-approval wire controls, and a sanction-screening process. Disbursements to non-residents for services, interest, rents, or royalties must be reviewed for Part XIII withholding and, where applicable, Form NR4 reporting and treaty-relief certifications using Forms NR301 or NR302; all third-party disbursements require invoice support and beneficial-ownership verification.Deliverable FX forwards (spot-setting and forward-date contracts) with Canadian Schedule I bank counterparties under an ISDA Master AgreementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with convertibility or liquidity restrictions not applicable to CAD/USD, where settlement in CAD equivalent is requiredCross-currency swaps (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD) to align REIT debt service and capital commitments with property-level cashflowsFX options and collar structures to protect distribution yields while preserving upside and downside toleranceFX swaps for temporary conversion of USD/EUR capital proceeds into CAD while retaining a re-conversion obligation

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognise offshore e-money institutions (EMIs) as deposit-taking institutions. An offshore EMI may operate in Canada only through a FINTRAC-registered money-services business (MSB) and must comply with applicable provincial MSB registration and, where applicable, the Retail Payment Activities Act registration and supervision framework administered by the Bank of Canada. For a BC REIT LP, offshore EMI rails are not acceptable for investor escrow, capital calls, or core REIT distribution operations; they may be used only for non-core FX execution or incidental small-value disbursements, and even then the EMI must demonstrate FINTRAC MSB registration, clearing through a Canadian Schedule I bank, and full transactional transparency.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.0% combined federal-provincial general corporate rate (15% federal Part I plus 12% British Columbia provincial corporate tax). The BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (Canada) section 96, so this rate applies at the partner level rather than to the LP itself.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source income distributions paid or credited to non-resident partners and unitholders; treaty-reduced rates (typically 5% for qualifying 10% corporate shareholders and 15% otherwise under the Canada-US DTA) apply where the beneficial owner is a treaty resident.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 (Related Party Transactions Return) is mandatory when controlled cross-border transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate market value per fiscal year; group Master File and CbC reporting obligations are engaged at CAD 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue; contemporaneous Local File documentation is expected where any controlled transaction exceeds the T106 threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) and section 247 arm's length recharacterization; although no standalone Economic Substance Regulations code exists, the CRA scrutinizes the BC LP for genuine Canadian business activity (registered office, local property management, books and records in Canada, substantive board and committee decisions). The REIT characterization additionally requires satisfaction of the SIFT regime's asset, diversification, and distribution tests; a bare conduit LP lacking substance faces GAAR annihilating the tax benefit and reclassification of distributions as fully taxable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misstatement of Revenue

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the tax evaded under ITA section 163(2); upon criminal conviction under section 239(1), fines from 50% to 200% of the amount of tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: The general partner (and every controlling director where the general partner is a corporation) is jointly and severally liable for the deficiency; CRA may pierce the corporate general partner, obtain civil restitution and asset freezing orders, impose court-ordered travel bans pending recovery, and pursue imprisonment of directing minds.
Unremitted Part XIII Withholding / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The payer becomes personally liable for the full unwithheld amount, plus a 10% penalty and arrears interest under ITA section 227; GAAR and treaty anti-abuse clauses can stack the 25% statutory withholding on an expanded assessed base.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding under section 227.1, irrespective of corporate form; civil enforcement includes director bank account seizures, exit interception via court-ordered travel bans, and denial of bankruptcy discharge for withholding debts.
Shell Limited Partnership / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA recharacterization under section 247 and GAAR under section 245 cancels the partnership status and the REIT distribution deduction, imposing Part I tax at the 27% headline rate plus Part XIII withholding on the deemed distribution base and 50% gross negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: The unlimited liability of the general partner extends through the corporate veil when the corporate general partner is a sham; CRA may pursue controlling foreign beneficiaries as de facto directors for civil restitution, impose asset freezes and travel bans, and seek criminal liability for conspiracy to evade tax.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and T106 Omissions

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment plus a 10% penalty on the net adjustment under section 247(3); T106 late filing accrues CAD 100 per day up to CAD 2,500 per return; undisclosed transactions extend the CRA reassessment window and escalate the interest base.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the pricing and failed to ensure filing face joint and several liability for the TP penalty and civil seizure of Canadian assets; CRA enforcement may restrict foreign travel and impose personal restitution orders against the approving directors.
AML / Transparency Registry Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; BC Land Owner Transparency Act violations carry additional fines, rectification orders, and a restriction on transferring the property; criminal money laundering convictions attract fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly fail to implement AML controls are personally liable for penalties and face imprisonment; enforcement tools include personal asset forfeiture, property liens, court-imposed travel bans, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Canadian entity.
