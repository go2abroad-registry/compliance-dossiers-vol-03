Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; BC Registry Services (Corporate Online) for the limited partnership charter and BC Business Number alignment.

E-government filing portal

For a British Columbia extra-provincial limited partnership: (1) file the registration of the LP with BC Registry Services via Corporate Online and obtain the BC registration number; (2) apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online (BRO), electing a GST/HST program account (RT); (3) identify the general partner as the tax representative of the partnership; (4) non-resident partners must complete applicable CRA non-resident registration forms and confirm whether the partnership carries on business in Canada (permanent establishment analysis).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: fiscally transparent for federal and provincial income tax; the LP itself does not pay income tax, but each partner is taxed on its allocable share. However, for GST/HST purposes the limited partnership is a separate 'person' with mandatory registration and separate filing obligations once the registration threshold is exceeded.

Available election forms

Income Tax Act s. 96(7) election to deem a partnership to be a corporation (requires ministerial concurrence; rarely available to operating LPs); GST/HST 'closely related' election under ETA s. 273 enabling qualifying members to treat inter-party supplies as non-supplies; voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240 before exceeding the small supplier threshold; no Canadian analogue to the US 'check-the-box' election — entity classification is governed by ITA and ETA statutory definitions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148, measured on worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter and over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; mandatory GST/HST registration is due within 29 days after the threshold is exceeded. BC PST registration is triggered at CAD 10,000 for qualifying vendors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of hotpot condiments and goods are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V where the supplier sells the goods to a non-resident and the goods are exported directly or constructively before consumption or use. Restaurant management, advisory, and supply-chain services to the overseas dining operation qualify for zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, s. 7 if performed wholly outside Canada or under s. 23 if supplied to a non-resident person not registered for GST/HST. Dining services rendered physically outside Canada are deemed supplied outside Canada (ETA s. 142) and fall entirely outside the GST/HST net. Input tax credits are recoverable only where export documentation substantiates the zero-rated status.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) CAED/G13 declaration or automated export reporting; simplified reporting may apply for goods valued under CAD 2,000.
  • Bill of Lading — original or electronic through bill of lading / air waybill showing the overseas consignee, Canadian point of origin, and foreign destination.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank or customs broker record evidencing receipt of foreign exchange or remittance of the export proceeds into a Canadian bank account.

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or CRA invoice-authorization API regime. CRA accepts electronic invoices under ETA s. 286 requirements for prescribed records. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing (Peppol network) is voluntary. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE; BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC. Neither portal performs item-level invoice clearance.

Canada imposes no statutory 180-day export FX repatriation deadline under the ITA or ETA; the 180-day benchmark is the customary substantiation window applied by CRA auditors and Canadian banks to export remittance trails. Failure to document zero-rating exposes the taxpayer to reassessment and clawback of input tax credits. Penalties include: ETA s. 285 false-statement penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded); ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; criminal liability under ITA s. 239 (fines up to 200% of evaded tax and up to 5 years imprisonment); and Customs Act false-declaration penalties on the exported goods themselves. CRA may reassess outside the normal 4-year limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency with unrestricted current and capital account flows. No exchange controls exist. Profit remittances, including dividends and partnership distributions to non-resident limited partners, are permitted without foreign exchange approval; however, they remain subject to non-resident withholding taxes under Section 212 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), and non-resident corporations may be subject to Part XIV branch tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, conversion mandate, or prescribed timeline for export proceeds. Canadian businesses may retain export receipts in foreign currency. Cross-border wire transfers and currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and related regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); policy and enforcement coordination with Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border currency reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate. Canada does not provide a standalone electronic money institution (EMI) licence. Digital cross-border payment service providers must operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC. They typically require sponsorship from a Canadian bank for settlement services and cannot offer deposit-taking capabilities. Legal constraints include FINTRAC reporting, sanctions screening, and provincial consumer protection rules. EMIs may be used as clearing rails but are not a substitute for a full corporate banking relationship.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

N/A at the BC Limited Partnership level (flow-through entity). Corporate partners are taxable at the combined federal and BC general corporate rate of 27% (federal 15% + BC 12%); BC-resident individual partners face a top combined marginal rate of 53.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Canada Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident is 25% of the gross dividend. Many DTAs reduce this to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for substantial shareholdings (normally 10% or more of voting shares). For the BC LP, profit distributions to non-resident limited partners are treated as allocations of Canadian-source business income subject to 25% Part XIII withholding, unless treaty relief reduces the rate.

If any partner is a non-resident or the fiscal period does not end on December 31, the return is due within five months after the end of the fiscal period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50,000 aggregate annual threshold for T106 filing. Canada does not statutorily prescribe a separate Local File/ Master File monetary threshold; the CRA requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all controlled cross-border transactions, and a complete documentation package is essential to avoid the 10% penalty adjustment shield.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245), the sham doctrine, and the principal purpose test in DTAs enable the CRA to deny treaty benefits where a structure lacks genuine commercial substance. For an overseas hotpot restaurant chain, the BC LP must demonstrate real economic presence: physical premises, employees, management decisions made in Canada, local bank accounts, and complete books and records to support profit attribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act (Canada), s.239(1): summary conviction fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus up to 2 years imprisonment; on indictment, fine up to CAD 200,000 and/or up to 5 years imprisonment. Civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) is 50% of understated tax; daily prescribed-rate interest applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in, or acquiesce to, the filing of false returns may be convicted personally under s.239 and under Criminal Code s.380 (fraud) with imprisonment up to 14 years. CRA may independently assess directors for unremitted withholdings and GST/HST, and travel bans can be imposed in criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abusive Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the arrangement under the sham doctrine and GAAR (s.245), reallocate income to the entity with genuine substance, deny DTA benefits, impose gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2), and add interest. Criminal referral is possible for artificial avoidance schemes.

Director personal liability: The general partner is liable for all LP obligations under the BC Partnership Act; directors of a corporate general partner who knowingly participate in a sham structure risk personal reassessments under s.160, director liability for unremitted GST/HST, and travel bans in active criminal investigations.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance on Cross-Border Condiment / IP Transactions

Statutory sanction: CRA makes a transfer-pricing adjustment to the arm's-length price; a 10% penalty under s.247(3) applies to the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue. Additional T106 late-filing penalties up to CAD 5,000 and daily interest on reassessments apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause T106 to be filed with false or misleading statements may incur personal liability under s.163.2, and can be assessed jointly and severally for penalties if the corporate veil is pierced for gross negligence.
