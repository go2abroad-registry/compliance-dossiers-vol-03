Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed; a nominal share subscription is acceptable. No escrow or capital deposit account is required, and the Corporate Registry does not monitor paid-in capital. Under BCBCA s. 51.3, shareholders of a ULC remain personally liable without limit for company obligations; common shares may be issued with no par value.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification under the British Columbia Fire Code (Division B, Sections 3.1 and 6.1) for high-piled combustible storage; structural load inspection report by a registered professional engineer for racking and flooring; WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety registration and workplace clearance under the Workers Compensation Act (RSBC 1996, c. 492); emergency egress plan and spill response drill records maintained on site.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and corporate income tax; British Columbia Ministry of Finance for Provincial Sales Tax (PST). Portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and BC eTaxBC Taxpayer Portal (https://www.gov.bc.ca/pst).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishes its Canadian tax identity by applying to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or through the CRA Non-Resident corporate registration process. The BN is used for federal corporate income tax, GST/HST (RT program account), and import/export (RM program account) purposes. No physical Canadian presence is required where the foreign entity is carrying on a commercial activity in Canada. The same BN is used for payroll and other program accounts as needed. A separate BC PST seller registration must be obtained through the BC Ministry of Finance eTaxBC portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have an IRS-style check-the-box election; a BC/AB ULC is domestically taxed as a taxable Canadian corporation. Pass-through eligibility is a foreign tax classification concept, principally available under U.S. federal tax law via Form 8832 check-the-box election where the ULC is treated as a disregarded entity or partnership. Canadian statutory elections do not permit a ULC to elect domestic passthrough treatment; hybrid mismatch rules under the OECD BEPS project may restrict deduction/timing outcomes where the ULC is fiscally transparent in another jurisdiction and opaque in Canada.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada over the preceding four calendar quarters for the small supplier exemption; a non-resident making taxable commercial activity in Canada must register once this threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration may be prudent for exporters to claim input tax credits. BC PST: registration is generally required for a vendor carrying on business in BC; no single uniform economic turnover threshold applies and remote seller nexus obligations must be assessed separately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST-registered Canadian supplier may zero-rate the export supply of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules under section 22 of the Excise Tax Act where the tangible personal property is exported from Canada as part of a commercial transaction, possession is transferred to the foreign purchaser in Canada, and the goods are delivered outside Canada within the permitted export period, generally within 60 days of the supply. Goods exported directly by the supplier for delivery to a foreign buyer may also qualify. BC PST does not apply to goods exported from BC as local commercial export. The supplier must retain documentary proof of physical export and foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through CBSA/CARM (Canadian Customs and Border Services Agency) or an approved Canada Border Export Reporting system when required by value or destination
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent contract of carriage indicating shipment from Canada to a foreign destination and naming the customer or consignee
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and proof of foreign destination or foreign purchase order evidencing the export sale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment/settlement evidence confirming receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • CRA-required proof of export package under GST/HST Memorandum 17-3, including carrier records and delivery confirmation

Canada and British Columbia do not currently impose a real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate for any sellers in this industry. GST/HST invoices must contain the prescribed statutory information under the Excise Tax Act and supporting accounting records must be retained for six years. CRA accepts electronic invoices and requires an audit-complete electronic record trail. PST invoices must show BC PST separately where applicable. No government API clearance or digital fiscal stamp is required before issuing invoices.

CRA may scrutinize export zero-rating claims and retroactively deny zero-rating where the exporter fails to maintain sufficient contemporaneous evidence of physical export, foreign destination, or settlement of proceeds. The result is a reassessment of GST on the full consideration plus statutory interest and remission assessment. Where a false claim is made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence, CRA may impose civil gross-negligence penalties up to 25% of the tax amount at issue, extend the normal reassessment period indefinitely, and pursue criminal penalties for tax evasion or willful participation in a false statement. BC PST non-compliance may separately create liability for uncollected PST, interest, and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital accounts. Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations effective December 15, 1952; no exchange allocation, pre-approval, or capital controls apply to inbound equity, outbound trade payments, dividend/ULC distributions, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance from a BC ULC to a US shareholder is unrestricted at the FX level and subject only to treaty-based tax withholding and a market-rate conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. The ULC may retain 100% of receipts in CAD, USD, EUR, or CNY accounts, onshore or offshore, indefinitely. The only residual control is the dormant wartime exchange-control power under the Bretton Woods Agreements Act, which is not in effect.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as monetary authority; the Department of Finance Canada administers the Exchange Fund Account for official reserves; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) performs prudential bank supervision; FINTRAC enforces AML/KYC/PCMLTFA compliance. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange administration agency; CAD policy is fully market-determined.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operational FX execution and small-ticket cross-border disbursements; Low as a standalone clearing rail for LC-backed petrochemical trades. Canadian law does not recognize the EU/UK 'EMI' licence; equivalent providers (Wise Business, Airwallex, Corpay, OFX, Knightsbridge) operate as Money Services Businesses regulated by FINTRAC and, in BC, licensed under the Money Services Business Act with Retail Payment Activities Act registration (effective 2024). Legal constraints: no Bank Act banking powers, no SWIFT membership, no Bank of Canada settlement account, and no authority to issue or confirm SWIFT LCs (MT700). Recommended use: multi-currency collection and outbound CNY/USD disbursements below CAD 250,000; never for capital escrow, LC collateral, or high-value commodity settlements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no express statutory de minimis threshold under ITA section 247; contemporaneous documentation is required for all reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions. In administrative practice, a full OECD-style Local File benchmark is expected when the aggregate annual value of related-party polymer transactions exceeds C$1,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue equal to or greater than C$750,000,000, triggering the need for a Master File and CbCR filing through CRA.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) equivalent to those in UAE or other finance centres. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA section 245 allows the CRA to re-characterize a transaction that lacks economic substance. For a BC ULC engaged in wholesale polymer distribution, CRA will examine actual employees, physical office or warehouse premises, board decision-making, bank accounts, contractual allocation of risk, and the business rationale for using an unlimited liability entity. A ULC that merely issues invoices and routes profits to foreign affiliates without business activities may be disregarded as a sham, leading to reinstatement of taxable income in Canada and re-characterization of cash flows as dividends or taxable benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under ITA section 239, on conviction for tax evasion, a person is liable to a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. The CRA may also impose a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax under ITA section 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, authorize, or acquiesce in false reporting may be prosecuted as parties or counsellors. Civil restitution may be sought against directors under ITA section 227.1 for unremitted source deductions; criminal proceedings may result in bail conditions, travel bans, seizure of passports, orders of restitution, and imprisonment of the directors involved.
Shell Company / Sham ULC Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The court may disregard the ULC as a sham and attribute its income directly to the foreign beneficial owners. The CRA may apply GAAR to deny the tax benefit, re-assess the income, impose Part XIII withholding on deemed dividends or benefits, and apply transfer-pricing penalties of 10% of the gross misallocation under ITA subsection 247(3).

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is available where the entity is a mere facade. Directors and controlling shareholders may be jointly and severally liable for the ULC's unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest. In fraudulent conveyance scenarios, courts may issue freezing orders, examination orders, travel bans, and orders requiring personal restitution of diverted funds.
Anti-Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing (AML/FT)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, an administrative monetary penalty can be imposed, up to C$2,000,000 for non-compliance. Criminal laundering of proceeds under Criminal Code section 462.31 is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment for up to ten years and fines at the court's discretion.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly direct or knowingly fail to prevent money-laundering transactions can be charged as parties to the offence. FINTRAC compliance orders may name officers personally. Convictions may trigger criminal asset forfeiture, bail conditions, travel bans, and imprisonment, in addition to civil claims by counterparties for participation in illicit flows.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Violation

Statutory sanction: The CRA may re-compute taxable income under ITA subsection 247(2) using the arm's-length principle. A penalty under ITA subsection 247(3) of 10% of the gross misallocation applies where the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to determine and use arm's-length pricing. Late or false T106 disclosure adds C$100 per month up to C$2,500, and false statements can raise gross-negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally authorize unsupported intercompany pricing or who destroy records may be found personally liable for penalties in a culpable-conduct audit. Criminal liability may arise for obstruction under the Criminal Code or for false statements to a public official, leading to fines, restitution orders, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
