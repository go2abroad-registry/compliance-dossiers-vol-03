Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; British Columbia Ministry of Finance – provincial sales tax (PST). Primary portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); BC provincial registration via BC OneStop (https://www.onestop.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

For a BC-incorporated ULC, the incorporation process with the BC Corporate Registry transfers the company data to the CRA, which automatically issues a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and a separate denominator for each CRA program account (e.g., GST/HST, corporation income tax, payroll). The GST/HST account becomes mandatory once taxable sales exceed the small supplier threshold or immediately for non-resident vendors carrying on business in Canada. For non-resident foreign entities without a Canadian legal presence, registration is completed through CRA Form RC4601 (Application for Non-Resident Registration) or through the Non-Resident Registration service available via My Business Account. The BC PST account must be separately obtained through BC OneStop if the ULC makes taxable retail sales in BC, including online sales to BC residents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists; a ULC is a domestic corporation subject to entity-level taxation under the Income Tax Act (Canada). For a BC ULC with US shareholders, the shareholders may elect under US tax law (Treasury Regulation §301.7701-3) to classify the ULC as a disregarded entity, creating a hybrid entity. In Canada, such an entity is treated as a corporation. The ULC may also elect under section 4611 of the Income Tax Regulations in limited cross-border planning contexts. No statutory hybrid mismatch election exists, but sections 18.4 and 20.9 of the Income Tax Act and the OECD hybrid rules apply to prevent deduction/non-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters (excluding zero-rated exports); once exceeded, mandatory registration. BC PST: no small-supplier threshold for retail sellers with a physical or economic presence in BC; online marketplace sellers are required to register if making taxable sales to BC consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of automotive parts is zero-rated for GST/HST when the goods are exported or delivered outside Canada by the supplier as part of the sale, as provided under section 16 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The supplier must be the exporter (or acting as principal), the recipient must be a non-resident outside Canada, and the goods must be exported 'in the regular course of business' as a commercial transaction. Services are zero-rated only if performed for a non-resident and related to goods exported, and must not be performed in Canada for a resident. The zero-rating will be denied unless proper documentary evidence of export is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form A/D or Canada Export Reporting Program – CAED) for shipments requiring export reporting, or the standard commercial export documentation retained for parcel shipments
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill / courier consignment note evidencing physical delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer / foreign-exchange documentation evidencing settlement from the non-resident buyer

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, electronic stamping, or a national e-invoicing system. The CRA accepts electronic invoicing and records. GST/HST returns must be filed and remitted through the CRA's My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE, using approved XML or web-forms. BC PST returns are filed through BC OneStop or the PST portal. No API-based transaction-by-transaction clearance is required.

If export documentation is incomplete or non-contemporaneous, CRA may reassess the zero-rated supply at the full GST rate. Reassessments may be made within four years of the date of notification, extendable to seven years or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act, knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence making false statements or omissions attracts a penalty of up to CAD 25,000 plus an amount equal to 100% of the net tax understated. Gross-negligence penalties under subsection 280(2) may also apply. BC PST false reporting penalties may reach up to 200% of the tax evaded. Interest under prescribed rates is recoverable from the date the tax was originally collectible.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital accounts under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no applicable Canadian foreign exchange controls, no residual surrender obligations, and no central bank approval requirements for inbound or outbound commercial payments. Profit remittance from a British Columbia ULC to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted; however, dividends are subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax under section 212 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), and a BC ULC triggers deemed-dividend characterization under subsection 84.1(1) upon certain share redemptions, wind-ups, or capital reductions, with reduced rates only where an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada-U.S. Treaty) provides relief. The capital account is open for equity injections and direct investment under the Investment Canada Act thresholds; no reporting to the Bank of Canada is required beyond standard FINTRAC EFT reporting for transactions of C$10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to surrender export proceeds for foreign currency receipts. A BC-resident exporter may retain proceeds in USD or other foreign currency accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks without forced conversion. The only binding compliance relates to FINTRAC reporting of cross-border EFTs equal to or exceeding C$10,000 within a 24-hour window, and the preparation of commercial documents (invoices, bills of lading, customs entry summaries) evidencing trade transactions. Export transactions must be assigned the appropriate Statistics Canada balance-of-payments purpose code for online merchandise retailing, and any sample or warranty related flows must be separately documented to avoid currency misclassification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in cooperation with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange administration is also subject to the Currency Act, the Exchange Fund Act, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI services without a Canadian money services business (MSB) registration have low standalone feasibility for a BC treasury entity because the PCMLTFA requires FINTRAC registration, and Canadian payments system access is limited to banks and regulated payment service providers. A hybrid model is feasible: use licensed third-party processors (Stripe, PayPal, Wise Payments Ltd. operating as a FINTRAC-registered MSB) solely as merchant acceptance settlement rails that sweep daily into a Schedule I bank account. Feasibility rating: Medium, subject to the EMI maintaining a Canadian branch presence and a dual settlement instruction to the primary domestic bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory T106 (T2 Schedule 20) filing when the aggregate value of transactions with each non-arm's length non-resident exceeds C$1,000,000 per year. CRA may require full contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation and Local/Master-file-equivalent support in large audits; country-by-country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of C$750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose annual Economic Substance Regulations filing akin to offshore centres, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245) applies to deny tax advantages from transactions lacking business purpose and substantive economic activity. A BC ULC deriving online retail income must maintain real Canadian employees, inventory, purchasing decisions, logistics management, and risk-bearing functions in BC; otherwise CRA can recharacterize income and treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239(1), tax evasion penalties include a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, or both; summary conviction may carry a C$1,000 minimum to C$25,000 maximum fine plus up to 2 years imprisonment. CRA may reassess beyond the normal 3-year period where misrepresentation is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted withheld amounts. Because a BC ULC shareholder has unlimited liability, shareholders may be personally liable for the corporation's unpaid CRA debts and restitution; travel bars, asset injunctions, and extradition can follow indictable convictions.
