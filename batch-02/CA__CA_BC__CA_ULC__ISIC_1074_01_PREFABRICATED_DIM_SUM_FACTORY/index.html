Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration portal: 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO); BC provincial corporate income tax is administered by the CRA under the Canada-British Columbia tax collection agreement; BC corporate and provincial registrations are handled by BC Registries and Online Services (Corporate Online); food-processing establishment licensing is jointly regulated by the Canadian Food Inspection Agency and the BC Centre for Disease Control.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the ULC under the BC Business Corporations Act via Corporate Online to obtain the BC incorporation number. Step 2 — Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA's BRO service or by filing Form RC1 (Request for Business Number Registration), selecting the corporation income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts. Step 3 — Register the ULC for GST/HST; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits even below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. Step 4 — File the T2 corporate income tax return within six months of the fiscal year-end; a BC ULC is a Canadian resident corporation by virtue of incorporation in Canada, notwithstanding unlimited shareholder liability. Step 5 — Register with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST if making taxable retail sales of prepared meals or taxable goods in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under Canadian domestic income tax law the ULC is a corporation subject to entity-level federal and BC corporate income tax (no Canadian check-the-box or partnership election exists); the pass-through eligibility arises exclusively under U.S. Treasury Regulation § 301.7701-3, which permits a Canadian ULC to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes via an IRS Form 8832 election, enabling hybrid cross-border structures.

Available election forms

IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election for U.S. classification of the ULC; Income Tax Act (Canada) elections: section 85 rollover on transfers of property to the corporation, section 85.1 share-for-share exchange, section 93(1) election regarding foreign affiliate distributions, and subsection 256(9) loss-streaming elections on acquisitions of control; GST/HST elections: section 156 (supplies between closely related corporations deemed for nil consideration), section 167 (transfer of a business), and the Quick Method election for registrants with annual taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000; for cross-border reorganizations, section 88.1 of the Income Tax Act deems a dividend when ULC shares are converted or the ULC is wound up, and mitigation requires Canada-U.S. Treaty Article IV resident-entity planning; BC does not provide a provincial check-the-box regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST small-supplier threshold: mandatory registration is triggered when the person's worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports, other than supplies by charities and certain financial services) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is subject to the same threshold; the BC PST requires registration at a CAD 0 threshold for any vendor making taxable sales in BC, with separate rules for casual and out-of-province sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 of the Excise Tax Act zero-rates the supply of tangible personal property (frozen dim sum and dumplings) where the supplier exports the goods by commercial conveyance as part of the supply to a foreign destination. The supplier must retain prescribed evidence of export, normally within a reasonable time after the supply, per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17-4 (Export of Goods); acceptable proof includes a customs export declaration, through bill of lading or airway bill, freight/insurance contracts, and carrier certificates. Qualifying services performed on the exported goods (e.g., freezing, packing, labelling) are zero-rated under Schedule VI section 6, and services supplied to a non-resident recipient qualify under section 7 where the recipient is not registered for GST/HST and the service is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Zero-rated exports are taxable supplies and therefore count toward the CAD 30,000 mandatory registration threshold; the ULC claims input tax credits on production inputs and charges 0% GST on export sales.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed with CBSA (ACI eManifest data submission or paper Form B13A) evidencing the goods departed Canada
  • Through Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued to the foreign buyer/consignee showing destination and export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank documentation (SWIFT MT103, letter of credit, or foreign-currency bank statement) evidencing payment by the overseas purchaser

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API invoice-clearance regime exists in Canada. GST/HST invoices must satisfy the prescribed information requirements under sections 169–170 of the Excise Tax Act and the GST/HST Information Requirements Regulations (seller name and BN, date of supply, description, consideration, GST payable or included, and the statement 'GST/HST included' where applicable). Electronic invoicing and digital records are expressly permitted. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol BIS 3.0) is optional and promoted by the CRA for interoperable B2B e-invoicing. Export shipments are cleared through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the ACI eManifest portal or paper Form B13A; the CRA performs post-clearance transactional audit matching rather than invoice pre-approval.

Canada does not legislate a specific 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is adopted as an internal compliance control for this dossier. CRA audits apply a substantive-evidence standard: if export documentation is not retained, the supply is reassessed as taxable at 5% GST, with arrears interest and penalties, within the normal four-year reassessment window under section 298 of the Excise Tax Act (unlimited window for misrepresentation or fraud). Civil penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act apply for false statements or omissions — the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the tax sought to be evaded (10% for repeat offenders); gross-negligence penalties under subsection 285(1); and knowing participation in false statements attracts penalties of the greater of CAD 250 and the full understated tax or overstated rebate. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may be prosecuted under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, carrying fines of 50%–200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years, together with denial of GST/HST rebates, clawback of input tax credits, and publication of the taxpayer's identity by the CRA.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized free-floating currency regime with no exchange controls since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951. Both current-account and capital-account transactions (paid-up capital injection, dividends, branch profits, royalties, interest, shareholder loans) are freely convertible in CAD/USD/EUR/CNY/HKD without Bank of Canada or Finance Canada approval. Repatriation to a non-resident parent is unlimited but triggers Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%; treaty-reduced to 5% on dividends for a US or China corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the ULC voting equity under the respective tax treaty). No capital-transaction reporting or exchange-control permit is required for profit remittance; only CRA NR4/NR6 reporting and transfer-pricing documentation apply to the frozen-food manufacturing cash flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory deadline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement on export proceeds. The dim-sum factory may retain 100% of export receipts in foreign-currency accounts at any licensed Schedule I bank. Obligations are limited to FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports (Form 257) for international wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, CBSA declaration for cross-border physical currency movements above CAD 10,000, and OSFI recordkeeping rules applicable to high-value B2B settlement flows.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). No dedicated foreign-exchange control authority exists; FX administration and AML/CFT enforcement are distributed among the Department of Finance Canada, OSFI, and FINTRAC.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and provincial (BC) headline rate: 27% (federal 15% + BC general corporate rate 12%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end; any balance of taxes payable must be paid within 2 months after the fiscal year-end for an unlimited liability company (ULC) that is not a CCPC (or within 3 months if CCPC eligible and no instalment obligations)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million aggregate annual value of reportable transactions with non-resident related parties triggers mandatory filing of Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions); CRA requires rigorous contemporaneous documentation for any transaction above this threshold. Canada does not legally prescribe separate Local File / Master File thresholds, but a ULC meeting the OECD ALP conformity standard will typically prepare a Local File when related-party transactions exceed CAD 1 million, and a Master File is advisable when group revenue exceeds CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and a commercial substance test under Part XVI.1; no separate Economic Substance Regulations exist, but an offshore or non-resident-owned ULC must maintain real offices, employees, operational decisions, and risk management in Canada; absence of substance may lead to GAAR recharacterisation, withholding on deemed dividends, or the CRA attacking 'artificial transactions' under section 245

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239 of the Income Tax Act; fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil reassessment and interest, plus penalties up to 50% of additional tax assessed under s.163(2)

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable if they approved or acquiesced in the false return; CRA may assert civil restitution, obtain travel bans under the Civil Remedies for Foreign Corruption, and pursue personal bankruptcy through negligence claims
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterisation of the structure under s.245; disallowance of expenses; withholding tax on all distributions; Part IV tax (33 1/3%) on non-eligible dividends in certain non-arm's length case; CRA can also impose transfer pricing penalty of 10% of gross adjustment (section 247)

Director personal liability: Directors exposed to liability for unremitted withholding tax (up to 100% of amount due) and for gross negligence penalties; court may issue a management or travel prohibition, and personal assets can be seized under shareholder oppression remedies
Failure to Withhold and Remit Dividend WHT

Statutory sanction: Non-deductible penalty of 10% of remittance deficiency (20% if deliberate); daily interest persists; criminal charge possible under s.238

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the full unremitted amount plus interest and penalties; CRA may seize personal and trust assets without court order after liability certification; travel ban under the Federal Court may prohibit departure
Inaccurate Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment by CRA plus penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment (s.247(3)); also recharacterisation of transactions; interest and late payment penalties apply from the original filing date

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for deficient corporate tax due if they signed false certificates or transferred funds abroad while tax was unpaid; CRA can pierce the ULC's corporate veil only if the entity is a 'sham' or used for fraudulent credit cause; unlimited liability nature of the ULC directly exposes shareholder-directors of the underlying business to creditors and, in extreme cases, to tax authority claims
Late Filing or Failure to File T2 Return

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months (maximum 17%); for large corporations, penalty may be based on fixed dollar amounts; CRA may also issue an arbitrary assessment under s.152(8)

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for certain unremitted amounts (source deductions and GST), but not for unpaid corporate income tax unless the company engaged in 'tax evading' conduct; a director who causes the corporation to fail to file may face criminal prosecution if the intent is to evade
