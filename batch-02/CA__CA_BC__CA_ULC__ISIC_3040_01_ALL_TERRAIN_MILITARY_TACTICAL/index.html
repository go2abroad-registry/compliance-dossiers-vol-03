Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance – eTaxBC portal for provincial PST registration.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign investor establishing a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation must obtain the corporation's 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC4601 (Request for a Non-Resident Business Number) or by using the Business Registration Online service. The BN is the statutory TIN for all federal tax accounts, including GST/HST, import/export, and payroll program accounts. The BC ULC must also register for the British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) account through eTaxBC using the BN and supporting corporate registration details. Required supporting documentation includes the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, registered office address, director information, and any foreign registration documents. No minimum capital is required for a ULC, and non-resident ownership is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Canadian domestic law the BC/AB ULC is a corporation and is generally subject to entity-level Canadian income tax; there is no domestic CRA check-the-box regime. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the Canadian ULC may be an eligible entity permitting an election on IRS Form 8832 to classify it as a disregarded entity or a partnership. Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.4, 18.5, and 20.2, together with the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty, may restrict deduction stripping and impose withholding or income adjustments where the ULC is treated as flow-through in a foreign jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies for CRA GST/HST registration under the small-supplier threshold; BC PST registration is required for sellers with taxable sales in BC exceeding C$10,000 per calendar year, including non-resident remote suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods by the ULC are zero-rated under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V where the supplier makes a taxable supply by sale, ships or delivers the goods to a common carrier or consignee outside Canada, and retains documentary evidence that the goods physically left Canada and were not subject to use in Canada after export. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act when the recipient is outside Canada and the service is performed for use outside Canada, excluding services relating to real property situated in Canada. The zero-rating eligibility allows the manufacturer to claim full input tax credits or refunds for GST paid on production inputs used to manufacture the armored tactical vehicles.

  • Canada Customs Coding Form B13A (Export Declaration) filed with CBSA through the Single Window Initiative or Customs Self-Assessment program
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export and destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or other foreign exchange repatriation record
  • Proof of payment from the foreign buyer, including Government Direct Wire or Diplomatic Letter of Credit
  • Carrier's proof of export or foreign customs entry documentation from the destination country

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing validation APIs, or digital stamping for GST/HST. E-invoicing is voluntary and aligns with the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework / Peppol standards. CRA mandates electronic filing and remittance for GST/HST registrants above prescribed thresholds and requires taxpayers to retain prescribed records and invoices. For export and import shipments, the Canada Border Services Agency's Single Window Initiative and eManifest require electronic customs declaration data through the CBSA portal.

CRA may audit any zero-rated export transaction and deny the 0% GST/HST rate if the exporter fails to substantiate the goods were actually exported and the consideration was received from a non-resident buyer. Fraudulent or inflated export claims trigger reassessment of uncollected GST/HST, arrears interest, gross-negligence penalties, director liability, penalties of up to 50% of refunds claimed under the Excise Tax Act, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Export proceeds not repatriated within the 180-day benchmark may be treated as unsubstantiated and the zero-rating may be clawed back retrospectively.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange-rate regime under IMF Article VIII with full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There is no exchange-control law, no prior approval regime for capital movements, and no restriction on profit remittance, dividend repatriation, or loan repricing by a British Columbia ULC to its foreign shareholders. The practical hurdles for a defense-tactical entity are not currency convertibility but: (i) mandatory FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more (or foreign-currency equivalent); (ii) elevated AML/KYC classification of the ISIC 3040 / NAICS 336992 sector; (iii) sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act, the Criminal Code, and the United Nations Act. Accordingly, the ULC must evidence end-use, beneficial ownership, and source-of-wealth before rails are activated; capital-account convertibility itself is absolute and unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio in Canada. Exporters may hold, invest, or convert foreign currency freely. Nevertheless, for the armored all-terrain military tactical sector the proceeds trail is governed by: (i) the Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA) requiring valid Export Permits for controlled defense articles on Canada's Export Control List; (ii) Controlled Goods Registration under the Defence Production Act for any possession or examination of controlled goods in BC; (iii) US International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) or Export Administration Regulations (EAR) re-export authorization where US-origin components are embedded; and (iv) documentary record retention (sales contracts, commercial invoices, bills of lading, end-user certificates, and permits) subject to CBSA verification and bank compliance review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); macro-prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF transaction reporting administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); cross-border trade and defense export control shared jointly with Global Affairs Canada (Export Controls Division) and the Controlled Goods Directorate (CGD) of Public Services and Procurement Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Very Low for the defense-tactical sector. FINTRAC-registered Canadian digital money-service businesses and offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Mercury, Airwallex, Currencycloud) almost universally prohibit weapons, military equipment, and defense goods in their acceptable-use policies and decline NAICS 336992 / ISIC 3040 exposures due to sanctions and dual-use risk. As a legal matter, an offshore EMI may operate as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail only if it holds a Canadian MSB registration and direct SWIFT access; however, no major EMI currently underwrites Controlled Goods/ITAR-linked manufacturers at commercial scale. The ULC must therefore designate a tier-1 domestic bank as the primary capital and government-payment rail and use EMIs, if at all, solely for low-value FX-efficient disbursement to named and pre-screened counterparties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 wires (or, for domestic Canadian initiations, via the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Real-Time Rail correspondent) into a dedicated CAD capital-escrow account. The receiving bank requires the full 9-digit routing identity formed by the 3-digit institution number and 5-digit transit number plus the ULC account number, the legal entity name as registered in BC, and the SWIFT BIC of the beneficiary institution. The OECD/ISO 20022 Transaction Purpose Code should identify the equity injection explicitly in the remittance information field; FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reporting is triggered automatically at the CAD 10,000 threshold. The ULC must pre-clear USD-denominated injections with the relationship manager, upload the board resolution and OBO statement, and execute any FX conversion at the receiving bank's spot desk at a transparent rate. Payments from a parent in a high-risk jurisdiction require a Canadian legal opinion confirming the solvency of the ULC unlimited shareholders and the absence of circular financing or reverse-APA concerns.Clearing rails for outbound trade settlements should follow this descending preference: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wire for allied-government sub-accounts where end-user certificates are irrevocable; (ii) documentary commercial letter of credit (LC) advised or confirmed by the buyer's correspondent bank, payable at sight in USD or EUR, with presentation of clean bills of lading, defense export permits, certificate of origin, and security/classification packing clauses; (iii) standby LC or bank guarantee for advance and performance payments; (iv) LVTS/Real-Time Rail same-day CAD wires for domestic Canadian government disbursements (DND/DFO); and (v) diplomatic LC routing through SWIFT MT700/MT707 with embassy treasury authentication. Settlement by personal remittance, cryptocurrency, third-party factoring without bank approval, or netting against unrelated receivables is prohibited absent a tripartite clearing agreement.Deliverable CAD/USD plain-vanilla forwards (fixed-date or spot-deferred), documented under the ISDA 1998 FX Definitions or the bank's Master FX Agreement, with tenors of 12-24 months permitted strictly on a no-speculation basis tied to underlying export invoices.European-style USD/CAD FX options and zero-premium structures (participating forwards, collars, and seagulls) permitted when executed bilaterally with a tier-1 bank and subject to hedge-accounting and derivatives-capacity limits.Cross-currency interest rate swap (USD/CAD) for long-term project-finance or LC-discounting on defense contract receivables, requiring the ULC to satisfy the bank's derivatives eligibility (net-worth, collateral, or parent guarantee).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not market-standard and are generally unavailable for CAD because the Canadian dollar is a fully convertible G10 currency with a deep onshore deliverable market; any offshore NDF would be redundant and is not recommended.

FINTRAC-registered Canadian digital money-service businesses and offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Mercury, Airwallex, Currencycloud) almost universally prohibit weapons, military equipment, and defense goods in their acceptable-use policies and decline NAICS 336992 / ISIC 3040 exposures due to sanctions and dual-use risk. As a legal matter, an offshore EMI may operate as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail only if it holds a Canadian MSB registration and direct SWIFT access; however, no major EMI currently underwrites Controlled Goods/ITAR-linked manufacturers at commercial scale. The ULC must therefore designate a tier-1 domestic bank as the primary capital and government-payment rail and use EMIs, if at all, solely for low-value FX-efficient disbursement to named and pre-screened counterparties.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28.00% combined headline before credits: Federal Part I rate 15.00% plus British Columbia general corporate income tax rate 13.00% effective for taxation years beginning on or after January 1, 2025 (BC general rate increased from 12.00% under BC Budget 2024). The BC rate was 12.00% for taxation years ending before January 1, 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax under ITA Part XIII on dividends paid or credited by the ULC to non-resident shareholders, before treaty reduction. Under the Canada-US tax treaty (Article X): 5% where the beneficial owner is a US resident corporation that owns 10% or more of the ULC's voting stock; otherwise 15%. No statutory exemption exists for ULC dividends; dividends are not recharacterized as interest solely on account of the unlimited liability feature.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s. 247 applies to all cross-border non-arm's length transactions without a statutory de minimis threshold; however, the CRA's disclosure and penalty regime triggers at specific amounts: Schedule T106 filing is mandatory when goods transactions reach CAD 1,000,000, or services/rents/royalties reach CAD 50,000. A Country-by-Country (CbC) report is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000; the ULC must provide its ultimate parent with financial data to support such filing. For a mid-cap defense manufacturer, the operational standard is: maintain a Local File for all related-party transactions, a Master File where group-wide business data is shared, and a CbC report if applicable, all prepared before the T2 due date.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no codified Economic Substance Regulation in the style of the UAE, but applies a functional equivalent through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) - where a ULC is interposed without a bona fide business purpose, the CRA can recharacterize the arrangements and deny treaty benefits, per Copthorne Holdings v. The Queen (SCC 2011) and Alta Energy Luxembourg SARL (SCC 2021); (ii) the 'sham' doctrine requiring actual legal relationships consistent with documentation; (iii) the OECD permanent establishment threshold in Article V of the Canada-US DTA; and (iv) industry-specific substance obligations under Canada's Controlled Goods Program (CGP) - registration, physical security, and access controls at the Canadian armouring facility constitute a meaningful substance proxy. BC also mandates a transparency register under the Business Corporations Act disclosing significant controlling individuals; non-compliance with the register invites administrative dissolution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False or Deficient T2 Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil: accuracy penalty under s. 163(3) of 10% of any understated tax attributable to false entries; gross negligence penalty under s. 163.2 of 50% of the understated tax, with no defence available where the taxpayer failed to take reasonable care. Criminal: under s. 239(1) of the Income Tax Act, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment on indictment up to 5 years; Criminal Code s. 380 (fraud on the government) carries imprisonment up to 14 years where the value exceeds CAD 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for the ULC's unremitted source deductions and Part XIII taxes, even absent fraud. Where asset transfers have been made to the US parent at undervalue, s. 160 applies to render the transferee jointly liable. Critically, the BC ULC's unlimited liability structure eliminates the corporate veil: every shareholder, including the foreign parent, is personally liable for the ULC's tax debts and penalties, and the CRA may execute directly against personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Defective Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under s. 247(2), the CRA may reprice all non-arm's length transactions to an arm's-length amount, increasing taxable income. A separate transfer-pricing penalty under s. 247(3) of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment applies where the adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 50,000 or 10% of the ULC's gross revenue and the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts. Interest is compounded daily at the prescribed rate plus 4%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign and certify the T2 with knowledge of fabricated transfer prices may be assessed gross negligence penalties under s. 163.2 as penalty-eligible persons, with penalties up to 50% of the misstatement. In a ULC structure, Canadian and foreign shareholders bear personal, joint, and unlimited liability for the resulting reassessment, and CRA collection action can extend to foreign-held bank accounts through the tax treaty mutual collection assistance provisions.
