Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported finished garments is available under Part V, Schedule VI, ETA s. 1 when the registrant supplies goods in Canada and (a) ships or delivers them outside Canada as commercial cargo, (b) delivers them to a common carrier for immediate export, or (c) otherwise meets the prescribed proof-of-export rules (ETA Regulations ss. 8–10). The goods must leave Canada without further processing, transformation, or alteration, and the exporter must retain contemporaneous documentary proof. Ancillary services (freight, logistics, inspection) supplied to non-resident unregistered buyers qualify for zero-rating under Sch. VI, Part V, s. 7. Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0%, preserving full input tax credit recovery on inventory purchases, warehousing, freight, and brokerage fees.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada imposes no general exchange control legislation on current or capital account transactions. CAD is freely convertible for trade settlements, profit remittances, dividends, interest, and royalties. There are no repatriation approvals or quantitative limits on outbound stale transfers. However, reporting-based friction remains: cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); transfers exceeding CAD 100,000 may also trigger the Bank of Canada's International Transactions Reporting System (BOP-gathering). Profit remittances from a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) are not subject to Canadian withholding tax when the recipient is a resident of a jurisdiction with a Canada tax treaty, provided the ULC is treated as a corporation. A ULC electing pass-through treatment under section 216.1 of the Income Tax Act may alter the payment path, requiring careful structuring of dividends to preserve treaty benefits. Sanctions screening under the United Nations Act and the Special Economic Measures Act may delay transfers involving designated persons or jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratios into CAD. Exporters may retain export earnings in any currency and deposit them in domestic or offshore bank accounts. The only compliance requirements relate to export declaration filing under the Customs Act for goods exiting Canada, record-keeping for Canada Revenue Agency audit purposes, and FINTRAC reporting of cross-border transfer amounts. Goods exported from a BC ULC in the off-price apparel wholesale sector require an Auto-export reporting or Canadian export declaration if valued above the prescribed threshold, but no foreign exchange repatriation certificate is needed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange administration; FX policy coordination resides with the Department of Finance Canada, prudential supervision with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money laundering supervision with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible currency under IMF Article VIII.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability for domestic Canadian fintech payment service providers is High when the entity is registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and banks through a schedule I institution; such providers can offer ACH, wire initiation, and virtual account numbers for receivables concentration. Viability for offshore digital EMIs is Low. Canada does not accept European EMI passports under the Bank Act; a foreign fintech clearing CAD must separately register as a Foreign MSB with FINTRAC, maintain a Canadian presence, and establish a settlement account at a licensed Canadian financial institution. The ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) and LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) access is restricted to Canadian-regulated institutions; therefore offshore EMIs cannot clear CAD directly and may only offer prepaid card or non-settlement wallet solutions, which are inadequate for CHIPS-level wholesale liquidation flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the BC ULC should be executed via SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 purpose code 'C01' (Issue of Shares) or 'C08' (Other Equity Injection) where required by the receiving bank. For CAD convectors, the transfer will clear by wire over LVTS or by institutional internal book transfer if the shareholder banks at the same Canadian institution. A FINTRAC report is triggered at CAD 10,000; no prior regulatory approval exists for an equity injection. The ULC must deposit and file a Signed Shareholder Resolution approving the issuance, a share subscription agreement, and update the BC Corporate Registry with issued capital details if required. If the subscriber is a non-resident corporation, a certificate of identity and, if applicable, a Canada Revenue Agency new resident/entity determination for tax purposes must be secured. Verification of source of funds includes account statements demonstrating the origin of wealth and tax residency proof of the contributing shareholder.Settlement of trade disbursements for outbound purchases of finished garment inventory will primarily occur through (i) SWIFT MT103 payments via correspondent banking; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C) issued by a Canadian chartered bank and advised through the beneficiary's bank; and (iii) domestic CAD ACH debits/credits through ACSS for any local payments. For USD-denominated trades, the ULC may hold a USD operating account at the tier one bank, and disbursements can take the form of an internal USD wire to a US correspondent or a Fedwire/CHIPS clearing if the bank is a US member. All outbound wires must carry the correct purpose code, e.g., 'T101' or 'General Trade Payment', and supporting trade documents should be archived to satisfy FINTRAC and bank surveillance groups. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general rate on taxable income (federal 15% net of General Rate Reduction + British Columbia provincial 12%); 11% combined rate for the first C$500,000 of Canadian-controlled active business income under the Small Business Deduction, unavailable to a foreign-parented ULC.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII, ITA s.212(2)) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, including deemed dividends on a share redemption or wind-up.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no fixed monetary threshold for a Local File or Master File; contemporaneous documentation under s.247(4) ITA and CRA IC 87-2R is expected for every non-arm's length cross-border transaction with non-residents, and must be produced within 3 months of a CRA written demand. The s.247(3) penalty (10% of the net TP adjustment) is triggered only where the adjustment exceeds the greater of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue and the taxpayer was grossly negligent. Country-by-Country reporting and notification (Form RC4649) apply to MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of C$750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the Gulf type. Instead: (i) ITA s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) may re-characterize a ULC purely used as a loss-conduit or profit-stripper; (ii) common law sham doctrine applies; (iii) the Canada-US Treaty dividends/interest/royalty benefits require fulfilment of Art. XXIX-A LOB 'qualified person' tests; (iv) the ULC must hold real substance — Canadian warehouse space, employees making inventory and resale decisions, invoicing from BC, and actual bearing of market risk. A US parent's US check-the-box election has no effect on Canadian taxing jurisdiction; the ULC remains a deemed resident of Canada under s.250(4)(a).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / wilful understatement

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years; plus s.163(2) civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax; CRA may also invoke Criminal Code s.380 fraud where the evasion is large (imprisonment up to 14 years).

Director personal liability: Officers, directors, and agents who directed, authorized, assented to, or participated in the evasion are personally liable for the same fines and imprisonment (ITA s.242); in a BC ULC, the US parent shareholder is jointly and severally liable for the ULC's full tax debt as a matter of corporate statute.
Unremitted Part XIII withholding tax

Statutory sanction: ITA s.215(1) and s.227(8): the CRA may recover the full unwithheld amount from the ULC plus interest and a 10% penalty under s.227(9); the corporation's tax debt is a statutory liability of the ULC's directors and shareholder.

Director personal liability: Under s.227.1 ITA, each director is jointly and severally liable for the unremitted Part XIII amounts; for a ULC, the sole shareholder inherits unlimited liability, making the US parent directly suable by the CRA without a 'piercing the veil' analysis.
Transfer pricing misrepresentation and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) re-computation of arm's length price, debt, and returns; s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net TP adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the greater of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue and gross or negligent misrepresentation is found; T106 penalties up to C$10,000 per failure; interest accrues.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false T106 declarations or knowingly directed transfer-pricing schemes can be prosecuted for fraud/evasion; in a ULC, the unlimited shareholder must indemnify the CRA for any adjusted tax, penalty, and interest if the ULC cannot pay.
