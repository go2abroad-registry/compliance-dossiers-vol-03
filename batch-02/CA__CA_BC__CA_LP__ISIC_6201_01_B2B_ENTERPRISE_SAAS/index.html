Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); national portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE. BC provincial retail sales tax (PST, 7%) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC); British Columbia does not participate in the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST), so only the 5% federal GST applies in BC.

E-government filing portal

The LP must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST Program Account (account type RT). Steps: (1) register the limited partnership under the BC Partnership Act via the BC Corporate Registry (OneStop BC); (2) apply to CRA through Business Registration Online, Form RC1, or the BC registry's one-stop data transfer; (3) CRA issues the BN and activates the GST/HST RT account upon mandatory registration or election. Mandatory registration arises when worldwide taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or C$30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA small-supplier rule); voluntary registration is available under ETA s. 240(2). For GST/HST purposes the partnership is deemed a person (ETA s. 272.1), so the GST/HST account is held in the LP's own name. Non-resident LPs may register directly with CRA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The LP is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96; no entity-level federal or BC income tax is imposed on the partnership. Each partner includes its distributive share of Canadian-source income or loss in its own return; non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on their share of the LP's Canadian business income, and certain allocations may attract Part XIII withholding. The LP generally files Form T5013 (Partnership Information Return). For GST/HST, the same LP is a separate registrant and must file its own GST/HST returns, so income-tax fiscal transparency coexists with entity-level GST/HST compliance.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a check-the-box entity classification regime. Relevant statutory elections: (i) ITA s. 96(3) - a partnership that is not a Canadian partnership but carries on business through a permanent establishment in Canada may elect to be treated as a separate person resident in Canada, centralizing compliance for non-resident partners; (ii) ETA s. 273 - election among closely related registrants (including qualifying corporate partners and the LP) to treat certain supplies as made for nil consideration; (iii) ETA s. 240(2) - voluntary GST/HST registration for small-supplier LPs; (iv) the hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4) operate mandatorily without election for cross-border arrangements exploiting deduction/non-inclusion mismatches.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; no prior approval or exchange control license is required for profit remittance or capital repatriation by a British Columbia limited partnership, but distributions to non-resident partners are subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (generally 15% under the Canada-USA treaty, 25% otherwise, unless reduced by treaty).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada. Export receipts may be retained in any foreign currency; however, FINTRAC mandates reporting of all cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more per transaction, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) or Statistics Canada requires exporter declarations for certain goods (not applicable to software services).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; foreign exchange supervision administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex) are feasible as supporting payment rails for customer collections and vendor payables, but they are not licensed as Canadian Schedule I or II banks under the Bank Act. They operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and cannot provide a regulated deposit account for capital subscription proceeds or hold client trust funds. Legal constraints include Canadian anti-money-laundering (AML) rules, transaction volume caps, and the prohibition on taking deposits without a banking license.
