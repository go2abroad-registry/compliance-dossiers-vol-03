Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A non-resident ULC must first obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA by filing Form RC1 or registering online through My Business Account. The same BN is used to open a GST/HST account. If the ULC makes taxable supplies in British Columbia, it must separately register for PST with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC using the BN and the entity's legal articles. Supporting documents include the ULC articles of incorporation, unlimited liability declaration, registered office address, and names of all directors with non-resident ownership declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Canadian domestic tax law, an Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is an entity-level taxable corporation. No Canadian check-the-box election exists. For U.S. federal income tax purposes, however, the ULC is an eligible entity that can file Form 8832 to elect disregarded entity or partnership status, creating hybrid mismatch potential. Canadian anti-hybrid rules under the Income Tax Act (including sections 18.4 and 18.5) may deny deductions related to the resulting mismatch if a foreign jurisdiction treats the ULC as fiscally transparent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide gross taxable supplies, including exports, before mandatory registration as a Canadian registrant; BC PST: out-of-province or remote sellers must register if gross sales into British Columbia exceed CAD 10,000 in a 12-month period, while in-province suppliers with a physical presence must register regardless of sales volume

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST applies to tangible personal property exported from Canada by or on behalf of the supplier, provided delivery occurs outside Canada and the supplier retains documentary proof of export within a reasonable time. The zero-rated category covers cosplay costumes, prop apparel, and synthetic wigs sold to foreign customers when shipped directly from Canada to an international destination. Services performed on goods for export (e.g., tailoring, wig customization) may also be zero-rated if the tangible property is subsequently exported as part of the service. BC PST does not apply to goods delivered to a foreign destination outside Canada, provided the vendor records the out-of-province destination and evidence of shipping.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13) or automated export report through Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, or truck transport) showing foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/clearing statement evidencing final receipt of payment from the overseas buyer

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, digital stamping, or government-issued invoice authorization regime. GST/HST registrants are required to keep invoices and supporting records and to file returns electronically through CRA-approved software or CRA's GST/HST NETFILE. BC PST returns are filed online via eTaxBC; there is no electronic invoice pre-clearance obligation for PST.

The CRA conducts targeted audits of zero-rated export claims and may deny zero-rating where the supplier fails to maintain adequate export evidence. Penalties under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act apply for false or negligent statements, up to 25% of the net tax sought, in addition to interest and gross-negligence penalties. Criminal sanctions are available for fraudulent schemes involving simulated exports. Retrospective reassessments may claw back input tax credits and refunds for four years, extended to ten years for willful misrepresentation or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime, and Canada has accepted Article VIII (IMF) obligations, meaning no exchange controls apply to current or capital transactions and no central-bank preclearance is required for capital injections, trade payments, or profit remittances. For a BC ULC remitting profits to a non-resident shareholder, the only frictional cost is Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding: statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 15% under Article X of the Canada-US Tax Convention, and generally 5% where the US beneficial owner is a company owning at least 10% of the voting stock of the Canadian payor. The treasury must distinguish dividend remittances (Part XIII-visible) from return of paid-up capital through a BC Business Corporations Act share-capital reduction (Part XIII-exempt), and must recognize that although a Canadian ULC may be treated as a disregarded/disregarded-entity for US federal tax purposes, it remains a resident corporation for Canadian tax and its distributions are still characterized as Canadian-source dividends.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; the BC ULC may retain and operate foreign-currency accounts (USD, CNH, EUR) without restriction. Residual compliance obligations include: (1) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting where any international EFT of C$10,000 or more is transmitted (the financial institution files the report, but the client must provide source/proceeds detail); (2) Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) capturing export settlement flows through the clearing bank; and (3) conversion of export proceeds into Canadian dollars at the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate for CRA income-tax purposes under Income Tax Act s. 261. No statutory period requires repatriation of offshore receivables, and export invoicing in USD or any freely traded currency is permitted.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); however, the Canadian exchange-control mandate was abolished by repeal of the Exchange Control Act in 1951, and residual regulatory, licensing, and financial-crime reporting powers are administered by the Department of Finance (Minister of Finance) jointly with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viability: LOW as a primary treasury rail; MEDIUM as an ancillary settlement channel. Canadian-licensed fintech entities such as Wise Payments Canada and Payoneer operate as Foreign Money Services Businesses (FMSBs) registered with FINTRAC, not as chartered banks under the Bank Act, and therefore provide no CDIC deposit insurance, no true SWIFT MT103 correspondent trail, no Part XIII withholding capture, and no fully compliant purpose-code messaging. Such EMIs may be used only for low-value contractor payouts or virtual-card spend, while all transactions equal to or exceeding C$10,000 and all supplier settlements must be routed through a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank to maintain FINTRAC EFT-report symmetry and an audit-grade clearing trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The receiving bank will classify the credit as foreign direct investment under Statistics Canada ITRS rather than as a loan or trade receivable; no Bank of Canada approval is required. Treasury must instruct that MT103 field 71A charges be on an OUR basis so that the credited amount equals the issued share proceeds; a SHA basis can reduce received capital and inadvertently create a shareholder-advance classification. The evidentiary record comprises the share-subscription agreement, director resolution, issued share certificates, BC register update, and the bank's source-of-funds file; where the transfer is C$10,000 or more, the bank files the FINTRAC cross-border EFT report automatically.Use two complementary clearing rails.
