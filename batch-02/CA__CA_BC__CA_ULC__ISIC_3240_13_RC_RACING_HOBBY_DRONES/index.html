Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); national portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/business-account.html). British Columbia sales tax is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident ULC applying for a Canadian tax identifier must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA. Procedure: (1) Determine GST/HST registration requirement: any non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada who is not a small supplier (worldwide taxable supplies exceeding C$30,000 in the preceding four quarters or in a single quarter) must register; (2) submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or apply electronically through the Non-Resident Business Number registration service; (3) provide the foreign certificate of incorporation/continuing certificate, proof of name, registered office in BC, and a Canadian contact address for service; (4) CRA activates program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RM for customs import/export) and the BN serves as the federal TIN; (5) separately register for BC PST via eTaxBC when BC taxable sales exceed C$10,000 in the preceding 12 months, where BC assigns a PST number. If the ULC carries on a business in Canada, it must also file a T2 corporation income tax return unless exempt by treaty.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For U.S. federal tax, the BC/AB ULC is an eligible foreign entity that defaults to a disregarded entity (single member) or partnership (multi-member) unless a check-the-box election under U.S. Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3 is filed to treat it as a corporation. Under Canadian domestic law, the Income Tax Act does not permit check-the-box: the ULC is always an entity-level taxpayer for Canadian income tax. Hybrid entity treatment can give rise to Canada's hybrid mismatch provisions (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4/18.5) and U.S.-Canada treaty documentation requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 small-supplier threshold (worldwide taxable supplies); BC PST: C$10,000 small-seller threshold (taxable sales into BC in the preceding 12 months)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant ULC may apply 0% GST to the supply of goods (FPV drones) when the goods are exported by the supplier or an agent, delivered outside Canada by shipping/airway, and the supplier retains documentary evidence of export (transfer of title outside Canada) promptly after the sale. Services supplied to non-residents (e.g., engineering/offshore services) are zero-rated when the recipient is not registered for GST/HST and the service is performed wholly outside Canada or is not connected to Canadian real property. Eligibility requires a valid commercial invoice showing 'zero-rated export' and the correct arrangement for foreign transfer of consideration.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CAED) or commercial customs entry
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing Slip
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Proof of Foreign Payment (for non-resident consideration and FX repatriation)
  • Contract or export order confirming foreign consignee and Incoterms

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants with annual taxable supplies above C$1.5 million. E-invoicing through the Pan-Canadian Peppol network is voluntary. BC PST requires registration and returns electronically through eTaxBC but does not impose invoice clearance.

The zero-rated export status is self-assessed and subject to CRA audit. If documentation is missing, CRA reassesses the supply at full GST/HST (5% GST plus possibly PST impact), denies the corresponding input tax credits, and charges arrears interest. In cases of misrepresentation or fraud, the normal four-year reassessment period is waived, allowing retroactive clawbacks. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act can reach 25% of the tax sought or C$10,000 for knowingly false statements, with criminal prosecution potentially resulting in fines and imprisonment. All export records must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating currency with no general Foreign Exchange Control Act. Both capital-account and current-account transactions are freely convertible into CAD or foreign currency. Dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees may be remitted to non-resident shareholders without exchange-control approval; the substantive hurdles are treaty-based withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and PCMLTFA reporting thresholds. There is no central-bank permit requirement for profit repatriation by a BC-incorporated ULC, although the ULC's unlimited liability attributes and cross-border ownership pattern may attract enhanced AML/UBO scrutiny from the receiving bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Canada to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for holding foreign currency earned from abroad. Exporters may retain USD/EUR receivables in foreign-currency bank accounts or convert them at negotiated market rates. Banking counterparties nevertheless require an underlying source-of-funds explanation, and all international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to reporting by the financial institution to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of financial institutions is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while cross-border transactional screening and suspicious transaction reporting are enforced by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech and electronic money institution (EMI) platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or Stripe-linked treasury products are viable as secondary settlement facilitators, with a feasibility rating of High for retail receivables and supplier-disbursement acceleration. Their viability as a primary deposit-account substitute is Medium-to-Low because they are not Canadian Schedule I banks, are not CDIC-insured, and must either hold a Canadian money-services-business (MSB) license or rely on FINTRAC-registered partner banking. Legal constraints include maintaining segregated client funds, compliance with BC Money Services Business licensing expectations, and the inability to offer full commercial credit facilities or LC services. The recommended treasury architecture is a regulated Tier 1 Canadian bank as account house of record with an EMI fintech overlay for currency conversion and low-cost cross-border payment execution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for ULC capital injections is an inbound SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the shareholder's bank to the ULC CAD settlement account via Payments Canada Lynx correspondent clearing. The remittance narrative (Field 70) must clearly state 'ULC Shareholder Capital Contribution' or 'Director Shareholder Loan Agreement No. [X]' to match the ULC's corporate resolutions and financing documents. Canada does not mandate a government-issued purpose code for capital account receipts; the bank will populate internal transaction codes for FINTRAC and sanctions screening. Supporting documentation must include a board resolution authorizing the capital injection, share certificates or a signed loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and the ULC's most recent financial statement. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: No automatic independent audit is imposed on a private BC ULC under the BC Business Corporations Act. An audit may be compelled by a shareholder holding at least 5% of issued shares demanding audited financial statements, by the articles or a unanimous shareholders' agreement, or by lender and investor covenants. Public reporting issuers, licensed financial intermediaries, and companies subject to prescribed regulatory frameworks are mandatorily audited.
