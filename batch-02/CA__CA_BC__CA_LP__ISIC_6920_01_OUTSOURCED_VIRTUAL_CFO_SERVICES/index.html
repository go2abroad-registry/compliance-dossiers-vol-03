Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a British Columbia limited partnership; limited partners may contribute money or real/personal property as stated in the Partnership Agreement, with no escrow account requirement for service-sector limited partnerships. Each limited partner must refrain from participating in control of the business to preserve limited liability under ss. 58-62 of the Partnership Act.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification as required under the BC Fire Code for the commercial office occupancy, evidenced by a municipal fire department inspection; structural load and life-safety clearance established by the building occupancy permit under the BC Building Code; workplace health-and-safety clearance through WorkSafeBC registration and an occupational health and safety program; where the registered office is a home-based operation, a municipal home-occupation permit, fire alarm inspection, and egress verification are mandatory.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a British Columbia limited partnership; limited partners may contribute money or real/personal property as stated in the Partnership Agreement, with no escrow account requirement for service-sector limited partnerships. Each limited partner must refrain from participating in control of the business to preserve limited liability under ss. 58-62 of the Partnership Act.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST, excise, and federal income tax administration; online portals: Business Registration Online, CRA My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE. British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administration; portal: eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) as its federal tax identifier. Procedure: (1) Determine registration requirement: GST/HST registration is mandatory if the LP is not a 'small supplier' (worldwide taxable supplies exceeded CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters) and makes taxable supplies in Canada; non-residents making taxable supplies may also register voluntarily, and registration may be required if the LP has a Canadian permanent establishment or carries on business in Canada. (2) Complete Form RC1, or register through Business Registration Online (BRO), providing partnership particulars and appointing a Canadian representative/agent if no Canadian address; no fee is charged. CRA issues the 9-digit BN and a GST/HST program account (RT). (3) Register with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch through eTaxBC before making PST-taxable sales; no small-supplier threshold applies. (4) Upon registration, file separately for federal GST/HST and BC PST, and comply with information notices; the BN may also be used for import/export customs programs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. A limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent; no election can convert it to corporation-classified treatment. Relevant elective or hybrid-mismatch mechanisms: ITA 18.4/18.6 elections to avoid double-imputation of hybrid instruments/entities; partnership fiscal-period elections (ITA 249.1) to select non-calendar year-end; information-reporting election for partnerships under T5013 rules; GST/HST election under ETA s.156 to simplify closely related registrant supplies; BC PST direct-pay election for qualifying purchasers of taxable goods/services where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (including those of associated persons) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters determines 'small supplier' status for federal GST/HST. BC PST has no analogous threshold; a PST registrant must register before first taxable sale of goods or specified taxable services in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rated if the supplier sells to a non-resident client, the goods are shipped/delivered directly outside Canada, and are exported within 60 days, supported by transportation/export documentation; the supply must not be for subsequent use in Canada. For services: an outsourced virtual CFO, fractional CFO advisory, or multicurrency bookkeeping service is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.9(b) when supplied to a non-resident person and the service is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not considered supplied to a Canadian resident. The LP must maintain evidence that the recipient is non-resident, that the service is performed/consumed outside Canada, and that consideration is received from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Automated Export Declaration/B13 for goods; not required for pure cross-border service supplies)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or equivalent shipping evidence for goods exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, or equivalent bank SWIFT MT103 receipt confirmation demonstrating foreign-currency receipt from the non-resident client
  • Executed service agreement and invoices identifying the non-resident recipient and place of supply

Canada and British Columbia do not currently mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API validation, or digital stamping. GST/HST invoices may be paper or electronic and must contain: supplier BN, date of invoice, vendor name, description, volume or unit price, tax charged, and total consideration. Suppliers file GST/HST returns electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE and remit payments; BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC. Pan-Canadian e-invoice adoption is voluntary (Peppol BIS 3.0/Canadian PINT format).

CRA auditing GST/HST zero-rating will require contemporaneous export/service evidence; if requirements are not met, the supposedly zero-rated supply is reassessed at 5% GST/HST (or 12% in BC if PST-taxable), with interest from the original due date and potential clawback of refunded input tax credits. Civil false-statement penalties under the Excise Tax Act (generally 25% of the tax at issue) and gross-negligence or evasion penalties apply, plus possible Canadian criminal prosecution for wilful evasion. Although Canadian federal law does not prescribe a strict statutory FX repatriation deadline, the 180-day documentation benchmark is commonly used to corroborate genuine export status; unexplained late repatriation increases audit risk. BC Ministry of Finance can similarly reassess PST, impose penalties and interest, and pursue collection for tax evaded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There is no exchange-control regime, no prior approval for profit remittances or capital movements, and no prohibition on holding foreign currency in Canada. The only substantive hurdles are Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners (25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty) and statutory reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and cross-border service providers may retain foreign currency indefinitely in Canadian or offshore accounts. However, SWIFT and other cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC as large cross-border transactions, and an aggregated reporting threshold also applies to consecutive smaller transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border transaction reporting; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank prudential oversight

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-to-Medium for primary banking but workable for ancillary disbursements. Canada does not recognize the EU EMI licence; a Canadian fintech money services business such as Wise for Business is registered as an MSB with FINTRAC and may execute cross-border MT103-style transfers, pay out contractor/subcontractor invoices, and issue multi-currency virtual accounts. It cannot lawfully hold partnership escrow balances, provide commercial lending, or give CDIC deposit insurance; treasury policy should therefore keep the operating float at a Schedule I bank and use the fintech only as a payment channel.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-corporate limited partner structure with passive investors (beneficial-ownership opacity)

Root cause: Non-corporate limited partner structure with passive investors (beneficial-ownership opacity)

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the BC Partnership Registration certificate, the limited partnership agreement, investor profiles, source-of-funds declarations from each limited partner, and a general-partner resolution naming the authorized signatories and confirming that the account is the sole partnership treasury account.
Foreign partners or counterparties in FATF-grey-listed or watch-list jurisdictions

Root cause: Foreign partners or counterparties in FATF-grey-listed or watch-list jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Partner passports, tax identification numbers, utility-verified residential addresses, a statement of source of wealth, and documentation of the commercial rationale for the cross-border structure, accompanied by a lawyer's legal-opinion letter acceptable to the bank's AML officer.
Disbursements to third-party payment processors (e.g., Wise, Stripe) for outbound virtual-CFO service fees

Root cause: Disbursements to third-party payment processors (e.g., Wise, Stripe) for outbound virtual-CFO service fees

Defense evidence: Executed client service agreements, approved fee schedules, detailed settlement reports from the payment processor, invoice-to-settlement reconciliation, and a written AML/ATF compliance policy confirming the flow of funds is not being used to avoid FINTRAC thresholds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital contributions should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the overseas partner's bank to the limited partnership's segregated BC operating account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The SWIFT message must include the beneficiary legal name and LP registration number, the bank's BIC and Canadian branch details, and a clear 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION UNDER PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT DATED [date]' reference; Canada does not use harmonized EU purpose codes, but the sender must state the payment reason in the remittance information field. The LP must hold the partnership agreement, partner source-of-funds letters, and a capital account ledger showing the allocation for tax filing and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting where the transfer is CAD 10,000 or more.Trade payables and profit-distribution wires should be released via SWIFT MT103 using the general partner's commercial-banking treasury platform (e.g., RBC Express, TD Cash Management, or BMO Online Banking for Business). For larger and recurring supplier flows, banks accept batch-processing MT101 instructions or MT103 individual transfers; commercial letters of credit are permitted but not customary for service-based virtual-CFO invoices. Before any outbound remittance to a non-resident partner, the general partner must calculate and deduct Part XIII withholding tax under the Canada-BC partnership rules and ensure the payment descriptor records the tax withheld.
