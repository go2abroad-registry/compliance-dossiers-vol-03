No special civil defense clearance is demanded for low-risk e-commerce operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, and the Business Number; accessible via Business Registration Online and My Business Account. For British Columbia provincial sales tax: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, administered through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

For a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Company (BC ULC), after incorporation under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia), the statutory tax identifier is the federal Business Number (BN) issued by the CRA. The procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate via BC Corporate Online or corporate agent; (2) register for the CRA Business Number using Business Registration Online, selecting the income tax account and the GST/HST account; (3) register separately for BC PST through eTaxBC. For foreign entities without a Canadian incorporation, the path is: (a) file extra-provincial registration with the BC Registrar of Companies through Corporate Online if the entity is carrying on business in BC; (b) apply directly to the CRA for a Business Number and GST/HST account; (c) register for BC PST when the small-seller exemption no longer applies. The same BN serves as the TIN/VAT ID for all federal tax obligations and as the reference number for the BC PST account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Canadian domestic law, a BC ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation and is subject to entity-level income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act; Canada has no domestic check-the-box election and the ULC is never fiscally transparent for Canadian tax purposes. However, for U.S. federal income tax, the BC ULC is an eligible entity that may file U.S. Form 8832 to elect classification as a corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity; because the ULC has unlimited liability, absence of limited liability permits a check-the-box election. Such an election can produce hybrid entity outcomes under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention, and Canada's pending hybrid mismatch arrangements legislation, together with the OECD BEPS Hybrid Mismatch Rules, will govern any resulting income adjustments. No statutory 'hybrid mismatch election' exists under Canadian domestic rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: mandatory registration once worldwide taxable turnover exceeds CAD 30,000, measured either in a single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; BC PST: mandatory registration once gross revenues from taxable sales in British Columbia exceed CAD 10,000 in the preceding 12-month period under the small-seller exemption, with marketplace and non-resident seller obligations applying from a lower nexus on digital platform sales

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada qualify for a 0% GST/HST rate when the supplier exports the goods in the course of a commercial activity or delivers the goods to a common carrier for export to a non-resident destination, provided the supplier retains documented proof of exportation. BC PST does not apply to goods the supplier ships directly to a non-resident outside British Columbia. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under the GST/HST when the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada and not in respect of tangible personal property located in Canada at the time the service is rendered; this can cover cross-border e-commerce logistics, order processing, data management, and advisory services related to maternity and infant baby supply operations performed for non-residents.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic export reporting data filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System/CERS)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent carriage document evidencing export from Canada to a foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank credit confirmation, remittance record, and internal reconciliation demonstrating receipt of foreign currency funds from the offshore buyer)

Canada has no federally mandated continuous transaction control (CTC) or real-time electronic invoicing and fiscal clearance regime. The CRA does not require real-time API approval, digital stamping, or pre-clearance of invoices for GST/HST purposes. Electronic invoices are permitted and must contain the required GST/HST information, including the Business Number, the amount of tax, and, where appropriate, the supply description. For PST, invoices must separately state or show the PST amount. Export data must be retained in an auditor-compatible format, and customs export declarations must be filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) where applicable.

The CRA and the British Columbia Ministry of Finance may audit export zero-rating claims retrospectively; misstated export documentation, missing proof of export, or failure to establish a genuine foreign destination will cause reversal of the 0% GST/HST treatment, reassessment to 5% GST/HST plus interest, and clawback of any previously claimed input tax credits or refunds. Civil penalties may include a 25% penalty on the net tax misstated under the Excise Tax Act, a 50% gross-negligence penalty on tax sought to be evaded under the Income Tax Act, and monthly late-filing penalties. Knowingly false or fraudulent export declarations expose the principal and directors to criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code and the Excise Tax Act, fines, imprisonment, asset seizure, and permanent denial of relief. The BC Ministry of Finance may similarly reassess unpaid PST and impose daily penalties and interest where goods were not shipped as designated zero-rating export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has no exchange controls under the Foreign Exchange Control Act; the Canadian dollar is freely convertible and both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted. Profit remittance is subject to corporate solvency tests, directors' resolutions, and non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act; no prior central bank approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender or conversion requirements for export receipts; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely. Enterprises must still maintain adequate books and records for revenue recognition under the Income Tax Act and report cross-border cash/concurrency movements above CAD 10,000 under the PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no designated foreign-exchange administration; banking conduct is regulated by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/ATF reporting is administered by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — offshore digital EMIs may operate as foreign money services businesses and must register with FINTRAC; they can support multi-currency payout cards and payment gateways, but cannot provide CDIC-insured Canadian dollar deposits or clear CAD payroll directly. A BC-registered ULC as merchant of record is required, and robust evidence of commercial purpose and supply-chain documentation is mandatory.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined: federal general corporate income tax rate 15% plus British Columbia provincial general corporate income tax rate 12%. A BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and is taxed on its Canadian-source business income at these rates; the ULC cannot be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity for Canadian tax purposes even if a US shareholder has made a US check-the-box election.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing and balance-due date is no later than the last day of the 6th month after fiscal year-end. For a December 31 year-end, the T2 is due on June 30 of the following year. Installments, if any, are due by each month-end or quarter-end throughout the year; late or deficient instalments attract interest and instalment penalties under ITA s. 163.1.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 101 - Sales, revenues and services Gross e-commerce revenue from the sale of baby and maternity supplies net of refunds, chargebacks and sales allowances, reconciled to Stripe and PayPal settlement reports, credit-card batch files and bank statements. Report only Canadian-source business income of the Canadian operations; sales funneled to a foreign affiliate or to a foreign warehouse must be supported by a transfer-pricing analysis and reported on Form T106. Under-declaration of PayPal/Stripe volumes is the primary audit trigger for an online retailer. Late filing penalty under ITA s. 162(1): 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% for each full month late, maximum 12 months; on repeat failure, s. 162(2) imposes 10% plus 2% per month (maximum 20 months). Arrears interest at the CRA prescribed rate compounds daily. Late or deficient instalments attract a s. 163.1 penalty capped at 50% of the shortfall interest. Failure to file Form T106 for reportable cross-border related-party transactions carries a transfer-pricing documentation penalty under ITA s. 247(10) of 10% of the adjustment (capped at C$10,000). Gross negligence on the T2 return triggers a s. 163(2) penalty of 50% of the understated federal and provincial tax, and criminal prosecution under s. 239(1) may add fines of 50%-200% of evaded tax and imprisonment.
