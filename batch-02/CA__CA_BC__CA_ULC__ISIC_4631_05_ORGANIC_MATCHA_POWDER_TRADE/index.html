Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal GST/HST, Part I corporate income tax, payroll, and Business Number (BN) administration via the My Business Account portal (canada.ca/revenue-agency); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) - customs export declaration and import/export (RM) account administration; British Columbia Ministry of Finance - Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration and wholesaler/reseller administration under the Provincial Sales Tax Act.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act via BC Corporate Online or counsel; no minimum paid-up capital is required. Step 2: Apply to CRA for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, requesting the mandatory corporation income tax account (RC), the GST/HST account (RT), and the CBSA import/export account (RM). Step 3: Register with the BC Ministry of Finance for a PST wholesaler/reseller account and obtain the applicable resale exemption certificate so that inventory purchased for resale or export is acquired without BC PST. Step 4: For customs purposes, the ULC must reference its BN-RM account on all export declarations; for bank settlement, open a Schedule I Canadian bank account. Step 5: Non-resident shareholders must observe BC director residency requirements, which generally require at least one director ordinarily resident in Canada, and ensure central management and control or the place-of-incorporation rule under the Canada-US Tax Treaty supports Canadian residency. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (domestic Canadian default: the BC ULC is a corporation under the Income Tax Act and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income at combined federal and provincial rates; the ULC is Pass_Through_Eligible only for US federal tax classification via an IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election, which has no effect for Canadian income tax purposes).

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box regime exists. Available elections include: (i) IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election by US owners to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity for US federal tax, without altering Canadian entity-level taxation; (ii) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240 for a small supplier below the CAD 30,000 threshold; (iii) joint election under ETA s. 156 for nil consideration on supplies between closely related corporations for intra-group GST/HST neutrality; (iv) where applicable, an election under ITA s. 216(1) permitting a non-resident owner to file a net-basis return for certain Canadian-source income; and (v) standard capital cost allowance and input tax credit allocation elections available in annual GST/HST and T2 filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 (a person is a small supplier if total taxable supplies in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters do not exceed CAD 30,000; mandatory registration applies once this threshold is exceeded for a person carrying on business in Canada). BC PST: no small-supplier exemption; every seller making taxable retail sales of tangible personal property in British Columbia must register, but a wholesaler/reseller account permits tax-free purchases of inventory for resale or export.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of matcha powder exports applies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where: (i) the supplier is a GST/HST registrant; (ii) title and risk of loss pass to the purchaser outside Canada or to a non-resident person; (iii) the goods are delivered for export by the supplier, shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, or transferred to a non-resident for immediate export; and (iv) the export evidence package is obtained within two years of the end of the reporting period. Additionally, matcha powder sold for human consumption in Canada is zero-rated as a basic grocery under Part I of Schedule VI unless it is supplied in a taxable presentation such as a carbonated or ready-to-drink beverage, confectionery, snack food, or single-serving beverage format; organic certification does not alter the zero-rating analysis but is substantively required for any organic label claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or electronic ACI/AES export report; mandatory where shipment value exceeds CAD 2,000, for controlled goods, or under Canada-US export reporting protocols)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing Canadian port of loading and destination outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (HS 0902.10 for green tea/matcha powder, net weight, unit values in foreign currency)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-issued SWIFT MT103 / foreign exchange transmittal record evidencing receipt of export proceeds into the ULC's Canadian bank account
  • CBSA Import/Export (RM) Account reference with exporter identification code

Canada has no national e-invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance requirement, and no digital stamping or continuous transaction control system; paper, PDF, EDI, and PEPPOL-based e-invoices are all valid. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million must file returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE, and corporations with gross revenue exceeding CAD 1 million must e-file their T2 returns. All invoices, sales records, export documents, and supporting data must be retained in English or French and must be capable of being produced to CRA and CBSA in an auditable format.

CRA may retrospectively reassess zero-rated exports as taxable at 5% GST, deny input tax credits, and recharacterize transactions as domestic taxable supplies wherever export documentation is deficient; interest accrues on all reassessed amounts. Penalties include 25% of the net tax understated for false statements or omissions (ETA s. 280.1), 50% for repeat false statements (ETA s. 281), and gross-negligence penalties under ETA s. 281.1 of 50% plus a minimum CAD 100 penalty; deliberate evasion may result in criminal prosecution with fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. CBSA may impose administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 25,000 per violation for false export declarations and may seize goods under the Customs Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under the IMF Articles of Agreement (Article VIII). Both current account and capital account transactions are generally free from exchange control approval. Profit remittance by way of dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees is not blocked but is subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (base 25% on dividends, subject to treaty reduction under the Canada-US Tax Treaty, as applicable) and to transfer pricing / thin capitalisation rules. A BC ULC may remit funds abroad without Bank of Canada authorisation; only tax clearance and AML reporting obligations must be managed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or retention timeline for export receipts. Export proceeds earned by the matcha distribution ULC may be held in USD, JPY, CNY, or any freely convertible currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts. Reporting is limited to FINTRAC cross-border currency reports for physical cash or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more, and to EFT reporting for transfers in/out in prescribed circumstances; no export surrender certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority). Foreign exchange regulation in Canada is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential matters and by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border AML/CTF and large currency transaction reporting. There is no dedicated foreign exchange control authority; Canada operates under a liberalised foreign exchange regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not Canadian banks and are not eligible for CDIC deposit insurance. If an EMI proposes to provide cross-border payment services to the BC ULC, it will generally be treated as a foreign money services business (MSB) and may require registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Such providers may be used only as a supplementary settlement rail, not as the primary treasury lender or custodian of matching trade funds; legal viability is improved if the EMI has a Canadian MSB registration or operates through a federally or provincially licensed payment service provider with FINTRAC reporting obligations.

Use an inbound SWIFT MT103 wire from the foreign shareholder / investor to the BC ULC's primary corporate operational account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.00%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, unless a tax treaty reduces the rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return is due no later than six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. The balance-due date is two months after the year-end for most corporations, or three months after the year-end for a CCPC eligible for the small business deduction. Instalment payments are due monthly or quarterly when the estimated tax payable exceeds CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate fair market value of controlled transactions with non-resident related parties in the taxation year is the CRA administrative threshold above which contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Local File documentation is expected. Master File documentation is expected for multinational groups with consolidated annual group revenue exceeding CAD 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation for ordinary trading entities. However, the Canada Revenue Agency applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, and treaty limitation-on-benefits principles, to disregard or recharacterize arrangements lacking genuine economic substance. A BC ULC engaged in matcha powder trade must maintain physical office space, employed personnel in Canada, local management decisions, an active bank account, and documented commercial contracts; otherwise treaty benefits and deductions can be denied, and the payments may be reclassified as deemed Part XIII withholding-amount dividends.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act carries a fine of not less than 50% and up to 200% of the tax evaded, and imprisonment of up to five years, or both. Civil gross-negligence penalties under section 163(2) add an additional 50% of understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who wilfully participate in evasion are personally liable for criminal penalties, regardless of the corporate veil. CRA may also assess directors under section 160 for property transferred at undervalue to shareholders; travel bans, asset freezes, and seizure orders can be imposed by a court.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Breach

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under section 247 of the Income Tax Act, impose a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment where adjustments exceed a specified threshold, disallow deductions, and charge prescribed interest on the resulting deficiency. Continued non-compliance after an information request can lead to additional penalties and restriction on further documentation-related defences.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false transfer-pricing documentation or direct the manipulation of intercompany invoices face criminal liability under section 239. Civil liability may be imposed jointly on a director where the director was negligent and the corporation cannot pay the tax, including the 10% penalty and accrued interest.
Shell Company Arrangement and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CRA can apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to disregard the ULC or recharacterize the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, and reassess the ULC on all income at the 27% combined rate. The deemed dividend distribution to the non-resident shareholder is then subject to 25% Part XIII withholding tax; a gross-negligence penalty of up to 50% of the understated tax may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate a sham arrangement can be held personally liable for the ULC's unremitted tax liabilities under the director liability provisions of section 227.1; courts may pierce the corporate veil where the ULC is an instrument of fraud, exposing directors to restitution orders and equitable compensation.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, FINTRAC can impose an administrative monetary penalty of up to CAD 500,000 for a corporation per violation. Criminal money-laundering convictions carry fines of up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to ten years. Provincial securities or BC corporate registries may also suspend or strike the ULC for repeated non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly engage in or are grossly negligent in respect of record-keeping failures are personally liable for FINTRAC penalties; criminal liability attaches to individuals in the money-laundering chain. A court may issue restrictive orders, travel bans, and mutual legal assistance requests compelling the director's return to Canada for prosecution.
