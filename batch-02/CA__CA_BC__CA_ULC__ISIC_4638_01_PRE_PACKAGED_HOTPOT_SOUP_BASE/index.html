Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Canadian entities including British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporations obtain their statutory Tax Identification Number via Business Number (BN) registration. The SOP requires online submission through the CRA My Business portal using Form RC59 together with Articles of Incorporation and supporting documentation; approval and BN issuance occur within ten business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Canadian entities including British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporations obtain their statutory Tax Identification Number via Business Number (BN) registration. The SOP requires online submission through the CRA My Business portal using Form RC59 together with Articles of Incorporation and supporting documentation; approval and BN issuance occur within ten business days.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Pre-packaged goods exported from Canada to non-resident purchasers qualify for zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act when title passes outside Canada and goods are shipped abroad. Offshore services may qualify if performed entirely outside Canada.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Proof of Export including Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement confirming foreign exchange repatriation

All GST registrants must file returns electronically via the CRA My Business portal. Real-time digital stamping and API integration are not mandated; large-volume filers may be required to use electronic data interchange (EDI) for certain transactions.

CRA post-audit reviews of GST returns; fraudulent zero-rating claims result in penalties up to 50 percent of evaded tax plus interest, retrospective GST liability, and potential criminal prosecution for willful misrepresentation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible de jure and de facto. Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations at the Fund's founding in December 1945 and imposes no restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions or on capital-account movements; there are no licensing, surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirements for foreign-currency earnings. Profit remittance from a BC ULC to a non-resident shareholder is unrestricted under exchange-control law; the only hurdles are fiscal: Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%; treaty-reduced to 15% portfolio dividend / 5% direct-ownership dividend and 0% to 10% for interest under the Canada-US Tax Convention, with special ULC fiscal-transparency look-through considerations), the excess interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) rules for shareholder debt for tax years commencing after March 31, 2024, and Investment Canada Act net-benefit review thresholds for acquisitions of control of a Canadian business (organic start-up of a new export-distribution ULC is not an acquisition and is not notifiable; cross-border trade payments are not restricted). The BC ULC may freely hold CAD, USD, EUR, or RMB/CNH balances in Canadian or offshore accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory: none. The wartime Canada Foreign Exchange Control Act lapsed in 1951; the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Income Tax Act impose no mandatory conversion ratio, no surrender timeline, no forced repatriation, and no designated export-proceeds account for Canadian exporters. The BC ULC may retain, denominate, and disburse export receipts freely in any currency. Remaining mandatory compliance duties for cross-border export receipts: (1) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for every international EFT of C$10,000 or more; (2) Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration (B13A) and Statistics Canada trade reporting for consignments valued above C$2,000; (3) Safe Food for Canadians Act (SFCA) export licence and lot-level traceability documentation for the pre-packaged hotpot soup-base shipments (with CFIA inspection where required); (4) books-and-records retention of the underlying sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and LC/collection documents to evidence the trade purpose of each receivable; and (5) IFRS/ASPE treasury presentation of FX gains, hedges, and net translation exposure. No ratio or statutory timeline applies to converting USD or CNY export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), is the sole central bank and note-issuing authority, responsible for monetary policy, payments-system risk oversight, and lender-of-last-resort functions. Canada does not operate a dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration agency and maintains no administrative exchange-control permit system. Foreign-exchange policy authority sits with the Minister of Finance; prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions sits with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision sits with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); clearing and settlement-system regulation is exercised by the Bank of Canada and Payments Canada.
