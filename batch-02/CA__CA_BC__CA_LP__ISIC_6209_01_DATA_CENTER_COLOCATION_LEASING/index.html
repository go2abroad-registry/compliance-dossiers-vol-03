Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and income tax administration, accessed through CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, accessed through eTaxBC for BC PST; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export reporting and customs administration.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax but is itself a registrable person for GST/HST and BC PST. Step 1: Ensure the BC limited partnership is validly formed under the BC Limited Partnership Act, or is extra-provincially registered with BC Registry Services, before applying for tax accounts. Step 2: Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA via Business Registration Online; if online access is unavailable to the non-resident owners, file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' and specify the LP’s program accounts, including RT for GST/HST and RP for T5013 partnership information returns. Step 3: CRA issues the 9-digit BN and program account numbers. Step 4: Register for GST/HST under the same BN when taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000, or voluntarily elect registration to claim input tax credits. Step 5: Register with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC for a PST vendor account before selling taxable goods or services in BC; BC has no small-supplier PST threshold. Step 6: Maintain the partnership agreement, extraprovincial registration evidence, and CRA/BC tax account records for audit, KYC, and T5013 filing purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), a BC limited partnership is not a taxpayer; income and loss are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners. The LP is nevertheless the registered person for GST/HST and PST purposes and must file a T5013 partnership information return if it carries on business in Canada or has Canadian-source income. Non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding or a section 216 net-income tax election on allocated Canadian-source amounts.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity election and no elective hybrid mismatch regime; hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3 apply automatically to cross-border partnership arrangements. Relevant statutory elections include: (1) ITA section 216 election by non-resident partners to file a net Canadian rental/business income return rather than paying flat Part XIII withholding; (2) ITA section 249.1(4) election to select a non-calendar partnership fiscal period; (3) ITA section 97(2) rollover election by partners contributing real property or equipment to the LP; (4) ETA section 240(3) election for a small-supplier LP to voluntarily register for GST/HST and recover input tax credits; and (5) related-party and administrative elections available under the Excise Tax Act where the LP transacts with affiliated Canadian service providers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration threshold: CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada in a calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. A small supplier may elect to register voluntarily. BC PST: no economic threshold; a PST vendor account is mandatory before making PST-taxable sales of goods or services in BC, including most data processing, computer equipment leasing, and related taxable services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI Part V, goods are zero-rated if they are sold to a non-resident and physically exported from Canada as part of the same transaction, with contemporaneous export documentation. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated only if they are not performed in Canada and are not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. Data center colocation and bare-metal hosting in BC involve server equipment and rack space physically located in Canada, so recurring colocation/hosting services are generally taxable at 5% GST plus 7% PST and cannot be zero-rated. Distinct sales of computer equipment exported out of Canada may qualify for zero-rating, and the LP may claim input tax credits for goods and services used in making taxable supplies; any net GST/HST refund requires six-year substantiation of revenue, input costs, foreign customer status, and Canadian property situs.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System or approved third-party portal)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, truck manifest, or rail waybill showing physical movement out of Canada and delivery to the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/foreign-exchange transaction record evidencing receipt of the export consideration within the 180-day deadline
  • Foreign customs entry or destination-country import certificate, where available, and the corresponding commercial invoice and purchase order
  • Export contract, LP service/sale agreement, and CRA export-support worksheet maintained in the permanent tax file

Canada does not operate a national real-time fiscal clearance/e-invoicing mandate. CRA permits electronic invoices if they contain the prescribed GST/HST information and are supported by a complete audit trail; records must generally be retained for six years. BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC and may be paid through ePayBC. For exports of controlled or non-controlled goods, CBSA requires electronic export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System or approved third-party systems; no federal fiscal stamping or continuous transaction control requirement applies in British Columbia.

CRA and CBSA will deny zero-rating if export documentation is missing, incomplete, or not contemporaneous; affected supplies are reassessed at the regular 5% GST rate and, if located in BC, may also attract BC PST at 7%, plus prescribed arrears interest. The GST/HST gross-negligence penalty for false or misleading statements is generally not less than CAD 250 plus 25% of the additional tax payable; knowing tax evasion may lead to fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment under the Excise Tax Act. BC PST audits may assess unreported tax with penalties and interest, and credibility is enhanced by electronic sales records, partner/director accountability, and complete export evidence. CRA reassessment periods are normally four years, but remain open indefinitely where a taxpayer has made misrepresentations or committed fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible under IMF Article VIII, and Canada maintains no exchange-control approval regime for current or capital account transactions. Both residents and non-residents may convert CAD to USD or other major currencies without central-bank allocation, surrender, or prior permit. Profit remittances from a British Columbia limited partnership to non-resident partners are subject not to FX restrictions but to Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, which generally imposes 25% withholding on distributions that are not reduced by an applicable income-tax treaty; the remittance may be made in CAD or USD at a freely negotiated bank FX spread. The only true hurdles are tax documentation (Form NR301, treaty declarations), bank-level sanctions screening, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds, not exchange-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, no forced conversion, and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds. For the data-center colocation leasing scope, cross-border service export receipts may be invoiced in USD, EUR, or CAD and retained in a Canadian bank account or sold forward at the LP's discretion. Bank and money-services businesses must file FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Reports for cash transactions of C$10,000 or more, and FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for cross-border international EFTs of C$10,000 or more; these are information-reporting obligations, not conversion requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada and Department of Finance Canada, with delegated regulatory functions to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally chartered banks and to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT reporting and FX transaction surveillance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-to-Medium for primary settlement. Offshore digital EMIs are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and cannot accept Canadian deposit liabilities or issue Canadian clearing accounts without OSFI authorization. They may be used as auxiliary FX conversion and payout processors if they are FINTRAC-registered money services businesses, maintain segregated client funds, and do not serve as the LP's main treasury account. For a BC limited partnership engaged in Tier-3/4 colocation leasing, the prudent design is a domestic Schedule I bank account for CAD/USD settlement with an EMI used only for vendor payments in jurisdictions where the Canadian bank lacks local clearing coverage.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be routed by SWIFT MT103 in USD, CAD, or EUR from the investor's bank to the BC LP's account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The SWIFT fields must show the LP's full legal name exactly as registered on the Bankers' Agreement; field 70/72 must include a purpose-code narrative such as 'CAPINJT // PARNERSHIP CAPITAL INJECTION' or 'BUSINV // DIRECT INVESTMENT'. The bank will require a certified copy of the LP's subscription book, source-of-funds evidence, and the electronic funds-transfer record if the wire equals or exceeds C$10,000. No Bank of Canada permit is required; for receipt in CAD, the clearing rail is the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for settlement values above C$250,000; for USD, clearing occurs via the bank's US correspondent (typically through Fedwire or CHIPS). Sanctions screening and FINTRAC EFT reporting are mandatory.For vendor and infrastructure payments, the primary rail is a SWIFT MT103 corporate debit from the LP's Canadian account to the vendor's bank; domestic and cross-border CAD payments are cleared through ACSS/LVTS, while USD payments are cleared through CHIPS or Fedwire via the account bank's US intermediary. For high-value server and power-equipment imports, a commercial documentary letter of credit issued under UCP600 by a Canadian Schedule I bank is advisable; required documents include the commercial invoice, packing list, transport document (bill of lading or airway bill), insurance certificate, data-center equipment compliance documentation, and a bank-confirmed LC authority. Vendor disbursements must be supported by a board-approved treasury mandate, invoice matching against the colocation lease or purchase order, beneficiary bank credentials in safe custody, and FINTRAC EFT reporting for any international wire of C$10,000 or more.Deliverable CAD/USD FX forwards with maturities from 1 to 24 months, executed under an ISDA master agreement or bank FX-line confirmation, where the LP can demonstrate post-trade commercial purpose from USD lease receivables and CAD operating costs.USD/CAD cross-currency swaps, including fixed-for-floating or floating-for-floating structures with notional exchange, used to hedge long-term capital expenditure debt or completed data-center lease receivables, subject to OSFI counterparty credit limits and Canadian trade-repository reporting for derivatives.Vanilla USD call / CAD put or USD put / CAD call options, where permitted by the bank's treasury policy as a hedging overlay for colocation pricing risk; these are permissible as bilateral options and are separately documented under the Canadian foreign-exchange derivative framework.Construction-period hedging via a rolling forward-rate agreement with a non-deliverable settlement mechanism, available under the bank's ISDA/CSA protocols when the underlying physical delivery is not economically efficient, though CAD is not a restricted currency and physical forwards remain the standard rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal small business rate on first $500,000 active business income; 38% federal general rate on income above $500,000; British Columbia provincial corporate tax rate of 11% general and 2.2% small business rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal withholding tax on dividends from Canadian corporations to non-residents, subject to reduction under applicable income tax treaties

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50 million in related-party transactions or gross revenue exceeding CAD 50 million triggering Master File and Local File requirements

Economic substance scope: Canada lacks a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations regime; instead applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), transfer pricing adjustments, and Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules to prevent artificial reduction of Canadian tax base

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file tax returns or information returns

Statutory sanction: Penalties of $100 per day (maximum $2,500) plus interest on taxes owing; possible criminal prosecution for willful evasion

Director personal liability: General partners in LP face unlimited personal liability for partnership debts and taxes; potential director liability if misclassified as corporation
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income, penalties up to 10% of transaction value, and possible 50% gross negligence penalty

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes if they authorized or acquiesced in non-compliance
Economic substance and GAAR application

Statutory sanction: GAAR can deny tax benefits, recharacterize transactions, and impose penalties of 50% of tax benefit plus interest

Director personal liability: Officers and directors can face personal liability for tax shortfalls and potential criminal charges for fraudulent misrepresentation
