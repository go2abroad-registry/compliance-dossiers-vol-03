Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), federal tax administration for income tax, GST/HST, and Business Number program; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST). Primary portals: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online; BC eTaxBC for PST.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the BC Unlimited Liability Company (ULC) under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act through BC Corporate Online. Step 2: Apply for the federal Business Number (BN) using CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1; the ULC must provide certified articles of incorporation, registered office information, and an authorized representative with a Canadian address. Step 3: CRA issues a nine-digit BN and relevant program accounts, including RT for GST/HST and RP for payroll where applicable. Step 4: Register separately for BC PST through eTaxBC using the same BN; a PST program account (PD) is established. A foreign-owned ULC is required to register for GST/HST once its worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or in its first calendar quarter, exceed CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits and to zero-rate export sales.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC ULC is eligible to elect pass-through classification under U.S. check-the-box rules for U.S. federal tax purposes; however, the Canadian default classification is Entity_Level_Taxation because the ULC is a separate taxable corporation under the Income Tax Act and the BC Income Tax Act for domestic Canadian tax purposes. No Canadian tax statute treats the ULC as fiscally transparent.

Available election forms

Canada has no general check-the-box election for entity classification under federal income tax. A ULC may make certain elections under the Excise Tax Act, including the closely related corporation election under section 156 to treat taxable supplies between qualifying registrants as made for nil consideration, and annual or quarterly filing elections. For U.S. federal purposes, the ULC may file IRS Form 8832 to elect status as a partnership or disregarded entity, but that election has no effect on Canadian taxation. Hybrid mismatch restrictions in Income Tax Act sections 18.4–18.6 limit deductions related to hybrid instruments and hybrid entities; there is no statutory hybrid-mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in any four consecutive calendar quarters or in the first calendar quarter, after which GST/HST registration is mandatory. BC PST: no fixed economic threshold; registration is required before carrying on PST-taxable selling activity in BC, and non-resident sellers soliciting orders and delivering into BC must register and collect PST regardless of revenue volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate an export supply of goods under the Excise Tax Act when the goods are exported directly by the vendor or on behalf of the vendor, or are delivered outside Canada to the non-resident purchaser, and the vendor retains written evidence of export. Services performed wholly outside Canada, services supplied to a non-resident with respect to foreign real property, and certain intellectual-property and cross-border services may also be zero-rated. BC PST does not apply to tangible personal property delivered by the seller or common carrier to a destination outside British Columbia or outside Canada. The zero rate is conditional on documentary proof; without substantive export documentation, CRA will treat the supply as taxable at 5% GST, and BC PST may also apply if the property is used, consumed, or delivered in BC.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A, Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED), or ACI eManifest where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, truck waybill, or other carrier-issued transport document
  • Original commercial invoice bearing export sale terms and non-resident purchaser address
  • Packing list identifying the exported resin statues and quantities
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank foreign-exchange advice, or SWIFT credit showing receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Export sales contract, purchase order, and any export allowance or permit
  • Delivery confirmation and destination-country customs entry, if readily available

Canada and British Columbia do not currently impose a real-time fiscal clearance mandate, digital stamping requirement, or mandatory continuous transaction control regime. Electronic invoices are acceptable if they satisfy the information requirements of section 223 of the Excise Tax Act. Certain large GST/HST registrants above CAD 1.5 million in taxable revenues are required to file and remit GST/HST electronically through CRA, using web services/API where applicable. PST returns and remittances are filed electronically through BC eTaxBC. Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing is voluntary and does not constitute fiscal clearance.

The CRA audits export zero-rating strictly and may reassess a supply as taxable within the normal four-year GST/HST assessment period, or indefinitely where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. Unsupported zero-rating is clawed back with net tax, interest, and penalties. Civil penalties for false statements or omissions under the Excise Tax Act may reach 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, and late or unpaid net tax carries daily compound interest. Criminal tax evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act or section 239 of the Income Tax Act can result in fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. BC PST authorities may also reassess uncollected tax, impose late-filing penalties and interest, and require security for non-resident registrants.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current-account items (goods, services, royalties, interest, dividends) and capital-account items (share capital, shareholder loans, portfolio transfers, M&A). The Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act impose no exchange-control permits, prior approvals, surrender duties, or repatriation gates on cross-border fund movements. Profit-remittance hurdles are exclusively tax and verification based: (i) Part XIII withholding tax at a statutory 25% on dividends paid to non-residents, reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty when the shareholder is a qualifying US corporation; (ii) Part XIV branch tax at a statutory 25% (treaty-reduced to 15%) on after-tax business profits not reinvested in Canada when the ULC has made a subsection 216(1) election under the Income Tax Regulations, thereby being treated as a partnership for Canadian tax purposes; (iii) FINTRAC reporting for international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iv) bank-side KYC/AML 'purpose of payment' substantiation. Canada follows an open-account, negative-approval model; the Investment Canada Act applies only to acquisitions of control of a Canadian business above monetary thresholds, not to passive treasury inflows into a newly incorporated ULC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement: there is no mandatory timeline, no forced conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate or exchange export receipts. A British Columbia ULC may invoice in CAD, USD, EUR, or CNY and retain export proceeds in domestic or offshore foreign-currency bank accounts at its discretion. The only compliance duties are the PCMLTFA's 'international electronic funds transfer' report to FINTRAC for any single incoming or outgoing EFT of CAD 10,000 or more, plus standard record-keeping for large cash transactions (CAD 10,000+) and currency transaction reports. Export receipts are freely convertible at the commercial spot rate without central-bank intervention.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority and operator of the Lynx high-value payment system; prudential supervision of Schedule I banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF transaction monitoring and reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); derivatives and securities oversight by the Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA), including the British Columbia Securities Commission (BCSC). Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control agency or central foreign-exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary collections and payout rail; Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021, c. 23), non-bank payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada; offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and Stripe Treasury may lawfully hold funds and execute CAD/USD/CNY transfers without holding a Bank Act banking licence, but they are strictly prohibited from taking deposits in Canada. EMI balances are not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation, fall outside OSFI prudential supervision, and may trigger CRA, CRS, and FATCA foreign-account reporting. Legal constraint: the ULC must maintain a domestic CDIC-covered operating account at a Schedule I bank as the ultimate treasury sink; EMIs are recommended only for preorder collection (Stripe and PayPal workflow), logistics marketplace payouts, and low-value cross-currency sweeps, with each inter-EMI transfer traceable to a specific trade invoice or fulfilment event.

Turnover: No monetary thresholds apply to a BC private unlimited liability company. The ULC must appoint an auditor at each Annual General Meeting unless, as an eligible private company, all shareholders pass a unanimous resolution waiving the audit (BCBCA s. 207(1)). A single dissenting shareholder or any shareholder requisitioning an audit under s. 210 makes a full audit mandatory. Public companies and regulated licensees cannot waive.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (total value of transactions with non-residents in a taxation year)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific ESR but applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits; transactions must have commercial rationale

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties up to 200% of tax evaded, fines up to CAD 1,000,000, imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they failed to remit withholdings or knowingly participated in evasion
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 50% of tax benefit

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for negligence or fraud if they approved transactions without substance
AML

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations, imprisonment for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly participated or failed to implement controls
