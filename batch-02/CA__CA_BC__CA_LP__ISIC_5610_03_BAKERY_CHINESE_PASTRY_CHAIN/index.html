Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) via canada.ca; British Columbia Ministry of Finance – Consumer Taxation Branch for provincial PST (www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account ('RT') through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'. Non-resident limited partnerships may be registered through the CRA Non-Resident Registration service, supplying the partnership's legal name, foreign address and, if applicable, an appointed Canadian resident representative. Registration must be completed before making taxable supplies in Canada; the BN must appear on invoices, import documents and GST/HST returns. A separate BC PST registration is obtained through the eTaxBC portal and is required once taxable sales exceed CAD 10,000 in any 12-month period.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign entities must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account ('RT') through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'. Non-resident limited partnerships may be registered through the CRA Non-Resident Registration service, supplying the partnership's legal name, foreign address and, if applicable, an appointed Canadian resident representative. Registration must be completed before making taxable supplies in Canada; the BN must appear on invoices, import documents and GST/HST returns. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; the limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for Part I income tax, while for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act the partnership is a distinct person and must register and remit on its own behalf. Available elections and compliance mechanisms include: (i) election under section 216 of the Income Tax Act for a non-resident partner to compute Part I tax on Canadian rental income; (ii) appointment of a resident representative for GST/HST compliance where required by the Canada Revenue Agency; (iii) the section 156 Excise Tax Act election for affiliated small suppliers; and (iv) hybrid mismatch arrangement disclosure obligations under sections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (global annual taxable supplies in Canada for GST/HST small-supplier threshold); BC PST requires registration once taxable retail sales exceed CAD 10,000 in any 12-month period; franchise royalties from Canadian franchisees are subject to GST at 5% and PST is generally not applicable to royalties unless associated with selling goods at retail.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST) applies to (i) goods exported by the supplier to a non-resident customer, (ii) supplies of goods to a non-resident that are subsequently exported as part of the supply, (iii) specified services performed on movable goods that are exported, and (iv) certain intellectual property or franchise rights supplied to a non-resident for use exclusively outside Canada. The bakery central kitchen and master franchisor must establish that title in goods (pastries, baked goods) passes to the non-resident and that the goods leave Canada within a reasonable time. Royalties from foreign franchisees under a master franchise agreement may be zero-rated if the intangible is supplied for use wholly outside Canada and the recipient is a non-resident not in Canada at the time of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA G13 form or approved electronic / e-manifest filing via CARM)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Canada Border Services Agency certified export manifest
  • Commercial Invoice (complete with supplier BN, description, quantity, value, destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank proof of receipt of foreign currency / SWIFT advice for export payment)
  • Non-resident purchase order or executed master franchise / licence agreement identifying the offshore supply and intended foreign use

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for GST/HST invoices. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing the supplier's name/BN, date, description of goods/services and tax charged, and generally must file GST/HST returns electronically (NETFILE/GST/HST TELEFILE; mandatory e-filing for registrants with taxable turnover above CAD 1 million). There is no API-based or government clearance requirement at the federal level; British Columbia's PST requires the PST registration number to be quoted on invoices. A Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework is under consultation but not yet legislated for BC.

The CRA may reassess zero-rated export supplies within four years (or beyond in cases of misrepresentation, deceitful conduct or fraud), with retrospective clawback of input tax credits and net tax deemed payable. Section 280 of the Excise Tax Act imposes a penalty equal to 25% of the amount of any false statement or omission, plus arrears interest. Knowingly creating false export documentation can lead to prosecution under the Excise Tax Act with fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to two years; repeated wilful evasion may attract additional civil penalties. CRA audits using trade data and customs records verify export claims, and all export records, including customs export declarations, bills of lading, payment evidence and franchise royalty contracts, must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII obligations and the Currency Act. There are no mandatory exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on profit remittance by non-resident Limited Partners. Remittances are taxed at source via Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced) but are freely payable in Canadian dollars or any freely convertible currency. The Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency; CAD/CNY convertibility is unrestricted at negotiated commercial rates through Schedule I banks. Inbound foreign direct investment is reportable to Statistics Canada and may be reviewable under the Investment Canada Act only above specified enterprise value thresholds; a bakery franchise central kitchen operation is expected to fall below those thresholds. Outbound remittances of royalties and dividends require no prior regulatory approval but must be supported by transfer-pricing documentation and the applicable Canada-China income tax treaty protocol.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into Canadian dollars. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely with any Schedule I or authorized foreign bank. Reporting obligations are limited to (i) customs export declarations (B13A) for goods exported from Canada, (ii) FINTRAC reporting of cross-border currency movements above CAD $10,000, and (iii) electronic funds transfer reports (EFTR) for international wire transfers above CAD $10,000. Domestic intra-bank records must support source-of-funds verification but no conversion band or surrender percentage is prescribed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (primary monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (regulatory oversight of deposit-taking banks); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML/CTF supervision); Global Affairs Canada (export/import permit administration)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury and escrow rails; MEDIUM as an auxiliary cross-border payment layer. Non-resident digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are generally licensed as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC rather than as deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act. They cannot legally offer Canadian deposit accounts or operate as the settlement bank for the BC Limited Partnership's capital escrow or intercompany liquidity obligations. They may be used for low-value vendor disbursements and cross-border FX conversion support, but the master royalty wire and capital injection rails must run through a Schedule I bank or an authorized foreign branch operating in Canada to preserve creditor protection and regulatory clarity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: Chinese parent obtains SAFE ODI registration for the BC LP capital injection; remittance is effected in CNY or USD via Bank of China, ICBC, or other Chinese correspondent bank using China's capital account remittance purpose code (e.g., 'Overseas Investment Capital'). The SWIFT MT103 must reference the LP's full legal name, the BC LP registration number, the underlying capital contribution clause in the LP Agreement, and the 'Capital Injection' narrative. Funds are routed through the Chinese bank's Canadian correspondent relationship (commonly with RBC, TD, or BMO) into a designated Capital Escrow Account of the BC LP. Prior to confirmation of credit, the receiving bank will require the source-of-funds declaration, the executed LP Agreement, and the Investment Canada Act notification filing receipt (if triggered). Settlement is T+1 or T+2 on Canadian business days; no FX approval or central bank permit is required in Canada, but the transfer must be recorded by FINTRAC if equal to or above CAD $10,000 under EFTR rules.Royalty remittances to the Chinese master franchisor and equipment imports originate from the operating account via SWIFT MT103 using Lawful Money of Canada as settlement currency or USD/CNY as contract currency. The standard clearing rail is the Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication (SWIFT) network through the LP's Schedule I relationship bank, with same-day settlement in CAD via Lynx (the Canadian high-value payment system). International trade settlements for central kitchen equipment from Chinese suppliers may be executed via SWIFT MT103 direct wiring or by Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by the LP's bank and advised through the exporter's confirming bank in China (commonly under ICC UCP 600). For recurring Chinese supply-chain payments, an open-account SWIFT MT103 with a clean F/X conversion mandate is recommended, while major capital equipment should be ring-fenced through a documentary LC to condition payment on presentation of compliant shipping documents (B/L, commercial invoice, CIQ inspection certificate, phytosanitary certificate). POS receivables are settled daily through the acquiring bank (e.g., TD Merchant Solutions or Chase Paymentech) into the operating account via ACH/EFTPOS batch settlement. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to File Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Penalties of $50 per day up to $1,000 for the partnership; personal liability for partners including fines and interest

Director personal liability: General partners in a limited partnership may face personal liability for partnership tax obligations; limited partners generally protected but must report allocated income
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustments to income, penalties up to 10% of transaction value, and potential criminal penalties

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for penalties and interest if they authorize non-compliant transactions
Shell Company or Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of deductions, and penalties under GAAR

Director personal liability: General partners could face civil restitution claims and potential criminal charges for willful non-compliance
AML and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines up to $100,000+, imprisonment for serious offenses, and asset forfeiture

Director personal liability: All parties involved, including directors, may face personal criminal liability, travel bans, and restitution orders
