Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes/register.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada through a limited partnership must register with the CRA for a Business Number (BN), which is the sole identification number for GST/HST, payroll, corporate income tax, and import/export accounts. For a limited partnership, the partnership itself must register for a GST/HST account if it is making taxable supplies in Canada or if its worldwide gross revenue exceeds $30,000 CDN in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters. The registration must be completed using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or via the CRA Business Registration Online portal, and the foreign entity must provide its foreign legal name, governing jurisdiction, and the name and Canadian contact address of its tax representative (agent) if the non-resident partner is required to have a representative. A separate CRA payroll account may be required if the partnership has employees. The partnership’s BN/RT (GST/HST) account must be prominently displayed on all invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada through a limited partnership must register with the CRA for a Business Number (BN), which is the sole identification number for GST/HST, payroll, corporate income tax, and import/export accounts. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Canadian tax law, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax; the partners, not the partnership, are subject to tax on partnership income. The partnership is not eligible for a check-the-box election under Canadian law, but the partners may be subject to classification under the U.S. check-the-box rules for U.S. federal tax purposes. Under Canada’s hybrid mismatch rules (which follow the OECD BEPS Action 2 recommendations), a taxpayer may be subject to disallowance or denial of deductions where a payment gives rise to a deduction in Canada but the corresponding income is not included in a foreign jurisdiction without a corresponding inclusion or if the payment is deductible twice. No statutory ‘check-the-box’ election exists within the Canadian domestic system to alter the pass-through status of a limited partnership for GST/HST; however, for GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate person and may be elected to be a member of a closely related group for simplified GST/HST relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (a person is a registrant if, at the end of any calendar quarter, the total value of all taxable supplies made in Canada in that quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters exceeds $30,000 CDN, or, if taxable supplies exceed $30,000 CDN in a single calendar quarter, the person is required to register effective immediately; this threshold also applies to non-residents engaged in Canadian commercial activity, except where the non-resident supplies solely zero-rated goods/services or has a qualifying condition under the GST/HST rules).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports from Canada are generally zero-rated for GST/HST purposes. A zero-rated supply of goods is reported on the GST/HST return at the zero rate, allowing the registrant to claim input tax credits for GST/HST paid on inputs used in connection with the export. Qualifying export supplies include: (1) goods exported by the supplying vendor from Canada as evidenced by export documentation in the ordinary course of business; (2) services supplied to a non-resident person who is not present in Canada at the time the service is performed, provided the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or a service to be performed wholly or partly in Canada, unless there is a specific statutory exception; (3) digital services and software subscriptions provided to non-residents when the Canadian vendor charges a nominal or no separate fee for the Canadian component, and the service is not for the benefit of a person in Canada; and (4) supplies of intellectual property rights and other intangible personal property supplied to non-residents if the purchaser is not in Canada and the royalty or licence fee is not attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment. The zero-rating provision applies only if the vendor has in its records sufficient evidence to establish that the goods or services were actually exported or received outside Canada.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration (B13A form) for goods exported via registered custom brokers or motor carriers, where required
  • Commercial invoice or pro forma invoice showing the vendor, purchaser, description, quantity, price, and Zero-rated Export indication
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill (sea, air, truck, rail) with Canadian port of export and recognized foreign destination
  • Proof of export customs data (Electronic Export Report or CAED e-manifest record for large exporters)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank wire/payment confirmation evidencing payment from outside Canada
  • Service contract or work order confirming the non-resident status of the purchaser and the offshore performance of the service for software-as-a-service and no-code builder subscriptions

Canada has no legislative real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CIC) regime for e-invoicing at the federal or provincial level. Electronic invoices are permitted if they include all prescribed GST/HST information and are accessible in a readable format. There is no CRA API mandate for invoice clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, and no government pre-clearance of invoices. However, all GST/HST registrants are required to issue or provide an invoice containing the registration number, date of supply, vendor and purchaser identity, and the amount of tax (or zero-rating indication) within 15 days after the supply. Electronic records must be retained in a format that will be accepted by the CRA on audit.

The CRA actively audits zero-rated export claims, especially for digital services and software export transactions. A GST/HST registrant who fails to acquire and retain the prescribed export evidence may be reassessed to remove the zero-rating, resulting in a liability for unremitted GST/HST plus interest (prescribed rate, compounded daily) and potentially significant penalties. For wilful misrepresentation, the Exchequer Court may impose a civil penalty of up to $25,000 or 200% of the tax evaded, as applicable, and criminal prosecution may result in fines up to $100,000 to $500,000 or imprisonment, or both. In addition, retroactive clawbacks may cover up to a statutory limitation period of four years (ordinary assessment) or seven years (for negligence, misrepresentation, or fraud). Penalties for false statements in an invoice or return under sections 283, 285, and 327 of the Excise Tax Act may apply, and a serious or repeated non-compliance may lead to termination of a GST/HST registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account purposes. No exchange controls, surrender requirements, or prior-approval licenses apply to inbound capital contributions or outbound profit remittances by a British Columbia Limited Partnership. There is no statutory profit-repatriation permission requirement under the Bank of Canada Act or the Currency Act. Non-resident limited partners receiving allocations from a BC LP are subject to Canadian income tax by way of Part XIII and Part I rules; the practical repatriation hurdles are limited to satisfying the Canada Revenue Agency's non-resident reporting and withholding requirements (e.g., Form NR4, Section 216 election, or an NR6 arrangement) and evidence of a properly documented capital account. The Canada-US Tax Treaty may reduce the withholding rate for US-resident limited partners, but treaty relief requires a valid Form W-8BEN or similar certification. Because the LP is pass-through for Canadian tax purposes, partnership allocations are not generally characterized as dividends, and the primary compliance focus is on filing an agreed tax return or withholding remittance accurate to the partnership agreement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not mandate the surrender of export proceeds and does not impose a statutory conversion ratio. A BC-incorporated or BC-registered export-facing entity may retain foreign currency receipts in foreign-currency-denominated accounts at Schedule I banks. Payments into and out of Canada above CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; the report is a regulatory filing, not an exchange-control requirement. For trade-statistical purposes, exporters must file the Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A, and the ultimate CAD conversion may be effected at market rates without central-bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC); the prudential supervision of banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while the Department of Finance Canada administers policy on capital movements. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority or exchange-control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium — Offshore digital EMI/neo-bank providers may be used for treasury optimization, disbursement pooling, and foreign-exchange execution, but they must not act as the primary clearing rail for the BC Limited Partnership. Canadian-dollar clearing and settlement obligations should be held with a Payments Canada participant or a Schedule I bank that is subject to OSFI regulation and FINTRAC AML/ATF supervision. Offshore EMIs generally cannot issue CAD-denominated commercial letters of credit or provide statutory escrow accounting, and they may introduce jurisdiction-of-funding complications during bank underwriting. A dual-rail structure is recommended: a Canadian Schedule I bank for capital injection, profit distributions, and commercial payment obligations, with a regulated FATF-jurisdiction EMI only for collection consolidation from global payment providers such as Stripe and Paddle, subject to prior disclosure to the Canadian relationship manager.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners must be wired by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank-transfer protocol to the BC LP's segregated capital account. The reference field must explicitly identify the LP registration number, the fund description (e.g., 'CA LP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION'), and the purpose code. Although Canada does not maintain a standardized exchange-control purpose-code list, the remitting bank's purpose code should reflect 'EQUI' (equity-related capital) or 'DCCT' (deposit to current account) for debt-funding. The bank will require evidence of the underlying commercial rationale and once the wire is received, a written capital-contribution ledger entry dated to the value date is necessary. No B2B or B2C transaction permission from a central bank is required; however, any reversal or deemed distribution to the foreign partner must be documented as a capital reduction, loan repayment, or dividend according to the LP Agreement to avoid mischaracterization under the Income Tax Act.The BC LP's outbound disbursements to foreign vendors, resellers, and non-resident partners must be cleared through the following priority rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for individually valuable disbursements, including profit allocations, fixed-fee vendor payments, and acquisition consideration; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued via MT700/MT710 for procurement contracts, particularly where the merchant requires a bank undertaking or a documentary condition; (iii) batched local EFT through Payments Canada for Canadian domestic payments only; and (iv) cross-currency sweeps via the same-day CAD/USD settlement account to minimize conversion spreads. For any non-resident partner distribution, the bank must receive a signed payment instruction that mirrors the LP Agreement's waterfall; a certified copy of Form NR4 or an NR6 election notice must be retained in the bank file for withholding transparency. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Nil at the BC limited partnership entity level because the LP is fiscally transparent. For corporate partners resident in Canada, the combined federal plus BC headline corporate income tax rate is 27% (federal Part I general rate 15% plus BC general corporate rate 12%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Canadian Part XIII dividend withholding tax rate is 25% on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Because the BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent, distributions of previously taxed partnership profits are not legal dividends and are generally not subject to Part XIII dividend withholding. Any interest, rent, royalty, or management fee paid by the LP to a non-resident may attract Part XIII withholding at 25% unless reduced or exempt under a tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transfers, services, loans, and IP arrangements with non-arm's-length non-residents are reportable once the aggregate value of reportable transactions exceeds C$5,000,000 in a fiscal period. Mandatory Master File and Local File documentation applies to reporting taxpayers whose annual group revenue is at least C$50,000,000 and whose reportable transactions exceed C$5,000,000. Even below these thresholds, CRA can require contemporaneous documentation prepared before the filing deadline.
