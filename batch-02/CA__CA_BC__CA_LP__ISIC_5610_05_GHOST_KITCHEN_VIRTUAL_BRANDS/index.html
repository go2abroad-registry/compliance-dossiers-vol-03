Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal GST/HST, income tax and Business Number administration; portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html). E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with the CRA before carrying on taxable activity. The BN, plus the applicable program account code (e.g., 'RT' for GST/HST), functions as the Canadian TIN/VAT identifier. Apply online through Business Registration Online (BRO), by mail using Form RC1, or by CRA telephone registration; non-residents without a Canadian address must provide a Canadian agent/representative address or authorized representative mandate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A British Columbia Limited Partnership is not a taxpayer for Canadian income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act, s.96; income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners, who include their shares in income. Non-resident corporate partners may additionally be subject to Part XIV branch tax on Canadian-source business income. Supply-chain nexus: the BC LP is itself a person for GST/HST purposes and may be required to register as a GST/HST registrant. No federal tax-exempt zone applies to a standard BC LP.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election. A BC LP is statutorily flow-through; there is no election to treat the partnership as a taxable entity. Hybrid mismatch provisions (ITA ss.12.7 and 18.4) may defer or recharacterize deductions arising from cross-border partnership/hybrid arrangements, but they do not permit entity-classification changes. For GST/HST, joint elections are available for certain deemed supplies and closely related parties; however, none alter the underlying partnership status or GST/HST registration obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 taxable-supply threshold for federal GST/HST registration (mandatory once exceeded in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive consecutive quarters); BC PST has no small-supplier registration threshold and generally requires PST registration before making any taxable sales into British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, supplies of prepared food or beverages by a restaurant or delivery-oriented ghost kitchen consumed in Canada are taxable at 5% GST and 7% BC PST. Export zero-rating at 0% GST applies where tangible personal property is sold by the BC LP and exported from Canada by commercial export or by the supplier, with documented proof of delivery outside Canada. Services supplied to a non-resident qualify for zero-rating when the service is performed wholly outside Canada or otherwise falls within Schedule VII of the ETA. Zero-rated export suppliers may claim input tax credits for GST/HST paid on inputs attributable to those exports, while BC PST generally does not apply to goods delivered outside BC.

  • Canada Export Declaration / CBSA Customs Self-Assessment or Canada Border Services Agency export documentation (when required, including B13A or electronic CAED)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Waybill / Airway Bill with consignee outside Canada
  • Packing List
  • Proof of Export such as customs stamps, terminal receipts, courier delivery confirmation, or export broker certificates
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign-exchange records evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds

Canada has no real-time electronic invoice clearance mandate, no centralized digital stamping platform, and no API-based pre-clearance requirement analogous to Latin American fiscal regimes. CRA accepts electronic invoices and requires businesses to retain books and records in a readable and readily accessible format. GST/HST registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or CRA My Business Account when required. BC PST registrants must file/remit PST through BC Ministry of Finance online services; taxable invoices or receipts should show the vendor's GST/HST BN and PST registration number and separately state the 7% PST charged on prepared restaurant/food-service meals.

CRA and BC Ministry of Finance closely audit export claims, digital delivery records, payout reconciliations from UberEats/DoorDash, and linked foreign-exchange remittances. If export zero-rating is disallowed, CRA will reassess the supply at 5% GST (and PST may also be asserted by BC) plus compound daily interest and may impose negligence penalties. Gross-negligence penalties can reach 25% of the amount under Excise Tax Act s.285; failure to register or remit can attract separate penalties under s.280. Fraudulent export claims may expose the registrant and principals to prosecution, BC PST assessments with penalties and interest, and retrospective clawbacks of input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no exchange-control approval for inbound capital contributions, trade payments, or profit remittance. A British Columbia Limited Partnership may distribute cash flows to foreign partners by wire without Bank of Canada authorization, provided that tax compliance is satisfied: allocated partnership income is reportable by each partner, while remittances of interest, rent, royalties, management fees, or other specified Canadian-source amounts to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory timeline for bringing export receipts onshore, and no minimum conversion ratio into Canadian dollars. Export and delivery-aggregator proceeds may be retained indefinitely in US-dollar or other foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks. Physical import or export of currency or monetary instruments of CAD $10,000 or more must be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), and financial institutions must report prescribed electronic fund transfers and large transactions to FINTRAC as applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada is the central monetary authority; there is no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated deposit-taking institutions, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing compliance for banking and money-services businesses in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Stripe may be used as secondary settlement or payout rails for UberEats and DoorDash receipts, but they are not authorized Canadian banks under the Bank Act (Canada). If operating into Canada, they must register as money-services businesses with FINTRAC and cannot accept deposits or act as the principal treasury custodian for the BC Limited Partnership. Inbound capital injections should be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor to the BC LP's designated CAD or USD account at a Canadian tier-1 bank. Remittance Information (Field 70) must clearly state: 'LP capital contribution, [legal name of limited partnership], BC LP registration number'. Intermediary routing should avoid co-mingling personal funds; the remitter name in Field 50K must match the partner or investor executing the capital call. For USD settlement, banks generally clear through their New York correspondents; for CAD settlement, Payments Canada ACSS or Lynx rails are used. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory audit is imposed by the BC Limited Partnership Act or the Partnership Act. An independent audit becomes compulsory when: (a) the partnership agreement or a unanimous partner resolution requires audited financial statements; (b) a limited partner validly exercises inspection rights and the partnership agreement grants an audit remedy; (c) institutional lenders or delivery-platform financing agreements require a certified audit; or (d) the LP becomes a reporting issuer under Canadian securities law. For federal information purposes, a T5013 with complete financial statements is mandatory when gross revenue exceeds CAD $2,000,000 or when any partner is a corporation or another partnership (ITA s. 233.5), but CRA does not require those statements to be audited.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined Federal (15%) + British Columbia (12%) general corporate income tax rate; 0% at the limited partnership itself because Canadian LPs are fiscally transparent entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under ITA Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents. For a BC limited partnership, distributions of allocated business income to non-resident partners are not dividends: the non-resident partner is taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income, and the partnership generally has no Part XIII withholding obligation on the profit distribution itself.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate reportable transactions with a single non-arm's length non-resident in a taxation year triggers Form T106 filing with CRA. Canada does not impose separate Master File and Local File forms, but contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation sufficient to support the arm's length result is mandatory where transactions exceed this threshold. CRA may request an OECD-compliant Master File and Country-by-Country Report when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia imposes no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those in Cayman/BVI jurisdictions. Canada instead applies ITA s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule, common law concepts of sham and substance over form, and the OECD permanent establishment/business nexus principles. A BC LP operated as a shell through multiple virtual brand entities without real employees, kitchen assets, delivery management, or operational substance is vulnerable to reassessment, penalties, and treaty override.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.238/s.239, a conviction for tax evasion can result in a fine equal to 50%–200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA may also impose a civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors of the LP's corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted employee source deductions, CPP/EI, and GST/HST under s.227.1 and s.323 of the Excise Tax Act. CRA can assess directors personally, garnish bank accounts, place liens on personal assets, and seek court orders restricting travel when prosecution is pending.
Shell Company / Artificial Conduit

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the GAAR to cancel the tax benefit, reassess the entire arrangement, disallow foreign tax credits, and impose a 50% gross negligence penalty. Transfer pricing rules under s.247 may also result in upward adjustment plus a 10% penalty on excessive profits.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the LP was used as a sham or vehicle for fraud, making general partners and directors jointly and severally liable for the full reassessed tax and penalties. Directors can be subject to personal bankruptcy, management-ban orders, travel bans, and extradition proceedings where offshore conduit activity is criminalised.
AML / Structuring of Settlement Payouts

Statutory sanction: Under Criminal Code s.462.31, laundering proceeds of designated offences is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years. FINTRAC compliance penalties for reporting entities can reach CAD 1 million per violation, with additional forfeiture orders for traced proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or direct such settlement schemes may be charged as principals or parties to the offence. Personal assets may be subject to restraint or forfeiture, and Canadian criminal courts may impose a pre-trial travel ban and require surrender of passports.
Mischaracterization of Delivery Worker Remuneration

Statutory sanction: CRA can reassess the LP for up to 12 months of unremitted source deductions, interest, and a 10% failure-to-remit penalty. Provincial employment standards can also impose unpaid wages, reinstatement orders, and administrative fines for sham contractor arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally and jointly/severally liable for unpaid source deductions, CPP, EI, and GST/HST remittances. In addition, directors may be subject to provincial enforcement remedies including personal recovery orders, prohibition on business travel, and, in aggravated cases, criminal prosecution.
