Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and federal income tax registration, administered through the CRA Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal; BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity deriving Canadian-source taxable revenue must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA by filing Form RC1 or by using CRA Business Registration Online. The BN is the statutory TIN for federal tax and the GST/HST account uses the BN plus the two-letter program identifier 'RT'. Non-resident vendors making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register for GST/HST unless they qualify as small suppliers. For BC PST, the vendor must separately register through eTaxBC and obtain a PST account number before selling taxable goods or services in British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic 'check-the-box' election; a BC ULC is generally a resident corporation taxable at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act. For non-Canadian tax classification, the ULC may be eligible for a U.S. check-the-box election under Treasury Regulation §301.7701-3 by filing IRS Form 8832, electing to be treated as a corporation or as a disregarded/partnership-type flow-through entity. Canadian anti-hybrid mismatch rules remain applicable and may disallow tax advantages arising from conflicting entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada during a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. BC PST: no general small-supplier exemption; a vendor selling taxable tangible personal property or taxable services in British Columbia must register regardless of revenue level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of tangible personal property, including musical instruments and studio audio gear, qualifies for 0% GST/HST when sold to a non-resident recipient and the goods are exported from Canada as part of the supply. The seller must retain evidence satisfactory to CRA showing delivery to an address outside Canada, export by common carrier or courier, or transfer of ownership outside Canada before the goods are consumed or used in Canada. Offshore services supplied to non-residents may also qualify for zero-rating when the services are performed outside Canada and available only for use outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Automated Export Declaration/eManifest record)
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods, buyer, value, and ISIC 4791 classification
  • Bill of Lading or courier airway bill evidencing export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/FX receipt showing payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Payment and purchase records proving consideration was received and the goods were not returned for use in Canada

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time electronic invoice clearance regime, government pre-approval, API fiscal stamping, or digital tax stamp for GST/HST. Electronic invoicing is accepted provided records are complete, accurate, legible, and retained in a manner that satisfies CRA audit requirements. Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is voluntary, not a statutory fiscal clearance mandate.

CRA may reassess export zero-rating within the normal four-year reassessment period, extended to eight years where misrepresentation or fraud is involved. If export evidence is insufficient, the export is reclassified as a taxable domestic supply and the vendor is assessed the applicable GST/HST and, if applicable, BC PST. A knowing or grossly negligent false statement or omission can trigger a civil penalty of $100 plus 50% of the tax amount that would have been assessed under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, together with interest and potential criminal prosecution for fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act. There are no exchange-control laws, no conversion licenses, and no restriction on repatriation of equity capital or remittance of profits by a British Columbia ULC. The only fiscal constraints are Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding taxes on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents (25% statutory, commonly reduced to 5-15% under applicable tax treaties) and ULC-specific provincial liability-survival rules. A ULC is a pass-through-eligible hybrid; capital withdrawals must be documented as share-capital reductions or unpaid-dividend elections to avoid s. 84(2)/(3) deemed-dividend recharacterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A BC ULC may retain foreign-currency balances onshore or offshore indefinitely and may settle in USD, EUR, JPY, or any freely convertible currency. Regulatory duties are limited to reporting: financial institutions must report single electronic fund transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC, and the ULC itself must maintain complete books and records tracing the source, conversion, and disposition of export proceeds for Canada Revenue Agency audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central monetary authority; foreign-exchange administration is decentralized, with prudential oversight exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) over deposit-taking Canadian financial institutions and AML/ATF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rated MEDIUM-HIGH as an auxiliary FX/settlement corridor and LOW as a primary treasury anchor. FINTRAC-registered digital payment providers (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) may lawfully hold CAD and route cross-border supplier wires for a BC ULC, but the Retail Payment Activities Act supervision regime requires providers serving the Canadian market to establish and register a Canadian-nexus entity. Offshore FCA- or ECB-licensed EMIs can be used for supplier disbursements, yet offshore balances are subject to FATCA/CRS transparency and FINTRAC bank-file reporting. Prudent design: maintain the core treasury and merchant settlement at a Schedule I chartered bank, and use one FINTRAC-registered EMI corridor exclusively for high-frequency, low-value overseas supplier payments where all-in FX-plus-fee cost is at least 60 basis points below the chartered-bank SWIFT tariff.
