Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online portal; for British Columbia provincial sales tax: British Columbia Ministry of Finance - eTaxBC portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned ULC carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account from the CRA before or within 30 days of commencing taxable activities. Registration is effected via CRA's Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1, accompanied by proof of legal existence (e.g., BC incorporation certificate, certified articles, and foreign certificate of status if recently migrated). If the ULC is a non-resident for tax purposes, Form RC471 and a completed NR301 treaty-position form may also be required. The BN is the single tax identifier for corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts. Separately, the entity must register for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through eTaxBC before selling taxable goods or services in BC; a distinct BC PST account number is issued. There is no statutory minimum share capital for a BC ULC, and a non-resident owner may hold 100% of the shares without special approval, but a registered address and a BC-resident authorized representative are required for service of process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not permit a domestic Check-the-Box election for ULCs; a BC/AB ULC is a separate legal person and is taxed on its taxable income at entity level under the Income Tax Act. The 'pass-through eligible' characteristic of a Canadian ULC arises under foreign tax systems (notably the U.S. Check-the-Box rules) where an election on IRS Form 8832 can treat the ULC as a partnership or disregarded entity for U.S. federal income tax purposes. Such an election creates hybrid and branch-level mismatch exposure under Canada's anti-hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.4 to 18.6) and the EIFEL interest-stripping provisions. Canadian statutory election mechanisms that may be relevant include the s. 216 election for non-resident rental income and the s. 216.1 election for non-resident treaty-based relief, as well as the s. 219 non-resident corporation election; none converts the ULC into a domestic pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); non-residents that make taxable supplies in Canada and exceed this threshold must register, and voluntary registration is available. BC PST: registration is mandatory for any person making taxable sales of goods or services in BC, regardless of revenue level; there is no general small-supplier exemption, although isolated or occasional sales exemptions may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For a BC-based ULC manufacturing aseptic hydrolyzed oat milk, zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported by the supplier from Canada under section 22 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The supplier must export the goods as part of the same supply transaction and retain adequate evidence of export, including that the goods were shipped to a non-resident purchaser and left Canada within a reasonable time. Services supplied to non-resident persons (including toll manufacturing, aseptic filling, packaging, and quality-control services performed for a non-resident principal) may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, section 7, provided the services do not relate to real property situated in Canada or other excluded categories. All zero-rated inputs must be substantiated with ordinary commercial records; any input tax credits related to exempt or non-qualifying supplies must be properly proportioned.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA CERS electronic export filing or Form B13A)
  • Commercial invoice with full description, value, HS classification, and origin
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill or other shipping receipt proving departure from Canada
  • Packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT / TT wire confirmation showing receipt of export proceeds
  • Sales contract or purchase order evidencing the foreign buyer and terms of sale
  • Insurance certificate (where applicable)
  • Certificate of origin (if claiming preferential tariff treatment)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing clearance regime. GST/HST registrants must issue and retain invoices in a form that satisfies the Excise Tax Act, and electronic invoices are acceptable if fully legible and retained for six years. Exports of goods are subject to electronic export reporting with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), but there is no pre-shipment fiscal approval or government 'digital stamp'. BC PST returns, remittances, and registration filings are processed electronically through eTaxBC.

The CRA, BC Ministry of Finance, and CBSA may jointly audit export claims. Where a supplier fails to produce satisfactory evidence of export within the applicable retention period (six years), the CRA will deny the zero-rating and reassess GST at 5% on the sale value, plus interest and penalties under the Excise Tax Act (including s. 285 penalties of up to 25% of the amount claimed for knowing or fraudulent misstatements). Negligent or willful misrepresentation may also lead to penalties under s. 239 of the Income Tax Act, monetary fines, and criminal prosecution. BC PST may be assessed on goods found to have been diverted to domestic use in BC, with penalties up to 20% of the tax due. Although Canadian law does not impose a strict statutory FX repatriation deadline, failure to receive or repatriate export proceeds within 180 days of shipment may be treated as a strong indicator that no true export occurred, leading to retrospective clawback of zero-rating, VAT reassessment, penalty assessments, and possible loss of the small-supplier threshold for future periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible, market-determined currency regime for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely floatable with no surrender, surrender-equivalent conversion, or capital-account licensing framework. Non-resident profit remittance, ULC dividend distributions, shareholder loan repayments, and foreign direct investment repatriation are legally unrestricted; the only material hurdles are Canada's Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on certain outbound payments, transfer-pricing compliance on related-party transactions, and bank-level AML/KYC documentary requirements. There is no requirement to use official exchange rates or to obtain approval for currency conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada under federal or British Columbia law. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, converted freely through any Schedule I Canadian bank, and used for offshore trade settlement. A UHT plant exporting oat milk will still be required to report exports in customs records and to FINTRAC for any single cross-border transfer equal to or greater than CAD 10,000, but this is an AML reporting obligation, not a foreign-exchange control.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act as the central monetary and note-issuing authority. Foreign exchange administration is decentralized: the Minister of Finance retains policy authority, OSFI supervises federally regulated banking institutions, and FINTRAC enforces AML/ATF reporting. No Canadian entity requires central bank approval for cross-border payments or current-account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium as a standalone account platform for a BC ULC treasury structure. Offshore electronic money institutions and neobanks are not authorized to accept deposits in Canada under the Bank Act and cannot independently provide CAD-denominated deposit insurance or SWIFT corporate clearing. An offshore EMI may be used only as a payment facilitator for small disbursements if it is registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC, has verifiable AML controls, and maintains a settlement account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. For capital escrow, supplier payments, and sight-letter-of-credit settlements, regulated Canadian bank infrastructure is mandatory.

In the remittance field, bankers should include the ISO purpose code 'EQUI' for equity injection or 'LOAN' for shareholder subordinated debt, together with the memorandum 'BC ULC capital contribution' and the BC incorporation number. The receiving Canadian bank requires pre-funded KYC clearance and source-of-funds documentation before the first wire; single transfers at or above CAD 10,000 trigger the FINTRAC electronic funds transfer report.
