Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. There is no Exchange Control Act or statutory foreign-exchange licensing regime; both capital injections and profit repatriation are freely permitted. For a British Columbia limited partnership with non-resident partners, distributions are subject to Part XIII withholding tax unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty, and Form NR4 must be filed. Convertibility and transfers do not require Bank of Canada approval, but cross-border wire transfers over C$10,000 must be reported by the receiving financial institution to FINTRAC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for conversion of foreign-currency export receipts. Digital app subscription revenue earned via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing may be retained in USD or other foreign currency, converted at commercial rates, or kept in multi-currency treasury accounts without prior regulatory authorization. Standard CRA reporting for income and GST/HST remains the only compliance obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, operating in concert with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML oversight, and the Department of Finance for exchange-control policy formulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Canada does not grant foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) bank status or the ability to take unrestricted deposits. An offshore EMI may operate as a money-services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC and offer payment-clearing services, but it cannot provide regulated deposit services, escrow, or lending products. For this BC LP, an offshore EMI is viable only as a supplementary disbursement or payout engine; the primary treasury relationship and clearing infrastructure must remain anchored with an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Wire instructions must reference the LP's legal name, Canadian Business Number, and an alphanumeric capital-call reference. The receiving bank should be supplied with the following for routing and FINTRAC due diligence: the capital contribution statement, the limited partnership certificate, a board or general-partner resolution approving the capital call, and the underlying subscription agreement. Purpose codes should identify the transfer as investment/capital, such as the SWIFT purpose code 'INVS' (investment) or 'CAPT' (capital transfer), consistent with the bank's fixed wire-transfer formats. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and British Columbia headline corporate income tax rate of 27% (15% federal general rate plus 12% BC general corporate rate). The BC limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent; each corporate partner will be subject to corporate rates on its allocated share, while individual partners are taxed at personal progressive rates (top combined BC marginal rate approximately 53.5%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source income allocated to a non-resident partner, including income flowing from the fitness app partnership. Where a corporate partner owns at least 10% of the votes of the recipient payer and a DTA applies, the treaty rate is 5%; for portfolio distributions the treaty rate is 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For Canada, a Local File and contemporaneous transfer-pricing study is expected where aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions of the LP exceed CA$1,000,000 in the fiscal year. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are mandatory for members of an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of CA$750,000,000 or more. Transactions between the LP and a related foreign IP-holding entity should be supported by a benchmarked arm's-length pricing analysis.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not implement Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead, substance is tested through ITA s.247 transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule in s.245, the 'sham' doctrine, and the 'brass-arrow' and limited-partner control rules. For a BC limited partnership, limited partners lose liability insulation if they participate in control of the business; the LP must also maintain real premises, employees, books, and contractual decision-making in BC to resist treaty-shopping and substance-based assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Non-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Indictable conviction under Income Tax Act s.239(1) exposing a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; CRA may also impose gross negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax plus compound arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be assessed jointly and severally under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted withholding; civil restitution may attach to personal assets; conviction will cause criminal record, restrictions on serving as a director, potential detention under the Criminal Code, and, for proven flight risk, a Canadian travel ban under s.225.2 departure order.
Shell Company / Artificial IP Planning

Statutory sanction: CRA will apply s.247 to reallocate profits, s.245 GAAR to disregard the arrangement, and reassess with late-filing penalties; under the OECD multilateral instrument, such a hybrid or shell arrangement may be denied treaty benefits, resulting in full 25% Part XIII withholding on payments historically treated as exempt.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or approve artificial arrangements face personal assessments for negligent planning under the Canadian common-law duty; a BC court may pierce the LP veil, rendering general partners and controlling directors jointly liable for full corporate and tax debts, and may order a non-resident director's equitable compensation under cross-border arbitration letters.
AML / FINTRAC Reporting Breach for Digital Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CA$500,000 for an individual and CA$1,000,000 for a partnership, plus criminal fines or imprisonment for money laundering under the Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors of an unregistered MSB face personal liability for unreported capital gains when proceeds are traced; an inability to produce records can lead to an initial injunction freezing the partnership's bank accounts and personal bank accounts of directors under the Criminal Code proceeds-of-crime provisions.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under s.247(2)/(3) adds the transaction adjustment to income; a penalty under s.247(3) equals 10% of the adjustment for failing to make a reasonable effort to determine arm's-length price; in aggravated cases, s.163(2) negligence penalties of 50% of understated tax apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of the LP's general partner may be sued in civil courts for knowing participation in a s.247 arrangement; if the LP becomes insolvent because of the reassessment, the directors can be held personally liable for unpaid employee source withholdings and unremitted royalties through the Crown's priority charge over partnership assets.
