Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal GST/HST and income tax administration; registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). Provincial: BC Provincial Sales Tax Branch, British Columbia Ministry of Finance — PST administration and registration portal: eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the BC Limited Partnership under the Partnership Act (BC) and obtain a Partnership registration number from the BC Corporate Registry. Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) for the partnership via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO); the LP, as a 'person' under the Excise Tax Act, opens a GST/HST Program Account (RP account) on the BN once taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed CAD 30,000 in the small-supplier look-back (four consecutive calendar quarters or a single calendar quarter). Step 3: Register as a BC PST vendor through eTaxBC; registration is mandatory on commencement of retail sales at the BC tea salon (no monetary threshold for a vendor with a BC place of business; remote sellers without BC presence must register once BC taxable sales exceed CAD 10,000 in the preceding 12 months). Step 4: The LP also maintains a Payroll (RP? no—RT) program account if employees are engaged in the salon. Step 5: Non-resident partners must disclose their foreign TINs to the partnership for information return purposes; each non-resident partner is directly assessable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on its allocated share of the LP's Canadian-source business income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax: the LP computes income or loss at partnership level under ITA s.96 and allocates items to partners, with no entity-level tax; limited partners are subject to at-risk loss restriction rules (ITA s.96(2.1)). For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is a distinct 'person' that registers, collects, and claims input tax credits independently. For BC PST, the LP is the 'vendor' liable to collect and remit PST. Territorial scope: the LP is taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source allocations; non-resident partners pay Part I tax on their Canadian business income shares and Part XIII withholding on passive allocations.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections for the CA_LP include: (1) ITA s.97(2) partnership roll-over election to defer recognition on property contributed to the LP; (2) ITA s.216 election by a non-resident partner to file a Part I return for Canadian rental income otherwise subject to Part XIII withholding; (3) GST/HST Quick Method of accounting election (ETA ss.227-228) for eligible small retail/food suppliers (remittance rates 2.5%-3.6% depending on supply type, subject to CAD 400,000 annual threshold); (4) GST/HST filing frequency elections (monthly vs. quarterly vs. annual) under ETA s.245; and (5) formation of a special purpose corporation for ring-fenced rental property if the LP needs to limit non-resident partner obligations. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s.18 (related-party financing), ss.94-94.3) apply to constrain interest deductibility on cross-border partnership financing arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — federal small-supplier threshold for mandatory GST/HST registration (total worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or a current calendar quarter once exceeded; voluntary registration is available below the threshold). PST: nil threshold for a vendor with a place of business in British Columbia; CAD 10,000 in BC taxable sales in the preceding 12 months triggers mandatory PST registration for an out-of-province/remote vendor selling into BC without a BC place of business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating may be claimed by the LP on: (i) tangible specialty tea merchandise (packaged loose-leaf tea, ceramic teaware sold as goods) physically exported from Canada, provided the LP holds documentary proof of export that the goods were delivered outside Canada to a foreign address by the LP, consignee/agent, or common carrier, and the supply is not made to a Canadian consumer (Schedule VI Part V, ss.20-21, ETA); and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person where the service is performed entirely outside Canada or relates to real property/tangible personal property located outside Canada and the service is for the foreign customer's consumption outside Canada (Schedule VI Part V, s.7, ETA). On-premises tea ceremony experiences, salon lounge service, and food/beverages prepared and consumed in the BC salon are taxable at 5% GST + 7% PST and are not zero-rated. GST input tax credits are recoverable on the LP's taxable purchases; BC PST is not recoverable as input tax (retail nature), but purchased tea inventory for resale may be acquired PST-free using a PST Resale Exemption Certificate (Form PST-Resale-001).

  • Customs Export Declaration: CBSA B13A paper form or CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) electronic export transaction reference
  • Commercial Invoice (bilingual EN/FR) identifying the foreign buyer, description, HS tariff classification, quantity, and value
  • Bill of Lading / Through Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or courier proof-of-export receipt evidencing physical departure of the goods from Canada
  • Cargo Control Number / carrier dispatch and destination record confirming delivery address outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/payment gateway statement evidencing receipt of FX export proceeds from the non-resident buyer (commonly benchmarked to a 180-day repatriation expectation)
  • Foreign customer purchase order/contract and packing slip, with Certificate of Origin or CPTPP/Canada origin certification where claimed

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or digital stamping mandate exists under federal GST/HST law or BC PST law. CRA requires GST/HST invoices to contain prescribed information under ETA s.169(2) and the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations; invoices may be paper, PDF, or structured e-invoice (e.g., XML/PEPPOL pilot formats). BC PST requires a vendor to issue a completed PST sales slip/tax invoice for each taxable retail transaction and to retain transaction records for audit. Digital records must be retained for six years per ETA s.286 and CRA record-keeping requirements.

CRA may reassess to claw back the 0% GST rate and apply 5% GST and 7% PST to the full export consideration whenever export documentation is incomplete or the supply fails the zero-rating conditions; reassessment is available within the normal four-year limitation period (ETA s.298), extended to ten years for neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Civil penalties include: a gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the net tax understatement (ETA s.285.2); false-statement penalties of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax (ETA s.280); daily compound interest on outstanding amounts; director/officer joint and several liability for unreported GST/PST (ETA s.323; BC Social Service Tax Act); and criminal prosecution for knowing tax evasion (ETA s.381). Export zero-rating files are a CRA high-priority audit area for food/beverage and retail exporters; documentary insufficiency triggers automatic recharacterization as domestic taxable supplies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada maintains no exchange control regime and no restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or capital repatriation. Non-resident partners are subject to the withholding tax provisions of Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), but no central-bank approval or statutory conversion license is required. Treaty relief under the Canada–US Tax Convention or other applicable tax treaties may reduce withholding rates; Forms NR301/NR302 are filed with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into Canadian dollars. Export receipts may be retained, settled, or converted at the entity's discretion. However, all cross-border currency transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and the underlying export transactions must be substantiated in accordance with CRA record-keeping requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act, with prudential regulation of federally chartered banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Canadian law permits non-bank financial technology companies and electronic money institutions to offer payment services as Registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), supervised by the Bank of Canada, and as MSBs registered with FINTRAC. Foreign digital EMIs may maintain settlement accounts in Canada only if they are duly registered and licensed; otherwise they cannot lawfully provide payment services to a Canadian resident entity. For a specialty tea salon lounge, fintech EMIs are viable for payment aggregation and foreign-currency expense management, but they are not substitutes for a full-service Schedule I commercial bank when treasury clearing, credit facilities, and trade instruments such as letters of credit are required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident partners should be wired using the SWIFT MT103 format directly to the LP's designated capital escrow or operational account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The remittance must be prefaced with a textual purpose of payment clearly describing the transfer as an 'unregistered Limited Partnership capital contribution' and must cite the BC registration number of the entity. The receiving bank should be advised in advance through a ‘pre-advice’ notice containing the full legal entity name, account number, authorized signatory details, and the expected credit date. For partner contributions from the United States, USD may be cleared via correspondent banking arrangements or converted to CAD using the bank's standing foreign-exchange instruction. Any contribution exceeding CAD 10,000 is reportable to FINTRAC; the bank may request the capital commitment agreement and source-of-funds documentation to satisfy the enhanced verification requirement.Trade disbursements for imported high-end loose-leaf tea, ceremonial ceramics, and associated merchandise shall be executed through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open-account and advance-payment suppliers, supported by SWIFT MT799 pre-advice where the counterparty requires security. For larger or first-time transactions, a commercial documentary letter of credit (L/C) using SWIFT MT700, with subsequent MT707 amendments as needed, provides secure bilateral settlement. Payment terms should be structured as 20% advance / 80% at-sight L/C to balance supplier risk and liquidity efficiency. Settlement accounts for CAD and USD are held at the primary Canadian bank, while EUR and RMB disbursements are made through the bank's correspondent network.
