Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal GST/HST, income tax, and payroll accounts. Operational portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html) and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html). British Columbia provincial tax authority: Ministry of Finance, Revenue and Tax Branch for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) via eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca); BC Corporate Registry (https://www.corporateonline.gov.bc.ca) administers limited partnership formation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the Declaration of Limited Partnership under the BC Partnership Act (RSBC 1996, c. 348) with the BC Corporate Registry; obtain the BC registration number; no minimum capital contribution is statutorily required. Step 2: Register the LP with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online, by telephone, or by mail; open the GST/HST program account (RT), adding payroll (RP) and customs (RM) accounts only where applicable; a partnership is a registrable person for ETA purposes and may hold its own BN/RT. Step 3: Register for BC PST via eTaxBC quoting the federal BN when taxable BC supplies of PST-designated goods or designated services are made; commercial 3D animation/VFX production services are generally not PST-designated services. Step 4: Non-resident partners are permitted without federal authorization; where the LP carries on business in Canada but has no Canadian residence, a non-resident GST/HST registrant must appoint a Canadian resident agent under ETA s. 273 and file the applicable agency election; the general partner's home-jurisdiction registration or certificate of good standing should be kept on file. Step 5: Maintain the T5013 Partnership Information Return and BC filing obligations, allocating income and losses to partners for direct inclusion in each partner's return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: ITA s. 96(1) and (2) compute income at the partnership level and allocate it to partners; the LP is not an income taxpayer unless it becomes a specified investment flow-through (SIFT) partnership under ITA s. 197, which is not applicable in this scope. For GST/HST, the partnership is itself a person and can be a registrant, file returns, and claim input tax credits, while partners remain jointly and severally liable for partnership GST/HST obligations. Territorial scope: BC-source income allocated to each partner is taxed in each partner's hands; foreign limited partners may also be exposed to Canadian branch withholding/composite issues on allocated income but the LP itself has no entity-level tax.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box mechanism exists in Canada; classification is dictated by the legal form and by ITA/ETA rules. Key elections and designations: (i) alternative fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 with Form T1139 where all partners qualify; (ii) income-allocation designation under ITA s. 96(3) evidenced through the T5013 Partnership Information Return; (iii) nil-consideration election under ETA s. 156 between closely related registrants; (iv) going-concern election under ETA s. 167 to zero-rate the supply of substantially all of the LP's commercial activity; (v) non-resident agent election under ETA s. 273 for foreign registrants to file through a Canadian agent; (vi) hybrid-mismatch provisions in ITA ss. 18.2 to 18.4 operate as automatic anti-avoidance deduction-disallowance rules rather than elective regimes, though the taxpayer may file designations under applicable hybrid-mismatch forms where an adjusted alternative treatment is legally available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) made in Canada or worldwide, as applicable, over the current calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148(1)). Zero-rated supplies count for this threshold; a registrant LP that crosses the threshold must charge and remit GST. PST (BC): no de minimis monetary threshold; registration through eTaxBC is required before making taxable supplies of PST-designated goods or designated services in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible property is zero-rated under ETA s. 21 where the supplier exports the goods or the goods are shipped by common carrier at the supplier's risk. For offshore services, a supply of services to a non-resident person is zero-rated under the ETA place-of-supply rules and Schedule VI Part V when the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant and the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or intangible personal property for use in Canada. For the commercial 3D animation/VFX segment, this includes rendering, animation, motion-graphics, and post-production services delivered digitally to foreign studios, broadcasters, or production companies, provided the non-resident recipient does not acquire the service for use in Canada and the work does not relate to Canadian-situated property. The exporter must retain contemporaneous records showing the non-resident status of the recipient, the Canadian-located rendering activity, the deliverable, and the consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A or equivalent export reporting evidence) for any tangible deliverables or media goods physically exported from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export movement from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank-payment-rail export receipts (e.g., SWIFT MT103, Wise B2B confirmation) evidencing remittance and repatriation of export proceeds
  • Signed contract, statement of work, or purchase order with the non-resident recipient describing the 3D animation/CGI services and delivery terms
  • Non-resident status evidence of the recipient (foreign business registration number, foreign tax ID, or certificate of residence) and a declaration that the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant
  • Zero-rated invoice annotation and internal export substantiation file maintained in the LP's CRA-compliant electronic books and records

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, VAT stamping, or mandatory electronic-invoice clearance mandate. The CRA requires GST/HST registrants to file returns electronically under SOR/2012-316 and to preserve books, records, and source documents, including electronic invoices, in a durable and readable electronic format in accordance with the federal electronic books and records regulatory framework. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing operates on the voluntary Peppol-BIS network; there is no CRA digital clearance or hash-stamping obligation. BC PST returns are likewise filed and paid electronically through the eTaxBC portal; exporters should nonetheless retain full digital audit trails for zero-rated invoices.

CRA's ordinary GST/HST reassessment window is four years under ETA s. 298(2); the window extends indefinitely (ten years or more) where a return is false, fraudulent, or omits material information under ETA s. 298(5). Zero-rating claims are audited against the contemporaneous export file; failure to substantiate export eligibility triggers reassessment of unreported GST at 5%, compounded daily interest, and denial of related input tax credits with retroactive clawback of any refunds paid. False-statement penalties under ETA s. 285.1 (typically the greater of CAD 1,000 and a percentage of the tax or refund misstated) and income-tax gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the understated amount under ITA s. 163(2) can attach to the LP and its partners. Criminal tax evasion sanctions under ETA ss. 327-329 and ITA s. 239 expose fines of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment. Although Canadian law does not prescribe a fixed 180-day statutory repatriation deadline, this dossier adopts the 180-day inward-remittance benchmark as a disciplined treasury control; delayed or non-existent foreign-currency repatriation may additionally attract transfer-pricing, thin-capitalization, or foreign-affiliate scrutiny under ITA ss. 212, 233.4 and 95 where non-arm's length relationships are involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains free convertibility for both capital and current account transactions under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. No exchange control legislation is in force. Profit remittances and capital distributions by a BC Limited Partnership are not subject to FX licensing; however, non-resident partners must account for Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII on certain distributions characterized as amounts not on account of partnership income, and capital repatriation must satisfy solvency and creditor-priority tests under the BC Partnership Act and the Limited Partnership Act. Funds invoiced in foreign currency may be freely held in the United States or other foreign accounts, and there is no obligation to convert export receipts to CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion period, and no forced repatriation timeline for export proceeds earned by a British Columbia resident or non-resident partnership. Receipts may be maintained in USD or any other convertible currency. For practical banking purposes, exporters must report certain cross-border electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC (via the bank) and maintain sufficient records to evidence commercial transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign investment screening administered by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) and the Investment Review Division, Canadian Heritage for cultural businesses

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary operational uses but Low as a primary banking rail. Companies (EMIs) registered as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC may offer foreign payment execution and multi-currency settlement, but they are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and cannot hold full Canadian deposit balances or originate CAD wires through LVTS. A BC LP engaged in commercial 3D animation may use such non-bank rails for small vendor disbursements and inbound client collections, but the vehicle maintaining the capital escrow account must be an OSFI-regulated Schedule I or II bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound contributions must be remitted to the LP's capital escrow account using SWIFT MT103 messages. Field 59 (Beneficiary) must name the BC Limited Partnership and include the LP's operational account number. Field 50K (Ordering Customer) must display the legal name, address, and account number of the remitting partner. The bank will ask for an instructed purpose code in accordance with ISO 20022 / industry practice; typical descriptions include 'Capital contribution', 'Foreign direct investment in limited partnership', or 'Contribution to partnership capital'. The receiving bank verifies entitlement through the partnership agreement and the authenticated instructions of the general partner. For CAD-denominated injections, the correspondent route can use a Canadian credit union or federal bank with access to LVTS; for USD injections, RBC, TD, or BMO will clear through their US correspondent networks. No approval from the Bank of Canada or ISED is required unless the transaction, alone or in combination with other investment, exceeds the national security review thresholds or falls within Canada's cultural-sector screening rules (which cover visual effects and animation distribution rights); in such cases, a filing under the Investment Canada Act may persist.For settled production invoices, the LP should execute payments by SWIFT MT103 using standard clearing rails; USD payments settle through the US Federal Reserve via correspondent banking or the bank's own US branch network; CAD payments settle via LVTS or Real-Time Rail. For large animation sub-production contracts and cloud capacity purchases, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) is appropriate where the supplier requires payment assurance; Documentary Collections (URC 522) may be used for moderate value work-in-progress advances. Disbursements for global VFX talent will typically be routed through payroll or business payment APIs (e.g., Wise B2B, Deel) but must preserve the underlying invoice and retain an MT103 or an equivalent record for bank audit and FINTRAC cross-border funds transfer reporting. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate (federal 15% plus BC 12%). The BC limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent; income is allocated to partners, and a corporate partner taxable in BC on its share pays at the applicable combined corporate rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. A BC limited partnership is tax-transparent; cash distributions of previously taxed Canadian business income are not statutory dividends and no Part XIII withholding applies on the allocation itself.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013 must be filed within five months after the partnership's fiscal year-end; for a December 31 BC fiscal period, the return is due on May 31.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 is due within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. A non-resident corporate partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP files on the same six-month rule. Any corporate tax balance is due two months after year-end (three months for an eligible CCPC claiming the small business deduction).

Deadline: · Frequency:

T106 is filed with the associated T5013 or T2 return: for LPs, within five months after fiscal period-end; for corporate partners, within six months after taxation year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate reportable non-arm's length cross-border transactions per taxation year; above this threshold the taxpayer must file T106 and maintain contemporaneous OECD-aligned Master File and Local File in Canada. Country-by-country reporting applies to Canadian groups with consolidated revenue of C$750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia is not an offshore Economic Substance Regulations jurisdiction. The equivalent Canadian doctrine is found in the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), sham transaction doctrine, and section 247 transfer-pricing rules. A BC limited partnership used for 3D animation/VFX production must have real BC employees, studios or office premises, hardware, production decision-making, contracts, and bank accounts; otherwise CRA will recharacterize the structure as a conduit or sham and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Omission of Partnership Income

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 50% of the misrepresented amount under subsection 163(2) of the Income Tax Act, plus daily compound interest. Criminal conviction under subsection 239(1) carries a fine of at least 50% and up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment or 2 years on summary conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of the corporate general partner who directed, assented to, or acquiesced in the false filing are jointly and severally liable for unremitted taxes under section 227.1, may be prosecuted personally, and may be subject to court travel bans, passport seizure, and personal asset-freezing orders.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess under subsection 247(2) using the arm's length result; subsection 247(8) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net understatement/overstatement adjustment where reasonable-efforts TP documentation requirements are not met; interest accrues retroactively from the reassessed year.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false T106 declarations or instruct subordinates to create false intercompany agreements may be treated as participants in tax evasion; the corporate veil is pierced where the general partner is found to be a sham or fraud, exposing directors to civil restitution and criminal liability.
