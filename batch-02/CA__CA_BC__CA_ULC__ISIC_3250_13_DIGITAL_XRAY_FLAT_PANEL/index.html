Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) administered federally; BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for provincial PST. Official portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html), CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), and BC Corporate Online (https://www.corporateonline.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the ULC under the BC Business Corporations Act via BC Corporate Online and appoint at least one director ordinarily resident in Canada. 2) Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by telephone; the BN is the statutory TIN for GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, and import/export accounts. 3) Activate program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RC (Part I corporate tax), RP (payroll), RM (customs/import-export), and obtain a separate BC PST Vendor Number from the Consumer Taxation Branch. 4) A non-resident corporate shareholder that itself carries on business in Canada must file its own Form RC1 and appoint a Canadian representative for service; the foreign-owned ULC follows the same domestic BN procurement path as a BC-incorporated resident corporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - hybrid ULC structure: the ULC is an opaque resident corporation for Canadian income tax and GST/HST purposes; a single-member or multi-member BC ULC is nonetheless eligible for US 'check-the-box' classification as a disregarded entity or partnership, providing pass-through eligibility for US federal tax while remaining entity-level taxable in Canada.

Available election forms

No domestic CRA 'check-the-box' election exists; a ULC is always a corporation under the Income Tax Act. Available elections: (1) US IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election to classify the ULC as a pass-through for US tax, creating the characteristic Canada-US hybrid profile; (2) Excise Tax Act section 156 election for GST/HST grouping of closely related corporations, suppressing intercompany GST; (3) Income Tax Act hybrid mismatch rules (sections 18.4-18.6, enacted 2022) apply mandatorily to deny deductions in cross-border double-deduction or deduction-non-inclusion arrangements; no elective opt-in is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small-supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including associated entities) in the current calendar quarter and the four preceding quarters. BC PST: vendor registration required for PST-taxable sales in BC unless the vendor qualifies as a small seller (annual BC taxable sales of CAD 10,000 or less) and does not sell through a marketplace.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods (digital X-ray flat panel detectors) qualify for 0% GST under Excise Tax Act (ETA) Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 where the supplier exports the goods from Canada as part of the sale by shipping to a non-resident buyer; ancillary software and installation services supplied to a non-resident qualify under the corresponding Schedule VI provisions when performed or delivered outside Canada. The exporter must retain conclusive proof of export and recover input tax credits through a net-refund GST/HST return.

  • B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration (or CAED/electronic export report) for goods valued above CAD 2,000, plus export permits for controlled or regulated goods if applicable
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the digital X-ray flat panel detectors under HS heading 9022 (parts and accessories of X-ray apparatus)
  • Clean Ocean Bill of Lading or Through Air Waybill evidencing shipment to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 or TT wire confirmation evidencing foreign collection of the purchase price
  • Non-resident buyer declaration or foreign customs import certificate confirming that the goods have left Canada permanently

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or e-invoicing interoperability mandate. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account, or EDI); electronic invoices and source records are acceptable and must be retained for six years for CRA audit review.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims and may demand proof-of-export documentation at any time; unsupported claims are recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST, the corresponding input tax credits are clawed back, and arrears interest runs from the original due date. The normal four-year reassessment period is extended indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Penalties include the ETA section 280 false-statement penalty (the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understated net tax), daily compound arrears interest, criminal prosecution under ETA section 327 for wilful evasion with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment, and director liability under ETA section 323 for unremitted GST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency (CAD) under a free-floating exchange rate regime. Capital and current account transactions are unrestricted; there are no exchange control laws or prior approval requirements for inbound or outbound transfers. Profit remittance to non-residents may trigger withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (e.g., dividends, royalties, interest), but no FX conversion hurdles apply. A BC ULC can hold multi-currency bank accounts domestically and repatriate funds freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings indefinitely in foreign currency accounts at Canadian chartered banks. As a BC-incorporated ULC, no provincial foreign exchange controls exist; reporting obligations are limited to CRA customs documentation and trade statistics (Statistics Canada).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with supervisory oversight by OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) for federal banks and FICOM (Financial Institutions Commission of BC) for provincial credit unions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for core banking functions. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business) cannot act as substitutes for a domestic Canadian bank for a BC ULC engaged in medical device export. They lack authority to operate as Canadian deposit-taking institutions, cannot issue letters of credit governed by ICC rules, and do not settle through Payments Canada's real-time rail (RTR) or ACSS. They may supplementary support for multicurrency sweep and remote contractor payments, but all regulatory capital escrows, LC issuance, and trade settlements must route through a prudentially regulated Canadian bank or a licensed foreign bank branch.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders or a US parent must be made via SWIFT MT103 or MT103 Plus using the beneficiary's bank's institution (Institution #) and transit (#5) number, quoting the ULC's account and purpose code. Banks typically reference a corporate reason code such as 'N031 – Paid-Up Capital Subscription' or 'Shareholder Loan Injection' for internal reconciliation and CRA reporting. A board resolution authorizing the injection, the precise capital contribution amount, an issued share certificate for subscribed shares, and the remitting bank's KYC reference (SWIFT term 57C/50F) must be retained. For USD injections, the CAD-equivalent is converted at the bank's daily rate, and for cross-border accounting, the ULC should either record the amount in USD with a corresponding FX gain/loss or open a USD sub-account at the receiving bank to avoid forced conversion.For manufacturing inputs (e.g., amorphous silicon, cesium iodide, glass substrates) and overseas logistics, SWIFT MT103 remains the standard immediate wire transfer rail, cleared via Payments Canada ACSS for CAD or via the US correspondent network (Fedwire/CHIPS) for USD. For large procurement orders exceeding the bank's internal threshold or where the supplier demands documentary security, an irrevocable confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit (C-LC) issued by the Canadian issuing bank and transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT710 is appropriate. The beneficiary's bank will effect settlement through the documentary delivery process. For export-side receivables, a seller's draft (sight or term) under an LC/DC can be discounted at the Canadian bank to accelerate cash flow.
