A British Columbia ULC is generally required to maintain a private transparency register identifying each significant individual. The register is not a general public beneficial ownership database. The federal Canada Business Corporations Act transparency-register regime does not directly govern a corporation incorporated under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A British Columbia company must maintain a registered office and records office in British Columbia under the Business Corporations Act. A commercial virtual office may be acceptable if it is a genuine address capable of receiving legal service and statutory notices, the company has a compliant records office arrangement, and the provider permits the company to maintain required corporate records. A mailbox, accommodation address, nominee address, or address incapable of receiving service is not sufficient. For an Amazon FBA cross-border retailer, a virtual office is generally legally permissible because the activity is not ordinarily subject to a sector-specific British Columbia premises licence. It may nevertheless be rejected or subjected to enhanced review by banks, Amazon, Stripe, Airwallex, insurers, tax authorities, or payment processors. Maintain evidence of management, accounting records, inventory ownership, contracts, decision-making, tax registrations, and operational control. A ULC is not automatically fiscally transparent for Canadian tax purposes; its Canadian corporate tax status and any foreign classification or treaty treatment require separate analysis.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (federal tax authority; online: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE at canada.ca/revenue-agency); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs and export declarations; British Columbia Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC) for Provincial Sales Tax (PST).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian domestic default: a BC/AB Unlimited Liability Corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act; U.S. check-the-box pass-through eligibility exists for U.S. federal purposes via Form 8832, but this does not alter Canadian entity-level taxation).

Available election forms

No domestic Canadian check-the-box election exists. For U.S. tax purposes, the BC ULC may elect to be classified as a partnership or disregarded entity by filing IRS Form 8832 with an effective date. Canada's hybrid mismatch and treaty rules operate automatically and may restrict deductions in cross-border ULC structures; there is no affirmative domestic Canada election to override hybrid mismatch treatment, although a ULC can be structured so that its shareholders bear the economic liability for Canadian tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold based on taxable supplies made in Canada; non-residents carrying on business in Canada, including holding inventory in a Canadian fulfilment centre, should confirm whether they are small suppliers or are nonetheless required to register. BC PST: CAD 10,000 annual remote sales into British Columbia for out-of-province sellers and marketplace sellers, effective July 1, 2023.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act when sold for export and actually exported from Canada by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf, supported by contemporaneous shipment evidence. Deliveries to Canadian consumers from Amazon FBA Canadian inventory are not exports and are subject to GST/HST and, in BC, PST where applicable. Services supplied to non-residents can be zero-rated if the recipient is outside Canada and the service is not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property in Canada and is not for the benefit of a Canadian business; cross-border digital services supplied to non-residents can qualify if those conditions are met.

  • Customs export declaration (CBSA B13A via the Canadian Export Reporting System, or equivalent recorded declaration in the CARM portal) with full exporter details, tariff item number, quantity, value and destination.
  • Transport evidence (bill of lading, airway bill, or courier/FBA shipment manifest) proving the goods physically left Canada and identifying the foreign destination.
  • Commercial payment/remittance evidence (bank transfer/SWIFT record, Amazon seller settlement statement, credit card settlement report, and, where applicable, an inward remittance confirmation or foreign-exchange record evidencing receipt of export proceeds).

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal invoice clearance, digital stamping, or per-transaction tax API approval for GST/HST or BC PST. Registrants must issue appropriate invoices/records and retain them electronically with integrity controls. GST/HST registrants generally file periodic returns electronically via CRA; BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC. Cross-border export reporting is electronic through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System and the CARM portal, with EDI/API capabilities for high-volume traders.

CRA and CBSA verify export declarations against shipping data and GST/HST refund claims; false or unsupported zero-rating is subject to reassessment (ordinarily 4 years; unlimited in cases of misrepresentation or fraud), denial of refunds, assessment of unremitted GST/HST, interest, penalties for false statements or gross negligence (typically the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax understated or refund claimed), and criminal prosecution for fraud. Clawbacks may also trigger BC PST liabilities and disqualification from CRA administrative relief programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are free from exchange controls, quotas, licensing, or surrender requirements. Dividend and profit remittances by a BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) to non-resident shareholders are permitted without Bank of Canada approval, subject to corporate solvency tests under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) and to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), potentially reduced by an applicable tax treaty. The Canadian dollar floats; Bank of Canada interventions occur only in exceptional market disorder and do not constitute convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligation. Export proceeds need not be repatriated within any timeline, and no mandatory percentage must be converted to CAD. Exporters may retain foreign currency in Canadian bank accounts or offshore accounts, subject to FINTRAC record-keeping and income/corporate tax reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central monetary authority; foreign exchange policy is administered by the Department of Finance Canada with operational supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada has no designated foreign-exchange control administration and no obligation to obtain central bank approval for current-account or capital-account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-to-high only as a supplemental payment and treasury rail; low as the sole primary banking relationship. A foreign or offshore digital EMI is not a Canadian chartered bank and cannot lawfully accept Canadian deposits or provide deposit insurance (CDIC). EMI accounts may be used for FX conversion and cross-border disbursements, provided (i) the EMI is not presented as a licensed Canadian bank, (ii) transaction records are accessible for FINTRAC, CRA, and BC compliance, and (iii) settlement into a Canadian bank account is maintained for GST/HST, payroll, and CAD clearing. Canadian-regulated fintechs and approved MSBs are preferable for Amazon Pay, Stripe, and Airwallex settlement flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection is executed by SWIFT MT103 / ISO 20022 pacs.009 credit transfer from the foreign shareholder's bank to the ULC's CAD or USD account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The payment message should include the ISO purpose code 'EQUI' (equity investment) or an explicit memo 'ULC share capital contribution', together with the share subscription reference. No Bank of Canada exchange-control approval is required; however, the beneficial owner and source of funds must be verified by the Canadian bank, and FINTRAC large-value EFT reporting may apply.
