Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration for GST/HST, income tax, payroll, and non-resident Part XIII withholding. Portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html; cra-arc.gc.ca/my-business-account). British Columbia's 7% provincial Retail Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca) and is not part of the federal GST/HST system because BC withdrew from the Harmonized Sales Tax effective 1 April 2013. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs export declarations, which are relevant to export zero-rating evidence.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Provincial registration: register the Limited Partnership under the BC Partnership Act (forms LP1/LP2) with the BC Registrar of Companies via Corporate Online to obtain a BC LP registration number; non-resident partners are permitted, but the partnership must maintain at least one general partner resident in BC (or a resident agent) for service of process. Step 2 – Federal Business Number: apply to the CRA via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 (non-resident applicants use Form RC1A) for a 9-digit Business Number (BN); request the 'RT' GST/HST program account and, if applicable, 'RP' payroll, 'RM'/'RZ' non-resident withholding, or 'RC' corporation program accounts. For a partnership, the application requires the general partner's identifier and each partner's name; foreign partners without a Canadian SIN may provide their foreign TIN or, where unavailable, the CRA may issue a temporary 'trust account number'; the BN issued to the LP is separate from the partners' identifiers. Step 3 – GST/HST registration: the LP registers as a 'person' under ETA s. 240; a non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada registers as a 'non-resident registrant' under ETA s. 241 and may be required to post security (ETA s. 296) at the CRA's discretion; the registration effective date is the earlier of the date of first taxable supply and the date the person becomes a registrant (ETA s. 242). Step 4 – BC PST registration: open an eTaxBC account and apply for a PST number before making any taxable retail sale, lease, or specified taxable service in BC; B2B consulting services that do not relate to tangible personal property, software, or repairs are not PST-assessable. Step 5 – Non-resident partner compliance: file CRA Form NR301 to claim reduced Part XIII withholding under an applicable income tax treaty and register the partnership for Part XIII if it pays management fees, rents, or royalties to non-residents (ITA s. 212). Step 6 – Records: retain the partnership agreement, partners' TINs, CRA BN confirmation notices, and GST/HST election forms in the registered office records to substantiate flow-through classification and registration status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax purposes: under ITA s. 96, income, taxable capital gains, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners, who are taxed at the partner level; the LP itself files an information return (T5013) and pays no entity-level federal income tax. For GST/HST, by contrast, the LP is a separate 'person' (ETA s. 123(1)) and must register, collect, and remit GST in its own name independent of its partners. If all partners are resident in Canada, the LP is a 'Canadian partnership' (ITA s. 102(1)); if any partner is non-resident, the LP is a non-Canadian partnership deemed to be a non-resident person for the purpose of computing partnership income (ITA s. 102(2)), which preserves the non-resident partners' Canadian-source taxation under ITA ss. 2(3) and 115(1)(a)(iv) and may make a non-resident general partner taxable on the LP's Canadian business income. The LP's proposed commercial activity (ISIC 7490 – Medical Device FDA 510(k) and CE Mark regulatory consulting) is a taxable commercial activity for GST/HST purposes; the LP is not in a tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' entity-classification election analogous to US Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3; classification is determined by legal form under the Income Tax Act and the BC Partnership Act. The LP may nevertheless make the following statutory elections: (1) Partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 to adopt a non-calendar-year partnership year-end (subject to the reference-period test and partner consent), which may defer partner income inclusion. (2) Section 97(2) rollover election on property (including intellectual property related to FDA/CE regulatory methodologies and know-how) transferred to the LP by a partner, deferring capital gain until disposition. (3) GST/HST annual-filing election under ETA s. 237 for eligible registrants with taxable supplies of CAD 1,500,000 or less in Canada. (4) Related-party election under ETA s. 156.1 to deem supplies between closely related registrants (corporations and partnerships) to be made for nil consideration, reducing cash GST flow within the group. (5) Quick Method remittance election under ETA s. 229 (subject to eligibility conditions including the CAD 400,000 threshold) to simplify GST/HST remittance for a small consulting registrant. (6) Hybrid mismatch rules: Canada's BEPS Action 2 measures (ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.21 and Part XII.6) apply automatically to disallow deductions for hybrid financial arrangements and hybrid transfers involving non-resident partners or reverse hybrids; no election is available to avoid the disallowance. (7) Functional-currency election under ITA s. 261 where the LP maintains books in a foreign functional currency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (thirty thousand Canadian dollars) of taxable supplies made in Canada in any single calendar quarter or in any consecutive four-quarter period (ETA s. 148). A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada is subject to the same CAD 30,000 threshold; once exceeded, the LP is a mandatory registrant and may no longer claim 'small supplier' status (ETA s. 148(1)-(4)). For BC PST there is no monetary registration threshold: a PST account must be obtained before the first taxable retail sale, lease, or specified taxable service is made in BC, regardless of revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI Part V, ss. 1–5 where the LP exports tangible personal property to a destination outside Canada (continuous-export condition) and retains the export evidence set out in GST/HST Memorandum 17-4. Services: under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7, a supply of a service made to a non-resident is zero-rated if (i) the non-resident recipient is not registered for GST/HST, (ii) the service is not received in whole or in part in Canada, and (iii) the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. For the LP's ISIC 7490 line (FDA 510(k) and CE Mark regulatory consulting), the supply of professional advice, dossier preparation, gap assessments, quality-system documentation, and regulatory-strategy reports to a foreign medical device manufacturer generally qualifies for zero-rating where the deliverables are transmitted offshore and the external client has no Canadian establishment receiving the service. If the LP also performs on-site inspection/testing of physical medical device prototypes physically located in Canada, that segment is taxable at 5% GST and may be subject to BC PST as a taxable service performed on tangible personal property. A separate zero-rating head applies to the supply of an intellectual-property right (patent, trade secret, know-how) for use wholly outside Canada (Schedule VI Part V s. 20). The LP must (a) allocate each invoice between zero-rated regulatory services and taxable in-Canada segments, (b) obtain a written non-resident declaration confirming the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant and will not use the report in Canada, and (c) maintain a zero-rating reconciliation schedule for each reporting period; input tax credits attributable to the zero-rated export business remain fully claimable, and a refund arises where net tax is negative.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (CAED/B3-3) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) — mandatory only where physical goods (medical device samples, prototypes, software media) are exported; services-only exporters must instead maintain the commercial export certificate and service contract.
  • Proof of export and possession transfer: original Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or alternative carrier receipt evidencing shipment of any exported goods to a foreign destination and the export date.
  • Commercial invoice and fully executed service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, the ISIC 7490 regulatory deliverables, the place of receipt, payment terms, and a statement that the supply is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 or s. 20.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC): SWIFT MT103, bank advice, or foreign-exchange conversion record evidencing payment of funds from the non-resident client to the LP's Canadian bank account.
  • Non-resident status declaration: a certificate from the non-resident recipient confirming that it is not registered for GST/HST and that the supply was not received in whole or in part in Canada.
  • GST/HST return workpaper and zero-rating reconciliation register matching each zero-rated invoice to the corresponding evidence file, proof of payment, and the relevant GST/HST NETFILE filing.

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, a digital stamping regime, or per-invoice government pre-validation (no equivalent to CFDI, SII, or a mandatory continuous-control system). E-invoicing is voluntary in the private sector. However: (i) GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or approved software once taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,500,000? No — electronic filing is required for registrants with total taxable supplies of CAD 1,500,000 or more (GST/HST Memorandum 17-1?); for smaller registrants paper filing remains available. (ii) The Government of Canada mandates that suppliers to federal departments transmit electronic invoices through the federal e-invoice portal using PEPPOL BIS 3.0 (invoicing.canada.ca), and the CRA offers the Business Gateway API for machine-to-machine submission of GST/HST and BN-related data. (iii) A registrant must issue a taxable supply invoice with the prescribed particulars (ETA ss. 223 and 166.1), but there is no statutory requirement for each invoice to be electronically cleared, stamped, or confirmed by the CRA before issuance. (iv) BC PST does not require electronic invoice clearance but requires the seller to issue a PST receipt-invoice showing the 7% PST separately or inclusively as prescribed by the BC Sales Tax Act Regulations.

CRA audit exposure: export zero-rating and ITC claims are a designated high-risk audit area; the CRA reconciles GST/HST returns against CBSA CERS export data, partner-level T5013 allocations, and banking source documents using data analytics. Reassessment window: the normal GST/HST reassessment period is four years from the end of the reporting period (ETA s. 298); the window is unlimited if the LP has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud (ETA s. 298(4)). An unsupported zero-rating claim is reassessed at 5% GST on the gross invoice value plus compounded daily arrears interest (ETA s. 280). Civil penalties: failure to file a GST/HST return attracts 5% of the unpaid tax plus 1% per full month (maximum 12 months); knowingly making a false statement or omission on a return attracts a penalty equal to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (ETA s. 280(1)); participation in a sham or avoidance transaction may also trigger the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), which could deny the zero-rating result entirely. Criminal exposure: wilful evasion of GST/HST under ETA s. 380 is punishable on summary conviction by a fine (up to CAD 25,000) and/or imprisonment (up to 2 years), and on indictment by a higher fine and/or imprisonment (up to 5 years). Recovery tools: CRA may offset unpaid GST/HST against refunds, garnishee the LP's bank accounts, and hold the general partner jointly and severally liable for collections where the LP fails to remit. There is no federal statutory FX-repatriation deadline, but the 180-day evidence window is the audit convention for accepting foreign-currency receipts; stale receivables exceeding 180 days without remittance will be scrutinized to determine whether the supply was in fact made to a person unrelated to the LP and whether the invoice is collectible (accrual-basis reassessment under ITA s. 9 may otherwise be triggered).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. The federal Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1952, and no exchange controls, FX licensing requirements, or mandatory reporting of contract-level currency movements exist today. Profit remittances by a British Columbia Limited Partnership to non-resident partners are freely permitted through the banking system without central bank authorization; however, Part XIII withholding tax may apply on deemed distributions or remitted partnership profits, and a non-resident partner earning Canadian-source income without a Canadian Permanent Establishment may be subject to Part XII.6 tax or a Section 216 income tax return. These are tax obligations, not exchange-control barriers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts in Canada. A BC Limited Partnership may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency, hold multiple denominated accounts, and convert balances at the prevailing market exchange rate. Settlement occurs via the Lynx high-value payment system for CAD wires or through correspondent banking channels for USD and EUR clearing. No central bank approval or repatriation filing is required; standard FINTRAC thresholds apply to large transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money-laundering surveillance by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with strict legal constraints. Canada does not operate an EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) passporting regime. Any foreign fintech EMI or digital-banking platform must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) or Foreign Money Services Business (FMSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and may require provincial registration with the BC Financial Services Authority. Non-compliant offshore EMIs face enforcement risk and are therefore admissible only as a supplementary inbound/outbound collection rail, never as the primary treasury bank for capital injection or trade disbursement activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed via direct SWIFT MT103 wires from the contributor's bank to the LP's CAD or USD account at a Canadian tier-1 bank. No exchange-control approval is required. Standard SWIFT category purpose codes should be applied: 'CAAB' for capital account borrowing, 'ICDA' for direct investment, or 'SERV' for professional consulting fees. Beneficiary name and account number must exactly match the registered LP name and bank profile to avoid clearing delays. All inbound wires equal to or greater than CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC transaction reporting, and the remitter is subject to OFAC/UN sanctions screening.Outbound trade settlements for US and EU vendor disbursements use SWIFT MT103 in USD or EUR through the bank's correspondent clearing network (US Fedwire/CHIPS and EU TARGET2). For documentary, goods-backed transactions, a commercial letter of credit (MT700) is the permissible rail; for recurring consulting disbursements, direct MT103 wires remain the preferred method. CAD domestic disbursements clear through Lynx for high-value payments and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for retail volumes. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium with strict legal constraints. Canada does not operate an EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) passporting regime. Any foreign fintech EMI or digital-banking platform must be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) or Foreign Money Services Business (FMSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and may require provincial registration with the BC Financial Services Authority.
