Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a British Columbia limited partnership. The initial limited partner's contribution may be in cash, property, or services as fixed in the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow account is mandated by the Limited Partnership Act; however, if the Limited Partnership administers an Employee Stock Ownership Trust, employee contributions intended for share purchases should be held in a segregated Canadian trust bank account pending execution of share transfers so as to comply with fiduciary and securities settlement requirements.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and partnership information returns; online portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). Provincial counterpart: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (BC PST via eTaxBC portal); corporate/registry: BC Registry and OneStop BC; customs: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Register or continue the limited partnership with the BC Registry (Corporate Online) and obtain the BC LP registration number; a BC resident LP files a Declaration of Limited Partnership, while a foreign LP carrying on business in BC registers as an extra-provincial limited partnership. Step 2 — Obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, using the LP's legal name, BC registration number, and jurisdictional information. Step 3 — Open required CRA program accounts under the BN: RT (income tax / T5013 partnership information return), RC (GST/HST), RP (payroll source deductions), and RM (import/export account) as applicable. Step 4 — Register for GST/HST when taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold) or voluntarily to recover input tax credits. Step 5 — Register for BC PST at eTaxBC if the LP makes retail sales of taxable tangible personal property or enumerated taxable services or rentals in BC, applying also to out-of-province and remote sellers. Step 6 — Register for BC Employer Health Tax if annual BC remuneration reaches the CAD 500,000 exemption level. Step 7 — If the LP acts as trustee or administrator of an employee share incentive plan / Employee Ownership Trust, the underlying Canadian resident trust obtains its own BN/trust account via Form RC1 (trust return T3); no minimum capital is required for a BC LP and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act: for Canadian income tax, partners compute and report their shares of income/loss (ITA s.96), the LP does not pay federal income tax, and the partnership files a T5013 information return only where prescribed (5+ members or a corporate/partnership member). BC resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source partnership income, are subject to Part XIII withholding on certain distributions, and may be required to file Canadian returns. For GST/HST and BC PST, the LP is a separate taxable registrant in respect of its own supplies, and the sale/administration of employee share incentive plan interests does not qualify as an exempt financial service unless the supply is predominantly the making of loans, custody, or trading in securities.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal law; entity classification follows the Income Tax Act and administrative practice. Available elections and elective mechanisms for a BC LP in the ESOP administration business include: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration while below the CAD 30,000 threshold and the Excise Tax Act s.156 election for closely related registrants to value internal supplies at nil for ITC purposes; (ii) the ITA s.216 election enabling a non-resident partner to file a net-basis Canadian tax return for Canadian rental income; (iii) trust elections — a resident trust may be constituted and elected as an employee trust (ITA s.144), an Employee Ownership Trust (ITA s.144.1 et seq., effective 1 January 2024, with prescribed elections and forms), or an employee benefit plan, each having distinct deduction, withholding, and distribution consequences; (iv) election to file combined GST/HST returns (annual, quarterly, or monthly) and the use of the net tax calculation; and (v) no elective relief for hybrid-mismatch or excessive-financing rules — ITA ss.18.4/18.5 (hybrid mismatch) and the EIFEL/interest-stopping rules apply mandatorily to disallow deductions in cross-border financing arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold) — measured on total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies, excluding exempt supplies) over the trailing four calendar quarters and the current quarter; exceeding the threshold obliges registration. BC PST: no general dollar threshold for sellers of taxable goods in BC — out-of-province and remote vendors and marketplace facilitators must register for PST when making taxable retail sales in BC, with sector-specific thresholds and exemptions (e.g., certain services are taxable only if enumerated).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated supplies require the supplier to be registered and to retain contemporaneous export evidence. Goods: Excise Tax Act s.22 zero-rates tangible personal property sold by the registrant and delivered by the vendor, or caused to be delivered, to an address outside Canada, with export completed within the statutory timeline and no subsequent use/consumption in Canada. Services: ETA s.23 zero-rates a supply of a service to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant (or, in certain cases, registered but acquiring the service for consumption outside Canada), where the service is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada, not performed in connection with goods located in Canada at the time the service is performed, and not rendered to an individual present in Canada. For the LP's ESOP administration business, an administrative or management service supplied to a non-resident employer/trustee (e.g., foreign parent or a non-resident plan sponsor) may be zero-rated if the non-resident status and the performance location are documented; if the supply is a 'financial service' under the Excise Tax Act, it is generally exempt, but ETA s.23.1 zero-rates financial services supplied to non-residents that are not related to Canadian risks or Canadian real property. Non-resident status must be evidenced by incorporation documents, tax treaty residency certificates, or CRA Form NR301.

  • Customs export declaration filed through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), including the unique transaction/reference number and HS classification
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract identifying the exporter, non-resident buyer, Incoterms, value, and description of goods or services
  • Proof of foreign currency / export proceeds receipt: bank wire confirmation or Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent Canadian bank foreign-exchange advice) evidencing actual receipt and repatriation of proceeds
  • Non-resident status evidence: certificate of incorporation/registration of the foreign recipient, tax treaty residency certificate, and CRA Form NR301 (Non-Resident Declaration)
  • Delivery and customs-release records, including carrier tracking, warehouse export receipts, and CBSA cargo control / release documents, demonstrating that export occurred within the statutory period

Canada does not require mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal digital stamping, or clearance pre-approval by the CRA. The CRA mandates that electronic invoices and books/records be preserved in a readable and retrievable format, and that prescribed returns (GST/HST, T5013, T3, T2) be filed electronically when the filing volume threshold is met. A voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service (PCI) using the Peppol network is being implemented; B2B e-invoice exchange between the LP and clients may be contractually adopted. There is no 'calculo' pre-approval or CFDI-style digital certificate; the authorized channels are CRA Business Registration Online, My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE, and, for BC PST, eTaxBC.

CRA scrutinizes zero-rating claims at the transaction level and requires an unbroken chain of export documentation retained for at least six years. A denied zero-rating claim results in a retrospective clawback of the 5% GST/HST that was not remitted, plus prescribed interest (compounded daily). The CRA's normal four-year reassessment window applies; the limitation period is unlimited where the LP, its partners, or agents misrepresent facts due to neglect, carelessness, willful default, or fraud. Civil penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act: the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understatement for false statements or omissions, increased to 50% where the misstatement is made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence. Willful tax evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act exposes the LP and involved partners/directors to fines of 50%–200% of the amount evaded and/or imprisonment. Partners are also jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted GST/HST net tax, penalties, and interest, and the CRA may offset any allowed input tax credits or refunds against the assessed amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current and capital accounts; no advance approval, exchange-control licence, or repatriation permit is required for dividends, interest, fees, capital distributions, or loan repayments by a British Columbia limited partnership. Profit-remittance hurdles are exclusively tax-driven: a non-resident limited partner must obtain a Canadian tax identification number, file an NR6 and Part I income tax return for its Canadian-source partnership income where applicable, and the LP must issue T5013 information returns and comply with withholding obligations under the Income Tax Act. No foreign-exchange authorization is triggered by remitting CAD proceeds to a foreign partner; the practical steps are a standard SWIFT MT103 wire and execution of the spot FX transaction through a Schedule I bank or an IIROC-regulated dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender regime for export proceeds: there is no mandatory timeline for repatriation and no minimum conversion ratio into Canadian dollars. Export declarations are filed electronically through the Canada Border Services Agency Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), and the balance-of-payments value of transactions is captured by Statistics Canada through the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS), with the reporting obligation generally discharged by the client's financial institution. For the ESOP Trust LP, sponsor subscriptions, participant contributions, and equity settlement amounts are capital-account inflows rather than export proceeds; they may lawfully be retained in CAD, USD, or any other freely convertible currency without conversion mandate, subject only to FINTRAC record-keeping and large-cash-transaction reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada as the central monetary authority; prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); administration of Canadian economic sanctions by Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority because the currency is fully convertible.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury or deposit bank; MEDIUM as a supplemental FX execution and deal-sourcing venue. A non-resident digital EMI that does not hold a Canadian Schedule I bank subsidiary is not a member of Payments Canada and therefore cannot settle through the ACSS, Lynx, or AFT rails, and it cannot legally hold the ESOP trust corpus in a Canadian insured deposit. Such an EMI may, however, execute SWIFT MT103 outbound wires and provide competitive CAD/USD quotes. If the EMI engages in money transfer, foreign exchange dealing, or payment account provision for Canadian residents, it must register with FINTRAC as a money services business and, where conducting business in British Columbia, obtain a licence under the BC Money Services Act; failure to do so renders the EMI non-compliant and unusable for trust assets.

BC Limited Partnership Annual Report Annually on or before the 30th day after the anniversary of the limited partnership's registration date in British Columbia $42 USD Late filing after the 30-day window incurs an administrative late fee; failure to file annual reports for two consecutive years allows the Registrar to strike the limited partnership off the register after notice, resulting in loss of good standing and limited-partner liability protection.
Canada Partnership Information Return (T5013) Within 5 months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period; a December 31 fiscal period end requires filing by May 31 of the following calendar year $0 USD Failure to file is subject to a CRA penalty of CAD 10 per day up to CAD 2,500, plus additional gross-negligence penalties and possible prosecution under the Income Tax Act; the filing default does not trigger BC administrative strike-off.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory audit threshold is imposed by the BC Partnership Act or the Income Tax Act for a private BC limited partnership. A mandatory independent audit arises only when the LP is a reporting issuer under provincial securities legislation, when the partnership agreement or an employee-share-plan trust deed requires an audit, or when financing covenants or an applicable pension/benefits statute imposes an audit obligation.
