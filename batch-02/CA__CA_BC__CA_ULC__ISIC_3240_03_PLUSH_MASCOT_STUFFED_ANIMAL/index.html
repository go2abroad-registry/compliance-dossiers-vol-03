Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - Federal GST/HST administration and Business Number registration via Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); provincial component for British Columbia: Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, PST registration via BC Tax Registration portal (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst)

E-government filing portal

A BC ULC incorporated under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act first obtains its incorporation certificate from BC Registry Services, then registers for a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account (RM) through BRO, by telephone, or by mail. Required data: incorporation number, authorized representative, principal business address in Canada (or designated Canadian agent/representative for non-resident ULCs), estimated annual worldwide taxable supplies, and the anticipated date of the first taxable supply. The effective GST/HST registration date is the earlier of the date of first taxable supply or the requested date; registration is mandatory once the ULC ceases to be a small supplier or carries on a commercial activity in Canada, using the $30,000 CAD worldwide gross revenue threshold. Separately, a PST vendor registration with BC Consumer Taxation is required once the ULC exceeds $10,000 CAD of annual taxable sales sourced to British Columbia, with monthly/quarterly PST remittance and a distinct BC PST account number tied to the same BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - For U.S. federal tax purposes, the BC ULC is eligible for check-the-box flow-through classification (disregarded entity or partnership via Form 8832), but for Canadian domestic tax purposes the ULC is a corporation and an entity-level taxpayer under the Income Tax Act, subject to full federal and provincial corporate income tax, Part I tax, and GST/HST as a separate legal person. It is neither a Territorial_Scope nor a Tax_Exempt_Zone entity; the pass-through election is cross-border only and does not affect Canadian domestic entity taxation.

Available election forms

U.S. Election: IRS Form 8832 Entity Classification Election filed within 75 days of the desired effective date to treat the BC ULC as a partnership or disregarded entity, creating a hybrid entity when combined with Canadian entity-level taxation. Canadian Elections: no domestic check-the-box regime exists; the ULC's corporate status is fixed under the ITA. Available GST/HST elections: annual/quarterly/monthly reporting frequency elections, quick method election for small suppliers, and joint filing election under subsection 225(2) ETA for closely related corporations. Foreign-entity elections: the non-resident ULC may elect under ITA Part I to file consolidated or separate foreign affiliate accrual returns as applicable. Hybrid mismatch exposure: the ULC's dual characterization is assessed against Canada's hybrid mismatch and general anti-avoidance rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6, 21.1, and s. 245 GAAR), which may deny deductions or recharacterize payments where the pass-through treatment results in no corresponding inclusion in the United States.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: $30,000 CAD of annual worldwide taxable supplies (small supplier threshold; non-residents must register immediately upon commencing commercial activity in Canada even if below threshold). BC PST: $10,000 CAD of annual taxable revenue sourced to British Columbia, with registration and remittance obligations applying to manufacturers, wholesalers, and retailers of tangible personal property, including plush toys.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of plush mascots qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate where the ULC exports the goods directly to a non-resident recipient or delivers them by common carrier to a foreign destination (ETA Schedule VI, Part V). Conditions: the exporter possesses documentary evidence of export (CBSA export declaration, bill of lading, or foreign customs entry), title passes to the non-resident buyer outside Canada, the goods leave Canada within a commercially reasonable time, and no further processing occurs in Canada after export. Related offshore services, such as custom embroidery design, prototyping, ultrasonic seaming, and OEM production coordination for foreign clients, are also zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under GST/HST and who does not receive the supply in the course of a Canadian business. The ULC may claim input tax credits for all GST/HST incurred on raw materials, embroidery machinery, freight, and overhead, and any resulting net GST refund is paid by the CRA on a monthly or quarterly basis.

None mandatory. Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API clearance regime. The CRA mandates electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE or XML EDI filing) and electronic remittance of payments above $10,000 CAD, but e-invoicing is voluntary. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing is being adopted through the PEPPOL/OASIS UBL standard primarily for procurement, and no fiscal clearance API is applicable to ISIC 3240 exports of custom plush mascots.

CRA export-verification and international tax audits apply heightened scrutiny to zero-rated tangible goods exporters. If the mandatory documentation bundle is incomplete or unreliable, the 0% rate is denied, the ULC is reassessed for the uncollected 5% GST (and, for BC-destined sales, the 7% PST) plus interest, and the exporter must claw back input tax credits previously claimed on related inputs. Administrative penalties include false statements or gross negligence penalties of up to 25% of the tax amount under Excise Tax Act s. 285, monetary penalties for CERS filing failures under s. 280.1, and a standard 4-year reassessment window, which becomes unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating claims can result in fines up to $25,000 and imprisonment, and the CRA may additionally cancel the BN and GST/HST program account, apply seizure or garnishment orders, and impose transfer-pricing adjustments with s. 247 penalties where the 180-day repatriation practice is persistently abused through related-party receivables.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible across both current-account and capital-account headings under a pure floating exchange-rate regime; there are no registration, licensing, or prior-approval requirements for foreign-currency remittances. Profit repatriation by a BC ULC to non-resident shareholders is constrained exclusively by Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%, reduced to 15% portfolio / 5% significant-holding under the Canada-US Treaty) and, for branch-equivalent structures, Part I branch tax at 25%. No material administrative hurdles attach to the dividends or management-fee outflows provided the paying matrix is documented and the ULC files its T2/T106 information returns.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to trade foreign currency receipts through any designated bank. Export invoices for plush-toy OEM shipments may be settled in USD, EUR, or CNY and the proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency ledger accounts at Canadian banks or swept into CAD when commercially advantageous.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting concurrently with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking conduct; Canada maintains no exchange-control authority in the style of SAFE or BOK because the Currency and Exchange Control Acts were liberalized in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High as a treasury supplement but not a primary settlement rail. Canada does not license standalone 'electronic money institutions' under EU-style directives; offshore EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Capital on Tap, Payoneer, Intergiro) must be FINTRAC-registered as Foreign MSBs or rely on the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) transitional licensing.
