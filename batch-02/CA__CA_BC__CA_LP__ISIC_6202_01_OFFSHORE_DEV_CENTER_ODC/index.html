Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office is permissible as a registered records office only, provided a physical address in British Columbia is designated for service of process and records inspection. BC does not require full-time staff, but the partnership must maintain office premises reasonably accessible. Pure virtual addresses (e.g., PO box only) violate the Partnership Act requirement for a registered office address and provincial corporate registry rules. Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal income tax and GST/HST; British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST). Portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services/business/business-account.html), GST/HST NETFILE, and BC eTaxBC (https://etaxbc.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

The BC Limited Partnership (LP) is a separate GST/HST registrant (ETA 'person') but a pass-through vehicle for income tax. SOP: (1) Determine small-supplier status: mandatory GST/HST registration is required where worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in any trailing four calendar quarters; once registered, all taxable supplies are reported and input tax credits may be claimed. (2) Obtain a 15-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA by filing Form RC1 or using the online 'Register for a business number (RC160)' service; select program accounts RT (GST/HST) and RM (customs/export) at activation. (3) A non-resident entity may obtain a BN with a Canadian postal address or a duly appointed tax representative/agent; no separate partner-level TIN is required for the LP's GST/HST account. (4) Register for BC PST separately through eTaxBC or BC OneStop if the LP makes taxable retail sales or prescribed taxable services in BC; PST applies from the first taxable retail sale and has no low-supplier threshold equivalent to the GST small-supplier rule. (5) Zero-rated export supplies count toward the CAD 30,000 GST/HST threshold; exempt supplies of financial services generally do not trigger registration. (6) Maintain partner allocation schedules for Canadian-source income apportionment and for Part XIII / Part XIV withholding obligations on non-resident partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime. Classification follows domestic legal character: a BC LP is transparent under Income Tax Act s.96 and is not itself liable to income tax; members are taxed on their allocated share. Available elections and mechanisms: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration election by a small supplier; (ii) closely related corporation election under ETA s.156 (generally inapplicable to an offshore dev center unless a foreign parent and the LP form a closely related GST/HST group); (iii) partnership capital property election under ETA s.273; (iv) ITA s.216 (net rental income) and ITA s.217 (elected taxable Canadian income) elections available to non-resident partners; (v) no hybrid-mismatch election exists at the LP level because Canada applies BEPS Action 2-aligned anti-hybrid rules mandatorily; (vi) non-resident partners may seek treaty-based exemption or reduction of Canadian withholding if the allocated income is not characterized as Canadian-source dividends, royalties, or rental income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST small-supplier registration threshold based on total worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four calendar quarters; zero-rated offshore services count toward this threshold). BC PST has no equivalent registration threshold; PST registration is required from the first taxable retail sale or prescribed taxable service performed in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST is available for: (a) exported goods under ETA Schedule VI Part V when exported before the supply or within prescribed delays with proof of export; (b) exported services supplied to a non-resident and not delivered to, or to be performed for, a person in Canada at the time of supply; and (c) software, IT support, and intellectual property transfers consumed entirely outside Canada by a foreign principal. The offshore dev center model qualifies where the service contract, performance location, delivery, invoicing, and payment point to a non-resident recipient and no Canadian person acquires or consumes the service in Canada. BC PST does not apply to exported services; PST is limited to tangible personal property and prescribed services physically consumed in BC.

  • CERS B13A export declaration filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System for any physical goods or hardware shipped cross-border
  • Commercial invoice expressly stating 'Zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI - exported service/goods' and showing the client's foreign VAT/GST registration number
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / proof of delivery for goods; for services: signed SOW, milestone acceptance certificates, and server/time-log records demonstrating offshore delivery
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the client (foreign certificate of incorporation, foreign tax ID, registered address, beneficial ownership structure)
  • Bank transfer records over Wise B2B / Payoneer / wire showing receipt of consideration from non-resident accounts (Canada does not require a formal inward remittance certificate, but traceable FX payment evidence is essential)
  • Executed service contract documenting governing law, place of performance, IP ownership/assignment, and destination of the services

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice clearance, or digital stamping mandate. GST/HST must be reported on periodic returns (Form GST34) filed electronically via NETFILE or CRA-approved software on a monthly, quarterly, or annual cycle depending on revenue and registration profile; records must be retained in readable format under ETA s.286. CRA uses e-audit and program data requests to substantiate invoices and zero-rating claims. BC PST returns are filed via eTaxBC. Under the OECD BEPS digital services framework, non-resident suppliers of digital services to Canadian consumers may use a simplified CRA GST/HST registration, but an offshore dev center supplying only to non-residents outside Canada is outside that simplified nexus unless the Canadian-consumer threshold is crossed.

CRA's normal reassessment window is three years, extended to four years for partnerships, but becomes unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. False statements or omissions attract a penalty of 50% of the understated tax under ITA s.163(2). Late GST/HST filings incur a 5% penalty plus 1% per month (maximum 12 months), and improperly claimed export zero-rating is reassessed to collect the 5% GST, input tax credit clawbacks, and arrears interest from the original due date. Where export substantiation is deficient, CRA treats the supply as taxable in Canada; intentional export fraud can lead to criminal prosecution and gross-negligence penalties. For BC PST, unreported taxable sales incur a 10% penalty on the tax due, rising to 20% for a second occurrence within two years, with retrospective reassessments. Although Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline for export proceeds, the 180-day parameter in this dossier is a pipeline-standard control; failure to repatriate funds within that window attracts transfer-pricing and thin-capitalization scrutiny, not an automatic VAT clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member maintaining full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no exchange-control licensing, no forex budget, no end-use documentation, and no prior declaration regime for capital inflows or profit repatriation. CAD is free-floating in a deep USD/CAD and EUR/CAD interbank market. A British Columbia Limited Partnership may receive capital contributions and remit dividends/partnership distributions abroad without central bank permission; the only practical frictions are (a) bank-level beneficial-ownership/KYC reviews and (b) Part XIII non-resident tax withholding on Canadian-source income paid to non-resident partners at the statutory treaty rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency receipts into CAD, and no retention quotas. Software-development invoices generated by the CA_LP in USD, EUR, or CAD may be held indefinitely in foreign-currency treasury accounts, swept to CAD only when operational cash flow optimizes conversion, and dividend/profit remittances are subject solely to corporate-tax-context compliance under Part XIII withholding and the Canada-US/Canada-EU tax treaties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC), with prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/ATF supervision by FINTRAC, and sanctions administration by Global Affairs Canada; the Bank of Canada does not impose exchange controls or foreign-exchange approval requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for operational disbursement rails (payroll, contractor payments, low-value intercompany expenses) and LOW for primary corporate liquidity or capital escrow. Foreign digital EMIs operating without FINTRAC registration as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) cannot lawfully provide payment services denominated in CAD without regulatory presence under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and the Retail Payment Activities Act. Wise B2B and Payoneer are acceptable as secondary rails for outbound supplier disbursements, subject to a dual-verification policy that keeps the primary clearing obligation with OSFI-regulated bank-held settlement accounts; their statements must reconcile directly to the underlying SWIFT/delivery log.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: SWIFT MT103 from the overseas parent or partner bank to the CA_LP's BC settlement account (with bank and transit/branch number, swiriting to the CAD or USD account) using remittance information line 70: 'Capital Contribution under LP Agreement dated [date] — CA_LP__ODC'. Canada has no mandatory purpose-code regime; however, the receiving Canadian bank's sanctions and BO monitoring requires an accompanying board resolution acknowledging the partner capital contribution, the executed subscription in the LP register, and a funds-origin memorandum for transfers exceeding CAD equivalent USD 100,000. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for operational disbursement rails (payroll, contractor payments, low-value intercompany expenses) and LOW for primary corporate liquidity or capital escrow. Foreign digital EMIs operating without FINTRAC registration as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) cannot lawfully provide payment services denominated in CAD without regulatory presence under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and the Retail Payment Activities Act. Wise B2B and Payoneer are acceptable as secondary rails for outbound supplier disbursements, subject to a dual-verification policy that keeps the primary clearing obligation with OSFI-regulated bank-held settlement accounts; their statements must reconcile directly to the underlying SWIFT/delivery log.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (for goods) or CAD 500,000 (for services) in cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires that transactions have genuine economic substance. The LP must demonstrate real business activity, employees, and decision-making in Canada to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties for gross negligence (50% of understated tax).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they fail to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete), and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in or acquiesced to the non-compliance.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and application of GAAR; penalties up to 25% of the tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for taxes and penalties if they are found to be the directing mind behind the artificial arrangement.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for individuals and CAD 10 million for entities under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; criminal charges possible.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned for up to 5 years for willful non-compliance.
