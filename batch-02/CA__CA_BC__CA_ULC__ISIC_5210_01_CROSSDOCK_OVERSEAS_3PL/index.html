Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permitted with limitations. BCBCA s. 71 requires every BC company to maintain a registered office at a recognizable physical street address in British Columbia capable of receiving legal process during ordinary business hours. A virtual-office provider may satisfy that requirement only if it supplies a true suite or street address, forwards mail continuously, and accepts service of process on the company's behalf. For a cross-border 3PL warehouse operator, the licensed and customs-registered physical warehouse (leased or owned) is the principal establishment and cannot be supplanted by a virtual office for municipal business licensing, Canada Border Services Agency registration, insurance, or employer compliance purposes. A non-resident ULC commonly uses the registered agent's office as the statutory registered office while operating the fulfillment warehouse at a separate BC location.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax authority administering GST/HST, corporate income tax (T2), payroll and import/export program accounts; primary portal: CRA Business Registration Online / My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). British Columbia provincial counterpart: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, via eTaxBC for Provincial Sales Tax; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs/exporter reporting.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the BC ULC under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) through BC Registries and Online Services or a licensed registry agent to obtain the certificate of incorporation and BC company number. Step 2: Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA via Business Registration Online or Form RC1, keyed to the BC corporate identity. Step 3: Open CRA program accounts on the same BN using Form RC1/RC1A: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RM (import/export) as required. Step 4: Register for BC PST (vendor number) via BC Tax Registration in eTaxBC if the ULC sells or leases taxable goods or provides designated taxable services at retail in BC. Step 5: File T2 corporate income tax returns and, where applicable, comply with Part XIII withholding on distributions to non-resident shareholders, holding required books and records for six years. Foreign entities do not obtain a separate TIN; the Canadian-resident ULC itself is the taxpayer and registration is entity-level.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: under Canadian federal and BC income tax law the ULC is a separate taxable corporation resident in Canada, subject to entity-level Part I taxation with no Canadian S-corporation analogue; however, the ULC is eligible for U.S. check-the-box pass-through classification, and under Article IV(6) of the Canada–U.S. Tax Convention it may be treated as fiscally transparent for treaty purposes so that a U.S. shareholder is attributed the Canadian-source items of the ULC. For GST/HST purposes classification is always entity-level: the ULC is a separate registrant entitled to full input tax credits on taxable inputs.

Available election forms

(1) U.S. Treasury Form 8832 check-the-box election: a single-member ULC may elect disregarded-entity status and a multi-member ULC may elect partnership classification for U.S. federal tax purposes; the ULC is not a per se corporation in the United States, so classification is elective. (2) Canada–U.S. Tax Convention Article IV(6)/(7) fiscally transparent entity claim: the U.S. owner may claim attribution of ULC items and treaty benefits subject to timely notice and existing CRA administrative positions. (3) Excise Tax Act s.156 election among closely related GST/HST registrants: intra-group supplies of warehousing/cross-dock/fulfillment services may be made for nil consideration, eliminating GST leakage. (4) Income Tax Act s.85(1) rollover election: tax-deferred transfer of eligible Canadian property into the ULC. (5) Voluntary GST/HST registration election (ETA s.240(2)) available to a small-supplier ULC to accelerate input tax credit recovery.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada (including zero-rated supplies) over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters triggers mandatory registration under ETA s.240 (small supplier threshold); voluntary registration is available below threshold. PST/BC: no equivalent economic-turnover threshold; PST vendor registration is mandatory as soon as the ULC makes retail sales of taxable goods or supplies designated taxable services in BC. Imported goods are subject to 5% GST at the border, with input tax credit recovery available to a GST-registered ULC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated under ETA s.6 if the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada or exports them under prescribed conditions and retains the complete proof-of-export package. Services: a supply of a service to a non-resident is zero-rated under ETA s.7(1) unless the service is, or is incidental to, a service in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Accordingly, BC cross-dock, storage, handling and fulfillment services performed on inventory physically located in Canada are taxable at 5% GST; separately stated international freight and transportation services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VII of the ETA. The underlying export of the goods by the owner or seller may be zero-rated where the exporter is registered and the export evidence is obtained in the relevant reporting period; drop-shipment deeming rules under ETA s.179 must be examined before a 3PL claims zero-rating on goods merely transiting Canada.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration / CUSDEC electronic export report evidencing that the goods departed Canada
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier naming a consignee and destination outside Canada
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the exported goods and stating the foreign consignee and export sale or transfer terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or Canadian banking equivalent: SWIFT MT103 credit advice, bank statement, or foreign-exchange conversion record proving receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident customer)
  • Cargo control documents, cross-dock sort manifests and, where applicable, customs bonded warehouse or free-zone entry-out records
  • For Canada–U.S. movements: carrier-generated ACE/CBSA joint export data and in-transit documentation

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory continuous transaction-control regime. CRA accepts paper-based and true electronic invoicing (PDF/XML/EDI) with the recipient's consent, provided the invoice contains the prescribed particulars under the ETA Input Tax Credit Information Regulations. CRA is progressively adopting a voluntary pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework aligned with PEPPOL (2024–2025), but there is no mandatory API clearance gateway for GST/HST. Exports must be reported electronically to CBSA via CUSDEC/eManifest/CADEX where required, while BC PST returns and remittances are filed through eTaxBC.

CRA requires the complete proof-of-export bundle to be in the exporter's possession before the GST/HST return claiming the 0% rate is filed; missing or late documentation triggers reassessment to 5% GST on the full consideration, plus compound daily interest (currently 0.7% per month) and retrospective clawback of any input tax credits previously allowed. False or negligent export claims expose the ULC to: (i) a 25% civil penalty on the amount of net tax sought to be evaded under ETA s.280; (ii) a 50% gross-negligence penalty on understated Part I tax under ITA s.163(2); (iii) an unlimited reassessment period where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default; (iv) criminal prosecution under ITA s.239 and ETA ss.327/328 with fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years; and (v) suspension of CBSA reasonable-endorsement statuses, denial of duty drawback, and loss of export-commercial documentation privileges. Books and records substantiating zero-rated exports must be retained for six years from the end of the applicable taxation or reporting period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current account and capital account. Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement since 1952, prohibiting restrictions on current international payments. No federal or provincial statute imposes exchange-control licensing, conversion quotas, or prior-approval requirements on capital inflows, profit remittances, external borrowing, or foreign-currency deposit holding by residents or non-residents. Profit-remittance hurdles are tax-driven exclusively: (i) Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by the BC ULC to a US corporate parent - statutory rate 25%, reduced to 5% under Article X(2)(a) of the Canada-US Tax Convention where the US parent beneficially owns at least 10% of the ULC's voting stock and qualifies for treaty benefits; (ii) NR601/NR4 filing and CRA remittance by the 15th day of the month following the dividend payment; (iii) thin-capitalization rules under s.18(4) of the Income Tax Act limiting deductible intercompany debt from specified non-resident shareholders to a 1.5:1 debt:equity ratio; and (iv) availability of a functional-currency election under s.261 ITA allowing the ULC to compute taxable income in USD, materially reducing FX conversion friction on cross-border treasury flows. No Bank of Canada, OSFI, or Investment Canada Act approval is required for routine capital movements into or out of the ULC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, conversion, or minimum-holding-period requirement on export proceeds. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% (statutorily N/A); statutory timeline: N/A. The BC ULC may retain USD and other foreign-currency settlement proceeds indefinitely, whether in onshore foreign-currency bank accounts or offshore clearing accounts. The only binding obligations are regulatory transparency and tax reporting: (i) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for each cross-border wire at or above CAD 10,000, filed within prescribed regulatory deadlines; and (ii) CAD-equivalent income recognition for the ULC's T2 corporate income tax return using the CRA daily exchange rate or the prescribed quarterly average rate under s.261 ITA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) as the sole central monetary authority and exclusive issuer of Canadian banknotes. Prudential supervision of federally chartered Schedule I/II banks is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision, including registration and examination of Money Services Businesses, is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control commission; the Minister of Finance retains residual authority over the Exchange Fund Account and official reserve management held at the Bank of Canada.
