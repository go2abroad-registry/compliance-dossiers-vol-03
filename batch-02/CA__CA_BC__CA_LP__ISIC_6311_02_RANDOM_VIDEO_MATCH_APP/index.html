Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box entity classification election. A limited partnership is statutorily transparent for income tax; partners report their respective shares of income and loss. Available elections are procedural or context-specific, such as designating a partner or agent to administer GST/HST registration and returns, partnership allocation provisions in the partnership agreement, and certain elections to realize capital cost allowance at the partnership level. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act apply by statute rather than by election and may recharacterize a foreign partnership or partner if a hybrid mismatch produces an inappropriate deduction/inclusion outcome.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-resident digital/e-commerce suppliers, the threshold is measured on sales of digital products or services to Canadian recipients, and simplified registration is available. BC PST: no small-supplier exemption for a person physically carrying on business in BC; remote vendors with no physical presence in BC are generally required to register when making PST-taxable sales into BC, and marketplace facilitator rules may impose separate collection and registration obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: physical goods exported by or on behalf of the supplier, delivered to a destination outside Canada, and exported as soon as practicable are zero-rated under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Services and intangibles: a supply of a service or intangible made to a non-resident may be zero-rated where the supply is not supplied partly or wholly for the benefit of a person in Canada, is not related to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada, and is documented as delivered outside Canada. Video-app/IAP transactions received from Canadian consumers cannot be zero-rated merely because the seller is a non-resident. BC PST: export zero-rating applies to tangible personal property delivered outside BC; digital services delivered to and enjoyed in BC remain taxable unless the recipient is a non-resident and the service is used wholly outside BC.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A/G13 electronic report) or approved CBSA export reporting system record
  • Commercial invoice showing exporter, importer, destination, value, and HS code
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier proof of export
  • Signed contract or purchase order with the non-resident customer
  • Proof of foreign-currency consideration: bank inward remittance certificate, SWIFT/MT103, or Apple IAP/Paymentwall payout and reconciliation report
  • For zero-rated video/app services: service agreement, non-resident status evidence, IP/geolocation delivery logs, and periodic usage/consideration reconciliation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital tax stamping, or API-based e-invoicing. CRA accepts paper or electronic invoices that satisfy the information requirements of the Excise Tax Act. For exported physical goods, the exporter or carrier must file a CBSA electronic export declaration (Form B13A/G13) through an approved CBSA export reporting system before goods leave Canada. No pre-clearance, fiscal stamp, or government invoice validation applies at the federal or BC provincial level.

CRA will reverse zero-rating and assess GST/HST, PST, arrears interest, and penalties where export documentation and non-resident consideration are absent, inconsistent, or unconvincing. The six-year record retention rule applies, and the audit look-back may extend further on misrepresentation. Penalties under Excise Tax Act section 280(1) may reach 25% of the net tax attributable to a false or negligent statement, and section 285 penalties apply to knowing or wilful evasion. BC PST reassessments add interest and penalties under the Consumer Taxation Act, including non-registration penalties. The 180-day benchmark reflects standard Canadian banking and FX inward-remittance proof practices; there is no federal statute making day 181 automatically fatal, but failure to provide contemporaneous foreign-currency receipt evidence within 180 days commonly triggers full clawback of zero-rating benefits and rejection of refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a free-floating and fully convertible currency accepted under IMF Article VIII. Canada maintains no exchange-control restrictions on current-account remittances (dividends, royalties, interest, partnership distributions) or capital-account transactions. Non-resident limited partners may repatriate profits without Bank of Canada approval, subject only to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax and treaty reductions. Practical hurdles are documentary rather than statutory: FINTRAC EFT reporting for transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, beneficial-ownership attestations, tax-residency self-certifications (W-8BEN-E), and the bank's Foreign Account Registration (FAR) package. No capital-account licensing or convertibility approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters may lawfully hold foreign currency in domestic Schedule I bank accounts. For this LP's digital services (ISIC 6311), Apple IAP and Paymentwall settlements in USD may be retained in USD operating accounts or swept via FX forwards. Only FINTRAC large-transaction and EFT reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000) apply, together with GST/HST zero-rating for exported digital services.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with OSFI for prudential supervision and FINTRAC for AML/CFT enforcement; Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM (conditional). A British Columbia limited partnership may use regulated Canadian MSBs (e.g., Wise Canada Limited) or foreign EMIs for ancillary vendor payments, creator payouts, and FX netting, but such accounts cannot serve as the primary treasury settlement account because EMIs are not banks under the Bank Act (Canada) and are not CDIC-insured. A foreign EMI soliciting Canadian customers must register with FINTRAC as a foreign MSB or operate through a registered Canadian partner; failure to do so violates PCMLTFA sections 11.1 and 11.4. High-volume Apple IAP and Paymentwall settlements must be received into a Tier I Schedule I bank account, with EMI rails used only as sub-channels for low-value cross-border creator disbursements and subject to upstream SWIFT MT103 audit trails.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

23.0% combined Canadian federal (15%) and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate (8%); applicable to a corporate partner's allocated share from the limited partnership. For individual partners, the top combined marginal rate reaches 53.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act for dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. For the limited partnership itself, distributions of partnership income and capital are not dividends and are not subject to Part XIII withholding; instead, the non-resident partner is direct-taxed under Part I on the Canadian-source business income allocated to it. If the entity is considered a corporation under a treaty's tie-breaker rule, the 25% statutory rate may apply to any deemed dividend distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is mandatory when the aggregate fair market value of a Canadian taxpayer's non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceeds CA$1,000,000; a Master File is expected for Canadian participants of large multinational groups, and Country-by-Country reporting is required where consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds CA$760,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) framework similar to offshore jurisdictions. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act denies tax benefits from transactions lacking real economic substance, and the CRA may recharacterize transactions under the transfer pricing rules and sham doctrine. British Columbia's Limited Partnership Act requires all partnerships to have at least one general partner, and a limited partner who takes part in control of the business forfeits limited liability. The CRA and BC Registrar may also disregard partnerships that are mere shells or fail to maintain genuine commercial operations in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act: a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus up to 5 years imprisonment. Civil gross-negligence penalty is 50% of the understated tax, plus daily compounding interest and CRA audit costs.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners cannot shield themselves through the corporate veil where they participated in deliberate tax evasion. Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act imposes personal liability on directors for unremitted withheld taxes; individuals may face asset seizures, travel restrictions, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard the partnership under GAAR and reallocate income; BC Registrar may refuse renewal or dissolve the partnership. Penalties include denial of loss claims, restated taxable income, and monetary penalties up to CA$250,000 under relevant administrative enforcement powers.

Director personal liability: A limited partner who takes part in the control of the business becomes personally liable for all partnership obligations. Directors of the general partner who authorize sham structures face personal assessments for tax shortfalls and may be subject to director penalty provisions and criminal charges if fraudulent intent is proven.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach CA$1,000,000 per violation; criminal money laundering under the Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to 10 years. The entity may be subject to compliance orders, suspension, and forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly direct, supervise, or facilitate money laundering can be charged as parties to the offense and sentenced to imprisonment. FINTRAC may publicly name individuals, and courts can impose travel bans, asset freezes, and civil liability for the full amount laundered.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Act imposes a special penalty of 10% of the understated transfer price adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue or CA$5,000,000. Additionally, the adjusted taxable income is subject to full Canadian corporate tax, interest, and arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the relevant returns or directed the pricing strategy may be held personally liable for withheld amounts and assessed penalties. Where the transfer pricing arrangement is found to be a sham, the corporate veil can be pierced, exposing directors and controlling partners to civil restitution and criminal sanctions for false documentation.
