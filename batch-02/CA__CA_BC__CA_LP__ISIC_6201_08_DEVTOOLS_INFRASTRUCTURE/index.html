Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online and GST/HST registry; British Columbia Ministry of Finance – Consumer Taxation Branch for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST), via eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors forming a British Columbia limited partnership should (1) confirm the partnership has Canadian GST/HST registration nexus by carrying on business in Canada; (2) obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1A with CRA or using CRA Business Registration Online, selecting the GST/HST program account as needed and using the non-resident application path where the LP is administered from abroad; (3) register for the GST/HST once taxable worldwide revenues exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, or immediately if taxable supplies in Canada commence; (4) register for BC PST via eTaxBC before making PST-taxable sales into or within British Columbia; (5) provide CRA with the executed BC limited partnership agreement, name and Canadian address of a general partner, and evidence of the LP's pass-through structure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – a BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and provincial income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act; taxable income or loss is allocated to partners for their respective taxation years. The LP itself is not subject to entity-level income tax, although it may have GST/HST and PST filing obligations as a registrant/collector. The LP is not a 'taxable Canadian corporation'; no small business deduction is available at the partnership level.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election or a statutory pass-through election equivalent to United States Subchapter K. A BC LP is automatically treated as a partnership. For U.S. members, an IRS check-the-box election on Form 8832 can affect U.S. classification but does not alter Canada's fiscally transparent treatment. Hybrid mismatch rules embedded in the Income Tax Act, including provisions coordinated with OECD BEPS Action 2, may adjust the deductibility of payments if the BC LP is treated as opaque in a foreign jurisdiction or where tax outcomes differ between Canada and the partner residence jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies for GST/HST (small-supplier threshold); BC PST does not apply a comparable small-supplier threshold and requires PST registration before making PST-taxable sales in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating may apply to qualifying export supplies, including supplies of intangible personal property and services supplied to non-residents of Canada where the place of supply is outside Canada and the recipient does not receive the benefit of the supply in Canada. For the LP's developer infrastructure, database, API monitoring and DevOps cloud tools, a supply to a non-resident B2B customer is generally zero-rated if the customer is a non-resident, the supply is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property located in Canada, and the service or digital product is used or consumed wholly outside Canada. A registrant must retain contemporaneous documentary evidence of the customer's non-resident status and the foreign point of consumption to support zero-rating on the GST/HST return.

  • Commercial invoice with the zero-rated export designation and GST/HST registration number
  • Signed cloud subscription / service agreement identifying the non-resident customer and specifying non-Canadian delivery or place of use
  • Payment settlement evidence from Stripe Billing, Mercury ACH, wire transfer, or bank statements showing foreign-currency inbound remittance from the non-resident customer
  • Foreign customer giro data or certification of non-residence for GST/HST audit support

Canada does not impose a mandatory national real-time e-invoicing clearance system, API-based fiscal stamping, or government pre-authorization of invoices. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices that satisfy the Excise Tax Act, including supplier name, BN, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, invoice number, and total tax charged for taxable supplies over CAD 30; digital and PDF invoices are accepted. Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing via Peppol is voluntary. BC PST is administered separately through vendor registration, returns, and audit, not through e-invoice clearance or digital stamping.

CRA can audit the LP's zero-rated claims using transactional export evidence, tax returns, payment records and foreign customer documentation. A typical statutory reassessment period is four years, but it is unlimited where the LP has made a misrepresentation attributable to fraud, neglect, or wilful default. In addition to restored GST/HST, net-of-ITC clawback and compounded daily interest, penalties can be assessed under the Excise Tax Act, including false statement penalties and gross negligence penalties related to tax evasion; BC PST audit adjustments carry separate PST, interest, and penalty exposure. Repeated or fraudulent zero-rating abuse may result in criminal prosecution, enhanced penalties, and removal of the LP's ability to issue zero-rated invoices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and investment transactions. Canada generally permits unrestricted inbound and outbound capital, dividends, interest, management fees, loan repayments, and trade payments, subject to corporate-law authority, partnership-agreement terms, tax compliance, sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, beneficial-ownership verification, and bank transaction monitoring. A British Columbia limited partnership ordinarily has no statutory minimum capital requirement, but the partnership agreement, general-partner authority, lender covenants, investor documents, and applicable tax rules govern capital contributions and distributions. Payments to non-residents may require Canadian withholding-tax analysis, including possible withholding on interest, dividends, royalties, management fees, and certain services. Treaty relief generally requires beneficial-owner, residency, and documentation support and is not automatic. Non-resident partners should separately assess Canadian permanent-establishment, partnership-allocation, filing, and withholding obligations. Regulated or sanctioned counterparties, unusual third-party funding, high-risk jurisdictions, and transactions involving virtual assets may trigger enhanced due diligence or payment rejection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory requirement to repatriate or surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline for converting foreign-currency receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may ordinarily remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the entity's discretion. The partnership should retain contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery evidence, payment confirmations, and accounting records supporting the commercial purpose and tax treatment. Goods exports may be subject to customs reporting and export-control requirements; services and software exports may require sanctions, controlled-technology, privacy, tax, and contractual review. Cash or monetary instruments physically imported into or exported from Canada with a value of CAD 10,000 or more must generally be reported to the Canada Border Services Agency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange control authority imposing currency conversion, capital-account approval, or mandatory export-proceeds surrender. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist-financing obligations for reporting entities, including banks and certain money-services businesses. The Canada Border Services Agency and the Canada Revenue Agency administer customs, tax, and information-reporting requirements relevant to cross-border transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for supplementary treasury and payment operations; low as the sole Canadian banking relationship. An offshore electronic-money institution or foreign fintech may provide a wallet, foreign-currency collection account, card, or payment service, but it does not automatically provide a Canadian deposit account, Canadian Payments Act access, CDIC deposit insurance, or authority to perform regulated payment activities in Canada. Canadian payment-service-provider obligations under the Retail Payment Activities Act may apply to providers performing covered payment functions for end users in Canada. Canadian banks, payment processors, Stripe, and Mercury may impose separate onboarding, residency, beneficial-ownership, permitted-use, reserve, and transaction-volume requirements. Funds should be routed only through the named legal entity and verified account holders; third-party collection, commingling, undisclosed pass-through activity, and unsupported marketplace flows should be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined headline general corporate rate (15% federal general corporate rate + 12% British Columbia general corporate rate). A BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent, so this is the benchmark rate applicable to a corporate partner on its allocable share, not a tax imposed on the LP itself.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 Partnership Information Return must be filed on or before the day that is five months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period. For a December 31 fiscal year-end, the due date is May 31 of the following calendar year.
