Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or capital trust account is required under the Partnership Act; each limited partner's contribution is declared in the Declaration of Limited Partnership and must be paid in cash or stated-value property under s. 53 of the Act, and a limited partner failing to make the declared contribution remains liable for the outstanding amount under s. 54.

Funding & unlock path: Not engaged for virtual-only operations; if a registered office is leased, the premises must satisfy BC Building Code and Fire Code occupancy requirements (Class B office occupancy), obtain municipal occupancy and fire-safety clearance, and, on engaging the first worker, the LP must register with WorkSafeBC under the Workers Compensation Act, RSBC 1996, c. 492.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or capital trust account is required under the Partnership Act; each limited partner's contribution is declared in the Declaration of Limited Partnership and must be paid in cash or stated-value property under s. 53 of the Act, and a limited partner failing to make the declared contribution remains liable for the outstanding amount under s. 54.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online, GST/HST NETFILE, and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); British Columbia Ministry of Finance - eTaxBC portal (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) - Canadian Export Reporting System for customs matters.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or paper Form RC1; the BC Registry may provide a provisional BN through the integrated provincial registration. Step 2: Activate the GST/HST programme account (RT) under the BN; registration is mandatory where worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the relevant four-quarter window, and voluntary below this threshold to claim input tax credits. Step 3: For a limited partnership with non-resident partners, file the partnership identification details on Form RC1 and ensure the partnership has a Canadian representative or resident agent for service as required under provincial law. Step 4: If Canadian employees are engaged, open a payroll account (RP) with CRA and a BC employer health tax account with the BC Ministry of Finance. Step 5: Register the BC limited partnership with the BC Registrar of Companies under the Partnership Act; register for BC PST through eTaxBC if the partnership makes taxable sales of physical or custom software in British Columbia. Step 6: If importing or exporting tangible goods, also obtain a CBSA import/export account and a customs programme account (RM) under the BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under Income Tax Act s. 96(2), the BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Part I income tax purposes: the partnership files Form T5013 (Partnership Information Return) but does not pay entity-level income tax; each resident and non-resident partner reports its agreed distributable share of Canadian-source income or loss. Territorial_Scope: non-resident partners remain taxable in Canada on Canadian-source business income allocated through the partnership, and limited partners' loss claims are restricted to the 'at-risk amount' under ITA s. 96(2.2). For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' under the Excise Tax Act and must register and account for GST in its own name. No Tax_Exempt_Zone applies to this entity in British Columbia.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; partnership transparency is statutory. Available elections and structuring tools: (i) allocation of partnership income and loss by partnership agreement under ITA s. 96(1); (ii) non-calendar fiscal year-end election under ITA s. 249.1(3) where all partners are Canadian residents; (iii) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(3) for taxable supplies below the CAD 30,000 threshold; (iv) rollover election under ITA s. 85 when transferring the partnership's cloud software business assets to a taxable Canadian corporation; (v) hybrid and anti-hybrid measures: Canada applies the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) and general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, ITA s. 245) to counter hybrid mismatch arrangements involving partnerships and non-resident entities; no election converts the LP into an entity-level taxed corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export revenue) measured on a trailing four-quarter basis, with mandatory registration once the threshold is exceeded and voluntary registration available below it. BC PST: registration through eTaxBC is required for any vendor making taxable sales of goods or taxable software in British Columbia; no separate minimal revenue threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, for supplies made to non-residents. For the ERP/CRM cloud service model: a supply of software-as-a-service or a license of intangible personal property to a non-resident person is zero-rated where (i) the recipient is not a resident of Canada at the time the supply is made; (ii) the recipient is not registered for GST/HST or does not acquire the supply for use in its Canadian commercial activity; (iii) the supply is not in respect of real or tangible personal property situated in Canada; and (iv) the supply is genuinely consumed or used outside Canada. The Canadian supplier must retain contemporaneous written evidence proving non-resident status, foreign place of supply, and offshore use, including executed contracts, foreign billing addresses, and international payment receipts. If the customer is a Canadian resident or a Canadian branch of a foreign purchaser, the supply is subject to 5% GST and cannot be zero-rated.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A/B13B or Canadian Export Reporting System filing) for physical media or controlled goods; not applicable to purely electronic software delivery
  • Commercial invoice or master contract stating supplier and recipient legal names, non-resident address, ERP/CRM license description, and consideration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill signed by the carrier where tangible media is shipped abroad
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the invoiced currency
  • Non-resident self-declaration and CRA-compliant export evidence file recording recipient GST/HST non-registration and foreign place of consumption

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping. The CRA accepts electronic invoices and supporting books and records in PDF, JSON, CSV, or similar machine-readable formats if they are complete and retained for seven years. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual gross revenues exceeding CAD 1.5 million, who must file via GST/HST NETFILE; smaller registrants may file electronically or on paper. The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and PEPPOL interoperability are voluntary and do not affect input tax credit validity. No API-based or government pre-clearance system is in force.

The CRA may reassess an export zero-rating claim within the normal four-year reassessment window, or without time limit in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Where the export evidence is insufficient, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% GST, and the supplier is assessed for uncollected GST plus daily compounding interest under ETA ss. 280 and 281. Knowingly or grossly negligently claiming zero-rating on a false basis attracts a penalty under ETA s. 285 equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understated net tax, alongside potential criminal prosecution for tax evasion under ITA s. 239(1), with fines up to 200% of the amount evaded and imprisonment. CRA commodity tax audits specifically examine SaaS export claims and will attribute cloud subscription revenue to Canadian commercial activity where the end user, server utilization, or contract performance is connected to Canada.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility; Canada accepts Article VIII obligations of the IMF, and the Canadian dollar floats freely. No general exchange-control law restricts settlement of software export invoices, equity capital subscriptions, partner loans, or licensing royalties. Profit remittance friction is exclusively fiscal, not administrative: payments of interest, royalties, management fees, or deemed distributions to non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding of up to 25%, with certain treaty rates reduced, while residual profit distributions carved from Canadian business income are allocated under s.96 of the Income Tax Act and taxed under Part I; the LP treasury must document the associated tax-residency certifications for the paying bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no repatriation ratio, and no mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds for software and cloud services (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210). Export receipts may be retained in USD or other foreign currency in an OSFI-regulated account; the only mandatory compliance includes FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer reporting for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, record-keeping under s.230 of the Income Tax Act, and the T1135 information return if foreign cash or deposits held by the LP or its foreign-related entities exceed CAD 100,000 in the aggregate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central bank; no separate foreign exchange administration exists. Policy and supervision of cross-border financial activity are shared with the Department of Finance Canada (Currency Act), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank FX-risk and prudential control, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border EFT reporting and AML/ATF enforcement.

Airwallex Fit: High

RATING: Low-Medium as a Schedule I bank substitute; Low as a primary liquidity depository. UK/EU e-money licences and US state money-transmitter licences confer no passport into Canada. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Submit a full UBO organogram through to ultimate natural persons, a certified partnership agreement, a beneficial ownership declaration for each layer, the BC Registry Certificate of Limited Partnership, and an independent source-of-wealth letter from every 25% or greater ultimate beneficial owner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Disbursement of foreign partners' capital contributions must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 from the partner's instructing bank to the BC LP's designated Schedule I receiving account, with the LP's full legal name and remittance text reading 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - LIMITED PARTNERSHIP SUBSCRIPTION / NOT A TRADE PAYMENT'. The receiving bank verifies originator and beneficiary identities and UBOs, screens against OFAC and Canadian Criminal Code Part II.1 lists, files the FINTRAC EFT report when the CAD equivalent is CAD 10,000 or more, and converts the received currency at the bank's intraday risk-free rate. Funds are held in the segregated capital escrow sub-account and released to the operational account only after completion of tax self-certifications (including W-8BEN-E for US partners or the equivalent non-resident declaration for other jurisdictions) and submission of the capital call notice, subscription agreement, partnership resolution, and BC Registry Certificate. No Bank of Canada approval or import authorization is required.Operational payables settle domestically through Payments Canada ACSS direct credits and PAD debits, with high-value payments through the Lynx RTGS system. Cross-border vendor and trade payments settle via SWIFT MT103 wires through the Canadian bank's US/New York correspondent or global clearing partner. Trade finance obligations are handled through irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the Canadian bank under UCP600, transmitted by SWIFT MT700 and payable through the beneficiary's confirming bank. Client receivables from cloud subscriptions are boarded on Stripe Billing as the primary card and ACH/PAD rail, with settlement in CAD to the LP's operational account; non-CAD supplier disbursements are converted by the treasury desk and executed as SWIFT MT103/MT202 cover messages with full audit trail retention for at least six years.OTC deliverable foreign exchange forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) executed with a Schedule I OSFI-regulated dealer under the Canadian FX Global Code and a signed ISDA 2002 Master Agreement.Cross-currency interest rate swaps for hedging multi-year USD-denominated capital injections, sub-license fees, and cloud infrastructure contracts, documented under ISDA 2002 and a Credit Support Annex.Vanilla FX options, zero-cost collars, and participating forwards with the relationship bank, subject to a board-approved hedge designation memo and IFRS 9 hedge documentation.Exchange-traded CAD/USD futures and options on the Montreal Exchange (TMX) for liquid tenors up to one year.Non-deliverable forwards for illiquid emerging-market currency pairs where the LP invoices in CAD but must pay an overseas subcontractor in a non-convertible local currency, settled in USD or CAD under ISDA documentation.FX tom-next rollovers and foreign exchange swaps used to roll hedge positions across value dates without settlement gap, settled through the CLS PvP system to eliminate counterparty settlement risk on institutional flows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: RATING: Low-Medium as a Schedule I bank substitute; Low as a primary liquidity depository. UK/EU e-money licences and US state money-transmitter licences confer no passport into Canada. To serve the BC LP, a foreign EMI would need FINTRAC registration as a foreign money services business under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, plus a Canadian agent and a compliant AML program; even then it cannot accept deposits or access Canadian clearing rails because only Payments Canada member banks access Lynx and ACSS. Use platforms such as Wise Business, Airwallex, or Revolut Business only for supplementary FX execution and low-value vendor payouts, never for primary capital custody.
