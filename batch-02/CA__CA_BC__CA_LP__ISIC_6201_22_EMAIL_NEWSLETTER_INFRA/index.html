Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal tax administration and Business Number issuance, administered through the CRA 'My Business Account' portal and Business Registration Online; British Columbia Corporate Registry (Registrar of Companies, BC Ministry of Finance) for limited partnership registration; BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for the separate British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administered independently of the federal GST.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Limited Partnership with the BC Corporate Registry via Corporate Online (no minimum capital); Step 2: Apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or Form RC103, including a GST/HST account where the partnership is or expects to become a registrant; registration is required once worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the trailing four calendar quarters (small supplier threshold, ETA ss. 148-149), with mandatory registration within 30 days of exceeding the threshold (ETA s. 240); Step 3: Non-resident partners who lack a Canadian tax identifier should obtain a CRA-issued identification number through the International Tax Services Office (Form NR3018 or correspondent request) and file Canadian returns as applicable; Step 4: For PST, evaluate alternative registration with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch only if the LP makes PST-taxable retail sales of pre-written software by download or other enumerated taxable supplies in BC; hosted remote-access SaaS services are generally not PST-taxable; Step 5: File annually the T5013 Partnership Information Return with T5013 slips to all partners, including non-resident partners, using the partnership BN.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible_With_Entity_Level_GST_Person_Status: The BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and provincial income tax purposes; it is not an entity-level income taxpayer, and each partner is taxed directly on its allocated share of profit or loss (ITA s. 96). The partnership must nevertheless file an informational T5013 return. For GST/HST purposes, a partnership itself is a 'person' and may hold its own Business Number, register for GST/HST, charge and recover GST/HST, and claim input tax credits as a registrant. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible_With_Entity_Level_GST_Person_Status: The BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal and provincial income tax purposes; it is not an entity-level income taxpayer, and each partner is taxed directly on its allocated share of profit or loss (ITA s. 96). The partnership must nevertheless file an informational T5013 return. For GST/HST purposes, a partnership itself is a 'person' and may hold its own Business Number, register for GST/HST, charge and recover GST/HST, and claim input tax credits as a registrant. Territorial scope: Canadian income tax reaches the partnership's Canadian-source income allocated to both resident and non-resident partners; non-resident partners are taxable on income from carrying on business in Canada unless relieved under an income tax treaty, and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on allocations of partnership income from Canadian sources (subject to treaty modification). The LP is not located in a tax-exempt zone; Canadian statutory rules apply in full.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box classification election; the LP's fiscal transparency is fixed by statute. Available elections and mechanisms include: (1) ITA s. 96(1)-(2) partnership income-allocation elections to agree on the computation of income and the fiscal period; (2) ITA s. 97(2) election allowing partners to transfer capital property to the partnership at an elected amount that differs from fair market value, triggering gain/loss at the partner level; (3) ETA s. 273/274 joint elections for closely related registrants (corporations and partnerships satisfying the 90% ownership threshold) to re-characterize taxable inter-entity supplies as supplies for no consideration, eliminating GST/HST leakage on internal infrastructure services; (4) ETA s. 156 election by a registrant to treat regularly available financial services supplied to a related registered financial institution as taxable (not relevant to the email infrastructure business unless financial services are later added); (5) ETA s. 191/192 ITC allocation methods and joint elections for partnerships using indirect apportionment of input tax credits for mixed-use inputs; (6) hybrid mismatch documentation and elections under ITA ss. 12.2(7)-(8) and 18.2-18.6 to neutralize deduction/non-inclusion mismatches arising from foreign partners treated fiscally opaque in their home jurisdiction; no domestic election can convert the LP into a taxable corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) in a single calendar quarter or over the trailing four calendar quarters is the small supplier threshold (ETA ss. 148-149). Once exceeded, the partnership must register within 30 days (ETA s. 240). Voluntary GST/HST registration is available even below the threshold to allow recovery of input tax credits and to zero-rate export supplies. PST has no equivalent economic threshold: PST obligations arise only where BC taxable retail transactions are made, and PST registration is not required for a pure remote-access SaaS provider absent a statutory taxable category.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The email API / newsletter hosting platform qualifies for zero-rating as a supply of a service made to a non-resident person under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (service supplied in Canada to a non-resident for payment, not falling within exclusions for services relating to real property situated in Canada, goods situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or services supplied to a non-resident individual who is present in Canada at the time of performance). Because the platform is delivered electronically and the recipient is a non-resident business customer, the supply is a zero-rated taxable supply: 0% GST applies and input tax credits remain claimable. The registrant must demonstrate that the recipient is not resident in Canada and that the service is not acquired for consumption, use, or supply in Canada. Tangible goods exported from Canada are separately zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part I, but the primary service here is borderless digital infrastructure. Where any integration hardware is supplied incidentally with the service, separate export documentation applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration: for any tangible goods exported from Canada, the CBSA Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration / CAED) or Form B13A as required; not applicable to pure electronic SaaS delivery
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill: transport document proving physical movement of goods out of Canada where tangible exports occur
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and/or foreign-currency bank receipts: wire-transfer records and bank account statements evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident customer in convertible currency
  • Commercially complete invoice and signed subscription or contract services agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, the electronic service description, and the place of supply
  • Recipient non-residency evidence: IP address logs, billing address, domain registration, government or corporate registry evidence of the customer's foreign establishment
  • ETA s. 286 general records: books and vouchers demonstrating the zero-rated nature of the supply and supporting any input tax credit claimed
  • CRA Form GST11 selected as applicable for documentary evidence in the event of a refund claim or audit review

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance system, no continuous transaction control regime, no mandatory government-linked e-invoicing clearance hub, and no digital tax stamp. Electronic invoices are accepted provided they are complete and retained. The operative mandate is documentary substantiation and record retention under ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230: the LP must retain all books, contracts, invoices, recipient-identification evidence, and bank records supporting the zero-rated export of services. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically where annual taxable revenue exceeds CAD 1,500,000, using GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account; electronic filing penalties apply under ETA s. 280.2. Filing and payment cycles are monthly, quarterly, or annual as assigned by the CRA, with remittance due on the last day of the month following the reporting period.

CRA audit scrutiny of export zero-rating is elevated for digital-service exporters. Where zero-rating is not substantiated, the CRA may assess the LP for uncollected GST/HST plus interest, and may disallow input tax credits (ETA Part IX). Penalties include: ETA s. 281.1 false-statement penalty of CAD 1,000 plus 25% of the tax that was or would have been collectible or refundable; ETA s. 280(1) gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the amount of tax collected or refund claimed attributable to careless or deliberate misstatements; criminal prosecution under ETA s. 285 and ITA s. 239 for willful evasion, with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment; and ITA s. 163.2 civil preparer/misrepresentation penalties. The four-year normal reassessment window under ETA s. 298 and ITA s. 152(4) is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, negligence, carelessness, willful default, or fraud is found. Repeated unsubstantiated export claims may also trigger the CRA's discretionary revocation of the GST/HST registration under ETA s. 241, causing irrecoverable GST on future supplies and loss of refund entitlements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible on both current and capital accounts (IMF Article VIII status since 1952). Canada operates a free-floating exchange rate with no repatriation permits, no deposit requirements, no central-bank approval for FX purchases, and no restrictions on holding foreign currency domestically. Cross-border capital injections, SaaS subscription receipts in USD/EUR, and LP profit distributions to non-resident partners are unrestricted. The only constraints are income-tax reporting obligations: Part I taxation of non-resident partners on Canadian-source business income, Part XIII withholding on amounts paid to non-residents that are characterized as interest, royalties, rents, or management fees, and treaty-based rate reductions (e.g., Canada-US Treaty).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules exist in Canada. There are no statutory deadlines for repatriating export or SaaS subscription receipts, no mandatory conversion ratio to CAD, and no minimum retention in domestic banks. A BC LP may keep unlimited USD/EUR balances in foreign-currency accounts at any Schedule I, II, or III bank or FINTRAC-registered MSB. The only obligations are accrual accounting in CAD for income-tax purposes and FINTRAC large-transaction or suspicious-transaction reporting at the CAD 10,000 threshold.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). FX policy is set under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act, administered jointly with the Minister of Finance. Prudential supervision of all Schedule I banks is performed by OSFI under the Bank Act; AML/ATF surveillance and MSB licensing are executed by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; the Investment Canada Act requires a 30-day notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada for a non-Canadian establishing a new Canadian business.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Canada has no separate 'EMI licence' class; digital cross-border providers (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Stripe Treasury partners) operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under provincial consumer-protection statutes (Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act in British Columbia) rather than as banks. They are legally usable for collections (Stripe Billing payouts), sub-ledger settlement, and low-value USD/EUR vendor disbursements, but they are not CDIC-insured custodians and cannot offer commercial letters of credit or deliverable FX forwards. A Schedule I chartered bank remains mandatory for the LP's capital accounts, statutory KYC proof, and treasury hedging; EMIs should be layered as auxiliary rails, not the primary treasury counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Investments route via SWIFT MT103 (single beneficiary credit) or MT202 COV from the investor's bank to the LP's account at a Schedule I British Columbia bank with purpose code 'Capital Contribution' and the LP's legal-entity identifier (LEI) in the remittance information field. The bank's FX and compliance team matches the wire to a General Partner resolution and an executed subscription agreement naming the LP and the investor; source-of-funds documentation (investor KYC pack, six-month bank statement, proof of legal existence) must be pre-submitted. Where the investor is non-Canadian, the LP files the Investment Canada Act establishment notification with ISED within 30 days of launch; no pre-approval is required for non-cultural IT infrastructure businesses. Contributions may be held in a segregated capital escrow sub-account until the capital call is satisfied, thereafter sweeping to the operating account.Outflow rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for international vendor settlements (cloud, email-infrastructure, data-center, licensing) in CAD/USD/EUR; (2) US ACH or Fedwire via the Canadian bank's US clearing network (TD Bank US, BMO Harris) for US-domiciled SaaS vendors; (3) Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS, >CAD 25M) for high-value Canadian-dollar trades; (4) irrevocable confirmed commercial letters of credit for material infrastructure contracts requiring trade-finance assurance (issued on bank paper; rarely needed for a SaaS business but available); (5) Stripe Billing engine settling collected subscription revenue directly into the LP's operational account in CAD, with periodic USD sweeps to the USD clearing account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Canada has no separate 'EMI licence' class; digital cross-border providers (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Stripe Treasury partners) operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under provincial consumer-protection statutes (Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act in British Columbia) rather than as banks. They are legally usable for collections (Stripe Billing payouts), sub-ledger settlement, and low-value USD/EUR vendor disbursements, but they are not CDIC-insured custodians and cannot offer commercial letters of credit or deliverable FX forwards. A Schedule I chartered bank remains mandatory for the LP's capital accounts, statutory KYC proof, and treasury hedging; EMIs should be layered as auxiliary rails, not the primary treasury counterparty.
