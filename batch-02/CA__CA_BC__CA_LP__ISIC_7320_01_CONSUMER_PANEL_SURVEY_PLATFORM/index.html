Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business-business-number-registration.html); for provincial sales tax, BC Ministry of Finance – eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

A British Columbia Limited Partnership (BC LP) obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online or, for non-resident applicants, by filing Form RC176 or equivalent. The applicant must supply the LP's constituent documents (registration statement, partnership agreement), identify a representative/resident agent if the LP has no Canadian residence, and elect the desired statutory accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RM (import/export). A GST/HST account (RT) is mandatory if the LP's gross taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a trailing 12-month period or if it engages in commercial activity in Canada. For BC PST, registration must be completed through eTaxBC once the LP makes taxable sales of goods or services in British Columbia; an in-province agent may be required for non-resident registrants.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canadian federal or BC provincial income tax law. A BC LP is fiscally transparent under ITA section 96; the partnership files an information return (T5013) but is not taxed as a separate entity. Available elections include: (i) ETA subsection 273(1) qualifying-partnership election to treat one designated member as the registrant for GST/HST purposes in respect of partnership supplies; (ii) ITA section 216 election for non-resident partners to report and be taxed on net Canadian-source rental or logging income; (iii) GST/HST quick method and streamlined accounting elections if eligibility thresholds are met; (iv) tax treaty transparent-entity elections/look-through notifications where applicable, including hybrid mismatch coordination under the Canada-US treaty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide gross taxable supplies (trailing 12-month period) triggers mandatory registration. BC PST: registration is required for sellers making taxable sales in BC, subject to the small-seller exemption for aggregate taxable sales of CAD 50,000 or less in the preceding 12 months; this exemption does not apply to services that are prescribed PST-taxable and is subject to current BC law.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate a supply of market research / consumer panel survey platform services under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act if the supply is made to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST, the service is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the service is not otherwise rendered to an individual physically present in Canada at the time the service is performed. CRA administrative practice requires documentation sufficient to show that the recipient is non-resident and that the supply is an export (e.g., contract, invoices, payment instructions, and evidence that the service's initiative or target audience is outside Canada).

  • Executed service agreement with the non-resident client identifying the recipient and terms
  • Commercial invoices and periodic service/usage reports evidencing that market-research deliverables were performed and supplied outside Canada
  • Non-residency evidence for the purchaser, such as W-8 forms, foreign registry extract, or equivalent self-declaration
  • Bank remittance records or payment confirmations from PayPal Mass Payout / Wise showing foreign-source payment in CAD or FX
  • Internal GST/HST zero-rating worksheet referencing the applicable Schedule VI, Part V provision and the CRA file number

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no national e-invoicing API requirement, and no digital stamping regime applies in Canada or BC. B2B and B2C invoices may be issued in paper or electronic format. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE. The pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework is available on a voluntary basis for B2G e-invoicing.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period (or longer in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud) and reverse zero-ratings, assess GST at 5%, charge arrears interest, and apply penalties. ETA subsection 285.1 imposes up to a 25% false-statement penalty on net tax misreported; ITA section 163(2) provides a 50% gross-negligence penalty on income misstatements; intentional evasion may lead to criminal prosecution and fines under the Criminal Code or the Excise Tax Act. Retroactive clawbacks are limited by the applicable statutory assessment window unless the taxpayer waives it.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account currency convertibility consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no exchange-control approvals for inward or outward transfers, and the Canadian dollar trades freely. Profit remittances by a British Columbia Limited Partnership to non-resident limited partners are legally unrestricted at the FX level. The only structural hurdles are tax-related: a non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP is generally taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on its share of Canadian-source business income and must file a Canadian return; if profit allocations are instead characterized as distributions not attributable to a permanent establishment, careful treaty analysis and, where applicable, Part XIII withholding or a section 216 election apply. Anti-money-laundering record-keeping and FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting are the only prudential friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no prescribed conversion ratio exists in Canada. Exporters and cross-border service providers, including the LP, may retain export earnings in any convertible currency and repatriate or disburse them freely. However, under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, every international electronic funds transfer (IEFT) of C$10,000 or more in a single transaction is reportable by the financial institution to FINTRAC, and the underlying payment documentation must include the originator, beneficiary, and transaction purpose to evidence the commercial export of market-research services.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and payments-systems authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential supervisor of federally regulated banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/ATF and cross-border EFT reporting authority. Canada does not operate a dedicated foreign-exchange control administration equivalent to an exchange-control bureau; foreign-exchange policy is effected through the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and OSFI/FINTRAC compliance requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Currenxie) used as payout-facilitation rails; Low as a primary depository or capital-escrow provider. Legal constraints: offshore EMIs are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act (Canada); they may operate as Money Service Businesses or foreign payment-service providers and, if servicing Canadian clients, generally require FINTRAC MSB registration unless a network/relaxation applies. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, payment services that are not deposit-taking are subject to registration and oversight by the Bank of Canada when in force. Consequently, the BC Limited Partnership should maintain its principal treasury and capital escrow with a Canadian Schedule I bank for CDIC-insured deposits, BC registry credibility, and investor protection, while using FINTRAC-registered EMIs solely for low-value cross-border mass payouts to consumer panelists and survey respondents (e.g., PayPal Mass Payout, Wise Batch).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) non-resident limited partners initiate a SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire/CHIPS for USD) to the LP's Schedule I bank account in British Columbia, quoting the exact beneficiary name and BC LP registration number in the beneficiary field; (2) the remittance information narrative should state 'LP capital contribution - BC registry [CA_BC entity identifier]' to facilitate Canadian balance-of-payments coding as direct investment/equity, as Canada does not mandate a numeric FX purpose code; (3) the receiving bank must report any international EFT of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC and will retain an originator/beneficiary record; (4) the treasury team must retain the capital-call notice, signed subscription documents, passports/tax IDs of the remitting partners, and the originating bank statement to substantiate the source of funds; (5) the CAD-denominated domestic leg settles via Payments Canada Lynx high-value system; USD legs settle through the bank's U.S. correspondent and are converted at prevailing market rates or under a pre-agreed forward; (6) the bank's credit advice should be filed to the LP's capital ledger to support future Part I/treaty filings and any capital-account attribution.Clearing rails: (1) high-value vendor settlements and fieldwork-procurement invoices are paid by SWIFT MT103 / ISO 20022 pain.001 through the Canadian clearing network (Lynx for CAD;
