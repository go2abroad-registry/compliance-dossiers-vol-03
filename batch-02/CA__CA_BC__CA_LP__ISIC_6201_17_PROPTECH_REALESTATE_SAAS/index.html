Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current account and capital account transactions. Canada does not maintain an exchange-control approval regime or foreign-investment screening requirement for ordinary PropTech SaaS operating capital. Profit remittance and partnership distributions to non-resident partners are lawful, subject to Canadian tax compliance (withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act where distribution is a dividend or interest-like amount), GST/HST reporting on SaaS revenue, and BN/NR registration. No Bank of Canada authorization is required for dividend, interest, or distribution payments. Practical hurdles are limited to demanding KYC documentation from the receiving bank and adherence to cross-border wire reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose statutory surrender, conversion, or holding-period rules for export proceeds. All USD and foreign-currency export receipts may be retained in non-CAD accounts, converted freely, or used directly for trade settlements. There is no receipt-surrender certificate, no CBI/FEMA-style valuation documentation, and no deadline for realization. The applicable obligation is record-keeping under the Income Tax Act and filing of cross-border transaction reports with FINTRAC for electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the Minister of Finance retains statutory authority over Canada's exchange control and monetary policy framework, while FINTRAC supervises AML/ATF obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary settlement, Medium as a supplementary collection channel. Offshore digital EMI platforms (including foreign-licensed electronic money institutions) are not licensed as banks in Canada and cannot provide the legal status of a Canadian deposit account. They may be used for small recurring SaaS subscription collections or for access to low-fee FX conversion, but the main treasury rails must sit with a Canadian Schedule I bank or a branch of an authorized foreign bank. Any EMI providing payment services to Canadian customers must be registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC and must identify the BC LP's beneficial owner consistent with PCMLTFA. Such EMI arrangements are legally viable only as ancillary rails, with funds swept daily to the Canadian LP operational account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% entity-level income tax for the British Columbia Limited Partnership; income or loss passes through and is allocated to each partner. Benchmark combined federal and BC headline corporate rates: 11% for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) on qualifying active business income up to CAD 500,000; 27% for general corporate income (federal 15% after provincial tax abatement plus BC 12%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII statutory rate is 25% for dividends paid by a taxable Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder. A BC limited partnership does not make dividend distributions; cash withdrawals by partners are treaty-privileged distributions or return of capital and are not subject to Part XIII withholding. Allocated profits of non-resident partners from a Canadian business may be taxable in Canada on a net basis under Part I unless eliminated by an income tax treaty’s business-profits article.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within six months after the partnership fiscal period end. For a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is June 30 of the following calendar year. Partner statements (T5013 slips) must generally be provided to each partner by March 31 after the fiscal period end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the corporation’s taxation year-end. Tax balance due dates are generally two months after year-end for ordinary corporations and three months after year-end for a CCPC claiming the small business deduction.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T106 must be filed within six months after the end of the fiscal period of the partnership or corporation, whichever reporting entity is required to file.

Part I — Reportable transaction amount Aggregate cost and/or proceeds for each non-arm’s length transaction with a non-resident, including SaaS licence fees, royalties, management fees, interest, software development buy-sell arrangements, and intercompany cloud services. Threshold triggering T106 is exceeded when the aggregate fair-market value or cost of reportable transactions with all non-arm’s length non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 per fiscal period. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 filing threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate value of reportable non-arm’s length transactions with non-residents in a fiscal period. Master File and Local File contemporaneous documentation should be maintained when the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000; below this threshold, the CRA may still request transfer-pricing documentation, and contemporaneous evidence must be available to support every non-arm’s-length cross-border transaction. A Local File is recommended for any Canadian target with substantial intercompany SaaS licensing, cloud hosting, or development fees.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act disallows avoidance transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose. CRA consistently applies substance-over-form and can re-characterize transactions under section 247 if the form conceals a different economic arrangement. A BC LP with no employees, no Canadian office, no books, and no genuine operational management can be treated as a shell; the limitation of liability may be lost under BC Partnership Act if a limited partner participates in control, and the corporate general partner must have meaningful substance to avoid agency/permanent-establishment exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file partnership information return and T106

Statutory sanction: T5013 penalty of CAD 25 per partner per day up to CAD 2,500; T106 documentation penalty of CAD 1,000 to CAD 24,000; gross negligence penalty under subsection 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; compounding daily interest; criminal penalties for deliberate failure to comply.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted payroll or withholding taxes under section 227.1; CRA may issue a director-assessable amount and enforce against personal bank accounts. Criminal prosecution can also result in imprisonment, fines, asset freezes, and judicial interim release conditions restricting travel.
