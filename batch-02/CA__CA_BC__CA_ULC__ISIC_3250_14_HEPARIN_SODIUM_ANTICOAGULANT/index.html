Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and corporate tax registration portal: CRA My Business Account / GST/HST Registry (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); provincial counterpart for BC: BC Ministry of Finance / BC Corporate Registry and BC PST Administration (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Company (BC ULC) must first incorporate or register extraprovincially with BC Corporate Registry, obtain a BC incorporation number, then apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for a federal Business Number (BN). For GST/HST purposes, the ULC must register through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) or by completing Form RC1 (Request for Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). The CRA will issue a 9-digit BN with an 'RT' account suffix for GST/HST, and an 'RC' account suffix for corporate income tax if the ULC is a resident of Canada for tax purposes. A foreign parent with no Canadian residence may require the ULC to file a Canadian corporate tax return and obtain a tax account even if treaty protection is claimed, and may need a separate CRA payroll account (RP) if it has employees. Non-resident directors or shareholders must provide passports, proof of registered office address in BC, and a completed Form RC459 (Canada–U.S. or other treaty waiver request) where applicable. Statutory registration is completed by furnishing the certificate of incorporation, registered office address, directors' information, and the foreign parent's jurisdiction and tax identification details.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The BC/AB Unlimited Liability Corporation is a hybrid entity eligible for U.S. federal check-the-box classification as a disregarded entity or partnership, while for Canadian tax purposes it generally remains a corporation resident in Canada. A BC ULC may elect under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada) if non-resident-owned; may elect under subsection 45(3) for deemed disposition on change of use; may make a joint election under section 216 re: rental income; may file an election under subsection 256(7) to be associated with another corporation; and for GST/HST, may elect under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act to treat closely related corporations as a single GST/HST registrant. Hybrid mismatch elections under Canadian rules (section 18.4 of the Income Tax Act) are available for foreign branch structures but generally deny deductions where there is a hybrid mismatch, requiring a joint election or documentation under s. 18.4(4) and 18.4(5) in limited circumstances.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies from Canada over a calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. BC PST registration is required immediately before making taxable sales/leases in BC unless an exemption applies; there is no general small-supplier threshold for PST, but specified exemptions apply for certain low-volume vendors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Heparin sodium anticoagulant exported by a BC ULC qualifies for zero-rating under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports tangible personal property by commercial shipment or by mail and retains evidence of export within prescribed time limits. Property shipped to a non-resident for processing and re-imported may be zero-rated under section 3 of Part V if the goods are exported in accordance with the regulations. Services performed on exported tangible personal property (e.g., fractionation/purification services on heparin crude destined for export) may be zero-rated under section 25 of Part V. To claim zero-rating, the supplier must have documentary proof satisfactory to the Minister that the goods were exported (generally within 60 days of the sale), and must retain evidence such as bills of lading, customs export declarations, and freight waybills showing the vendor as exporter or the purchaser as seller.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13 / Canadian Automated Export Declaration)
  • Automated Commercial Invoice or Canada Customs Invoice
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic negotiable/non-negotiable)
  • Waybill or freight forwarder cargo receipt
  • Insurance certificate showing Canadian place of shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment/confirmed Letter of Credit record
  • Certificate of origin or supply-chain documentation for U.S.-bound goods (CUSMA) if applicable

Canada does not mandate a nationwide real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital fiscal stamping regime for GST/HST. CRA accepts electronic invoices in PDF, XML, or EDI formats under the Electronic Commerce and Electronic Documents criteria. GST/HST registrants must retain complete invoice records in Canada or make them available electronically on request. BC PST does not require government pre-clearance of invoices; however, PST vendors must issue sales invoices showing PST separately. For pharmaceutical/heparin exports, no e-invoice clearance is required, but exporters must transmit Canada Customs Invoice / CUSMA or commercial invoice data to CBSA through the Canadian Single Window / eManifest portal for customs release.

CRA can reassess a zero-rated export within the normal reassessment period (generally four years) to deny the zero-rating and collect GST/HST, interest, and penalties if the exporter cannot substantiate export. For fraud or gross negligence in claiming export zero-rating, penalties can be up to 25% of the amount claimed under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act, plus wilful-evasion criminal fines under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act (up to 100% of the tax evaded or CAD 50,000 on summary conviction / CAD 500,000 on indictment, and imprisonment). CRA may also revoke GST/HST registrations and seize records. BC PST applies to goods delivered in BC; exports to foreign jurisdictions are exempt, but false evidence can trigger PST reassessments and penalties of up to 25% of the tax plus daily interest under BC's Provincial Sales Tax Act.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible on both current-account and capital-account under a floating exchange-rate regime, compliant with IMF Article VIII obligations. No exchange controls restrict dividend, interest, royalty, or profit repatriation beyond standard Canadian non-resident withholding tax (Part XIII) and tax treaty procedures. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. The only material limitation for a BC ULC is the Investment Canada Act review threshold for controlling investments by non-Canadians, and any regulated transaction paperwork required by CBSA for controlled goods and Health Canada for active pharmaceutical ingredients.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, repatriation-timeline, or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may hold foreign currency in authorized accounts, invoice in any currency, and settle through the interbank FX market. For a heparin-sodium producer, export documentation must comply with the Customs Act export reporting (CBSA Form B13/Canadian Export Reporting System) for goods valued above C$2,000, and any exportation of controlled or listed precursor/disinfectant items under Health Canada regulations must be covered by permits; the export proceeds themselves may be retained in USD/EUR without forced CAD conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; exchange control administration is shared with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange-control ministry.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as ancillary payment processor, Low as primary treasury bank. Canada does not license 'electronic money institutions' under EU-style EMI legislation; deposit-taking must be through a bank, trust company, or regulated credit union. Offshore digital payment platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, MultiPass, intergiro) may be used as FINTRAC-registered money services businesses or non-bank payment facilitators, but they cannot issue Canadian Letters of Credit or provide custodial escrow that satisfies Canadian securities/trust law. For deterministic clearing of pharmaceutical LC and high-value heparin settlements, a digital EMI should be limited to small USD collection and must remain subordinated to a Schedule I bank master account.
