Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) via BC Corporate Online / OneStop Business Registry; a BC registered office and records office are mandatory, but there is no BC residency requirement for directors and non-resident ownership is unrestricted. Step 2: Register the ULC with the CRA through the Combined New Business Registration (Business Registration Online or Form RC1) to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the BC ULC is the archetypal hybrid entity: a non-resident single member may treat it as a disregarded pass-through entity for U.S. federal tax purposes (default check-the-box outcome), while the Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 256.1 deems the same ULC to be a separate corporation taxed at the entity level, thereby preventing branch attribution to the foreign member. The Canadian statutory default is therefore Entity_Level_Taxation via the s. 256.1(2) deemed-corporation rule.

Available election forms

U.S. check-the-box: IRS Form 8832 may elect corporate (association) status for a ULC that otherwise defaults to disregarded status. Canadian s. 256.1(3) joint election: the ULC and its member may jointly elect that the deemed-corporation rule in s. 256.1(2) not apply, causing Canadian branch treatment with deemed-disposition consequences. GST/HST: the s. 156 (Excise Tax Act) closely-related-corporation election allows nil-consideration treatment for supplies between registrant members of a qualifying group. Hybrid mismatch: ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5 restrict deduction / non-inclusion arbitrage arising from the Canada–U.S. entity classification difference.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide revenue from taxable supplies (zero-rated export sales count toward the ceiling) in a single calendar quarter or the preceding four calendar quarters; crossing this small-supplier ceiling makes GST/HST registration mandatory. Note: the legislated threshold is scheduled to rise to CAD 50,000 effective January 1, 2025 and must be verified at the applicable filing date. BC PST: no monetary threshold — any vendor making taxable retail sales delivered to a purchaser in BC must register and collect PST before transacting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: sales of the AI pet robot to non-resident buyers are zero-rated under s. 6 of Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the vendor exports the goods or ships them to a destination outside Canada and the goods are not used in Canada before export; full input tax credit recovery is preserved. Services: associated cloud / speech-synthesis subscription services supplied to non-residents qualify for zero-rating under s. 7 of Part V, Schedule VI, subject to the exclusions for services supplied in connection with real or tangible personal property situated in Canada. BC PST: no PST applies where the vendor ships goods to an out-of-province or foreign destination, provided the delivery point and export evidence are documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) at or before export of the goods.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — carrier-issued document evidencing physical delivery of the AI pet robot units to a destination outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — banking record (SWIFT MT103 or foreign-exchange confirmation) evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment from the non-resident buyer within the repatriation window.

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API transactional validation, or digital stamping regime. GST/HST invoices must contain the prescribed CRA disclosure elements and may be issued electronically; GST/HST returns must be transmitted electronically once gross taxable revenue exceeds CAD 1.5 million; BC PST returns are filed online through PST BC Online. Voluntary B2B invoice interoperability is available through Canada's PEPPOL network; CBSA CARM handles customs account and export declaration processing.

Export zero-rating claims are a priority CRA audit area. Unsubstantiated claims are reassessed at 5% GST with arrears interest, related input tax credits are clawed back, and the gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 285.1 (greater of CAD 100 and 25% of unreported net tax) may apply. Further exposure includes Customs Act misdeclaration penalties (up to CAD 25,000 per export), ITA s. 163.2 civil penalties for false statements (up to CAD 100,000 and 50% of the benefit), and criminal tax-evasion prosecution with fines up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment. CRA may also invoke the general anti-avoidance rule (ETA s. 274 / ITA s. 245) to deny artificial export structuring.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible. Canada maintains no exchange-control legislation: current-account payments (trade, royalties, services) and capital-account flows (equity injection, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation) are equally unrestricted. Profit remittance from the BC ULC is subject solely to Part XIII withholding tax under the federal Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) at the statutory 25% rate, treaty-reduced (typically 5% or 15%) for a US-resident parent claiming benefits under the Canada-US Tax Convention. The ULC must additionally satisfy the solvency test in s. 55 of the British Columbia Business Corporations Act (BCBCA) before any distribution. No FX license, capital-account filing, or pre-remittance approval is required; however, the Investment Canada Act notification thresholds must be monitored to avoid review of acquisitions of control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Bank Act and Currency Act impose no mandatory repatriation window, no surrender ratio, and no forced conversion of export receipts into CAD. Exporters may retain USD/EUR proceeds in domestic or foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. For the ULC this permits Stripe and credit-card settlement flows to accumulate in USD and to be converted only when operational CAD liquidity is required. Foreign exchange gains on such balances are recognized as ordinary income under s. 39(2) of the Income Tax Act and should be revalued quarterly for CRA reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Prudential supervision of banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); the Department of Finance administers the Exchange Fund Account; FINTRAC enforces anti-money-laundering reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium; suitable as a supplementary collection and FX conversion rail, not as the primary treasury counterparty. Canada has no separate EMI license class. Fintech payment firms operate either as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or, since 2024, as registered payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (SC 2021, c. 23, s. 211). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File / Master File mandatory if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million, or cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties exceed CAD 5 million (goods) or CAD 1 million (services/intangible/interest) in the fiscal year; BC has no separate threshold; smaller ULC still requires contemporaneous documentation if transactions are above de minimis limits and are not at arm's length.

Economic substance scope: Canada’s general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under ITA s.245 plus the 'economic substance' concept in treaty abuse rules require that the ULC has real business purpose, staff, premises, and risk-bearing capacity; ULC is an unlimited liability entity but must avoid being characterized as a 'conduit' or 'shell' for transfer pricing adjustments; CRA may apply s.247(2) to recharacterize transactions lacking commercial rationality; ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) of offshore jurisdictions do not apply, but CRA’s jurisprudence applies similar substance-over-form approach for intercompany service charges and royalty payments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Willful Non-Reporting / False Statements)

Statutory sanction: Federal: 50% gross negligence penalty (ITA s.163(2)); criminal prosecution under s.239: fine up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; BC: additional provincial penalty of 50% of federal penalty; interest compounded daily on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate withholdings (Part XIII, GST) and employee source deductions; CRA can assess director personally without wind-up; travel bans under s.238(2) for failure to comply with orders; criminal imprisonment for directors who knowingly participate in evasion.
Transfer Pricing Penalty (Unsupported Intercompany Pricing)

Statutory sanction: 10% of gross adjustment amount (the re-assessed income increase) plus 50% of amount of gross negligence portion of adjustment; interest on reassessed tax; denial of deduction for unreasonable royalties; potential double counting with GAAR.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable if sign false declaration; CRA can assess under s.226.1 for failure to withhold on deemed dividend; seizure of personal assets and passport denial in serious cases.
Shell Company / Conduit Arrangement (GAAR and Treaty Abuse)

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization denying tax benefit, resulting tax reassessed at 25% (or 27% corporate rate) plus 50% penalty under s.163(2) if misrepresentation is grossly negligent; interest from original due date; CRA may also impose Part XIII withholding on recomputed deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors potentially liable for foreign reporting penalties (T1134/T1135) up to CAD 24,000 per year; court may order personal restitution for taxes avoided; travel ban and contempt of court in CRA enforcement.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering (Sale of High-Tech Consumer Product with Payment Rails)

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) up to CAD 500,000 for corporations; criminal fine up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for persons; BC has additional Corporate Transparency Act penalties for failure to maintain register of individuals with significant control.

Director personal liability: Directors can be fined individually up to CAD 250,000 and imprisoned; personal liability for AML compliance obligations where they knowingly fail to report suspicious transactions; seizure of corporate assets and dissolution of ULC.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Failure (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax assessed against payer (ULC) at full 25% statutory rate plus daily compound interest; 10% late-filer penalty and 20% plus 50% for gross negligence if deliberate; administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 10,000 per failure.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for Part XIII amounts withheld or required to be withheld under ITA s.227.1; CRA may issue director’s liability assessment without exhausting corporate collection remedies; travel ban under s.238(2) for failure to pay.
