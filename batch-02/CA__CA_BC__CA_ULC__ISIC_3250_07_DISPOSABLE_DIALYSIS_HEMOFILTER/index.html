Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the BCBCA. A BC ULC may be incorporated with one or more classes of no-par-value shares carrying unlimited shareholder liability. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility without standing exchange-control approval, licensing, or surrender obligations. Profit remittance is constrained only by income tax withholding at source: dividends and other Part XIII payments from the BC ULC to non-resident shareholders are subject to 25% withholding under the Income Tax Act R.S.C., 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.), as territorially reduced by an applicable tax treaty (e.g., Canada-US treaty reduced rates of 15% generally and 5% for a 10% corporate shareholder). No Bank of Canada permit is required for an inbound equity or outbound dividend flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender period or mandatory conversion ratio for Canadian exporters. Exporters may hold any foreign currency indefinitely in a Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency bank account at an authorized institution. Reporting obligations are statistical only: exporters must file Balance of Payments and international merchandise trade surveys under the Statistics Act, and advance cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in one day must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising monetary and exchange-rate authority jointly with Department of Finance Canada; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) charters and supervises federally regulated clearing banks; FINTRAC is the AML/KYC and cross-border wire report administrator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low primary feasibility. Unregulated offshore digital EMI platforms have no legal status under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); they cannot take Canadian deposits or hold Canadian-dollar clearing accounts in their own name unless chartered or registered as an MSB with FINTRAC. An EMI may serve only as an ancillary collection corridor, and only if registered under FINTRAC and subject to OSFI/Payments Canada perimeter requirements; corporate treasury should maintain all liquidity at a tier-1 chartered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a tier-1 Canadian chartered bank's CAD/USD concentration account with SWIFT membership. Inbound equity capital is sent by SWIFT MT103 via GPI with payment purpose coded as 'Capital injection — direct investment equity' (Bank of Canada BoP code F2). The correspondent should transmit a UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference); the Canadian receiving bank will credit the ULC's account through Lynx (the successor high-value settlement system to LVTS) for CAD, or via U.S. correspondent Fedwire/CHIPS for USD. Compliance screening executes against OFAC/EU/Canada consolidated sanctions lists; a FINTRAC electronic fund transfer report must be filed when the wire exceeds CAD 10,000 in a single day. Before the first wire, the bank requires a source-of-funds declaration and authorization of capital call.Preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct TT wire settlements with advance-payment or open-account tenor; for purchase-order-backed transactions from U.S./EU hospital groups, use commercial letters of credit confirmed by the issuing bank, transmitted by SWIFT MT700/MT701/MT707 subject to UCP 600, with CAD clearing through Lynx/Real-Time Rail and USD clearing via the bank's U.S. correspondent through Fedwire/CHIPS. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50,000 aggregate per non-resident non-arm's length party triggers mandatory T106 information return filing for services and other intangibles; CAD 500,000 for goods. Contemporaneous Local File / Master File documentation is recommended where annual related-party transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, GAAR (ITA s.245) denies tax benefits from avoidance transactions lacking genuine economic substance. A BC ULC must demonstrate substantive Canadian business activity (facility, employees, manufacturing risks, decision making) to avoid treaty-shopping under the Canada-US Treaty and OECD Base Erosion rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of the misstatement amount; criminal liability under ITA s.239; fine up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may face asset seizure, travel bans, and civil restitution orders; controlling directors can be personally prosecuted for willful participation in tax evasion, with imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: 10% transfer pricing penalty under ITA s.247(4) applied to the net adjustment, plus interest and reassessment of taxable income for all open tax years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed if they directed mispricing and caused the corporation's insolvency; travel restrictions and prohibition from leaving Canada may be ordered by tax authorities under jeopardy collection.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of all tax benefits under ITA s.245; 25% penalty for misrepresentation under s.163.1; possible 15% penalty for gross negligence in tax reporting; all amounts payable with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who structured the abuse can be held personally liable for the resulting tax if corporate assets are insufficient; asset preservation orders, civil restitution, and denial of business travel may apply.
Part XIII Withholding and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Payer is liable under ITA s.227(8) for the full unremitted withholding amount plus 10% penalty and daily interest. Failure to file information returns attracts CAD 100 per day; AML violations under PCMLTFA carry fines up to CAD 500,000 for corporations and CAD 500,000 for directors.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII source deductions and GST/HST under s.227.1; severe cases can trigger criminal conviction, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years for willful non-compliance.
