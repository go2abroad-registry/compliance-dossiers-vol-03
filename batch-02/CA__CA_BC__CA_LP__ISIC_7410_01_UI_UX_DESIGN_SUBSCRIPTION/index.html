Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A commercial virtual office is permissible for a British Columbia limited partnership only if it is a physical street address in British Columbia at which legal process and notices can be accepted during normal business hours and promptly forwarded to the general partner. Mail-forwarding-only addresses, post office boxes, or addresses outside British Columbia are not permissible. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) — federal gateway: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; British Columbia Ministry of Finance for provincial PST/EHT: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Business Number (BN) and register for the GST/HST account where its taxable supplies made in Canada exceed $30,000 in a four-calendar-quarter period, or earlier if it elects to register. The non-resident LP files Form RC165 (Non-Resident Registration for GST/HST) and Form RC166 (Request for Business Number) or uses the CRA Non-Resident portal. If the LP is not carrying on business in Canada, it may still register voluntarily. The BN serves as the TIN for GST/HST and payroll purposes; the partnership itself is not a Canadian income taxpayer, but files annual Partnership Information Return T5013 (with schedules for non-resident partners) and must report allocations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not employ check-the-box entity classification elections. Limited partnerships are statutorily fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes. Available elections include: subsection 216(1) non-resident elected return for Canadian-source rental income; subsection 249.1(6) partnership fiscal-period election; section 95(2.6) or related hybrid-mismatch allocation elections under the OECD BEPS rules; and subsections 603(1)/(2) of the Income Tax Regulations for elective deemed dispositions at fair market value in limited partnership rollovers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD of worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada (excluding zero-rated supplies) over any four consecutive calendar quarters

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) when the vendor ships or delivers the goods to a location outside Canada in accordance with the customs and export evidence requirements. Services: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part VII when the supply is to a non-resident recipient who is not registered under Part IX of the ETA, and the service is performed for a person located outside Canada, or the supply relates to real/external goods located outside Canada. For digital design subscription services, general rule requires supply to a non-resident recipient and no delivery/performance in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration, Form B13A or electronic CERS declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank remittance evidence (where required by CRA audit practice for proof of proceeds)

Canada has no federal mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime. GST/HST registrants are required to file returns electronically under the ETA if annual taxable supplies exceed $1.5 million CAD or as a non-resident in certain cases; otherwise paper filing is available. New GST/HST registration is executed online via the Business Registration Online service. No API clearance is mandated by CRA.

CRA may reassess GST/HST returns within 4 years from the original assessment date; the limitation is extended indefinitely where a misrepresentation has been made fraudulently or with gross negligence. False export zero-rating claims attract a civil penalty under ETA s.280(1)(a) of the greater of $250 and 25% of the net tax understated, plus arrears interest. Intentional evasion is a criminal offence under ETA s.327, punishable by a fine of 50%–200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to two years. Records substantiating the zero-rated export must be retained for six years; failure to maintain records gives CRA the right to disallow the zero-rating and recover the full GST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII (accepted 1951); no prior approval, central bank permit, or Canadian funds authorization is required for capital injections, dividend/distribution remittances, or service trade payments. CAD is freely floating with a 1-3% inflation-target band. Profit remittances from a BC limited partnership to non-resident limited partners are permitted without exchange approval; Part XIII withholding applies only to passive Canadian-source payments (e.g., interest, royalties, rents) at 25% (or reduced treaty rate, e.g., 15% royalty / 10% interest under the Canada-US treaty), while allocated active business income is reported through a CRA T5013 partnership return with no Part XIII withholding. No capital controls on CAD holdings, no requirement to repatriate FX, and no restriction on maintaining foreign-currency accounts in Canada or abroad.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, or statutory timeline for export service receipts. Subscription revenues (deemed export of design services under ISIC 7410/NAICS 541430) may be held in USD or any currency; FINTRAC large-transaction reporting applies to cash/EFT movements of CAD 10,000 or more, and CBSA requires declaration of physical cross-border currency of CAD 10,000+.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the central monetary authority issuing and managing the Canadian dollar; the Department of Finance Canada retains statutory FX policy authority, OSFI supervises prudential FX risk of banks, and FINTRAC administers cross-border currency movement reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no exchange-control commission; the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act establish the free-floating CAD regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a supplemental settlement rail only. Wise Payments Canada Corp. (FINTRAC-registered money services business, and subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime administered by the Bank of Canada) legally provides local CAD/USD account-like balances and low-value cross-border payments, but is not a Schedule I/II bank, is not CDIC-insured, extends no credit, and cannot hold the LP's primary liquidity for treasury-grade clearing. Wise should be used strictly as the merchant-of-record payout feeder for Stripe Billing settlements below the bank's SWIFT wire threshold; the Tier-1 bank account remains the sole concentration and disbursement account. Legal constraints: no deposit-taking license under the Bank Act; e-money balances are unsecured contractual claims; FINTRAC/RPAA registration obligations apply to Wise, not to the LP, provided the LP transacts only for its own subscription receipts.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Certified BC Certificate of Limited Partnership (extract dated within 30 days); executed Limited Partnership Agreement naming the GP and limited partner liability schedule; current beneficial-ownership register; passport/government ID and proof of address for every 25%+ owner; IRS Forms W-8IMY (partnership), W-8BEN-E (if applicable) or W-9 for US persons; completed CRS self-certification; source-of-funds statement for capital contributions; declaration that limited partners take no part in management.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A British Columbia limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and is not itself subject to corporate income tax. For a taxable Canadian corporation, the combined general corporate income tax rate is 27%: 15% federal and 12% British Columbia. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation may access an approximately 11% combined small-business rate: 9% federal and 2% British Columbia, subject to the federal and provincial business limits, associated-corporation rules, and active-business-income requirements.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident payer to a non-resident, subject to a lower treaty rate. Common treaty outcomes include 5% where the recipient is a qualifying corporate shareholder meeting ownership requirements and 15% in other qualifying cases. The Canada-United States treaty commonly provides 5% for qualifying corporate ownership and 15% otherwise.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not impose a universal monetary threshold below which contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is unnecessary. Income Tax Act section 247 requires reasonable efforts to use arm's-length terms and maintain contemporaneous documentation for relevant non-arm's-length cross-border transactions. Form T106 commonly applies at an aggregate reporting threshold of CAD 1,000,000, but that threshold does not eliminate the underlying arm's-length requirement. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational enterprise groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Canada generally has no broad mandatory Master File requirement equivalent to all OECD jurisdictions; additional information may nevertheless be required by CRA.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone regime formally titled Economic Substance Regulations. Substance is evaluated through the common-law sham doctrine, beneficial-ownership principles, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, specific anti-avoidance provisions, treaty limitation and purpose provisions where applicable, transfer-pricing rules, and permanent-establishment analysis. CRA may disregard arrangements lacking commercial purpose, personnel, functions, risks, decision-making, or documentary support. A limited partnership should maintain evidence of genuine design operations, customer contracts, personnel or contractors, management decisions, IP ownership or licensed rights, service delivery, pricing, and banking activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and deliberate misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment, full tax recovery, interest, gross-negligence penalties of up to 50% of understated tax, and criminal penalties under the Income Tax Act. Conviction may result in a fine ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years for indictable offences.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face criminal liability for personal participation, authorization, acquiescence, or wilful blindness. A corporation or partnership structure does not shield an individual from personal criminal liability. There is no automatic tax-debt travel ban, but court orders, bail conditions, immigration consequences, or criminal proceedings may restrict travel.
Failure to file partnership or corporate returns

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties, information-return penalties, interest, denial or restriction of certain claims, reassessment using available CRA information, and possible prosecution for persistent non-compliance. A required T5013 may attract CAD 25 per day, generally subject to a CAD 100 minimum and CAD 2,500 maximum.

Director personal liability: Directors are generally not personally liable merely because a corporation filed late, but may be liable where they authorized or participated in the offence. General partners remain personally exposed for partnership obligations; limited partners may lose practical liability protection if they exercise management functions beyond permitted limits or act as the effective general partner.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under Income Tax Act section 247, deny or recharacterize deductions, assess interest, and impose a transfer-pricing penalty generally equal to 10% of the adjustment where statutory conditions are met and reasonable efforts were not made.

Director personal liability: Individuals can face director or officer liability for knowingly participating in false records, fraud, or evasion. Ordinary commercial disagreement over pricing does not automatically create personal liability, but deliberate concealment or sham documentation can support civil and criminal proceedings.
Improper treaty relief and withholding failure

Statutory sanction: The Canadian payer may be assessed for the unremitted withholding, applicable interest, penalties, and information-return failures. Contractual gross-up obligations may increase the economic cost. Anti-avoidance and beneficial-ownership principles may deny treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unremitted source deductions and withholding under the director-liability provisions where statutory conditions are met and due diligence is unavailable. Personal liability can survive corporate dissolution or insolvency.
Payroll and source-deduction trust liability

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unremitted deductions, employer contributions, interest, penalties, payroll audits, and possible prosecution. Directors may be assessed personally for corporate source deductions under the Income Tax Act, Canada Pension Plan Act, and Employment Insurance Act.

Director personal liability: Personal director liability is a principal risk. A director may avoid liability only where the statutory due-diligence defence is established. Resignation does not automatically eliminate liability for amounts arising during the directorship.
GST/HST non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unremitted GST/HST, interest, input-tax-credit denial, filing penalties, gross-negligence penalties, and prosecution. Late GST/HST filing penalties generally include 1% of the amount owing plus 25% of that 1% for each complete month late, subject to statutory limits.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for unremitted GST/HST where the statutory director-liability provisions apply. General partners are exposed for partnership liabilities according to provincial partnership law and the partnership agreement.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny deductions, reallocate income, find a Canadian or foreign permanent establishment, apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, deny treaty benefits, impose withholding, and assess interest and penalties. Corporate registration alone does not establish tax residence or commercial substance.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct sham arrangements may face civil restitution, tax penalties, prosecution, and asset-recovery proceedings. No automatic veil piercing follows from low substance alone, but fraud, sham conduct, wrongful trading, or statutory breaches can create personal exposure.
AML, proceeds of crime, and sanctions exposure

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties, compliance orders, reporting restrictions, account termination, asset forfeiture, and criminal prosecution under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, Criminal Code, and sanctions legislation. Criminal laundering offences can carry substantial fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, compliance officers, and actual controllers may be prosecuted personally. Conviction can result in imprisonment, forfeiture, restitution, and court-imposed travel restrictions; there is no automatic travel ban solely from an ordinary FINTRAC administrative penalty.
Improper limited-partner conduct and corporate veil abuse

Statutory sanction: Potential loss or expansion of limited-liability protection under applicable British Columbia partnership law, civil damages, fraudulent-conveyance remedies, accounting orders, insolvency claims, and tax reassessment.

Director personal liability: The general partner is ordinarily personally liable for partnership obligations. A limited partner may incur personal exposure if it participates in management in a manner that defeats statutory protection, gives personal guarantees, commits torts, or engages in fraud. Courts may impose restitution and asset-recovery remedies; imprisonment generally requires a separate criminal offence.
