Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (For Canadian federal and provincial income tax purposes the ULC is a Canadian-resident corporation subject to entity-level tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act; however, the ULC is eligible for US check-the-box treatment as a disregarded entity. For GST/HST purposes, the ULC is a distinct registrant with no look-through to shareholders; the 'Pass_Through_Eligible' classification is available only as a cross-border US tax planning attribute.)

Available election forms

1. US check-the-box election under Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3 (Form 8832) to treat the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity for US federal tax purposes, preserving the intended cross-border pass-through structure. 2. Joint election under subsection 273 of the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST on supplies between closely related corporations, or the joint election for nil consideration, to neutralize GST/HST on intercompany transfers of components and finished lenses. 3. Election under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act for any net rental income from Canadian real property, if applicable. 4. Hybrid mismatch defensive elections under sections 18.4–18.6 of the Income Tax Act to realign deductions and avoid double-counting on hybrid financing arrangements of the ULC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 (GST/HST small-supplier threshold under subsection 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act; registration becomes mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ULC's supply of intraocular acrylic lenses is zero-rated under section 1 of Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act when the goods are exported by the supplier within 60 days of the supply and the supplier maintains evidence of export. Contract manufacturing and lathe-turning services performed on tangible personal property for export are zero-rated under section 7 of Schedule VI. BC PST does not apply to goods sold for export delivery outside British Columbia, provided the vendor retains the prescribed evidence. Zero-rating is denied if the goods are not exported within 60 days or if the documentation continuity is broken.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (or electronic CADEX/ACROSS export reporting), including the HS 9001.30 statistical code for intraocular lenses
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing a foreign consignee, port of exit, and date of export
  • Commercial Invoice in English or French stating HS Code 9001.30, unit value, quantity, and terms of sale (Incoterms)
  • Foreign payment receipt, comprising SWIFT wire, letter of credit, or bank remittance advice, substantiating the inward foreign exchange flow
  • Freight forwarder's certificate of export or third-party logistics proof of delivery to the non-resident purchaser

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping mandate equivalent to CFDI or SDI. Registered suppliers must nevertheless issue invoices containing the supplier's name and BN, the purchaser's name, the date of supply, the total consideration, and a description of goods/services, as required by section 223 of the Excise Tax Act. Electronic records must be retained in a readable and processable format under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act; CRA accepts PEPPOL BIS / EDI transmission for B2B e-invoicing and conducts e-audit extraction through GST/HST NETFILE and EDI channels.

CRA may reassess any zero-rated export lacking substantiation, claw back full input tax credits previously allowed, and assess uncollected GST plus compound daily interest. Penalties for false statements or omissions under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act include the greater of $250 and 25% of the net tax misstatement, escalating to a gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; wilful evasion is a criminal offense under section 327, punishable by unlimited fines and imprisonment for up to five years. BC PST audits may impose a 10% penalty on unreported tax plus 1% monthly interest, with separate penalties for failure to register or file under the PST Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible current account regime with no capital controls on routine trade and profit remittances. Capital account transactions involving non-resident shareholders require FINTRAC and OSFI review for anti-money laundering and national security compliance. Profit remittances to foreign owners are permitted under NAFTA and bilateral investment treaties but must be supported by audited financial statements and shareholder resolutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian exporters must repatriate foreign exchange proceeds to authorized dealers within 90 days of receipt. Mandatory surrender occurs at prevailing market rates with no forced conversion ratios. All transactions require export documentation and are subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds. Non-compliance may trigger penalties under the Proceeds of Crime Act and potential license suspension.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for offshore digital EMIs due to strict FINTRAC AML requirements and regulatory oversight by OSFI. Canadian fintech platforms offer limited cross-border services but require full KYC and are unsuitable for high-volume trade settlements or medical device disbursements.
