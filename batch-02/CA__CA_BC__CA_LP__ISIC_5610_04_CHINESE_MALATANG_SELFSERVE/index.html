Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a British Columbia limited partnership; the limited partnership agreement must specify the capital contribution of each limited partner, which may be cash or property of any lawful value, and no escrow account is required by the Registrar, although capital contributions should be documented by bank or wire records for limited-liability protection.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local fire department (including fire suppression hood inspection for the broth station), structural load inspection of the premises floor for the self-serve buffet line and broth kettles, WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety clearance for kitchen operations, and a municipal building department Certificate of Occupancy for the premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a British Columbia limited partnership; the limited partnership agreement must specify the capital contribution of each limited partner, which may be cash or property of any lawful value, and no escrow account is required by the Registrar, although capital contributions should be documented by bank or wire records for limited-liability protection.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – My Business Account portal, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity establishing or carrying on business in British Columbia must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and, if making taxable supplies exceeding the small supplier threshold or otherwise required/electing, a GST/HST account number. For partnerships, apply to CRA using Form RC2 'GST/HST Application for Non-Resident' and obtain a BN through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or by mailing Form RC1; provincial PST registration for BC sales is obtained separately through the British Columbia Ministry of Finance eTaxBC portal. The non-resident must provide legal name, partnership agreement excerpt, foreign tax identification documentation, Canadian business address or agent information, and anticipated Canadian taxable supply amounts.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident foreign entity establishing or carrying on business in British Columbia must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and, if making taxable supplies exceeding the small supplier threshold or otherwise required/electing, a GST/HST account number. For partnerships, apply to CRA using Form RC2 'GST/HST Application for Non-Resident' and obtain a BN through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or by mailing Form RC1; provincial PST registration for BC sales is obtained separately through the British Columbia Ministry of Finance eTaxBC portal. The non-resident must provide legal name, partnership agreement excerpt, foreign tax identification documentation, Canadian business address or agent information, and anticipated Canadian taxable supply amounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian law does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' entity election; a British Columbia limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax and is generally not itself the taxpayer. However, for GST/HST purposes, the partnership may elect to be treated as a person and apply for registration as a selected partnership under Division V of the Excise Tax Act. For partnership tax rollovers, a s. 97(2) income tax election may be filed to transfer property into the partnership on a tax-deferred basis. Also available are partnership fiscal-period elections and CRA-permitted elections under the Excise Tax Act for closely related persons and supplies via an agent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable revenues over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (excluding zero-rated exports and financial services) before mandatory GST/HST registration as a non-small-supplier; BC PST registration is required when taxable sales exceed CAD 10,000 in a calendar quarter or immediately for certain taxable activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada where a supplier obtains documentary evidence that the goods are exported for delivery outside Canada as part of continuous shipment, including goods sold to a non-resident for subsequent export and certain services supplied to non-residents where the services are performed wholly outside Canada (or relate directly to exported goods). For a fast-casual food franchise/royalty structure, royalty and intellectual property supplies to non-resident franchisees are generally zero-rated when they are made to a non-resident and are not used in Canada; food/beverage supplies served on premises in BC remain taxable at GST 5% plus PST where applicable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency form B3-3 or authorized e-manifest/export reporting record)
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list identifying goods, quantities, values, and HS classification
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill indicating place of delivery outside Canada and foreign buyer
  • Proof of export, such as carrier certificate, customs stamp, border crossing report, or foreign customs entry document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign bank wire/ MT103 / banking statement conclusively linking payments received to the export transaction
  • Executed export sales agreement or purchase order with incoterms and destination clauses

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Non-resident registrants must issue a valid invoice showing the BN, date, description, GST paid/payable (if applicable), and tax rate, and must retain books and records in Canada or be subject to approved CRA record-retrieval arrangements. British Columbia PST requires separate registration via eTaxBC, with monthly/quarterly remittance and compliance under the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act; no live government digital clearance of invoices is applicable.

CRA can reassess and claw back zero-rated treatment with interest and penalties where export documentation is deficient within statutory assessment periods. Fraudulent or negligent export zero-rating claims attract a civil penalty of not less than 25% of the amount claimed (Excise Tax Act s. 285.1 or 280), gross negligence penalties, daily compounding interest, and possible criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Export permits can be cancelled and the taxpayer’s clearance history red-flagged; directors can face vicarious liability for partnership GST/HST deficiencies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada fully subscribes to IMF Article VIII and imposes no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible; CAD/CNY trades are transacted at market rates through Canadian Authorized Foreign Exchange Providers. Profit and royalty remittances to non-resident partners are subject only to applicable withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Part XIII) and any treaty relief under the Canada-China Tax Convention. Practical hurdles arise from AML/KYC information requirements and sanctions screening, not from currency convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratio applies under Canadian law. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. For the People's Republic of China counterparties, SAFE rules govern outbound investment and remittance documentation, but Canada imposes no equivalent requirement on export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange oversight coordinated with the Minister of Finance, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking, and Global Affairs Canada for sanctions administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut Business) can supplement operational clearing and FX conversion but cannot fully replace a Schedule I Canadian bank for capital escrow, letter-of-credit issuance, or regulatory reporting. Any provider must be registered as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act if serving Canadian clients. Due-diligence and liability limits make EMIs suitable for low-value float and trade payments, not for core fiduciary treasury functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound CAD capital contributions from China should be wired via SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code consistent with foreign direct investment (code QD) or capital input (code JN), and routed through a Chinese bank that is a direct settlement participant in CIPS or via CNH clearing through Bank of China (Canada). The BC LP must provide its authorized Canadian Schedule I bank account details in advance. All wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC; advanced notifications include bank account statements, board resolution authorizing the contribution, and expected-source-of-funds declaration.Royalty and franchise fee payments to the Chinese brand owner are cleared via SWIFT MT103 with country payment code 'Royalty' (GDP trade category 2.2), attached to a signed royalty declaration and invoice.
