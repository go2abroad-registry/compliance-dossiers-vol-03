Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (for U.S. check-the-box purposes; a BC/AB ULC is eligible to be treated as a disregarded entity). For Canadian Income Tax Act purposes, the ULC is a separate taxable corporation subject to Part I entity-level tax; for BC PST and GST, it is a taxable supplier.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election under the Income Tax Act. A ULC may file a U.S. Form 8832 to elect disregarded-entity status for U.S. federal tax. For Canadian tax, no election is available to classify the ULC as a partnership; the ULC is taxable at entity level. GST/HST registrants may use the simplified quarterly filing election if under threshold, and may elect the quick method where eligible. BC PST has no election to defer registration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies (non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada); BC PST: no statutory threshold, registration is mandatory before making taxable sales in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V if the exporter has proof of export and tangible personal property is delivered outside Canada. Wholesale fertilizer (urea/potash) exported by ULC is zero-rated if exported by the supplier, delivered to an overseas buyer, and supported by documentary evidence. Offshore services to non-residents may also be zero-rated if effective use is outside Canada and any supply is made to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) written export declaration (B13/A) when required
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill showing export from Canada to foreign port
  • Commercial invoice describing product, HS codes (3102/3104), quantities and value
  • Packing list and carrier contract
  • Proof of payment / inward remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103, bank confirmation, or letter of credit settlement record)
  • GST/HST export documentation retained in books and records per ETA section 169.1

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping under federal GST/HST or BC PST. CRA requires GST/HST registrants to file returns electronically when eligible (NETFILE) and to preserve all books and records, including electronic records, for six years; e-invoicing is voluntary unless CRA issues a formal requirement. BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC.

CRA audits export substantiation strictly. Missing proof of export reverses zero-rating and triggers GST/PST remittance plus interest. Penalties under Excise Tax Act s. 280-289 include 5% penalty plus 1% monthly interest on unreported amounts, 25% gross-negligence penalty, and up to CAD 25,000 fine and/or imprisonment for making false statements; directors may be liable for unremitted GST; retrospective clawbacks apply for the full limitation period (4 years, longer for misrepresentation).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized foreign exchange regime with unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital accounts. Profit remittances, dividends, and interest payments are permitted without prior approval or surrender requirements, subject only to standard beneficial ownership verification, tax reporting to the Canada Revenue Agency, and anti-money laundering compliance under FINTRAC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is required under Canadian foreign exchange regulations. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in full. However, all export transactions must be reported to FINTRAC for AML purposes, and proceeds are subject to GST/HST collection and remittance obligations where applicable. Commercial invoices and contracts must be retained for audit and tax verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions and fintech payment platforms face strict legal constraints in Canada. Operations require licensing as a money services business under the Proceeds of Crime Act or formal partnership with a federally regulated Schedule I bank. Unlicensed cross-border EMI activities are prohibited and expose the entity to significant AML, sanctions, and consumer protection violations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal-provincial general corporate income tax rate for British Columbia; composed of 15% federal general rate plus 12% BC provincial general rate, before any applicable abatements and refundable tax adjustments. The federal general rate reduction yields 15% (38% less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general reduction). BC general corporate income tax rate is 12%. Total: 27%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid by a corporation resident in Canada to a non-resident shareholder, subject to reduction under the Canada-BC treaty or applicable DTA. For a ULC, dividends include all distributions of profits; ULC's unlimited liability status does not alter the dividend withholding characterization.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; no provision for extension. Balance of tax payable due two months after fiscal year-end (three months for certain Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) with eligible business limits), regardless of filing date; interest and penalties accrue on late payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File must be prepared if aggregate gross revenues of all transacting cross-border non-arm's length parties exceed CAD 10 million, or gross assets of the taxpayer and related parties exceed CAD 10 million in the fiscal year. Reporting in CAD and transfer pricing documentation must be contemporaneous, prepared no later than the corporation's income tax filing due date (six months after fiscal year-end), and provided to CRA within three months of a request; failure to produce documentation within the prescribed period triggers a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment, minimum CAD 5,000, no maximum, plus erosion of balance-of-evidence and reverse onus.

Economic substance scope: Applicability of Economic Substance Regulations (ESR): Canada does not implement a formal OECD-style Economic Substance Regulation for wholly domestic IP/commercial structures, but the doctrine applies through (1) CRA GAAR (General Anti-Avoidance Rule, Income Tax Act s.245) requiring genuine economic substance and business purpose for any avoidance transaction; (2) Transfer pricing substance and risk allocation requirements under s.247(2.1)-(2.4), requiring that actual risks assumed by the tested party are supported by real control over risk and financial capacity, and that contract terms reflect economic reality; (3) the BC ULC may be subject to US-style check-the-box or CFC reclassification if the ULC is owned by a US parent; no separate BC ESR filing is required unless the ULC holds BC mineral or marijuana licenses, which do not apply. For wholesale supply of urea and potash, ESR relevance is low unless the ULC is a conduit in a back-to-back invoicing structure where the CRA will disregard it under preamble to s.247.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Aggressive Transfer Pricing / Duty of Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: CRA assessment under s.247(2) adjusting gross revenue to arm's length price; net adjustment penalty of 10% of the upward adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000); interest on reassessment plus compounded daily prescribed-rate interest; if the adjustment exceeds CAD 1 million and the transaction is misdocumented, penalty of 10% of the adjustment; CRA also derecognizes intentional mispricing and disallows deductions

Director personal liability: Directors of the ULC may be liable under the Income Tax Act s.227.1 for unpaid corporate withholding tax (payroll/service fees to non-residents) if not remitted; for ordinary corporate tax, directors are not strictly liable but may be liable for negligence under the BC Business Corporations Act s.147 for authorizing transfer pricing misstatements; CRA may obtain civil recovery orders and, in tax evasion cases, personal fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act s.239; travel bans under the Government of Canada/BC Tax Enforcement Program apply where a director is investigated for cross-border evasion.
Thin Capitalization / Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses

Statutory sanction: Interest deductions disallowed under s.18(4) for the excess of the 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio; under new EIFEL (excessive interest and financing expenses limitation) for corporations with net interest expense exceeding CAD 250,000, denial of interest deductions above the 30% EBITDA threshold (effective for taxation years beginning on or after October 1, 2023); 10% misstatement penalty if a reasonable taxpayer would not have treated the interest as deductible; recharacterization of interest as dividends (25% withholding) when the debt is not arm's length.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally for unpaid corporate income tax arrears only if they failed to withhold on interest paid to non-resident shareholders (via Form NR4) or in prescribed cases under the Canada Business Corporations Act (or BCBCA) for wrongful indemnification; civil fraud or negligence claims by CRA are available; travel ban possible when large evasion escalates to criminal s.239(1)(d), plus prison up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency / AML Red Flag

Statutory sanction: CRA may ignore the ULC as a conduit under GAAR and allocate income to the parent; under the federal Income Tax Act s.238, failure to provide information returns (e.g., T106) attracts penalties; under the BC Business Corporations Act, a company that is not carrying on business with the intention to derive income may be struck from the register, for an unlimited liability company its assets and obligations fall to shareholders; if transactions involve a sanctioned entity, FINTRAC/OSFI referrals and OFAC sanctions blocking assets and fines of up to CAD 1 million or 5 years imprisonment under the Canada Criminal Code and Special Economic Measures Act.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell ULC are personally liable for T2 penalties if the company fails to remit after assessment; under the BC Business Corporations Act, unlimited liability means shareholders (not just directors) are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's debts, including taxes, to the extent of the corporation's deficiency; criminal AML exposure: directors who knowingly direct fraudulent invoicing may be prosecuted under Criminal Code s.380 (fraud) with imprisonment up to 14 years and personal fines; CRA may request Federal Court Mareva injunctions and travel bans where substantive profits are being moved offshore.
Failure to Report Foreign Affiliate or Treaty Claim Made in Error

Statutory sanction: T1134 failure penalties: CAD 25 per day for up to 100 days (CAD 2,500), then CAD 25 per day for each additional 100 days without maximum; subject to additional penalty of 10% of any understated tax due to non-reporting; s.162(10) and s.162(10.1); CRA may also impose a two-year reassessment bar for the foreign affiliate if no report was filed, and interest/penalties accrue on omitted income.

Director personal liability: Directors who have authorized the ownership of a foreign affiliate without filing T1134 are jointly liable for the penalty in the absence of reasonable care; for deliberate misrepresentation, director liability under s.239(1) for tax evasion and false statements extends to a fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years.
