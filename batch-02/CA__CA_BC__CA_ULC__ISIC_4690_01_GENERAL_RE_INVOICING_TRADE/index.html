Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporate Registry of British Columbia, operated as BC Registry Services by the Land Title and Survey Authority of British Columbia (LTSA) on behalf of the Province of British Columbia.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (British Columbia), SBC 2002, c. 57, ss. 1, 10, 51-52, 55, 97; Business Corporations Regulation, B.C. A BC Unlimited Liability Company is incorporated under the same Act with Articles expressly providing that shareholders' liability is unlimited.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 - Incorporation Application and Form 2 - Notice of Articles, e-filed through the BC Corporate Online portal; Certificate of Incorporation issued electronically upon acceptance.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a BC ULC; shares may be issued for such consideration as the directors determine, including cash, services, debt or property. No escrow account or capital trust deposit is required at formation. Issued share consideration must be documented in the share register and by officers' certificates to support subsequent CRA and tax classification review.

Funding & unlock path: Commercial office occupancy: BC Building Code fire safety clearance and local fire marshal inspection are required before occupancy. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - the national tax administrator for federal income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and customs excise. Provincial counterpart for British Columbia: BC Ministry of Finance (BC PST and Employer Health Tax). Digital access is via the CRA My Business Account (MyBA) portal and the GST/HST NETFILE platform; BC corporate registration is executed through BC Registry Corporate Online.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the ULC under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act via BC Registry Corporate Online, obtaining the 9-digit incorporation number. (2) Submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) to the CRA, or apply online through MyBA, to obtain the 9-digit statutory Business Number (BN). (3) Activate the GST/HST program account (BN with suffix RT0001) and, where applicable, the customs and export reporting account (suffix RM0001) for B13A/CAED filing. (4) Register for BC PST separately with the BC Ministry of Finance only if taxable in-province sales are made; a pure export re-invoicing hub with no BC delivery point holds no PST nexus. (5) For non-resident control, provide a Canadian service address and an authorized agent for CRA correspondence; no minimum capital is required and 100% non-resident ownership of the ULC is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For Canadian domestic statutory purposes the BC ULC is a legal person subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (federal Part I) and the BC Income Tax Act (provincial Part 4), and is a registrant under the Excise Tax Act for GST/HST. However, the ULC is eligible for flow-through classification at the foreign owner level: under US check-the-box rules (Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3) a wholly foreign-owned ULC is by default disregarded, or may be affirmatively elected as a partnership/pass-through entity, with Canadian taxable attributes flowing to the US owner. This hybrid status engages BEPS Action 2 and the Canada-US Treaty limitation-on-benefits and transparent-entity provisions. Territorial scope: the ULC as a Canadian resident is taxed on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Canadian-source distributions and specified income under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a statutory 'check-the-box' election; the relevant mechanisms include: (1) US Form 8832 entity classification election whereby the BC ULC is treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax purposes, creating the hybrid used in re-invoicing structures; (2) Election under Excise Tax Act s. 156 to deem supplies between closely related registrants to be made for nominal consideration (intercompany margin simplification); (3) Election under Income Tax Act s. 216 for a non-resident to compute Part I tax on net Canadian rental income (inapplicable to trading operations but structurally available); (4) BC PST direct-pay permit election permitting self-assessment of PST on goods destined for export; (5) Treaty elections under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (including the LOB 'derivative benefits' or 'active trade or business' elections) to secure reduced withholding on cross-border flows.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s. 148(1); a domestic or non-resident person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the aggregate over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters, computed on a related-group basis. BC PST registration is technically required from the first taxable sale in BC with no general monetary threshold, but a re-invoicing hub that transships or delivers goods outside BC creates no PST liability or filing nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, a BC ULC may claim the 0% GST export rate when (i) goods are supplied in Canada and are exported as part of the supply arrangement, (ii) the goods are exported as soon as practicable and no later than 60 days after delivery (unless a customs-authorized extension applies), (iii) the goods have not been used or consumed in Canada before export, and (iv) the supplier retains documentation proving the goods left Canada. Qualified services are zero-rated when performed wholly or mainly outside Canada, or performed directly in respect of exported movable property. For a re-invoicing hub, the ULC must take title and risk as principal (not agent) in an uninterrupted chain from the Canadian vendor to the offshore buyer; where goods move directly from the foreign supplier to the foreign buyer without entering Canada, the transaction falls outside the GST net and must not be denominated a Canadian export.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration - Form B13A (or Automated Export Declaration/CAED filed via the CBSA CARM portal) certified by the Canada Border Services Agency, showing the ULC as exporter
  • Bill of Lading (through transport or combined document) evidencing delivery of the goods to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - SWIFT MT103 or bank exchange-control receipt evidencing foreign currency proceeds received by the ULC from the offshore buyer
  • Commercial invoice and matching purchase orders establishing the re-invoicing chain (upstream Canadian vendor invoice to ULC; ULC re-invoice to offshore buyer)
  • Cargo control document / packing list with delivery-slip date proving export within the prescribed 60-day window

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-authority clearance, or digital stamping regime. GST/HST-registered suppliers must issue invoices conforming to the Excise Tax Act (s. 221) and retain full audit trails. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory through the CRA GST/HST NETFILE portal for all registrants above the CRA electronic-filing threshold, and the CRA recommends e-filing for all. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is available voluntarily for B2B interoperability, but there is no mandatory platform. Records must be retained for at least six years and be prepared in Canadian dollars.

CRA may reassess GST/HST and income tax within four years from the date of the original assessment, and may reassess without time limit where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. If the ULC claims export zero-rating without the documentary bundle, CRA will reverse the zero-rating, assess 5% GST on the gross re-invoicing consideration, and apply daily compound arrears interest. A gross negligence penalty for false statements or omissions is the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax under Income Tax Act s. 163(2); the Excise Tax Act imposes an additional gross negligence penalty of 5% of the net tax under s. 285.1, while knowingly creating a false export certificate attracts the s. 285 penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 5% of the tax sought to be concealed. Criminal exposure under ITA s. 239 and ETA s. 327 carries fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and up to five years imprisonment, and a fraudulent export-claim finding will also invalidate the foreign owner's check-the-box pass-through comfort for prior open years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Complete free convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Canada imposes no exchange controls on capital or current account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, fees, and capital repatriation from a British Columbia ULC are freely transferable abroad without central bank approval. Tax-based hurdles remain in the form of Part XIII withholding tax, the deemed-dividend rules under section 86.1/84 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), and Special Rules for ULCs under subsection 89(1) including absorbed cost base adjustments for shareholder basis on corporate distributions. Foreign Exchange control regulations are limited to Canadian sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act, which may freeze or prohibit transactions with sanctioned jurisdictions. For ULC re-invoicing platforms, fund flows must be backed by real commercial transactions to satisfy tax treaty non-resident claims and avoid GAAR re-characterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not mandate surrender, conversion, or liquidation of export proceeds. Export receipts may be held in any freely convertible currency onshore or offshore. The ULC may retain foreign currency balances for working capital; however, for financial reporting under IFRS and for purposes of the CRA, monetary foreign currency gains/losses must be booked on a functional currency basis. In BC, no provincial statute imposes conversion deadlines or ratios. To be audit-ready, companies should maintain export invoice/contract files, proof of shipment, and bank-inbound remittance narratives.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; Foreign Exchange Administration: none dedicated; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises AML/KYC reporting; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally licensed banks and maintains prudential oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada has no European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing but does have a federal money services business (MSB) registration regime and the Retail Payment Activities Act. A foreign offshore digital EMI is not permitted to take deposits or operate as a deposit-taking institution in BC unless it becomes an authorized foreign bank under the Bank Act. MSB-based fintech rails may be used for remittances and settlement, but they are not FDIC-equivalent deposit insurers and may expose trade balances to uninsured principal risk. Viability for ULC re-invoicing is therefore rated Medium only for transaction-oriented non-deposit flows; banks remain the high-grade treasury backbone. In all cases, the EMI or MSB must register with FINTRAC, maintain Canadian sanctions screening, and use segregated trust accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Standard inbound path is SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the foreign shareholder bank to the BC ULC operational or escrow account. As a Directive under the Bank Act, banks cannot require CAD conversion; funds may settle in CAD/USD. Use MT103 beneficiary name matching the registered BC ULC, full BIC and account/transit number. Canada does not assign a mandatory payment-purpose code like an emerging-market FX code, but the underlying remittance narrative must clearly state 'Capital Contribution – No Taxable Distribution' or 'Intercompany Loan – Non-Resident Lending', and supporting corporate resolution must be pre-uploaded to the bank. For amounts above C$100,000, related-party information reporting may apply under section 233.3 of the ITA (Form T106). The bank will verify that no separate foreign disclosure or custody rule is breached; all inbound capital should be structured as paid-up capital to avoid re-characterized dividends and Part XIII withholding. Treasury should send a pre-notification with the irrevocable funding notice, confirm source of foreign currency, and secure an inward remittance certificate from the receiving bank for subsequent external audit and transfer-pricing SMEAR file.Primary clearing rails for trade settlements are: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfers for operational supplier payments; (ii) MT700/MT707 Commercial Letters of Credit for documentary trade payments to Asian/Pacific suppliers; (iii) MT202 COVER for bank-to-bank correspondent funding; and (iv) Canadian Payments Association (CPA) Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) wire transfers for CAD-denominated settlements. For BC re-invoicing operations, the standard workflow is to receive dollars from buyer, then instruct a MT103 to the final foreign supplier after reconciliation, ensuring invoice narrative contains the underlying commercial invoice number and Incoterms. All outbound wires must be screened against the Canadian sanctions list and the United Nations Security Council sanctions; error/mismatch in beneficiary name or purpose narrative is the most frequent cause of rejection. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal (15%) and British Columbia (12%) general corporate income tax rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 annual gross value of related-party cross-border transactions triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and prudent Local File documentation; Master File and Country-by-Country Report required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above €750 million.

Economic substance scope: No formal ESR regime applies in Canada. CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), residence analysis based on central management and control, and the arm's length principle under ITA s.247. A BC ULC used as a pure re-invoicing conduit with no employees, premises, risk assumption, or substantive control over its core functions may be disregarded as a sham or subject to recharacterization, recapture of deductions, and double tax adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax under ITA 163(2); criminal prosecution under ITA 239(1) is indictable or summary, with fines ranging from 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed or acquiesced to false filings are jointly and severally liable under ITA 227.1/239 for tax, penalties and interest; can face extradition trigger, bank account garnishment, and criminal records; travel bans may be imposed as part of bail or court order.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) reassessment adjusting income and deductions; penalty under s.247(3) of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment amount where no reasonable efforts; interest accrues from original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for taxes withheld or collected but not remitted; for TP penalties, CRA may hold director for the penalty if signatory to false or misleading schedule T106 under joint liability provisions.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Circularity adjustments, GAAR reassessment under ITA s.245, denial of treaty benefits, and income re-attribution to actual economic owner under Canadian common-law agency principles.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for resulting taxes through 227.1 for unremitted withholdings; unlimited liability ULC shareholders become directly exposed to corporate creditors and tax obligations under BC law because liability is not limited.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for summary conviction and CAD 2,000,000 for indictable offences per violation; FINTRAC compliance orders and naming on a public compliance list.

Director personal liability: Individuals who directed or participated in money-laundering offences are liable to criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 10 years; directors held civilly liable for fines if they knowingly failed to act; seizure and restraint orders can apply to personal assets.
