Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and Business Number registry; online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account. British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (eTaxBC) for provincial sales tax (PST) registration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Form the BC limited partnership with BC Registry Services and obtain the BC registration number. Step 2: Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) via BRO or by filing Form RC1 with the CRA; non-resident partners without a Canadian establishment may use Form RC471 to reach the CRA Non-Resident Registry. Step 3: Open program accounts with the BN — RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) if employees work in Canada. Step 4: Register for BC PST through eTaxBC only if the LP makes taxable retail sales or leases taxable goods/software services in BC, and register with WorkSafeBC if payroll in BC. Step 5: File the T5013 Partnership Information Return within 5 months after partnership fiscal year-end; a BC LP carrying on business in Canada with non-resident partners must also apply Part XIII withholding under ITA s. 218 for the non-resident partners' shares of Canadian-source partnership income.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the BC LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax: the partnership computes income at the T5013 level but each partner is taxed on its share of Canadian-source income (ITA ss. 96–98); Canadian-resident corporate and individual partners report directly; non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on business income carried on in Canada (ITA s. 2(3)) with s. 218 withholding mechanics. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the BC LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax: the partnership computes income at the T5013 level but each partner is taxed on its share of Canadian-source income (ITA ss. 96–98); Canadian-resident corporate and individual partners report directly; non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on business income carried on in Canada (ITA s. 2(3)) with s. 218 withholding mechanics. For GST/HST, however, the LP is an independent person and can be the registered supplier of zero-rated BPO services.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. Available elections and statutory characterizations: (i) ETA s. 273 election — a partner is treated as the partnership's agent for GST/HST filing and remittance; (ii) ETA s. 156 nil-consideration election for closely related corporations (relevant if a Canadian corporate general partner is used); (iii) ITA s. 24.1/25? no; (iv) hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 restrict interest deductions on payments to hybrid entities; ITA s. 94.2 can deem non-resident entities with Canadian-resident participating interests to be corporations; each partner should map its home-jurisdiction classification against Canadian treatment to avoid income/hybrid mismatches; (v) transfer pricing contemporaneous documentation under ITA s. 247(4) protects against penalties on cross-border BPO fees; (vi) ITA s. 249.1 permits a non-calendar fiscal period election in narrow circumstances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII, allowing unrestricted conversion of CAD for trade in goods and services. Capital account transactions are liberalized with notification requirements for certain foreign investments; profit remittances by Canadian limited partnerships to foreign partners are permitted without prior approval when supported by tax filings and AML documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion applies to export proceeds under Canadian law. Service exports such as BPO call center revenues may be repatriated freely through commercial bank channels without statutory timelines or conversion ratios, subject only to beneficial ownership verification and tax compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for cross-border digital EMIs via platforms such as Wise Business or bank-affiliated fintech arms; legal constraints include FINTRAC registration for money services businesses and restrictions on offshore issuance of electronic money instruments without Bank of Canada approval.
