Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal tax authority administering GST/HST, Part I corporate income tax and payroll under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act; CRA portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); customs collection at importation is delegated to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through its CARM single-window portal; British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

1) Complete BC incorporation with the BC Corporate Registry and obtain the incorporation number, registered office and Notice of Articles. 2) Apply for the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; this opens the RC0001 (corporate income tax), RT0001 (GST/HST) and RP0001 (payroll) program accounts. 3) Open a CBSA RM (import/export) account under the same BN for customs declarations and post required security (surety bond or cash) for duty and GST/HST deferral on green coffee imports. 4) Elect the GST/HST reporting period via CRA My Business Account or Form GST20 (monthly, quarterly or annual); voluntary registration Form GST114 may be filed if the ULC remains a small supplier but wishes to claim input tax credits. 5) Register for BC PST only if taxable non-food BC sales are made; sales of green coffee beans as basic groceries and export sales are PST-exempt. 6) Foreign owners of the ULC remain permitted, but their presence disqualifies the corporation from Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status, so no small-business deduction applies and the combined federal/provincial corporate rate is 27% in BC.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — 1) Complete BC incorporation with the BC Corporate Registry and obtain the incorporation number, registered office and Notice of Articles. 2) Apply for the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with CRA using Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1; this opens the RC0001 (corporate income tax), RT0001 (GST/HST) and RP0001 (payroll) program accounts. 3) Open a CBSA RM (import/export) account under the same BN for customs declarations and post required security (surety bond or cash) for duty and GST/HST deferral on green coffee imports. 4) Elect the GST/HST reporting period via CRA My Business Account or Form GST20 (monthly, quarterly or annual); voluntary registration Form GST114 may be filed if the ULC remains a small supplier but wishes to claim input tax credits. 5) Register for BC PST only if taxable non-food BC sales are made; sales of green coffee beans as basic groceries and export sales are PST-exempt. 6) Foreign owners of the ULC remain permitted, but their presence disqualifies the corporation from Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) status, so no small-business deduction applies and the combined federal/provincial corporate rate is 27% in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box election. GST/HST elections available to the BC ULC include the closely related corporations election under ETA s. 156 (nil consideration for intercompany supplies) and the business-transfer election on supplies of a business. Income Tax Act elections include the s. 216 election for non-resident shareholders on Canadian rental/timber income. For U.S. federal classification, a Canadian ULC is a listed per se corporation under Treas. Reg. s.301.7701-2(b)(8)(i) (Canada list), so IRS Form 8832 pass-through treatment is generally unavailable except in the narrow subsection 216(1) election case; nevertheless, the business metadata taxonomy labels the ULC as Pass_Through_Eligible for hybrid-structuring design. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.5 and Part XIII s. 212) require the ULC to document arm's length terms of any cross-border intercompany coffee procurement and financing to avoid deduction denial or double non-taxation adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; mandatory registration arises when worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated green coffee) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident vendors selling goods through Canadian fulfillment warehouses must register without threshold since July 2021. BC PST: registration required for sellers making taxable PST supplies in BC in the ordinary course of business; small-seller relief applies to out-of-province sellers with modest BC sales, and green coffee food products and exports are exempt, so the ULC generally requires no PST account for core coffee operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods by a GST/HST registrant are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. For green coffee bean containers the ULC must: (a) be registered or required to be registered for GST/HST; (b) sell the goods to a non-resident purchaser and ship or deliver them directly outside Canada without domestic consumption; (c) ensure export occurs within a reasonable period after the supply; (d) retain documentary proof of export (customs export declaration, bill of lading, commercial invoice, payment evidence) for the statutory six-year record retention period; and (e) reconcile export declaration line items with GST/HST return amounts. Domestic supplies of green coffee beans to Canadian roasters and intermediaries are separately zero-rated as basic groceries under Schedule VI, Part III. Offshore services (logistics coordination, sourcing brokerage, quality control) are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and performed entirely outside Canada and are not in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; input tax credits on expenses supporting zero-rated exports are fully recoverable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) declaration or Form B13A for non-automated exports, including cargo control number)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean original or telex release, air waybill, or truck manifest evidencing actual movement of the container from Canada to the foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice, documentary letter-of-credit settlement advice, or FX completion certificate showing proceeds received from the non-resident buyer)
  • Commercial invoice with foreign buyer identity and Incoterms indicating export
  • Packing list and container load plan showing container number and seal number
  • Buyer tax or trade-registration evidence (foreign VAT/GST ID or trade register extract) confirming non-resident status

  • CRA export record reconciliation schedule linking customs declaration line items to GST/HST net tax filings

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, prior invoice authorization, or digital stamping regime for GST/HST. Compliance is periodic: GST/HST returns are filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE, CRA My Business Account or EDI) and payments are remitted through a financial institution; invoices must contain prescribed GST/HST disclosure information but require no fiscal stamp. Import and export clearance is electronic through CBSA's CARM (Assessment and Revenue Management) platform using EDI/API declarations (release, accounting, and exports through CERS), generating release notifications and a unique customs transaction reference; the voluntary Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing initiative does not create tax clearance or authorized fiscal receipt requirements.

CRA and CBSA may audit export transactions post-clearance; if the exporter cannot substantiate physical export and payment from a non-resident buyer, the supply is reassessed at 5% GST plus applicable provincial sales tax, with compound daily interest from the original remittance due date. Gross-negligence penalties of 25% of the evaded GST/HST apply for false statements or omissions under ETA s. 285.1; intentional evasion is a criminal offence attracting fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Patterned abuse such as phantom exports, circular offshore funds, invoice-only arrangements, or diversion of containers to Canadian consumers triggers full zero-rating clawback, denial or forfeiture of input tax credits, suspension or revocation of the GST/HST registration, termination of the CBSA duty-deferral and GST-deferral privileges, seizure of goods, and referral to the CRA Criminal Investigations Division; in parallel, a BC PST audit may impose 10% penalties and interest on any mischaracterized taxable BC supplies.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The CAD is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account; Canadian law imposes no exchange-control approval, surrender, or central-bank licensing requirement for coffee-import trade payments, foreign investment, or dividend and interest repatriation by a BC unlimited liability corporation. Repatriation restrictions are limited to (i) the corporate-law solvency and declaration tests under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act, and (ii) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the federal Income Tax Act; both are administered by revenue authorities, not by an FX regulator. Funds may be freely received and dispatched; the only administrative frictions are FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting thresholds and the Canada Border Services Agency currency declaration requirement at CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to Canada. There is no statutory timeline for repatriating or surrendering export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for receipts in foreign currency. For the subject import trade (green Arabica coffee), the corresponding obligation is the payer's duty to maintain accurate FINTRAC transaction records for each cross-border transfer equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 and to prepare large-cash and wire reports; no surrender of USD export proceeds is required, and a ULC may hold USD settlement balances offshore or onshore without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act and the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act, together with the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate policy counsel and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border transaction reporting; chartered bank supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. In Canada, a fintech EMI (non-bank payment service provider) cannot accept deposits, issue documentary letters of credit, or provide credit, because acceptance of deposits is reserved for CDIC-member banks chartered under the Bank Act; the Retail Payment Activities Act now requires PSPs to register for retail payment services but confers no FX or trade-finance bank powers. Fintech platforms are therefore viable only for secondary MT103-style disbursements and spot FX conversion under a funding wallet, not for LC issuance, shipping-document negotiation, or collateralized hedging; those functions must sit with a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for member capital contribution: SWIFT MT103 from the member's home bank to the ULC's Canadian account with Field 72 referenced to 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - EQUITY / BC REGISTRY NO' so the credit is mapped to the balance-of-payments equity category on the bank's records; the member name must match the ULC's register of members and the banking instructions must tag FINTRAC reporting thresholds. For same-day CAD value, use Payments Canada high-value clearing (Lynx) rather than a wire with multiple intermediaries; for USD contributions, book via the Canadian bank's US correspondent or internal sweep account to avoid daylight clearing risk; a contemporaneous board resolution, the bank's source-of-funds questionnaire, and a disbursement schedule are required to evidence non-taxable contribution treatment and to satisfy the Business Corporations Act (BC) solvency confirmation.Two primary clearing rails. (1) Documentary credit: the Vancouver trade desk issues a commercial LC via SWIFT MT700 to the origin-country confirming bank; negotiation is settled under MT707, MT754, or an MT752 reimbursement claim and the final transfer is a SWIFT MT103 or intra-bank book transfer against compliant documents (commercial invoice, clean on-board B/L, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate). (2) Open-account or advance-payment purchase: MT103 instructions routed over CHIPS or Fedwire for USD, or over Payments Canada Lynx for CAD, each with the contract and invoice reference in Field 70 and compliant currency-conversion booking where USD proceeds are insufficient. For all outflows the ULC must preserve FINTRAC records for transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 and align the payment currency with the origin-country export proceeds regulations so that confirmed LC proceeds, NDF realignments, and the coffee exporter's local-currency receipts remain fully matched to the physical shipment.Deliverable FX forwards (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) with a Schedule I bank dealing room, documented under ISDA 1998 FX Definitions with no optionalityFX swaps (swap of notional CAD against USD) for short-dated pre-settlement liquidities, permitted without OSFI margin if within the bank's dealing limitNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing BRL, COP, and ETB for the coffee-origin currencies, transacted through an approved derivatives dealer and cash-settled in USDCross-currency swaps (CAD/USD) for steady-state hedging of recurring import ticket and capital-account mismatches, centralized under ISDA/CSA with collateral in a segregated escrow accountVanilla FX options (CAD/USD calls and puts) on a premium-paid basis to protect a confirmed LC pipeline, subject to the ULC's board-approved hedging policy and National Instrument 52-110 audit-committee oversight

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: $500,000 CAD in related-party transaction value triggering local file requirements

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain full Economic Substance Regulations; however, GAAR and transfer pricing rules mandate economic substance and arm's-length dealings

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction; fines up to $5 million and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable; possible jail time, restitution orders and travel bans
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment; penalties up to 50% of tax plus interest and possible dissolution

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced; directors face civil claims and regulatory sanctions
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to $500,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years under PCMLTFA

Director personal liability: Senior officers personally criminally liable; possible asset freezes and travel bans
