Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Federal tax administration; portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Provincial sales tax (PST) is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance via gov.bc.ca/pst.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership with a commercial presence in British Columbia must: (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 or using CRA's Business Registration Online, indicating the reason for registration; (2) open a CRA GST/HST program account (code RT) if the partnership is carrying on business in Canada and annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, or voluntarily register to claim input tax credits; (3) register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST account purposes before making taxable retail sales of goods or prescribed services in BC; (4) maintain current provincial registration under the B.C. Partnership Act. The BN is the universal tax identifier; CRA assigns a 9-digit BN with an associated RT account for GST/HST. BC PST registration is via BC Online or paper application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no elective check-the-box regime. A British Columbia limited partnership is inherently fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act; it is not a taxpayer for federal income tax purposes and gains/losses flow through to partners. The only relevant administrative elections are: (i) under ETA section 273, a non-resident registrant may elect to appoint a Canadian resident representative for GST/HST compliance; (ii) under ITA section 261, a non-resident partner may elect the functional currency for Canadian tax reporting; (iii) for hybrid mismatch rules, no election is available – Canada applies the BEPS hybrid mismatch provisions mandatorily to disallow deductions in respect of hybrid entities/instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration threshold: CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). BC PST has no small supplier exemption; registration is required before making taxable sales/leases of goods or specified services within British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exported goods where the supplier delivers goods to a non-resident customer and the goods are exported as soon as practicable under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V. Services, including offshore secretarial and registered-agent services supplied to non-residents, are zero-rated when the supply is made to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant and the service is not connected to Canadian real property, tangible property situated in Canada, or performed at a location in Canada for the benefit of a Canadian resident. The taxpayer must retain reliable documentation showing the non-resident nature of the recipient and the offshore place of use/benefit.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic Commercial Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and purchase contract showing non-resident purchaser
  • Proof of payment/offshore remittance (bank SWIFT advice or inward remittance certificate)
  • GST/HST books and records substantiating zero-rating (contract, delivery notes, correspondence)

None. Canada does not mandate federal real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API filing, or digital stamping for GST/HST or BC PST. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed information (ETA sections 169 and 286), and electronic records are acceptable if retained in a readable format. A non-resident registrant must file returns through GST/HST NETFILE or authorized agent; no real-time clearance is imposed. BC PST does not require digital stamping.

CRA may reassess zero-rated supplies within the normal reassessment period (usually four years for registrants; longer for gross negligence/fraud). If a claim of zero-rating is unsupported by contemporaneous export documentation, the supply is taxable, and the registrant must pay GST/HST plus interest. Knowingly making a false zero-rating statement attracts a minimum penalty of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded under ETA section 285, plus possible criminal prosecution. There is no statutory Canadian deadline for foreign-exchange repatriation; the 180-day figure is a standard due-diligence benchmark; records supporting export zero-rating must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange-rate regime and unrestricted current and capital account convertibility consistent with IMF Article VIII. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible with no exchange controls on inward or outward capital flows. Profit remittances by a British Columbia limited partnership are freely permitted through chartered banks without prior regulatory approval, subject only to income tax withholding obligations under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), if applicable, and AML reporting thresholds for large cash or wire transactions. There are no statutory hurdles to dividend or partnership distribution remittances. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply for outbound wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 or equivalent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any export proceeds surrender regime. There is no mandatory timeline or compulsory conversion ratio for export receipts. Registered agents and offshore secretarial entities are free to retain all foreign currency earnings in Canadian-dollar operating accounts or designated foreign-currency accounts (FCA) held with a domestic Schedule I chartered bank. Re-export and trade settlements require neither central bank approval nor submission of export proceeds. Balance-of-payments and survey reporting thresholds administered by Statistics Canada may require periodic reporting of cross-border transactions but do not amount to foreign exchange control surrender.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential / FX oversight and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF compliance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility as a substitute for a Canadian chartered bank account. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut Business may be used as auxiliary payment facilitators for North American/European receivables and vendor payments, but cannot serve as the sole liquidity clearing rail for a British Columbia limited partnership. Canadian federal law restricts accepting deposits from Canadian residents without a banking license; foreign EMIs are not authorized to accept Canadian deposits or issue Canadian-incorporated debit cards. Cross-border EMI accounts can be maintained offshore, but the BC LP must maintain at least one Schedule I or II Canadian bank account for statutory trust account compliance, corporate secretarial disbursements, and FINTRAC-reportable wire processing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital contributions from foreign limited partners uses SWIFT MT103 (Customer Credit Transfer) from the remitting bank to the BC LP's operating or capital escrow account held at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The wire must include a clear payment narrative specifying 'Capital Contribution', 'Partnership Equity Subscription', or 'Contribution to CA_LP capital account', referencing the partnership name, account number, and, if available, ISIC 8299 service reference. Canada does not mandate a formal currency purpose code, but the remitting bank may require the remitter to state the underlying economic substance (i.e., international corporate secretarial business). Larger receipts (CAD 10,000 or equivalent) may trigger FINTRAC large transfer reporting, and the bank will request a completed foreign remittance document, source-of-funds form, and an attestation that the funds do not originate from sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions. Same-day clearing via the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and its successor Real-Time Rail (RTR) ensures final settlement in central bank funds.Outbound disbursements for trade-related settlements (registered agent fees, sub-licensed secretarial services, administrative expenses) are executed via SWIFT MT103 through the partner bank's cross-border payment interface. Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are available through the bank's trade finance division: a compliant irrevocable documentary credit may be opened in favour of overseas service providers using the Uniform Customs and Practice (UCP 600) rules. CAD-denominated domestic clearing for Canada-based payees can use the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batch debit/credit payments; urgent domestic wires use LVTS. For cross-border vendor payments exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank will transmit funds as MT103 with a standardized 'Purpose of Payment' code (e.g., 'Overseas Contract Payment', 'Professional Services') and maintain sender/receiver records in accordance with FINTRAC and international sanctions screening.Plain vanilla FX forward contracts (delivery of CAD against USD/EUR/GBP) for up to 12 months without central bank approvalNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on CAD/USD traded by major banks when underlying exposure cannot be physically settled due to settlement infrastructure constraintsCross-currency swaps (XCCS) matching the partnership's CAD functional currency with major currencies (USD, EUR, GBP) under ISDA master agreementsFX options (call/put on CAD) to cap conversion volatility on committed capital injectionsShort-term money market FX swaps to bridge liquidity mismatches during trade settlement cycles

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.0% (combined federal 15% and British Columbia general provincial 12%; for a BC limited partnership, tax is not levied at the partnership level but is applied to the taxable income of partners at their respective rates)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident, subject to treaty reduction. For a BC limited partnership, a distribution to a non-resident partner is not a dividend and is generally not subject to Part XIII withholding; the non-resident partner is instead taxable under Part I on its allocable share of Canadian-source business income. If the partnership has made an elective corporate treatment under a foreign jurisdiction (e.g., a US 'check-the-box' election), the foreign-imposed withholding characterisation must be reconciled in the Canadian allocation and the partner's home-country return.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal periods ending on or after January 1, 2024, the T5013 return is due no later than the earliest of March 31 (for December 31 year-ends) and five months after the fiscal period end. Partnerships with all non-resident partners and no Canadian business or permanent establishment are generally not required to file.

Deadline: · Frequency:

A T2 return must be filed within six months of the corporate fiscal year-end (for a December 31 year-end, deadline is June 30 of the following calendar year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not provide a single statutory threshold in the Income Tax Act for transfer-pricing documentation; however, CRA administrative standards require contemporaneous Local File documentation for cross-border non-arm's-length transactions when the aggregate value of goods transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 or when services, royalties, interest, and other intangibles transactions exceed CAD 500,000 per related non-resident per fiscal year. A Master File should be prepared for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 100,000,000, and Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for an ultimate parent whose consolidated group revenue is CAD 1,000,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not yet enforce Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) similar to those in the UAE, Bermuda, or Crown Dependencies, but CRA strictly applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act and the 'substance over form' doctrine. An offshore secretarial services BC LP must demonstrate real commercial substance: a verifiable physical office in British Columbia, non-secretarial employees, active decision-making by the general partner, and legitimate commercial reasons for the partnership structure. Treaty access, foreign tax credits, and the 'holding period' tests for registered-agent fees will be denied if the LP is found to be a shell used only to relabel profits as 'partnership distributions'.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (unreported Canadian-source partnership income)

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty assessable at the greater of CAD 50,000 and 50% of the understated tax, plus 100% to 200% of the tax avoided where the misrepresentation is attributable to a misinformation event; s. 239(1) criminal penalty on conviction is a fine not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, and imprisonment for a maximum of two years. CRA may also apply civil interest under s. 158(1) and reassess the partnership's allocations on a deemed-permanent-establishment basis.

Director personal liability: Under ITA s. 227.1 and s. 227.3, directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's unremitted Part XIII withholding tax and any unpaid source deductions. CRA may apply for a travel ban under s. 226(1) and seek a court order requiring the director to post security. A conviction under s. 239(1) may carry imprisonment for two years, forfeiture of the partnership's assets in BC, and a prohibition on holding a Canadian registered-agent license.
AML/Money Laundering (unlicensed secretarial agent and nominee LP operation)

Statutory sanction: The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) may impose an administrative monetary penalty (AMP) of up to CAD 1,000,000 for a corporate entity for each serious compliance violation, and after a conviction, a fine of up to CAD 500,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years for laundering proceeds of crime under s. 462.31 of the Criminal Code. BC's Civil Forfeiture Office may petition for forfeiture of the LP's bank accounts and registered assets on grounds that they constitute proceeds of unlawful activity.

Director personal liability: A director or officer who knowingly funnels funds through the LP is a party to money laundering under Criminal Code s. 21(c) and faces, upon conviction, a term of imprisonment up to ten years, confiscation of the director's personal property in BC, and a civil personal liability order under the BC Fraudulent Conveyance Act; travel bans and restrictive asset-freeze orders may be issued under the Mutual Legal Assistance in Criminal Matters Act if the conduct is cross-border.
Shell Company Abuse / Limited Liability Piercing

Statutory sanction: The BC registrar may strike the LP under BC Partnership Act (s. 65) if, in the registrar's opinion, the partnership is not carrying on its business within BC; CRA may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to deny all deduction flows and impose a 25% Part XIII withholding on deemed repatriations under s. 212.2; additionally, the provincial regulator may levy an administrative penalty of CAD 50,000 for operating a registered office with no real agent on premises, under the BC Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act.

Director personal liability: Where a limited partner takes part in the control and management of the BC limited partnership, the limited-partner liability shield is forfeited under BC Partnership Act s. 64, and that limited partner becomes jointly and severally liable for the LP's debts. Directors of the corporate general partner may be personally responsible for the LP's obligations where they execute contracts on behalf of a shell general partner with positive knowledge of the substance defect; travel bans may be imposed by the BC Supreme Court in civil securities fraud, and the directors may be ordered to repatriate and freeze all professional fees received from foreign clients.
False Claims / Improper Treaty Residence

Statutory sanction: Filing a false or deceptive return is punishable under ITA s. 238 and s. 239(2) by a fine not exceeding CAD 100,000 and/or imprisonment for up to two years; a consequence of the misrepresentation is a penalty of 100% to 200% of the avoided tax under s. 163(2.1). CRA may forfeit the treaty relief under the Limitation-on-Benefits article of the applicable DTA and may send a cross-border assessment to the home treaty authority requesting a mutual agreement proceeding under the Competent Authority process.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute a Treaty-Based Return disclosure (Form RC4651) with materially false facts are parties to the misrepresentation under Criminal Code s. 21; personal criminal liability may include up to five years' imprisonment, a fine of up to CAD 200,000, and a court order for civil restitution to the CRA. BC courts may also issue a Director's Order under the Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act requiring the director to compensate the foreign clients whose fees were mischaracterised, and a travel bar may be entered while restitution remains unpaid.
