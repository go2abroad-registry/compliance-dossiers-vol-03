Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes: the partnership computes income under the Income Tax Act and allocates the relevant amounts to partners under the partnership agreement and statutory rules; the partnership itself generally does not pay ordinary income tax. There is no Canadian equivalent to the United States check-the-box election for a domestic limited partnership. Classification may be affected by the partnership agreement, provincial legal status, foreign-law treatment, treaty analysis, and applicable anti-hybrid rules. A partnership may be required to file a T5013 Partnership Information Return, and it may elect or apply statutory rules concerning fiscal periods, method elections, and specific partnership transactions where available. Partner-level corporate, non-resident withholding, treaty, and permanent-establishment consequences remain separate from the partnership's flow-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST, the general small-supplier threshold is CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies over the relevant preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter, subject to statutory aggregation and special rules. Registration may be required earlier where the partnership ceases to be a small supplier, voluntarily registers, or falls within a special category. There is no general CAD 30,000 threshold that permits a registrant to disregard GST/HST on taxable Canadian commercial activities once registration is required. British Columbia PST is separate: the partnership must register where it makes or facilitates taxable PST supplies or is otherwise required to collect or self-assess PST; ordinary freight transportation services are generally outside the core PST tax base, but taxable equipment, software, bundled supplies, and specified services require separate analysis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are generally zero-rated for GST purposes where the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including delivery or transfer of possession in Canada to a purchaser for export in circumstances prescribed by the Excise Tax Act and its regulations, or exportation by the supplier as required by the applicable rule. International freight transportation services and certain related services may be zero-rated where the service meets the statutory definition and documentation requirements for international transportation. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where a specific statutory rule applies; non-resident status alone is insufficient. The supplier must verify the recipient, destination, contractual scope, place of supply, export or cross-border movement, and exclusions for services relating to Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or other excluded activities. Input tax credits and GST/HST refunds require valid tax documentation, commercial-purpose evidence, and proof that the inputs were acquired, imported, or brought into a participating province for consumption, use, or supply in commercial activities.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, importer or consignee, purchaser, goods, value, currency, terms of sale, origin, and destination
  • Canadian Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) where export reporting is legally required, including applicable controlled-goods, permit, or restricted-goods information
  • Bill of lading, sea waybill, air waybill, rail document, truck manifest, or other carrier evidence showing cross-border movement
  • Packing list, cargo manifest, project-cargo instructions, delivery records, and proof of export or foreign delivery
  • Purchase order, transportation contract, freight-forwarding agreement, subcontractor records, and customer correspondence evidencing the international movement
  • Proof of payment and accounting ledger reconciliation; an inward-remittance certificate is not generally a statutory Canadian GST zero-rating document, and Canada has no general foreign-exchange repatriation certificate requirement
  • Import entry, customs release, destination customs record, or foreign import evidence where relevant to substantiate the exported status and destination
  • Recipient's non-resident status evidence and contractual documentation for services supplied to non-residents
  • GST/HST registration details, tax invoices, input tax credit support, and any certificate, permit, or written declaration required by the particular zero-rating rule

Canada has no general federal real-time electronic-invoice clearance, fiscal-stamping, or mandatory business-to-business e-invoicing regime for ordinary GST/HST transactions. Electronic invoices and electronic accounting records are generally acceptable if they contain the required GST/HST particulars and remain accessible, reliable, and auditable. GST/HST registrants must retain supporting records for the statutory retention period and be able to substantiate tax charged, input tax credits, zero-rating, place of supply, and commercial purpose. Commercial import and export data must be transmitted through CBSA systems, including the CARM Client Portal and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), where applicable; customs reporting is not equivalent to invoice clearance. Electronic customs declarations, carrier documents, commercial invoices, permits, bills of lading, and accounting records must be retained and reconciled.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement for an inward-remittance certificate. CRA may deny or reverse zero-rating, assess unpaid GST/HST, deny input tax credits or refunds, and apply arrears interest where exportation, international transportation, recipient status, or documentary support is inadequate. Incorrectly claimed refunds or credits may be reassessed retrospectively within the applicable assessment period, with extended or unlimited periods available in cases involving misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful blindness, or fraud. Gross-negligence penalties can generally equal 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit or refund, in addition to interest; other administrative penalties may apply for false statements, failure to file, failure to remit, or failure to keep adequate records. Knowingly false GST/HST claims may also trigger criminal prosecution and fines or imprisonment. CBSA may impose customs AMPS penalties, seize or detain goods, reassess duties and taxes, and suspend or restrict customs privileges for inaccurate export declarations or supporting records. Provincial PST assessments, penalties, and interest may apply separately where taxable British Columbia supplies or self-assessment obligations were omitted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Canada imposes zero exchange-control restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions; capital injections, distribution remittances, and profit repatriation by a British Columbia limited partnership are lawful without central-bank approval. The only remittance frictions are tax-derived: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (treaty-modifiable) on passive payments such as interest, rents, and royalties, and Part I Canadian-source business income taxation allocated to non-resident limited partners carrying on business in Canada. A pure capital contribution to an LP is a non-taxable equity infusion and is not subject to withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation windows on export receipts. The CA_LP may retain 100% of USD/EUR project-cargo proceeds in domestic or offshore accounts indefinitely. Residual obligations are informational: transactions exceeding CAD $10,000 require FINTRAC recordkeeping and SWIFT message archiving; holdings of specified foreign property above CAD $100,000 cost must be reported via CRA Form T1135 by the Canadian-resident partnership.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising monetary authority jointly with the Minister of Finance. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control commission analogous to a capital-controls authority (e.g., SAFE/DIREX). Prudential supervision of banks sits with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF transaction oversight is vested in FINTRAC; physical cross-border currency movement is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary operational disbursements; Low as a primary treasury anchor. FINTRAC-registered money-services businesses (e.g., Wise Business) may provide multi-currency virtual accounts (USD/EUR/GBP) and low-cost SWIFT/Fedwire routing for small vendor payments. Legal constraints: EMIs are not chartered banks under the Bank Act, hold no CDIC deposit insurance, cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, and lack direct access to Canada's Lynx RTGS. They are therefore non-viable for milestone escrow or LC-based heavy-haul settlement and must be subordinated to a Schedule I bank core account.

Turnover: No statutory audit is required for a British Columbia limited partnership under the Partnership Act solely because of size or revenue. Compulsory audit may arise only from the partnership agreement, a lender finance covenant, or a requirement imposed by foreign partner tax-exemption or transfer-pricing documentation under the Income Tax Act. For sub-threshold private partnerships, a review engagement is commonly used, but an audit is not statutorily mandated.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate rate (federal Part I 15% + British Columbia provincial 12%). The BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent: no entity-level income tax; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of business income in its own hands.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Part I – Identification (Boxes 100–103) Legal name, business number (BN), BC LP registration number, fiscal period start/end dates, mailing address of the head office, and the general partner's name/address. Enter the BC limited partnership registration number and indicate the entity type as 'Limited Partnership'. Disclose the country of residence of each non-resident partner on the T5013 Summary to trigger s.153(1)(p) withholding. Late T5013 filing: penalty of CAD 25 per day per partner, minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per partnership (ITA s.162(7)); failure to provide T5013 slips: CAD 100 per slip (maximum CAD 7,500 in aggregate). A late or incomplete Form T106: CAD 100 per day, maximum CAD 5,000. Subsequent years: automatic 'failure to file' offence with criminal exposure under s.238.
Schedule 1 – Statement of Business Income (equivalent of T5013 lines 8500–8600) Gross OOG project-cargo haulage revenue, empty repositioning charges, demurrage/freight income, and deductible expenses (crew, fuel, third-party trucking, port storage, permits for over-dimensional loads). All amounts in Canadian dollars; include only Canadian-source income. Foreign-exchange gains/losses on USD milestone wires reported as income/expense; no Canadian tax is imposed at the LP level on offshore income if the LP is a Canadian partnership — allocation passes to partners. Late T5013 filing: penalty of CAD 25 per day per partner, minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per partnership (ITA s.162(7)); failure to provide T5013 slips: CAD 100 per slip (maximum CAD 7,500 in aggregate). A late or incomplete Form T106: CAD 100 per day, maximum CAD 5,000. Subsequent years: automatic 'failure to file' offence with criminal exposure under s.238.
Schedule 50 – Allocation of Partnership Income and Loss to Partners Name of each general and limited partner, partner type (individual/corporation/LLC/trust), percentage profit share, capital contributed, and each partner's share of income or loss for the fiscal period. For a non-resident limited partner, complete the 'non-resident partner' lines and enter the amount of withheld tax under s.153(1)(p) (15% threshold) and the treaty country of residence. The loss allocated to a limited partner is capped at its 'at-risk amount' under s.96(2.1) — any excess is deferred and carried forward. Late T5013 filing: penalty of CAD 25 per day per partner, minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per partnership (ITA s.162(7)); failure to provide T5013 slips: CAD 100 per slip (maximum CAD 7,500 in aggregate). A late or incomplete Form T106: CAD 100 per day, maximum CAD 5,000. Subsequent years: automatic 'failure to file' offence with criminal exposure under s.238.
Box 940/941 – Canadian-source payments to non-residents Report management fees, interest, royalties, or rent paid or credited to non-resident non-arm's length persons. Identifies Part XIII obligation and requires Form T106 if the aggregate cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000. Reduces statutory withholding only upon a valid NR301 in file. Late T5013 filing: penalty of CAD 25 per day per partner, minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per partnership (ITA s.162(7)); failure to provide T5013 slips: CAD 100 per slip (maximum CAD 7,500 in aggregate). A late or incomplete Form T106: CAD 100 per day, maximum CAD 5,000. Subsequent years: automatic 'failure to file' offence with criminal exposure under s.238.
T5013 Summary – Registered limited partnership indicator (Schedule 91 / box 103) Indicates whether the partnership is a 'registered limited partnership' under BC law and the number of limited partners; used by the CRA for at-risk and aggregate investment plan rules. If the ownership is syndicated, attach the BC LP registry's last annual confirmation; CRA use of box 103 affects the limited-partner loss restriction for promoters. Late T5013 filing: penalty of CAD 25 per day per partner, minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500 per partnership (ITA s.162(7)); failure to provide T5013 slips: CAD 100 per slip (maximum CAD 7,500 in aggregate). A late or incomplete Form T106: CAD 100 per day, maximum CAD 5,000. Subsequent years: automatic 'failure to file' offence with criminal exposure under s.238.
Section 3 – Non-resident counterparty identification Legal name, jurisdiction of residence (e.g., US LLC, Panama entity), country of permanent establishment, and relationship test (shareholder, partner, common control). Identify each foreign partner/affiliate transacting with the BC LP; if a foreign partner controlled the LP's GP, the transactions are reportable even if priced at arm's length. Failing to file a complete T106: CAD 100 per day up to CAD 5,000 per person (ITA s.162(8)); knowingly filing false information: 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated tax; the TP adjustment penalty under s.247(3) (10% of the net adjustment) applies if no reasonable-effort documentation was kept.
Section 4 – Transaction description Nature and volume of the transaction (freight, subhaulage, charter, management fees, loan interest), consideration paid, date of the transaction, and the transfer-pricing method selected. Describe the value chain with an OECD six-factor functional analysis; attach a pricing benchmark if the transaction value exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Failing to file a complete T106: CAD 100 per day up to CAD 5,000 per person (ITA s.162(8)); knowingly filing false information: 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated tax; the TP adjustment penalty under s.247(3) (10% of the net adjustment) applies if no reasonable-effort documentation was kept.
NR4 slip Box 26 – Other income Non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source partnership income that was subject to the 15% withholding. Issue an NR4 to each non-resident partner annually; the withheld 15% is creditable against the partner's Part I tax (if a Canadian return is filed), and treaty relief may reduce the rate upon NR301 submission. Failure to remit withheld non-resident tax: interest (average prescribed rate + 4%) and 10% late-withholding penalty (s.227(9)); further 20% penalty for repeated failures; director liability under s.227.1 for the corporate general partner.
