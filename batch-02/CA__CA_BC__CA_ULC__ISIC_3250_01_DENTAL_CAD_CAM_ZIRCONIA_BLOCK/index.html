Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration and portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/business-registration-online.html; British Columbia corporate registration via BC Corporate Online/BC OneStop at https://www.onestop.gov.bc.ca; BC provincial consumption taxes (PST, Employer Health Tax) administered by the BC Ministry of Finance, Provincial Tax Programs, via eTaxBC at https://etax.fin.gov.bc.ca.

E-government filing portal

A BC ULC is created as a resident Canadian corporation under the BC Business Corporations Act. The standard procurement sequence is: (1) File incorporation/registration through BC Corporate Online or an authorized BC lawyer; the system will concurrently issue the BC incorporation number and, if requested, pre-register the CRA Business Number (BN). (2) Complete CRA registration for the 9-digit BN program accounts: RC (T2 corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll, if employees), and RM (import/export). (3) File Form RC1 or register online via BRO; CRA requires completion of the GST/HST Registration Questionnaire to set filing frequency (annual, quarterly, or monthly). (4) Open CRA My Business Account for filings/remittances. (5) Register separately for BC PST via eTaxBC if the ULC makes taxable retail sales in BC. Foreign shareholders may hold 100% of the ULC and do not require a Canadian BN merely as passive owners; if the foreign parent itself carries on business in Canada, it needs its own BN. No minimum capital is required, and no immigration/visa condition applies to incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian domestic law) — the ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under ITA s. 89(1), resident in Canada by place of incorporation, and is taxed on worldwide income; Canada does not apply a territorial regime. Unlimited liability does not alter Canadian tax characterization. Combined federal (15%) and BC (12%) general corporate income tax rate is approximately 27% for 2025; if directly or indirectly controlled by non-residents, the ULC is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation and cannot claim the small business deduction. Pass-Through_Eligible status is available only for US federal tax purposes via check-the-box; it is not recognized under Canadian domestic tax law.

Available election forms

Available elections: (1) US check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. s. 301.7701-2(b)(8) — a BC ULC is not a per se corporation, so a US shareholder may file Form 8832 to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for US tax purposes, creating a hybrid entity that remains a Canadian taxpayer; Canadian hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4 et seq.) must be assessed. (2) ITA s. 85 rollover election permitting tax-deferred transfer of eligible business assets into the ULC upon incorporation. (3) ETA s. 273 GST/HST grouping election allowing related Canadian registrants to be treated as a single registrant for GST/HST purposes. (4) ETA s. 273 joint venture election allowing a single participant to account for GST/HST on a qualifying joint venture. No separate election is required to claim export zero-rating; the zero-rated treatment is self-assessed and substantiated by documentary evidence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148: GST/HST registration becomes mandatory when the ULC's worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. Voluntary registration is strongly recommended before the threshold is reached to claim input tax credits on manufacturing inputs (zirconia powders, CAD/CAM equipment, milling tools). Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies where annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated exports apply to tangible personal property exported from Canada under section 1, Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act, where: (i) the ULC transfers ownership of the dental zirconia blocks to a non-resident purchaser; (ii) the goods are shipped directly out of Canada by the ULC or delivered to a common carrier for export as soon after transfer as reasonably possible; and (iii) the ULC retains contemporaneous documentary proof of export (customs export declaration, bill of lading, purchase order, invoice, and payment evidence). Services performed on goods for export (e.g., CAD/CAM milling or customization performed before shipment) may be zero-rated under section 2, Part V, Schedule VI. Supplies to non-residents for use wholly outside Canada may be zero-rated under section 7, Part V, Schedule VI. BC PST does not apply where the vendor ships goods to a destination outside BC; if the goods are subsequently diverted to domestic use, the ULC must self-assess both GST and PST. The ULC may claim a refund of GST paid on inputs attributable to zero-rated exports via the ordinary GST/HST net tax return (input tax credits); no separate refund application is required for the export portion.

  • Canadian export evidence: Canada Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), or ACI/eManifest routing instructions where an exemption applies, evidencing the dental zirconia blocks left Canada and identifying the foreign destination.
  • Transport document: Ocean or multimodal Bill of Lading, or IATA Air Waybill issued by a carrier, showing the blocks were shipped from a Canadian port/airport to the non-resident consignee outside Canada and naming the exporter ULC and foreign buyer.
  • Payment and foreign-exchange evidence: SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation, bank credit advice, or remittance certificate proving receipt of the foreign-currency consideration for the exported blocks, retained to substantiate the for-consideration sale requirement.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing API exists in Canada. CRA accepts paper, PDF, XML, or EDI invoices that satisfy statutory content requirements under ETA s. 221 and records that must be retained for six years in English or French at the ULC's Canadian records address (ETA s. 286). The federal Pan-Canadian e-Invoice pilot (Pandae) is voluntary and aligned with Peppol BIS 3.0; exporters may use Peppol-compatible digital invoices, but commercial invoices, waybills, and customs declarations remain the operative export evidence.

CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years of the return due date (ETA s. 298); the limitation period is unlimited where the ULC has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud (ETA s. 298(2)). If export zero-rating is disallowed, CRA assesses 5% GST on the full export proceeds, plus BC PST exposure if goods were diverted domestically, with arrears interest compounded daily at CRA prescribed rates. Administrative penalties: failure to file or provide information (ETA s. 280); false statements or omissions (ETA s. 285) at the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax that would have been payable; gross negligence penalty for income tax (ITA s. 163(2)) at 50% of the understated tax. Although the ETA does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, collection periods materially exceeding 180 days invite recharacterization as a loan, lease, or domestic sale and trigger transfer-pricing and uncollected-amount scrutiny (ITA s. 78); export proceeds should be collected within the stated commercial terms. Criminal evasion or false statements (ETA s. 327; ITA s. 239) carry fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years. The ULC bears the burden of proving that goods were in fact exported; failure to maintain the documentation bundle for six years in Canada is an offence and may independently invalidate zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no restrictions on current account convertibility or capital account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Agreement, and there is no exchange control approval requirement for inbound/outbound transfers, profit remittances, or dividend repatriation. A non-resident shareholder of a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation may freely receive dividends, but such distributions are subject to Canadian Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% unless reduced by tax treaty), and the ULC must satisfy the solvency test in the BC Business Corporations Act before declaring a dividend. In practice, banks will apply standard AML due diligence rather than government permission to incoming/outgoing capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not require exporters to surrender export proceeds, convert foreign currency to Canadian dollars, or repatriate such proceeds within a set timeline. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio and no settlement period imposed by the Bank of Canada. Exporters may retain revenues in foreign currency accounts maintained at a Canadian chartered bank. However, all cross-border electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and supporting trade documentation (invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations) must be retained for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). The regulatory framework is polycentric: the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises chartered banks; FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and collects cross-border wire transfer reports; Global Affairs Canada administers export/import permits and economic sanctions. There is no dedicated foreign exchange control authority or exchange control statute in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium for offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) as the primary or sole treasury rail. Legal constraints are substantial: an offshore EMI cannot clear CAD through Payments Canada because only domestically regulated chartered banks and designated clearing members participate in the Canadian clearance and settlement system. In addition, any digital money service provider serving Canadian clients must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and must obtain any applicable provincial investment/money-lending licences. An offshore EMI may be used as a supplementary subsidiary account for USD/EUR receipts, subject to the bank's correspondent relationships and the ULC's overall treasury policies, but it cannot replace a Tier 1 domestic commercial bank account for statutory compliance or operational reliability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injections into a British Columbia ULC, route the funds by SWIFT MT103 to the receiving bank's SWIFT BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC) using a CAD or USD corporate operational account. In field 70 (Remittance Information), state 'EQUITY INJECTION FOR ULC SHARE SUBSCRIPTION' and include the subscription agreement reference number. Where the correspondent bank requires an ISO 20022 purpose code, use 'EQUI' (equity securities / share capital) or, if unsupported, use the narrative 'Capital subscription; non-resident direct investment'. The beneficiary bank will screen against sanctions lists and conduct CDD verification; FINTRAC receives an Electronic Funds Transfer Report for all incoming wire transfers equal to or greater than C$10,000. Underlying verification documentation should include the board resolution, share subscription agreement, a source-of-funds declaration, and proof of registration of the BC ULC.Trade settlement for raw materials and dental consumables should be executed through the ULC's Tier 1 operational account using the following rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 Customer Credit Transfer for open-account and T/T settlements in CAD, USD, EUR, or JPY; (b) SWIFT MT700 Documentary Credit issued according to the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP600) where a supplier requires a commercial letter of credit; (c) MT760 Standby Letter of Credit or guarantee for performance security on purchase agreements; and (d) Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for high-value CAD obligations that require real-time clearing. Each outgoing wire must comply with bank-specific AML and sanctions screening, and amounts over C$10,000 must be aggregated and reported to FINTRAC. Field 70 should reference the commercial invoice number and the underlying purchase order.Deliverable FX forward contracts (plain vanilla) with a Canadian Schedule I bank under a bilateral ISDA/CSA or bank master agreement for CAD/USD, CAD/EUR and CAD/JPY hedges.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies not freely deliverable in Canada (e.g., CNY, INR, KRW) settled in Canadian dollars or US dollars via a correspondent bank.Over-the-counter plain-vanilla FX options (puts and calls) with a Canadian bank or authorized derivatives dealer, subject to ISDA documentation.Cross-currency swaps and long-dated forward-start currency swaps to align USD/CAD cash-flow mismatches over the ULC's procurement-to-receivable cycle.Exchange-traded FX futures and options on regulated Canadian or US futures exchanges for liquid currency pairs, subject to the ULC's investment policy and derivative reporting obligations.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low to Medium for offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) as the primary or sole treasury rail. Legal constraints are substantial: an offshore EMI cannot clear CAD through Payments Canada because only domestically regulated chartered banks and designated clearing members participate in the Canadian clearance and settlement system. In addition, any digital money service provider serving Canadian clients must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and must obtain any applicable provincial investment/money-lending licences. An offshore EMI may be used as a supplementary subsidiary account for USD/EUR receipts, subject to the bank's correspondent relationships and the ULC's overall treasury policies, but it cannot replace a Tier 1 domestic commercial bank account for statutory compliance or operational reliability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian dollar (CAD) 1,000,000 aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with any single non-resident related party triggers contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (the closest equivalent to a Local File). Country-by-Country reporting obligations apply to a Canadian ultimate parent or surrogate parent with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), the sham doctrine, and the Canadian source rules require a ULC to possess real economic substance in BC, including physical manufacturing premises, employees, board meetings, bank accounts, and decision-making functions. Interposing a BC ULC principally to secure treaty benefits or defer Canadian tax without operational substance will trigger CRA reassessment, GAAR application, and possible denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Income Tax Evasion (ITA s.239)

Statutory sanction: On indictment: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Summary conviction: fine in the same percentage range and imprisonment up to 2 years. Civil gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2): 50% of the understated tax or overstated refund.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed or participated in tax evasion are personally liable for civil penalties and may be criminally prosecuted. BC courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders, and extradition orders. Directors can be held for unremitted source deductions under ITA s.227.1, and corporate veil-piercing claims may be made by CRA and creditors.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (ITA s.247)

Statutory sanction: CRA adjustment to taxable income; penalty equal to 10% of the net upward transfer-pricing adjustment when the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts; additional per-information-return penalties of up to CAD 2,500 per failure and monthly penalties for demand failures; interest on reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing certificates may be subject to gross negligence penalties. The foreign parent may be found to have a Canadian permanent establishment, and the ULC's directors may face director liability for wrongfully distributing corporate funds while transfer pricing tax debts are outstanding.
Part XIII Withholding and Treaty Abuse (ITA s.227)

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is liable for the full unwithheld amount, plus daily prescribed interest; under ITA s.227(8), a penalty of 10% of the amount for ordinary negligence and 20% where the failure was known or grossly negligent; repeated offences carry criminal prosecution and loss of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for the corporation's failure to withhold and remit Part XIII amounts. This liability extends to non-resident parent corporate directors if they directed a Canadian subsidiary. CRA may freeze personal assets, impose travel bans, and seek civil restitution before issuance of criminal charges.
BC Corporate Registry Default and ULC Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: The BC Registrar may dissolve the ULC after a default period; reinstatement requires payment of accumulated fees and penalties. During a period of non-compliance, directors and shareholders may be treated as carrying on business personally and may face creditor claims. The statutory sanction includes revocation of the company's legal status and loss of the benefit of limited liability to third parties.

Director personal liability: Given the ULC's infinite liability structure, directors and shareholders become personally liable for the entire corporate debt load if dissolution occurs or if the corporate form is abused. Creditors can pierce the veil; courts may grant civil restitution, impose a ban on leaving Canada, and, in fraud cases, issue criminal convictions.
AML / FINTRAC Cross-Border Wire Reporting Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, assessable per contravention, up to CAD 500,000 for an entity; criminal prosecution possible for knowingly conducting financial transactions with proceeds of crime under Criminal Code s.462.31, punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be named in FINTRAC penalty proceedings and are personally liable for AML program failures if they participated in the contravention. Money-laundering convictions trigger immediate travel bans, asset seizure orders, and mandatory forfeiture. Directors' personal bank accounts can be frozen during investigation.
