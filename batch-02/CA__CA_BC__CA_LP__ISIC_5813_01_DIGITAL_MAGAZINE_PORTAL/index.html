Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a British Columbia limited partnership. Each limited partner must contribute money or property (services are prohibited) as recorded in the Declaration and the Partnership Agreement pursuant to s. 54 of the Partnership Act. No escrow, deposit, or trust account rule applies to a digital-publishing LP; initial contributions and capital accounts must be documented in the partnership records and the operating bank account.

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defense or structural clearance applies to a remote-first digital operation. If the LP leases office space, the landlord must certify compliance with the BC Building Code and BC Fire Code (fire safety, means of egress, occupancy limits) before occupancy; WorkSafeBC registration and compliance with the Workers Compensation Act, RSBC 1996 c. 492, and the OHS Regulation, B.C. Reg. 296/97, apply once employees are hired, including a first-aid assessment and workplace safety program.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST, income tax, and customs/trade administration; British Columbia Ministry of Finance (BC Tax Services Online / eTaxBC) — administration of Provincial Sales Tax (PST) in British Columbia.

E-government filing portal

The limited partnership must first apply for a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account through CRA's My Business Account or the Business Registration Online service. For foreign entities, the initial application may be made by mail with Form RC1, appointing a Canadian representative if required. Once a GST/HST account is active, the partnership must separately register for BC PST with the BC Ministry of Finance using the online eTaxBC system, providing the federal BN and a BC address (registered office/place of business or appointed agent). If no Canadian permanent establishment is maintained, the LP should apply using the non-resident registration channel; proof of foreign legal existence and partnership agreement must accompany the application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; the limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for income tax. The partnership may, however, exercise targeted elections: (i) a joint election by all partners to change the partnership's fiscal period under subsection 249.1(3) of the Income Tax Act; (ii) GST/HST elections under subsections 156(2) and 273(1) of the Excise Tax Act for closely related entities and joint ventures; and (iii) hybrid-mismatch neutralizing adjustments are non-elective, following OECD recommendations incorporated into Canadian domestic rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 gross annual taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold per Excise Tax Act); BC PST: taxable sellers must register when making taxable supplies in BC, with limited exemptions for occasional sellers and a CAD 0 registration threshold for regular sellers of digital goods and taxable services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital magazine subscriptions, sponsored media placements and related advertising services supplied to non-resident recipients for use or consumption wholly outside Canada are generally zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. To qualify, the supply must not be connected to a Canadian-targeted audience, the subscription/advertising inventory must be delivered to a person outside Canada, and the supplier must obtain ordinary records evidencing non-resident status. PST determination: sales of digital content delivered to a non-resident customer outside BC are not taxable under the PST; domestic BC sales remain subject to PST where the digital magazine service falls within a taxable service class.

  • Customs Export Declaration (where tangible/corporate advertising assets are shipped or deemed shipped outside Canada)
  • Bill of Lading / digital delivery log and proof of out-of-country consumption
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (to evidence receipt of export proceeds in the partnership's Canadian bank account)

Canada does not currently mandate a national e-invoicing clearance platform or continuous transaction control gateway. Suppliers must issue invoices that comply with GST/HST and PST information rules (e.g., vendor name, BN, tax number of the recipient, tax amounts and the applicable rate). For digital magazine portals using Stripe and AdSense payout rails, payment gateway records must be retained in a format accessible to CRA and the BC Ministry of Finance; there is no government API stamping requirement in force for B2B or B2C digital supplies as of the current assessment period.

The CRA and BC Auditor General may reassess zero-rated supplies if documentation is insufficient, applying the full 5% GST and 7% PST without export relief, plus daily compounding interest and penalties. Civil penalties for knowingly, falsely claiming zero-rating include penalties under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act (up to CAD 8,000 or 10% of the tax claimed) and gross negligence penalties of 50% of the tax under section 285. Criminal investigation can result in fines and imprisonment for deliberate tax evasion; retrospective clawback periods extend to at least three years, or six years for misrepresentation, and indefinitely where fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime; both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted, and no statutory approval is required for non-resident limited partners to inject capital or repatriate allocable profits in CAD, USD, EUR or other convertible currencies. The only practical frictions are (i) FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports (EFTRs) for cross-border electronic transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, (ii) standard AML/KYC due diligence at Canadian deposit-taking institutions, and (iii) Canadian income tax under Part I/Part XIV on partnership business income attributable to a British Columbia permanent establishment, with tax-treaty relief available; these are tax and reporting obligations, not currency-control constraints. Digital magazine portal revenues (subscriptions, sponsored media, advertising placements) may be settled in USD/EUR without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export or digital-services receipts. A BC limited partnership engaged in digital magazine publishing may retain export proceeds (advertising, subscription, sponsored-media income) indefinitely in USD-denominated non-resident accounts or convert them at its own discretion. The Bank of Canada's floating-rate regime subjects retained foreign currency to market fluctuation, and financial institutions must file FINTRAC EFTRs for cross-border electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; there is no classic 30/60/90-day surrender framework.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border transaction monitoring and reporting is supervised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control ministry or exchange-control permit system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a supplementary payment rail, not as a replacement for a domestic Schedule I chartered bank account. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, PayQin) provide multi-currency settlement accounts, virtual USD/EUR IBANs and cloud-based onboarding workflows. Platform providers operating into Canada must be registered with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses and licensed by Consumer Protection BC where applicable; the BC limited partnership is not itself an MSB if it uses EMIs solely for collections and payables. Key constraints: (i) foreign EMIs do not provide CDIC deposit insurance or Lynx/ACSS direct clearing, (ii) CAD-denominated payroll and domestic recurring obligations require a domestic bank account, and (iii) some Canadian banks view frequent EMI sweeps as a negative underwriting signal; therefore the LP should maintain its primary treasury ledger at a domestic bank and use EMIs only for niche multi-currency payout corridors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partner capital contributions should be routed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the LP's designated Schedule I bank account using clear remittance information such as 'PARTNER CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - CA LP [NAME] [ACCOUNT]'. Canadian banks are required to file a FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) for any international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, which is executed by the financial intermediary and requires the LP's compliance file to be updated with source-of-funds evidence. The LP should pre-advise the relationship manager of the expected wire, the contributor's identity, and the purpose code 'TREA' where the correspondent-bank network requires one, to avoid a 24-48 hour compliance hold.
