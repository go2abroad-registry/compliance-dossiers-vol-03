Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; official portals: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE. Provincial counterpart for retail sales tax: British Columbia Ministry of Finance — eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned BC ULC obtains its CRA Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST account by filing Form RC1 or by using CRA Business Registration Online; non-resident ownership is permitted, but the applicant must disclose non-resident status and may be required to post security under ETA s. 240(1). After the BN is issued, the entity must separately register through eTaxBC for a BC PST collector number before making any taxable sale into British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible under foreign check-the-box law only; for Canadian domestic tax purposes the BC ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation resident in Canada under ITA s. 250(4)(a), taxed at Entity_Level_Taxation on federal Part I income tax and BC provincial corporate income tax (administered by CRA under a collection agreement). No territorial or tax-exempt-zone status applies.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box statute. A US shareholder of a BC ULC may file US Form 8832 to treat the ULC as disregarded, creating hybrid mismatch outcomes governed by Canada's hybrid mismatch arrangement rules (ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.7), effective for payments in taxation years beginning after 30 September 2022. GST/HST s. 156 closely-related corporation election is available for BC-resident affiliates; no general flow-through election exists for Canadian tax filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: mandatory registration when a person's worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a four-calendar-quarter period (small-supplier threshold). BC PST: no equivalent small-supplier threshold; a vendor, including a non-resident vendor, must hold a PST collector registration before making taxable sales into BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods sold and exported from Canada qualify for 0% GST/HST under s. 7, Schedule VI, ETA when the supplier, or an agent or related party acting for the supplier, ships the goods outside Canada and holds contemporaneous evidence of export; such exports are also outside BC PST. Services generally qualify for zero-rating under s. 6, Schedule VI when supplied to a non-resident person, unless the service relates to real or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or to intangible property for use in Canada; ss. 8, 9 and 10 add specific zero-rating for services on exported tangible property, foreign real property, and foreign-use intangible property.

  • Commercial Invoice / Canada Customs Invoice
  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (Form A64) filed via CARMA/CERS, with CERS export number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to a foreign destination
  • Carrier terminal receipts and port loading documents proving physical departure of the goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank confirmation evidencing payment from the foreign buyer

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory API invoice clearance, and no digital stamping. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE; registrants must retain machine-readable invoices and export proof for six years under ETA s. 286. A voluntary Peppol-based national e-invoicing framework is under development but is not mandatory; BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC.

CRA exporter audits require production of the full documentation bundle; missing or inconsistent proof results in denial of zero-rating, reassessment of 5% GST with prescribed compound daily interest, recovery of input tax credits, and extension of the normal four-year reassessment period to seven years for misrepresentation. Knowingly false statements or gross negligence attract a 25% GST/HST penalty; wilful evasion is prosecutable with fines from 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to two years. Canadian law does not prescribe a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is a documentation-control benchmark, and delayed inward remittance beyond 180 days weakens evidentiary correlation and invites clawback. False BC PST declarations also attract provincial penalties and reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and affords complete convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control permits, no capital-import registration gates, no quantitative withdrawal limits, and no convertibility quotas applicable to a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation. Profit and liquidation remittances from a BC ULC may be executed freely through any Schedule I authorized bank, subject only to Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding (10%-25% depending on treaty entitlement) and to sanctions enforcement under the United Nations Act, Special Economic Measures Act, and Criminal Code Part II.1. The substantive operational challenge for this IoT retail file is therefore not legal convertibility, but bank-level KYB classification and the need to establish a clean correspondent clearing trail for USD, CNY, and GBP settlement flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes zero statutory export-proceeds surrender obligations, zero mandatory conversion ratios, zero repatriation timelines, and zero central-bank deposit requirements. Exporters may retain foreign-currency balances offshore or onshore without restriction. The only thresholds are: FINTRAC reporting for single currency transactions equal to or above CAD 10,000; CBSA export reporting (Canadian Export Reporting System/Customs Automated Data Exchange) for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 or requiring an export permit; and six-year record retention under the Customs Act. Recommended treasury policy for ISIC 4791 retail flows: sweep USD settlement proceeds from Shopify Payments and Stripe into the CAD operating account within one business day to eliminate FX remeasurement volatility in the ULC's functional currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - monetary and note-issuing authority under the Bank of Canada Act. Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange-control commission; the FX-related regulatory perimeter is distributed across Global Affairs Canada (autonomous sanctions and dual-use export controls), FINTRAC (PCMLTFA large-transaction and electronic funds transfer reporting), and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of clearing and settlement participants.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium - auxiliary settlement channel only; not a substitute for a Canadian chartered bank account. Canada does not issue EU/UK-style Electronic Money Institution licences. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Intergiro) operate either as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA or as unlicensed foreign payment-service providers. For a BC ULC, an offshore EMI is acceptable for localized supplier disbursements and marketplace collection wallets, but carries: (i) no CDIC deposit insurance; (ii) floating-balance counterparty risk; (iii) emerging Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration expectations for payment service providers in Canada; and (iv) elevated bank risk-appetite flags when EMI float exceeds roughly 30% of monthly turnover. The recommended structure is Shopify Payments and Stripe settling directly to an RBC or BMO CAD account, with EMIs restricted to sub-CAD 50,000 disbursement floats swept within 24 hours.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route each shareholder capital injection as a single SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.009) from the parent institution's global intermediary, using the structured remittance narrative: EQUITY CONTRIBUTION / BC ULC REGISTRY [number] / ISSUANCE OF CLASS COMMON / NON-RESIDENT INVESTMENT. Canada requires no pre-registration or central-bank approval for the inflow, but the receiving Schedule I bank will internally classify the credit as capital payment; attach the board resolution, updated share certificate, and, where shares are issued at a premium, a contemporaneous valuation. Request that credit be posted to a segregated Capital Escrow Account, with drawdown only upon operating-account requisition, to prevent commingling of unallocated injections. Any wire at or above CAD 10,000 triggers automated FINTRAC reporting; screen the remitter, beneficiary, and all intermediary banks against Global Affairs Canada's Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List and UN Security Council sanctions before dispatch. CAD-denominated credits settle same-day through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value payment system.Two complementary clearing rails support the ISIC 4791 IoT retail supply chain: (1) SWIFT MT103 for trade payables, logistics, licensing, and low-value supplier settlements under USD 100,000, cleared through RBC/BMO USD correspondent networks within one to two business days; and (2) irrevocable, confirmed commercial Letters of Credit issued by BMO or RBC trade finance for Shenzhen/Guangdong hardware batches above USD 100,000, payable at 30-90 days against marine bills of lading, packing lists, and supplier inspection certificates. Domestic consumer-market settlements occur via the card rails operated by Shopify Payments and Stripe, with batch payouts to the ULC's CAD settlement account typically T+2; chargebacks and consumer refunds must be executed exclusively through the original processor rails to preserve a continuous audit trail. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium - auxiliary settlement channel only; not a substitute for a Canadian chartered bank account. Canada does not issue EU/UK-style Electronic Money Institution licences. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Intergiro) operate either as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA or as unlicensed foreign payment-service providers. For a BC ULC, an offshore EMI is acceptable for localized supplier disbursements and marketplace collection wallets, but carries: (i) no CDIC deposit insurance; (ii) floating-balance counterparty risk; (iii) emerging Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration expectations for payment service providers in Canada; and (iv) elevated bank risk-appetite flags when EMI float exceeds roughly 30% of monthly turnover. The recommended structure is Shopify Payments and Stripe settling directly to an RBC or BMO CAD account, with EMIs restricted to sub-CAD 50,000 disbursement floats swept within 24 hours.
