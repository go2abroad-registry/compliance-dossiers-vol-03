Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal income tax, payroll, and GST/HST; British Columbia Ministry of Finance for provincial corporate and sales tax matters. Primary portals: CRA My Account and BC Tax Portal.

E-government filing portal

Canadian-resident Limited Partnerships obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA via the My Account portal or by submitting Form RC59. Non-resident entities register for a GST/HST number if carrying on business in Canada through the CRA online portal or by mailing Form GST400.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Canadian-resident Limited Partnerships obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA via the My Account portal or by submitting Form RC59. Non-resident entities register for a GST/HST number if carrying on business in Canada through the CRA online portal or by mailing Form GST400.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada qualify for zero-rated GST. Offshore services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated provided the service is not consumed in Canada and no tax is charged in Canada.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible floating currency. Canada imposes no general foreign-exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittances by non-resident limited partners are permitted without foreign-exchange approval, subject only to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), transfer pricing compliance, and standard tax information reporting. No material capital account convertibility hurdles exist for a British Columbia limited partnership engaged in cross-border anti-dumping trade defense consulting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not mandate surrender or conversion of export proceeds into CAD. Residents may maintain foreign-currency accounts. However, exporters must retain full documentation of export transactions for Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) audits, customs verification under the Customs Act, and FINTRAC monetary reporting. Large cross-border wire transfers may trigger FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting obligations, but there is no statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with delegated foreign exchange administration, monetary policy, and financial stability oversight; anti-money laundering supervision is coordinated through the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMI providers are generally not regulated in Canada as deposit-taking institutions and may not offer Canadian-denominated clearing or CAD settlement. Non-resident fintech EMIs seeking to serve a BC limited partnership must either obtain registration as Money Service Businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, or operate exclusively through a Canadian Schedule I bank as the underlying clearing sponsor. Operational and AML compliance friction is substantial, and such entities are not viable substitutes for a Canadian domestic bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident investors and client retainers must route via SWIFT wire transfers using purpose codes aligned with Canadian banking practice, such as Code 10020 (Capital investment) or Code 10014 (Legal and consulting fees), depending on transaction substance. Banks require a detailed remittance note, supporting underlying agreements, and evidence of the LP's business purpose. Inbound FX conversion from USD, EUR, or CNY to CAD is executed via the custodian Canadian bank at prevailing market rates with a standard spot contract. Funds may be credited to a segregated Capital Escrow Account or Operational Account after successful KYC and OFAC/FINTRAC sanctions screening verification.Outbound trade-defense settlements, regulatory filing fees, expert witness payments, and correspondent counsel fees are cleared via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for cross-border payments, and through the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Real-Time Rail for domestic CAD transactions. For high-value transactions, Canadian banks issue SWIFT MT202 covers for USD clearing via US correspondent banks or use CLEAR (formerly CLS) for dual-currency settlements. Commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) are available through the Canadian issuing bank for client-side security deposits and indemnity obligations; these are advised through foreign correspondent legal or banking channels. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services and CAD 5 million for goods (per transaction or series of transactions) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation under CRA's transfer pricing rules (Section 247 of the Income Tax Act).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) which requires genuine economic substance and business purpose. For partnerships, the CRA may disregard transactions lacking commercial substance. Additionally, the partnership must be carried on with a view to profit; otherwise, it may be considered a shell entity and subject to reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s. 227.1) and for penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and seizure of personal assets may apply.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny tax benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties of 25% of the denied benefit. The partnership may be dissolved or disregarded.

Director personal liability: General partners may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if the partnership is deemed a sham; courts may pierce the corporate veil.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations and CAD 500,000 for individuals; criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly or negligently fail to comply; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation inadequate), and apply interest on reassessed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to non-compliant transfer pricing; personal liability for gross negligence penalties (up to 50% of the tax payable).
