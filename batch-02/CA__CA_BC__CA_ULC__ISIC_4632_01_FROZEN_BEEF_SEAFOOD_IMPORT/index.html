Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online portal; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs and import/export commercial systems; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on commercial activity or importing goods into Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the Canada Revenue Agency. The BN is integrated with GST/HST program account registration. Non-residents may register through CRA's Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1; GSTM (GST/HST Internet Filing) may be used. An authorized Canadian representative may be required if no Canadian address exists. For customs importation, the BN is required by CBSA for release, and a Customs Identification Number (CIN) may be issued by CBSA. If BC PST applies to taxable sales in British Columbia, separate registration is required with the BC Ministry of Finance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity carrying on commercial activity or importing goods into Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the Canada Revenue Agency. The BN is integrated with GST/HST program account registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

For Canadian domestic federal and provincial income tax purposes, a BC Unlimited Liability Corporation is a corporation taxable at the entity level; there is no domestic check-the-box or S-corporation election. For US federal tax classification, the BC ULC is an eligible entity, and Form 8832 may be filed to elect partnership or disregarded-entity status, creating hybrid entity planning opportunities. Canada's hybrid mismatch regimes (Income Tax Act sections 18.2, 18.21, 18.4, 18.5) may restrict deduction/non-inclusion benefits. No formal corporate-class election is available under CRA law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a person must register for GST/HST if it makes taxable supplies other than zero-rated in Canada and is not a small supplier, i.e., total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated) exceed CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter within the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; importers below this threshold may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate supplies of frozen meat and pelagic fish exported from Canada where the supplier transfers ownership to a non-resident and the goods are exported as part of the supply; supplies of services performed on export goods for a non-resident are also zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For goods exported by the registrant itself, zero-rating requires evidence of physical exportation to a destination outside Canada within a reasonable time.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment transfer evidence
  • Commercial invoice with complete seller/buyer and destination details
  • Packing list and documentary proof of export (e.g., weight/temperature manifest for frozen cargo)
  • CBSA release documentation or eManifest confirmation for imported raw product, if applicable
  • Supplier's GST/HST invoice and Form GST11 or rebate/ITC application records

Canada has no mandatory real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or fiscal receipt clearance mandate at the federal level. GST/HST invoices must include the supplier BN, date, description, amount, and tax; paper or electronic format is acceptable. For customs, CBSA mandates electronic submission of import/export declarations and e-Manifest (ACI) data through the CBSA Secure Portal or approved service providers; physical release of frozen beef and seafood consignments requires electronic customs clearance via the Integrated Import Declaration system.

CRA can audit zero-rating and input tax credit claims; where export substantiation is deficient, CRA will deny zero-rating, reassess GST at 5% (or HST rate if applicable), claw back refunds and ITCs, and charge interest from the due date. Penalties under Excise Tax Act section 280 may be imposed for false or misleading claims, including 50% of the refund/rebate claimed or greater of CAD 5,000 and 50% of the net tax intentionally understated. Gross negligence and tax evasion can also lead to Income Tax Act penalties, prosecution, and criminal fines; directors of a ULC may be jointly and severally liable for excise tax penalties due to unlimited liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both capital and current account. Canada maintains no exchange control legislation or currency restrictions. Legal tender conversion is unrestricted, and profit distributions may be remitted without prior regulatory approval. Hurdles are limited to tax compliance, specifically withholding tax on deemed dividends from a BC ULC, and anti-money laundering due diligence. For a BC unlimited liability corporation taxed as a pass-through entity, careful planning is required to prevent unintended taxable withholding on remittances to foreign shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion, and no time-limited repatriation requirement. Export receipts may be retained in CAD or any foreign currency. Mandatory declarations and record-keeping arise under the Customs Act and FINTRAC reporting obligations for cross-border funds transfers if amounts equal or exceed CAD 10,000. The BC ULC should maintain all bank statements and proof of settlement to support tax reporting and sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); for payments system oversight, Bank of Canada under the Canadian Payments Act, with FINTRAC exercising surveillance for cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 10,000.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are viable only as non-bank payment service providers within limited scope. They cannot offer deposit-taking services or CAD clearing directly as Canadian banks, but they may operate in Canada as money services businesses if registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada and remain compliant with FINTRAC's MSB framework. For BC ULC treasury purposes, offshore EMIs are rated Medium-Low due to core limitations: no Canadian chequing or settlement accounts, no access to Lynx or the Automated Clearing Settlement System, higher friction for receiving bank wires, and elevated compliance scrutiny by third-party correspondent banks. They may serve as supplementary collections vehicles but not as the primary treasury bank.

Capital injections into the BC ULC must be routed via secure SWIFT MT103 wires from the authorized parent or shareholder account into the bank's Canadian clearing system. Use strict corporate wire instructions in the shareholder resolution. The beneficiary account must be in the legal name 'CA__CA_BC__CA_ULC__ISIC_4632_01_FROZEN_BEEF_SEAFOOD_IMPORT' as registered. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined Canadian statutory headline — federal 15% and BC provincial 12% (no small-business deduction availability because a non-resident-owned ULC is not a CCPC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder of a Canadian-resident corporation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed by the later of 6 months after the fiscal period-end and the date on which Form T2 is due; balance-due taxes must be paid within 2 months of fiscal year-end (non-CCPC), extending to 3 months for a CCPC. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold — every controlled cross-border transaction requires contemporaneous TP documentation under s. 247(4) IC 87-2R; the Master/Local-File-style documentation (incl. CbCR declaration) is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ C$750,000,000 (CbCR).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no Caribbean-style ESR statute, but substance is central to (i) corporate residence ('central mind and management' test), (ii) GAAR (s. 245) — a ULC with no employees, premises, or business decision-making in Canada is an audit target, and (iii) treaty LOB — a pure conduit ULC may be denied US treaty benefits. The ULC's unlimited-liability feature heightens the risks: if the CRA recharacterizes the entity as a conduit/agent, the foreign shareholder becomes directly collectible.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Indictable offence under ITA s. 239(1): fine of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years; summary conviction alternative: fine up to C$25,000 and/or 6 months. Civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2): 50% of the full understated tax; refunds denied and reassessment window extended to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct the evasion qualify as 'controlling minds' and are charged as parties; on conviction they face imprisonment, asset confiscation, and travel bans as bail/parole conditions. In a BC ULC, the corporate veil is pierced by statute — every shareholder is jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax liabilities; the CRA may seize the foreign parent's Canadian receivables and bank balances directly.
