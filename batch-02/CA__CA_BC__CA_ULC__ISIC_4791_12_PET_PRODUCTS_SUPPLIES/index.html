Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); BC Ministry of Finance – Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration via BCeID (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst/register)

E-government filing portal

For a British Columbia ULC, after BC incorporation under the Business Corporations Act, apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) and a corporate income tax program account (RC0001). Registration is completed online through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). The ULC must also register for GST/HST (using the same BN with an RT program account) if annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 or voluntarily. For BC PST, registration with the BC Ministry of Finance is mandatory prior to making retail sales in British Columbia; there is no PST registration threshold. The BN serves as the statutory TIN for federal and provincial tax filings. Non-resident shareholders/directors are permitted; no minimum capital requirement applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A BC ULC is a corporation for Canadian domestic income tax purposes (ITA 89(1)); no domestic election exists to treat it as a partnership or branch. However, for US federal tax purposes, the ULC is eligible for a check-the-box election (Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3) to be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership, creating hybrid mismatch potential. Canada’s hybrid mismatch rules (ITA 94B–94K) apply when the ULC is fiscally transparent in another jurisdiction. A ULC may also make elections under ETA section 150 for related corporations to treat certain supplies as non-taxable, and under ITA subsection 256(1) for loss utilization across group members.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual global taxable supplies (tangible personal property and services) for mandatory GST/HST registration; no financial threshold for BC PST registration — any person making retail sales in BC must register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for GST applies to supplies of goods that are exported from Canada (s. 1, Part V, Schedule VI, ETA) and to certain services provided to non-residents that meet the 'exported service' tests (ss. 7 and 10, Schedule VI). For e-commerce physical shipments, the supplier must export the goods within 60 days of the supply and retain documentary proof that the goods were delivered to a destination outside Canada and that the purchaser is a non-resident. Services supplied in respect of goods exported outside Canada may also qualify. BC PST does not apply where tangible personal property is delivered at a point outside British Columbia, provided the seller maintains shipping and export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (G4) filed via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) or authorized service provider
  • Bill of Lading (through or direct waybill showing destination outside Canada)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment record evidencing settlement of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice with export declaration number, INCOTERMS, and description of goods
  • Proof of Export (carrier’s signed attestation or CBSA coded export report)

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing pre-approval. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed particulars (ETA s. 223) and may be issued on paper or electronically. CRA supports optional e-invoicing via the Canadian PEPPOL framework; no government clearance of invoices is required.

The CRA may deny zero-rating if the supplier fails to produce required export evidence on demand, and may reassess the supply as taxable, resulting in GST liability, interest, and penalties. Under ETA s. 280(1) and s. 281, amounts may be recovered as GST owing; gross negligence penalties of up to 50% of the amount under-assessed can apply (ETA s. 285); criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 may result in fines and imprisonment. BC PST audits may not allow credit for uncollected PST; the Provincial Sales Tax Act permits penalties up to 10% of the tax due plus interest, and fraud penalties up to 100% of the tax. Reassessment periods generally extend 4 years (7+ years for fraud or misrepresentation).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully free-floating currency regime with unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. The Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and the Income Tax Act impose no licensing, prior approval, or quantitative restriction on purchase or sale of foreign currency or on profit remittance by a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (BC ULC) to non-resident shareholders. Dividends and capital distributions are freely repatriated subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, potentially reduced under the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention to 15% or 5% with proper eligibility) and standard anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Investment Canada Act applies only where the transaction constitutes foreign investment of a reviewable threshold and is otherwise a non-financial regulatory hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriating export receipts. A BC ULC engaged in cross-border e-commerce of pet supplies may maintain foreign currency revenue in Canadian Schedule I bank foreign currency accounts or USD clearing accounts indefinitely. Reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, CBSA export reporting for commercial goods leaving Canada, and Statistics Canada Balance of Payments survey obligations when applicable. No Bank of Canada authorization is required for retention or conversion of USD, CNY, or other trade settlement currencies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no centralized foreign exchange control authority; the Bank of Canada operates the national monetary framework and oversees the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) through Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for collection and payment processing, Low for primary deposit taking. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, and Stripe Treasury are not authorized to accept Canadian dollar deposits from Canadian residents without a deposit-taking license under the Bank Act. They may operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) in Canada only upon FINTRAC registration under the PCMLTFA. For the BC ULC, the legally compliant architecture is to use a Schedule I chartered bank as the master treasury account and settlement ledger, with EMIs used solely as disbursement and receivables aggregation rails. The ULC should ensure that Canadian dollar stored value is not held downstream of the EMI as that may constitute illegal deposit taking; USD-denominated settlement balances held at an EMI are generally permissible when the EMI operates through licensed US money transmitter entities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection for the BC ULC should be executed as a share subscription wire in USD or CAD from the non-resident shareholder. The preferred rail is SWIFT MT103 for transfers below CAD 500,000, cleared through the beneficiary bank's foreign correspondent. For larger injections, SWIFT MT202 COV with a clear chain narrative is acceptable. Recommended receiving bank: RBC Royal Bank, Vancouver Main Branch, SWIFT BIC ROYCCAT2; beneficiary account number to be opened as an Operational Account. The beneficiary reference must explicitly state 'CA-ULC Capital Injection - Share Premium under BC Business Corporations Act' and include the ULC's Business Number. Payment purpose code must be aligned with Canadian Balance of Payments reporting, using code 'P52' or narrative 'equity capital contribution'. Verification requirements: source of funds declaration, certified copy of the share subscription resolution, and confirmation that proceeds are credited to share capital and share premium accounts. For amounts above CAD 10,000, the bank is obligated to file an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in annual gross revenue thresholds for mandatory contemporaneous documentation per CRA transfer pricing rules: Master File required for multinational groups with aggregate related-party revenue exceeding CAD 1 billion and Canadian revenue exceeding CAD 50 million; Local File required for Canadian-resident taxpayers with related-party cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 (exclusive of certain exempt finance costs). BC does not impose a separate provincial transfer pricing documentation threshold; CRA TP documentation is the relevant standard.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE, but Canadian tax law enforces economic substance through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, the transfer pricing rules under section 247, and the CRA's substance-based approach to treaty shopping and permanent establishment attribution. A BC ULC that operates as a pass-through or conduit for e-commerce revenue without real office, employees, inventory, or decision-making functions in BC may be recharacterized by CRA; the Canada-U.S. DTA's limitation of benefits and the OECD BEPS Action 6 treaty anti-abuse rules also require substantive business purpose, real economic activity, and beneficial ownership for entitlement to treaty withholding rate reductions. Foreign-owned BC ULCs must maintain governance records proving that board meetings, inventory management, payment reconciliation, and strategic decisions occur in Canada, and contractual risk cannot be shifted offshore without compensating functions, assets, and risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Canadian Income Tax Act, tax evasion is punishable on summary conviction by a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years; on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years plus fines up to 200% of the tax evaded. CRA civil penalties include gross negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax and misrepresentation penalties with daily interest accrual. HST/GST evasion separately triggers comparable criminal liability under the Excise Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a BC ULC who authorize or acquiesce to tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid taxes, interest, and penalties under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act; CRA can assess director liability certificates and register them with the Federal Court. The unlimited liability feature of the ULC removes the statutory corporate veil for all shareholders, meaning shareholders and directors may be personally pursued for the entire tax debt. Criminal prosecution can result in travel bans, extradition treaties being invoked, forfeiture orders, and imprisonment of responsible executives.
Shell Company / Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the transaction, reassess the ULC as having a permanent establishment offshore, and impose transfer pricing adjustments with 10% gross negligence penalties. The BC Business Corporations Act imposes fines for failure to maintain a transparency register of up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years for contravention. A ULC that is a foreign-controlled private company under the Income Tax Act may lose the small business deduction on disallowance; Canada-U.S. DTA limitation on benefits provisions can render the entity ineligible for all treaty relief, resulting in full 25% withholding and recapture of previously refunded taxes.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and shadow directors are personally liable for false declarations, non-disclosure of beneficial ownership, and aiding tax evasion; shareholders of a ULC cannot rely on limited liability, and personal assets may be attached under creditor and tax collection proceedings. Directors may be subject to travel bans and denial of departure under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act where criminal investigation or extradition is pending.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), failure to report a suspicious transaction or large cash transaction triggers administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for corporations and up to CAD 250,000 for individuals; unregistered MSB activity carries criminal penalties of up to CAD 500,000 or imprisonment of up to 5 years upon indictment. The Criminal Code of Canada permits seizure, restraint, and forfeiture of property derived from unlawful activity. FINTRAC compliance deficiencies attract penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Corporate directors, officers, and compliance officers are personally liable for failure to implement an AML compliance program, file suspicious transaction reports (STRs), or maintain third-party due diligence records; personal fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years apply. Bank of Canada and BC Securities Commission enforcement may ban individuals from acting as directors of any corporation in Canada, issue travel bans, and trigger extradition proceedings under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA transfer pricing adjustments apply at the full 27% combined corporate rate plus applicable provincial allocation; gross negligence or serious misrepresentation penalties under subsection 247(3) for valuations or documentation failures are charged at 10% of the adjusted amount where the net income adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue. Interest and penalties compound until paid. Reassessed years can be reopened indefinitely if the taxpayer has committed misrepresentation or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign certificates approving tax returns with false transfer pricing positions face joint and several liability for the reassessed tax and penalties; audit partners and tax preparers may face professional discipline. Under the Canada-U.S. DTA mutual agreement procedure (MAP), competent authorities may deny dual-resident claims; criminal referral for transfer pricing fraud can expose directors and shareholders of a ULC to personal asset forfeiture and imprisonment.
HST/GST and Payroll Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA penalty for failure to register for GST/HST is the greater of CAD 500 and 1% of the GST/HST payable on taxable supplies; section 280 of the Excise Tax Act adds a penalty of 6% plus 1% per month up to 12%. Wilful failure to remit payroll source deductions carries criminal penalties of up to 200% of the amount not remitted and imprisonment up to 2 years on summary conviction. BC PST evasion is punishable under the Provincial Sales Tax Act with a penalty equal to 200% of the tax evaded, and unregistered PST activity at the BC level carries daily fines.

Director personal liability: Directors of a BC ULC are personally liable for unremitted GST/HST under section 323 of the Excise Tax Act and unremitted payroll source deductions under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act, without the protection of corporate limited liability in an unlimited liability company. For the BC employer health tax, directors are liable for unpaid tax, interest, and penalties. Travel bans may be issued for failure to appear at CRA collection interviews, and imprisonment is available for contempt of court or criminal non-remittance.
