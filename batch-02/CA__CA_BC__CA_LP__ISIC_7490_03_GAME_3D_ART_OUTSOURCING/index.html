Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal income tax and GST/HST administration; online portals: My Business Account (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE, and Represent a Client. For British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST): B.C. Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, via BC TaxOnline / eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A British Columbia limited partnership is fiscally transparent and does not itself hold a Tax Identification Number; each partner holds or must obtain a Canadian TIN (Social Insurance Number for individuals, Business Number program account for corporations/trusts). The partnership itself must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) for GST/HST and payroll/potential corporate accounts by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' through MyBA or by mail; the general partner executes the application and lists all partners, including non-resident corporate and individual partners (with foreign tax identification numbers, jurisdiction of residence, and address). If the LP carries on taxable activity in British Columbia beyond the small-seller threshold, a separate BC PST account is required via eTaxBC. Non-resident partners without Canadian residency status may additionally need a CRA non-resident account for withholding and treaty relief documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — B.C. limited partnerships are fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 96; income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to partners per the partnership agreement, and the LP files only an information return (T5013, with supplementaries as applicable). For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is nonetheless a separate 'person' under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 272(1) and must register if it makes taxable supplies in Canada. Provincial territorial scope: B.C. PST applies to certain consumer and business purchases but generally not to exported services.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a U.S.-style check-the-box election; partnership transparency is mandatory and non-elective. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) ITA s. 96(3), allowing partners to elect a specified fiscal-period basis for computing partnership income; (ii) ETA s. 156, available to related registered persons for deemed-taxable supplies (generally unavailable to an arm's-length BPO); (iii) ETA s. 179(?) for certain financial services elections, not applicable here. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3, 20.2) operate automatically without elective relief; treaty-shopping and conduit arrangements are constrained by the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) in the preceding four calendar quarters, measured on a 12-month trailing basis; once exceeded, mandatory registration applies unless all supplies are exclusively zero-rated (discretionary registration remains available). PST: B.C. small-seller exemption threshold of C$10,000 in annual worldwide taxable sales; the exemption is not available for certain listed services, and a person required to register for GST/HST and making taxable sales in B.C. must separately hold a PST vendor account.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Outsourced 3D sculpting, character rigging, and digital game-art services supplied to a non-resident game publisher qualify for zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI Part V, s. 7 where (i) the recipient is a non-resident for GST/HST purposes, (ii) the non-resident recipient is not registered under Part IX, and (iii) the service neither relates to real property situated in Canada, nor to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time performed, nor is supplied to an individual present in Canada at the time the service is rendered. Digital asset deliverables conveyed electronically with IP assignment/licencing are also within the zero-rated 'intangible personal property' rules of Schedule VI Part V, provided the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is not attributable to Canadian use. Place-of-supply rules under ETA s. 142 deem such services intra-territorially supplied outside Canada.

  • Executed master service agreement / statement of work identifying the Canadian LP as supplier, the non-resident publisher as recipient, deliverable milestones, IP ownership, and consideration expressed in CAD or foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and digital-delivery evidence (digital asset handoff manifests, file-transfer logs, repository/ticket records)
  • Proof of non-resident recipient status (foreign certificate of incorporation or residency certificate, sworn statement that the recipient is not registered for GST/HST)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 / foreign-currency advice) and Bank of Canada exchange-rate conversion records for funds received from outside Canada
  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration (B13A / G13) and Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — required only where physical media or tangible deliverables are shipped; not required for purely electronic delivery
  • CRA T5013 partnership information return and partner allocation schedules supporting the export revenue classification

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime. CRA operates a self-assessment, invoice-based control system; e-invoicing is permitted and encouraged through Peppol BIS 3.0 / the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework but remains voluntary. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE / Electronic Filestone) by registrants whose gross annual revenue exceeds C$1M; non-compliance attracts penalties under ETA s. 280.1. The LP should maintain digital records and reproduce any original paper invoice on request; all records must be retained for six years.

CRA may reassess out-of-time (up to ten years) where the exporter misrepresents facts, denies zero-rating, and assesses 5% GST plus, where applicable, 7% B.C. PST on the full invoice amount, together with compound daily interest (ETA s. 280) and penalties for late or deficient registration and filing (ETA ss. 280.1 and 280.2). False statements, fictitious documents, or wilful participation in tax evasion trigger civil gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of the understated tax (ITA s. 163.2; ETA s. 285) and criminal prosecution (ITA s. 239; ETA s. 327) carrying fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be avoided and imprisonment. Zero-rating claims premised on circular or round-tripping arrangements may be repelled under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) with full clawback. Although Canada has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the 180-day settlement convention is a best practice; CRA transfer-pricing review may also impute arm's-length consideration where revenue or pricing is misstated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency and no general exchange control legislation restricts inward or outward capital movements. Profit remittance is unrestricted, but for a British Columbia limited partnership, business income is taxed in the hands of partners; distributions to non-resident partners may trigger Part XIII withholding tax under Income Tax Act s.212(13.1), subject to reduction under the Canada-US tax treaty where applicable, with relief claims supported by Forms NR301/NR302/NR303. No prior Bank of Canada or government approval is required for any remittance, USD account maintenance, or conversion transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A BC LP may lawfully retain USD or other foreign currency export proceeds in designated foreign-currency bank accounts, convert at commercial rates at its discretion, and repatriate funds freely. However, cross-border transactions are subject to the International Transaction Reporting System (ITRS) administered by Statistics Canada and the Bank of Canada, requiring statistical classification and reporting for all international wire transfers when solicited by the authorities or when bank threshold reporting applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC), acting jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF compliance. No dedicated foreign exchange control agency exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise and Payoneer may serve as supplementary collection and low-value disbursement rails, but in Canada they are regulated as money services businesses (MSBs) and must be registered with FINTRAC. They do not provide CDIC-insured deposits, direct Lynx RTGS clearing, correspondent-bank US clearing, or credit facilities. For an ISIC 7490 art-outsourcing LP, an EMI is acceptable only for small client payments and must never replace a Schedule I bank account as the principal treasury relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital injections must arrive as SWIFT MT103 credit transfers into the LP's designated CAD or USD operational account. The wire message must carry an explicit purpose description: 'Partner capital contribution - ISIC 7490 game 3D art outsourcing.' The bank must classify the flow using its BPM6/ITRS financial-account purpose code for direct investment equity capital, not as goods or services revenue, and the LP must supply the BC registration certificate and partner KYC to support that classification. Client receipts for 3D art services must be coded under the computer and information services cluster (ITRS purpose family 231/232) to avoid misclassification as merchandise export proceeds. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (flow-through entity; partners taxed at their respective marginal rates)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (for distributions to non-resident partners, treated as business income; not dividends per se, but subject to Part XIII withholding on the partner's share of Canadian-source income)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for goods; CAD 500,000 for services; CAD 250,000 for intangibles (transactions with non-arm's length non-residents)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to disregard transactions lacking economic substance. Partnerships must demonstrate genuine business purpose and substance to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they participated in the evasion; civil restitution and travel bans may be imposed.
Failure to File T5013

Statutory sanction: Penalty of $25 per day per partner, up to $2,500 per partner; additional penalties for failure to provide information.

Director personal liability: Partners who are also directors may be personally liable for the penalties if they were responsible for filing.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties up to 10% of the net adjustment, and apply interest on reassessments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing mispricing.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR may deny tax benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the tax benefit denied if they were involved in the arrangement.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, fines up to CAD 2 million for individuals and CAD 10 million for entities; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for fines and imprisonment if they knowingly fail to comply.
