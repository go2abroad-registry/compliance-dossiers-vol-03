Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)/Agence du revenu du Canada - federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administration; official portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). British Columbia Ministry of Finance - provincial corporate income tax and PST administration; official portal: eTaxBC (https://www.etax.gov.bc.ca). BC corporate income tax is collected by the CRA on behalf of BC under a federal-provincial collection agreement; BC PST is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the BC ULC through BC Corporate Online/OneStop BC Registry; no minimum paid-up capital, non-resident (including US) shareholders permitted, name must include 'Unlimited Liability Company' or 'ULC'. Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 within 21 days of incorporation; the BN is the master statutory tax identifier and automatically activates federal corporate income tax account. Step 3: Register the GST/HST program account (RT) through BRO/RC1; registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters (aggregated with associated Canadian registrants under ETA s. 148(2)); voluntary registration is recommended for an export manufacturer to claim input tax credits (ITCs). Step 4: Register for BC PST vendor account via eTaxBC using Form FIN 418 if BC taxable retail sales exceed CAD 10,000 over 12 months; register for BC corporate income tax (automatic with BN). Step 5: Register with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for an import/export program account (RM/RS) or appoint a licensed customs broker; non-resident applicants must provide a Canadian registered agent/contact address and complete CRA Form RC459 where directors are non-residents. No economic substance declaration is required at registration, but CRA anti-avoidance and OECD transfer pricing documentation will apply.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A BC ULC is a resident Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act (ITA), subject to CRA entity-level taxation on worldwide income: federal Part I general rate 15% (38% less 10% abatement and 13% general rate reduction) plus BC provincial general corporate rate 12% = combined 27% on active business income; the small business deduction (federal 9% + BC 2% on first CAD 500,000 of active business income) is unavailable because a ULC controlled by a non-resident US shareholder is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC). Canada taxes on a residence/territorial basis for foreign affiliates only through the s. 113 foreign affiliate exemption and foreign tax credit mechanisms; no tax-exempt zone applies. For US federal tax purposes only, the ULC is 'Pass_Through_Eligible': a wholly owned ULC is a disregarded entity by default for US tax unless the US shareholder files an election; this US classification has no impact on Canadian entity-level taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A BC ULC is a resident Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act (ITA), subject to CRA entity-level taxation on worldwide income: federal Part I general rate 15% (38% less 10% abatement and 13% general rate reduction) plus BC provincial general corporate rate 12% = combined 27% on active business income; the small business deduction (federal 9% + BC 2% on first CAD 500,000 of active business income) is unavailable because a ULC controlled by a non-resident US shareholder is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC). Canada taxes on a residence/territorial basis for foreign affiliates only through the s. 113 foreign affiliate exemption and foreign tax credit mechanisms; no tax-exempt zone applies. For US federal tax purposes only, the ULC is 'Pass_Through_Eligible': a wholly owned ULC is a disregarded entity by default for US tax unless the US shareholder files an election; this US classification has no impact on Canadian entity-level taxation. Unlimited liability of shareholders does not alter corporate tax status in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' regime; entity classification follows corporate law. Applicable elections: (i) US shareholder IRS Form 8832 election to treat the ULC as disregarded or as a corporation for US federal tax, triggering OECD/G20 hybrid mismatch characterization under ITA s. 18.7 (imported mismatch) and s. 94B (hybrid instruments) where the ULC is treated as non-taxable by the US owner; (ii) ETA s. 273 election for closely related corporations to zero-rate intercompany GST/HST supplies; (iii) ITA s. 216 election on net rental income, if applicable; (iv) transfer pricing Advance Pricing Arrangement (APA) and Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP) filings with the CRA; (v) Part IV dividend refund and RDTOH elections for a ULC paying dividends; (vi) no CCPC small business election is available. Hybrid mismatch rules must be tested on any intercompany interest, royalty, or service fee paid to the US parent (ITA s. 247(2) arm's length standard).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters (aggregated with associated persons under ETA s. 148(2)); registration deadline is 30 days after the threshold is exceeded; the export-oriented ULC should register voluntarily to claim ITCs on inputs. BC PST: CAD 10,000 in taxable retail sales of tangible personal property delivered into BC over a 12-month period. BC corporate income tax: no registration threshold; a resident BC corporation is taxable from the first dollar of taxable income, and no payroll/capital taxes apply to this entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, Section 1 when the supplier ships the tangible personal property (microfluidic cartridges, immunoassay analyzers, reagents) directly out of Canada, or the recipient exports it as part of the sale, provided the goods leave Canada within 60 days of delivery and the supplier retains evidence satisfactory to the CRA. Services: zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, Section 7 when supplied to a non-resident recipient and the services (offshore installation, training, software licensing, remote calibration of IVD equipment) are performed entirely outside Canada or in respect of exported goods; CRA Policy P-130/P-208 governs warranty and 'software-as-a-service' edge cases. BC PST: no PST applies on goods shipped outside BC. Refund mechanics: the ULC claims ITCs on all GST-bearing inputs (clean-room consumables, assay reagents, capital analyzers, freight) through its net-filed GST/HST return; because export supplies are zero-rated, the ULC will generate recurring net GST refunds. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the value '0' below reflects that no such legal deadline exists - OECD transfer pricing and arm's length settlement timing apply instead, with imputed interest under ITA s. 247(2) for unreasonably deferred intercompany payments.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (G7/ECI eExport) for shipments over CAD 2,000; low-value CAED declaration for shipments under CAD 2,000, cross-referenced with an export transaction number
  • Commercial invoice/Canada Customs Invoice stating HS classification (9027.80 for analyzers; 3822.00 for reagents), declared value, exporter name, and BN
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air) or Cargo Control Document/waybill evidencing direct shipment out of Canada within 60 days of sale
  • Proof of shipment and delivery (airway bill, container manifest, courier tracking log) retained for the ETA s. 286 six-year period
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103/credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) or Medical Devices Regulations s. 37 export-only declaration, plus ISO 13485 quality certificate, for the IVD analyzer and microfluidic cartridge export shipments
  • Purchase order and sales contract referencing Incoterms (FOB/Ex-Works/CFR) and the non-resident buyer identity

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance in Canada: the CRA does not require continuous transaction controls, fiscal digital stamps, or API pre-clearance of invoices. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (Peppol BIS 3.0/Peppol PINT, launched 2023); a ULC exporting to EU/APAC customers may voluntarily adopt Peppol interoperability but is not legally required. Statutory record-keeping: ETA s. 286 requires retention of all invoices, customs documentation, and export evidence for six years; GST/HST invoices must state the supplier BN, date, description, GST amount, and rate (ETA ss. 169(4), 221(1)); BC PST invoices must state the PST registration number and collect 7% PST separately. Returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account; no CRA clearance of invoices occurs prior to filing.

CRA reassessment window is four years (ETA s. 298(1)); the limitation period is unlimited for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. A denied zero-rating triggers retrospective reassessment of 5% GST on the full export invoice value (plus PST where applicable) with compounded daily interest at the CRA prescribed rate (approximately 5% per annum). Gross negligence penalty under ETA s. 285: 50% of the tax sought to be evaded; false-statement penalties under ETA s. 280(1) range from CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 per false statement; criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 282 carries fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. Clawback mechanics: the CRA will deny corresponding ITCs on inputs attributable to the rejected export supply, assess interest on all affected historical returns, and escalate to a full exporter audit of G7/B13A declarations; CBSA may separately impose penalties under Customs Act s. 109 (up to CAD 25,000 or 50% of the value of goods) for false export declarations, and Health Canada may suspend or cancel the MDEL for misdescribed medical devices, halting future exports. Documentary failure alone (missing B13A or bill of lading) is sufficient to overturn zero-rating regardless of commercial good faith.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full current account convertibility for resident corporations. Capital account transactions are subject to prior notification or approval requirements administered by the Bank of Canada for non-resident investments exceeding prescribed thresholds. Profit remittances to shareholders are permitted without restriction for Canadian-resident entities provided all tax filings under the Income Tax Act are current and anti-money laundering reporting obligations are satisfied. Hurdles for profit repatriation are minimal for domestic ULCs but may include exchange control filings if funds originate from foreign sources.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds to the Bank of Canada is required. Exporters must remit proceeds through authorized foreign exchange dealers at prevailing market rates. Statutory timeline is immediate upon receipt of funds. No mandatory conversion ratios apply; full transaction documentation and timely reporting to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act are mandatory.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for offshore digital EMIs. Canadian regulatory framework under the Payment Card Networks Act and FINTRAC rules prohibits unlicensed offshore issuance and requires full compliance with domestic AML/KYC standards for any cross-border digital payment rails.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal charges under Income Tax Act; fines up to CAD 100,000+; imprisonment up to 5 years; corporation liable for 50% penalty on evaded tax

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for penalties and imprisonment if willful evasion proven; possible travel bans
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: 50% penalty on transfer pricing adjustments; administrative penalties up to CAD 250,000 per transaction; interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for due diligence failures; potential director disqualification
Late Filing or Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: CAD 100 per day late (maximum CAD 25,000); plus interest on unpaid tax; 50% penalty for non-filing

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for late filing penalties in cases of corporate non-compliance
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA recharacterization; penalties for sham transactions; potential criminal charges under Criminal Code; fines up to CAD 1,000,000

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors face civil restitution, imprisonment, and travel bans for fraudulent conduct
