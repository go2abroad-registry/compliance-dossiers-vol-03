Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST, income tax, and excise duties; portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST); portal: eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Use the joint federal-provincial OneStop Business Registry or CRA Business Registration Online (BRO). A BC limited partnership registers through its general partner: (1) obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account using Form RC1; (2) provide the partnership agreement and proof of the general partner's authority and Canadian nexus; (3) if the partnership sells taxable SaaS, pre-written software, or AI/API digital services to BC consumers, open a BC PST account through eTaxBC. Non-resident partnerships without a Canadian permanent establishment can obtain a CRA non-resident BN and may be required to appoint a Canadian representative for service of process and tax administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – A BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax: no entity-level tax; each partner reports its distributive share. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is a registrable person acting through the general partner, while the provincial sales tax treatment follows the underlying supply into British Columbia.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; partnership pass-through classification is mandatory under the Income Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4–18.7) apply to neutralize deductions from arrangements exploiting entity or instrument classification differences. For GST/HST, a registrant may elect the Simplified GST/HST method or Quick Method within prescribed turnover thresholds, but these are cash-flow calculation elections and do not alter entity classification. Non-resident limited partners may apply treaty tie-breaker rules for income tax only; GST/HST registration status remains determined by Canadian Excise Tax Act rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide supplies over the current four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); exceeding it triggers mandatory GST/HST registration. BC PST registration is required for taxable sales of pre-written software, SaaS, and specified digital products delivered to BC customers; the nominal small-supplier concept is applied by the BC Ministry of Finance with provincial discretion.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the AI/LLM API platform, supplies of API access to non-resident customers qualify as zero-rated commercial services exports under Excise Tax Act s. 21.1 if: (a) the recipient is a non-resident; (b) the service is not performed in Canada, or is delivered outside Canada; and (c) the non-resident does not use or consume the service in Canada. Zero-rating also applies to exported goods upon proof of export and to software or digital content provided electronically to non-residents where the use occurs outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) where physical goods are exported
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or equivalent transport document showing foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, API subscription contract, and statement of work identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Non-resident status evidence, such as a foreign tax residency certificate or equivalent documentation, with records demonstrating use outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate or payment trace evidencing settlement from outside Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime exists for GST/HST or BC PST. E-invoicing is voluntary; CRA accepts electronic invoices in standard commercial formats, including EDI/XML and Peppol-based interoperability, and offers API-based filing via GST/HST NETFILE and My Business Account. BC PST imposes no e-invoicing or fiscal clearance requirement.

CRA may reassess GST/HST net tax and claw back zero-rated refunds within four years, with no limitation period in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties under Excise Tax Act s. 285 include 50% of the amount claimed or improperly refunded, subject to a CAD 200 minimum, for false statements. Tax evasion prosecutions can result in fines up to CAD 200,000 or double the amount of tax evaded, plus imprisonment. BC PST audits may reassess retroactively and impose civil penalties and interest under the Provincial Sales Tax Act and the Tax Administration Act. Canada has no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day field is retained as a contractual and banking benchmark, not a CRA or BC enforcement requirement.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary commercial and financial transactions. Canada generally permits unrestricted conversion between Canadian dollars and foreign currencies and does not impose a general approval, registration, or repatriation requirement for foreign capital, shareholder funding, partnership contributions, service receipts, or business profits. Capital-account transactions, including acquisition or disposal of Canadian assets, cross-border loans, guarantees, and related-party funding, remain subject to ordinary Canadian tax, anti-money-laundering, sanctions, beneficial-ownership, securities, competition, foreign-investment, and financial-reporting rules. A British Columbia limited partnership may generally receive non-resident capital contributions and remit available cash to partners, subject to the partnership agreement, creditor protection, solvency, applicable withholding and information-reporting obligations, and any contractual restrictions imposed by the bank or payment provider. Canadian-source amounts paid or allocated to non-residents may attract withholding or partnership-related reporting depending on the recipient, payment type, treaty position, and applicable provisions of the Income Tax Act. Service revenue received in foreign currency may be converted at the entity's discretion; tax reporting must use appropriate Canadian-dollar conversion methodology and maintain auditable exchange-rate records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export or service receipts into Canadian dollars. An AI API platform may generally retain foreign-currency receipts in foreign-currency accounts or convert them when commercially appropriate. The business must retain contracts, invoices, usage records, settlement statements, and banking evidence sufficient to support revenue recognition, GST/HST treatment, income-tax reporting, transfer-pricing positions, and source-of-funds reviews. Banks and payment providers may impose contractual settlement, reserve, reconciliation, or currency-conversion requirements. Cross-border transactions may also trigger FINTRAC reporting, CRA information-reporting, Statistics Canada surveys, tax withholding, sanctions screening, and foreign-exchange transaction records, but these requirements do not constitute an export-proceeds surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing routine exchange controls, capital controls, mandatory export-receipt surrender, or general currency-conversion licensing. Relevant supervisory and compliance authorities may also include the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), depending on the transaction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection and payout services, subject to provider eligibility, underwriting, sanctions screening, reserve requirements, and geographic availability. Stripe Canada may be viable for card and alternative-payment acceptance if the limited partnership, beneficial owners, business model, website, refund terms, customer terms, data practices, and expected transaction profile satisfy onboarding requirements. An offshore electronic-money institution does not automatically provide a Canadian deposit account, Canadian payment-system access, or authority to conduct regulated payment activities in Canada. A payment service provider serving end users in Canada may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and Bank of Canada registration, operational-risk, safeguarding, reporting, and incident-management requirements once applicable. FINTRAC registration may be required where the provider conducts money-services-business activities, including dealing in virtual currency or remitting/transmitting funds. USDC is not Canadian legal tender or a bank deposit; acceptance, custody, conversion, and payout arrangements require separate contractual, AML, sanctions, consumer-protection, accounting, tax, and potentially securities or derivatives analysis. The entity should not represent USDC balances as insured deposits or guaranteed fiat funds.
