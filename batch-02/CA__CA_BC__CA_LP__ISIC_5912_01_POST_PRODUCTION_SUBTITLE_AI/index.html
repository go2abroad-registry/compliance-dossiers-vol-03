Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - My Business Account; British Columbia Ministry of Finance - Consumer Taxation Branch (eTaxBC)

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in British Columbia: (1) register extra-provincially with the BC Corporate Registry through OneStop BC to obtain the BC LP registration number; (2) apply for the Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online or Form RC1, selecting the Partnership, GST/HST, and applicable program account types; (3) register separately for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through the BC Taxpayer Service using the BN; the CRA BN with GST/HST and PST program accounts is the statutory tax identifier for federal and BC tax administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax purposes; each partner is liable on their share of income. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate 'person' and may be a registrant in its own name.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. The LP's status is governed by the Income Tax Act partnership definition. Available elections include: (a) income-sharing allocation election under ITA section 96; (b) GST/HST election under Excise Tax Act section 273 for one partner to supply on behalf of the partnership; (c) Canada-U.S. treaty 'fiscally transparent entity' certification for non-resident partners; (d) filing of prescribed Form T5013 partnership information return designating an electing partner for administrative purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (exclusive of zero-rated supplies) in the preceding four calendar quarters; a non-resident partnership exceeding this threshold while carrying on business in Canada must register. BC PST: no generic monetary threshold; registration is required as soon as taxable leases or sales of taxable services occur in BC in the course of business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under sections 21 to 23 of the Excise Tax Act, automated subtitle transcription and AI voice-clone licensing services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated where the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and the service or intangible property is not supplied for use in Canada and is not in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The exporter must maintain contemporaneous evidence that the foreign client has no Canadian nexus and that the intellectual property rights and dubbing/subtitle deliverables are exploited wholly outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA CADEX/CARM) for physical masters, hard drives, or media shipped abroad
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by a common carrier for physical exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/FX payment evidence from the non-resident client
  • Executed Statement of Work / License Agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and territorial scope
  • Non-resident declaration or GST/HST exemption certificate signed by the non-resident client
  • Zero-rating substantiation report including service delivery logs, IP exploitation records, and AI voice-clone usage scope

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime exists at the federal or BC level. GST/HST is self-assessed and remitted through periodic GST34 returns; BC PST is remitted through periodic provincial returns. E-invoicing is voluntary and aligned with the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard for business-to-government supply. Tangible media exports require electronic declaration through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform, with optional system-to-system API integration.

CRA may reassess and claw back zero-rated treatment where the documentary burden is not met. Penalties under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act for false statements include a gross negligence penalty equal to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded and a minimum penalty of CAD 1,000. Interest accrues at prescribed rates for deemed unpaid tax. Section 285 imposes penalties for failure to collect or remit. Criminal prosecution is possible for fraudulent zero-rating. Partners of the LP are jointly and severally liable for its GST/HST obligations under section 272 of the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Current account transactions, including profit remittances from Canadian operations to foreign owners, face no regulatory restrictions. Profit repatriation is unrestricted subject only to standard corporate tax compliance and FINTRAC anti-money laundering requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds or foreign exchange conversion requirements. Receipts from services or goods exports may be retained in foreign currency accounts without statutory timelines or conversion ratios. All FX transactions remain free of surrender mandates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability for cross-border electronic money instruments through regulated platforms such as Wise and Stripe. Legal constraints include mandatory FINTRAC registration for money services businesses, strict beneficial ownership verification, and compliance with Canada's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act. Offshore digital EMIs are permissible for non-resident limited partners provided full KYC and source-of-funds documentation is supplied.
