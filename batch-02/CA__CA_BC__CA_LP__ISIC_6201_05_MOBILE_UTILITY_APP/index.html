Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and Income Tax directorates; federal portal: Canada.ca / CRA My Business Account (GST/HST NETFILE); provincial consumption tax component: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administered separately via the BC Tax Portal.

E-government filing portal

Register the BC limited partnership under the BC Partnership Act (declaration accepted by BC Corporate Registry; LP number issued). Apply to CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) and a separate GST/HST account (RT prefix) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the Partnership option; non-resident applicants may use the CRA Business Registration telephone line or file electronically. Since 1 July 2021, non-resident suppliers of specified digital products (including mobile-app In-App Purchases) must use the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration route when their taxable supplies to Canadian recipients exceed CAD 30,000; registration is completed online through My Business Account or via Form RC1J. Where the partnership makes direct (non-marketplace) taxable sales into BC, obtain a BC PST vendor number from the Consumer Taxation Branch; for pure app-store distributions, Apple App Store and Google Play are treated as online-marketplace vendors for BC PST collection, generally relieving the LP of a BC PST registration. Thereafter, file GST/HST returns (Form GST34) on the applicable cycle and the federal partnership Information Return (T5013) allocating each partner their share of income, loss, and tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: Under the Income Tax Act (ss. 96–103), a BC limited partnership is a fiscally transparent flow-through vehicle — the LP computes income at the partnership level but bears no separate income tax; each general and limited partner (resident or non-resident) is taxed on their allocated share. For GST/HST purposes, the Excise Tax Act treats the partnership as a distinct person that registers and files in its own name separate from its members. Territorial scope: a non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source business income is taxable in Canada unless relieved by an applicable tax treaty; the LP itself is not a resident taxpayer for treaty purposes. The BC limited partnership is not located in a tax-exempt zone and enjoys no provincial income-tax holiday.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. Available elections: (i) GST/HST group registration election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) to treat qualifying closely related entities as a single registrant; (ii) partnership rollover elections under Income Tax Act section 98 for tax-deferred transfers of partnership property on admission, withdrawal, or continuation; (iii) partners may adopt an agreed partnership allocation, respected unless Income Tax Act section 103 anti-avoidance limits are offended; (iv) the hybrid mismatch deduction-denial rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4–18.7, OECD BEPS Action 2) constrain cross-border hybrid structures but cannot elect entity-level taxation for a Canadian LP — flow-through treatment is mandatory. No election exists to override the LP's GST/HST separate-person status or to shift PST obligations to the platform in direct-supply scenarios.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold on worldwide taxable supplies (trailing four calendar quarters or a single calendar quarter test, including supplies by associated persons). For non-resident suppliers of specified digital products (e.g., mobile app downloads and in-app functionality), the threshold is CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made to Canadian recipients in the preceding 12 months; once exceeded, simplified non-resident GST/HST registration is mandatory. BC PST: no general registration threshold for marketplace-mediated app-store sales because Apple and Google are deemed vendors; a developer making direct taxable digital-product sales into BC must register for PST from the first taxable sale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of intangible personal property (downloadable software, in-app licenses, and digital content) made to a non-resident person who is outside Canada and not registered for GST/HST is zero-rated at 0%. The freemium mobile utility app can claim 0% GST/HST on Apple IAP and Google Play Billing transactions where the end user's storefront, billing address, IP address, bank-card country, and platform settlement reports evidence a non-resident recipient in a foreign storefront. Subscriptions and cloud-based performance-optimization features supplied to foreign corporate clients are likewise zero-rated. Supplies to Canadian consumers remain taxable at 5% GST, with BC PST collected by the online marketplace where applicable. CRA audit positions in this sector focus on the ultimate recipient's ordinary residence and the merchant-of-record chain between Apple/Google and the developer.

  • App Store Connect and Google Play Console territory-level sales and settlement reports showing storefront, billing territory, currency, and transaction identifiers per end user
  • Apple IAP / Google Play Billing merchant-of-record invoices and payout statements evidencing foreign-currency net settlements remitted to the developer
  • Consolidated commercial invoice or sales ledger reconciling zero-rated export sales by storefront to foreign recipients
  • Inward remittance certificates and bank/FX statements proving repatriation of Apple and Google payouts into the LP's or partners' bank accounts
  • GST/HST return (Form GST34) zero-rated claim worksheet with Schedule VI export-substantiation schedules retained for six years
  • Note: no physical Customs export declaration or bill of lading arises for intangible software delivered online; the above digital documentation bundle replaces the traditional goods-export document set

Canada does not mandate real-time e-invoice clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), fiscal digital stamping, or API-based fiscal reporting. E-invoicing is voluntary and B2B/B2G adoption is progressing through the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework (Peppol BIS 3.0). Fiscal compliance is return-based: GST/HST returns (Form GST34) must be filed annually, quarterly, or monthly via GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account; registrants must retain books and records (including App Store Connect and Google Play Console settlement files) for a minimum of six years and make them available electronically to CRA upon request. No value-added tax invoice clearance infrastructure exists at the federal or provincial level.

CRA export-verification audits scrutinize whether the zero-rated supply was genuinely made to a non-resident recipient and whether platform data (storefront, IP address, card country, settlement currency) supports the Schedule VI claim. Where the zero-rating claim is unsupported, CRA reassesses the supply at 5% GST/HST, denies offsetting input tax credits, and charges prescribed arrears interest. The gross-negligence penalty under ETA subsection 285(1) applies at 50% of the net-tax understatement (minimum CAD 100) for false statements made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; wilful evasion is prosecuted under ETA section 327, attracting fines from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment. The normal reassessment window is four years, extended without limitation where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established. For BC PST, the Consumer Taxation Branch may assess unreported tax, penalties, and interest, and can recharacterize an online-marketplace arrangement as a direct supply by the developer, triggering retroactive PST clawback with no prior-notice relief where the platform collection evidence is deficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD maintains IMF Article VIII full convertibility. Both current-account flows (App Store/Google Play remittances, royalties, trade settlements) and capital-account flows (partner capital contributions, loan repayments, partnership distributions) are unrestricted; there are no exchange-control permits, quotas, or forced conversions. Profit remittance hurdles are taxation-only: (i) non-resident partners are liable under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on Canadian-source income, with treaty-based withholding reductions (e.g., 15% for US residents under the Canada-US treaty when income is business-profit connected to a permanent establishment it is taxed under Part I instead); (ii) Part I instalment withholding may be required on a non-resident partner's share of Canadian partnership income; and (iii) FINTRAC reports (international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more) apply to any wire regardless of convertibility. The LP may hold USD, CAD, or other currency accounts freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender, conversion-timeline, or mandatory-ratio obligations. App-store and subscription proceeds from the freemium model may remain in USD indefinitely in a Canadian- or foreign-based account. Reporting duties are limited to: (i) accurate books and records under the Income Tax Act; (ii) Form T1135 disclosure of the Canadian-resident partners (the partnership itself does not file; the general partner must coordinate) when aggregate specified foreign property attributable to each partner exceeds CAD 100,000; and (iii) FINTRAC EFT reporting by the financial institution on inbound/outbound wires of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); payment-system oversight via the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and the Canadian Payments Act. No dedicated foreign-exchange administration agency exists; the Canadian dollar is market-convertible. Banking prudential supervision is delegated to OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) and AML/CFT supervision to FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada), both applying to all Schedule I banks used by the BC limited partnership.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary rail, Low as the primary treasury spine. Canada has no EU-style electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence; retail payment services fall under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime, administered by the Bank of Canada, while money-services businesses (MSBs) register with FINTRAC. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Nium) may supply USD/CAD virtual accounts and SWIFT visibility, but they cannot clear through Payments Canada Lynx without a Schedule I partner bank. Legal constraints: (i) Apple IAP and Google Play Billing payouts require a designated bank account in the developer's name, and EMI virtual accounts are frequently rejected or subject to platform review; (ii) flowing third-party funds through an EMI can cause the LP to be treated as an unregistered MSB; (iii) funds held at a foreign EMI are not eligible for Canadian deposit insurance (CDIC) and are exposed to the EMI's insolvency; (iv) any EMI used should be RPAA-registered and hold a Canadian-issued MSB licence for money remittance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A Canadian limited partnership is generally fiscally transparent and is not subject to Canadian income tax at the partnership level. Taxable income and losses are allocated to partners. A taxable Canadian corporation carrying on business in British Columbia is generally subject to 15% federal corporate income tax plus 12% British Columbia corporate income tax, for a combined general rate of 27%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax generally applies to taxable dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident. An LP does not pay dividends in the corporate-law sense; distributions retain their tax characterization and may require withholding depending on the underlying income and applicable rules. A treaty commonly reduces dividends to 5% for qualifying corporate ownership and 15% for other direct investments, subject to the particular treaty and limitation-on-benefits or anti-abuse provisions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally within 5 months after the end of the partnership fiscal period. Partnership slips and required information must be provided to partners by the same general deadline. The LP must maintain a fiscal period permitted under the Income Tax Act; a calendar-year period is commonly used.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally within 6 months after the end of the corporation's taxation year. Any balance of tax is generally payable 2 months after year-end, or 3 months for qualifying CCPCs subject to the statutory conditions.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally one month after the end of the reporting period for monthly or quarterly filers, with special rules for annual filers. GST/HST remittance deadlines and return deadlines must be separately verified.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada has no general statutory Master File or Local File filing threshold in the OECD terminology. Section 247 requires contemporaneous documentation for non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents. Form T106 reporting is generally triggered where the total value of reportable non-arm's-length non-resident transactions, or the relevant property, services, financing, or royalty categories, exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year, subject to the detailed T106 rules. The documentation must be prepared by the tax-return filing date and provided to CRA within 3 months of a written request. A Canadian entity that is a member of a multinational group may also have Country-by-Country Reporting obligations if the group meets the applicable EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a general standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore ESR systems. Substance is nevertheless relevant under section 247 transfer pricing, the general anti-avoidance rule, treaty principal-purpose and anti-abuse provisions, beneficial-ownership doctrine, partnership allocation rules, debt characterization, foreign-affiliate rules, and residence or permanent-establishment analysis. The LP should maintain evidence of Canadian decision-making, personnel, development functions, intellectual-property control, risk assumption, contractual authority, bank activity, books and records, and commercially supportable partner allocations. A pass-through label does not protect an arrangement lacking commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalties can generally equal 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit. Tax evasion can result in fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act, in addition to tax, interest, and other penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, signing officers, and other participants may face prosecution where personal participation, authorization, knowledge, or wilful blindness is established. A corporate veil is not automatically pierced, but civil restitution and criminal liability can attach personally. No automatic tax-related travel ban applies solely because a return is late; court orders, bail conditions, immigration consequences, or other proceedings may restrict travel.
Failure to Withhold or Remit Part XIII Tax

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for the unwithheld amount, applicable penalties, and prescribed interest. Information-return failures can attract additional penalties. Directors and responsible officers may face derivative liability for source deductions and remittances where statutory conditions are met.

Director personal liability: Director liability for unremitted source deductions can arise under the Income Tax Act despite separate legal personality, subject to statutory due-diligence defenses and limitation periods. Criminal prosecution is possible for deliberate obstruction, fraud, or evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may make a section 247 transfer-pricing adjustment, impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the applicable adjustment where statutory conditions are met, deny deductions, assess withholding tax, and apply interest. Penalties may be reduced or avoided where reasonable efforts and contemporaneous documentation requirements are satisfied.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically personally liable for an assessment, but personal exposure may arise for knowing participation in false records, fraud, obstruction, or criminal tax conduct. Professional advisers may also face statutory or regulatory consequences.
Undocumented Partnership Allocations and Disguised Distributions

Statutory sanction: CRA may reallocate partnership income, deny losses, apply the partnership anti-avoidance rules or GAAR, assess tax and interest at partner level, and impose gross-negligence or evasion penalties where warranted.

Director personal liability: General partners and persons directing the arrangement may face civil claims, tax-related derivative liability, and criminal exposure if the arrangement involves deliberate falsification or fraud. Limited liability does not protect a person from personal wrongdoing.
Shell Company, Treaty Shopping, and Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty benefits may be denied under beneficial-ownership, limitation-on-benefits, principal-purpose, or domestic anti-avoidance principles. CRA may assess withholding tax, interest, penalties, and reassessed income.

Director personal liability: Individuals may face personal civil and criminal exposure for knowingly using false residence certificates, nominee arrangements, or fabricated ownership records. There is no automatic imprisonment or travel ban for treaty denial alone; such consequences require applicable criminal or court processes.
GST/HST and Digital-Supply Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may assess uncollected tax, interest, late-filing and late-remittance penalties, and penalties for false statements or failure to register. Input tax credits may be denied for inadequate documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate supplier may be assessed for unremitted GST/HST under statutory director-liability provisions, subject to due-diligence defenses. An LP's general partner and responsible persons may face personal exposure for fraud or deliberate non-remittance.
Books, Records, and CRA Obstruction

Statutory sanction: CRA may impose record-keeping penalties, deny deductions or credits, issue compliance orders, reassess using available information, and prosecute obstruction or failure to produce records. Criminal fines and imprisonment may apply for deliberate obstruction.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, general partners, officers, and agents may be personally prosecuted for obstruction or destruction of evidence. Separate legal personality does not protect deliberate personal misconduct.
AML, Proceeds of Crime, and Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include administrative monetary penalties, compliance orders, asset seizure or forfeiture, prosecution, substantial fines, and imprisonment under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, the Criminal Code, and sanctions legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, general partners, and beneficial owners may be personally liable or prosecuted for authorization, participation, recklessness, or wilful blindness. Criminal convictions can result in imprisonment and court-imposed travel or bail restrictions; no automatic travel ban follows solely from a compliance deficiency.
Unlawful Distribution and Insolvency Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Transactions may be challenged, reversed, or subordinated under partnership, insolvency, fraudulent-conveyance, and creditor-protection laws. Directors or partners may face contribution, restitution, oppression, or fraudulent-transfer remedies.

Director personal liability: General partners generally bear broader liability than limited partners. Directors and officers may face personal statutory liability for wages, source deductions, GST/HST, or knowing fraudulent conduct. Imprisonment is reserved for applicable criminal conduct, not ordinary commercial insolvency.
