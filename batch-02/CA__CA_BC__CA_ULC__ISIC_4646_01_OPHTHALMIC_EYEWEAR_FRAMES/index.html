Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – national tax authority and tax portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Provincial sales tax administration for British Columbia: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch – https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-established or locally incorporated entity must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) as the master statutory tax identifier. For a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (BC ULC), the procedure is: (1) incorporate or file annual corporate maintenance through BC Corporate Online, obtaining the BC incorporation number; (2) apply to CRA for a BN using Form RC1 or online through CRA's My Business Account, requesting a GST/HST program account (RT) if taxable supplies exceed or will exceed the registration threshold; (3) if collecting BC PST, register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance for a PST number; (4) for export transactions, a CRA import/export account (RM) is required only if the entity is an importer of record, but exporters should record their CRA BN for customs declarations. Non-resident entities without Canadian incorporation may apply for a 'non-resident BN' by filing Form RC1015? (if applicable) and must follow CRA's non-resident account procedures.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (with Pass_Through_Eligible under U.S. check-the-box rules). For Canadian federal and BC income tax, a BC ULC is a resident corporation taxable on worldwide income at the entity level. It is not statutorily a conduit, but it may be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for foreign income tax purposes where a foreign jurisdiction applies a check-the-box or analogous unlimited-liability classification.

Available election forms

For U.S. federal income tax, the entity may file IRS Form 8832 under Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3 to elect disregarded-entity or partnership classification ('check-the-box'). For Canadian tax purposes, no general check-the-box election exists; the ULC remains a 'corporation' under the Income Tax Act. If the ULC has hybrid attributes, hybrid mismatch rules under BEPS Action 2 and Canadian measures in sections 135 to 135.3, 18.4, 20.77, 12.77, and 68.0.1-68.0.9 of the Income Tax Act may restrict undue double non-taxation. A ULC should also review Part IV.1 and dividend-related elections, and any treaty provisions affecting deemed-dividend or participation-exemption treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – GST/HST small-supplier registration threshold over four calendar quarters of taxable supplies; for BC PST there is no universal turnover threshold, and a vendor making taxable sales in BC must register under the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act; a wholesaler selling solely for export generally will not have PST collection obligations if exempt-resale documentation is preserved.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supply of goods qualifies for the 0% GST/HST rate when the supplier sells the goods and exports them as part of the sale, as set out in section 22, Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The goods must be delivered, shipped, or mailed by the supplier to an address outside Canada, or the supplier must provide the goods to a carrier or common common-carrier for export, and the exporter must retain proof of exportation within a reasonable time. For BC PST, exports of tangible personal property are exempt if the sale is for export and the seller documents the foreign destination. Offshore services are zero-rated when they meet the place-of-supply rules, including services performed wholly outside Canada or services supplied to non-resident persons that are not likely to be consumed in Canada.

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamp, or biometric invoice clearance mandate exists in Canada. CRA requires electronic GST/HST return filing for certain taxpayers and provides Bulk and Business Gateway APIs for program-account administration. E-invoicing is voluntary; the CRA does not require government real-time invoice approval for export zero-rating. Taxpayers must maintain digital or paper-proof of export with normal accounting records, but no real-time CRA stamp is required.

CRA rigorously audits export zero-rating claims and may retroactively reassess GST/HST at the full 5% rate plus compound arrears interest if the exporter cannot substantiate physical exportation and receipt of settlement. The ETA imposes civil penalties for false statements or misrepresentations, including a penalty of up to 25% of the net tax for gross negligence and up to 50% for fraudulent conduct; CRA can extend the normal four-year reassessment period if a taxpayer has made a misrepresentation, or at any time in circumstances amounting to fraud or wilful default. A refund or input-credit claim that is later clawed back may also be subject to reviewed-in-file penalties, collection enforcement, and, where fraud is established, prosecution under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains free current and capital account convertibility with no foreign-exchange control or prior approval regime under the Currency Act and Bank Act. Profit remittances, dividends, royalties, and other passive payments to non-residents are unrestricted but subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced where applicable) and to the BC corporate-law solvency and balance-sheet tests for distributions. No exchange-control bottleneck applies to a BC ULC distributing funds to an unlimited-liability shareholder.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no requirement to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds. A ULC may retain foreign currency balances in Canadian Schedule I banks or offshore accounts. Cross-border reporting obligations apply under FINTRAC rules to international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and to physical cross-border monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more, but such filing requirements are not monetary surrender or conversion mandates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for low-value platform-pay receipts. A fintech EMI in Canada cannot take deposits or lend unless licensed as a bank; most are registered Money Services Businesses under FINTRAC. Offshore digital EMIs face Canadian regulatory friction because they are not eligible to hold a corporate-operating account at a Schedule I bank and cannot provide cheque or CAD clearing-rail access. The ULC may use a licensed Canadian EMI for platform-pay collection and interbank settlement only, while the primary treasury account must remain at a chartered bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined standard corporate rate (15% federal General Rate Reduction plus 12% British Columbia provincial general corporate rate). The Canadian-controlled private corporation small business deduction is not available to a non-resident-controlled ULC or to passive wholesale distribution activities in respect of that reduced rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid or credited by the ULC to a non-resident shareholder. This statutory rate applies unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty and assumes the shareholder is the beneficial owner of the dividend. Deemed dividends from stock redemption, paid-up capital reductions, or winding-up distributions are similarly subject to 25% withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. Tax payable under Part I and Part VI.1 must generally be received by the CRA no later than two months after the fiscal year-end (one month for certain Canadian-controlled private corporations claiming the small business deduction); any balance-due after installments is subject to prescribed daily compound interest and late-payment penalties.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Form T2, Box 17 — Business Number (BN) Enter the 9-digit CRA-assigned business number followed by the 4-letter program account identifier 'RP01' for regular federal corporate tax. The legal name and the CRA program account must match the BC-registered ULC name and business address. A ULC that is resident in Canada must obtain its own Canadian BN regardless of foreign ownership. Do not use a US Employer Identification Number or another country's tax identification number in this field; register via Form RC243 or CRA's Business Registration Online system. Failure to file the T2 on time attracts a penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% for each full month that the return is late, up to 12 months. For a second offence within three years, the penalty increases to 10% of unpaid tax plus 2% per month up to 24 months. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 per non-resident related person in a taxation year (annual aggregate of reportable transactions). Transactions below this threshold do not require Form T106, but contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File / Master File components) must be kept where the threshold is met. For global multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding CAD 750,000,000, Canada also requires country-by-country reporting and a CRA Master File provision on request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245), the transfer-pricing rule (s.247), and common-law substance-over-form doctrines to disregard artificial or shell arrangements. A ULC not actually managed or operating in BC, or with no economic reason for its interposed existence, will attract GAAR and treaty-shopping challenges. For a wholesale distributor of acetate eyeglass frames, sufficient substance includes a real head office, active decision-making, order processing, inventory risk, title passage, personnel, banking and physical or commercial activity in BC.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of up to 300% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA also assesses full tax, penalties under subsections 162(2) and 163(2), and compound daily interest.

Director personal liability: A director who participates, assents, or acquiesces to evasion is jointly and severally liable with the corporation for all taxes, penalties and interest where the act involves fraud or gross negligence. The corporate veil does not protect a ULC's directors under subsections 227.1 and 160(1) of the Income Tax Act. Moreover, for an Unlimited Liability Corporation, shareholders may be directly liable for the corporation's debts upon dissolution or enforcement by creditors.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The CRA may reassess the transaction, apply a transfer-pricing adjustment, and impose a penalty equal to 10% of the amount of the adjustment if the adjustment exceeds the arm's-length amount and the taxpayer is unable to show reasonable efforts. Interest accrues on the reassessed tax from the date it should have been paid; no reduction or waiver applies for transfer-pricing penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly instruct execution of false transfer pricing documentation can be held personally accountable under common law liability for breach of fiduciary duty and under wilful-blindness doctrine. For the ULC, a shareholder who is also a director can be pursued directly if the CRA lifts the statutory limitation periods or if the transaction is found to be a sham.
Treaty Shopping and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of the tax benefit under GAAR s.245(2), plus a penalty equal to 50% of the amount of the tax benefit (s.245(3) and s.245(8)); the underlying Part XIII withholding tax of 25% remains payable. Where cross-border payments are recast, the CRA may also assess non-resident withholding tax and failure-to-remit penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal restitution in civil proceedings by the Crown if they are found to have caused the corporation and shareholders to receive non-taxable treaty benefits, especially when funds are distributed as shareholder loans. In an ULC, unlimited liability permits the CRA to pursue shareholders directly for any corporate taxes resulting from the collapsed planning.
Anti-Money Laundering / Inadequate Books and Records

Statutory sanction: Failure to keep adequate books and records is an offence under s.235.1 of the Income Tax Act, carrying a fine of up to CAD 10,000 and imprisonment. FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties for non-compliance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act can reach up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for entities. Money laundering offences under the Criminal Code are punishable by imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who knowingly direct or participate in financial concealment or record destruction are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's AML fines; they may also be criminally liable as principals. A ULC structure does not shield such personal liability; for unlimited liability companies, the shareholder liability extends to tax and AML-imposed debts if the corporation is unable to pay.
