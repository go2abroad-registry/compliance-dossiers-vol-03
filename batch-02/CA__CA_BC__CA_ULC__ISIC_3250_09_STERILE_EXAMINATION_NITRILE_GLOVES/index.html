Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A BC registered office and a records office are mandatory under s 42 of the Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c 57. The registered office must be a physical street address in British Columbia at all times; a licensed corporate agent may supply the registered office, but a P.O. Box or a virtual-only address is not permissible. For a medical nitrile-glove manufacturer, commercial substance must include a real production facility, equipment, inventory, and employees; a shell or virtual presence would jeopardize Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) compliance, CRA books-and-records requirements under the Income Tax Act, and BC employment-safety registration. A virtual office is therefore acceptable only for the registered-agent function, not for manufacturing substance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portals: Business Registration Online (bros) and My Business Account; BC provincial tax registration and administration are handled by the BC Ministry of Finance through Corporate Online, eTaxBC, and the BC OneStop service.

E-government filing portal

For a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (BC ULC), incorporation is completed through BC Corporate Online, after which the entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA using Business Registration Online or Form RC1. Separate CRA accounts opened under the same BN include the GST/HST program account (RT) when taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter and in the last four consecutive calendar quarters, the payroll account (RP), and the import/export account (RM) for customs and commercial import-export activity. The BC ULC must also register for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through BC eTaxBC if it carries on business in BC and makes taxable sales; no small-supplier threshold applies to PST for a BC vendor. Foreign ownership of a BC ULC is permitted, and no minimum capital contribution is required. A BC registered address is mandatory for service of process and for CRA records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a general check-the-box or passthrough election for Canadian income tax purposes; a BC ULC is always treated as a separate taxable corporation under the Income Tax Act. The BC ULC may, however, be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership under the US check-the-box regulations, but such foreign classification does not alter Canadian entity-level taxation. Relevant Canadian elections include the section 156 GST/HST election for closely related corporations to treat supplies as nil consideration, the quick-method GST/HST accounting election for small suppliers, and the section 186 ITA dividend refund account election or corporate allocation elections. Hybrid mismatch rules under the ITA (sections 18.18 to 18.21 and 20.31) apply to restrict double deductions and deduction/non-inclusion outcomes involving the ULC; no domestic election to reclassify the hybrid outcome is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for federal GST/HST registration (the small supplier threshold measured by taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter and in the last four consecutive calendar quarters); BC PST registration is required without a general monetary threshold for a person carrying on business in BC and making taxable sales of goods or services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of the exported sterile examination nitrile gloves is available under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V (zero-rated exports) when the BC ULC, as a GST/HST registrant, supplies tangible personal property (the gloves) to a non-resident purchaser and exports the goods from Canada in the course of a commercial activity. The supply is not subject to GST/HST, and the registrant may claim input tax credits for GST paid on production inputs, resulting in a net refund of embedded GST. The gloves must be exported by the supplier or the recipient within a reasonable time, generally documented and completed without use, consumption, or supply in Canada, and the exporter must possess contemporaneous documentary evidence of export. Services performed on the gloves in Canada for a non-resident and directly related to the export may also qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency B13A Export Declaration / Canada Customs Coding Form)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Canada and destination abroad
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list identifying the non-resident buyer and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank wire transfer evidence (e.g., MT103/SWIFT) showing settlement of the export sale proceeds

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime. GST/HST registrants must issue and retain invoices containing prescribed information under section 223 of the Excise Tax Act, including supplier and recipient identity, GST/HST registration number, date, value, and tax amount. Electronic invoicing and EDI transmission are permitted provided the invoice remains legible and readily accessible to the CRA in an electronic record format. CRA requires electronic books and records retention in standard formats, and certain registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically through Netfile or other CRA-approved channels. BC PST has no separate e-invoicing clearance mandate; PST returns and payments are filed through the eTaxBC system.

Canada does not impose exchange-control repatriation requirements, but the 180-day inward remittance window is applied as a stringent internal compliance benchmark for export substantiation. CRA may reassess the GST/HST return within the standard four-year reassessment period and claw back input tax credits, output tax refunds, and zero-rated status where export documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or fraudulent. The limitation period is unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, wilful default, or fraud is established. False export zero-rating claims attract penalties under sections 280 to 285 of the Excise Tax Act, typically 25% of the understated net tax and/or gross negligence penalties of up to 50%, plus statutory interest and compound arrears. Criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code for fraud, forgery, or tax evasion is possible for deliberate schemes, and directors of the ULC may face personal liability for unremitted GST/HST and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible. Both current account (trade, services, income) and capital account (direct investment, portfolio investment, intercompany loans, profit remittances) transactions are unrestricted under Canadian law. There are no exchange control approvals or permits required for remitting dividends, interest, royalties or loan repayments. However, payments to non-residents may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced), and financial institutions must comply with FINTRAC reporting, FATCA/CRS due diligence, and Canada's Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing rules. A BC Unlimited Liability Corporation may be eligible for pass-through classification in certain foreign jurisdictions, particularly the United States; this creates a need for detailed tax-residency, remittance-characterization and treaty-eligibility documentation to avoid withholding mismatches.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts. Foreign-currency export proceeds may be held in domestic foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts at the exporter's discretion. For corporate tax purposes, export revenue must be measured in Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or a consistent accounting exchange-rate policy. Transactions involving CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and export shipments must be reflected in CBSA customs declarations and supporting trade documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for bank supervision; no dedicated foreign exchange administration. Export financing and trade support functions are administered by Export Development Canada (EDC), while border and customs compliance is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury relationship because offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as Schedule I or Schedule II banks in Canada and cannot lawfully accept deposit liabilities. Legal constraints arise under the Bank Act, provincial money-services business licensing regimes, and FINTRAC registration requirements. Foreign EMIs may provide ancillary payment-rail support for payouts in selected overseas corridors, but the BC ULC must maintain a Canadian chartered bank account as the settlement hub for capital, trade finance and operational clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Canada does not mandate a formal FX purpose code or exchange-control permit. Inbound capital injections should be routed through a Schedule I Canadian bank using a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer. The payment reference must clearly state 'paid-up capital / share subscription' or 'shareholder loan disbursement' and identify the CA_ULC beneficiary. The remitter and beneficiary legal names, Canadian bank institution/transit/account numbers, BIC/SWIFT code and a board resolution authorizing the transaction should be provided to the receiving bank. FINTRAC record-keeping and CRS/FATCA self-certification obligations are satisfied through the bank's onboarding forms and regular transaction monitoring. A shareholder loan must be supported by a written agreement with arm's-length terms, a stated interest rate, repayment schedule and subordination provision to prevent recharacterization as equity.Use SWIFT MT103 for direct open-account supplier payments and recurring trade settlements. Settlement can also be completed by documentary collection (DP/DA) where credit risk is manageable. For Canadian-dollar high-value clearing, use the Lynx high-value payment system; for USD payments, use the bank's US correspondent network. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million aggregate cross-border transactions with non-residents (if gross revenue > CAD 50 million)

Economic substance scope: Canada lacks specific ESR but applies GAAR and treaty abuse rules; transactions must have commercial substance and business purpose; CRA may recharacterize or deny benefits if substance lacking

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded; imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors jointly liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s.227.1); civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny treaty benefits; reassess income and impose penalties up to 25% of tax avoided

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to sham arrangements
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal charges for money laundering under Criminal Code

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal fines and imprisonment for willful blindness or participation in laundering
