Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) via Canada.ca; account administration: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance under the Provincial Sales Tax Act and is not collected by the CRA.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian domestic law) with Pass_Through_Eligible (US tax treaty layer): A BC ULC is a corporation resident in Canada and is subject to Part I Income Tax Act on worldwide income at the federal general rate of 15% plus BC provincial general rate of 12%, for a combined headline rate of 27%. Canada does not grant the ULC domestic pass-through status. For US federal tax purposes, the ULC is an eligible entity that defaults to pass-through treatment (partnership or disregarded entity) because its shareholders bear unlimited liability, and it may elect entity-level corporation status via IRS Form 8832. Territorial_Scope does not apply: the ULC is taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits available under ITA sections 113 and 126.

Available election forms

IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election permits the BC ULC to elect corporation, partnership, or disregarded-entity classification for US federal tax purposes; default classification is pass-through due to unlimited shareholder liability. Canada-US Tax Convention Article IV(6) fiscally transparent entity rule enables US shareholders to claim treaty benefits on ULC income as if earned directly. The ULC may not elect out of Canadian corporate status under the Income Tax Act; no domestic check-the-box exists. ETA section 156 election allows GST/HST grouping of closely related corporations so intercompany supplies are deemed made for nil consideration. ITA section 216 election is available to non-resident shareholders with Canadian rental income, but does not alter ULC classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold): mandatory GST/HST registration is triggered when a supplier's worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act subsection 240(1) and section 148). Non-residents carrying on business in Canada that make taxable supplies in Canada are required to register regardless of the small supplier threshold where they solicit orders through a permanent establishment or are engaged in specified commercial activities (ETA subsection 240(1) and 240.1). For the resident BC ULC, the applicable threshold is CAD 30,000 based on global taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V: section 1 for goods shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, and section 5 for goods supplied to a non-resident who acquires them for export, delivered in Canada to a common carrier or freight forwarder. The self-heating instant meal and exothermic heating component (CaO sachet) must physically leave Canada, be exported without use or consumption in Canada before export, and be supported by documentary proof of export obtained within a reasonable time (CRA administrative practice commonly expects export evidence within 60 days). Zero-rated supplies permit full input tax credit (ITC) recovery, generating refund claims where input GST exceeds output GST. Services supplied to non-residents for use exclusively outside Canada (Schedule VI, Part V, section 7) and services performed in respect of exported goods (sections 5 and 6) are also zero-rated. CFIA export certification under the Safe Food for Canadians Act is a condition precedent for lawful export of the food product.

  • Commercial invoice (customs invoice) reflecting sold-to and ship-to parties, INCOTERMS, and transaction value

No continuous transaction controls (CTC), no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based pre-clearance, and no digital stamping requirement exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed tax invoices (ETA subsection 169(4) and the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations) containing the supplier's name and GST/HST registration number, invoice date, recipient name, description of goods, rate of tax, and tax amount or tax-included statement. Invoices and all export substantiation records must be retained for six years. Canadian e-invoicing is voluntary and based on the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework; CRA APIs support registration and account filing, not transactional clearance.

CRA may reassess within 4 years of the original assessment under normal review, and without statutory time limit where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established (ITA subsection 152(4)). Invalid export zero-rating triggers reassessment of net GST, clawback of input tax credits, and arrears interest compounded daily at the prescribed rate plus penalty interest. Gross negligence penalties apply at 50% of the overstated refund or understated tax (ITA subsection 163(2); ETA subsection 285.1). False statements or omissions attract ETA section 285 penalties equal to 5% of the amount of tax sought to be evaded (minimum CAD 100). The CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) imposes penalties up to CAD 25,000 per violation for deficient export declarations. Fraudulent export arrangements (dummy exports, carousel schemes, falsified bills of lading) are prosecuted under Criminal Code section 380 (fraud), with potential director and shareholder criminal liability, seizure of assets, and permanent VAT registration revocation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains full current and capital account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, surrender obligations, repatriation quotas, or prior approval requirements for dividends, royalties, or capital distributions. The principal hurdles are tax-based: a British Columbia ULC can be tax-transparent for certain non-resident members, so outbound profit remittances may be recharacterized as deemed distributions subject to Part XIII withholding tax. An advance income tax ruling or treaty-based waiver is advisable before final clearing of dividend-type flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law does not require export proceeds to be repatriated, surrendered, or converted within any statutory timeline. Exporters may retain foreign currency offshore, net balances against payables, or convert to CAD at prevailing market rates. Only standard FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting and sanctions screening obligations apply when proceeds cross via a Canadian regulated bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in policy coordination with the Department of Finance Canada; prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); financial crime reporting administered by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for Canadian-incorporated FINTRAC-registered money service businesses and e-money platforms; Low for offshore digital EMIs. Offshore EMIs are not authorized deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot hold capital escrow, issue commercial letters of credit, or operate as a primary clearing bank in Canada. They may be used only as auxiliary payment providers through a regulated Canadian financial institution. For ISIC 1079 low-risk standard, an offshore EMI is acceptable for incidental supplier disbursements and refund processing, but all regulatory capital and strategic liquidity must reside with a Tier 1 Canadian Schedule I bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general corporate rate 15% plus British Columbia general corporate rate 12%, producing a combined headline rate of 27% on taxable income. A ULC controlled by a non-resident is generally not a Canadian-controlled private corporation and therefore cannot access the 11% CCPC small-business rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on gross dividends paid to non-residents; British Columbia does not impose a separate dividend withholding tax. Under the Canada-US tax treaty, the general withholding rate is reduced to 15%, and to 5% where the beneficial owner is a US corporation that owns directly at least 10% of the voting stock of the paying ULC and satisfies applicable treaty conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The T2 return must be filed within six months after the end of the fiscal year. The final balance of federal and BC corporate income tax is due within two months after fiscal year-end; a Canadian-controlled private corporation eligible for the small-business deduction may pay within three months. A BC/AB ULC controlled by a US parent should assume the two-month standard deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate annual fair-market value of non-arm's-length controlled transactions triggers the CRA T106 reporting expectation and the practice of maintaining contemporaneous OECD-style Local File documentation. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of C$750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: No formal Economic Substance Regulation exists in British Columbia or Canada. The CRA applies economic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, permanent-establishment analysis, and treaty-shopping limitations. A BC ULC should demonstrate real BC premises, employees, operating decisions, and risk-bearing with respect to self-heating pot manufacturing to avoid re-characterization as a shell or conduit entity.
