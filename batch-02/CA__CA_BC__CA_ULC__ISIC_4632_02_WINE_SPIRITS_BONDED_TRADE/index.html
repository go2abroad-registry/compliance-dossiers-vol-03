Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal Business Number (BN), GST/HST, income tax, and excise duty administration; BC Ministry of Finance for provincial sales tax (PST), BC liquor tax, and eTaxBC portal; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs bonded warehouse licensing and export controls.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity first obtains a statutory Business Number (BN) from CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, simultaneously applying for a GST/HST account. For British Columbia, the entity must register for PST and BC liquor tax accounts through BC OneStop or eTaxBC. For bonded wine and spirits trade, the entity must obtain a CBSA customs bonded warehouse licence and an excise warehouse licence from CRA under the Excise Act, 2001, with separate registration for the Excise Duty Program if manufacturing or importing alcohol. Non-resident corporations may apply using a non-resident representative and must provide proof of legal existence, including certified copies of the ULC memorandum or articles of incorporation, foreign certificate of status, and powers of attorney.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a general check-the-box regime, but a British Columbia ULC is eligible for U.S. federal check-the-box treatment and may elect to be disregarded or classified as a partnership for U.S. tax purposes. In Canada, the ULC is a separate taxpayer subject to entity-level corporate income tax. Available statutory elections relevant to cross-border trade include: Excise Tax Act section 273 (election by closely related registrants to treat supplies as non-taxable), Income Tax Act subsection 160.4 (associated corporation income allocation), and hybrid mismatch reporting and disquallifying rules under Income Tax Act sections 163.2 and 247 for branch or hybrid entity mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property (including wine and spirits) made to a non-resident person for shipment outside Canada, provided the property is exported as a consequence of the supply and the supplier maintains records proving export to a destination outside Canada. For bonded trade, the goods must remain under CBSA warehouse control until physical export and the exporter must hold the authority of the excise warehouse licence permitting duty-free transfer. Supplies to registered non-resident persons may also be zero-rated under the drop-shipment rules in Excise Tax Act section 179, and services directly related to the export sale are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or equivalent electronic export declaration where required)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill showing consignee outside Canada and port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign exchange/bank wire receipt demonstrating payment from the foreign buyer
  • Zero-rated GST/HST invoice issued in Canada with tax rate shown as 0%
  • CBSA bonded warehouse transfer authorization and excise movement certificate proving duty-suspended export
  • Certificate of non-resident status and underlying purchase order or contract
  • Proof of delivery to carrier and export date if alternate evidence is used

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoice approval for GST/HST. However, GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing mandatory information, file returns and information electronically when meeting the prescribed criteria, and retain machine-readable books and records. Customs bonded warehouse operations for wine and spirits must use CBSA's Single Window Initiative (SWI) and Automated Customs Declaration System (ACEDS), submitting a cargo and conveyor declaration and warehouse control documents. Excise duty is tracked through CRA's Excise Duty Program electronic returns; physical excise stamps are not universally required for export goods, but warehouse inventory and transfer records must support duty relief.

CRA may reassess a claimed zero-rated supply to tax at the applicable rate (5% GST plus applicable BC liquor tax or PST if in scope) with compound interest, if the exporter fails to provide valid export evidence within 180 days, or if the export is found to be a circular or sham transaction. A knowingly false zero-rating claim triggers the wilful default penalty under Excise Tax Act subsection 280(1), calculated as 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, doubled to 50% on a second occurrence, plus gross negligence penalties under Income Tax Act subsection 163(2) if income is understated. Criminal prosecution under Excise Tax Act section 327 or Income Tax Act section 239 may result in fines and imprisonment. CRA and CBSA jointly audit exporters of alcoholic products by cross-matching customs declarations, bonded warehouse transfers, bills of lading, and foreign currency remittance records; in the event of a retroactive clawback, the tax becomes due as of the original date of supply, with daily compound interest and arrears interest at the prescribed rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations of the International Monetary Fund since 1952, providing full convertibility on both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange-control licences, no capital-account approval gates, and no statutory restriction on profit or dividend remittance. A British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation may freely repatriate profits and subscribed capital to non-resident shareholders; the only relevant fiscal impediments are Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25 per cent, reducible under an applicable income tax treaty) and the standalone thin-capitalisation and general anti-avoidance rules. Foreign-currency denominated accounts and fungible FX conversion are available without Bank of Canada approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: none; mandatory conversion ratio: 0 per cent. Canadian exporters are not required to surrender export proceeds, repatriate them within a statutory window, or convert them into Canadian dollars. Export invoices may be denominated and collected in any freely convertible currency, and proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency operating accounts at domestic banks. The only incidental obligations are ordinary commercial record-keeping under the Income Tax Act and statistical reporting to Statistics Canada where a transaction meets the threshold of inward or outward foreign direct investment.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the monetary authority and fiscal agent of the federal government; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) exercises prudential supervision over all federally regulated banks. Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration because the Exchange Control Act lapsed in 1951; the Minister of Finance retains residual statutory powers under the Currency Act, and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers cross-border AML obligations and international electronic funds transfer reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium as a secondary settlement rail; Low as the sole banking custody. Cross-border digital EMIs and money-service businesses such as Wise, Airwallex and Payoneer may lawfully serve Canadian corporate clients only after registration as money-service businesses with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); there is no separate federal EMI licence. Material legal constraints include internal high-risk policies that commonly refuse alcohol distribution and bonded-warehouse counterparties, the inability to hold customs-bonded or excise-due monies, and the operational requirement to embed a domestic Schedule I settlement account. A BC ULC should use EMIs solely for small-value supplier payments and never for capital injections, excise duty, or LC-backed imports.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or subordinated loan capital is routed as a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202/COV for bank-to-bank credit) credited to the ULC's CAD operating account at a Schedule I bank using the bank's SWIFT BIC and institution/transit/account numbers. The receiving bank classifies the payment as a direct-investment capital contribution and files an International Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC when the amount equals or exceeds CAD 10,000. No exchange-control approval is required. The non-resident investor must file a New Canadian Business notification with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act if the ULC is newly established, and must register the position with Statistics Canada's foreign-direct-investment survey programme where ownership exceeds 10 per cent. For escrow-funded subscription structures, the capital wire is directed first to an RBC Trust or comparable licensed escrow account and released upon satisfaction of closing conditions, with the release evidenced as a book transfer from the escrow ledger to the ULC operating ledger.Trade disbursements for aged Scotch whisky and Bordeaux wine purchases use segregated rails: (a) direct supplier payments via SWIFT MT103 settled in CAD through LVTS or Lynx and, for USD, through Fedwire or CHIPS at a United States correspondent bank; (b) irrevocable confirmed documentary letters of credit issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank via SWIFT MT700 and confirmed by a UK or EU bank, payable at sight under UCP600 against presentation of the commercial invoice, full-set clean on-board bill of lading, certificate of origin, certificate of age or appellation, and marine insurance certificate; (c) documentary collections under MT400/MT412 for repeat suppliers migrating to open-account terms; and (d) bonded-duty transfers between CBSA-licensed warehouses effected as in-bond movements and cleared through the CBSA CARM electronic entry system with excise-duty deferral, requiring no bank clearing rail.
