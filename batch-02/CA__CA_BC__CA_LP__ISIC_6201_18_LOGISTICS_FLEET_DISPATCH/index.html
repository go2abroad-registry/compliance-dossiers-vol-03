Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required for a BC limited partnership; the capital is fixed by the partnership agreement and may be contributed in money, property, or services.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: compliance with the BC Fire Code and a fire inspection clearance for the leased office premises. Structural: building occupancy certificate and post-disaster structural certification from the local building authority. Occupational: WorkSafeBC registration and occupational health and safety clearance under the Workers Compensation Act as soon as employees are engaged in British Columbia.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required for a BC limited partnership; the capital is fixed by the partnership agreement and may be contributed in money, property, or services.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and Income Tax; British Columbia Ministry of Finance – Provincial Sales Tax (PST); CRA Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal; BC eTaxBC for PST registration

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1. The BN is used for GST/HST registration; a separate GST/HST account is required if taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 small supplier threshold. PST registration is completed via BC eTaxBC or the BC Tax Registration form. Supporting documentation includes the LP agreement, evidence of registration in home jurisdiction, identification of non-resident partners, and appointment of a Canadian representative for service of process and tax administration.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign limited partnership must obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1. The BN is used for GST/HST registration; a separate GST/HST account is required if taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 small supplier threshold. PST registration is completed via BC eTaxBC or the BC Tax Registration form. Supporting documentation includes the LP agreement, evidence of registration in home jurisdiction, identification of non-resident partners, and appointment of a Canadian representative for service of process and tax administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal or BC tax law. A Canadian limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; income allocations are taxed at the partner level under the partnership allocation rules of the Income Tax Act (ITA). Partners may elect to allocate income in accordance with the partnership agreement subject to ITA section 103 reasonableness constraints. Hybrid mismatch rules under the ITA deny deductions in respect of dual-entity and dual-income arrangements consistent with BEPS Action 2. The LP may also file a GST/HST election with the CRA if applying an intercompany simplified tax treatment under the Elections and Material Transactions rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies); registration is mandatory once exceeded. PST for British Columbia has no general numeric registration threshold but registration is required for suppliers making taxable sales of software, services, or goods in BC through a permanent establishment or significant economic presence; BC has economic nexus rules requiring out-of-province suppliers to register if they solicit sales from BC customers and maintain inventory or agents in the province.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of software-as-a-service (fleet dispatch telematics SaaS) is zero-rated under section 23 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when made to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and is not a prescribed registrant, provided the supply is not in respect of Canadian real property, the service is not physically performed in Canada, and the beneficiary of the service is outside Canada. Zero-rating also applies to a supply of intangible personal property such as software licences to non-residents. Exported goods are zero-rated under section 20 of Schedule VI if sold to a non-resident for shipment to a destination outside Canada and delivered at the exporter's or agent's place of business for export. The LP must retain evidence that the recipient was a non-resident at the time of the supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / CAED electronic reporting for goods)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Bill of Lading or waybill evidencing international shipment
  • Proof of delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation showing foreign exchange repatriation
  • Non-resident status certificate or written declaration from the SaaS recipient
  • IP usage and licence documentation demonstrating beneficial use outside Canada

No government-operated real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing framework applies in Canada. CRA requires electronic GST/HST filing and remittance for registrants with gross annual revenue exceeding CAD 1 million. GST/HST registrants must issue retrievable electronic invoices containing prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act; invoices may be stored in English or French and produced on audit. No API-based pre-clearance mandate exists, though CRA supports electronic tax returns, business registry integration, and SST1Z-style reporting for exported technology services.

False or unsupported export zero-rating claims are subject to reassessment on a taxable basis, denial of input tax credits, and recovery of refunds with interest. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a gross negligence penalty equal to 25% of the tax amount understated (cap referenced as 35% for repeat violations), false statement penalties up to 25% of the refund claimed, and criminal penalties under section 329 for willful evasion, including fines up to CAD 250,000 and imprisonment. BC PST misrepresentation penalties of 10% of tax due plus interest apply. CRA and CBSA may jointly audit export documentation; inadequate transport records result in full tax clawback and denial of statutory grace.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, prior regulatory approvals, or repatriation taxes at the FX level. Non-resident limited partners may contribute capital to a British Columbia LP without Bank of Canada authorisation. Profit and distribution remittances are fully convertible; however, a Canadian-source income distribution to a non-resident partner is subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25%, treaty-reduced where applicable, and must be reported on NR4/NR6 information returns. The LP and its financial institutions remain subject to CRS and FATCA registration and reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or prescribed holding timelines for export receipts. Exporters and logistics SaaS providers may retain, borrow against, hedge, or convert foreign currency freely. Export Development Canada (EDC) provides trade credit insurance, bonding, guarantees, and structured finance support for cross-border disbursements, while the non-resident financial institution reporting burden remains limited to CRS/FATCA and FINTRAC record-keeping requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy is administered jointly with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Canada operates a liberalised, fully convertible currency regime without a dedicated foreign-exchange control agency. The prudential regulator for Schedule I banks is the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) is the AML/ATF supervisor and financial intelligence unit for all treasury operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary rail, Low as a primary treasury solution. Canada does not issue the EU-style EMI licence. An offshore digital EMI is not recognised as a deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act, and holding client funds in an unlicensed foreign entity exposes the LP to OSFI enforcement and FINTRAC compliance risk. Offshore EMIs may be engaged only as payment facilitators, with settlement routing to a Canadian Schedule I bank account; the BC LP must use a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) if any payment-processing activity is conducted in Canada. Third-party payment platforms such as Stripe Billing are acceptable provided funds settle to the LP's domestic bank account and the platform partner is a licensed payment service provider in Canada.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident limited partner structure with no apparent Canadian tax or operational nexus

Root cause: Non-resident limited partner structure with no apparent Canadian tax or operational nexus

Defense evidence: BC Registry registration certificate, final limited partnership agreement and current schedule of partners, organisational chart evidencing ultimate beneficial ownership, statutory declaration of beneficial ownership, passport copies of all individuals holding 25% or more of the economic interest, proof of BC registered office, and CRS/FATCA self-certification forms for every non-resident partner.
Mixed capital contributions and operational receipts flowing through a single escrowed account structure

Root cause: Mixed capital contributions and operational receipts flowing through a single escrowed account structure

Defense evidence: Capital call notices matched with bank records, subscription and capital contribution agreements, partnership capital account roll-forward, independently prepared financial statements, and a legal opinion from Canadian counsel confirming that the LP is not engaged in unlawful deposit-taking and that escrow balances are contractually segregated.
Cross-border SaaS subscription and logistics-related wire traffic without a complete invoice and service trail

Root cause: Cross-border SaaS subscription and logistics-related wire traffic without a complete invoice and service trail

Defense evidence: Master service agreements with logistics clients, itemised invoices tied to fleet dispatch usage and telematics subscriptions, route optimisation deliverables and dispatch records, fee schedules, customer acceptance documentation, and a written source-of-funds narrative describing the enterprise structure and revenue drivers.
High-risk jurisdiction exposure through freight forwarding or international logistics counterparties

Root cause: High-risk jurisdiction exposure through freight forwarding or international logistics counterparties

Defense evidence: FINTRAC and OSFI-compliant sanctions screening policy and audit log, restricted-party screening results against United Nations, OSFI, OFAC, and Global Affairs Canada (DFAT) lists, counterparty end-user declarations, negative-media and adverse-information monitoring reports, and Export Development Canada due-diligence files for international trade service suppliers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident limited partners must be routed by SWIFT MT103 with (i) remitter details exactly matching the partner's KYC record, (ii) beneficiary field identifying the BC Limited Partnership by registered legal name and BC Registry number, and (iii) a purpose narrative such as 'Contribution to Limited Partnership Capital' rather than a loan or service payment. Settlement occurs through the Canadian Lynx high-value payment system for CAD or through CHIPS/Fedwire correspondent clearing for USD. The LP must retain the SWIFT confirmation, capital call notice, partner subscription agreement, and source-of-funds declarations to satisfy FINTRAC CDD and the bank's transaction monitoring requirements. Banks classify partner capital as non-deposit funding and require transparent escrow accounting separate from operating revenue.Outbound disbursements for fleet dispatch software licensing, vendor payables, and logistics trade settlements are executed through (1) SWIFT MT103 covered payments in CAD, USD, or EUR via the Schedule I bank's correspondent network; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit advised by SWIFT MT700 for high-value freight or fleet-technology vendor transactions, commonly confirmed by a Canadian west-coast bank; and (3) domestic clearing through Payments Canada Lynx for high-value CAD wires and the Real-Time Rail or retail interbank systems for smaller disbursements. Interac and batch remittance products may be used for recurring SaaS settlements and contractor payouts.
