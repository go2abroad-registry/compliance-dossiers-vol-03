E-government filing portal

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 GST/HST small supplier threshold (worldwide taxable supplies other than financial services in preceding four calendar quarters); no threshold for BC PST vendor registration before making PST-taxable sales; non-resident suppliers of digital services to Canadian consumers must register under simplified GST/HST rules without threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating is available for goods exported from Canada and for services or intangible personal property (e.g., music licences and stock-audio subscriptions) supplied to a non-resident recipient where the property is for use exclusively outside Canada and, for intangible property, the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant acquiring for use in Canada. The supplier must report the supply as zero-rated in its GST/HST return and retain evidence. Supplies to non-residents are not zero-rated if they relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, or if the recipient is a registrant acquiring for commercial use in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13) - only where physical media or hardware is exported
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill - only for physical export shipments
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/Stripe/PayPal settlement statement showing foreign-source payment
  • Executed licence/subscription agreement with non-resident customer identifying jurisdiction and permitted territory
  • Non-residency declaration or certificate of residence from the licensee

No mandatory continuous transaction controls or real-time fiscal clearance in Canada. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; electronic records must be retained under ETA s. 286. Optional PEPPOL B2B e-invoicing is accepted by CRA but not mandatory.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST return within four years (unlimited in fraud or willful misrepresentation). Incorrect zero-rating converts the supply to taxable at 5%; the supplier becomes liable for the unremitted GST, interest under ETA s. 280, and penalties under ss. 280-285, including 25% for false statements or omissions and up to 100% for repeated violations. Willful evasion is a criminal offence with fines up to double the evaded amount and imprisonment. Export refund claims attract enhanced document review and may be held pending substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; no exchange control permits or approval mechanisms exist for ordinary commercial payments or profit remittances. The Canadian dollar is freely floating and can be bought and sold in efficient interbank markets. For the BC limited partnership, subscription revenue earned in CAD and foreign currencies is fully convertible. Profit distributions to non-resident limited partners are subject to the normal withholding tax provisions of the Income Tax Act and to the BC Partnership Act filing requirements, but there is no FX conversion barrier or cooling-off period. The only practical hurdles are standard bank compliance procedures, including source-of-funds documentation, sanctions screening, and FINTRAC large transaction reporting for amounts exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion quotas, or a repatriation timeline on export receipts. The BC limited partnership may maintain foreign-currency accounts with Schedule I banks or authorized service providers, retain USD or EUR subscription earnings offshore, and remit or convert funds at commercially reasonable times. There is no obligation to surrender proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any other government entity.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign-exchange and cross-border transaction regulatory oversight coordinated through the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Investment Review Division of Global Affairs Canada under the Investment Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Medium. Canada does not recognise the European electronic-money institution (EMI) passporting model. An offshore EMI may be used as a payment aggregator or collection gateway only if it is registered as a Foreign Money Services Business with FINTRAC and, where applicable, licensed under the Retail Payment Activities Act. It cannot legally hold deposit balances or provide Canadian domestic bank account services. For treasury-grade liquidity, the BC limited partnership should use a Schedule I bank as the primary account holder and use fintech rails such as Stripe or PayPal solely as downstream collection settlement channels, with all balances swept daily to the commercial bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.0% combined (Federal 15.0% + British Columbia 12.0%) applied at the corporate partner level; the BC Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and pays no federal or provincial income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-resident shareholders; the LP itself pays no dividend withholding tax because it is pass-through. Distributions of LP profits to non-resident partners are generally treated as allocated business income (not dividends) and are taxed under Part I or Part XIII depending on whether the partner is a resident/qualifying treaty partner and the source of the income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013 must be filed with the CRA by the last day of March following the calendar year in which the partnership's fiscal period ends; for non-calendar fiscal year-ends, the return is due within five months after the fiscal period-end. T5013 slips must be provided to each partner by the last day of February following that calendar year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. The tax balance is due within two months (3 months for qualifying CCPCs) after the fiscal year-end; instalments may be required quarterly.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 per transaction or per series of transactions between the partnership or its partners and non-resident related parties triggers the need for contemporaneous documentation under CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; country-by-country (CbC) reporting is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain an ESR filing regime as in the UAE, but underlying economic substance is enforceable through (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), which permits the CRA to disregard transactions lacking economic reality, and (ii) the treaty 'beneficial owner' and permanent establishment concepts. For the BC LP, commercial substance is evidenced by the general partner exercising central management and control in BC, employing local staff, maintaining Canadian books and records, and making business decisions in Canada. A 'royalty-free' shell LP that holds only a bank account and routes revenue through Stripe/PayPal without Canadian operations is vulnerable to GAAR reassessment and potential treaty override.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Incomplete Returns

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of the additional tax assessed, plus arrears interest; criminal conviction under ITA s. 239 carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. CRA may also seize bank accounts and garnishee the partnership's payment processing receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who directed or acquiesced in the misreporting are jointly and severally liable for the associated penalties; Canadian criminal courts may impose restitution orders, asset freezes, travel bans as bail conditions, and, upon conviction, custodial sentences.
Shell Company Arrangement / GAAR

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under ITA s. 245 denying treaty benefits and loss deductions, without the three-year limitation period where misrepresentation is involved; a GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit is levied. The CRA may also apply the 'agency permanent establishment' rules to force full Part I taxation of the non-resident partner.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for GAAR penalties if they authorised the structure; courts may order civil restitution of the avoided taxes, and travel bans may be imposed where criminal charges for inducing tax evasion are laid.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for corporations and CAD 1,000,000 for individuals; criminal prosecution for failing to report suspicious transactions or knowingly making false statements in an MSB report; seizure of funds held in the payment processor accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be prosecuted personally for directing concealment of beneficial ownership; Canadian courts can issue restraint orders over personal assets, impose travel restrictions while charges are pending, and sentence directors to imprisonment for money laundering offences.
Failure to Remit GST/PST/Payroll/Part XIII

Statutory sanction: The unremitted amounts are assessable with interest; a failure-to-remit penalty of 10% of the deficiency (plus 5% of the arrears) applies under the Excise Tax Act. The CRA may issue third-party demands to Stripe, PayPal, or financial institutions, garnishee receivables, and register a lien against the partnership's property.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 and ETA s. 323 for the full amount of unremitted GST/PST, payroll, and Part XIII withholdings, without a due-diligence defence if they failed to take reasonable care; directors may also face prosecution, court-ordered restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for participating in the concealment.
