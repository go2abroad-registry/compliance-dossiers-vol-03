Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA) — federal tax authority administering corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and CBSA import/export accounts; primary online portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). British Columbia's Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, through the eTaxBC portal (https://etaxbc.gov.bc.ca/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains the Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN), a 9-digit statutory identifier, with program account suffixes including RT (GST/HST), RP (corporate income tax), RC (payroll), and RM/RD (import-export). Step-by-step procedure for a non-resident corporation: (1) determine non-residence status and whether the corporation carries on business in Canada, has a permanent establishment, or makes taxable supplies in Canada; (2) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Registrations' with the CRA or complete online registration through the CRA Business Registration Online service; (3) provide certified copies of the foreign certificate of incorporation/articles, proof of legal existence, registered office address, officers' names and addresses, and the name and address of the Canadian agent for service (if any); (4) declare the expected first date of taxable activity and projected four-quarter worldwide taxable supplies; (5) on issuance, use the BN for GST/HST registration (RT program account) and, where relevant, corporate income tax (RP) and CBSA import/export accounts; (6) separately register for BC PST through eTaxBC using the BN and PST registration form; registration is mandatory before making taxable supplies in Canada once the small-supplier threshold is exceeded (Excise Tax Act, s.240).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is a 'taxable Canadian corporation' subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and BC provincial corporate income tax at the entity level (Entity_Level_Taxation under Canadian domestic law); it is not a statutory pass-through entity under the Income Tax Act. Cross-border, the ULC is eligible for deemed pass-through/disregarded-entity status for U.S. federal tax purposes under the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty combined with an IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election, thereby operating as a hybrid entity. GST/HST and BC PST are transactional indirect taxes and do not affect entity income classification.

Available election forms

Available elections and anti-hybrid mechanisms: (1) IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election permitting a BC/AB ULC to be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes while remaining a corporation under Canadian law — the principal pass-through/hybrid election; (2) GST/HST joint election under ETA s.156 for supplies between closely related corporations to be made for nil consideration and disregarded for net-tax purposes; (3) election to treat supplies between closely related GST/HST registrants as zero-rated; (4) no domestic Canadian check-the-box election exists — a ULC cannot elect out of corporate status for Income Tax Act purposes; (5) hybrid-mismatch rules: Canada has published revised draft legislation (2022-2024) implementing OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch recommendations, while existing anti-hybrid constraints include the foreign-affiliate-dumping tax (ITA s.212.3), the excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) rules effective for taxation years beginning after 30 September 2023, and branch-mismatch/disposition rules targeting loss duplication.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible with no distinction between capital and current account transactions for regulatory purposes. There are no surrender requirements, no quantitative restrictions on outward remittances, and no prior approval requirements for profit distributions, dividends, or capital repatriation by non-residents. Legal restrictions are limited to (i) the corporate law solvency and board-authorization tests under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act applying to a ULC, and (ii) any applicable Canadian withholding tax or Part XIII tax obligations on dividend, interest, or management-fee remittances. In practice, an inbound dividend or return of capital to a foreign parent from an unlimited liability company is permitted, but careful documentation of retained earnings and paid-up capital is required to satisfy the remitting bank's compliance diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign-currency earnings in Canadian or offshore accounts, repatriate them in whole or in part, or enter into forward contracts to manage exchange risk. The only obligations are accounting, tax reporting, and anti-money-laundering records: every transfer of CAD $10,000 or more (or its foreign-currency equivalent) must be reported to FINTRAC, and underlying trade documentation must be maintained for the statutory six-year retention period. A BC ULC exporting medical monitoring devices under ISIC 3250 must ensure that its export invoices, customs declarations, and proof of delivery are aligned with the financial settlement instructions to avoid false marking or misdeclaration in cross-border wire messaging.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; foreign exchange controls and financial-sector regulatory supervision are administered jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering compliance. Canada does not maintain a separate exchange-control authority and imposes no general foreign-exchange licensing regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for offshore digital EMIs as primary operating accounts; Medium as supplementary payment rails. Canada law does not permit a foreign non-bank e-money institution to accept deposits or provide Canadian banking-style accounts without becoming a federally incorporated bank or a licensed money-services business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. An offshore EMI can be used as a payment facilitator for incoming and outgoing transfers, but it must not hold client funds in a manner resembling a deposit, and the EMI itself must be able to provide a clear regulatory passport or proof of its own licensing status to satisfy the ULC's auditor and FINTRAC reporting framework. For an established medical-device manufacturer, reliance on an offshore EMI as the primary treasury hub is inadvisable due to instrument availability, SWIFT membership limitations, and the absence of a regulatory moat for holding capital reserves or escrowing customer deposits.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-border shareholder or director tax nexus in a jurisdiction with perceived higher money-laundering risk, or an unexplained source of funds for the inbound capital injection.

Root cause: Cross-border shareholder or director tax nexus in a jurisdiction with perceived higher money-laundering risk, or an unexplained source of funds for the inbound capital injection.

Defense evidence: Provide: certified board resolutions for the capital contribution; a complete corporate ownership chart from the ULC to its ultimate individual beneficial owners; three years of audited financial statements or a sworn statement of net worth for the parent entity; bank references from the parent's home jurisdiction; and a narrative source-of-funds letter explaining the accumulation of capital from verified operating revenues, loans, or bond issuances. Export-trade settlement mismatch, including discrepancies between commercial invoices and the stated purpose code on inbound or outbound wires related to medical-device sales.

Root cause: Export-trade settlement mismatch, including discrepancies between commercial invoices and the stated purpose code on inbound or outbound wires related to medical-device sales.

Defense evidence: Maintain a trade-file package for each export operation: signed purchase order, commercial invoice (INCO-coded), bill of lading or airway bill, customs export declaration, inspection certificate if applicable, and a bank-payment instruction clearly marked with the medical device HS/ECCN classification and end-user certification. This package should accompany every SWIFT transaction to prevent the bank from blocking a payment for reason of an unsubstantiated goods description or a dual-use product concern.
Sanctions-list hits or politically exposed person (PEP) matches on customers, suppliers, or intermediaries in the cross-border distribution network.

Root cause: Sanctions-list hits or politically exposed person (PEP) matches on customers, suppliers, or intermediaries in the cross-border distribution network.

Defense evidence: Compile a global sanctions-screening report using a commercial database (e.g., Dow Jones, Refinitiv, or Exiger), a written adverse-media check, and a documented compliance policy that screens all counterparties at onboarding and on a rolling basis. Provide the bank with a list of the ULC's direct suppliers, logistics providers, and end customers, together with a certification that the medical monitors are not destined to a sanctioned country or government end-user. If any name is a flag, the bank will require a legal opinion from Canadian counsel establishing that the transaction is permissible and eligible for a regulatory license or exception.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital to a BC ULC should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent's bank to the ULC's Canadian operational bank account. The wire instruction must clearly identify the beneficiary as the ULC, include the ULC's account number and bank identifier, and use a purpose code or description such as 'Capital Injection for Manufacturing Operations' or 'Loan Repayment - Operating Funding.' Prior to the transfer, the ULC must complete a FINTRAC large-corruption-reporting diligence if the amount exceeds CAD 10,000, and the receiving bank will require a copy of the corporate resolution authorizing the capital contribution and an updated register of shareholders. If capital is injected as a shareholder loan, a formal written loan agreement must be executed to distinguish interest and principal for Canadian withholding tax purposes; if equity, a resolution amending the share capital or an appendix to the ULC's constating documents is necessary. The routing should avoid any intermediary that is not a SWIFT member, as non-bank money transmitters may trigger manual review and delay the availability of funds.For trade settlements to suppliers and distributors, the preferred clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers and documentary commercial letters of credit (L/C) for high-value or risk-mitigated transactions. For a BC ULC exporting ECG monitors, an irrevocable, confirmed L/C opened by a foreign importer in favor of the ULC provides settlement security and is a recognized rail for cross-border bank financing. Inbound L/Cs should be advised and confirmed through the ULC's Tier-1 Canadian bank to reduce letter-of-credit confirmation charges and document-negotiation delays. Outbound disbursements for raw materials and components should be executed via MT103 with the standard 'S.W.I.F.T. GPI' tracking mechanism, and each payment must be matched to an underlying commercial invoice and customs entry to satisfy the Customs Act and the ULC's auditor. Turnover: Under the BC Business Corporations Act, there is no revenue or asset threshold that automatically imposes a statutory audit. The default rule requires an auditor at the annual general meeting; a non-reporting-issuer ULC may waive the auditor by unanimous shareholders’ resolution. A mandatory audit applies if the waiver is not unanimous, the ULC is a reporting issuer, or a financing or investor agreement requires audited financial statements.
