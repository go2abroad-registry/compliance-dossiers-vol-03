Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal GST/HST and Income Tax administration via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance — Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administration via BC eTaxBC (My Tax Account) and BC Registries.CA.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Register the limited partnership under the BC Partnership Act with BC Registries (Corporate Registry), obtaining the BC incorporation/registration number. Step 2 — Apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online, opening the GST/HST account ('RT' program) and, where applicable, payroll ('RP') and import/export ('RM'/'RE') accounts. Step 3 — Non-resident applicants must provide the foreign registration/constituency certificate, a non-resident indicator on Form RC1, appointment of a representative (RepID or Form AUT-01), and tax-residency certificates of the partners when requested by the CRA International Tax Services Office (Intake Centre). Step 4 — Register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST through eTaxBC, obtaining a BC PST vendor number, unless the business is a qualifying small seller or makes only exported non-taxable BC supplies. Step 5 — Maintain the BN for GST/HST filing, annual T5013 Partnership Information Return, payroll remittances, and zero-rating audit substantiation.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — for federal income tax, the BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent: it is not a taxpayer, the partnership computes income/loss under ITA s. 96, and each partner, including non-resident partners, is allocated a share for Canadian tax purposes; for GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate deemed 'person' only when it carries on business in Canada and has a place of business in Canada (ETA s. 272.1), and the partnership itself registers for GST/HST with its own BN. — Step 1 — Register the limited partnership under the BC Partnership Act with BC Registries (Corporate Registry), obtaining the BC incorporation/registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — for federal income tax, the BC limited partnership is fiscally transparent: it is not a taxpayer, the partnership computes income/loss under ITA s. 96, and each partner, including non-resident partners, is allocated a share for Canadian tax purposes; for GST/HST, the partnership is treated as a separate deemed 'person' only when it carries on business in Canada and has a place of business in Canada (ETA s. 272.1), and the partnership itself registers for GST/HST with its own BN.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election; classification is statutory. Relevant elections: (1) joint GST/HST election under ETA s. 273 to designate one partner as the reporting/collecting partner for the partnership's taxable supplies; (2) optional fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1 to fix the partnership's taxation year; (3) ITA s. 216(1) election enabling a non-resident partner to file a Canadian return and pay tax on certain Canadian-source passive income; (4) hybrid-mismatch relief is non-elective — ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5 deny a Canadian deduction where the corresponding amount is not included in the counterparty jurisdiction, including through reverse-hybrid limited partnership structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST — CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the four preceding calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold; non-residents not carrying on business in Canada are not required to register but may voluntarily register). BC PST — no general small-supplier exemption for in-BC vendors; out-of-province and foreign sellers of taxable services and goods (including software and telecommunications services) must register once annual B.C. gross taxable sales exceed CAD 10,000; registration is compulsory once the applicable threshold is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST/HST) applies to a supply of consultancy and advisory services (ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541110) made to a non-resident recipient where: (i) the recipient is not resident in Canada and is not registered, and is not required to be registered, for GST/HST; (ii) the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada; and (iii) the service is performed and delivered to the non-resident client outside the Canadian consumption nexus, supported by contemporaneous contractual and delivery evidence. BC PST does not apply to exported services performed or delivered outside British Columbia to a non-resident customer; where PST has been charged in error, the vendor may issue a credit note and claim a refund/relief through the BC Ministry of Finance, subject to the filing of an amended PST return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System CERS filing) — required only where the cross-border engagement includes physical goods; pure consulting services ordinarily require no customs export declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or equivalent service-delivery record — for services, a signed Statement of Work, delivery log, and electronic transmission record evidencing offshore performance and delivery to the non-resident recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank confirmation — documentary proof of receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident payer, with the invoice reference linked to the engagement file

Canada has no continuous-transaction-control (CTC) real-time clearance, no digital fiscal stamping, and no mandatory pre-clearance API for e-invoices at the federal or provincial level; the CRA operates a self-assessment, return-and-audit system with mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants; BC PST returns and remittances are filed through eTaxBC; commercial invoices must display the vendor's GST/HST registration number, the applicable PST registration number, the rates, and the tax amounts separately, and for export transactions the invoice should expressly identify the non-resident recipient and the zero-rated nature of the supply to preserve the audit trail.

The CRA may reverse the zero-rating where the audit trail fails to prove the recipient's non-resident status, foreign destination, or extraterritorial delivery, reassessing GST/HST plus compound interest within the normal four-year reassessment period (seven years for misrepresentation or neglect, and without limitation for fraud). Clawback exposures include: (i) false-statement and omission penalties equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the reassessed net tax; (ii) failure-to-register and failure-to-remit penalties together with daily interest under the Excise Tax Act; (iii) gross-negligence penalties; and (iv) criminal prosecution for tax evasion, carrying court-imposed fines and imprisonment. Fabricated, backdated, or internally inconsistent export documentation — including uncollected foreign currency, stale delivery logs, or invoices to shell recipients — is a recurring audit red flag, and the CRA may exchange non-resident recipient information with partner treaty jurisdictions under tax-information-exchange arrangements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million aggregate transactions with non-residents or CAD 50 million gross revenue

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific ESR but applies GAAR and substance-over-form; partnerships must have real economic substance, including office, employees, and business purpose

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239 with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s.227.1; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 25% of tax benefit; CRA may impose third-party penalties

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in sham arrangements
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations, CAD 500,000 for individuals; criminal charges under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years for willful non-compliance
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of income, penalty of 10% of net adjustment (if exceeds CAD 5 million), plus interest; double taxation risk

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they approved or ignored TP policies; CRA can pursue personal assets
