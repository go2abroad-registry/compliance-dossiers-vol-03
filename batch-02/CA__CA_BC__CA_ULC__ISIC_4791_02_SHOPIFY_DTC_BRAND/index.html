Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under Part I Income Tax Act (federal 15% net of abatement + BC provincial 12% ≈ 27% combined) for Canadian domestic purposes; for US purposes it is eligible for check-the-box treatment as a disregarded entity or partnership, and a Canada-US Tax Treaty Art. IV(7) election can confirm fiscally transparent treatment. Not eligible for domestic Canadian pass-through.

Available election forms

US Form 8832 check-the-box election (disregarded entity/partnership classification); Canada-US Tax Treaty Article IV(7) fiscally transparent entity election for ULCs; ETA s. 156 deemed nil-supply election between closely related corporations; ETA s. 157 small supplier election to defer GST/HST registration (imprudent for exporters needing ITCs); BC PST has no analogous elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST (ETA s. 148(1) small supplier threshold); CAD 30,000 of taxable sales in a single calendar year for BC PST. Shopify DTC revenue from goods, shipping, and digital products counts toward both thresholds on a rolling basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods shipped by the ULC to destinations outside Canada are zero-rated (0% GST, no BC PST) under s. 1, Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided export occurs within 60 days of the sale or within such period as is reasonable in the circumstances. Services performed on goods destined for export and digital services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under ss. 2–3 and ss. 7–10, Part V, Schedule VI. The ULC must prove the goods were actually exported and that title transferred outside Canada; exports via Shopify to foreign consumers qualify where the seller delivers to a foreign address or international carrier.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) for shipments valued at CAD 2,000 or more
  • Commercial invoice itemizing product description, value, HS tariff classification, seller and foreign buyer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international carriage
  • Packing list
  • Carrier customs manifest or FedEx/UPS worldwide export confirmation
  • Shopify Payments / PayPal / Stripe settlement statement showing foreign-currency remittance
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a Canadian bank confirming conversion of export sale proceeds

None — Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing API, or digital stamping. CRA requires electronic NETFILE for GST/HST when taxable supplies exceed CAD 1M; all Shopify order records, invoices, and payment settlement files must be retained six years under CRA books-and-records rules. BC PST returns and payments are filed through eTaxBC on a monthly, quarterly, or semi-annual schedule determined by the Consumer Taxation Branch.

CRA may reassess within four years for ordinary audit and without time limit for misrepresentation or fraud; export zero-rating is retroactively clawed back if documentary evidence is missing, resulting in 5% GST assessed on the gross sale plus compound daily interest (currently approx. 9% per annum) and penalties of 6% of unreported tax plus 6% per full month late (ETA s. 280), 50% gross negligence penalty (ETA s. 285), and criminal fines up to CAD 25,000 plus imprisonment for wilful evasion (ETA s. 327.1). BC PST audit has a five-year lookback, 13.2% annual interest, and penalties up to 20% of tax, rising to 25% for gross negligence. CRA data-mining of Shopify merchant records and carrier customs manifests is routine for DTC exporters; while Canada has no statutory FX repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is used by CRA as an indicator of a bona fide export sale.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible reserve currency under the Currency Act and Canada's International Monetary Fund Article VIII obligations. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are liberalized; no exchange-control approval is required for trade proceeds, dividends, interest, royalties, or capital repatriation. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%, generally reduced to 5% under the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention for a U.S. corporate parent beneficially owning at least 10% of the voting shares, with 0% potentially available in qualifying holding structures). A British Columbia unlimited liability corporation (BC ULC) remains a Canadian resident corporation for non-resident withholding purposes notwithstanding any U.S. check-the-box pass-through election, so distributions to a U.S. parent are subject to Canadian withholding and must be remitted through a Canadian authorized bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within a prescribed timeline, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian authorized financial institutions. However, international electronic funds transfers of CAD $10,000 or more (or the foreign-currency equivalent) must be reported to FINTRAC within statutory timeframes, and the exporter must maintain Canadian-dollar books and records for Income Tax Act purposes unless the functional-currency reporting election is made for the business.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with supervisory coordination by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and cross-border transaction reporting administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not operate a centralized foreign exchange administration or exchange-control licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie) as the primary operating banking layer. Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), any entity carrying on a money-services business in Canada must be registered with FINTRAC, maintain a Canadian compliance program, and have a principal place of business in Canada; pure offshore EMIs without Canadian MSB registration may not lawfully hold the BC ULC's operating funds or originate settlement payments on its behalf. Offshore EMIs are acceptable only as auxiliary clearing agents for supplier settlement or cross-currency conversion, while the ULC must maintain statutory capital and transaction accounts at a Canadian-authorized bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Current good-standing certificate from the BC Corporate Registry; notice of articles and articles of incorporation; unanimous shareholder agreement; corporate register listing all directors and ultimate beneficial owners; IRS Forms 8832/5472 demonstrating the pass-through election; 24-month financial projections evidencing commercial substance; statutory declaration of source and purpose of injected capital; and confirmation of the registered and records office in British Columbia.
High-volume, low-value cross-border e-commerce receipts through Shopify Payments, PayPal, or Stripe without formal commercial contracts.

Root cause: High-volume, low-value cross-border e-commerce receipts through Shopify Payments, PayPal, or Stripe without formal commercial contracts.

Defense evidence: Executed Shopify Payments merchant processing agreement and platform terms; documented settlement schedules and payout statements; sample order history, refund policy, and chargeback report; payment processor terminal reports; mapping of platform settlement to the ULC's operating account; and a transaction-monitoring narrative covering velocity, average ticket size, and geographic concentration.
Third-party or pre-paid disbursements to overseas suppliers and associated-party payees characteristic of DTC supply-chain models.

Root cause: Third-party or pre-paid disbursements to overseas suppliers and associated-party payees characteristic of DTC supply-chain models.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the U.S. parent must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (Fedwire for U.S.-dollar legs) to the BC ULC's Canadian-dollar or U.S.-dollar account at a Schedule I bank, with full beneficiary name, the ULC's BC incorporation number, and the payment narrative 'Unlimited liability company capital contribution / equity funding' (country purpose code 27A or equivalent). International EFTs of CAD $10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC. For U.S.-dollar injections, banks may credit a U.S.-dollar operating account and subsequently perform a spot conversion, or the sender may prepay the bank's standing conversion instruction to avoid double FX conversion. The sending institution is required to screen the U.S. parent against OFAC and other sanctions lists, and the receiving bank independently screens the ULC. A corporate resolution authorizing the capital contribution and the underlying share subscription agreement should be kept in the KYC file, although no exchange-control approval is required.Supplier trade settlements are cleared principally through SWIFT MT103 single-payment credits for optical-goods components, accessories, and freight forwarders; MT202 treasury transfers may be used for interbank cover where the vendor maintains a concentration account. Payments Canada rail infrastructure (Lynx for high-value real-time gross settlement and the Real-Time Rail for domestic fast payments) handles the Canadian leg, while correspondent banks complete the cross-border leg.
