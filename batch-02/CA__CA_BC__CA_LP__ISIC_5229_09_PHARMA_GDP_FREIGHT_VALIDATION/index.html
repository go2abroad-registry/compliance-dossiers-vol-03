Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); portal: Canada.ca Business Registration Online (BRO) via Business Registration Online at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; provincial PST administered by BC Ministry of Finance via eTaxBC (etax.gov.bc.ca)

E-government filing portal

The LP must first register a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with the CRA. For a partnership, the partnership itself obtains a BN in the name of the LP; each general partner (and any corporate partner) must have its own separate BN/TIN. Registration can be completed online through Business Registration Online (BRO) in one session; alternatively file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'. The LP must also open a GST/HST account (RT prefix) unless eligible for the $30,000 CAD small-supplier threshold; if the LP will provide zero-rated exported freight services, voluntary registration is strongly advised to reclaim input tax credits. For non-resident partners, the partnership must provide each partner's foreign tax identification number and may need a CRA non-resident account. The BC PST account must be registered separately via eTaxBC (BC Financial Services Authority / Ministry of Finance), and a PST registration is mandatory regardless of volume for any person making taxable sales of goods in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The LP is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c. 1). No entity-level federal or BC corporate income tax is imposed on the partnership; each partner computes and reports its allocable share of partnership income/loss, and the fiscal year-end is mandated as December 31 (deemed year-end rule, s. 99 ETA does not apply; Income Tax Act s. 249.1). For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is an independent 'person' and must generally account for GST/HST on its supplies, with an entitlement to input tax credits. The LP is resident in Canada for tax purposes if the majority of its general partners are resident in Canada; non-resident limited partners are subject to Canadian tax on Canadian-source income through deemed carrying-on of business rules and Part XIII withholding on certain allocations (with s. 115 and s. 2(3) application). Territorial: income earned inside Canada is taxed on a residence-based/worldwide basis for resident partners; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Canada. Elections available include: (i) GST/HST quick method election (election to use a simplified accounting method); (ii) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations (not available to LPs directly); (iii) section 216 election for non-residents with rental income; (iv) deemed partnership year-end election under s. 249.1(2); (v) for GST/HST, an election to file annually/monthly, and the 'net tax' calculation election under ETA s. 225.1; (vi) the BC PST does not provide pass-through or grouping elections - registration and remittance is mandatory. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (s. 18.2, 18.3 of the Income Tax Act, effective 2023) disallow certain deductions in dual-entity/dual-inclusion mismatches; no elective hybrid alignment system exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) for GST/HST small-supplier threshold; BC PST has no small-supplier exemption - registration is mandatory for any vendor making taxable sales of goods in the province; however, a PST exemption certificate (Export) applies to goods delivered outside BC

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Canada qualify for 0% GST/HST under s. 6(1) of Schedule VI to the ETA, provided the goods are exported 'as soon as is reasonable' after transfer of possession/ownership and the supplier retains proof of export. Freight transportation services for goods exported from Canada (origin in Canada, destination outside Canada) are zero-rated under s. 7 of Schedule VI. Freight forwarding and arrangement services rendered regarding exported goods to a non-resident are zero-rated under s. 9 of Schedule VI where the service is supplied to a non-resident and the service relates to tangible personal property exported from Canada. A GDP pharma logistics provider arranging cold-chain export shipments for a non-resident principal may therefore zero-rate both the arranging fee and the underlying transportation/handling charge, provided the supporting documentation links each supply to a specific export movement. Health Canada's Good Distribution Practice (GDP) licence is a prerequisite for handling pharma products but is not a tax condition. If the service is supplied to a Canadian resident recipient, it is taxable at 5% GST regardless of the goods' destination.

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government API stamping, and no compulsory digital signature regime for GST/HST. The CRA accepts electronic invoices under the ETA s. 169 record-keeping rules; suppliers must retain readable records in Canada for 6 years. Mandatory e-invoicing is being phased in only for federal government procurement through the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network (voluntary for businesses). BC PST similarly has no clearance mandate. For pharma GDP freight, a Mandatory electronic data interchange (EDI) or GLN-based temperature records are a Health Canada GDP requirement, not a CRA tax mandate. Customs export reporting must be filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES) - the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) requires eManifest for all commercial carriers; the exporter must transmit export data to CBSA within 15 days of export (General Export Permit No. 45 - Export Reporting).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible current-account and capital-account regime without any requirement for exchange-control permits. Inbound capital contributions to a British Columbia Limited Partnership (CA_LP) are freely credited to the operating account; repatriation of contributed capital and distribution of partnership profits to non-resident limited partners are unrestricted. Fiscal friction points are tax-only, not FX-based: (i) a non-resident corporate partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP is taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on its share of Canadian-source business income and may be liable to Part XIV branch tax (25%, treaty-reduced); (ii) partnership distributions are not subject to Part XIII withholding, but interest, royalties, or management fees paid to non-resident partners could attract 25% treaty-reduced Part XIII withholding; (iii) the Investment Canada Act applies separate notification thresholds for acquisitions of control of a Canadian business - a passive, non-controlling capital contribution for GDP freight operations requires no pre-clearance. No profit-remittance hurdles exist at either the federal or the British Columbia provincial level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio in Canada. All export receipts earned by ISIC 5229 freight-arrangement and GDP logistics activities may be retained in the invoiced foreign currency or converted at the operator's discretion; there is no legal timeline for repatriation. Supporting obligations are documentary: foreign-currency invoices must be translated using Bank of Canada daily noon exchange rates for GST/HST and income-tax filings, and records must be retained for at least six years under the Income Tax Act. Cross-border monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC reporting by the financial intermediary, not by the exporting entity.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and issuer of the Canadian dollar; prudential supervision of chartered banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border transaction monitoring and currency-transaction reporting are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and no foreign-exchange administration or capital-control approval authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (restricted to the disbursement layer only). Canada does not issue EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences. Cross-border fintech providers (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Ebury) must register as Money Services Businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC and either rely on partnership banking or obtain restricted deposit-taking authorisation; they cannot hold funds on the same legal footing as a Schedule I bank, are not CDIC-insured, and provide no statutory trust-account or escrow capability. They are legally acceptable for routine operational supplier payments in multiple currencies, but are not viable for the capital-escrow base, Health Canada GDP-licensed regulatory deposits, or Investment Canada Act evidentiary capital clearing. The statutory liquidity and capital base must sit in a regulated Canadian financial institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) remittance must originate exclusively from the partner's own verified bank account, never from a third-party aggregator; (2) route via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's designated CAD operating account at a Schedule I chartered bank, with the beneficiary field carrying the LP's exact legal name and BC registry number; (3) the payment narrative must state 'Capital contribution - investment in Canadian limited partnership (ISIC 5229 freight forwarding services) - non-controlling' and must never be abbreviated to 'consulting' or 'fees'; (4) the receiving bank will report each incoming international electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC, and the LP should provide its capital-account statement line confirming the booking; (5) the bank will verify the wire against the capital-call resolution and the executed partnership agreement; (6) escrowed injections close through a short-form escrow agreement with the bank's commercial division, releasing upon presentation of the BC registration and CRA GST/HST account confirmation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (restricted to the disbursement layer only). Canada does not issue EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences. Cross-border fintech providers (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Ebury) must register as Money Services Businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC and either rely on partnership banking or obtain restricted deposit-taking authorisation; they cannot hold funds on the same legal footing as a Schedule I bank, are not CDIC-insured, and provide no statutory trust-account or escrow capability. They are legally acceptable for routine operational supplier payments in multiple currencies, but are not viable for the capital-escrow base, Health Canada GDP-licensed regulatory deposits, or Investment Canada Act evidentiary capital clearing.
