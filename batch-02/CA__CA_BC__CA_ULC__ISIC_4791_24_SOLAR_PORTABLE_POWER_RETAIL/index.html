Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) via 'My Business Account' portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial sales tax authority for British Columbia: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (gov.bc.ca/consumertaxes).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN), which serves as the federal tax identification number. For GST/HST, the entity registers for a federal account (RT) using form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'. Registration can be completed online through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO, now integrated into My Business Account). For BC PST, the entity must separately register with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch via the provincial eTaxBC system and obtain a PST registration number. ULCs, as Canadian corporations, require a BN, a GST/HST account if taxable supplies exceed the threshold, and a BC PST account if making PST-taxable retail sales in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is a corporation under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and is generally subject to federal corporate income tax on its worldwide income (as a resident corporation). Non-resident shareholders may also be exposed under subsection 2(2) for Canadian-source income. However, for US federal tax purposes a ULC may achieve pass-through via a check-the-box election; for Canadian income tax it remains a separate taxpayer.

Available election forms

No general 'check-the-box' regime exists in Canadian federal income tax. A ULC may elect under subsection 34.1(1) or similar provisions only in limited circumstances. The 'check-the-box' classification available under US Internal Revenue Code Section 7701 is elected at the US shareholder level, not at the Canadian entity level. Hybrid mismatch rules under section 18.1 of the Income Tax Act apply to deny deduction of interest or royalty payments that give rise to non-inclusion or double deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies of goods and services over a consecutive four-calendar-quarter period (or a single supply over that amount). BC PST: no general small-supplier exemption applies for e-commerce sellers; a 'seller' carrying on business in BC must register and remit PST on taxable sales, including portable battery generators and solar panels, unless a specific exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for GST/HST applies to 'exports' where tangible personal property is exported from Canada by the supplier or a related party and is not acquired by a consumer in Canada. For goods sold online and shipped from Canada to a final consumer outside Canada, the supply meets the export criteria under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the goods are exported within 60 days of the invoice date and documentary evidence is retained. Offshore services that are performed for a non-resident, and where the benefit is received outside Canada, may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI. BC PST does not apply to goods sold and shipped outside BC; however, if goods are also for consumption in BC, PST is collectible.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13) or authorized alternative proof of export
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill showing delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Outbound customs invoice or commercial invoice
  • Proof of payment (e.g., Stripe settlement record or bank remittance credit)
  • Written evidence that the buyer is not a Canadian resident and the goods are not acquired for use in Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (if required by financial institution for supporting export proceeds and FX repatriation)

Canada has no mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, government clearance gateway, or digital stamping requirement. E-invoicing is optional and primarily used in B2B procurement; CRA accepts supported PDF or XML invoices. As of the knowledge cut-off, there is no mandatory e-invoice clearance model comparable to Italy or Spain. BC PST does not impose invoice stamping or real-time reporting.

CRA may audit export zero-rating claims and deny zero-rating if documentary evidence is insufficient or false. Penalties include: (i) 5% penalty on net GST/HST remittance error plus 1% per month interest (up to 12%) under the Excise Tax Act; (ii) serious false statements penalties under subsection 285.01(1) of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 5,000 and 25% of the quantum; (iii) notice of assessment reopening within 4 years (extended to 7 years for misrepresentation); (iv) income tax gross-up and interest; (v) criminal prosecution for fraudulent export schemes. Retrospective clawback of input tax credits and disallowance of zero-rated status can occur if the exporter cannot demonstrate export within the statutory timeframe or cannot link payments to specific outbound shipments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility consistent with IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange controls, no prior approvals for foreign direct investment, and no statutory restrictions on inbound equity subscription, dividend repatriation, interest, royalties, intercompany loan servicing, share redemptions or liquidation proceeds. The Canadian Dollar (CAD) is the functional currency of the CA_ULC, while USD-denominated operating accounts are freely permitted at Schedule I banks. Profit remittance from a British Columbia ULC is legally unrestricted, but outbound dividends to arm's-length non-residents attract 25% Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), subject to treaty reduction: under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention, dividends beneficially owned by a U.S. corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the ULC's voting shares are generally reduced to 5%, and portfolio dividends to 15%. Given the ULC's unlimited liability and check-the-box flow-through attributes, distributions, redemptions and capital reductions must be carefully documented to avoid recharacterisation as deemed dividends under section 84 of the Income Tax Act and to preserve treaty eligibility. Remittances in cash or bearer negotiable instruments equal to or above CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC large transaction reporting; all cross-border wires are subject to OFAC/UN/Canada sanctions screening by the clearing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, no mandatory FX conversion ratios, no repatriation windows and no requirement to route export receivables through domestic bank accounts. Export receipts earned by the CA_ULC from U.S. and global customers via Stripe and Affirm may be retained in USD sub-accounts or converted freely. However, commercial goods exported from Canada with a value of CAD 2,000 or above require a CBSA B13-A Export Declaration with harmonized system classification of the portable solar power equipment, and the corresponding merchandise trade statistics must be reported to Statistics Canada. Evidence of export (bills of lading, courier manifests, customs entry copies) must be retained to substantiate zero-rated GST/HST treatment of exported goods and to support income sourcing on the T2 corporate return. No separate FX reporting to the Bank of Canada is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising monetary and financial stability authority under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act; banking prudential supervision delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act; AML/CFT supervision, large transaction reporting and currency flow monitoring administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no standalone FX administrative authority comparable to a dedicated exchange-control regulator.

Medium-High as a treasury overlay rail, but legally unsuitable as the primary deposit-taking bank for the CA_ULC. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) may not carry on deposit-taking business in Canada without a charter under the Bank Act, and are prohibited from using the word bank to describe their services (Bank Act, s. 687). Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share subscription proceeds from the U.S. parent or non-resident shareholders must be wired by SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary being the CA_ULC's designated capital escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, using the remittance information field: CA_ULC SHARE SUBSCRIPTION 2025-X, SHARE CLASS, NUMBER OF SHARES AND ISSUE PRICE. Canadian banks require a source-of-funds declaration for inbound wires of CAD 10,000 or above, and the account file must contain board resolutions subscribing the shares, an updated share register identifying each beneficial owner at the 25% threshold, and the ULC's tax classification election. Investment from a non-resident holding 10% or more of the ULC's voting equity must be reported to Statistics Canada in the quarterly balance-of-payments direct investment survey. Routing all subscriptions through the capital escrow account preserves the clean equity tracking required to avoid deemed dividend issues under section 84 and supports the thin capitalisation 1.5:1 debt-to-equity safe harbour if intercompany debt is later advanced.Import disbursements for portable solar generators and solar panels should be executed via SWIFT MT103 (GPI-enabled) in USD or CAD, with the 70 remittance information referencing the purchase order number and model SKUs. For larger or extended-term settlements, a sight commercial letter of credit (ICC UCP 600) or a Bank Payment Obligation (BPO) may be used where the supplier's bank participates on the ICC URBPO framework. Domestic Canadian disbursements (payroll, logistics, provincial corporate costs) settle via EFT over the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS for high-value same-day items; USD payables to U.S. fulfillment centres and third-party logistics providers clear via the ULC's correspondent Fedwire/CHIPS relationship. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High as a treasury overlay rail, but legally unsuitable as the primary deposit-taking bank for the CA_ULC. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) may not carry on deposit-taking business in Canada without a charter under the Bank Act, and are prohibited from using the word bank to describe their services (Bank Act, s. 687). Balances held with offshore EMIs are unsecured contractual claims outside CDIC deposit insurance. Providers offering money services in Canada must be registered as Money Services Businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC, or rely on a substantially similar foreign AML regime. The recommended structure is to maintain the SA_UOC settlement account, CAD operating account and USD account at a Schedule I domestic bank, and to use EMI rails exclusively for low-cost FX conversion, cross-border supplier payouts and marketplace sweeps, never for treasury cash concentration.
