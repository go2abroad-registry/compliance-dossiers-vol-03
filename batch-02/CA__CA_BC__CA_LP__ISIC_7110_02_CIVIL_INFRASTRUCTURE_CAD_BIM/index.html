Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - federal income tax, GST/HST, and payroll administration. Registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO); ongoing compliance portal: CRA My Business Account (GST/HST NETFILE, RP/RT/RC program accounts). The provincial retail levy is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Register the BC Limited Partnership under Part 3 of the Partnership Act (BC) using BC Corporate Online / OneStop Business Registry; obtain the BC registration number and the LP's legal-name certificate. Step 2 - Apply to the CRA for the partnership's 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Accounts) or BRO; request program accounts RP (partnership income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RC (payroll) if the LP will have employees. The BN is issued in the partnership's legal name; non-resident limited partners do not automatically receive a Canadian tax ID and file income tax only if they derive Canadian-source income from the LP. Step 3 - If the LP lacks a Canadian place of business for correspondence, appoint a Canadian tax representative and file Form RC59 (Business Consent) so CRA documents may be served. Step 4 - Register for BC PST via eTaxBC only if the LP makes enumerated taxable retail sales in BC (exported CAD/BIM services to non-residents are not PST-taxable). Step 5 - File the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return; the partnership's fiscal period is statutorily the calendar year (ITA s. 249.1(1)) and the LP must allocate income/loss in accordance with the partnership agreement and ITA s. 96.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax: the LP computes income as if it were a separate person but pays no entity-level income tax; each partner reports its allocated share of income and may claim foreign tax credits where applicable (ITA s. 96). For GST/HST purposes, the LP is statutorily deemed a distinct 'person' (Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 272.1(2)) and, if it is a registrant, invoices and accounts for GST/HST in its own name. Territorial scope: a Canadian partnership managed and controlled in BC; non-resident ownership is permitted and does not disturb the LP's transparent classification or its eligibility to zero-rate exports.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' election; classification follows domestic legal form. Practical elections and deeming rules applicable to the LP: (1) Voluntary GST/HST registration by a 'small supplier' (ETA s. 240(1)) to claim input tax credits on Canadian inputs; (2) Joint election under ETA s. 156(2) for supplies between 'closely related persons' (generally inapplicable to arms-length BIM clients); (3) No election is available to depart from the calendar-year partnership fiscal period without CRA concurrence; (4) Where a foreign jurisdiction characterizes the LP as an entity (hybrid), CRA follows Canadian law and applies the hybrid mismatch ordering rules (ITA ss. 18.2-18.3) only to interest/deduction mismatches; no classification election exists; (5) A partner may elect to compute its share of partnership income using an alternative reasonable allocation method only with CRA acceptance in a disclosure filed with the T5013 return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - GST/HST registration threshold (ETA s. 148(1), 'small supplier'): the LP must register if its worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) in the current calendar quarter and all four preceding calendar quarters exceed CAD 30,000. Zero-rated supplies count as taxable supplies for this test. If the LP's only supplies are zero-rated exports, it remains eligible for voluntary registration but is not obliged to register unless the threshold is crossed; a Canadian-resident partnership carrying on business in BC that exceeds CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies is mandatorily registrant. PST registration is separate and arises only on enumerated taxable retail sales or taxable services with a BC nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP's offshore BIM, structural drafting, and CAD services supplied to a non-resident client are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 (a supply of a service to a non-resident person, unless the service is supplied to an individual present in Canada, relates to real property situated in Canada, relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is a personal service rendered to the non-resident in Canada). If the deliverable is transferred as downloadable CAD models/software, zero-rating may alternatively apply under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 10 (supply of intangible personal property to a non-resident who is not registered and who does not acquire the property for consumption/use in Canada). Conditions: the recipient must be a bona fide non-resident, not registered for GST/HST, and must not acquire the service for use in Canada; the service must not relate to Canadian real estate or goods (a Canadian infrastructure site would cause the supply to be taxable at 5% GST); the exporter must retain contemporaneous documentary evidence. No CRA export permit is required for services, but a B13A Export Declaration is required if physical deliverables (e.g., hard drives containing CAD files) leave Canada with a value exceeding CAD 2,000.

  • Executed commercial contract / statement of work identifying the non-resident client, the offshore deliverable, and the technical specification of the BIM/CAD services
  • Commercial invoice bearing the LP's BN and the notation 'GST/HST zero-rated - ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 / s. 10'
  • Evidence of the recipient's non-resident status: foreign certificate of incorporation, foreign business registry extract, or a written attestation on client letterhead
  • B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration (only when physical CAD media crosses the border with value exceeding CAD 2,000)
  • Inward remittance records evidencing foreign-source payment: SWIFT MT103, Wise transfer receipt, or bank statement showing funds received from abroad
  • T5013 Partnership Information Return and partner allocation schedules (to enable CRA cross-verification of reported offshore revenue)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing API exists in Canada. The CRA accepts paper, PDF, EDI, XML, and JSON invoices; cross-border interoperability is being adopted voluntarily via the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network (PEPPOL BIS 3.0), with phased federal procurement use expected by 2025-2026, but there is no pre-clearance or CRA authorization step. Statutory invoice content under the GST/HST Regulations s. 3: supplier legal name and BN, invoice date, description of services, amount, GST rate and tax amount, and an express 'zero-rated' notation citing ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 and/or s. 10. Electronic records must be retained in an intelligible format for six years (ITA s. 230; ETA s. 286(1)) and produced on CRA demand.

CRA audits zero-rated export claims against the evidentiary framework in GST/HST Memorandum 4.5.2: recipient identity, non-Canadian use, and place of performance. If substantiation fails or the arrangement is a sham, CRA will: (1) reassess the supply as taxable at 5% GST and collect unremitted tax plus daily compounding interest (ETA s. 296); (2) deny input tax credits allocated to the export and claw back refunds for the four-year assessment window, with no time limit where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud exists (ETA s. 298(3); ITA s. 152(4)(a)(i)); (3) impose civil penalties - ETA s. 280 (CAD 250 to CAD 25,000 for false statements), ETA s. 285 (up to 100% of the tax sought to be evaded), and ITA s. 163(2) (50% of the understated tax for gross negligence); and (4) prosecute criminally under ETA s. 327 / ITA s. 239 (fines from 50% to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment up to 5 years). Note on the 180-day field: Canadian indirect-tax law imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline for exported services; the 180-day figure reflects banking/AML settlement policy and transfer-pricing prudence - unreasonably deferred collection of foreign receivables may trigger adjustment under ITA s. 247(2) (arm's-length pricing of intercompany receivables) but is not an independent GST/HST clawback trigger.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating currency under an open capital-account regime; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, and both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without prior approval, licensing, registration, or quota. The only remittance hurdles are tax-related rather than exchange-related: distributions of Canadian-source income to non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding under the Income Tax Act (Canada), normally reduced to 15% or less under an applicable tax treaty, and each cross-border electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more triggers a FINTRAC EFT report by the financial institution. Non-resident limited partnerships and non-resident partners face no restriction on holding, converting, or repatriating CAD or foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None apply in Canada. There is no statutory timeline, no minimum surrender ratio, and no mandatory conversion requirement for export receipts. BIM and engineering drafting services exported to non-residents are zero-rated for GST/HST purposes where the recipient is not registered and is not present in Canada, but the CAD proceeds may be retained in any currency and any licensed financial institution. The only compliance obligations are FINTRAC EFT reporting above CAD 10,000, the five-year record-retention duty under the PCMLTFA, and standard corporate record-keeping for the British Columbia limited partnership.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary and currency-issuance authority, with prudential bank supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and physical-currency declaration enforcement by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration or exchange-control ministry.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium, with strict scope limitations. Wise Payments Canada Inc. is registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC but is not a Canadian Schedule I bank, holds no OSFI banking charter, is not a member of Payments Canada clearing systems, and carries no CDIC deposit insurance. Feasible use: low-value operational disbursements (small subcontractor payments, minor CAD vendor invoices) as a secondary rail alongside the primary banking relationship. Not feasible as the primary treasury or escrow rail because it cannot issue commercial or standby letters of credit, cannot participate in Lynx-based CAD clearing, offers no committed credit or FX line, and fails the bank-control expectation for capital-contribution routing. Recommended policy: route all inbound LP capital contributions exclusively to a Schedule I bank account and use Wise only for already-cleared operating surplus funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route as a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202/202 COV for interbank transfers) to the LP's CAD or USD account at the chosen Schedule I bank. Remittance information in field 70 must state: 'LP capital contribution - subscription under Limited Partnership Agreement dated [DD MMM YYYY]'. Include the ISO 20022 ExternalPurpose code, commonly CAPT (capital payment) or INTC (intercompany payment), in the bank's purpose-code tag or field 77B. Canada has no SAFE-style purpose-code registry; however, the receiving bank will file a FINTRAC EFT report for any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more and will require: (i) the BC partnership registration extract; (ii) the executed subscription document; (iii) FATCA/CRS self-certifications (CRS-A01/CAC forms) for each partner; and (iv) written confirmation that the funds are an equity contribution, not a loan. CAD credits typically settle same-day or T+1; USD credits settle T+1 to T+2 through New York correspondent clearing (e.g., the bank's U.S. affiliate). Capital received before the operating drawdown schedule should be parked in a separately titled Capital Escrow Account to prevent contamination of operating cash-flow reporting.Clearing rails for the BIM service-export model: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct credits for one-off subcontractor and vendor payments (e.g., Autodesk licensing, structural-engineering consultant fees) with appropriate ISO 20022 purpose codes such as TRAD or ROYA and a full field-70 narrative; (2) Payments Canada Lynx for CAD disbursements above CAD 100,000 and ACSS/EFT for smaller CAD payments, executed through the bank's host-to-host e-payment files rather than individual MT103s; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) or Standby LCs (ISP98) only when a client or supplier contractually requires bank-issued payment assurance, which is atypical for pure CAD/BIM drafting; a documents-against-payment collection or direct SWIFT credit is normally preferred; (4) SWIFT GPI tracking for time-critical cross-border disbursements. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (LP is fiscally transparent; income taxed at partner level. For reference, combined federal and BC corporate rate is 27%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII withholding on non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source income; treaty may reduce to 5%–15% for dividends, but for partnership allocations, the rate is typically 25% unless treaty applies)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for goods, CAD 500,000 for services, CAD 250,000 for intangibles, interest, or royalties (per transaction with non-arm's length non-residents)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal ESR regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. The LP must demonstrate real economic activity (e.g., employees, office, decision-making) in BC to avoid recharacterization of transactions or denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest and penalties. Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239) can result in imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the evasion. Travel bans and seizure of personal assets may apply.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize transactions, and impose transfer pricing penalties (up to 10% of the net adjustment). GAAR can apply to nullify tax benefits.

Director personal liability: General partners may be held personally liable for the partnership's tax liabilities if the partnership is deemed a sham. Directors of corporate partners may face similar liability.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities. Criminal penalties for money laundering under the Criminal Code can result in imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners can be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly failed to comply. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA can adjust prices, impose penalties up to 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation is inadequate), and charge interest on reassessments.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate partners may be liable for penalties if they directed the non-compliant pricing. Personal liability for the partnership's tax debt may arise if the partnership is insolvent.
