Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office satisfies the registered office requirement under BCA s. 43 only if a physical British Columbia street address is provided for service of process and the premises are staffed or monitored during normal business hours. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada); portal: My Business Account (canada.ca/my-business-account) and Business Registration Online; BC PST administered separately by BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A BC ULC incorporated under the Business Corporations Act (SBC 2002, c. 57) is a taxable Canadian corporation. Procedure: (1) incorporate via BC Corporate Online; (2) obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or via CRA Business Registration Online, receiving the RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RM (import-export) program accounts; (3) register for GST/HST once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000, or voluntarily; (4) register for BC PST once taxable sales exceed CAD 10,000 in 12 months and for BC Employer Health Tax when payroll exceeds CAD 500,000; (5) for non-resident foreign entities, file BC extra-provincial registration first, then apply for a BN via Form RC1 with proof of incorporation and a Canadian representative address, and open an RM account with CBSA for export eligibility.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The BC ULC is taxed in Canada as a corporation resident in Canada (federal Part I rate 15% general; BC provincial rate 12% general; combined ~27%; T2 return). Pass_Through_Eligible applies only under US federal tax: a US member may file IRS Form 8832 to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity, creating a hybrid entity (corporation for Canadian tax; transparent for US tax). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The BC ULC is taxed in Canada as a corporation resident in Canada (federal Part I rate 15% general; BC provincial rate 12% general; combined ~27%; T2 return). Pass_Through_Eligible applies only under US federal tax: a US member may file IRS Form 8832 to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity, creating a hybrid entity (corporation for Canadian tax; transparent for US tax). No territorial exemption or tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

US check-the-box election (IRS Form 8832) to treat the ULC as disregarded or as partnership for US purposes; ITA s. 85 rollover election for property transfer to the corporation; ETA s. 156 joint election for GST/HST grouping of closely related corporations; voluntary GST/HST registration election for small suppliers; BC PST direct-pay permit election for qualifying businesses; hybrid-mismatch set-off election under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 (payments on or after July 1, 2022) where the US owner is a hybrid object.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for GST/HST registration (aggregate worldwide taxable supplies in preceding four calendar quarters and current quarter per ETA s. 123); BC PST small-seller threshold is CAD 10,000 gross revenue in any 12-month period before registration becomes mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI Part V: (i) tangible personal property supplied to a non-resident and exported as part of the supply (para 1); goods must leave Canada within a reasonable time (CRA position: normally 60 days; exporter must retain documentary evidence at the time of filing); (ii) goods exported in the purchaser's baggage with a declaration; (iii) services supplied to a non-resident and used exclusively outside Canada (paras 20-23). The ULC must hold evidence of physical export and foreign-currency payment; online sales via Etsy Direct Pay or Stripe are zero-rated only on proof of shipment to a foreign address.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System/CERS) for goods valued above CAD 2,000 or controlled goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill from the common carrier or courier (tracking showing foreign destination)
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order and contract demonstrating a sale to a non-resident purchaser
  • Proof of foreign-currency receipt: SWIFT MT103, inward remittance certificate, or payment-processor settlement report (Stripe / Etsy Direct Pay)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or government API stamping in Canada. Electronic invoices in PDF/XML/EDI/Peppol formats are accepted where they meet record-keeping under ETA s. 286; GST/HST returns are filed through CRA GST/HST NETFILE API (TEL) or EDI, with mandatory electronic filing for returns over CAD 1 million; the Pan-Canadian Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoicing framework is voluntary, not clearance-based.

CRA auditors match CERS export declarations against GST/HST returns and payment records. If export cannot be substantiated within the reasonable-time benchmark (180 days for FX receipt evidence), the zero-rating is reversed and the supply assessed at 5% GST (plus BC PST where applicable), with compound interest; the normal 4-year reassessment window (ETA s. 298) is unlimited for misrepresentation or fraud. Penalties: false-statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax (ETA s. 280); gross-negligence and evasion prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 with fines up to CAD 250,000 (ETA) or 50%-200% of evaded federal tax (ITA) and imprisonment up to five years; CRA may also disallow input tax credits and claw back any refunds previously paid.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange-rate regime and the Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Both current-account payments (goods, services, investment income) and capital-account transfers may be executed without Bank of Canada approval, with no general exchange-control authorisation or registration requirement. Profit remittance obstacles are fiscal rather than monetary: a BC ULC must effect non-resident withholding tax on dividends at the statutory 25% under the Income Tax Act (Canada), subject to treaty reduction, and must obtain a Non-Resident Withholding Certificate (NR4) for each remittance. There are no convertibility restrictions or currency-rationing rules, and the Canadian Payments Authority does not impose sector-specific conversion quotas.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation; no statutory timeline or conversion ratio applies to foreign receipts. Exporters may retain proceeds in Canadian or foreign-currency accounts at their discretion. Compliance obligations are informational: goods exports exceeding CAD 2,000 must be reported through the Canada Border Services Agency export declaration for Statistics Canada trade data; any international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more is reportable to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. BC ULCs may hold multi-currency operating accounts without prior regulatory consent.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury) are legally viable for incidental cross-border collections and FX conversion, but they are not licensed as Schedule I banks in Canada and cannot issue commercial letters of credit, cheques, or direct-settlement instruments in CAD. A non-resident EMI soliciting Canadian clients must register as a money services business with FINTRAC and comply with FINTRAC’s Virtual Currency or Money Transfer reporting requirements. For the configured low-risk enamel-pin export activity, use EMIs solely for marketplace (Etsy) receivable settlement and sweep balances to a Canadian commercial bank operating account to ensure full clearing capability and access to trade-finance instruments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services; CAD 5 million for other transactions (thresholds for mandatory transfer pricing documentation).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance. The CRA may also apply the 'substance over form' doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if they failed to exercise due diligence. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustments to taxable income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete), and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in or authorized the non-compliant pricing.
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities, plus criminal sanctions for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned for up to 5 years if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent AML violations.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR may deny tax benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit. CRA may also apply the 'sham' doctrine to void transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they are found to be the directing mind behind the sham arrangement, including civil restitution and potential criminal charges.
