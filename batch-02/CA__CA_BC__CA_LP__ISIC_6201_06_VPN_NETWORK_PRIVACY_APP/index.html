Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and GST/HST Registry (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); for British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST), the BC Ministry of Finance eTaxBC portal (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst)

E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident entities obtain a 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account program code 'RT' by applying online through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by completing form RC160 for non-residents. A BN/GST/HST account is mandatory where the entity supplies VPN/digital services to Canadian recipients under the July 2021 remote supplier rules; the entity may also apply for an import/export account (RM) and BC PST account via eTaxBC if it establishes a physical presence or asset nexus in British Columbia. Registration must be completed prior to the first taxable supply in Canada; retroactive registration is available within certain time limits but exposes the entity to unpaid tax, interest, and penalties.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax purposes: the partnership computes income/loss but is not a taxable person; each partner (including non-resident limited partners) is taxed on its allocated share. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a separate 'person' and must register and account for tax in its own right; for BC PST the partnership is a vendor only where it has a permanent establishment or other statutory nexus. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax purposes: the partnership computes income/loss but is not a taxable person; each partner (including non-resident limited partners) is taxed on its allocated share. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a separate 'person' and must register and account for tax in its own right; for BC PST the partnership is a vendor only where it has a permanent establishment or other statutory nexus. The LP is not entitled to a tax-exempt zone status.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows legal form. Available statutory elections include: Income Tax Act section 96(2) election to adopt a non-calendar partnership fiscal period; section 216 election for non-resident partners to file net-income returns on Canadian-source rental/royalty income instead of Part XIII withholding; Excise Tax Act section 273 election for a partnership to be treated as a registrant for GST/HST with joint and several liability; and the hybrid mismatch rules (ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3) which provide only substantive carve-outs with no elective relief. The limited partnership agreement should include tax matters partner provisions and Partner/Representative authority to bind the partnership for CRA filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold over four consecutive calendar quarters for Canadian-resident suppliers; however, non-resident remote suppliers of specified digital services (which include VPN and SaaS-like network privacy software) are deemed non-small-suppliers and must register for GST/HST regardless of revenue threshold once they make taxable supplies to Canadian recipients; BC PST registration is separately required only when the non-resident has a permanent establishment, employee, agent, or other physical nexus in British Columbia

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VPN/digital service supplied by a Canadian-registered supplier is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V (and/or treated as made outside Canada under the specified digital service place-of-supply rules) when the recipient is a non-resident not present in Canada at the time of supply, the service is not performed in Canada, does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not a personal service rendered to an individual in Canada. The supplier must obtain documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status — primary residential address, IP geolocation, billing address, bank/card details, and contract terms — and retain such records in Canada or accessible electronically to CRA.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Canada Customs Automated Export Declaration / CAED) for physical exports; for pure digital exports, the equivalent retained customer records evidencing non-resident status and the supply contract
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued by the carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent — bank advice, SWIFT MT103, or payment gateway settlement statement (Stripe/Paymentwall) evidencing receipt of the export consideration in Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API invoice validation exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file returns electronically (NETFILE/XML); all registrants must maintain electronic invoices and books accessible to CRA. The Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing framework is optional and non-statutory. BC PST requires standard tax invoice content and electronic filing via eTaxBC but imposes no pre-clearance mandate.

CRA may reassess within the normal 4-year period, or without time limit in cases of misrepresentation, and retroactively claw back zero-rating refunds with interest; gross negligence penalties of 50% of tax avoided apply under ETA section 285.1; false or misleading statements carry fines up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; partners may be jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST obligations; CRA audits of VPN/software exporters focus on place-of-supply logs, IP address geolocation, network access records, and beneficial ownership to detect sham export arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating currency regime with full convertibility on both current account and capital account items, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. There is no exchange-control permit system, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency receipts, and no restriction on repatriation of profits, capital, dividends, interest, or partnership distributions from a British Columbia limited partnership to non-resident partners. Practical constraints are indirect: (i) FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer (EFT) reporting applies to international wires of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) the Investment Canada Act may require enterprise-value review of non-Canadian acquisitions of control of a Canadian business, although routine capital contributions into a BC limited partnership for software development are not notifiable unless the total enterprise value exceeds the annually indexed threshold or national-security review is triggered; (iii) under the Income Tax Act, Part XIII withholding at 25% (subject to treaty reduction) applies to certain outbound passive payments such as interest, royalties, and management fees, and a Canadian-resident partnership is deemed to carry on business in Canada such that non-resident partners are taxable on their share of Canadian-source partnership income under Part I; and (iv) Canadian-resident partners with specified foreign property over CAD 100,000 must file CRA Form T1135. Profit remittance hurdles are therefore tax-driven and documentation-driven rather than FX-driven; treaty-eligible partners receiving allocable partnership income can generally repatriate without Canadian withholding when the distribution is characterized strictly as a partnership allocation, while guaranteed payments to non-arm's-length foreign partners trigger administrative withholding obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no export-proceeds surrender or retention requirement for software, VPN subscription, licensing, or digital-encryption export revenues. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency operating accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks. The only compliance obligations are: (i) FINTRAC EFT reporting by the receiving bank for international transfers of CAD 10,000 or greater; (ii) CRA Form T1135 reporting by Canadian-resident partners where stipulated; and (iii) transfer-pricing documentation under Income Tax Act s. 247 for any outbound licensing or intercompany transactions. The BC limited partnership may hold USD revenue in segregated USD clearing accounts and convert to CAD at market rates with no surrender timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (Canada's central bank) and the Department of Finance Canada; foreign-exchange control administration is exercised through the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52), the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), the Investment Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 28), and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Export controls affecting VPN/encryption software are administered by Global Affairs Canada under the Export and Import Permits Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary payment collection; Low for primary treasury custody. Legal constraints: under the PCMLTFA, any foreign fintech or electronic-money institution (EMI) providing money-services business (MSB) services to Canadian clients must be registered with FINTRAC and maintain Canadian AML/ATF compliance, including a designated compliance officer and reporting regime. Offshore EMIs without Canadian registration cannot lawfully hold the core operating funds of a British Columbia limited partnership. The recommended architecture is: (i) primary settlement into an RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, or CIBC CAD/USD account; (ii) merchant acquiring via Stripe (Stripe Payments Canada, Ltd., a licensed/registered Canadian PSP) and Paymentwall, with funds swept daily into the operating account; and (iii) use of a regulated fintech such as Wise Platform only as a collection gateway or for low-value non-core vendor disbursements, always downstream of the primary Canadian treasury pool.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: (1) The foreign limited partner instructs its financial institution to send a SWIFT MT103 to the BC limited partnership's operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank, e.g., RBC (SWIFT ROYCCAT2), TD, BMO, Scotiabank, or CIBC; beneficiary Field 59 must exactly match the registered legal name of the BC LP; Field 70 must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - LP AGREEMENT dated [date] - Partner Ref [number]' and must not be left blank or labelled as a trade invoice. (2) CAD receipts are cleared through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value system upon the bank's conversion of USD at the prevailing indicative rate; USD receipts are cleared through the bank's US Fedwire/CHIPS correspondent and credited to a segregated USD sub-account without conversion. (3) Any international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more automatically triggers FINTRAC reporting by the receiving bank; the LP must pre-submit to the bank the executed LP Agreement, the partner's KYC/UBO pack, and the source-of-funds letter so that the bank's compliance team can clear the wire within 24 hours. (4) If a Capital Escrow Account is used, funds must remain uncommingled and released only upon the dual-signature instruction of the General Partner and an independent officer, with release conditions expressly documented in a disbursement instruction letter; no operational receivables may be deposited into an escrow account. (5) For capital contributions exceeding CAD 1 million, a pre-wire onboarding package containing audited organizational financials, notarized resolutions, and a Canadian tax-clearance confirmation (where applicable) should be delivered at least five business days before the wire date.Outbound treasury disbursements for the VPN SaaS business use three primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor payments (hosting, bandwidth, legal, accounting, and overseas contractor salaries) with Field 70 containing the standard memo 'INVOICE/PO [number] - VPN INFRASTRUCTURE [service name]'; (ii) Commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 for procurement contracts exceeding CAD 50,000 with foreign hardware or infrastructure counterparties, issued by the Canadian bank and advised through a confirming or advising bank in the vendor's jurisdiction; and (iii) Canadian domestic settlement via Lynx for CAD-denominated payables to local vendors, plus ACH/EFT through Payments Canada's retail clearing systems for recurring small-balance payables. The USD clearing leg should be executed through the Canadian bank's US correspondent or US-domiciled subsidiary (e.g., RBC's US operations) with a standing instruction to sweep USD surplus balances above a CAD 500,000 equivalent into an interest-bearing USD money-market facility. Settlement risk is mitigated by using SWIFT gpi tracking for all outbound MT103s and by requiring the reverse confirmation before releasing funds for any new vendor. Merchant acquirer Stripe and Paymentwall remit directly to the LP operating account in CAD via standard payout files; these incoming merchant settlements are not treated as capital injections and do not require source-of-funds escalation, but must be reconciled daily against the merchant dashboards to preserve the audit trail for FINTRAC and CRA.Outright deliverable CAD/USD forward contracts with a Canadian Schedule I bank or an OSC-registered derivatives dealer, maturities up to 24 months, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex; ideal for hedging USD-denominated subscription revenues against CAD operating costs.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on CAD for counterparty or regulatory contexts where a deliverable USD leg is unavailable or where the counterparty cannot settle CAD; permitted in Canada but functionally unnecessary for a BC-resident entity because CAD is freely deliverable under a floating regime.Cross-currency swaps (CAD/USD currency interest-rate swaps) to convert foreign-currency debt or capital-account exposures into the partnership's functional currency, preserving cash-flow matching on long-term hosting or infrastructure financing; executed under the same ISDA/CSA framework.Vanilla and barrier FX options purchased from a federally regulated Canadian bank for asymmetric protection of the VPN subscription revenue stream, with premium payments treated as treasury hedging expense rather than speculative activity.Rolling spot-deferred and flexible-average forwards contracted through the bank's commercial treasury desk, specifically sized against the actual monthly Stripe/Paymentwall settlement volumes to avoid classification as a required hedge of a speculative nature.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary payment collection; Low for primary treasury custody. Legal constraints: under the PCMLTFA, any foreign fintech or electronic-money institution (EMI) providing money-services business (MSB) services to Canadian clients must be registered with FINTRAC and maintain Canadian AML/ATF compliance, including a designated compliance officer and reporting regime. Offshore EMIs without Canadian registration cannot lawfully hold the core operating funds of a British Columbia limited partnership. The recommended architecture is: (i) primary settlement into an RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, or CIBC CAD/USD account; (ii) merchant acquiring via Stripe (Stripe Payments Canada, Ltd., a licensed/registered Canadian PSP) and Paymentwall, with funds swept daily into the operating account; and (iii) use of a regulated fintech such as Wise Platform only as a collection gateway or for low-value non-core vendor disbursements, always downstream of the primary Canadian treasury pool.
