Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (myBA) portal; concurrently, BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for the separate Provincial Sales Tax (PST) program.

E-government filing portal

The BC-incorporated ULC is deemed resident in Canada under Income Tax Act subsection 250(4)(a). Procedure: (1) maintain valid BC incorporation certificate and registry extract; (2) obtain the CRA Business Number (BN) with corporate income tax account by filing Form RC1 or via Business Registration Online; (3) register the GST/HST account under the Excise Tax Act (Form RC1) once taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold or where cross-border e-commerce supply rules require registration; (4) register as an out-of-province seller for BC PST with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch when cumulative BC retail sales exceed CAD 10,000 in a calendar year or earlier where a taxable sale imposes collection duty; (5) file CRA Form T2 annually and remit GST/HST using GST/HST NETFILE through My Business Account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: CRA treats the BC ULC as a resident taxable corporation under Income Tax Act subsection 250(4)(a). The US pass-through designation under IRS Form 8832 (check-the-box) is not recognized for Canadian federal or provincial income tax or GST/HST purposes; the ULC itself is the person required to register, collect, and report tax.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic check-the-box election. Available mechanisms: (a) voluntary GST/HST registration under ETA subsection 240(1) for below-threshold suppliers; (b) Quick Method of GST/HST accounting election (Form GST74); (c) IRS Form 8832 entity classification election exercised by the US shareholder (US side only, relevant to hybrid mismatch characterization); (d) Income Tax Act elections made on the T2 return (fiscal period, associated-corporation allocation under section 256, and late-filing relief) — none alters the CRA entity-level tax status of the ULC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a trailing four-calendar-quarter period, with special registration obligations for cross-border e-commerce suppliers of digital and physical goods to Canadian consumers; BC PST: CAD 10,000 cumulative taxable sales into British Columbia within a calendar year for out-of-province sellers, with immediate registration required when the first taxable BC sale occurs for sellers without a physical presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V. Tangible personal property is zero-rated where the supplier or recipient exports the goods as part of the supply and documentary proof of export is retained; goods shipped by the supplier to a foreign COD consumer destination are eligible where title passes outside Canada and the goods are transferred to a common or international carrier for remittance abroad. Services supplied to a non-resident customer are generally zero-rated except services rendered to persons present in Canada or services with a substantial Canadian connection. The mail-order COD export model qualifies when the international logistics contract and payment rails evidence delivery to the emerging-market destination.

  • CBSA export declaration (CADEX report or B13A Customs Coding Form, or courier manifest where value exceeds CAD 2,000) containing tariff classification, value, and destination country
  • Original commercial invoice and transport document (Bill of Lading or Air Waybill) identifying the exporter, consignee, Incoterms rule, and Canadian port of exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank / licensed FinTech (Airwallex or local logistics remittance provider) FX settlement receipt evidencing full CAD or foreign-currency consideration for the COD export proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government e-invoice authorization regime exists in Canada. CRA prescribes minimum invoice content under GST/HST Memorandum 12-2; e-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-Canadian Peppol interoperability framework. GST/HST returns and payments must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account for registrants, and substantive business records must be retained in readable paper or electronic form for six years under ETA subsection 286(1).

CRA may reassess within four years (unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is established) and claw back zero-rating-derived refunds and input tax credits with prescribed interest when export documentation is deficient. False statements or omissions attract a 25% penalty under ETA subsection 285(1); wilful evasion is a criminal offence under ETA section 327, punishable on indictment by a fine of up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to five years, and by summary conviction to a fine of up to CAD 25,000 and up to 12 months imprisonment. BC PST non-collection penalties and interest apply separately at a 25% penalty base under the PST provincial framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There is no exchange-control license, no capital repatriation approval, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds. Profit remittance frictions are limited to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced where applicable) and, in the specific case of a BC ULC, the ability to defer withholding under the unlimited-liability flow-through election in subsection 34(2) of the Income Tax Act. FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply to cross-border electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more, and the Investment Canada Act may require review for larger inbound equity injections into a Canadian entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts. Proceeds from mail-order retail and logistics-related export sales may be retained in foreign currency in any authorized bank account. Financial institutions must still comply with FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting duties for incoming EFTs over C$10,000, and the payment instruction should accurately describe the underlying cross-border sale of goods.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; foreign exchange and AML/CFT compliance is administered through the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Currency Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High for treasury efficiency but Low-Medium for pristine capital-flow integrity. Airwallex and comparable overseas electronic money institutions can be used for operational collection from logistics remittance agents and for small-ticket supplier disbursements. They are not licensed Canadian banks, cannot hold CDIC-insured deposits, and cannot serve as the sole clearing rail for statutory capital escrow, tax remittances, or dividend flows. The EMI must be registered with FINTRAC as a money services business if it serves Canadian residents, and the ULC must maintain a parallel Schedule I bank account for regulatory and audit-grade transactions.

Line 100 – Gross Revenue Total gross revenue from all business operations, including mail-order retail sales, cash-on-delivery settlements, logistics recovery charges, and ancillary fees. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada report gross revenue attributable to the Canadian permanent establishment; foreign-source COD sales not attributable to Canada are excluded where Canada has no taxing right under the applicable DTA. Late filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delay up to 12 months; a repeat late-filing penalty increases this to 10% plus 2% per month up to 20 months. Late or incomplete T106 transfer-pricing information returns attract C$100 per day per form, minimum C$1,000 and maximum C$25,000. CRA may also issue arbitrary assessments and charge compound arrears interest.
Line 200 – Total Expenses Claim all deductible operating expenses, including COGS, COD fulfillment costs, delivery agent fees, payment processing charges, chargebacks, marketing, and administrative overhead. Expenses must be incurred for the purpose of earning Canadian-source income, be reasonable, and be allocated to the Canadian PE; intercompany payments for logistics or platform services must meet arm's-length standards and be supported by contemporaneous transfer pricing records. Late filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delay up to 12 months; a repeat late-filing penalty increases this to 10% plus 2% per month up to 20 months. Late or incomplete T106 transfer-pricing information returns attract C$100 per day per form, minimum C$1,000 and maximum C$25,000. CRA may also issue arbitrary assessments and charge compound arrears interest.
Schedule 3 – Corporate loss continuity and taxable income reconciliation Reconcile accounting net income to taxable income after capital cost allowance, cumulative eligible capital, reserves, and other adjustments; apply non-capital loss carryforwards within the 20-year limit. Non-resident corporations may apply Canadian business losses only against income taxable in Canada; losses of a foreign branch or non-Canadian source are not claimable unless permitted by the DTA. Late filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month of delay up to 12 months; a repeat late-filing penalty increases this to 10% plus 2% per month up to 20 months. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file and contemporaneous documentation are mandatory when reportable transactions with a single non-arm's-length non-resident exceed C$1,000,000 in fair market value per year; master file documentation is triggered where the multinational group's annual consolidated revenue is C$500,000,000 or more; country-by-country returns are required where group revenue equals or exceeds C$750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime equivalent to offshore financial centers; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act s.245) and the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test require genuine business purpose, employees, premises, and decision-making in BC. A ULC with no physical BC activity or no cash-on-delivery fulfillment substance may be recharacterized and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil: 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) of the Income Tax Act. Criminal: conviction under s.239(1) may lead to imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded. CRA may also reassess beyond the normal reassessment period and charge compound arrears interest.

Director personal liability: The ULC's unlimited liability means shareholders and directors can be pursued directly for corporate obligations; for knowing participation in evasion, the corporate veil is pierced and CRA may name senior directors in the criminal indictment, seek civil restitution, and obtain travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: CRA may reprice the transaction under s.247(2), apply a 10% transfer pricing penalty under s.247(3) where the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts, disallow excess interest under thin capitalization and earnings-stripping restrictions, and assess related tax and interest from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false transfer pricing records or cause the corporation to pay excessive fees while insolvent may be assessed personally for unremitted source deductions and face director liability under s.227.1 of the Income Tax Act; courts may pierce the unlimited liability structure to enforce restitution.
Treaty Shopping, Shell Company Abuse, and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the general anti-avoidance rule under s.245 to deny the tax benefit; Part XIII withholding tax is restored at 25%; treaty relief is denied for lack of beneficial ownership or failure to satisfy the Principal Purpose Test; administrative penalties and interest will also be assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell ULC who sign false treaty declarations may be assessed as trustees of withheld amounts; fraudulent surplus-stripping transactions may result in civil disgorgement, freezing orders, travel bans, and, in aggravated cases, director-level criminal liability.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance in COD Settlements

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, FINTRAC may impose administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for serious compliance violations; criminal prosecution can result in fines and imprisonment for individuals who knowingly weaken AML controls.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are required to take reasonable care to ensure corporate AML compliance; willful blindness or direct participation in unregistered remittance activity exposes them to personal fines, imprisonment, and permanent exclusion from regulated payment networks.
