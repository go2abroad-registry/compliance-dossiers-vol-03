Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office addresses are permissible for the registered office of a BC limited partnership only if (i) the address is a physical street address in British Columbia where the registered office is actually maintained; (ii) process can be served on a natural person during regular business hours; and (iii) records, including the register of partners and the partnership's books and returns, are physically accessible at that location. The business's core reverse-logistics operations may be located outside Canada provided the registered office and record-keeping functions remain in BC.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, BC Corporate Registry, Ministry of Citizens' Services, Province of British Columbia; electronic portal: BC Registry Online (bcregistry.gov.bc.ca).

Governing statute

Limited Partnership Act, RSBC 1996, c. 249, particularly ss. 4–7 (Declaration, registration, limited partner capital and general partner liability); Partnership Act, RSBC 1996, c. 348, s. 3 (residual application of partnership law except where inconsistent); Business Corporations Act, RSBC 2002, c. 57, applicable only where a British Columbia corporation acts as the general partner.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form 1) filed under s. 4(1) of the Limited Partnership Act; the entity is evidenced by the Certificate of Limited Partnership issued by the Registrar.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 21 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a British Columbia limited partnership. The Declaration must state the capital contributed by each general and limited partner, which may be cash or property and may be nominal at formation. No escrow deposit or notarized capital certificate is required under BC law for this entity type.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the local Fire Marshal under the BC Fire Code; structural load inspection for warehouse racking and mezzanines as a condition of the building occupancy permit; WorkSafeBC clearance covering the employer account, first aid plan, and emergency preparedness; forklift and material-handling equipment certification under Technical Safety BC; hazard communication and worksite diligence program for the returns-grading line.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a British Columbia limited partnership. The Declaration must state the capital contributed by each general and limited partner, which may be cash or property and may be nominal at formation. No escrow deposit or notarized capital certificate is required under BC law for this entity type.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No provincial industrial licensing board regulates reverse logistics, freight arrangement or returned-goods grading (ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510); local municipal Business Licence offices and WorkSafeBC govern the physical facility, while Technical Safety BC covers regulated mechanical equipment.

Category: Not applicable — the activity is not statutorily licensed as a sectoral undertaking; a municipal Business Licence and standard occupational registrations constitute the only operating clearances.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal income tax, GST/HST, and Business Number (BN) administration; primary portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency and Business Registration Online (BRO). British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, via eTaxBC (gov.bc.ca/pst).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the Limited Partnership with the BC Registrar of Companies (Corporate Online) and obtain the BC registration number. Step 2: Apply for a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account (RT program) using BRO, by telephone at 1-800-959-5525, or by mailing Form RC1 to the CRA Business Number Office; non-resident applicants must attach the partnership agreement, BC LP registration certification, partner identification, certificates of status, and an authorized-person declaration. Step 3: The General Partner acts as registration agent for the LP; the LP may elect to register for GST/HST even when below the small-supplier threshold to claim input tax credits. Step 4: Register separately for BC PST (vendor 'P' number) via eTaxBC if the LP has a BC establishment or makes taxable sales of goods or services in BC. TIN structure: Business Number plus RT account suffix, e.g., 123456789 RT0001.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act section 96, the BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and BC corporate income tax: the LP itself is not a taxpayer; each partner reports its allocated share of income, loss, and deductions. For GST/HST (Excise Tax Act section 248), the partnership is a distinct person and may be a separate registrant. Canadian-source business income allocated to non-resident partners is taxable in Canada under Part I through partner-level filings, with Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reduced) applicable to designated amounts such as rent, royalties, or management fees. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act section 96, the BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal and BC corporate income tax: the LP itself is not a taxpayer; each partner reports its allocated share of income, loss, and deductions. For GST/HST (Excise Tax Act section 248), the partnership is a distinct person and may be a separate registrant. Canadian-source business income allocated to non-resident partners is taxable in Canada under Part I through partner-level filings, with Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reduced) applicable to designated amounts such as rent, royalties, or management fees. Territorial scope: Canada taxes income from a Canadian business of the partnership regardless of the partner's residence.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; LP transparency is statutory. Available elections and mechanisms: (1) Non-calendar fiscal period election for partnerships under subsection 249.1(4); (2) partnership deemed-year-end joint election under subsection 96(3); (3) non-resident partner section 216 election to file a Canadian Part I return in respect of net rental income instead of Part XIII withholding; (4) GST/HST registration designation election under subsection 240(4) of the Excise Tax Act allowing a partner or agent to register and file on behalf of the partnership; (5) related-party joint election under ETA section 156 to treat separate supplies as a single supply; (6) hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.2 and 18.3 apply automatically by mandatory adjustment, with no elective filing available for first-year application.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST small-supplier threshold under ETA subsection 148(1): mandatory registration is triggered once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A reverse-logistics LP is strongly advised to register voluntarily regardless of turnover to claim input tax credits and to zero-rate qualifying exports. BC PST: no general dollar threshold applies to in-province vendors carrying on business in BC; remote and non-resident sellers become registrable once BC taxable sales exceed CAD 10,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act as follows: (a) Exported goods — tangible personal property is zero-rated under Part V, sections 1-3 of Schedule VI where the goods are supplied to a non-resident recipient or delivered to a carrier for export and physically leave Canada without further use in Canada within 60 days of shipment; re-exported returned merchandise qualifies only where title passes to a non-resident and export is commercially documented. (b) Services to non-residents — logistics, quality grading, refurbishment, warehousing and related reverse-logistics services supplied to a non-resident principal are zero-rated under Part V, section 7 of Schedule VI provided the service is not supplied while the non-resident recipient is in Canada and the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada. Critical trap: grading, inspection, repackaging, and domestic warehousing performed on goods physically situated in Canada are excluded from zero-rating and are taxable at 5% GST even when billed to a non-resident, unless (i) the goods were imported into Canada exclusively for the purpose of the service and are exported within 60 days after the service is performed, or (ii) the recipient is a GST/HST registrant and the property is delivered for use exclusively outside Canada. (c) Transportation — international carriage and freight-forwarding segments beginning in Canada and ending outside Canada are zero-rated under Part V, sections 39-45 of Schedule VI. Claimed zero-rating must be supported by contemporaneous export records retained for six years under ETA section 286.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A / GCA e-export) for goods exceeding CAD 2,500 in value or controlled/regulated items
  • Commercial invoice or proforma invoice evidencing value, description, and consignee of the re-exported returned goods
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/rail) or Air Waybill (air cargo), with carrier eManifest / Cargo Control Document confirming physical movement out of Canada
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103/MT202, bank credit advice, or foreign exchange receipt demonstrating payment by the non-resident principal (CRA accepts commercial evidence; no statutory certificate form in Canada)
  • Grading report, inspection certificate, and warehouse receipt for the returned merchandise linking the shipment to the zero-rated service
  • GST/HST registration number and status certification of the recipient, or non-resident status certificate (tax residency certificate / corporate registry extract) for section 7 services
  • Service agreement and work orders with the non-resident principal, including contractual place-of-performance clauses
  • Customs import documents (Form B3 or casual goods entry) evidencing inward processing or repair importation to substantiate the 60-day re-export rule
  • Return Merchandise Authorization (RMA) records from the Canadian customer and the non-resident seller
  • Transportation contract and rate confirmation for the export leg

None. Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API pre-authorization regime for GST/HST invoices. CRA requires invoices to satisfy the minimum content rules under ETA sections 169 and 221 (supplier name, registration number, consideration, tax charged). Pan-Canadian e-invoicing through the Peppol framework is voluntary and progressively adopted for B2B transactions. Mandatory electronic filing applies to export controls through the CBSA Global Case Application (GCA), Canadian Export Reporting System (B13A), and eManifest / Advanced Commercial Information (ACI) cargo data, none of which constitutes a tax clearance mechanism.

CRA may reassess within four years (ETA section 298(1)); the period is unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is shown, with a 10-year extended reassessment window (ETA section 298(2)). Unsupported zero-rating is re-characterized as taxable at 5% and triggers assessment of unremitted GST (ETA section 296), arrears interest and penalties (ETA section 280.1), and a gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the understated net tax (ETA section 285), rising to 50% in cases of culpable conduct or second infractions. Criminal evasion under ETA section 327 carries fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years; input tax credits claimed in connection with the denied zero-rating are clawed back with interest. CRA audit practice for reverse logistics cross-validates B13A export data, eManifest cargo records, grading volumes, shipping weights, and inward remittances; the leading cause of reassessment is failure to evidence the non-resident recipient's status or the physical export of goods within the 60-day window. All substantiation records must be retained for six years under ETA section 286 and ITA section 230. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure reflects the prudent commercial evidence window against which CRA assesses export substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under the Currency Act and Canada's Articles of Agreement with the IMF. Both current-account transactions and capital-account operations are unrestricted. No capital controls or exchange-control licensing apply to capital injections, profit remittances, or foreign-currency settlements. Non-resident partners of a British Columbia limited partnership may receive profit distributions without Bank of Canada approval; however, agency or other payments to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax and treaty-based reduction unless properly documented. Anti-money-laundering and foreign-investment screening rules may impose reporting or approval steps for certain investments, but these are not currency-convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters are free to hold, convert, or remit foreign-currency receipts as commercial circumstances dictate. The only compliance obligations are ordinary tax and customs record-keeping, plus participation in Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys when the exporter is selected for reporting. No 0-day surrender rule applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange administration agency. Financial conduct in relation to payments and money laundering is also overseen by FINTRAC and OSFI for federally regulated financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High feasibility as a supplementary settlement gateway only. Canada does not recognize 'electronic money institution' as a distinct banking licence category. Offshore EMI platforms such as Airwallex and Payoneer may provide cross-border collections and disbursements, but they must rely on a regulated Canadian payment service provider or a Schedule I bank to hold CAD. Since the Retail Payment Activities Act came into force in 2024, Canadian PSPs must be registered with the Bank of Canada and comply with FINTRAC AML obligations. For this LP, an EMI account is viable for trade settlement and low-value reverse-logistics disbursements, but it cannot replace a regulated bank account for capital escrow, LC issuance, or FX hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the contributing partner's bank to the LP's designated Canadian bank account. The beneficiary account name and address must exactly match the BC limited partnership registration. The payment reference should identify the capital contribution, the executing LP agreement, and the capital call notice. Canadian financial institutions will apply FINTRAC EFT reporting for electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and the bank will conduct standard source-of-funds verification. Foreign-currency capital is freely accepted and can be converted to CAD at the bank's prevailing exchange rate or under a pre-agreed forward contract; no central bank authorization is required. To facilitate smooth clearing, the LP should provide its Canadian bank with a copy of the LP Agreement, beneficial ownership declaration, general partner resolution, and non-resident partner tax status documentation.Outbound payments for supplier invoices, freight costs, customs brokers, and reverse-logistics service providers should use bank-originated SWIFT MT103 where the beneficiary is outside Canada. For high-value purchase commitments tied to returned-goods resale, confirmed commercial letters of credit opened by the Canadian issuing bank provide secure settlement. CAD payments to domestic service providers may be cleared through the Lynx high-value payment system or the ACH network. USD payments to US-based counterparties can be cleared through the Canadian bank's US-dollar clearing account or a US correspondent. SWIFT gpi tracking should be enabled for time-critical supplier settlements. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High feasibility as a supplementary settlement gateway only. Canada does not recognize 'electronic money institution' as a distinct banking licence category. Offshore EMI platforms such as Airwallex and Payoneer may provide cross-border collections and disbursements, but they must rely on a regulated Canadian payment service provider or a Schedule I bank to hold CAD. Since the Retail Payment Activities Act came into force in 2024, Canadian PSPs must be registered with the Bank of Canada and comply with FINTRAC AML obligations. For this LP, an EMI account is viable for trade settlement and low-value reverse-logistics disbursements, but it cannot replace a regulated bank account for capital escrow, LC issuance, or FX hedging.
