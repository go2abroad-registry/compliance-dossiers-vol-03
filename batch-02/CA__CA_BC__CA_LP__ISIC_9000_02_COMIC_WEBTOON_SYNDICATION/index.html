Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account administered through CRA 'My Business Account' at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html and Business Registration Online (BRO). British Columbia Minister of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch administers PST via the eTaxBC portal at https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst; PST tax number is separate from the federal BN.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) using the BRO online service or Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number'). Step 2: Register the Limited Partnership for GST/HST under the same BN once worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s.148(1)); registration is effective on the day the threshold is exceeded and is compulsory in the following calendar month. Step 3: If the LP is a non-resident supplier of digital services (not the present fact pattern, as the LP is Canadian), use CRA's simplified non-resident GST/HST registration channel with no Canadian permanent establishment requirement. Step 4: For BC PST, register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance before making BC-source taxable sales; foreign sellers must register once gross BC taxable sales exceed CAD 10,000 in a calendar year. Step 5: File annual partnership information return (T5013) naming all partners with their residency, CRA BN (if any) and share of income; non-resident partners must provide foreign tax identification numbers and may face Part XIII withholding on certain payments.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. A BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax under ITA s.96(1); the LP itself pays no Part I income tax, and each partner is taxed on its allocable share of partnership income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax under ITA s.96(1); the LP itself pays no Part I income tax, and each partner is taxed on its allocable share of partnership income. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is a distinct 'person' under ETA s.272.1 and must account for GST/HST in its own capacity, with partners jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted GST/HST under ETA s.273. Territorial scope: Canadian-resident partners taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners taxed only on Canadian-source income (ITA s.2(3)/115), with the LP potentially classified as a 'non-Canadian partnership' under ITA s.102 if any partner is a non-resident. No tax-exempt zone or preferential regime applies to this entity in British Columbia.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows the mandatory flow-through rules of ITA ss.96-103. Available elections relevant to a cross-border webtoon LP: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s.240(3) before crossing the CAD 30,000 threshold, enabling input tax credit recovery on Canadian expenses; (ii) election to use the GST/HST 'net tax' simplified accounting? Not applicable to Large/medium; the standard net tax method applies automatically. A partnership may elect under ETA s.273(1) to treat a partner as acting as agent for collection only in limited circumstances. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss.18.4(3), 95, 247(2) and ETA s.12.6 are mandatory and cannot be elected out; however, a taxpayer may file a contemporaneous documentation election to self-identify mismatch treatment for penalty relief under the CRA's disclosure rules. No statutory check-the-box or 'hybrid entity classification' election exists in Canada.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for GST/HST small-supplier registration (worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; ETA s.148(1)). Non-resident digital suppliers cross the same CAD 30,000 threshold for simplified GST/HST registration. For BC PST: foreign sellers with BC taxable sales exceeding CAD 10,000 in a calendar year must register as PST collectors; Canadian sellers with a BC establishment must register before making their first taxable sale regardless of revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of intangible personal property (digital webtoon content licenses, subscriptions, and chapter-unlocking rights) made to a recipient who is a non-resident and not registered for GST/HST are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.25, where the supply is delivered or made available outside Canada. Digital services supplied to a non-resident are likewise zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.23, provided no supply of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada is involved. Eligibility is established by: (i) non-resident status of the counterparty (Apple/Google platform entities are typically resident in Ireland or the US; obtain W-8BEN-E or a Certificate of Residence); (ii) electronic delivery logs and IP geolocation showing downloads/access outside Canada; (iii) contract language expressing foreign distribution rights; (iv) foreign-currency proceeds from foreign payors; and (v) absence of any tangible medium crossing a Canadian customs border. Where Apple IAP or Google Play Billing is used, the LP's supply of distribution rights to the non-resident platform is zero-rated, and the platform may separately collect GST/HST from Canadian consumers under the digital platform rules. If any end-user access is traced to Canada, the consideration allocable to that access is subject to 5% GST and must be self-assessed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A) - mandatory only where physical media containing webtoon content is shipped internationally; for pure electronic delivery, an internal digital-export log with timestamps, file delivery server addresses, and IP geolocation report substitutes.
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill - mandatory only for physical shipments of comic/graphic novel media; for digital webtoon syndication, the contractual delivery certificate and platform download server logs are retained in lieu.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Bank Records (SWIFT MT103, bank credit advices, Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console payout statements denominated in foreign currency) - mandatory to evidence receipt of export consideration from non-resident platform operators.
  • Non-resident status evidence (W-8BEN-E, Certificate of Residence, or foreign tax identification number of each counterparty platform).
  • Signed electronic license agreement for multilingual webtoon syndication expressly stating foreign distribution territory and zero-rate GST exclusion.
  • Valid GST/HST invoice copies with 0% GST notation, itemized by transaction, together with the LP's periodic bank reconcilement.

None federally. Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government API pre-authorization of invoices, and no digital stamping for GST/HST. The CRA accepts standard paper and electronic invoices; each invoice must show the supplier's name and BN, date, description, consideration, and GST/HST charged or zero-rating indication. A voluntary PEPPOL-based 'Canada e-invoicing' framework exists and is CRA-compatible. Quebec's B2B e-invoicing mandate (2025-2026) does not extend to British Columbia. BC PST requires only standard invoices and periodic eTaxBC filing; no pre-clearance or stamping obligation.

CRA audit flags include continuous zero-rated export claims without documentary proof of non-resident customers, geolocation data showing Canadian downloading, and round-tripping of funds through foreign platforms. Legal exposures: (i) reassessment window extended to unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default (ITA s.152(4)); ordinary four-year limitation otherwise; (ii) 25% gross negligence penalty on the amount of tax misstated (ITA s.163(2)); (iii) 25% false-statement or omission penalty under ETA s.285.1; (iv) interest at the prescribed rate compounded daily on every GST/HST amount assessed (ETA s.280); (v) reversal of zero-rating, requiring the LP to remit 5% GST on gross export sales retroactively; (vi) joint and several liability of all general and limited partners for the LP's unremitted GST/HST under ETA s.273; (vii) criminal prosecution for wilful evasion (ETA s.327(1), ITA s.239) with fines up to 200% of the tax and imprisonment. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day inside-benchmark serves as a CRA anti-deferral documentation standard for export revenue substantiation. Export refunds claimed through net zero-rated GST returns are recoverable quarterly, but any tax refund obtained on the basis of false zero-rating claims is clawed back with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible, with Canada in full compliance with IMF Article VIII; there are no current-account or capital-account exchange restrictions and no requirement for central-bank approval of inbound equity or debt contributions. Profit remittance and capital repatriation by a BC limited partnership are unrestricted; however, outbound royalty, interest, management-fee, or similar passive payments to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to as low as 0%-15% where the recipient qualifies, e.g., under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention), and active business income allocated to non-resident partners is reportable on CRA T5013 Partnership Information Returns and taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act. No exchange-control permission, no residence-based ownership approval, and no profit-remittance licence is required for CA_LP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation on a BC limited partnership: there is no mandatory conversion ratio, no maximum foreign-currency retention period, and no requirement to repatriate export or digital-licensing receipts to Canada. Export receipts, webtoon syndication fees, and Apple/Google settlement funds may be retained indefinitely in CAD, USD, or other currencies; however, international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and worldwide income remains reportable to the CRA on account of residence regardless of repatriation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control administration. FX prudential oversight is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/ATF monitoring by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and monetary sovereignty by the Bank of Canada under the Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Conditional / Medium. Cross-border digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe) are legally viable as supplementary payout and collection rails but cannot serve as the sole treasury custodian for CA_LP. Since the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) came into force in November 2024, payment service providers that perform clearing, settlement, or fund transfers in Canada for end users must be registered or otherwise compliant with the Bank of Canada regime, and entities providing money-services business activities must be registered with FINTRAC. Foreign EMIs operating without Canadian registration or a Canadian payment-service licence may face BN/OSFI enforcement and deposit-taking restrictions; funds held with an EMI are not CDIC-insured and are not protected claims against a Schedule I bank. The recommended treasury design positions the EMI as a settlement adjunct for low-value creator payouts (KRW, PHP, IDR, THB) with daily sweep balances to a regulated Schedule I operational account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign partners' capital contributions route by SWIFT MT103 with strict reference to the registered legal name 'CA_LP' to avoid name-mismatch holds; recommended ISO 20022 purpose code 'INVC' (investment capital) or 'BEXP' (business expense) and the narrative 'LP capital contribution - webtoon syndication - [partner name]'. Pre-funding verification comprises (i) the bank's name-screening and OFAC/sanctions cross-check against the LP register, (ii) FINTRAC international-wire reporting for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, (iii) source-of-wealth documentation (capital call notice, LP agreement, partner declaration), and (iv) an Investment Canada Act classification memo confirming that a digital-publishing business below the 2025 WTO investor review threshold (approximately CAD 2.05 billion enterprise value) requires no pre-closing notification filing. No Bank of Canada approval is required.Outbound creator royalties, syndication fees, and content-licence payments settle via (i) SWIFT MT103 for bank-to-bank CAD/USD/EUR remittances with purpose code 'ROYL' or 'LIC' and contract-number references; (ii) MT202 cover payments where the instructing bank utilises a correspondent intermediary; (iii) commercial letters of credit (UCP600) only for bulk content packages in higher-credit-risk jurisdictions where the sub-publisher requires documentary settlement; (iv) Interac e-Transfer or Canadian EFT for domestic creators; and (v) regulated EMI payout rails (Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer) for low-value KRW, PHP, IDR, and THB disbursements where correspondent SWIFT economics are prohibitive. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditional / Medium. Cross-border digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe) are legally viable as supplementary payout and collection rails but cannot serve as the sole treasury custodian for CA_LP. Since the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) came into force in November 2024, payment service providers that perform clearing, settlement, or fund transfers in Canada for end users must be registered or otherwise compliant with the Bank of Canada regime, and entities providing money-services business activities must be registered with FINTRAC. Foreign EMIs operating without Canadian registration or a Canadian payment-service licence may face BN/OSFI enforcement and deposit-taking restrictions; funds held with an EMI are not CDIC-insured and are not protected claims against a Schedule I bank. The recommended treasury design positions the EMI as a settlement adjunct for low-value creator payouts (KRW, PHP, IDR, THB) with daily sweep balances to a regulated Schedule I operational account.
