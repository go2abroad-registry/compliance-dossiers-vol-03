Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (MyBA); GST/HST program account (RT prefix) federally administered. BC PST is separately administered by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account through CRA Business Registration Online (non-resident registration path) or by filing Form RC1 supported by the BC certificate of limited partnership, identification of the general partner, and a Canadian service address. The BN and GST/HST account become mandatory once the LP exceeds the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for taxable supplies made in Canada, calculated per calendar quarter and over the four preceding calendar quarters; voluntary registration remains advisable for exporters claiming zero-rating. BC PST registration through eTaxBC is required separately where the LP makes taxable PST supplies to BC consumers.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account through CRA Business Registration Online (non-resident registration path) or by filing Form RC1 supported by the BC certificate of limited partnership, identification of the general partner, and a Canadian service address. The BN and GST/HST account become mandatory once the LP exceeds the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold for taxable supplies made in Canada, calculated per calendar quarter and over the four preceding calendar quarters; voluntary registration remains advisable for exporters claiming zero-rating. BC PST registration through eTaxBC is required separately where the LP makes taxable PST supplies to BC consumers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Relevant statutory elections and alignment mechanisms include: (i) the GST/HST qualifying-partnership election (Form GST497) consolidating GST/HST registration and returns; (ii) the ITA s. 96(10) and s. 150(2) fiscal-period election allowing the partnership to compute income as if it were a separate person; (iii) the joint GST/HST election attributing taxable supplies to the partnership rather than to individual partners; (iv) group-ratio elections under the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) rules in ITA s. 18.2; and (v) hybrid-mismatch alignment rules under ITA s. 18.4, which operate mechanically without an elective carve-out and may deny deductions for structured replicated or hybrid payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) measured per calendar quarter and over the four preceding calendar quarters under ETA s. 148; the same threshold applies to non-resident registrants carrying on business in Canada. BC PST imposes a separate vendor registration obligation on taxable digital-product vendors without an analogous monetary exemption threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Commercial stock photography and generative visual assets qualify for zero-rating where the supply constitutes intangible personal property (downloaded digital images, licences, or AI visual asset usage rights) made to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and the property is not acquired for use in Canada (ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 and Schedule IX s. 23), or where the supply is a service rendered to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the service is not performed in respect of real or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The LP must obtain and retain a written declaration of the customer's non-resident status, substantively equivalent to Form GST312. Supplies made to Canadian resident consumers and B2B customers are taxable at 5% GST, and BC PST applies to digital products or licences sold to BC end consumers.

  • GST/HST export zero-rating declaration evidencing non-resident recipient status (Form GST312 or equivalent contractual statement)
  • Custom invoice and commercial invoice itemising licences, transaction values, currency, and GST/HST treatment for the digital stock-photo and AI visual asset scope (ISIC 7420)
  • Payment settlement and FX conversion records evidencing receipt of export proceeds from foreign counterparties
  • Digital delivery logs or, where physical storage media are shipped, CBSA export documentation (G13 or CAED) together with Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Licence agreement and scope-of-service records documenting ISIC 7420 exported-supply characterisation

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance regime, invoice pre-clearance approval system, or digital stamping mandate (no continuous transaction controls model). E-invoicing is voluntary and aligned with the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL BIS 3.0). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or CRA MyBA once annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million. Zero-rating documentation (non-resident declarations, invoices, payment evidence) must be retained for six years. BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC according to the applicable provincial filing calendar.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST and income tax for four years, extended to ten years where misrepresentation, neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established. Improper zero-rating claims expose the LP to unremitted GST plus interest under ETA s. 280, with penalties of 6% of the deficiency plus 6% per month for up to 24 months; false statements attract a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 or 5% of the tax at issue plus 5% per month under ETA s. 285, and gross negligence may add a further 50% penalty under ETA s. 285(2) and the analogous ITA s. 163(2). Retroactive clawbacks of export zero-rating can extend to ten years where the supporting documentation is deficient. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for exporters; the 180-day benchmark is applied as a documentary-consistency standard, because failure to evidence repatriated export proceeds undermines the non-resident-recipient evidence and may cause the CRA to recharacterise the supply as domestic with retroactive GST and PST assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible into CAD; there are no exchange-control licences, prior approvals, or foreign-investment exchange registration requirements for a capital contribution into a BC limited partnership. Profit remittance is permitted without a licence, subject to normal Part I / Part XIII Canadian income tax on Canadian-source income, treaty eligibility for reduced withholding, transfer-pricing compliance, and CRA information returns. The main practical hurdles are banking KYC, sanctions screening, and documentation proving the source and rationale of any funds transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender period and no conversion ratio for export receipts. Foreign-currency revenue may be retained in USD/EUR accounts. Exporters must file the prescribed CBSA B13A export declaration for controlled or threshold shipments for trade-statistics purposes; receipts received or sent by Canadian banks must be reported through FINTRAC IEFTR if they exceed CAD 10,000 in a single transaction. No CAD conversion timing applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada has no separate exchange-control administration; OSFI supervises banks, FINTRAC enforces AML/EFT reporting, and the Department of Finance sets monetary/exchange policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability, conditional on MSB licensing and low-value payment scope. An offshore digital EMI is not a Canadian bank or deposit-taking institution; to operate in Canada it must register with FINTRAC as a money services business (MSB), and may require provincial money-remitter licensing. It can be used for small/medium licensing transactions, cross-currency settlement, and distributed royalty payouts, but it cannot replace a Schedule I Canadian bank for physical CAD clearing, capital escrow, large SWIFT receipts, or commercial trade financing. It must be integrated as a settlement gateway only and remaining balances swept to the bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined headline rate for a taxable corporation in British Columbia (federal 15% + BC 12%); a BC Limited Partnership itself is flow-through and bears 0% entity-level income tax, with tax imposed at the partner level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act for dividend payments made by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner. In the case of a BC Limited Partnership, distributions are not dividends to the LP itself; the non-resident partner is taxed on Canadian-source partnership income either under Part I on a net basis or under Part XIII for certain passive-type payments. Where the LP or an underlying Canadian corporation makes a payment that is legally characterized as a dividend, interest, royalty, or deemed dividend, the statutory withholding rate is 25%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: the aggregate threshold for mandatory reporting on Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) for cross-border transactions with related or non-arm's-length parties. CRA contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation must be prepared where reportable cross-border transactions exceed this threshold; the CRA may also require Local File / Master File documentation in accordance with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and Canadian Income Tax Act s.247(4), particularly where the enterprise participates in a multinational group or transacts significant intangible-licensing flows.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Gulf-state Economic Substance Regulations, but the CRA applies the arm's-length standard, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245 of the Income Tax Act, and treaty anti-abuse rules (including Principal Purpose Test and Limitation-on-Benefits provisions). A BC Limited Partnership that exists solely as a conduit for stock-photography and AI visual-asset licensing revenue — without employees, office premises, operational functions, risk management, decision-making personnel, or Canadian banking activities — is vulnerable to recharacterization. CRA may disregard the LP for tax purposes, allocate income to the substantive owner, deny treaty benefits, impose Part XIII withholding on deemed payments, and assess penalties for abusive tax planning.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): on summary conviction, a fine equal to 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to two years; on indictment, a fine equal to 100% to 300% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to five years. The CRA may also require full restitution of taxes, interest, and penalties for each year under assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and general partners who authorized or silently benefited from the evasion face criminal prosecution in their personal capacity. The corporate veil is pierced for intentional wrongdoing, and courts may impose personal restitution orders, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Failure to File and Misreporting

Statutory sanction: T5013 late-filing penalty of CAD 25 per partner per day, capped at CAD 2,500 per partner; after demand, CAD 100 per partner per day, capped at CAD 2,500. T2 late-filing penalty is 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% monthly (maximum 12 months); minimum CAD 100 for nil-balance corporations. T106 non-filing penalties can reach CAD 2,500 per information return; interest runs on all unpaid balances.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are not automatically liable for filing penalties alone, but may become personally liable for unremitted source deductions, Part XIII withholdings, and GST/HST under s.227.1 if the failure is concurrent with non-withholding, and for fraudulent filing they may be prosecuted personally.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment and Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under s.247(2), the CRA may adjust taxable income to the arm's-length amount; a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment may apply, in addition to tax, interest, and gross-up consequences. Failure to provide documentation demanded by the Minister can trigger penalties and admissible adverse inferences during reassessment. T106 failure penalties up to CAD 2,500 per return may also be assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false transfer-pricing certifications, direct artificial royalty stripping, or conceal intercompany arrangements face personal tax liabilities via civil assessment and criminal exposure; CRA may pierce the LP veil to recover amounts from the corporate general partner's directors.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: Under s.215 and s.227(8), the payer remains liable for the full amount that should have been withheld; a 10% penalty is imposed on unremitted withholdings, plus daily interest. CRA can assess the payer as agent of the Crown and can collect by garnishment, bank seizure, or placing a lien on real property.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding taxes, with no requirement to prove intent; CRA may issue director-liability assessments permitting seizure of personal assets. The limited partners themselves are generally not liable unless they participated in management or directed the withholding failure.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) to deny the tax benefit, recharacterize the income, and assess tax as though the conduit transaction had not occurred; the LP may be denied treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test or Limitation-on-Benefits clause; Part XIII withholding at 25% may be assessed on deemed dividends or royalties; s.163.2 promoter penalties may reach CAD 100,000 or more for planners; the BC registrar may be notified and the LP dissolved for anti-abuse non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who establish or operate such a shell LP for tax-avoidance purposes are exposed to personal promoter penalties, civil restitution, and criminal prosecution for participating in misleading tax planning; courts will disregard limited liability where the corporate general partner acting as director knowingly enabled a sham or misrepresents material facts to the CRA.
