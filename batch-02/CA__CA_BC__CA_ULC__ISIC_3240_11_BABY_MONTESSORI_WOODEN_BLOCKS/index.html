Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online at cra-arc.gc.ca; My Business Account portal; PST registration with BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (BC does not participate in the federal-provincial harmonized sales tax collection agreement for PST).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or resident ULC must apply for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 or Business Registration Online, adding a 'RT' program account for GST/HST once registration is mandatory or elected. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment in Canada must provide a Canadian representative, proof of foreign registration, directors' particulars, and a non-resident address; the BN is issued immediately online. PST registration is separately required with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch before making taxable retail sales in BC; there is no small-supplier exemption for BC PST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (US federal check-the-box treatment under IRC § 7701 via Form 8832); for Canadian domestic income tax purposes, the BC ULC is mandatorily classified as a corporation taxable at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income, with BC provincial corporate income tax at the 12% general rate; the ULC is not itself a CCPC when controlled by non-residents.

Available election forms

US: Form 8832 election to treat the Canadian ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax purposes; Canada: no domestic check-the-box — the ULC remains a corporation under the Income Tax Act; GST/HST: voluntary registration election ETA s. 240(3), joint election for zero-rated related-party supplies s. 273, election for zero-rated intra-group services s. 274; treaty: Canada-US Article IV(7) dual-resident corporation tie-breaker election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies (including worldwide taxable supplies of the non-resident and associated persons) measured over four consecutive calendar quarters under the GST/HST small supplier rules (ETA s. 148); mandatory GST/HST registration once the threshold is exceeded; no equivalent threshold exists for BC PST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s. 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier ships baby Montessori wooden blocks to a destination outside Canada or transfers possession to a common carrier for direct export; services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under s. 7 of Part V Schedule VI unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada; the ULC must hold contemporaneous documentation proving physical export, foreign destination, and non-resident recipient status; the 180-day outward remittance benchmark is a commercial and transfer-pricing norm (including ITA s. 17 imputed-interest scrutiny on receivables from non-residents) rather than a standalone statutory repatriation deadline under Canadian law.

  • CBSA Customs Export Declaration filed electronically in the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES; G13/G14)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the non-resident buyer, foreign port of discharge, and export routing
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the goods, HS classification (e.g., 9503.00), quantity, and export sale price
  • Inward remittance record, bank transfer confirmation, or SWIFT/credit-card settlement evidence proving receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing clearance mandate currently applies in Canada; businesses may issue electronic invoices and must retain them and underlying records in a readable format for six years (ETA s. 286; ITA s. 230); CRA is progressively adopting the voluntary Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing framework, with filing through CRA systems voluntary during the transition; payment rails (Stripe, Amazon Pay) do not act as GST fiscal monitors, but for supplies made after July 1, 2021, a Canadian marketplace facilitator is deemed to make the supply for GST/HST purposes where a non-resident seller sells through the marketplace.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims rigorously and will reverse the 0% rate and assess GST plus interest where proof-of-export documentation is absent or stale; the normal four-year reassessment period is extended indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud; penalties include ETA s. 280 ($250 plus 25% of any tax sought to be evaded), ETA s. 285 gross negligence penalty of up to 50% of the unreported tax, ITA s. 163(2) false-statement penalty of up to 50% of the additional tax, and compounding daily interest under ETA ss. 280-281 and ITA s. 161; corporate directors may face personal liability for unremitted GST/HST under ETA s. 323 and income tax withholdings under ITA s. 227.1.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible under Article VIII, Sections 2, 3, and 4 of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Canada does not impose exchange controls or separate capital-control approvals. Current-account payments, profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, and repatriation of invested capital require no Bank of Canada or Finance approval. Statutory hurdles are limited to corporate law, not FX law: a British Columbia ULC may declare a dividend, redeem shares, or otherwise distribute funds only if the required solvency test under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) is satisfied. Inbound foreign equity investment remains subject to Investment Canada Act thresholds independent of FX convertibility. Outbound remittances to non-residents may be subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), generally up to 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty; goods-payment remittances for typical trade invoices are not subject to withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canadian law imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no prescribed repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign-currency receipts. Exporters may retain export proceeds in foreign currency or convert them freely. Compliance obligations are limited to accurate CAD reporting in income tax filings and to record-keeping and FINTRAC reporting when a single international electronic funds transfer exceeds C$10,000 or when physical currency or monetary instruments crossing the border exceed C$10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); the Canadian foreign-exchange control regime is effectively administered by the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act and the Bretton Woods and Related Agreements Act, with prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and financial-transaction reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. An offshore digital EMI is not a Canadian Schedule I or Schedule II bank and cannot independently access the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System or clear CAD payments. It may be used structurally if it maintains segregated client funds at a Canadian bank and acts only as an invoicing/treasury dashboard, or if it is registered as a money services business with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. In addition, any person that enters into a payment agreement with a Canadian merchant for payment processing may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act and must either be licensed or use a registered Canadian payment service provider. For the ULC treasury, an offshore EMI is not the recommended primary liquidity rail; downstream merchant collection should be limited to regulated payment facilitators such as Stripe or Amazon Pay.

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by the BC ULC to non-resident shareholders, including any deemed dividends arising from distributions in excess of stated capital or from the winding-up of the unlimited liability capital account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual fair market value of controlled transactions with non-resident shareholders triggers the duty to prepare contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation consistent with CRA Information Circular IC-87-2R; Country-by-Country Reporting on Form RC5200 becomes mandatory when the consolidated group revenue reaches CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s.245 and the statutory misuse-or-abuse standard deny tax benefits from transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose, and the BC ULC's unlimited liability structure is particularly vulnerable where bank accounts and invoicing exist without an actual Canadian employee presence or operational decision-making; anti-hybrid rules apply to a ULC whose US owner elects to treat it as a disregarded entity, and FINTRAC applies the beneficial-ownership test in Proceeds of Crime compliance reviews.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated federal tax under ITA s.163(2) plus a parallel 50% BC assessment; criminal prosecution under ITA s.239(1) carries fines up to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to five years; jeopardy orders under ITA s.225.2 permit immediate asset restraint.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted employee source deductions and Part XIII withholding, and such liability is certified and enforced against them personally in the BC Supreme Court; directors face court-imposed travel bans in asset-recovery proceedings, imprisonment upon criminal conviction, and civil restitution of the entire tax shortfall.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance (GAAR and Anti-Hybrid Rules)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under ITA s.245 denying the treaty benefit and restoring the full 25% Part XIII outflow to withholding; GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit; anti-hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s.18.2 and s.18.4 may recharacterize the royalty or interest payment as a non-deductible deemed dividend.

Director personal liability: Because the ULC provides unlimited shareholder liability, directors cannot shelter behind the corporate veil; the CRA sues individual shareholders directly for reassessed amounts, and courts may issue asset freezes and travel bans where a director refuses to post security for the confirmed Part XIII liability.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment, capped at CAD 250,000, with full reassessment interest; the penalty applies independently of any showing of intentional negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign deficient T2 returns face the 50% gross-negligence penalty on the transfer-pricing adjustment; the unlimited liability shareholder must cover the corporate shortfall and is susceptible to a CRA petition in the BC winding-up proceeding.
Anti-Money Laundering (FINTRAC) and Proceeds of Crime Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per contravention for entities and CAD 100,000 per contravention for individuals; continuing offences under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; penalties for false ownership declarations filed with the BC Corporate Registry.

Director personal liability: The ULC removes the corporate limited-liability shield, so directors are personally sued for FINTRAC penalties and for repatriated criminal proceeds; money-laundering offences carry imprisonment up to five years, and directors may be restrained from leaving Canada by court order during an active evasion investigation.
