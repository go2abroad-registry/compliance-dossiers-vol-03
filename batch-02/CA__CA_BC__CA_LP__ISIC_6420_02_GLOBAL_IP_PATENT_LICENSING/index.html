Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account is prescribed for a British Columbia limited partnership (minimum capital requirement: false). Capital contributions are contractual and recorded on partner capital accounts under the partnership agreement. Statutory conditions: at least one general partner must be an individual resident in British Columbia or a corporation with a BC registered office, and a limited partner may not withdraw contributed capital if the limited partnership would thereby become unable to pay its debts. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal income tax and GST/HST administration; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Revenue Division at https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export declaration requirements.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Confirm whether the non-resident Limited Partnership (LP) carries on business in Canada: a patent and trademark licensing business managed by a Canadian-resident general partner creates a Canadian permanent establishment and full filing nexus. Step 2 - Register the LP for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) submitted through BRO, by fax, or by mail; attach Form RC1A to open the GST/HST account (BN + RT program account) whenever worldwide taxable supplies, including royalties for IP used in Canada, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters. Step 3 - Register the LP with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST only if it makes taxable sales of goods or taxable services into BC (pure patent/trademark royalty income is PST-exempt; the remote-vendor PST registration threshold is CAD 10,000 of BC gross revenue in the preceding 12 months). Step 4 - If the LP has no physical presence in Canada, appoint a resident representative using Form RC59 and file a written mandate with the CRA. Step 5 - File the annual partnership information return (Form T5013) and issue T5013 slips to all partners; the T5013 filing confirms the pass-through classification and evidences each partner's share of Canadian-source income. Step 6 - Obtain the Canada partnership identification number issued by the CRA and use it as the partnership TIN for withholding, bank account opening, and intercompany royalty documentation. The BN is normally issued within 2 to 10 business days for online filings and within 4 to 6 weeks for paper filings; GST/HST registration is effective from the date the BN is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - For Canadian income tax, the BC Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; no entity-level income tax is imposed, the LP files only an information return, and each partner is separately taxed on its allocated share of Canadian-source royalty income. A non-resident partner is taxable in Canada on its share of the LP's business income to the extent the LP carries on business through a permanent establishment in Canada. For GST/HST, the LP is treated as an independent registrant or 'person', is required to charge GST on taxable Canadian supplies of intangible property unless zero-rated, and may claim input tax credits on Canadian legal, accounting, patent-administration, and advisory fees. For BC PST, the supply of intangible personal property (patent and trademark rights) is not a taxable supply, so the LP is not required to collect PST on royalty streams; British Columbia levies no entity-level capital tax on LPs.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime; classification is statutory. Available elections for the LP: (1) joint election under section 150 of the Excise Tax Act between closely related registrants (90% common ownership of value and control) to treat intercompany supplies of IP and administrative services as being made for nil consideration, eliminating GST leakage in the sub-licensing chain; (2) election under section 216 of the Income Tax Act for each non-resident partner receiving Canadian royalty income to file a net-basis Canadian return, displacing the 25% gross Part XIII withholding (treaty rates may reduce royalties to 10% or 0%) and allowing deduction of Canadian expenses with refund of over-withheld tax; (3) rollover election under section 97(2) of the Income Tax Act for tax-deferred transfers of Canadian capital property into the partnership, available only where all electing partners are Canadian residents and therefore generally unavailable where non-resident partners hold direct interests; (4) documentation of non-application of the hybrid mismatch rules under section 94.2 of the Income Tax Act where the same royalty income is deductible in the licensee jurisdiction, preserving the integrity of the Canadian pass-through treatment. The general anti-avoidance rule under section 245 remains applicable to any election or structure lacking economic substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including royalty income for IP used in Canada) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; the threshold is computed on worldwide revenue, so a global IP licensing LP will almost always exceed CAD 30,000 and must register. Registration is voluntary below the threshold but is recommended to recover input tax credits. Where the non-resident LP is not registered, the Canadian GST-registered licensee must self-assess the 5% GST under the imported taxable supply rules (sections 217 to 218.1 of the Excise Tax Act). For BC PST, the remote-vendor registration threshold is CAD 10,000 of gross revenue from taxable sales into BC in the preceding 12 months, but the supply of patent and trademark rights remains PST-exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 10 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of a right in respect of intangible personal property (patent, trademark, industrial design, or know-how licence) made by a GST/HST registrant to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant is zero-rated where all rights are for use exclusively outside Canada. If any right is exercisable in Canada, or the royalty is calculated by reference to Canadian sales, the supply is taxable at 5% GST unless a separate de minimis foreign-use rule applies. The LP must be a registrant to claim zero-rating; an unregistered supplier cannot zero-rate, and the Canadian recipient must self-assess the GST where the IP is acquired for use in Canada. Associated services such as IP enforcement, monitoring, and marketing support are zero-rated only if they relate to IP for use exclusively outside Canada and are supplied to the same non-resident; technical assistance performed partly in Canada remains taxable at 5% GST.

  • GST/HST zero-rated supply invoice (commercial invoice) identifying the licence, parties, royalty amount, and the non-resident status of the licensee
  • Executed patent and trademark licence agreement, together with any sub-licence chain documentation, evidencing the geographic scope of rights and exclusive foreign use
  • Certificate of residence of the licensee (CRA Form NR301 or equivalent treaty residence certificate) confirming the licensee is a non-resident person not registered for GST/HST
  • Inward remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation, bank certificate, or inward remittance advice) proving payment of the royalty from abroad in a convertible currency
  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration - required only where the licensing transaction embeds tangible deliverables (prototype, media, or equipment) exported from Canada; not required for pure IP royalty flows
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill - required only for accompanying physical exports under the same transaction; not applicable to services-only licence royalties
  • LP books and records evidencing input tax credit allocation and the basis for claiming the 0% rate, retained for six years as required by the Excise Tax Act

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice validation regime for GST/HST purposes. The CRA requires electronic filing and remittance of GST/HST returns (NETFILE/GST/HST or financial institution channels) for registrants with annual taxable turnover exceeding CAD 1.5 million. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework launched in March 2024 and aligned with the Peppol network is voluntary for commercial B2B transactions, while federal government procurement above CAD 25,000 requires electronic data interchange (EDI) or e-invoice submission. For cross-border patent and trademark royalty flows, the LP should issue standard commercial invoices and retain digitally signed licence agreements; no tax stamp, fiscal clearance certificate, or e-invoice authorization is required.

The CRA applies heightened audit scrutiny to zero-rated IP structures where the licensee is a related Canadian entity exploiting the IP within Canada, where royalty rates fall outside arm's-length ranges without a transfer-pricing study (section 247 of the Income Tax Act), or where the LP lacks commercial substance. Retrospective reassessment is permitted within 4 years of the original assessment, extended to 7 years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect or carelessness, and unlimited in time where fraud is alleged. On adverse reassessment, the LP must remit the uncollected 5% GST on the gross royalty with prescribed interest compounded daily; false statements attract a gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the net tax amount under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, and repeat or wilful evasion is an indictable offence under section 327 punishable by a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years. The burden of proof is on the taxpayer to substantiate zero-rating; failure to produce the complete documentation bundle results in automatic clawback. For FX repatriation, although Canada has no statutory export repatriation deadline, CRA transfer-pricing practice treats royalty receivables outstanding beyond 180 days from the invoice date as non-arm's-length loans or contributions, potentially triggering imputed interest and Part XIII withholding adjustments under sections 247 and 214 of the Income Tax Act.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. No exchange control legislation restricts the acquisition, holding, or disposition of foreign currency; the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act impose no approval, quota, or licensing requirements. A British Columbia Limited Partnership engaged in patent licensing may freely convert CAD to USD or other major currencies, and royalty or profit remittances to non-resident partners are unrestricted. The only applicable compliance thresholds are FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 and Part XIII income tax withholding (up to 25%, treaty-reducible) on outbound royalties and distributions with an income character.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation requirement for export proceeds or royalty receipts. Royalty income may be retained in foreign-currency accounts in Canada or abroad without central-bank settlement. Reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC large-value EFT declarations, Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys, and the annual BC Registry corporate filing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in conjunction with the Minister of Finance acting through the Exchange Fund Account; prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); and AML/CFT enforcement by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Foreign digital EMI licensees are not recognized as deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot access Bank of Canada settlement infrastructure. An offshore EMI (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Wise Business) may serve as a payment facilitation rail only if registered as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC and, when applicable, authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act. The primary treasury account must remain at a Schedule I Canadian bank to preserve regulatory substance, audit trail integrity, and lender comfort; EMIs are best limited to FX conversion and small-ticket royalty disbursement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign partners wire capital contributions in CAD or USD via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's designated BC operational or capital escrow account. Payments exceeding CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC large-value EFT reporting; each wire must cite the LP's corporate identifier, the capital-call reference, and purpose code 'Investment - Capital Contribution' consistent with Statistics Canada's FDI survey classification. Beneficiary account details must match pre-registered wiring instructions; a capital-call resolution approving the injection is retained as compliance evidence. Greenfield LP formation and passive capital inflows are not subject to Investment Canada Act review unless the transaction constitutes acquisition of control of an existing Canadian business.Royalty payments to non-resident licensors and partners settle via SWIFT MT103 for CAD and USD; Canadian-dollar clearing uses the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and US-dollar clearing uses CHIPS or Fedwire through correspondent banks. Each payment must bear the ROYA or 'licensing fee' purpose code, contract and invoice references, and apply the correct Part XIII withholding (25% statutory, treaty-reduced upon filing NR301/NR302). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility. Foreign digital EMI licensees are not recognized as deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot access Bank of Canada settlement infrastructure. An offshore EMI (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Wise Business) may serve as a payment facilitation rail only if registered as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC and, when applicable, authorized under the Retail Payment Activities Act. The primary treasury account must remain at a Schedule I Canadian bank to preserve regulatory substance, audit trail integrity, and lender comfort; EMIs are best limited to FX conversion and small-ticket royalty disbursement.
