Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under the BC Limited Partnership Act. A limited partner must contribute money or property with a determinable value, which may be nominal (e.g., CAD 100); the general partner is not subject to a minimum capital contribution. Contributions are generally locked in until dissolution or as provided in the partnership agreement.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax administration, including GST/HST, income tax, payroll and import/export program accounts; primary portal: 'My Business Account' and 'Business Registration Online' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial sales tax administration in British Columbia: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, via the 'eTaxBC' portal (https://etaxbc.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Formation: register the limited partnership with the BC Registrar of Companies under the Partnership Act (RSBC 1996, c. 348) through the Corporate Online registry; obtain the BC Limited Partnership registration number. Step 2 — Federal Business Number (BN): apply through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain the partnership's own 15-character BN, selecting program account 'RT' for GST/HST; select 'RM' only when physical goods are imported or exported. The partnership, not each individual partner, obtains the BN; non-resident partners are allowed and do not require a Canadian Social Insurance Number to activate the partnership's BN. Step 3 — BC Provincial Sales Tax: register for PST through an eTaxBC account with the Consumer Taxation Branch when the partnership first makes PST-taxable sales of digital goods, software, or taxable services in British Columbia; BC does not apply a monetary de minimis to the basic vendor registration obligation. Step 4 — Payroll: open an 'RP' payroll program account where Canadian employees or an agency relationship create a Canadian source of wages subject to withholding. Step 5 — Information returns: the LP must file T5013 (Statement of Partnership Income) within five months after the partnership fiscal year end and annually report all partners' allocations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No GAAR-styled 'check-the-box' election exists under the Canadian Income Tax Act. The BC limited partnership is a statutory flow-through entity: the partnership computes income at the entity level only for measurement under ITA s. 96, while each partner reports its allocated share. Available elections and certifications include: (i) a voluntary GST/HST registration election by a small supplier under ETA s. 241(1) to recover input tax credits; (ii) ETA s. 156/157 elections to treat closely related persons as a single or non-taxable group where strict corporate/partnership relationship tests are met; (iii) an election under ITA s. 103(2) to allocate partnership income or loss in accordance with the partners' economic arrangement, including preferential returns and carried-interest distributions; (iv) a partnership fiscal-year-end election under ITA s. 249(2) to align the LP's year end with its significant partners; and (v) a fiscal-transparency confirmation for cross-border partners under Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Tax Treaty. For US federal tax purposes, the BC LP is a foreign eligible entity that defaults to partnership classification under Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3(b)(1)(i) because it lacks continuity of life, but partners may file Form 8832 to affirmatively elect corporate classification (a non-default check-the-box election) if that is commercially advantageous.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (ETA ss. 148(1)-(6.2), 'small supplier' threshold, based on worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada in the current calendar quarter and the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters; for non-resident and offshore digital service suppliers, the simplified digital-services registration rules impose a similar CAD 30,000 Canada-source revenue threshold over a rolling 12-month period. Below this threshold registration is voluntary; above it, GST/HST registration is legally mandatory).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualification for the 0% GST/HST export rate is available under the following cumulative conditions: (1) where the anti-cheat engine is delivered as tangible media, hardware cryptographic dongles, or data-centre appliances, the physical export is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part I to the ETA upon proof of actual export from Canada; (2) where the middleware is supplied as a service, the service must be supplied to a recipient that is a non-resident, not an individual present in Canada, and the service must not be performed in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance — ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 5(1); (3) where the transaction is characterized as a supply of intangible personal property (software licence or SaaS access), zero-rating requires all commercial exploitation and operational use to occur outside Canada, including foreign gaming-operator servers, foreign head-office locations, and non-Canadian end-user points; (4) the non-resident customer must not be a GST/HST registrant at the time of supply; and (5) no part of the exported middleware may be provided to a Canadian permanent establishment or for Canadian domestic gaming operations. A properly zero-rated export allows the LP to offset or recover input tax credits on the creation, marketing, and maintenance costs of the anti-cheat engine; if the supply is outside the scope of GST/HST altogether, the same credit entitlement does not arise, so the foreign-facing B2B supply should be structured to meet the zero-rating conditions rather than falling outside scope.

  • Commercial invoice or GST/HST zero-rated export invoice stating the supplier's BN-RT account, purchaser name and address, 'Zero-rated Export' notation, and the applicable ETA Schedule VI Part reference

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, government API clearance, or digital stamping mandate on invoices as of the 2025 compliance cycle. The CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns (NETFILE/XML) for registrants whose total taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,000,000 and for all non-resident digital-service registrants. Remittance is accepted through CRA 'My Payment' or wire to the Receiver General bank account. The pan-Canadian PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 e-invoicing standard is adopted voluntarily, principally for provincial public procurements, and is not a statutory condition for GST/HST deduction or export zero-rating. Compliance is documentary: invoices and export evidence must be retained for six years under ETA s. 286(1).

The CRA audits export zero-rating claims with documentary substantiation at the forefront. Failure to establish that the goods or digital deliverables actually left Canada, or that the service was received and exploited wholly outside Canada, results in reassessment of the supply at 5% GST (or 12% HST where the customer is in an HST province) together with interest and the retroactive clawback of any input tax credits claimed. Reassessment windows are four years under ETA s. 298(2) for GST/HST and three years under ITA s. 152(4) for income tax; the limitation periods are extended indefinitely where false statements or misrepresentation are present. Civil penalties: ETA s. 280(1) imposes a penalty of CAD 4,000 or 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded for false statements and evasion; ITA s. 163(2) imposes a 50% gross-negligence penalty on the understated tax or overstated refund; criminal prosecution for knowing evasion may be pursued under ETA s. 327(3) and ITA s. 239, exposing the limited partners and managing general partner to fines up to CAD 500,000 and/or imprisonment. At the provincial level, reclassification of an exported digital product later used in BC triggers BC PST liability, plus interest and vendor penalties under the Consumer Taxation Act, and may also suspend the LP's eTaxBC registration. Retained records must prove the bona fide export within the 180-day commercial remittance window and the complete isolation of the middleware's operation from Canadian servers and players.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency regime under the Bank of Canada Act. Capital account transactions are unrestricted, permitting free repatriation of profits, dividends, and equity distributions without prior approval. Current account transactions, including export proceeds and service fees, face no convertibility restrictions. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal, limited to standard corporate tax filings, beneficial ownership verification, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export receipts applies under Canadian law. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, converted to CAD at market rates, or repatriated freely. Importers and exporters must file large cash transaction reports with FINTRAC if exceeding CAD 10,000; no statutory conversion ratios or surrender timelines exist.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
