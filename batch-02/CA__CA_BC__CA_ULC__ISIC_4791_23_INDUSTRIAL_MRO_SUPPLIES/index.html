Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal corporate income tax and GST/HST; British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) – provincial sales tax (PST). CRA portal: My Business Account / GST/HST e-services at canada.ca; BC portal: eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A foreign ULC must register with CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST account using Form RC1 or electronically via My Business Account if the entity carries on business in Canada or supplies taxable goods/services in Canada. The BN is the statutory tax identification number. For sales into British Columbia, the ULC must separately register for a BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) account through eTaxBC, generally using Form BC105. The ULC must provide a Canadian address for service and maintain corporate records in Canada. Registration is required before exceeding the GST/HST small supplier threshold or making taxable supplies in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (for U.S. federal tax purposes); Entity_Level_Taxation under the Canadian Income Tax Act as a corporation.

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists; classification under Canadian law is statutory. For U.S. tax purposes, a BC/AB ULC may file IRS Form 8832 to elect disregarded entity or partnership status. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.2 and 18.3) apply automatically to disallow certain deduction benefits and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in annual worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST small supplier threshold); BC PST has no monetary registration threshold — any vendor making taxable sales into British Columbia must register (subject to marketplace facilitator rules).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to tangible goods physically exported from Canada (Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act) and to supplies of services directly tied to exported goods (e.g., transportation, arrangement, MRO services) provided to non-residents. The supplier must obtain and retain documentary evidence of export; goods must be delivered outside Canada within a reasonable period.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA electronic export report
  • Commercial invoice showing exported goods, value, and destination
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or other transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire proof or payment confirmation from foreign customer

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping in Canada. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding $1M CAD must file returns electronically (Form GST34). Invoices must contain prescribed information under section 223(1) of the Excise Tax Act, including supplier name, BN, GST/HST registration number, transaction date, and tax charged. No government API clearance or pre-stamping requirement exists.

CRA may deny zero-rating on audit where export documentation is insufficient, and may reassess net tax plus interest within the normal assessment period (4 years, unlimited in cases of misrepresentation or neglect). Penalties include Excise Tax Act section 285 (12% penalty), section 281.1 gross negligence penalty (25% of the deficiency), and section 280.1 false statement penalties. Wilful evasion can lead to criminal prosecution under section 327. Delayed or incomplete FX repatriation may trigger heightened CRA audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under IMF Article VIII. The CAD is a free-floating, freely tradeable currency with no currency allocation, prior approval, repatriation licensing, or profit-remittance permit. A BC ULC may remit dividends, branch profits, management fees, royalties, intercompany loan repayments, and equity redemptions to non-resident shareholders without any foreign-exchange obstacle; the only frictions are tax-based under Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding (statutory 25% on payments to non-residents, reduced by applicable tax treaty) and FINTRAC reporting thresholds (record-keeping at CAD 10,000 and international EFT tracking at CAD 1,000,000). These are tax and AML controls, not exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion obligation on export proceeds. There is no mandatory timeline and no minimum conversion ratio for foreign-currency export receipts. A BC ULC may lawfully retain USD, EUR, or CNY receipts indefinitely in onshore foreign-currency deposit accounts or in offshore treasury accounts, subject to the general anti-avoidance rule in Income Tax Act s. 245 and internal corporate treasury policy.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). No dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration exists in Canada; foreign exchange policy is administered via the Currency Act, the Financial Administration Act, and the prudential supervision exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) over authorized dealers. FINTRAC enforces AML/CFT controls on cross-border wire transfers and large cash transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. The Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), with registration phased in from November 1, 2024, and supervision by the Bank of Canada, now governs payment service providers. Cross-border digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex, holding FINTRAC money-services-business registrations, are legally permitted for B2B collections and multi-currency supplier payouts; they are not authorized deposit-taking institutions and cannot issue documentary letters of credit or host the ULC's capital-escrow balance. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the sole shareholder executes the subscription resolution and wires equity via SWIFT MT103 with field 70 reciting 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — SHARE SUBSCRIPTION — CA_ULC TREASURY' and referencing the bank's escrow facility where applicable. The wire settles in USD at the Schedule I bank's New York or Canadian correspondent and is credited to the ULC's USD deposit or CAD operating account after a Funds Verification Memo and UBO check. For single wires above CAD 1,000,000, the bank must receive pre-notification plus an audited source-of-funds file; artificial wire splitting to evade FINTRAC EFT tracking thresholds is strictly impermissible. The ULC's board minutes record the subscription; a capital-declaration certificate is delivered to the bank to prevent re-characterization as debt under Income Tax Act s. 15(2), s. 80.4, and the thin-capitalization rules.
