Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration via Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-business/business-registration-online.html; GST/HST registration administered through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE and My Business Account portals.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership, the federal tax identifier is the CRA Business Number (BN), not a separate TIN. Foreign entities must first register the LP under applicable provincial limited partnership legislation, then obtain a BN through BRO or by filing Form RC1 with the CRA. Program accounts required include the RT (GST/HST) account and, where applicable, RC (corporate income) and RP (payroll). Non-resident partners should obtain an individual tax number (ITN) or use their foreign tax ID in T5013 partnership reporting where no ITN is assigned.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — under Canadian federal income tax law, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent; the partnership itself is not subject to income tax, and each partner is taxed on allocated income, gains, and tax credits. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is treated as a separate registrant 'person' and must hold its own GST/HST registration with the CRA when it ceases to be a small supplier.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or deemed-entity election corresponding to US Subchapter K. Entity classification follows provincial partnership law and CRA administrative practice. Relevant statutory elections for a Canadian LP include: (1) Income Tax Act s. 96(3) re partnership fiscal period; (2) s. 97(2) deemed disposition on property contributed to the partnership; and (3) s. 152(4) waiver or adjustment of assessment. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5 may apply to cross-border arrangements but do not permit elective entity reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including supplies of associated persons) made in the current or immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once this small supplier threshold is exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. Voluntary registration is available to non-residents carrying on business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule IX to the Excise Tax Act when the supplier exports the goods and retains proper documentary evidence. Services supplied to a non-resident are generally zero-rated under s. 7 of Part V of Schedule IX if the supply is made to a non-resident recipient, is not a supply made to an individual in Canada, and is not in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. AI data-labeling and RLHF training services delivered by a Canadian LP to foreign principals may qualify as zero-rated non-resident services where the contract, invoicing, payment, and actual use occur for the non-resident's benefit and the service is not consumed in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) and Canada Export Reporting System (CES) filing for physical goods exports above the CAD 2,000 threshold
  • Commercial invoice and signed service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, nature of the data-labeling/BPO service, and explicit 0% GST/HST supply
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill for physical exports; for cross-border digital services, a dated completion certificate or delivery log describing the service deliverable
  • FX settlement evidence: SWIFT payment confirmation, foreign exchange conversion record, or an inward remittance certificate tracing payment from the non-resident to the Canadian LP
  • Non-resident status declaration with the recipient's country of residence, proof of foreign business address, and contractual confirmation of non-Canadian use and export of the service output

Canada does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls at the federal level. GST/HST registrants above the threshold must file returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE. E-invoicing and EDI are accepted for record-keeping; CRA may require electronic retention of source records, but no statutory API clearance process applies.

CRA may reassess zero-rated supplies, deny input tax credits or refunds, and impose retrospective GST/HST, interest, and penalties for missing or insufficient substantiation. Under Excise Tax Act s. 285(1), a 20% gross negligence penalty may apply where a person knowingly or under circumstances of culpable conduct makes a false statement, omits information, or participates in a false zero-rating claim. Criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act can result in fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. The limitation period for reassessment is generally four years, but is extended indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation attributable to negligence, carelessness, or wilful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, meaning no restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions. Dividends, interest, royalties, and partnership profit distributions to non-residents are freely remittable subject to applicable withholding tax (Part XIII of the Income Tax Act) and standard AML/KYC screening. No approval, quota, or surrender obligation applies to capital inflows or outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Non-resident and resident exporters may hold foreign currency balances directly or through Canadian chartered bank accounts, and there is no obligation to repatriate or convert export proceeds into CAD within any prescribed period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary policy and currency issuance authority); banking and payments prudential regulation is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering transaction reporting is supervised by FINTRAC. Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange control authority or exchange control act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Foreign electronic money institutions and cross-border digital wallets are not recognized as chartered banks in Canada; to provide money-transfer or payment services to Canadian clients, a non-resident fintech must generally register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and may require provincial money-transfer licensing. Wise (operating in Canada as an MSB) is viable for operational disbursements and payroll-like outbound payments, but it is not suitable as the sole clearing bank for capital escrow, investment-grade treasury, or commercial letters of credit. PayPal Pay out is acceptable only for low-value mass disbursements and must be integrated with a Tier-1 bank for corporate liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections should be executed via SWIFT wire transfer from the foreign parent or partner's bank to the LP's Canadian-dollar Tier-1 corporate account. The wire must include the recipient's legal name exactly as registered, LP registered office address, account number, and a purpose-of-payment narrative stating 'capital contribution to Canadian limited partnership – AI data labeling BPO venture'. For regulatory clarity, the remittance should be reported in FINTRAC cross-border wire transfer reporting when above CAD 10,000, and the recipient bank should be provided with a capital call notice, partnership agreement extract, and beneficial ownership schedule to avoid placement in the AML escalation queue. No specific approval is required from the Bank of Canada or OSFI for a non-resident capital injection into a Canadian LP, but the bank may apply internal enhanced due diligence for jurisdictions outside Canada.Operational payroll and independent contractor disbursements within Canada should be executed via local EFT / direct deposit (Automated Clearing Settlement System). International trade settlements and vendor disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 corporate-to-corporate payment messages or commercial letters of credit for documented purchases of software, hardware, and vendor services.
