Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal taxpayer portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); business registration online: Canada Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business/business-number.html)

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96; the partnership itself is not subject to Part I income tax; income, losses, and tax credits are allocated to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement; for GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is a distinct 'person' under ETA s. 123(1) and must register and remit in its own name to the extent it is engaged in commercial activity)

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; classification of a limited partnership as flow-through is statutory and non-elective under the Income Tax Act. Relevant elections available: (1) Fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(1) permitting a non-calendar fiscal period where no partner is an individual; (2) GST/HST Quick Method election under ETA s. 227 for qualifying registrants, enabling simplified output-tax accounting; (3) Annual GST/HST filing election under ETA s. 238 for registrants with nominal taxable supplies; (4) ITA s. 216 election for qualifying non-resident partners to file Canadian tax returns on a net basis for Canadian-source rental income; (5) Hybrid mismatch integrity rules under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 apply nondiscretionarily to denied hybrid mismatch deductions and contain no elective carve-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Small supplier threshold: CAD 30,000. A person is a small supplier where the value of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated but excluding exempt and financial supplies) is equal to or less than CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter and in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters (ETA s. 123(1) 'small supplier', s. 148(1)); registration becomes mandatory on the day the threshold is exceeded, and the registrant must apply for GST/HST registration within 30 days of that day; volunteered registration is permitted for persons engaged in commercial activity (ETA s. 240(1)(b))

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: A supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated where the supplier exports the property in accordance with the conditions in Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1-6 of the Excise Tax Act (e.g., s. 1: possession transferred in Canada but property exported by the supplier; s. 10 for supplies of goods leased/loaned to non-residents); the supplier must retain proof of actual export within a reasonable time. Services: A supply of services made to a non-resident person is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7(a)(i) where the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, not a service of acting as an agent, not courier or postal, and not advertising directed at the Canadian market; a specific carve-out applies where the non-resident is registered for GST/HST and acquires the service primarily for consumption in Canada (s. 7(c)). Professional multilingual translation and media dubbing services delivered digitally to offshore broadcasters, streaming platforms, or localization distributors qualify for zero-rating under s. 7(a)(i) provided the client is a non-resident not acquiring the service for use in Canada; the master recording or digital deliverables are characterized as a service for GST/HST purposes. Zero-rating must be claimed at the time of invoicing; input tax credits on related costs remain fully available where the supply is zero-rated. Refund of net GST overpayments is claimed on the GST/HST return (GST34) on an accrual basis, subject to ETA s. 122(2) documentary substantiation.

  • Canada Export Declaration (B13A, Customs Coding Form) or CBSA automated export reporting through the Canadian International Trade Single Window for merchandise exports with a value above CAD 2,000
  • Commercial invoice evidencing the nature, value, and destination of the supply
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / waybill showing the foreign consignee and foreign destination
  • Proof of export mode and destination (carrier manifest and customs confirmation)
  • Executed localization, translation, or dubbing services agreement with the non-resident client, including the governing-law and delivery clauses
  • Bank transfer, SWIFT, Wise B2B, or Payoneer settlement records evidencing receipt of consideration from outside Canada attributable to the zero-rated supply

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or statutory B2B e-invoicing requirement. CRA accepts electronic records (EDI, XML, PDF) provided they contain the prescribed invoice particulars under ETA s. 221 and s. 169(4); the federal government has adopted Peppol interoperability on a voluntary basis through the Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing initiative; GST/HST returns are filed electronically (GST34 / NETFILE / online) and payments are due on the last day of the month following the reporting period.

The Excise Tax Act imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for zero-rated exports; the 180-day benchmark is adopted here as a best-practice documentary control to demonstrate the genuine offshore character of the supply before CRA audit. On audit, CRA applies the 'evidence satisfactory to the Minister' standard under ETA s. 169(4) and s. 122(2): where exports are not substantiated to the Minister's satisfaction, zero-rating is reversed and net GST at 5% is assessed on the full consideration, with arrear and late-filing interest calculated from the original due date. Reassessment may occur within four years of the relevant return date unless misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default extends the window indefinitely. Penalties include: (i) ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty of up to 20% of the amount assessed where false statements or omissions are made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to culpable conduct; (ii) ETA s. 280 failure-to-remit penalties of 1% of the unpaid amount plus prescribed interest; (iii) third-party civil penalties under ITA s. 163.2 for persons participating in false statements; and (iv) summary conviction or indictable offences under the Excise Tax Act, the Criminal Code (fraud), or the Income Tax Act for wilful evasion. CRA also scrutinizes Part XIII withholding on cross-border distributions where the partnership's allocation to non-resident partners is recharacterized as Canadian-source income; failure to file NR4 and to withhold may trigger penalties under ITA s. 227(8) and (9).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized capital-account and current-account regime for residents and non-residents. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible, there is no general exchange-control licensing, no prior approval requirement for foreign direct investment, and no demonstrable restriction on profit remittance, partnership distributions, or intercompany payments. A Canadian Limited Partnership is a pass-through vehicle, and repatriation by a non-resident limited partner is generally structured as a distribution of exempt partnership earnings; however, Part XIII withholding tax may apply in limited circumstances where a non-resident partner is allocated certain Canadian-source passive income such as royalties, interest, rents, or dividends. All transactions are also subject to FINTRAC anti-money-laundering reporting and CRS/FATCA due-diligence obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no requirement to sell foreign currency export earnings to a domestic financial institution. Export receipts in CAD or foreign currency may be freely retained, invoiced, settled, and hedged; the only relevant obligations are FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, routine customs declaration under CBSA rules, and Canadian corporate/partnership tax reporting of worldwide income by resident partners.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control administration, with prudential banking supervision exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Bank of Canada acting as the government's fiscal agent.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as an ancillary payment rail, Low as a primary bank-account replacement. Wise B2B and Payoneer are licensed money-service businesses and payment facilitators, not OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institutions; they cannot lawfully hold funds in the manner of a Canadian bank operating account or serve as the custodian of a capital escrow. They are viable for low-value outbound vendor disbursements, localization vendor payroll, and urgent international settlements, but only where the Canadian LP maintains a Schedule I Bank operating account as the master account and routes such fintech platforms under a client-agent arrangement that avoids any unauthorized deposit-taking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contribution from a foreign limited partner or parent entity is routed by SWIFT wire or correspondent banking transfer to the LP's CAD operating account at a Schedule I Bank. The wire must include a clear remittance rationale, preferably 'INVESTMENT / CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO CANADIAN LIMITED PARTNERSHIP,' and cite the LP's legal entity identifier and CRA business number where available. There is no central-bank approval, no surrender requirement, and no inbound capital verification certificate required at the federal level; however, the receiving bank must conduct sanctions and OFAC screening, and FINTRAC reporting is triggered for electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. Any USD inbound amount may be converted to CAD at the issuing bank's wholesale spot rate, with the deposit record retained as part of the transaction file.Outbound trade disbursements are cleared through Canada's high-value payment system (Lynx), which replaces the former Large Value Transfer System, for urgent domestic CAD payments. International vendor settlements and localization contract payments are executed via SWIFT MT103 through RBC, TD, BMO, or a comparable Canadian clearing bank, using the vendor's IBAN or US routing/Fedwire details as applicable. For larger production milestones, commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C) can be issued in CAD or USD through the Canadian bank's trade desk, especially when the counterparty supplier requires bank-validated payment undertaking. USD disbursements are settled through the Canadian bank's United States correspondent account using Fedwire or CHIPS as the underlying clearing rail.
