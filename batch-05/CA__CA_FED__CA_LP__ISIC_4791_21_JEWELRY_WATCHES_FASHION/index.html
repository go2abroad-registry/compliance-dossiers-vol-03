Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a Canadian Limited Partnership, the statute requires a registered office address within the province of registration. Virtual office services that provide a physical street address and accept legal service of process are generally acceptable, but only if there is a physical location used by a registered agent/manager and the LP can be served there. However, for medium-risk e-commerce jewelry/watches, a mere virtual office is permissible for registration; the likelihood of FINTRAC or CRA requiring corporate substance is moderate. The Canadian Revenue Agency (CRA) generally expects a place of business; a virtual office may be challenged if the LP has no real operations. For an online retail entity, no physical storefront is required, but a real operating management office may be expected.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies and Personal Property Security Branch (CPSPB), Ministry of Government and Consumer Services of the governing province, with federal Business Number coordination through the Canada Revenue Agency Business Registration Portal and Corporations Canada, Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada.

Governing statute

Canada has no federal limited partnership statute; formation is effected under the Limited Partnerships Act of the governing province (e.g., R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16, ss. 2, 3, 8, 23) including extra-provincial registration provisions. Federal recognition and tax treatment are governed by the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), ss. 96 and 102 (partnership income computation and fiscal period), and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, S.C. 2000, c. 17, s. 5(l) (dealer in precious metals and stones registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 Declaration of Limited Partnership under s. 8 of the Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16 (or equivalent provincial registration statement), supplemented by CRA Form RC1 Request for Business Number Registration.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account requirement applies to a Canadian limited partnership. Capital contributions are fixed contractually in the Limited Partnership Agreement; the general partner must maintain a capital account ledger, and each limited partner's liability is limited to its unpaid agreed contribution under s. 23 of the applicable Limited Partnerships Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; primary portal: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); GST/HST electronic filing via GST/HST NETFILE; customs-related filings via CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal operated by the Canada Border Services Agency.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian Limited Partnership is a distinct 'person' for GST/HST and for the Income Tax Act's partnership reporting regime. The LP must obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) with applicable program accounts before making taxable supplies, or immediately upon exceeding the small-supplier threshold. Step-by-step: (1) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and certain program accounts' through CRA Business Registration Online (electronic) or paper; (2) open the required BN program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RM (import/export account for customs transactions), and RP (payroll) where employees exist; (3) designate a partner as the 'designated member' responsible for partnership filings; (4) file Form T5013 / T5013 Summary (Partnership Information Return) annually with CRA, including schedules for non-resident partners; (5) issue T5013 slips allocating income, deductions, and credits to each partner. A non-resident partner with no Canadian permanent establishment is not separately required to obtain a BN for income tax, but the partnership's Canadian business presence creates filing and collection obligations at partnership level; non-resident partners may require treaty-based reduced withholding certificates (Forms NR301/NR601) for any Part XIII payments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box entity-classification election; classification flows from the Income Tax Act, which mandates pass-through treatment for a validly constituted LP under s. 96(1) (partnership income/loss computed as if the partnership were a separate person, then allocated to partners; LP itself is not a Part I taxpayer). Pertinent statutory elections: (i) s. 97(2) tax-deferred rollover on transfer of property to the partnership; (ii) s. 96(3) election on fiscal period / deemed year-end; (iii) Excise Tax Act s. 273 election for GST/HST supplies of capital property between closely related persons; (iv) marketplace platform 'deemed supplier' commercial election under ETA s. 211.21 for online sales; (v) for non-resident partners, treaty withholding reduction elections using Form NR301 with CRA; (vi) the partnership may elect under ETA s. 177(1.1) to have a single member operate the GST/HST account if the partnership satisfies the conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1)(a), determined by total worldwide taxable supplies (including associates) and any current-quarter amount over the four immediately preceding calendar quarters. For e-commerce, non-resident suppliers of goods into Canada (fashion jewelry and fine timepieces sold online, whether through marketplace or direct channel) must register for GST/HST once Canadian taxable sales exceed CAD 30,000 over any trailing 12-month period; where a third-party marketplace is used, the platform is deemed the supplier under ETA s. 211.21 for goods delivered in Canada, which shifts the registration burden to the platform unless the platform makes a commercial election with the seller.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (fashion jewelry and watches) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 of the ETA when: (i) the LP supplies the goods by way of sale and ships or delivers them to a destination outside Canada by common carrier or mail at the direction of the recipient; (ii) the goods are exported before being consumed or used in Canada; and (iii) the LP maintains the prescribed export evidence (records sufficient to prove that the supplier or an agent delivered the goods to a carrier for shipment outside Canada or sent them by mail). Offshore services such as marketing, web development, or design rendered by the LP are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where they are performed entirely outside Canada and are for the benefit of the non-resident recipient. For marketplace transactions, the deemed-supplier rule (ETA s. 211.21) may recharacterize the platform as the supplier for goods delivered in Canada; however, direct fulfillment by the LP from Canada to a foreign customer retains zero-rating eligibility. The LP should also verify the destination country's import VAT/customs treatment because zero-rating under Canadian law does not exempt jewelry exports from foreign import VAT, and GST/HST collected on non-qualifying domestic supplies must not be claimed as export zero-rating.

  • CBSA Commercial Export Declaration (CARM/CAERS filing) for exports valued at CAD 2,000 or more or for controlled goods
  • Commercial Invoice containing full description, HS classification (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 458310), quantity, unit value, currency, Incoterms, and seller/buyer details
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / courier manifest evidencing shipment to a foreign destination
  • Proof of delivery outside Canada (carrier tracking history, destination customs release, or signed delivery receipt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/payment gateway record showing receipt of consideration in foreign currency from the non-resident purchaser
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (where requested by foreign customs or for free-trade agreement preference claims)

No real-time tax-authority fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoice authentication is mandated in Canada (unlike Latin American or Italian tax-control regimes). Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE) applies to registrants whose annual gross revenue exceeds CAD 1.5 million; taxpayers below that threshold may file on paper. CRA requires full business records in readable electronic format for audit. For cross-border merchandise exports, CBSA operates the CARM system, which requires electronic submission of commercial export declarations through the CARM/API portal for goods valued at CAD 2,000 or more (or controlled goods). Voluntary PEPPOL-based electronic invoicing for B2G is emerging but not federally mandated; no nationwide B2B e-invoicing clearance obligation currently exists.

CRA conducts focused review of export zero-rating claims, particularly for high-value goods and e-commerce retailers. Where export substantiation is absent or deficient, CRA reassesses the outward supply at 5% GST (or applicable HST), claws back any input tax credits claimed on related purchases, and charges interest from the date the original return was due. A false or grossly negligent statement triggers the ETA s. 285.1 civil penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the net tax understatement; non-filing or late remittance invokes s. 280 penalties. Deliberate evasion schemes (falsified bills of lading, staged export transactions, or circular remittance) are indictable under Criminal Code s. 380 (fraud), with the general partner of the LP exposed to director-liability rules, monetary fines, and imprisonment. CRA may also issue a formal demand for records, conduct third-party audits of the LP's couriers and payment processors, and apply the sham/GAAR doctrines to claw back refunds for up to four years (or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada affords full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There is no exchange control legislation, no mandatory approval for ordinary cross-border trade or investment, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, partnership distributions, or invested capital in non-regulated sectors. The Investment Canada Act may require review where a non-Canadian acquires control of a Canadian business. The Canada Limited Partnership (CA_LP) is pass-through eligible; non-resident limited partners are generally permitted to hold interests. Distributions of Canadian-source income to non-resident partners may be subject to Canadian withholding or Part XIII tax and treaty relief rules under the Income Tax Act. Cross-border e-commerce receipts in Canadian dollars are freely convertible to USD or other currencies through Canadian chartered banks without Bank of Canada approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeframe, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds. Exporters and e-commerce operators may retain foreign currency in Canadian-dollar or foreign-currency bank accounts. For tax reporting, receipt of foreign currency must be converted to Canadian dollars at the Bank of Canada or a prescribed exchange rate as of the transaction date, and all supporting sales, customs, and settlement records must be retained for Canada Revenue Agency verification. No invoice matching or BOE surrender declaration exists under federal law.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) – central banking and monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) – prudential regulation and supervision of banking institutions; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) – AML/CFT intelligence and compliance monitoring. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for payment-processing functions; Low for bank-like deposit-taking functions. A foreign or offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) may provide payment services in Canada only after registration as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, compliance with the Retail Payment Activities Act and supervision by the Bank of Canada. A foreign EMI cannot obtain a Canadian bank licence under the Bank Act, cannot accept deposits in the course of a banking business, and cannot directly access Payments Canada clearing and settlement systems. It must work through a sponsoring Canadian Schedule I bank or credit union that acts as the settlement gateway. Stripe and PayPal, the target payment rails, are commonly used as PIISP or payment facilitators and must be integrated with a Canadian settlement account to avoid unlicensed deposit-taking and unreported processing of Canadian card transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 if originating outside Canada. Although Canada does not prescribe a statutory purpose code, the remittance instruction should include an explicit narrative field stating “CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO LIMITED PARTNERSHIP” and should reference the CA_LP legal entity and account number. The beneficiary Canadian bank will credit funds through Lynx, Canada's high-value payment system, generally on the same value date. To satisfy KYC and anti-money-laundering requirements, the bank will require the LP agreement, identification of the remitting partner, source of funds documentation, and a capital account entry in the partnership ledger. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium for payment-processing functions; Low for bank-like deposit-taking functions. A foreign or offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) may provide payment services in Canada only after registration as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, where applicable, compliance with the Retail Payment Activities Act and supervision by the Bank of Canada. A foreign EMI cannot obtain a Canadian bank licence under the Bank Act, cannot accept deposits in the course of a banking business, and cannot directly access Payments Canada clearing and settlement systems. It must work through a sponsoring Canadian Schedule I bank or credit union that acts as the settlement gateway. Stripe and PayPal, the target payment rails, are commonly used as PIISP or payment facilitators and must be integrated with a Canadian settlement account to avoid unlicensed deposit-taking and unreported processing of Canadian card transactions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate for taxable corporations; the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level Part I tax. Partners are taxed on allocated income at their applicable rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Not applicable to LP distributions; distributions of profits to partners are not 'dividends' for Part XIII purposes. Partners are taxed under Part I on their allocated share. If the LP holds shares of a taxable Canadian corporation, dividends paid by that underlying corporation to a non-resident partner-attributed investor bear Part XIII WHT at 25% absent treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act is required where the aggregate fair market value of reportable transactions with non-resident non-arm's-length persons exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed by the filing due date. For MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more, Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory (CRA Form in XML schema). Canada has no statutory Local File/Master File filing, but CRA expects OECD Local/Master File documentation to support the tax return position where the CAD 1,000,000 threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain sectoral Economic Substance Regulations akin to UAE/KSA or other common-law ESR regimes. Instead, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 ITA) which disallows tax benefits from transactions lacking bona fide business, investment, or family purpose. The Canada-OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) treaty abuse rules (principal purpose test) additionally deny treaty benefits where one of the principal purposes is to obtain treaty relief. A passive Canadian LP that merely holds e-commerce assets and whose non-resident partners are resident in a favourable treaty jurisdiction is exposed to GAAR, anti-avoidance rules for limited partnership losses, and Part XVI.1 'anti-fragmentation' rules if structured to undermine the CAD 1,000,000 reporting threshold.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Knowingly Making False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under ITA s.163(2) of 50% of the tax avoided where false statements or omissions are made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence. Criminal tax evasion under ITA s.239(1) is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and a fine not exceeding 200% of the tax evaded. Where the Crown proceeds by indictment, the maximum term is 5 years; on summary conviction, 2 years less a day.

Director personal liability: The director, general partner, or senior officer who directs, participates in, or acquiesces to the evasion is personally liable. The corporate veil is pierced under the act of the principal (attribution doctrine); the taxpayer may be assessed as an individual, and the director's assets are subject to levy under the Tax Collection Agreement. In egregious cases the court may also issue a travel ban pending criminal prosecution.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s.245 disallows the tax benefit, reassessing the LP and partner allocations to reflect economic substance. The MLI Principal Purpose Test may deny treaty relief on distributions and interest. Penalties under s.247(3) for failing to make reasonable TP adjustments are equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment plus interest, together with full correction of tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and limited partners who participate in or cause the artificial arrangement may be jointly and severally liable to pay the GAAR re-assessment plus interest under joint liability provisions of the applicable corporate tax law. They may separately face charges of counselling false statements (Criminal Code s.465) and money laundering if the arrangement is used to conceal beneficial ownership.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act carry administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for a first contravention and up to CAD 1,000,000 for repeat contraventions, plus a Treasury Board-imposed compliance order. Criminal laundering under the Criminal Code (s.462.31) is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years for the individual and confiscation of the corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Corporate officers who intentionally or recklessly fail to implement a compliance program are directly liable as accomplices; the corporate veil may be pierced in a regulatory action. Senior officers who are aware of and refuse to remedy the contravention can be personally assessed for the AML penalty and may be disqualified from acting as directors for a specified period.
Unfiled or Late T5013 / T2 Returns and Unremitted Withholdings

Statutory sanction: T5013 late file penalty: CAD 25/day per partner to a maximum of CAD 2,500. Failure to file a T2: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month for 12 months and 10% on repeat failure. Failure to remit GST/HST: penalty 5% of amount, increasing monthly to 12%; special penalty for unremitted source deductions is 10% of the amount. CRA may also demand a compliance payment plan.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 and ETA s.323 for all unremitted source deductions and net GST/HST, regardless of whether the corporation or LP has been assessed. This liability is absolute for directors of a corporate general partner if the deduction was not remitted and a CRA revenue demand was fully executed. Travel bans may be imposed under the Tax Enforcement Act for Canadian residents, and non-resident directors may be barred from re-entry under cross-border reciprocal enforcement agreements.
Cross-Border Transfer Pricing / False Intercompany Arrangements

Statutory sanction: CRA reassesses the Canadian LP to adjust the profit allocation, levying a 10% transfer pricing penalty under s.247(3) where the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 3 million or specified percentage of gross revenue (10% of the understated profit). Interest and late-filing penalties apply from the debt date. Form T106 must be filed; repeated failures trigger per-return penalties of CAD 2,500.

Director personal liability: For a corporate general partner, the director can be held personally liable for the transfer pricing penalty if the corporation fails to remit the assessed amount and the court finds gross negligence. Where the controller orchestrated the artificial pricing, they are also exposed to criminal prosecution for tax evasion and may be monetarily penalized under s.162(6.3), with potential imprisonment up to 2 years for a summary conviction.
