Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); GST/HST registrations and filings are administered through the CRA's My Business Account portal and GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership must first obtain a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN), which serves as the primary federal tax identifier. The partnership applies for a BN via the CRA online ‘Register for a business number’ service, Form RC1 Request for a Business Number, or through most provincial corporate registries. The partnership then files Form GST1 (Application for GST/HST Registration) either online, by mail, or by telephone to obtain its GST/HST registration number. Non-resident partners without a presence in Canada may be required to provide a non-resident tax identification number from their home jurisdiction and, if the partnership has Canadian-source income, a Canadian non-resident tax account may be required for withholding obligations. A limited partnership that is a registrant must also complete the CRA’s ‘Partner Information Return’ annually, providing the BN and tax identification information of each partner.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian federal income tax does not provide a full ‘check-the-box’ election comparable to the U.S. regime. However, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: income, losses, deductions, and credits are allocated contractually or by statute to partners, and the partnership itself is not subject to federal income tax. For GST/HST purposes, a partnership is generally treated as a separate person, but partnerships may elect under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to treat certain transactions between the partnership and a partner as taking place at fair market value. Hybrid mismatch rules in s. 18.4, 95 and the ‘anti-hybrid’ provisions of the Income Tax Regulations may deny deductions or require re-characterization where a partnership or partner is subject to hybrid mismatch treatment with another jurisdiction; affected partnerships should complete Form T5013 Schedule 13 and make any required disclosure under s. 247 of the Income Tax Act. Where a foreign entity participating in the LP would be fiscally transparent in Canada but opaque in its home jurisdiction, the Canadian partnership remains a partnership for Canadian tax purposes unless re-characterized under an applicable treaty; no statutory domestic check-the-box election is available at the federal level.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. The Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act impose no exchange controls, no surrender obligations, no prior authorization for profit distribution, partnership capital repatriation, or liquidation remittances. The compliance hurdles affecting cross-border flows are exclusively tax and reporting related: Part XIII withholding tax on Canadian-source amounts paid to non-resident limited partners, Part XII.1 tax on excessive distributions of certain specified entities, FINTRAC international electronic funds transfer reporting for every EFT of CAD 10,000 or more, and CBSA cross-border currency reporting for physical cash or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more. Capital mobilization by a foreign-controlled Canadian limited partnership is otherwise unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules exist in Canada. Air cargo charter brokerage revenues earned in USD or any foreign currency may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts, used directly to settle charter, fuel, or handling liabilities, or converted to CAD without Bank of Canada approval. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no repatriation timeline. For a federally scoped air cargo charter broker, USD commission receipts must nevertheless be traceable to the underlying charter contract in the SWIFT narrative so that the deposit pattern is coherent to FINTRAC scanning and to the bank's enhanced due diligence unit; contemporaneous booking, invoice, and charter agreement records must be retained for the statutory 5-year audit window.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Canada's central bank and sole issuing authority for the Canadian dollar; exchange control policy is formally rooted in the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and administered by the Minister of Finance, while prudential supervision of banks is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/CFT supervision by FINTRAC. The Canadian dollar floats freely; no Canadian authority issues FX transaction approvals for trade or capital payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium where a foreign digital EMI is proposed as the primary settlement rail; High only as a supplementary collection or low-value disbursement channel. Under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46), deposit-taking in Canada is reserved to Schedule I/II banks; a foreign EMI cannot open a Bank of Canada settlement account, cannot offer CAD current accounts to a Canadian LP as a primary bank, and must register with FINTRAC as a foreign Money Services Business (MSB) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and obtain payment service provider registration with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) before routing flows into Canada. Such EMIs provide no CDIC deposit insurance (CDIC cap of CAD 100,000 applicable only at chartered banks), no commercial credit, and no direct LVTS or Fedwire access, making them structurally inadequate as the treasury backbone for charter brokerage settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign limited partners wire capital in CAD or USD without Bank of Canada approval. Protocol: (i) execute the partnership closing certificate and reflect the contribution in the LP's capital account statement; (ii) issue a SWIFT MT103 designating the beneficiary Canadian bank transit number and account number, with field 70 remittance information stating 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [LP legal name] - [partnership agreement date] - [contributing limited partner name]'; (iii) USD injections settle via Fedwire through the receiving Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent and may convert to CAD at the bank's commercial spot rate or a pre-arranged forward; (iv) any single international wire of CAD 10,000 or more is reported to FINTRAC and may trigger a source-of-wealth request, so the partnership capital commitment agreement and the originating partner's bank provenance must accompany the transfer documentation; (v) for non-CAD/USD currencies, choose a direct corridor to the CAD account rather than a double EUR-to-USD-to-CAD conversion if a correspondent EUR/CAD route is available. Canada has no mandatory central-bank payment purpose code; the SWIFT field 70 narrative serves as the functional equivalent and must be consistent with the partnership agreement to prevent reclassification of a capital injection as taxable revenue.Outbound settlement to aircraft charter operators, fuel suppliers, and ground handling agents is executed via: (1) SWIFT MT103 for foreign-currency and CAD payments; (2) Fedwire for USD payments to U.S. beneficiaries through the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) issued under UCP600 for high-value charter deposits or operator performance security, together with standby letters of credit (SBLC) under ISP98 where the brokerage must guarantee payment; (4) the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for domestic CAD clearing, with the Real-Time Rail (RTR) used for lower-value domestic payments where available; (5) ACSS cheques only for residual monthly balances. Every MT103 must carry a remittance narrative that matches the underlying commercial invoice, charter agreement, and airway bill, because a mismatch is a FINTRAC hallmark of trade-based money laundering. The treasury mandate should include a USD disbursement approval cap, automatic correspondent-level OFAC screening, and a prohibition on splitting payments to circumvent thresholds.CAD/USD outright foreign exchange forwards (dated or rolled, executed under a bank-standard FX Master Agreement or an ISDA Master Agreement with credit support annex following OSFI margin rules)FX swaps (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) to bridge the maturity mismatch between USD commission receipts and CAD operating expenditureCross-currency interest rate swaps (CAD/USD) where enterprise debt or charter equipment financing exists and the interest rate exposure requires hedgingCurrency options (CAD puts / USD calls, including collars and zero-cost structures) to protect a concentrated USD charter commission streamNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only for genuine receivables or payables denominated in non-convertible settlement currencies (for example restricted-currency overflight or fuel receivables); CAD itself is fully convertible, there is no Canadian NDF market in CAD, and any NDF must be supported by an underlying commercial contract and an ISDA Master Agreement approved by the Canadian bank's credit and market risk functions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate after the General Rate Reduction (basic federal rate 38% less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction). A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) is subject to a 9% federal small-business rate on active business income up to the CAD 500,000 business limit; a Limited Partnership is not itself taxed as a corporation and its income is allocated to partners at their respective tax rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner. A Limited Partnership's profit allocation is not a dividend; LP distributions to non-resident partners are generally not subject to Part XIII withholding. Instead, non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on their allocated Canadian-source partnership income, subject to treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: the T106 filing threshold applies to total consideration for reportable non-arm's-length transactions with each non-resident counterparty in a fiscal year. CRA contemporaneous documentation requirements apply to all transfer pricing transactions of material size; Master File / Local File-style records must be available on request, and Country-by-Country Reports are mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime comparable to offshore centres; the operative doctrines are ITA s.245 GAAR, s.247 transfer pricing rules, and treaty anti-abuse principles such as beneficial ownership and limitation-of-benefits analysis. A Canadian LP engaged as an air cargo charter broker must demonstrate real employees, general partner control, contracts, invoices, bank accounts, and decision-making in Canada; a shell LP used solely to access treaty benefits will be recharacterized, denied treaty relief, and reassessed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s.239 with fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; CRA may reassess beyond the normal limitation period where misrepresentation is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are personally liable for unremitted source deductions, GST/HST, and other amounts under s.227.1; CRA may obtain civil restitution orders, freeze personal assets, and impose travel bans as bail conditions; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment and extradition proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA can adjust income to an arm's-length amount under s.247(2), deny deductions, impose a transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net upward adjustment, and charge interest on reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who approve or knowingly participate in deficient transfer pricing may be liable for gross-negligence penalties; CRA can assert director liability for unremitted corporate tax liabilities and seek court orders against personal assets.
