Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporations Canada, Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, for federal business registry and CRA linkage; ServiceOntario Companies and Personal Property Branch for the provincial limited partnership declaration.

Governing statute

Canada has no federal limited partnership registry; formation is under the designated provincial Limited Partnerships Act, assumed Ontario: R.S.O. 1990, c. L.7, ss. 2-10. Federal obligations include registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, S.C. 2000, c. 29, and Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1, s. 96 for partnership classification and information reporting.

Formation / constitutional filings

Limited Partnership Declaration (Form 1) under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act; federal registration is completed through the Canada Revenue Agency Business Registration Online system.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Canada.ca Business Registry portal; GST/HST and Payroll accounts via 'My Business Account'

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership, the partnership itself registers for a CRA business number (BN) and GST/HST account by submitting Form RC348 (Request for a Business Number) or online via the CRA's Business Registration Online. The partnership must provide its partnership agreement, identifying all partners and their residency status, and appoint a representative with authorized sign-off. A separate CRA program account (RT for GST/HST) is then opened.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a non-resident limited partnership, the partnership itself registers for a CRA business number (BN) and GST/HST account by submitting Form RC348 (Request for a Business Number) or online via the CRA's Business Registration Online. The partnership must provide its partnership agreement, identifying all partners and their residency status, and appoint a representative with authorized sign-off. A separate CRA program account (RT for GST/HST) is then opened.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act, a limited partnership is ordinarily a pass-through vehicle; no check-the-box election exists. The general partner may elect under section 216 to file a separate Part XIII return for non-resident partners, and partners may elect under subsection 96(3) to calculate income on a fiscal-period basis. No hybrid mismatch elections are available under Canadian domestic law beyond treaty-based tie-breaker rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada, or CAD 30,000 from a 'carrying on business in Canada' determination for non-residents; small supplier threshold is the same for domestic and foreign suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the goods are exported 'by or on behalf of the supplier' and the supplier has documentary evidence of export within the required time frame. For services, an 'exported service' is zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, and the service is not supplied to a Canadian resident or to a person present in Canada for consumption in Canada. For a helpdesk SaaS, zero-rating applies to supplies of 'a service' to non-residents where the service relates to software and the recipient is a non-resident non-registrant.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency - B13A Export Report, where required)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent shipping document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of payment in foreign currency from the non-resident recipient)
  • Commercial Invoice and contracts evidencing the service recipient's non-resident status

No national real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. The CRA requires electronic records (in any format) and XML/CSV if requested for audit. Digital stamping is not applicable. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; invoice content must satisfy CRA's information requirements.

CRA can assess GST/HST plus penalties under section 280 (5% of net tax + 5% per month up to 12 months) and interest; for fraudulent export zero-rating claims, a penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 or 50% of the amount improperly claimed may apply plus criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Retrospective clawbacks may extend four years (or more in cases of neglect or fraud) under subsection 298(1) of the Excise Tax Act.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Federal Part I general corporate rate after 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction); combined federal-provincial effective rate for corporate partners ranges from approximately 23% to 31% depending on the province of permanent establishment

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII statutory rate of 25% on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; treaty-reduced to 15% generally and to 5% under the Canada-US and Canada-UK treaties where the beneficial owner is a company controlling at least 10% of voting shares. Distributions from a CA_LP are not dividends; a non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source business income is taxed under Part I and non-resident corporate partners are additionally subject to Part XIV branch tax of 25% (treaty-reduced, commonly to 15%) on the after-tax amount

Deadline: · Frequency:

If the partnership fiscal period ends December 31, the T5013 return is due by March 31 of the following calendar year; if the fiscal period ends on any other date, the return is due within five months after the end of the fiscal period

Deadline: · Frequency:

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: aggregate fair market value of all transactions with each non-arm's-length non-resident in a taxation year triggers mandatory filing of Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents); contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation satisfying the eight elements of CRA Information Circular 87-2R is expected for all intercompany transactions with no de minimis audit protection; Country-by-Country Report is required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation statute; substance is tested through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), the jurisprudence on sham and factually-created partnerships, the OECD BEPS Action 6 treaty standard, and CRA's business-purpose audit practice. A CA_LP operating a Multi-Channel Omni-Helpdesk and Conversational Bot Cloud with no Canadian premises, no Canadian staff, no Canadian bank account, and no local management, while allocating all economic profit to non-resident partners, may be recharacterized as a conduit or shell. Consequences include denial of treaty benefits, reallocation of profit under s. 247, Part XIV branch tax, a GAAR penalty equal to 50% of any tax benefit, and full interest on reassessed amounts; large digital enterprises deriving Canadian user revenue above CAD 20,000,000 may also face the 3% Digital Services Tax Act exposure on deemed Canadian revenue

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment (2 years on summary conviction); CRA criminal investigation referral to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada; civil reassessment with compound interest and gross-negligence penalties of 50% of understated tax under s. 163(2)

Director personal liability: The general partner is jointly and severally liable for all partnership tax debts; a limited partner who participates in control or management loses limited-liability protection and is treated as a general partner; directors of a corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under ITA s. 227.1 and ETA s. 323; courts may impose travel prohibition orders under ITA s. 226(3) and freeze personal assets pending restitution
Failure to File and False Statements

Statutory sanction: T2 late-filing penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months; T5013 penalty of CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500; s. 162 penalties for missing information slips; s. 163(1) false-statement penalty of 50% of the understated tax; s. 238 summary conviction fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 12 months

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner may be assessed personally for penalties and interest; criminal conviction for falsification of books carries ancillary orders including restitution and travel bans under s. 226(3); the CRA may also pursue the foreign partner's Canadian assets directly
Transfer Pricing Abuse

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247(3): penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; reassessment at the arm's-length amount with interest; concurrent GAAR penalty of 50% of the tax benefit where the arrangement is abusive; loss of treaty relief under the principal purpose test

Director personal liability: Senior officers and the general partner face personal exposure for penalties if they signed or directed the defective documentation; CRA can pierce the partnership structure to assess the partners directly; travel bans and asset injunctions may be ordered where the Crown demonstrates an intention to leave Canada without payment
Shell Company and Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: Provincial limited partnership acts convert a controlling limited partner into a general partner with unlimited personal liability; CRA may disregard the partnership as a sham and assess the partners personally on all income; GAAR s. 245 reassessment with a 50% penalty; the Canada Business Corporations Act and provincial securities regulators may impose additional disqualification orders

Director personal liability: Full piercing of the corporate veil: civil restitution of all unpaid taxes and interest, personal liability of the general partner and controlling limited partners, travel bans under ITA s. 226(3), and imprisonment for wilful participation in a sham arrangement designed to evade Canadian tax
GST/HST and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA s. 280 penalty up to 25% of unreported amounts plus 6% interest; retroactive GST/HST assessment; AML administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for entities and CAD 500,000 for individuals; referral for criminal prosecution for GST evasion under s. 327 of the ETA with fines of 50%-200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: The general partner and any controlling limited partner are personally liable for unremitted GST/HST under ETA s. 323; directors of a corporate general partner face the same joint and several liability; the CRA can obtain travel prohibitions under ITA s. 226(3) and enforce judgment against personal assets of the directing mind
