Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Provincial corporate registry (e.g., Ontario Companies and Personal Property Branch or Service Alberta Corporate Registry) issues the Certificate of Limited Partnership; the federal interface is the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Business Registration Online system for the federal Business Number; a corporate general partner is registered with Corporations Canada, Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED).

Governing statute

The Limited Partnership is created under the applicable provincial limited-partnership statute — e.g., Limited Partnerships Act, RSO 1990, c. L.8, s. 3 (Ontario) or Partnership Act, RSA 2000, c. P-3, Pt. 4 (Alberta). The federal component is governed by the Excise Tax Act, RSC 1985, c. E-15 and the Income Tax Act, RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.) for the CRA Business Number and tax-account enrolment; a corporate general partner may be incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act, RSC 1985, c. C-44, ss. 5–6.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (provincial prescribed form) filed with the provincial registrar; Articles of Incorporation under the CBCA where the general partner is a corporation; federal Business Number registration via Form RC101 (BRN).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow-account requirement applies to a Canadian limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed by the partnership agreement and may be in cash or in kind. The federal Business Number registration is free of charge and imposes no capitalization threshold.

O.1, or the Alberta OHS Code) covering emergency plans, first-aid, and workstation ergonomics for the telework-based email-API platform.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — registration and filing portal: My Business Account (cra.gc.ca/mybusinessaccount); Business Registration Online (BRO) at bro.cra.gc.ca.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity first obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) by using Business Registration Online or filing RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). For a non-resident limited partnership, the BN is issued to the partnership as an entity; the general partner or a nominated representative submits the partnership agreement and identification documents. Where GST/HST registration is required or voluntarily elected, a separate RT program account is activated under the same BN. Provincial registry registration (e.g., Alberta Corporate Registry, Ontario Business Registry) may be prerequisite for maintaining the LP, but the CRA BN is the sole federal tax identifier. Non-residents may also contact CRA's International Tax Services Office or use Form NR7 for non-resident tax account matters.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — for federal income tax purposes the LP is tax-transparent; income/loss flows to partners under Income Tax Act s. 96. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — for federal income tax purposes the LP is tax-transparent; income/loss flows to partners under Income Tax Act s. 96. For GST/HST, the partnership itself is the registrant and is treated as a distinct person.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election in Canada. Relevant elections include: (i) partnership rollover election under Income Tax Act s. 97(2) on contribution of eligible property; (ii) deemed disposition and reacquisition election under s. 97(4); (iii) GST/HST election under Excise Tax Act s. 273 to authorize a general partner or representative to act for the partnership; and (iv) voluntary GST/HST registration election for a non-resident LP not yet exceeding the small-supplier threshold. Hybrid mismatch rules may recharacterize an LP that is treated as a tax-transparent partnership in Canada but as an entity elsewhere, but no proactive 'hybrid election' is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold). A non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada and exceeding C$30,000 must register and collect GST/HST; non-registered non-resident recipients of Canadian digital supplies may also create registration obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if shipped by the supplier and supported by proof of export. Services, including email infrastructure SaaS, are generally zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient that is not registered under Part IX and the place of supply is outside Canada; the supplier must demonstrate that the service is not performed in Canada and does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property. A declaration of non-residency, IP address evidence, and the IT service contract are typical substantive evidence for digital export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CBSA electronic export report (CEIRD)
  • Commercial Invoice / Sales Contract
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/SWIFT settlement proof

No mandatory continuous transaction control, real-time clearance, digital stamping, or CRA invoice-authorization protocol. Registrants must issue prescribed tax invoices and retain them for six years. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies above C$1,000,000 (GST/HST NETFILE), but invoice-level API clearance is not required. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Initiative is voluntary and primarily procurement-driven.

CRA may reassess the export supply, deny zero-rating, and assess uncollected GST/HST with arrears interest and penalties under Excise Tax Act ss. 280, 285, and 296. A willful false statement attracts an additional 50% penalty under Income Tax Act s. 163(2) for income tax misstatements, and s. 239 exposure applies to fraudulent evasion. Retroactive normal assessments may cover a four-year access period, extended to indefinite in the case of misrepresentation or fraud. Export documentation must be retained for at least six years from the end of the relevant tax year; failure to substantiate can result in full clawback of claimed zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized and freely floating currency regime with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. Foreign-exchange transactions require no central-bank approval, license, or declaration, and CAD/USD dealing is open-market through any Schedule I bank. There are zero legal obstacles to receiving USD subscription revenues, maintaining USD clearing balances, or converting to CAD at will; the CA_LP may even elect USD as its functional currency under ITA s. 261. The only genuine remittance hurdle is tax-structural, not FX: allocations of Canadian-source business income to non-resident limited partners are deemed payments under ITA s. 212(13.1), triggering Part XIII withholding (default 25%, treaty-reduced; commonly 0% under the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty for business profits or software royalties when no Canadian PE is attributable to the partner) and reported on CRA forms NR4 and NR601. Non-resident partners may elect s. 216 Part I net-basis taxation, with excess Part XIII withholding refunded. No capital-repatriation permit, blocked-funds regime, or currency-matching requirement applies to the CA_LP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender obligation, statutory timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. The CA_LP may retain USD indefinitely in a foreign-currency clearing account, convert opportunistically, and invoice non-resident newsletter-software subscribers free of exchange control. GST/HST is zero-rated on exported software services under Excise Tax Act s. 23, leaving the revenue stream federally tax-unencumbered. Concurrent obligations are ministerial, not restrictive: FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reports for cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, FATCA/CRS classification of the LP's financial accounts, and Statistics-Canada BOP purpose coding on wire entry to the Canadian banking system.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and retail-payments supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); prudential regulation of Schedule I/II/III banks rests with OSFI; AML/ATF reporting is administered by FINTRAC; high-value and retail clearing systems (Lynx, ACSS/RBS) are operated by Payments Canada; Canada has no exchange-control authority because the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW-TO-MEDIUM as a primary treasury rail; MEDIUM-HIGH as a settlement overlay. Canada does not operate an EU-style EMI licensing regime. Foreign digital banks and EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer, Brex) cannot hold deposits in Canada unless they operate through (a) a sponsored-deposit arrangement with a regulated Schedule I bank, or (b) a Schedule III authorized foreign-bank branch under the Bank Act. Since November 13, 2024, the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires any payment service provider performing payment functions for persons in Canada to be registered with the Bank of Canada; a foreign EMI offering multi-currency accounts and FX conversion to the CA_LP will likely fall within that registration orbit, and non-registration endangers settlement continuity. Recommended architecture: maintain legal operating and capital-escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank (OSFI-prudential, CDIC-eligible deposit insurance), while using an RPAA-registered or bank-sponsored EMI (Wise Platform/Airwallex) exclusively for low-balance USD-to-CAD conversion and cross-border supplier payouts. Do not hold subscription float on uninsured foreign EMI balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Subscription revenue: USD collections via Stripe Billing are settled from Stripe's settlement bank to the CA_LP's Schedule I USD account through SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 CategoryPurpose 'TECH' or 'LICF' (information-technology services / license fees), remittance fields carrying the merchant descriptor and invoice IDs for straight-through processing; CAD-denominated settlement flows via Stripe Payments Canada Ltd. through local ACSS/CAD rails. Partner capital injections: non-resident limited partners wire via SWIFT MT103/MT202 with CategoryPurpose 'CFDI' (capital financial investment) or 'CAPI', referencing the certificate of limited partnership and the specific capital-call notice; the GP completes source-of-funds declarations and the funds post to the partnership capital ledger. Verification protocol (3-point control): (i) sanctions screening of originator and beneficiary against the Canadian Consolidated Sanctions List, OFAC SDN list, and UN/EU lists; (ii) UBO re-verification of the wiring partner, including identity and source-of-funds for contributions above CAD 10,000; (iii) transaction-size comparison against the agreed capital schedule, with automatic hold or rejection on divergence and STR referral if structuring indicators appear. All inbound EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are FINTRAC-reportable and archived with the bank's SWIFT reference and UETR.Supplier and infrastructure disbursements (AWS, Mailgun, SendGrid, colocation) execute via SWIFT MT103 with Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR) and CategoryPurpose 'SUPP' or 'TECH', instructing the beneficiary bank at the G4 level for ISO 20022 transparency. Commercial letters of credit apply only if the platform expands into physical colocation or channel-partner distribution: a confirmed irrevocable LC is issued through the Schedule I trade desk under UCP 600 with FX-forward price-fixing, and documentary presentations (commercial invoice, transport document, inspection certificate) are examined against the LC terms with SWIFT MT700/MT799 messaging. Distributions to non-resident limited partners execute via MT103 with CategoryPurpose 'DIVI' or 'REDE' (capital redemption), with Part XIII withholding remitted through CRA (NR4/NR601) and treaty paperwork retained. Domestic CAD clearing uses Lynx (the high-value payment system, successor to LVTS) for wire-level transfers and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or Retail Batch Settlement for payroll, GST/HST, and partner advances. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW-TO-MEDIUM as a primary treasury rail; MEDIUM-HIGH as a settlement overlay. Canada does not operate an EU-style EMI licensing regime. Foreign digital banks and EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer, Brex) cannot hold deposits in Canada unless they operate through (a) a sponsored-deposit arrangement with a regulated Schedule I bank, or (b) a Schedule III authorized foreign-bank branch under the Bank Act. Since November 13, 2024, the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires any payment service provider performing payment functions for persons in Canada to be registered with the Bank of Canada; a foreign EMI offering multi-currency accounts and FX conversion to the CA_LP will likely fall within that registration orbit, and non-registration endangers settlement continuity. Recommended architecture: maintain legal operating and capital-escrow accounts at a Schedule I bank (OSFI-prudential, CDIC-eligible deposit insurance), while using an RPAA-registered or bank-sponsored EMI (Wise Platform/Airwallex) exclusively for low-balance USD-to-CAD conversion and cross-border supplier payouts. Do not hold subscription float on uninsured foreign EMI balances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No specific monetary threshold; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation required for material related party transactions

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime; compliance addressed through transfer pricing rules, General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR), and Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) provisions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file tax return

Statutory sanction: Penalty of $50 per day (max $1,000) for federal; provincial penalties vary; plus interest on taxes

Director personal liability: Partners may be personally liable for penalties and interest if partnership cannot pay; no director liability in LPs but general partners have unlimited liability
Underreporting of income

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 50% of the unreported amount plus 5% monthly interest; possible criminal prosecution for evasion

Director personal liability: General partners face personal liability for taxes, penalties, and interest; limited partners protected but may lose limited status
Non-compliance with transfer pricing rules

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income, penalties of 10-50% of the transfer pricing adjustment, interest

Director personal liability: General partners personally liable for any unpaid tax arising from TP adjustments
Lack of economic substance or tax avoidance

Statutory sanction: Application of GAAR to deny deductions or recharacterize transactions; penalties up to 50% of tax benefit; possible criminal charges

Director personal liability: General partners exposed to personal liability for taxes, fines, and imprisonment for willful evasion
