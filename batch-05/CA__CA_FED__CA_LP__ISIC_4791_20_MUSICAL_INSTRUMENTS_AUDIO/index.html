Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); CBSA portal for export reporting.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership registers with CRA using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' via Business Registration Online, by mail, or by telephone. There is no minimum capital requirement. The LP receives a single federal Business Number (BN) with program accounts such as the GST/HST 'RT' account, payroll, and import/export accounts. The general partner completes registration on behalf of the LP; non-resident partners may be involved and must provide foreign addresses and Canadian contact/agent details where required. The BN is the statutory TIN-equivalent for indirect tax filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, a limited partnership computes income/loss at the partnership level but is not a taxpayer; each partner includes its share in computing income. Canada taxes residents on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income, subject to treaty relief. The LP files T5013 Partnership Information Return. — A foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership registers with CRA using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' via Business Registration Online, by mail, or by telephone. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, a limited partnership computes income/loss at the partnership level but is not a taxpayer; each partner includes its share in computing income. Canada taxes residents on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income, subject to treaty relief. The LP files T5013 Partnership Information Return.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal law. The LP is a statutory pass-through entity. Relevant elections include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(6)-(7) with CRA approval; (ii) voluntary GST/HST registration election for small suppliers under ETA s. 240(2.1); (iii) GST/HST Quick Method accounting election; and (iv) elections prescribed for jointly-owned corporations and closely related persons where applicable. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 12.9, 18.4 and 96(2.1) apply mechanically and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when total taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter, or during the last four consecutive calendar quarters; persons below that threshold remain 'small suppliers' but may voluntarily register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V for supplies of tangible personal property that are exported by the supplier or at the supplier's direction as part of the supply. For online sales of musical instruments and audio gear, the physical goods must be shipped from Canada to a foreign customer and the exporter must retain evidence of export. Electronically supplied audio software, digital libraries, and offshore services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident, the supply is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property, and the service is not rendered to a person physically present in Canada at the time of performance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA export declaration / Canadian Export Reporting System record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement from Canada to foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment evidence from the non-resident buyer

No mandatory federal continuous-transaction-control (CTC) clearance regime and no digital stamping requirement. Peppol-based e-invoicing is accepted under Canada's Pan-Canadian Trust Framework; adoption is voluntary for most private-sector B2B/B2C transactions. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically in many cases through GST/HST NETFILE or EDI/API; prescribed invoice/record retention requirements apply under ETA ss. 286-286.2.

CRA treats export zero-rating as a high audit risk. If documentary proof is insufficient, CRA will reassess the supply at 5% GST (or applicable HST rate), disallow the related refund or input tax credit, and charge interest and penalties. A false or misleading statement or gross negligence can attract penalties of up to 50% of the net tax understated; deliberate fraud may lead to criminal prosecution under the Excise Tax Act. The normal reassessment window is four years, but the limitation period remains open indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility on both current and capital accounts. The Canadian dollar trades on a freely floating basis with no foreign-exchange controls, no exchange licensing, and no requirement for specific regulatory approval on profit remittance, dividends, interest, or liquidation proceeds. Non-resident investment is screened only under the Investment Canada Act for large acquisitions and national-security review; such screening does not affect FX conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or conversion requirement exists under Canadian law. Exporters may retain, convert, and repatriate all export earnings without any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange control administration. Bank supervisory AML/CFT oversight is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-constrained feasibility for offshore digital EMIs as a primary banking rail. An offshore EMI without Canadian MSB registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act cannot lawfully maintain CAD-denominated payment accounts or originate Canadian wires. Stripe and PayPal may be used as payment facilitators for e-commerce settlement, but they do not replace the required chartered-bank treasury account for capital escrow, FX settlement, and statutory liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections for the Canadian LP are routed through the SWIFT MT103 wire protocol into the beneficiary's CAD operational or escrow account at a Canadian chartered bank. Canada does not prescribe a statutory purpose-code list; the bank relies on the sender's narrative, SWIFT fields 20 and 72, and the beneficiary declaration, which should state 'Capital Contribution to Canadian Limited Partnership' and reference the entity's provincial LP registration number or CBCA incorporation number. Verification is accomplished by the bank's sanctions and FINTRAC screening, plus source-of-funds documentation supplied by the depositor.Outbound trade settlements are cleared via SWIFT MT103 payments to suppliers, and commercial letters of credit confirmed under UCP600 where documentary conformity is required. Domestic CAD settlements use Payments Canada rails: the LVTS for high-value time-critical wires, ACSS for batch payments, and the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for low-value instantaneous settlements.
