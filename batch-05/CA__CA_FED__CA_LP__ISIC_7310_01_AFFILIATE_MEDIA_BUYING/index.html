Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal income tax and GST/HST administration. Registration and filing portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE, and Represent a Client. Customs border and export declaration processing: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian Limited Partnership (LP) with non-resident owners: (1) Confirm the LP requires a 9-digit Business Number (BN); a BN is mandatory to open a GST/HST program account (suffix 'RT') and to file partnership information returns (T5013) when the LP has 6 or more partners or any corporate or partnership partner. (2) Submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online via BRO or by mail, attaching the partnership agreement, list of partners with legal names, addresses, domestic tax identifiers (e.g., US EIN/SIN), and a signed letter of authorization for any Canadian agent or CRA representative. (3) Indicate 'non-resident' status and, for GST/HST, apply under the non-resident registration path; CRA may request proof of Canadian business activity or the partner's foreign registration. (4) Register for GST/HST where taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive quarters; voluntary registration is permitted and is recommended for input tax credit recovery on domestic ad inventory purchases. (5) File T5013 partnership return and issue T5013 slips to partners when required. Typical BN issuance time: 5–15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — For Canadian income tax, the LP is fiscally transparent: income is computed at the partnership level and each partner is taxed on their allocated share of Canadian-source partnership income (Part I or Part XIII, subject to treaty). For GST/HST, the LP is a separate 'person' and registrant; it must charge and collect GST/HST on taxable supplies made in Canada and cannot flow through its indirect tax obligations to partners. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable only on income from carrying on business in Canada and specified Canadian-source property; however, the LP itself remains subject to Canadian registration, filing and collection duties whenever it carries on business in Canada.

Available election forms

Elections available to a CA LP with offshore owners: (i) Functional currency election under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 261, permitting Canadian tax results to be computed in US dollars where the LP's records are kept in USD — highly relevant for USD-dominated affiliate media arbitrage. (ii) Non-calendar fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) (Form T1139), subject to eligibility of all partners. (iii) Non-resident partner undertaking under ITA s. 194, whereby a non-resident partner undertakes to file a Part I return, avoiding the otherwise mandatory 15% withholding on the partner's share of partnership profits. (iv) GST/HST elections, including the closely related election (Excise Tax Act s. 156) and elections for deemed consideration among registrants in joint arrangements. (v) Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box system; hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.6 and 12.8) apply automatically to deny deductions or impose income inclusions in hybrid arrangements, so the LP should maintain a Canadian partnership tax opinion and entity classification certificate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada (excluding zero-rated and financial supplies) in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. For non-residents, only supplies made in Canada count toward the threshold. A non-resident LP providing digital advertising and lead-generation services exclusively to non-residents would be eligible for zero-rating and will generally remain below the registration threshold; voluntary registration is nonetheless available and advisable to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, a supply of a service is zero-rated when made to a non-resident of Canada, provided the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and the non-resident recipient is not registered for GST/HST. Digital advertising media arbitrage, performance marketing, and affiliate lead-generation services delivered to offshore affiliate networks qualify for 0% GST/HST. The LP must document the recipient's non-resident status at the time of supply (e.g., W-8BEN-E, foreign certificate of incorporation, or tax residency certificate). Canada does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline for zero-rated service exports; for this dossier, a 180-day internal benchmark for evidencing foreign payment receipt is applied to sustain commercial substance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) for physical goods, if any incidental export of tangible property occurs
  • Bill of Lading / House Airway Bill (BOL/HAWB) or courier waybill showing Canadian point of departure for goods
  • Commercial invoice referencing the service or goods, value, and zero-rating statutory basis (ETA Sch. VI, Pt. V, s. 7)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank ledger statement (SWIFT, Wise, Mercury ACH/wire) evidencing foreign-source payment flow
  • Executed service/media-buying agreement identifying the non-resident counterparty and delivery terms
  • Non-residency evidence of the recipient (W-8BEN-E, foreign business registry extract, tax ID, or residence certificate)
  • Geo-IP, campaign delivery logs, and performance records substantiating offshore digital service delivery

Canada imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime. Electronic invoices in PDF/EDI/XML are legally valid and auditable. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million (returns must be filed via NETFILE/XML) and to all GST/HST rebate applications above CAD 10,000. During audit, the CRA may require records in electronic or machine-readable format; the LP must retain a complete e-invoice trail and payment remittance evidence.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export beyond the normal 4-year limitation period for non-resident registrants where there is misrepresentation, false statement, or fraud. If zero-rating is denied, the LP is assessed for uncollected GST/HST at 5% (or the applicable HST rate) on the full invoiced value, plus compound daily arrears interest at the prescribed rate. Penalties: ETA s. 285(1) false statements or omissions — CAD 250 plus 5% of the tax; ETA s. 280(1) failure to register and s. 280(2) failure to file — CAD 250 plus 5% of net tax, with an additional 1% per month up to 12 months for late filing; gross negligence penalty under ETA s. 285(1.1) — up to 25% of the tax; willful evasion under ETA s. 282 — 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment. Critically, all partners, including non-resident limited partners (subject to the ETA s. 272.1(3) cap based on amounts received or entitled to be received), are jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST debts, interest, and penalties; clawback enforcement can extend to partner assets if the partnership is insolvent or fails to satisfy an assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely floating exchange rate regime and imposes no general foreign exchange controls. The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current and capital account purposes. Profit remittance, partnership distributions, loan repayments, and capital repatriation by non-resident partners are legally unrestricted, subject to normal tax withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for non-residents and sanctions screening under OFAC/UNSC-related Canadian measures. Inbound direct investment is not subject to currency licensing; only Investment Canada Act notification/review thresholds apply for acquisition of control of a Canadian business, which does not affect treasury mechanics. Financial institutions report cross-border wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC, and FATCA/CRS disclosure obligations apply to account holders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters and foreign-owned Canadian LPs may hold, convert, invest, or remit foreign currency proceeds freely. The only relevant obligations are the Cross-Border Currency Reporting Regulations for physical currency or monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 and bank-level reporting of large electronic fund transfers. This business scope, digital advertising media arbitrage and affiliate lead generation, is treated as an export/import of services and is subject to the same unrestricted FX treatment.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central bank); Department of Finance Canada (foreign exchange policy); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT and cross-border fund reporting; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision of Schedule I banks and authorised FX dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium viability as an operational payment gateway; low viability as the sole treasury or escrow repository. Canadian-regulated FinTech EMIs with FINTRAC MSB registration can support niche cross-border payout flows such as Wise Business and virtual payment cards, but they are not CDIC-insured, not Schedule I lenders, and do not provide secure Canadian clearing settlement. Mercury ACH, Wire Transfer, and Wise are acceptable supplementary rails for affiliate/media payouts and low-value disbursements, provided the primary capital escrow and operational treasury accounts sit with a Canadian regulated bank. Legal constraints include FINTRAC MSB compliance for any entity that performs remittance, currency exchange, or money transfer services; a pure media-buying business is not itself an MSB unless it moves funds for third parties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners are executed via SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire/SEPA to the Canadian LP's CAD or USD bank account. Wire remittance instructions must identify the payer, the Canadian LP, the contribution purpose, and the partnership capital account reference. Canada does not require exchange-control approval or purpose-code permits, but the receiving bank and the sending correspondent bank apply OFAC/sanctions screening and report electronic funds transfers over CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC. The bank may request a copy of the LP agreement, board/partner resolutions, and source-of-funds documentation to classify the credit as capital rather than revenue. FATCA/CRS forms for non-resident partners must be completed at account opening. Investment Canada Act implications arise only for acquisitions of control of Canadian businesses; pure capital funding of an LP media-buying operation generally does not trigger review.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for cross-border media supplier and affiliate payouts, Commercial Letters of Credit for high-value or contractually escrowed purchases, and the Canadian Lynx large-value system for domestic CAD settlements above thresholds. Retail CAD payments use the ACSS/ECHO rails; USD payments clear through CHIPS and Fedwire; low-value international affiliate payments may use Wise Business, Mercury ACH, or virtual accounts supported by the Canadian Schedule I bank. All outbound disbursements must include detailed invoice references, supplier contract identifiers, sanctions screening clearance, and confirmation that the payment is for actual services under the ISIC 7310 media-buying scope. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax rate before provincial/territorial allocation; because the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent, taxable income flows through to partners and is taxed at each partner's applicable federal/provincial rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Because the Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent, a direct distribution of its net income to partners does not ordinarily attract dividend withholding tax; however, when a non-resident partner is held through a Canadian corporation, downstream dividends to the foreign parent are subject to the 25% Part XIII rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties triggers the mandatory contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Local File; the OECD Master File and CBCR threshold is CAD 10,000,000 consolidated gross revenue for MNE groups.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone press-mark-specific Economic Substance Regulation; however, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), the doctrine of sham, and factual ignore/recharacterization principles. An affiliate media-buying LP must maintain real staff, genuine ad delivery, relevant market risk, physical or virtual substance, and consistent economic terms in order to sustain allocations and treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239: summary conviction fine up to CAD 200,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; indictment fine up to CAD 400,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA will also assess gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax and statutory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly authorize or acquiesce in non-disclosure can have the corporate veil pierced; civil restitution of the full undeclared tax, penalties, interest, travel bans, and imprisonment may be ordered under Canadian tax and criminal procedure.
Shell Company / Artificial Transaction lacking Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR under section 245 may deny the tax attributes of the transaction, disallow the related deductions, reassess open tax years, and impose a 50% misrepresentation penalty under subsection 163(2); common-law sham findings can void the entire structure.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate general partner is the directing mind, directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under subsection 227.1; section 160 can attach assets transferred to insiders; veil-piercing, civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment can result from fraudulent artificiality.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA fines of up to CAD 2,000,000 for entities and up to CAD 500,000 for individuals; Criminal Code money-laundering provisions impose up to 5 years imprisonment and mandatory asset forfeiture for proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or knowingly ignore AML deficiencies are personally susceptible to prosecution, civil asset forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition; courts may lift the corporate veil to achieve restitution of laundered funds.
Transfer Pricing Penalty / Contemporaneous Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Section 247 of the Income Tax Act imposes a transfer pricing penalty equal to 10% of the gross amount of the price adjustment if adequate contemporaneous documentation is not maintained; the underlying expense may be wholly denied, and the non-resident partner's treaty position can be prejudiced by the lack of substance.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner can be assessed jointly and severally for unremitted Part XIII taxes and can be named personally in restitution claims where they authorized related-party pricing; travel bans and imprisonment are available for willful participation in conduit pricing evasion.
