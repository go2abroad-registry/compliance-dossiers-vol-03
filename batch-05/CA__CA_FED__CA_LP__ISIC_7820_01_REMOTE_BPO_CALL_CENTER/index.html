Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Canadian LP generally requires a registered or principal office address in the province or territory of registration, with service-of-process capability and maintenance of required partnership and tax records. A commercial virtual office may be accepted for registry purposes if the applicable provincial statute and service provider permit it, but it is inadequate where the business requires operational staff, secure records, customer-contact infrastructure, employment compliance, privacy controls, or financial-institution verification. For a remote BPO call center, the operating model should maintain verifiable Canadian management, contractual records, payroll or contractor records, privacy and cybersecurity controls, and evidence of actual service delivery. A virtual office must not be used to misrepresent Canadian operational substance or evade provincial employment, privacy, telecommunications, or tax obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); primary online portal is Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration and GST/HST NETFILE at canada.ca/taxes.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned limited partnership registers with the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC3486 or via the BRO portal. If the partnership makes taxable supplies in Canada exceeding CAD 30,000 on a worldwide basis over four consecutive calendar quarters, it must register for GST/HST using Form RC1 or the BRO system. The application must identify the general partner and all prescribed partnership information; a non-resident partnership should appoint a Canadian representative for service of process and communications. The BN with the GST/HST account suffix is the statutory TIN for Canadian federal indirect tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax; the partnership itself is not an entity-level taxpayer, and partners are allocated income under the Income Tax Act. Under the Excise Tax Act, the partnership is treated as a separate person for GST/HST registration and collection. Partnerships may elect a non-calendar fiscal period under ITA s. 249.1(5), and related registrants may rely on ETA s. 156 for deemed supplies on inter-entity transfers. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed under ITA s. 18.6 and not via an affirmative election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies made in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters, excluding amounts of exported zero-rated supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 to a supply of a service made to a non-resident person who is not in Canada at any time while the service is performed, and where the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of the supply. Multilingual BPO call-center and tele-sales services rendered exclusively to non-resident clients from Canadian delivery infrastructure generally qualify if contractual evidence and service delivery records demonstrate non-resident receipt and offshore benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A Exit Report) if physical goods are exported; not required for pure services
  • Bill of Lading / International Waybill for physical goods movements; not required for pure services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange transfer evidence matching the exported-supply invoice
  • Fully executed offshore BPO services agreement identifying non-resident recipient and scope
  • Itemized commercial invoice stating the supply is zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V s. 7
  • Call-detail records, network logs, and telecommunications usage records evidencing service delivery outside Canada
  • Evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, including foreign registration documents or tax-residency certificate

No national real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate is in force. CRA requires electronic GST/HST filing (NETFILE or approved XML/API transmission) for most registrants depending on annual revenue and filing frequency. Invoices must contain prescribed information under the ETA, but invoice data is not cleared or approved by CRA prior to submission.

CRA may assess GST/HST, interest, and penalties on any exported BPO supply recharacterized as taxable if the documented evidence is insufficient. Under the Excise Tax Act, a registrant who knowingly makes a false statement in the calculation of net tax is liable to a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the amount sought; false export zero-rating claims trigger additional civil penalties for wilful participation, limitation-period extension, and potential criminal prosecution. Claimed input tax credits and refunds are subject to clawback retroactively, and directors and partners may be jointly liable under statutory trust and due-diligence rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility exists for both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII obligations. The Canadian dollar floats freely against all major currencies, and no exchange-control permission, prior authorization, or repatriation license is required for capital contributions, dividend or profit remittances, or intercompany settlements. Profit extraction from the Canadian Limited Partnership is unrestricted at the foreign-exchange level but remains subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) on distributions characterized as interest, rent, or royalty payments to non-resident partners where the applicable treaty or participation exemption does not reduce the rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio on foreign-currency receipts. Export and cross-border service revenues may be retained indefinitely in CAD or foreign-currency accounts maintained with a Canadian Schedule I bank. Mandatory reporting obligations arise solely via FINTRAC: large cash transactions and international electronic fund transfers (including SWIFT MT103 flows) of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported in accordance with Part 2 of the PCMLTFA. No filing obligation is triggered by holding, converting, or deploying export proceeds in foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign exchange and prudential supervisory oversight exercised jointly through the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Currency Act, Bank Act, and Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for outbound disbursement handling, Low for primary treasury custody. Wise B2B, Airwallex, and Payoneer operate in Canada as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and may supply multi-currency virtual account details and API payment rails well suited for high-volume, low-value BPO agent micro-payouts and vendor disbursements. They are not, however, Schedule I or Schedule II deposit-taking institutions under OSFI; they offer no Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) coverage, no committed credit capacity, and no direct membership in Payments Canada clearing systems. Accordingly, the Canadian LP must maintain the primary liquidity position in a Schedule I bank account and restrict the EMI/FinTech rail to pre-funded sub-wallets with a controlled float and documented reconciliations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires an inbound SWIFT MT103 wire credited to the LP's Schedule I CAD-denominated operational account, with the beneficiary line stating the LP legal name followed by 'Capital Contribution' and the remitter referencing the capital call notice and partner-number coding. Full source-of-funds verification must be appended: remitter bank statements, signed subscription agreement, capital call letter, and partnership registration extract. The Bank of Canada-operated Lynx high-value payment system provides final settlement in CAD; where the inbound wire is denominated in USD, the receiving bank converts at its intraday spot rate and the treasury must retain an FX contract memo for auditability. Any funding arriving through Wise B2B must be swept daily into the Schedule I account to preserve CDIC coverage and clearing finality.Primary clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 standard wires for vendor settlements, payroll, and corporate service fees above CAD 10,000; (2) irrevocable commercial letters of credit (sight or 30-day term) for outsourced technology equipment, telecom bandwidth, and infrastructure purchases; (3) Wise B2B API payments executing SEPA, local-rail, and SWIFT hybrid transfers for BPO agent micro-payouts below CAD 5,000; and (4) Lynx-enabled domestic EFT for Canadian counterparties. Each outbound instruction must embed in MT103 field 70 a harmonized remittance reference combining invoice number, purchase-order number, and the BPO engagement code. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if total value of cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties exceeds CAD 10 million; Master File required if total value exceeds CAD 100 million (for fiscal years beginning after 2021).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions require genuine economic substance. Partnerships must demonstrate real business activity, employees, and decision-making in Canada to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution and travel bans may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties under s.163.2 (misrepresentation) up to 50% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the sham arrangement; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to implement AML compliance programs; travel bans may be issued.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties under s.247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment (if gross negligence), plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they directed or participated in the transfer pricing mispricing; civil liability for restitution.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the amount not withheld, plus interest; criminal prosecution for willful non-remittance.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under s.227.1 of the Income Tax Act; travel bans and asset seizure possible.
