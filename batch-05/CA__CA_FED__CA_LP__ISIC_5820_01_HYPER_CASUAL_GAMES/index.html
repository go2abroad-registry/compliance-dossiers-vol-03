Governing statute

Canada has no federal limited partnership statute. The Limited Partnership is constituted under provincial law (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is imposed by Canadian federal law. The Limited Partnership Agreement determines capital contributions, which may be cash, property, or a promise to contribute, and no statutory escrow account is required. Limited partners retain liability protection only while capital contributions are substantively made and not withdrawn, and provided they do not take part in control of the partnership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Limited partnership is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada); each partner computes share of income/loss. No check-the-box regime exists federally; however, a partnership may elect under section 216(4) for non-resident partner income from property, and hybrid mismatch rules apply under subsection 18.1(1) and Part XII considerations for foreign-owned partnerships; GST/HST election under section 150 of the Excise Tax Act can be made to have closely related entities file combined returns where applicable.

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies (including exempt supplies disregarded for small supplier determination); once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration, unless the non-resident is engaged in a commercial activity and does not meet the small supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under sections 123(1)(f)(iii) and 167(1) of the Excise Tax Act for supplies of services exported to non-residents, including digital advertising monetization services provided to non-resident ad networks/publishers, provided the supply is made to a non-resident and the service is not performed in connection with real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property located in Canada, or a service rendered to persons present in Canada. Hyper-casual game publishing services delivered remotely to non-resident advertising platforms (AdMob, AppLovin) qualify as zero-rated exports if the recipient is a non-resident and is not registered for GST/HST.

Canada does not mandate real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance; GST/HST registrants must issue prescribed information on invoices (section 169 and 221 of the Excise Tax Act). Business-to-business electronic data interchange and EDI/API-based systems are voluntary. No digital stamping or pre-clearance requirement exists at the federal level for invoices; however, all records must be retained in Canada or made available electronically to CRA on request.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply within the normal reassessment period (4 years; 3 years for most corporations) and apply section 33.1 of the Income Tax Act for transfer pricing penalties up to 10% of net transfer pricing adjustment for cross-border service payments. Penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act for knowingly making false statements in a return are the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the net tax evaded. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract penalty under section 327(1) of the Excise Tax Act (CAD 10,000 or 100% of evaded tax) and possible criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act (up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years). Clawback of input tax credits and reinstatement of 5% GST plus applicable provincial HST component may apply to the full consideration of any mischaracterized supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible and Canada maintains International Monetary Fund Article VIII obligations. Both current account and capital account transactions are generally free from exchange control approval mechanisms. Profit remittance and capital repatriation from a Canadian Limited Partnership are unrestricted in form or amount, subject to Canadian tax withholding obligations under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on certain payments made to non-residents, any applicable income tax treaty reductions, and transaction-level FINTRAC reporting. Non-resident investment attracting control of a Canadian business may require notification or a net-benefit review under the Investment Canada Act; hyper-casual game publishing is not regarded as a regulated cultural industry for ICA purposes unless a distinct Canadian heritage or cultural business element is acquired. The practical hurdles are not exchange control but KYC/bank compliance, tax characterization of limited partnership income, and the need for a Canadian registered office and named agent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose export proceeds surrender requirements. There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or designated window within which export receipts must be converted into Canadian dollars. Export receipts can be retained in any permitted foreign currency, offset against payables or hedged privately, and converted at the commercial bank's prevailing rate. Reporting obligations are indirect only: FINTRAC monitors large cash and electronic funds transactions (C$10,000 or more) and suspicious transactions; Statistics Canada may require Balance of Payments reporting for merchandise and digital-services export receipts when Canadian transactors exceed prescribed thresholds. For a Canadian LP distributing hyper-casual games through foreign ad networks, the USD revenue stream can remain in USD accounts without any surrender or conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority and foreign exchange reserves); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federal bank prudential regulation; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/counter-terrorist financing supervision; Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) for Investment Canada Act review. Canada has no central foreign exchange control administration or surrender authorization.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium as primary treasury; Medium as a supplementary collection or vendor-payment rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie) are not authorized Canadian deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act, are not CDIC-insured, and generally cannot provide a Canadian personal or corporate clearing number for LVTS purposes. An EMI that accepts Canadian-dollar funds or executes remittances in Canada must itself be registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC and maintain Canadian AML program obligations; many offshore EMIs either avoid Canadian-domiciled clients or restrict access to Schedule I bank-linked funding. They may nonetheless be used for receipt collection from AdMob/AppLovin via USD virtual accounts, but all treasury balances should be swept daily to a Schedule I bank account through a compliant EFT/wire path. Do not rely on an offshore EMI for regulatory capital, intercompany loan servicing, or tax filings; the Canadian LP must retain a domestic Schedule I bank for statutory title, audit evidence, and treasury credibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Canadian LP, inbound capital injections are executed as SWIFT MT103 wire payments to the bank account of the partnership. Canada imposes no official 'purpose code' list comparable to certain Asian regimes, but the remittance field should use an explicit instruction such as 'Capital contribution to Canadian Limited Partnership CA-LP; not for export proceeds; no FX surrender required.' The bank's compliance team will rely on free-format OCR and call-report fields; use a precise Reference for Account or Beneficiary Information field with the LP legal name and capital call number. Direct routing should be via a correspondent relation: USD wires to Canadian banks are usually routed through each bank's New York/US clearing post (RBC via RBC Bank, TD via TD Bank in the US, etc.), with final settlement to CAD or to an omnibus USD account at the Canadian bank. If the LP is using a dedicated 'Capital Escrow/Contribution Account', the general partner should first sign a written escrow or capital call instruction and then wire same-day to the operational account. FINTRAC large notification is handled by the bank for C$10,000+ EFTs; the LP is nevertheless required to have its own AML records when it acts as a financial intermediary, which it does not. Non-resident direct investment triggers no exchange approval but may require Investment Canada Act notification or review if certain ownership thresholds are crossed; for hyper-casual games, the applicable review threshold as of 2025 for a WTO investor is C$5.0 billion enterprise value, while acquisition of a 'cultural business' still carries a lower net-benefit test, so an independent legal memorandum on ICA applicability should be included in the treasury opening pack.Clearing rails for cross-border trade settlements are dominated by (a) SWIFT MT103 via SWIFT gpi for high-value and medium-value payments to suppliers, contractors, SDK licensors, and overseas studios; (b) ACH or wire-based clearing for low-value recurring digital services; and (c) documentary Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for large fixed-amount purchases of intellectual property/studio assets or master licenses where supplier terms demand payment against presentation of documents. For a hyper-casual game publisher, Commercial LC utilisation is rare and typically limited to one-off licensing acquisition of an entire game catalogue from a foreign entity; in such cases the Canadian bank will issue an irrevocable LC to the foreign studio with CIP/CIF or similar delivery terms and an SWIFT MT103/MT202 final settlement. Recurring disbursements such as ad-network SDK fees, cloud hosting, code-signing services, and freelance artist payments should be cleared via a US-dollar account maintained at the Canadian bank, with Fedwire/CHIPS for same-day USD needed, or via a CAD SWIFT MT103 for counterparties without USD accounts. No cryptocurrency or private stablecoin rails are permissible in the primary treasury; Canadian banks will not clear digital-asset transactions for corporate accounts without enhanced OSFI-defined due diligence and likely exclusion. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium as primary treasury; Medium as a supplementary collection or vendor-payment rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie) are not authorized Canadian deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act, are not CDIC-insured, and generally cannot provide a Canadian personal or corporate clearing number for LVTS purposes. Do not rely on an offshore EMI for regulatory capital, intercompany loan servicing, or tax filings; the Canadian LP must retain a domestic Schedule I bank for statutory title, audit evidence, and treasury credibility.
