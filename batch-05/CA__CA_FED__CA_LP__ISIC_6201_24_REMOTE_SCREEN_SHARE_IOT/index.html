Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – administers federal income tax, GST/HST, and payroll accounts; digital portal is CRA 'My Business Account' / 'GST/HST NETFILE'. Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs and export documentation. The Department of Finance Canada controls tax policy but has no direct taxpayer-facing registration role.

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian limited partnership (CA_LP), provincial limited partnership registration is first required; there is no federal limited partnership incorporation. After registration, the LP must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or Form RC1. CRA issues a 9-digit BN plus a two-letter program account identifier, typically 'RT' for GST/HST. A non-resident entity or foreign partner must appoint a Canadian representative/agent with a Canadian address for service and must provide the provincial limited partnership registration certificate, partnership agreement, and identity documents of the general partner. If the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada and exceeds the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, GST/HST registration is mandatory; if not, the LP may still apply voluntarily to recover input tax credits. For income tax, the partnership itself is not a taxpayer, but it must file a T5013 Partnership Information Return and issue allocations to partners; non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source income via withholding or filing obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope. A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes; income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to partners. It is not subject to entity-level federal income tax. Non-resident partners are taxable on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment or on other Canadian-source amounts. For GST/HST, however, the partnership is a separate 'person' and may be a registrant in its own right.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. The partnership classification is statutory and not elective. A non-resident or small supplier may voluntarily elect to become a GST/HST registrant under the Excise Tax Act; once registered, the registrant generally must maintain registration for at least one year. For hybrid mismatch arrangements, Canada has enacted anti-hybrid rules under the Income Tax Act; there is no election to opt out, but taxpayers can structure interests and may request an advance income tax ruling to confirm treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, excluding zero-rated financial services and certain supplies by non-residents; a person who exceeds the threshold is a GST/HST registrant. For remote software/services supplied to non-residents, the place-of-supply rules determine whether the supply is made in Canada; a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register once the same CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian limited partnership's supply of remote screen-share, remote desktop access, and IoT telemetry software to a non-resident customer is generally zero-rated under GST/HST if the supply is made to a non-resident person and is delivered by wire, telecommunication, or similar electronic means. Section 23 of the Excise Tax Act zero-rates many services supplied to non-residents; section 23.1 zero-rates transfers/supplies of intangible personal property, including software licenses and cloud-based access, when supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and when the intangible is not substantially consumed or delivered in Canada. The supply must not be performed physically in Canada by the supplier's personnel engaged at a Canadian situs, and it must not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. To claim input tax credits on Canadian overhead expenses, the LP should be registered for GST/HST; zero-rated exports are reported on the regular GST/HST return and create refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) and Bill of Lading/airway bill – required only if physical IoT hardware, diagnostic devices, or media are exported; not required for pure electronic software and remote screen-share services.
  • Executed written agreement / statement of work identifying the non-resident customer and describing the remote screen-share, IoT telemetry, and support services as supplied via telecommunications.
  • Non-resident status evidence file: foreign tax/VAT registration number, certificate of corporate status or partnership registry extract, and CRS/OECD self-certification.
  • Delivery and technical-use logs: session IP geolocation, server access records, telemetry endpoint addresses, and screenshots or system records proving delivery and use outside Canada.
  • Inward remittance and payment evidence: bank credit advice, SWIFT MT103, Stripe/Paddle payout reports, and account statements showing the foreign-sourced payments were received in the LP's Canadian bank account.   • Zero-rated GST/HST invoice: commercial invoice showing the supplier's BN/'RT' account and stating 'zero-rated export under Excise Tax Act section 23 / 23.1'.

Canada does not currently impose a general real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based transaction clearance regime for GST/HST. Tax invoices must be kept and contain prescribed information: supplier name, BN/GST/HST registration number, date, tax charged, and total consideration. For federal government procurement, suppliers must submit electronic invoices through the Government of Canada's PEPPOL-based solution, but this is a procurement requirement, not a CRA tax clearance mandate. No digital stamping is required for exports.

CRA audits zero-rating claims by examining the recipient's non-resident status, place of delivery, substantive use of the software, payment flows, and records of actual support sessions. If documentation is deficient or the recipient is found to be in Canada, CRA may retrospectively assess GST/HST at the applicable rate plus interest, deny input tax credits, and recharacterize the income as Canadian-source. A false or grossly negligent statement in a return can trigger a penalty under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act (and comparable Income Tax Act penalties) equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax understated. Knowingly evading GST/HST may lead to criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment. CRA may also extend the normal limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud is established, allowing unlimited retroactive clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalized currency regime under the Currency Act and the Investment Canada Act, with no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account flows. Software license revenue and remote support telemetry fees can be invoiced and received in CAD or foreign currency without restriction. Capital subscriptions (LP unit contributions), intercompany settlements, and eventual repatriation of partnership capital are freely convertible into CAD at floating market rates. Outbound profit remittance attracts only federal Part XIII withholding tax where applicable (25% statutory, reduced by treaty, e.g., Canada-US treaty: 15% on dividends, 0% on arm's length interest), never requiring FX licensing or administrative clearance. The Bank of Canada operates a flexible exchange-rate framework, making convertibility risk negligible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency earned from software exports or cross-border services. The Limited Partnership may lawfully retain USD and other foreign currency indefinitely in resident or non-resident accounts; any conversion to CAD is a treasury policy decision subject only to general tax record-keeping and financial reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT oversight; Canada maintains no separate centralized foreign-exchange-control administrative authority.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection routing operates as follows: (a) domestic CAD partner contributions use Payments Canada Lynx RTGS wire or tabled EFT naming the LP's exact legal name, Canadian registered office, and account number; (b) cross-border injections use SWIFT MT103 with a Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR), the beneficiary bank's SWIFT/BIC, and remittance information stating 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - LP UNITS - ISIC 6201 REMOTE TELEMETRY SOFTWARE' combined with the originating jurisdiction's tax purpose code (e.g., for US-source funds, reason code 'CF' capital transfer under US Treasury reporting rules); (c) every contribution must be accompanied by a partner capital-call resolution, subscription agreement, and FATCA/CRS certification; (d) incoming funds may not be characterized as shareholder loans absent an executed intercompany loan agreement at arm's length; (e) the receiving bank must verify that no undisclosed US person holds an interest in the LP so as to preserve treaty-eligible pass-through treatment and avoid IRS Section 1446(f) complications.Outbound settlement deploys multi-rail clearing: (i) domestic CAD payments via Payments Canada EFT and Lynx RTGS for Canadian vendors, payroll, and partner distributions;
