Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (Mon dossier d'entreprise)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian Limited Partnership obtains a statutory 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. The non-resident LP must designate a Canadian agent for service, identify its general partner, and submit certified evidence of legal existence from its home jurisdiction. The LP then opens a GST/HST program account (BN suffix RT0001) using BRO or Form RC459 (GST/HST Application for Non-Resident); if the salon will import tea or export goods, it must also register for an import/export program account (RM suffix) with the CBSA. The CRA confirms the BN immediately through the 'Confirm a BN' service for online registrations; GST/HST registration may be retroactive to the date commercial activity commenced upon written request.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-owned Canadian Limited Partnership obtains a statutory 9-digit Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada administers no U.S.-style check-the-box system; a Canadian Limited Partnership is mandatorily flow-through for income tax under section 96 of the Income Tax Act and is a distinct 'person' for GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act. Available statutory elections include: (i) subsection 216(1) ITA net-basis election for non-resident partners earning Canadian rental income; (ii) subsection 249.1(2) ITA partnership fiscal-period election; (iii) subsection 24(3) ETA election to file GST/HST monthly on a voluntary basis; (iv) section 156 ETA election among closely related entities to treat supplies as made for nil consideration; and (v) Article IV(7) of the Canada-United States tax treaty allowing members of fiscally transparent entities to claim treaty benefits on a look-through basis. Hybrid mismatch rules in sections 12.6 to 12.72 and 18.4 ITA apply mechanically to disallow deductions or impose deemed inclusions for cross-border hybrid entity and hybrid instrument arrangements; no elective re-characterization is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the LP becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when the consideration for its worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies, excluding exempt supplies) exceeds CAD 30,000 in either the current calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; registration is voluntary below this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (i) packaged loose-leaf tea sold to a non-resident and physically exported, provided the salon meets one of the export tests in Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (sections 15 through 18), including delivery to a common carrier destined outside Canada, shipment outside Canada, or export under customs documentation; and (ii) certain services supplied in Canada to a non-resident where the services relate directly to goods destined for export (Schedule VI, Part V, sections 22 through 24). On-premise brewed tea beverages, lounge admission, and cultural salon services delivered to customers present in Canada — including foreign visitors — remain standard-rated at 5% GST or the applicable HST. The salon must maintain a CRA-compliant documentary evidence package substantiating each export transaction; failure to do so results in reassessment of the supply at the standard rate plus penalties.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration or Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) transaction record for exports exceeding CAD 2,000, or the applicable ACI e-manifest/cargo control document for U.S.-bound low-value shipments
  • Commercial invoice describing the tea products, HS tariff classification, quantities, sale price, and agreed export delivery terms
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or carrier's cargo control document (CCD) evidencing physical departure of the goods from Canada
  • Signed sales contract or purchase order from the non-resident buyer establishing the export sale
  • Bank SWIFT MT103, remittance advice, or foreign exchange transaction record evidencing inbound cross-border consideration
  • Exporter record package contemplated by CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17-8 (Exported Goods), including carrier receipts, customs stamps, or broker attestations

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoice pre-authorization. E-invoicing is permissive; taxpayers may issue paper, PDF, EDI, or XML invoices without government pre-approval. The CRA participates in the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework pilot based on Peppol PINT and CII standards, but participation is voluntary as of 2025. The specialty tea salon must nevertheless issue compliant GST/HST invoices containing the particulars prescribed by subsection 221(2) ETA and Information Sheet GI-005 (supplier name, BN/RT registration number, invoice date, description, consideration, rate, and tax amount) and retain copies for at least six years.

Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; however, CRA verifies zero-rating claims through GST/HST export audits and may reverse the zero-rating and reassess at 5% GST or the applicable HST if documentary proof is deficient, where goods are diverted to domestic consumption, or where goods are returned to Canada without a tax-reversal mechanism. Reassessment carries compound daily interest under section 280.1 ETA, a gross-negligence penalty under section 280 ETA equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax amount, and additional penalties under section 285 ETA for failure to file or provide information (minimum CAD 250). Fraudulent export schemes are prosecuted under section 327.1 ETA and section 239 of the Income Tax Act, exposing the general partner and limited partners to fines ranging from 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years. The standard CRA reassessment period is four years from the filing date, extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada abolished its Foreign Exchange Control Act regime in 1951 and maintains no central-bank approval requirements, capital-account quotas, or profit-remittance exchange hurdles. Non-resident limited partners may freely repatriate distributions and capital, subject only to Canadian Part XIII withholding tax on specified partnership income streams (treaty-reduced relief available under the Canada-US Treaty via applicable CRA waiver forms). Sectoral licensing of the tea salon and lounge (e.g., municipal food-service and possible liquor lounge permits) does not restrict foreign exchange. The only compliance thresholds are FINTRAC large electronic-funds-transfer reporting at CAD 10,000+ and CBSA physical currency declarations of CAD 10,000+.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandated repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export or trade receipts. The LP may legally retain foreign currency proceeds in USD or other bank sub-accounts, although an election under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act is required if the business books are kept in a functional foreign currency rather than Canadian dollars for tax reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa; prudential supervision of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision and EFT reporting by FINTRAC; cross-border physical currency and monetary instruments control administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and the Currency Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime akin to the UAE; however, the Income Tax Act incorporates substantive economic reporting via the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245, the transfer-pricing rules under section 247, and the Canadian force-of-attraction rules. For a limited partnership, CRA requires that the partnership's Canadian permanent establishment carry on genuine commercial activity, maintain a Canadian premises, employ Canadian staff, and record actual business decisions in Canada. The absence of economic substance—e.g., a brass-plate address and no Canadian employees, with all management and IP held offshore—will be treated as a sham, disregarded as a partnership, or reattributed per paragraph 247(2)(b), with the consequence that profits are recharacterized as taxable to the beneficial owner or a deemed dividend subject to withholding tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under section 239 of the Income Tax Act, a person convicted of tax evasion is liable to a fine of not less than 100% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years (summary conviction) or 5 years (indictable offence). The CRA may also apply civil negligence penalties of 50% under subsection 163(2), restore unreported income, and levy interest compounded daily.

Director personal liability: For a limited partnership, the general partner's directors and officers may be personally liable if they participated in the evasion; section 160 of the Income Tax Act permits CRA to recover unpaid amounts from persons who transferred property from the partnership without consideration. Civil restitution via a CRA civil claim can include the full amount of tax, interest, and penalties; travel bans may be imposed under the Department of Justice procedures; imprisonment sentences of up to 5 years for indictable evasion; the corporate veil is pierced under the doctrine of fraudulent transactions and the partnership's separate existence is ignored where the limited partnership is used as a mere façade.
Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: The CRA will disregard the partnership under the sham doctrine and recharacterize income as earned directly by the non-resident principal, subject to withholding tax under Part XIII at 25%; additional penalties under section 163.2 for third-party civil penalties of up to CAD 100,000 for making false statements; the partnership may be de-registered under provincial partnership legislation; in Ontario and other provinces, an extra-provincial partnership licence may be revoked.

Director personal liability:
AML / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for an entity and CAD 500,000 for an individual; failure to register as an MSB is an offence punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years; the underlying property may be seized and restrained under the Criminal Code and provincial civil forfeiture statutes.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the AML breach face personal criminal liability under section 74 of the PCMLTFA; directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid FINTRAC penalties imposed on the entity; senior officers may be subject to a travel ban by court order under the Criminal Code recognizance provisions; imprisonment up to 5 years; personal assets may be attached to satisfy the statutory penalty.
Misuse of Treaty / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The Canada Revenue Agency may deny treaty benefits under the OECD Multilateral Instrument's principal purpose test (PPT) or under a limitation-on-benefits clause in the applicable DTA; the denial triggers application of the 25% Part XIII withholding, reassessment of the partnership, and penalties under subsection 163(2) of 50% for false statements. A serious misrepresentation may also result in criminal prosecution for tax evasion under section 239.

Director personal liability: The general partner's directors are liable for the partnership's withholding obligations under subsection 227(10) if they failed to deduct or remit; the CRA may assess directors personally for the unremitted amounts, including interest and penalties; an order under the Excise Tax Act may impose a travel ban for the director to compel payment; imprisonment for evasion up to 5 years if the arrangement is criminally prosecuted.
Unregistered GST/HST Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Excise Tax Act, the penalty for failure to register is the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of the value of taxable supplies made in the relevant reporting period. If the salon charges GST/HST but fails to remit, a penalty of 6% plus 1% per month of the unremitted amount is imposed (up to 12%); interest at the prescribed rate applies. CRA may also impose a 25% penalty on the amount of any misstatement made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally liable for the unremitted GST/HST amounts under section 323 of the Excise Tax Act, including penalties and interest; the CRA may seek a court order to freeze assets and a travel ban under the taxpayer-relief provisions to secure payment; no imprisonment for civil GST/HST liability alone, but failure to comply with a CRA production order or the use of fraudulent invoices, or the use of counterfeit GST/HST numbers, carries imprisonment up to 5 years.
