Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permissible with conditions. A Canadian limited partnership must maintain a registered office in the province of registration at a physical street address (a postal box alone is insufficient), but the business is not required to retain operational staff or physical asset presence in Canada for ISIC 5229 intermodal container leasing. Professional virtual office and registered agent arrangements are accepted by provincial registries. Nevertheless, Canadian chartered banks and FINTRAC-regulated payment intermediaries may demand a substantive business address (the office of the asset manager or counsel) and documented Canadian substance before opening SWIFT wire and ACH Debit settlement accounts. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO), accessible at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; GST/HST account registration and rulings are administered by the CRA through the same portal.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership applies for a Business Number (BN) and GST/HST account via BRO or CRA Form RC1, completed by the partnership or an authorized tax representative. The BN is the statutory TIN for federal tax purposes; for GST/HST, the CRA assigns a 9-digit BN followed by a two-letter program code (e.g., RT0001) upon approval. The application must include the partnership agreement, registered legal name, Canadian permanent establishment facts, and identification of all general and limited partners. If the LP is required to register, it will be assessed GST/HST on taxable supplies made in Canada, and may also need provincial registrations where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax purposes; income is computed at the partnership level but is allocated to partners and taxed as if earned directly by them, with no entity-level federal Part I tax.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists under the Income Tax Act; limited partnerships are statutorily pass-through. Available elections include: (i) the GST/HST partnership election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act, allowing supplies made by a partner to be treated as supplies of the partnership, and (ii) the section 216 election permitting a non-resident partner to file a separate net rental income return. Hybrid mismatch outcome denial rules under sections 18(4) and 18(4.1) operate automatically; no elective reclassification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (small-supplier threshold), measured by taxable supplies made in Canada during the preceding four calendar quarters. Once exceeded, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory for the limited partnership. Non-resident lessors of tangible personal property located in Canada that make taxable supplies are subject to this nexus rule.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply by way of lease or sale of tangible personal property (including intermodal shipping containers) is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the property is delivered to a carrier or otherwise exported as soon as reasonably possible after the supply and is not used in Canada after the supply. For container leasing, zero-rating can be claimed when the container is physically removed from Canada for use outside Canada, or is supplied to a non-resident recipient who exports it without Canadian use. The container must not become a 'self-supply' or be subject to Canadian consumption in a taxable manner.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / B13B)
  • Original Bill of Lading (ocean or multimodal through bill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange settlement voucher from a Canadian bank or authorized dealer)

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal invoice clearance or digital stamping requirement for GST/HST. Suppliers may issue invoices electronically under the Excise Tax Act prescribed information rules. CRA supports electronic filing through GST/HST NETFILE and offers digital account-management APIs; however, there is no government pre-authorisation of invoices at issuance and no stamping mandate.

CRA may audit export zero-rating claims, review shipping records, container movement logs and foreign exchange receipts, and may retrospectively claw back the zero-rating benefit if the export conditions are not met, resulting in reassessment of federal GST at 5% plus compound daily interest from the original reporting date. Civil penalties apply under sections 285 and 327 of the Excise Tax Act, including 20% to 25% gross-negligence penalties on the net tax and criminal fines up to 250% of the tax sought to be evaded; senior officers may also face imprisonment for wilful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There is no exchange-control legislation, no capital-repatriation licence requirement, and no prior approval for non-resident partners remitting profit, capital, lease fees, or loan repayments from a Canadian limited partnership. The only frictions are Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act on amounts characterized as interest, rent, or royalty (base 25%, generally treaty-reduced), and mandatory FINTRAC reporting of any cross-border transfer exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no duty to remit container-leasing service receipts into a designated bank account. Export and service revenue may be retained in USD or freely convertible currency in any authorized Canadian bank account; the sole administrative duties are timely Balance of Payments reporting to Statistics Canada on request and standard CBSA export documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada operates no dedicated foreign-exchange administration agency; FX and monetary supervision are distributed among the Bank of Canada (central bank and settlement agent), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank regulation, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border currency reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Offshore digital EMI rails (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may lawfully serve as FX-conversion and payment-orchestration supplements for a Canadian LP, but they cannot act as the primary settlement substrate because OSFI does not authorize foreign EMIs as Payments Canada clearing members. Holding an account with a regulated foreign EMI triggers no PCMLTFA breach per se; however, if the LP settles third-party container-lease payments or moves funds on behalf of unaffiliated lessees, it will become a money-services business and must register with FINTRAC and appoint a compliance officer. The compliant architecture is a tier-1 Canadian settlement bank as the holder of USD/CAD liquidity and SWIFT execution, with an EMI restricted to low-value disbursement optimization.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% federal general corporate income tax rate after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction; provincial and territorial corporate allocation rates apply separately to corporate partners with a Canadian permanent establishment, producing a combined all-in headline rate of approximately 26.5% to 31.0%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Canada's statutory non-resident withholding tax rate under Part XIII is 25% for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent, so distributions of operating profits to non-resident partners are generally not treated as dividends; however, a non-resident corporate partner's Canadian business income allocated from the LP is subject to Part I tax plus a 25% Part XIV branch profits tax on after-tax Canadian earnings, subject to treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than five months after the partnership's fiscal period end. The allocated income or loss is then reported by each partner on the partner's own T2 corporate return or T1 personal return.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the corporation's taxation year-end. Balance-due day is generally two months after year-end, or three months after year-end for a CCPC eligible for the small business deduction; quarterly instalments are required when the corporation's tax payable exceeds C$3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate annual fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions for purposes of Form T106; CRA may nonetheless require contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under s.247 for any non-arm's-length transaction regardless of amount. Country-by-country reporting applies to Canadian-parented multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least C$750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore financial centres. Instead, the CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule under s.245, the statutory transfer-pricing rule under s.247 and common-law substance-over-form principles to disregard artificial transactions. A Canadian limited partnership engaged in container leasing must demonstrate substantive business activity in Canada, including local management, employees, decision-making, physical premises and genuine commercial risk; otherwise the LP may be recharacterized as an agency or sham, treaty benefits may be denied under limitation-of-benefits or beneficial-ownership provisions, and a non-resident partner may be deemed to have a Canadian permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Upon indictment under s.239(1) of the Income Tax Act, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; summary conviction penalties may also apply. CRA may reassess beyond the normal limitation period where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect or wilful default, with daily compounding interest and civil penalties under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and controlling partners who authorize or participate in evasion may be personally prosecuted; the corporate veil is not a shield in criminal tax matters. Personal assets may be seized, funds may be frozen, travel may be restricted, and mutual legal assistance requests may support extradition in significant cross-border cases.
