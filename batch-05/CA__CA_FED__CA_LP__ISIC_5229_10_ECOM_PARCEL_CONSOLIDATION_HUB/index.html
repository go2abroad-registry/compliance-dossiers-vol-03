Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Canadian limited partnership; capital is fixed solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement in cash or property, and no escrow or deposit account is required by statute. Limited liability is preserved only to the extent each limited partner actually contributes; non-resident limited partners may subscribe but must maintain an Ontario address for service through the registered agent.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — the federal tax administration for GST/HST and Business Number (BN) registration; primary portals: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO); the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export declaration and customs-side reporting.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on commercial activity in Canada must: (1) confirm whether the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada and whether the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold has been exceeded; (2) obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) as the federal TIN and open an RT (GST/HST) program account, either online via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by mailing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' together with the executed partnership agreement and general-partner particulars; (3) if no Canadian resident representative is in place, consider appointing one and completing the CRA authorization; (4) after registration, file GST/HST returns through GST/HST NETFILE and file the annual T5013 partnership information return; the same BN supports customs, payroll, and other program accounts.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership carrying on commercial activity in Canada must: (1) confirm whether the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada and whether the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold has been exceeded; (2) obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) as the federal TIN and open an RT (GST/HST) program account, either online via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by mailing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' together with the executed partnership agreement and general-partner particulars; (3) if no Canadian resident representative is in place, consider appointing one and completing the CRA authorization; (4) after registration, file GST/HST returns through GST/HST NETFILE and file the annual T5013 partnership information return; the same BN supports customs, payroll, and other program accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; the LP is statutory flow-through for income tax while the Excise Tax Act deems the partnership a separate registrant. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST grouping election under ETA s. 156; (ii) partnership representative-member election for centralized GST/HST compliance; (iii) GST/HST joint-venture election under ETA s. 273; (iv) contractual allocation of partnership income, subject to the at-risk and limited-partner-loss restrictions (ITA s. 96); (v) hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed by mandatory anti-hybrid rules, not by elective reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 per calendar year in taxable supplies made in Canada (small-supplier threshold); mandatory registration applies to non-residents who carry on a commercial activity in Canada, solicit orders in Canada, or make taxable supplies of imported goods/services in Canada even in certain sub-threshold circumstances; voluntary registration is available and prudent for exporters seeking input tax credit recovery. Provincial harmonization may add HST or separate PST/QST registrations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies to: (i) tangible personal property (parcels) supplied for export and shipped or delivered to a destination outside Canada by the registrant or a common carrier; (ii) export-related freight transportation, freight-forwarding, consolidation, customs, and linehaul services supplied in respect of goods exported from Canada; and (iii) services performed in respect of goods located outside Canada, including services to non-resident e-commerce sellers. The 0% rate requires contemporaneous documentary proof of physical export and satisfaction of the place-of-supply rules.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed electronically through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) where a declaration is required
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list (HS commodity codes, declared value, country of origin, shipper and consignee data)
  • Bill of Lading or House Air Waybill issued by the carrier showing Canadian origin and foreign destination
  • Carrier-derived export confirmation — port/airport departure record, customs release data, or destination delivery scan evidencing physical export
  • FX and settlement records — Airwallex or local-clearing remittance documentation evidencing receipt of proceeds from non-resident principals (inward remittance proof)

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax stamping, or continuous transaction controls. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed particulars (BN, date, parties, description, tax charged) and electronic invoices are valid if legible and retained. Export declarations are filed electronically through CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS), with import processing via CBSA Single Window / Integrated Import Declaration (IDE); Canada continues to align public-sector e-invoicing with the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework.

Export zero-rating claims receive heightened CRA scrutiny. Missing or non-contemporaneous documentation, goods diverted back into Canada, or services tied to Canadian property trigger reassessment at the full GST/HST rate plus arrears interest. ETA s. 285 false-statement penalties apply at 25% of the net tax sought to be avoided, increasing to 50% for a repeat contravention within five years, and deliberate evasion may lead to criminal prosecution and joint and several liability of partnership members. CBSA may also issue administrative monetary penalties for export reporting errors. Although Canadian tax law imposes no formal FX repatriation filing requirement, the 180-day commercial settlement benchmark is treated by the CRA as a documentary-control proxy; unexplained non-repatriation materially increases the risk of denied zero-rating and input tax credit recovery.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada imposes no exchange controls on current account transactions or capital account movements. Dividends, management fees, royalties, and sale proceeds may be repatriated freely, subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and any treaty-based reduction. Non-resident partners of a Canadian limited partnership may remit distributable profits abroad without foreign-exchange approval. The only practical hurdles are standard banking compliance, tax withholding documentation, and FINTRAC reporting thresholds for large cross-border wire transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Canada. An exporter or parcel-consolidation hub may retain export earnings in foreign currency, maintain multi-currency accounts, and convert at commercial rates. Exports must be reported for statistical purposes through Statistics Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency export declarations, and payments received must be traceable through banking records for AML compliance, but no central bank conversion or repatriation deadline exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control authority. The financial regulatory and AML/ATF supervisory framework is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability for payment-rail integration, subject to legal constraints. Fintech entities such as Airwallex operate in Canada as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and may execute cross-border payments, FX conversion, and virtual account arrangements. They are not authorized to take deposits as banks and cannot provide Canadian deposit insurance, but they are acceptable for low-risk operational clearing and inbound/outbound wire management. Direct settlement of high-value trade obligations should still be backed by a tier-1 Canadian bank account to mitigate counterparty risk.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Full partnership agreement, certificate of limited partnership, register of partners showing ultimate beneficial owners with individual names, nationality, residential address, date of birth, and ownership percentage; notarized passports; bank reference letters; source-of-wealth statements; explanation of commercial purpose for the parcel-consolidation hub.

Root cause: Significant cross-border flows from multiple e-commerce counterparties without clear trade rationale

Defense evidence: Detailed client and counterparty list, commercial invoices, bills of lading, airway bills, customs declarations, and reconciliation of each inbound wire or clearing transfer to an identified commercial transaction; anti-money-laundering procedures and transaction monitoring policy; periodic third-party audit certificate.
Use of digital EMI or non-bank payment rails for high-frequency settlement

Root cause: Use of digital EMI or non-bank payment rails for high-frequency settlement

Defense evidence: FINTRAC MSB registration certificate for the EMI, proof that the EMI is subject to AML compliance, service-level agreements, daily reconciliation statements to the tier-1 Canadian bank account, and prior written approval from the bank for such payment-rail usage.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign partners or parent entities should be routed by SWIFT wire to the Canadian LP's Operational Account at a tier-1 bank. Each transfer must include a clear purpose code such as 'TM00' (transfers to non-residents for investment), a reference to the limited partnership agreement and investor identification, and supporting underlying documents such as the capital contribution schedule, source-of-funds evidence, and a certificate of limited partnership. The bank will screen against OFAC/US and Canadian sanctions lists, apply travel rule requirements for wire transfers, and may require SWIFT MT103 with full remitter and beneficiary naming in accordance with FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting rules.Clearing rails for outbound trade settlements include SWIFT MT103/MT202 for direct US-dollar and multi-currency wire disbursements, commercial letters of credit (LC) issued via the Canadian bank's trade finance desk for large supplier settlements, and local automated clearing systems (Payments Canada) for domestic CAD obligations. For cross-border small-parcel consolidation activities, the preferred rail is a bank-integrated payment platform or Airwallex virtual account combined with a master operational account at a tier-1 Canadian bank to clear aviation linehaul, last-mile delivery, customs brokerage, and freight-forwarder invoices. All disbursements must be matched to underlying invoices and export/import documentation. Currency conversion should be executed at competitive interbank rates through the bank's FX desk or via permitted fintech platforms.Plain-vanilla foreign-exchange forwards (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) with a Canadian Schedule I bank using the Canadian Foreign Exchange Committee standard master agreementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies without deliverable CAD markets, subject to bank credit approval and ISDA documentationCross-currency swaps (CCS) for CAD against USD or other major currencies, authorized for limited partnerships with documented exposure from parcel-consolidation receivables and payablesFX swaps and rolling spot contracts permitted under the Bank of Canada's market conventions, provided the LP has the required credit facility and board or general-partner authorization

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for services; CAD 5,000,000 for other transactions with non-residents (per CRA guidance)

Economic substance scope: Canada applies general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and treaty shopping provisions; no specific ESR, but substance required to claim treaty benefits; CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial rationale

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded; imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties up to 50% of tax

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate partners may be personally liable for unpaid taxes under s.227.1; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits under GAAR; reassess income and impose 50% gross negligence penalty; interest accrues

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced; potential criminal charges for fraud
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for administrative violations; criminal penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 and 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000; travel bans and extradition possible
