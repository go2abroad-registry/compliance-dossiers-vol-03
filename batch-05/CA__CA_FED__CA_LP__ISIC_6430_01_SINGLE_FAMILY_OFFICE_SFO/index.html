Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Canadian limited partnership; capital commitments are contractually determined in the Limited Partnership Agreement, and no escrow or mandatory deposit account is required at formation. Limited partner liability is limited to contributed capital.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy fire-safety certificate issued under the National Fire Code of Canada for the leased office floor area; structural-load inspection certificate covering any interior alterations, safes and filing installations; workplace health and safety clearance is governed by the Canada Labour Code, Part II (for federally regulated employers) or the applicable provincial OHS statute.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); primary online portals are 'My Business Account' and 'Business Registration Online' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Procedure: (1) Determine whether the LP is carrying on business in Canada or otherwise has Canadian-source tax obligations; partnerships may require a BN even where income is allocated to non-resident partners. (2) Submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' via mail, fax, or the Business Registration Online service, identifying the legal entity type as a limited partnership and designating a representative if the head office or all partners are outside Canada. (3) Select the applicable program accounts: RC for income tax obligations, RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, and RM for import/export activity. (4) The CRA issues the 9-digit Business Number; the GST/HST registration number is the BN followed by the two-letter program code 'RT' and a four-digit reference number. (5) The LP must also file annual T5013 Partnership Information Return, and non-resident partners should separately obtain/confirm their own Canadian tax identifiers and treaty protection.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election; a limited partnership is automatically fiscally transparent for federal tax purposes and no election can reclassify it as an entity taxable at the partnership level. Relevant statutory elections affecting the LP's tax profile include: (i) an election under subsection 96(3) of the Income Tax Act (ITA) to establish a common partnership fiscal period; (ii) an election under subsection 97(2) to transfer property to the partnership on a tax-deferred rollover basis; (iii) allocation elections under section 103 of the ITA where partnership income or loss is allocated otherwise than in accordance with the partnership agreement; and (iv) for non-resident partners, elections to file an income tax return under Part I instead of paying flat Part XIII or section 216 withholding tax where permitted. With respect to hybrid mismatch arrangements, the ITA anti-hybrid rules in section 18.4 and related provisions apply to deny inappropriate deductions; there is no elective opt-out or check-the-box mechanism for a Canadian limited partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in worldwide taxable gross revenues (including zero-rated supplies but excluding exempt supplies such as most financial services) from the business and associated persons over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters and the current quarter. If taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter, registration becomes mandatory effective at the beginning of that quarter. Non-resident suppliers carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold unless they meet the small-supplier exception; voluntary registration is available and is often advisable to claim input tax credits on Canadian operating costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may apply the 0% rate to exported goods if the goods are exported by the supplier or the recipient in a continuous and unbroken manner, or are delivered to a common carrier for export, and the supplier retains documentary evidence of export as required by CRA policy. Services, including investment management and family-office administrative services supplied to a non-resident client who is not registered for GST/HST, may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V if the service is performed for the non-resident and is not in connection with Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or tangible property delivered in Canada, and is not made to a non-resident registrant. Certain financial services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met. The supplier must establish eligibility at the time the supply is made, must not charge GST/HST, and must obtain and retain sufficient documentation to support the zero rating before the due date of the GST/HST return in which the supply is reported.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or electronic export declaration submitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System/CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management portal)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (cargo control document showing the foreign destination and consignee)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign-exchange remittance evidence (e.g. SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice in the exporting entity's name)
  • Commercial Invoice for goods, or signed service contract/work order and delivery records for offshore services
  • Packing list and export permit or licence where goods are controlled or regulated

No federal real-time fiscal clearance mandate, tax authority pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime applies to GST/HST invoices. Registrants may issue electronic invoices where the recipient consents, provided the invoice contains the prescribed information (supplier name or legal trade name, Business Number, date, description, and GST/HST amount) and records are retained in legible electronic or paper form. Canada is moving toward PEPPOL-based interoperable e-invoicing for procurement, but there is no general B2B e-invoicing mandate in force. For imports/exports, border documentation must be transmitted electronically through CBSA systems such as the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal and eManifest/ACI, but this is customs clearance, not an indirect-tax invoice clearance requirement.

CRA audit practice requires the exporter to produce proof of export and foreign remittance within a reasonable period; unsupported zero-rating is reversed on reassessment, resulting in GST/HST at 5% (or the applicable HST provincial component) together with compound arrears interest. The normal reassessment period is four years from the relevant return, but the limitation period is extended indefinitely where a taxpayer has made a misrepresentation or committed fraud. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the net tax understated for false statements made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence, plus additional late-filing penalties where practical. Where fraudulent export documentation is used to obtain a refund or claim a net tax remittance reduction, the CRA may deny the zero rating, claw back any refund paid, impose the above penalties, and pursue criminal prosecution for evasion, which can result in substantial fines and imprisonment. The SFO should therefore maintain a complete export dossier, including customs declarations, transportation records, bank remittance advices, and contemporaneous contracts, for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency regime with full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There are no foreign-exchange controls, central bank approval requirements, or capital repatriation permits for foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is legally unrestricted and may be conducted in CAD or any freely convertible foreign currency; however, such payments are subject to Canadian income tax withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)), reduced by any applicable tax treaty. The Single Family Office LP should therefore design its financing and distribution documentation to satisfy source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership due diligence rather than exchange-control approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts. Canadian exporters and trading entities may hold proceeds in foreign currency indefinitely, maintain multi-currency bank accounts, or convert as commercial judgment dictates. Reporting obligations are limited to customs declarations, FINTRAC currency transaction reports (transactions of CAD $10,000 or more), and general tax disclosure of foreign-source income and foreign bank accounts. The absence of surrender rules makes CAD/USD treasury management optional and driven solely by liquidity requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in conjunction with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Department of Finance Canada, and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). No separate foreign-exchange control authority exists; exchange and capital administration are effected through prudential supervision, AML/ATF legislation, and tax reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore or digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) cannot independently provide Canadian domestic clearing, LVTS settlement, or CAD-denominated regulated accounts. A foreign EMI must partner with a Canadian Schedule I bank or enter into a sponsorship arrangement with a chartered bank to access CAD clearing infrastructure. For the Single Family Office LP, the use of an EMI is feasible only as an ancillary collecting or disbursement vehicle in conjunction with a Tier-1 Canadian bank; reliance solely on an unregulated foreign EMI would fail enhanced due diligence, create Canadian MSB licensing exposure for the EMI, and raise material custody and sanctions-compliance concerns. FINTRAC registration as a Money Services Business (MSB) is required if the EMI itself is operating in Canada.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-transparent limited partnership ownership with a foreign corporate GP and indirect beneficial owners

Defense evidence: Notarized certificate of limited partnership, complete LP agreement, current register of partners and individuals with significant control, organizational chart tracing every 10%+ beneficial owner, certified copies of passport and proof-of-address for each UBO, and a written family-office governance charter.
High-risk industry or source-of-wealth characteristics involving wealthy families and private structured assets

Root cause: High-risk industry or source-of-wealth characteristics involving wealthy families and private structured assets

Defense evidence: Audited financial statements for the past three years, bank reference letters from the source bank, professional wealth-sourced statements prepared by a licensed accountant, evidence of legitimate family wealth origin (sale agreements, inheritance documents, dividend records, tax receipts), and a detailed written source-of-wealth analysis.
Sanctions, PEP, or negative-media flags associated with family members or counterparties

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP, or negative-media flags associated with family members or counterparties

Defense evidence: Sanctions screening results from a recognized screening provider, corroborating evidence of non-involvement, independent legal opinion from a reputable Canadian law firm, PEP status explanation with asset-verification documentation, and a mitigation letter confirming that no Canadian sanctions lists are implicated.
Reliance on offshore counsel, corporate service providers, or trust offices in low-tax jurisdictions

Root cause: Reliance on offshore counsel, corporate service providers, or trust offices in low-tax jurisdictions

Defense evidence: AML/KYC files obtained under professional-client confidentiality, notarized powers of attorney, certified extract from the corporate registry of the offshore jurisdiction, response letters to request-for-information under Canadian banking CDD, and undertaking to provide in-year updates on any change of control.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from the family or non-resident parent entity should be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the remitter's bank to the LP's Tier-1 Canadian account. The payment field must properly identify the Canadian LP as ultimate beneficiary, with beneficiary bank routing via a Canadian institution's CAD clearing mechanism (LVTS for same-day CAD; for USD, clearing via the U.S. Fedwire/CHIPS reciprocal banks and settlement in the Canadian bank's USD correspondent account). The recommended purpose code is 'Direct Investment Equity Contribution' (BPM6 category) using the remitter's purpose code for 'D12 – Equity and investment fund shares' or the Canadian bank's internal code for 'Capital subscription / additional paid-in capital'. Verification must include a board resolution, subscription agreement, executed limited-partnership agreement amendment, and proof of the remitter's own source-of-funds; no third-party payer is permissible.Trade and investment disbursements from the Canadian LP should be cleared through: (i) domestic CAD payments over LVTS or Real-Time Rail/Interac when below LVTS thresholds, using EFT or wire formats; (ii) cross-border USD disbursements via SWIFT MT103 for commercial payments or MT202 COV for bank-to-bank transfers, with a clearly stated invoice and contract number; and (iii) international trade conducted under commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) issued by a Canadian chartered bank subject to ISBP 745 and UCP 600.
