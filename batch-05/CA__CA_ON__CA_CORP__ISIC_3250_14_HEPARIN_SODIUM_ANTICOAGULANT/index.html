Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Ontario Ministry of Finance (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-finance) administers Ontario corporate income tax and Employer Health Tax (EHT) through the CRA under the federal-provincial single tax administration agreement; the 8% Ontario HST component is collected by the CRA as part of the harmonized value-added system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST 'RT' program account via BRO (electronic, immediate) or Form RC1 (paper, 4-6 weeks). Step 2: A foreign entity must appoint an authorized representative (Form RC59 or 'Represent a Client') and provide a Canadian registered office address or an attorney-for-service in Ontario. Step 3: Because the business scope is active pharmaceutical extraction, the entity must also obtain a Health Canada Drug Establishment Licence (DEL) and, for crude heparin importation/exportation, a dealer's licence under the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act (heparin is not a CDSA controlled substance, but the DEL/GMP certification is mandatory for API fractionation), and a CBSA importer/exporter program account. Step 4: Register for Ontario corporate tax (CRA administers the Ontario T2 corporate return) and, if payroll exceeds CAD 1,000,000, the Ontario EHT account. Step 5: Obtain a GST/HST refund window; zero-rated export account requires no separate 'export licence' beyond CBSA export reporting. Registration is mandatory once taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a quarter; the BN is used as the single tax identifier across federal and Ontario tax programs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | Territorial_Scope — A federal or Ontario Business Corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation subject to Part I federal corporate tax on worldwide income plus Ontario corporate income tax (allocated by permanent establishment); it cannot elect pass-through treatment (no check-the-box). Non-resident shareholders face Part XIII withholding on dividends; a non-resident tenant company operating through the Ontario branch would pay Part I tax plus Part XIV branch tax (25%, treaty-reduced). Ontario has no tax-exempt export zone or free-port regime; the heparin fractionation plant is fully within the ordinary territorial tax net.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. Relevant elections and regimes: (i) ITA s. 216/216.1 elections for non-residents to file on net Canadian investment income; (ii) ITA s. 85 rollover to incorporate a Canadian branch or asset transfer (requires a valid election with CRA); (iii) ETA s. 156 election — closely related corporations (e.g., foreign parent and Ontario operating subsidiary) may elect to treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration, zero-rating intra-group heparin transfers; (iv) GST/HST 'Qualifying Non-Resident' registration election allows filing on a simplified net-tax basis; (v) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2/18.21 operate by automatic disallowance of deductions for hybrid mismatches and are not elective; (vi) CRA's administrative 'section 217' election for non-resident pension trusts is not applicable to an active pharma exporter.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (ETA s. 148 small-supplier threshold; a person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; registration is per legal entity; non-residents carrying on business in Canada are subject to the same threshold, with a deeming rule for substantial presence in Canada; the threshold does not apply to the HST portion — once registered, the Ontario 8% HST applies to taxable supplies made in Ontario)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of heparin sodium low-molecular-weight crude qualify for 0% GST/HST as supplies of goods exported by the supplier under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 19, provided the goods are physically removed from Canada in the course of the supply and are not subsequently returned; services performed on the exported goods (e.g., QC analysis, fractionation validation) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 20-21; most services supplied to a non-resident (offshore customer) are zero-rated if the non-resident is not a GST/HST registrant and the service does not relate to Canadian real estate or goods situated in Canada at the time of performance. The exporter must hold contemporaneous documentary proof of export before claiming the zero rate; failure to do so converts the supply to 13% taxable and triggers reassessment. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST purposes; the 180-day benchmark is the documentary best-practice window for collecting and evidencing SWIFT/LC proceeds.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (G13) generated through the Canada Export Reporting System (CESR) with unique transaction reference
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure from Canadian territory
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list (HS 3001.90 heparin and its salts; country of origin Canada)
  • Inward remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103 or letter-of-credit settlement statement) mapping payment to the specific export invoice
  • Health Canada Drug Establishment Licence and certificate of GMP/MQP compliance for the fractionation facility
  • CBSA importer/exporter program account number and controlled-goods classification records

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping exists in Canada. The CRA does not pre-approve or clear B2B invoices. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing standard (Peppol BIS 3.0) is voluntary (adopted by the federal government as of 2023-2024 for public procurement). Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE) for registrants. Cross-border exporters must transmit export documentation to the CBSA via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CESR) / Export Reporting (G13) using EDI or the CBSA portal; no fiscal stamp is applied, but the export declaration reference must be preserved and linked to the invoice in the CRA audit trail.

CRA's International, Large Business, and Audit & Trade directorates run targeted export zero-rating audits comparing CESR declarations, bills of lading, HS classifications, and FX remittance records. If documentary proof of export is missing or inconsistent, the CRA reassesses the supply at 13% HST (or at the applicable provincial rate), denies associated input tax credits, and charges arrears interest on the deficiency. Statutory penalties: (i) ETA s. 280(1) — false statement/omission penalty of CAD 250 plus 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; (ii) ETA s. 327 — tax evasion fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; (iii) the limitation period for reassessment extends beyond the standard 4 years where misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect is established (ETA s. 298); (iv) a fraudulent export claim can trigger cancellation of the GST/HST account, suspension of CBSA exporter privileges, and referral to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada; (v) for heparin as a pharmaceutical precursor, the CRA coordinates with Health Canada, and non-compliant exporters may face DEL suspension and import/export permit revocation, freezing the entire zero-rating documentation chain.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for CAD and foreign currencies. Non-resident profit remittance does not require central-bank approval; however, dividends and branch remittances attract Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)), and filings must be current with the Canada Revenue Agency before transferring funds offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or forced repatriation holding period for export proceeds. All export earnings may be retained in CAD or foreign-currency deposits. Only standard FINTRAC reporting on transactions above CAD 10,000 and CBSA trade statistical thresholds apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-control supervision rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for chartered banks, while the Canada Border Services Agency and Department of Finance Canada administer trade-related FX reporting under the Currency Act and the Customs Tariff.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) are feasible only as ancillary cross-border collection or FX conversion channels. They are not viable as the primary operating bank for a licensed pharmaceutical extraction entity because they are not Canadian Schedule I banks, do not participate in CDIC deposit insurance, and cannot issue commercial letters of credit. Feasibility rating: Low for treasury-rail primacy; Medium for niche payment collections.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected once the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceeds C$1,000,000 in a taxation year, and Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with a Non-Resident Person) is due at that threshold; Country-by-Country reporting (Form RC4649) is triggered when the global group's consolidated annual revenue reaches C$750,000,000; a Local File-style functional and cost analysis, together with a Master File narrative where applicable, must be prepared to CRA Transfer Pricing Memoranda standards

Economic substance scope: Canada enforces substance through the general anti-avoidance rule (s. 245), the beneficial-ownership analysis, the place-of-effective-management test, and the doctrine of sham. The Canadian heparin extraction entity must maintain Canadian employees, leased premises, purchase and quality-control functions, and complete corporate records. A bare-boat contract manufacturer, invoice-only hub, or warehouse base will be recharacterized, and treaty relief denied, consistent with CRA interpretive positions applied in Prévost Car, Fécamp, and JinkoSolar

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: On conviction under s. 239(1) of the Income Tax Act, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, imprisonment for up to two years, or both; the CRA will also reassess the tax with compound interest and may apply the 50% gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2)

Director personal liability: Directors who directed, authorized, assented to, acquiesced in, or participated in the evasion are criminally liable and may be imprisoned; civil liability for unremitted withholding taxes arises under s. 227.1; passport suspension and travel bans can be ordered under s. 226.1 for unremitted arrears exceeding C$25,000
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s. 245 disallowing the tax benefit; treaty relief denied for lack of beneficial ownership; Part XIII assessments on deemed dividend or royalty payments; gross-negligence penalties and compound interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed for non-remitted Part XIII amounts under s. 227.1; directors who approved nominee or shamming arrangements risk criminal fraud liability under s. 380 of the Criminal Code and disqualification under the Canada Business Corporations Act
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: s. 247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the taxpayer failed to make reasonable efforts to determine arm's-length consideration; reassessment with interest; the normal three-year limitation period is extended indefinitely under s. 152(4)(b) for misrepresentation

Director personal liability: Where the transfer-pricing adjustment arises from wilful falsification of books or a deliberate profit-shifting scheme, directors can be assessed under s. 227.1, pursued under criminal fraud provisions, or referred by the CRA to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada
Anti-Money-Laundering / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties may reach C$1,000,000 per non-compliance occurrence plus compliance orders; deliberate money laundering is punishable by imprisonment for up to ten years under s. 462.31 of the Criminal Code

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally named in FINTRAC penalty proceedings, fined individually, and criminally prosecuted for wilful non-compliance; asset freezes and restraint orders may be imposed on personal assets, and travel bans are available in proceeds-of-crime litigation
