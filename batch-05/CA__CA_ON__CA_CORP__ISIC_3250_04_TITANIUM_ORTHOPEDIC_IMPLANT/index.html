Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box pass-through election for corporations; entities are taxed at the corporate level under the Income Tax Act. Available statutory elections include: (i) ETA s. 156 election for closely-related corporations to treat inter-company supplies as nil consideration for GST/HST; (ii) ETA s. 227 quick-method remittance election for small suppliers (annual taxable supplies of CAD 400,000 or less, excluding exports); (iii) ITA s. 93(1) election to treat a dividend from the foreign affiliate of a resident corporation as taxable dividend for surplus repatriation restructuring; (iv) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident to compute Part I tax on net rental income; and (v) hybrid mismatch offset elections under ITA ss. 18.4(2)-(4) to align the Canadian tax treatment of a payment with the counterparty jurisdiction's treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 240(1)); voluntary registration permitted for non-residents and exporters, including a non-resident with no Canadian permanent establishment that makes taxable supplies in Canada

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (0% GST/HST) where the supplier can establish the goods are supplied to a recipient outside Canada and physical export occurs as part of the sale. For offshore services (e.g., electron beam 3D melting prototyping services) zero-rating applies under ETA s. 7(a) of Part V where the service is performed for a non-resident and not in relation to property in Canada. Orthopedic hip joint prosthetics manufactured in Ontario regularly qualify for export zero-rating provided title passes offshore and the exporter retains contemporaneous export evidence.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A filed in the Canadian Export Reporting System) for goods valued over CAD 2,000; also triggered for controlled/prohibited goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Fully executed Commercial Invoice / Sales Contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank wire/indexed remittance evidence of export proceeds)
  • Overseas customs entry, port/airport proof of delivery, or carrier certificate of outbound shipment

No continuous transaction control (CTC) real-time fiscal clearance or compulsory digital stamping is currently implemented. The CRA requires periodic electronic filing and remittance for GST/HST registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1,500,000, with filings submitted via GST/HST NETFILE/REMITTANCE. The Budget 2024 announced a voluntary pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard; participation is optional for private business and mandatory only for federal procurement of participating vendors. No API-based clearance or pre-invoice validation is required at the time of issuance.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST within four years of an assessment, and without a time limit where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Improperly claimed export zero-rating results in reversal of input tax credits and refunds, assessment of the missing 13% HST, interest under ETA s. 280, and a penalty of 25% of the net tax attributable to knowingly false statements under ETA s. 285(1); gross negligence can invoke a 50% of net tax penalty under ETA s. 285.1. Fraudulent documentary schemes are also prosecutable as an indictable offence under ETA s. 327. Note: Canadian law imposes no statutory 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is retained as a documentary best practice window. Unusually extended foreign receivables and non-repatriation of proceeds nonetheless trigger enhanced audit scrutiny and rebut the export conditionally.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on the current account and on the capital account. Canada has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement and does not require exchange-control permits, central-bank approval, pre-settlement approvals, or repatriation licenses for cross-border economic transactions. Profit remittance from CA_CORP is structurally available through lawful dividends, return-of-capital, shareholder loans, or intercompany current-account repayments. The only financial hurdle is source-based Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (generally 25% domestic rate, usually reduced by an applicable income tax treaty); FX conversion into the shareholder's base currency is freely obtainable from any Canadian Schedule I bank. Medical-device export sales may be denominated in CAD, USD, EUR, or any freely accepted currency without triggering exchange-control review. Payment flows remain subject to ordinary financial reporting, CRS/FATCA information exchange, sanctions screening, FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting, and banking KYC, but these are compliance/AML controls rather than convertibility restrictions. Capital-account controls do not exist for a non-resident holding shares of CA_CORP, although an Investment Canada Act notification may be required for establishment of a new Canadian business or for a foreign investor acquiring control of a Canadian entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory central-bank conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline in Canadian law. A Canadian exporter such as CA_CORP may credit export proceeds to its domestic CAD account or retain them in a foreign-currency USD/ EUR account held at a Canadian bank. Export declarations to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) are for customs, trade statistics, and export-control enumeration, and are not treated as evidence of FX conversion. The exporter retains unrestricted legal use of the funds. Tax and transfer-pricing rules require arm's-length pricing on intercompany export transactions and reporting under Form T1134 and related provisions, but the physical disposition and conversion of the foreign-currency proceeds are commercially free. FINTRAC requires the reporting of an inbound or outbound electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more in a single transaction, so cross-border trade settlement wires will be monitored and reported by the bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), together with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential bank regulator and FINTRAC as the anti-money laundering regulator. Canada has no separate State Administration of Foreign Exchange or equivalent 'SAFE' authority; the Bank of Canada operates the Lynx high-value real-time settlement system and provides system-wide oversight, while monetary and exchange-rate policy is conducted under a floating Canadian-dollar regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low as a primary settlement/escrow foundation; Medium as a treasury overlay for supplier payouts. An offshore digital payment or electronic-money institution (EMI) is not authorized to hold insured Canadian deposits or to access Payments Canada clearing as a bank member. An EMI that carries on payment services into Canada must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, satisfy Canadian beneficial-ownership reporting, and may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and Bank of Canada retail-payment supervision. A passport-licensed EMI cannot replace a regulated deposit account for capital escrow under a Canadian corporate structure because it lacks CDIC deposit insurance, does not provide Lynx/SWIFT settlement-level access, and typically operates through an uninsured pool of funds. The prudent architecture is to use a Schedule I Canadian bank for the CA_CORP account, corporate treasury, letters of credit, and FX, and to use a FINTRAC-registered EMI only for low-value supplier disbursements or procurement cards where the bank's friction is commercially unacceptable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

CA_CORP may receive equity subscription or shareholder-loan proceeds from the foreign parent through a standard SWIFT MT103 wire. No Canadian capital-control approval or FX permit is required. The remitter must direct payment to the exact beneficiary name and account number at the chosen Canadian Schedule I bank, and the bank will rely on the KYC/Source-of-Funds file rather than a Chinese-style purpose code. The wire should include a descriptive Remittance Information field, for example 'Capital contribution to CA_CORP pursuant to directors resolution dated ...' or 'Shareholder loan under credit agreement dated ...'. The bank will verify that the beneficiary account is a corporate account in good standing and will report the transfer to FINTRAC as an electronic funds transfer if CAD 10,000 or more is crossed. If the funds arrive in USD, they should be credited to a USD sub-account wherever available; if CAD liquidity is required, a teller spot conversion is executed at the bank's prevailing rate without conversion restriction. For a share subscription by a foreign investor, an Investment Canada Act notification may be required for a newly established Canadian business, but no exchange-control permit is part of the routing. All inbound liquidity should be supported by a board-approved capitalization plan, bank-source-of-wealth forms, a shareholders capital narrative, and evidence that funds originate from a legitimate corporate account and have passed the bank's subsidiary KYC review.The core clearing rails for outbound trade disbursements are: (1) Payments Canada Lynx for large-value Canadian-dollar domestic settlement; (2) SWIFT, preferably SWIFT GPI, for cross-border USD/EUR and other convertible-currency wires to supplier banks; (3) MT103 messages for direct documentary remittances against commercial invoices; and (4) commercial Letters of Credit (sight, usance, and Confirmed Irrevocable LC) where the foreign medical-device or raw-material supplier requires bank payment undertaking. CA_CORP should establish CAD and USD operating accounts at the same Schedule I bank to minimize internal replacement cost and correspondent-fee drag. For titanium-powder, machining, finishing, sterilization, or logistics payments to U.S. and EU counterparties, the bank will initiate a SWIFT MT103 with structured field 70 remittance data, field 50K ordering party, field 59 beneficiary, and a beneficiary bank identifier, ensuring the payment references the invoice and purchase order. FINTRAC reporting is automatic for EFTs above CAD 10,000. For Letters of Credit, CA_CORP should negotiate a trade-finance facility with an availability period, confirmatory bank capabilities in the supplier's jurisdiction, electronic presentation (eUCP/ICC URBPO where accepted), and compliance with UCP 600. Outbound payments remain fully unrestricted under Canadian FX law; the only operational constraints are sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering confirmation, and the bank's ordinary fraud controls. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Low as a primary settlement/escrow foundation; Medium as a treasury overlay for supplier payouts. An offshore digital payment or electronic-money institution (EMI) is not authorized to hold insured Canadian deposits or to access Payments Canada clearing as a bank member. An EMI that carries on payment services into Canada must be registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, satisfy Canadian beneficial-ownership reporting, and may be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and Bank of Canada retail-payment supervision. A passport-licensed EMI cannot replace a regulated deposit account for capital escrow under a Canadian corporate structure because it lacks CDIC deposit insurance, does not provide Lynx/SWIFT settlement-level access, and typically operates through an uninsured pool of funds. The prudent architecture is to use a Schedule I Canadian bank for the CA_CORP account, corporate treasury, letters of credit, and FX, and to use a FINTRAC-registered EMI only for low-value supplier disbursements or procurement cards where the bank's friction is commercially unacceptable.
