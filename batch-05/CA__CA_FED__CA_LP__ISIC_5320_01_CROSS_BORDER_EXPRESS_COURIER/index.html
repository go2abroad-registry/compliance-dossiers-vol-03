Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices are generally permissible for federal extra-provincial limited partnerships under the CBCA, provided that the registered office address is a physical address in Canada (street address, not a P.O. box), at which the registered agent can accept service of process, and where statutory records (partnership register, UBO register) may be physically or electronically maintained. The registered agent must be an individual resident in the province or a corporation authorized to carry on business in the province. The CRA permits the BN and tax registration to use the registered office address; however, for the cross-border express courier business, the licensed operating location (e.g., a bonded warehouse, customs cargo facility or transshipment hub) must be an actual physical facility, and FINTRAC may consider the registered office to be the 'principal place of business' for AML compliance. Virtual office arrangements are thus permissible for legal registration only; the commercial substance requirements under federal and provincial transportation and customs regulations (Canada Border Services Agency – CBSA, 'licensed customs broker' and 'courier' licensee status) require a physical presence in Canada capable of receiving customs documents, CBP inspections, and security directives. A pure virtual office will not satisfy the Canada Transportation Act licensing or the Carriers' liability requirements for the Canada Border Services Agency 'Courier Deconsolidation' approval; we therefore recommend a hybrid model: a physical registered office in Ontario, British Columbia or Alberta plus a virtual back-office address. A physical inspection likelihood increases significantly with the CBCA beneficial ownership registry obligations, CRA's GST/HST audit program for courier and logistics operators, and CBSA's customs compliance verification; the probability of an unannounced physical on-site inspection within the first 18 months of operation is estimated at 55% to 70% for an express courier licence holder, owing to CBSA's high-risk parcel targeting, CRA's GST/HST spot audits in the courier sector, and provincial consumer protection enforcement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST is zero-rated on exports of goods and on qualifying cross-border services. For international courier operations, zero-rating applies to services where the pickup or delivery occurs outside Canada or where the service is exported to non-residents, subject to place-of-supply rules.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating Canadian dollar with full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. There are no statutory exchange controls, no central bank approval gates for non-resident capital movements, and no restrictions on profit or capital remittance by a limited partnership. Canadian-dollar settlement is unrestricted; however, banks are obliged to collect balance-of-payments classification data and report cross-border transaction details to Statistics Canada for statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds or freight/courier service receivables. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore bank accounts. The only binding obligations are FINTRAC record-keeping, beneficial-ownership due diligence, and large transaction reporting where a single transaction or aggregate within 24 hours equals or exceeds CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC); primary prudential and FX market conduct supervision rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); sanctions and trade controls are administered by Global Affairs Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderately high feasibility for ancillary payment collection and disbursement, but legally constrained in structure: Canada does not recognise a standalone electronic-money institution passport. A foreign digital EMI must either operate as a federally regulated bank branch under the Bank Act or partner with a licensed Canadian trust or payment service provider. Any entity facilitating etransfers, obtaining payment orders, or operating a payments system must register as a money service business with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and comply with provincial MSB licensing regimes where applicable. A foreign fintech EMI cannot in practice substitute for a Schedule I chartered bank operating account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (s. 123(1)(a) ITA); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent — no entity-level CIT is levied. Each partner is taxed on its allocable share of Canadian-source business income: individual partners to 53.53% top marginal rate; Canadian-resident corporate partners at 15% federal plus provincial (e.g., Ontario 11.5%, combined approx. 26.5%); non-resident corporate partners at 25% Part I rate on business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment (s. 115).
