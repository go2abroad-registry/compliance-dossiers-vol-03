Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) at www.canada.ca/taxes-business-registration; 'My Business Account' for ongoing filings; GST/HST NETFILE for electronic return submission. CRA tax services office for the registrant's province handles account administration.

E-government filing portal

The Canadian LP is a distinct 'person' for GST/HST purposes (ETA s.123(1)) and requires its own CRA Business Number (BN). Step 1 - Determine obligation: registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter (ETA s.148); for the digital magazine portal, registration should be obtained before charging GST/HST to Canadian subscribers so that input tax credits are claimable on launch costs. Step 2 - Apply through BRO using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts), electing the GST/HST 'RT' account; non-resident partners do not require a separate Canadian tax ID unless they earn separate Canadian-source income. Step 3 - CRA issues a 9-digit BN plus program-account suffix (e.g., 123456789RT0001) normally within 5-10 business days; the registration effective date is the earliest day the LP ceased to be a small supplier or, if voluntary, the requested date. Step 4 - CRA assigns monthly, quarterly (default), or annual GST/HST filing frequency based on revenue; mandatory electronic filing applies to all GST/HST returns. Step 5 - File the annual partnership information return (T5013) and related slips for the LP even though the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax; each partner reports its allocable share under ITA s.96.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - For income tax, a Canadian LP is fiscally transparent: income, gains, and losses flow to partners under ITA s.96, and the LP itself pays no Part I tax. Territorial scope: Canadian-source business income of the LP is taxable in Canada in the hands of both resident and non-resident partners; the non-resident partner must file a Canadian return on its share and is not subject to Part XIII withholding on business income, although fee-for-service payments may attract Part XIII 25% withholding unless relieved by treaty or elections under s.216.1. For GST/HST, the LP is nonetheless a separate registrable person. Tax_Exempt_Zone: None - Canada has no federal free-zone income tax exemptions relevant to digital publishing.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; classification follows legal form. Available elections: (i) ITA s.97(2) - tax-deferred contribution of capital property to the partnership; (ii) ITA s.98(3) - tax-deferred partnership wind-up; (iii) ITA s.96(3) - election respecting partnership year-end and partner fiscal period alignment; (iv) ITA s.216.1 - non-resident person's election to file a net-basis return for Canadian-source service income, eliminating gross Part XIII withholding; (v) ETA Quick Method election under s.228 - simplified GST/HST calculation for small suppliers (rarely optimal above the small-supplier threshold); (vi) ETA s.156 - inter-company election, generally unavailable to partnerships. Hybrid mismatch: Canada's 2018 rules (ITA ss.18(1)(r.1)-(r.2)) deny interest/royalty deductions in cross-border hybrid mismatch arrangements involving entity classification differences; the Canada-US Treaty Article IV(6) resolves partnership classification conflicts for US resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold, ETA s.148(1)), measured on worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter; charities and public institutions face a CAD 50,000 threshold. Since digital-economy rules took effect July 1, 2021, non-resident suppliers of specified digital supplies are subject to the same CAD 30,000 nexus threshold. Place of supply for the digital magazine subscription (intangible personal property) follows the recipient's ordinary residence in Canada (ETA Sched. IX Part 2), so sales to Canadian subscribers attract tax at the rate of the recipient's province; sales to foreign subscribers are outside the GST/HST net or zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Two grounds apply. (1) Non-application: a supply of intangible personal property (digital magazine licence) to a non-resident recipient is deemed made outside Canada (ETA Sched. IX Part 2), so no GST/HST is exigible and the supply is reported at 0%. (2) Statutory zero-rating: ETA Sched. VI s.7 - services supplied to a non-resident (subject to consumption-in-Canada exceptions); Sched. VI s.10 - supply of IPP to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada. Digital advertising: direct advertising services sold to a non-resident advertiser that do not primarily target Canadian audiences qualify under s.7; ad inventory directed at Canadian viewers attracts GST/HST even when invoiced to a non-resident intermediary such as an ad exchange. Digital editions of magazines do not qualify for the printed-publications zero-rating in Sched. VI Part II. The LP must be a GST/HST registrant to claim zero-rating and ITC recovery; non-registered small suppliers cannot recover ITCs on export inputs.

  • Bill of Lading - not applicable to intangible exports; substitute CDN/server delivery logs, IP geolocation evidence, and download/streaming access records proving foreign consumption.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - no statutory Canadian equivalent; substitute SWIFT MT103 credits, bank statements, Stripe payout reports, or AdSense remittance advices evidencing foreign-source funds.

None - Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) integration. E-invoicing is voluntary; CRA participates in the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 'Canada E-Invoicing Pilot' with British Columbia, Nova Scotia, and Quebec for business-to-government invoices. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns via NETFILE or CRA web-service APIs for tax preparers; invoices themselves may be paper, PDF, or electronic. Substantive invoice requirements: supplier BN/RT account number, transaction date, recipient identification, description, consideration, and tax charged (ETA s.223(3) / s.169(4)); records must be retained for six years (ETA s.286).

CRA audit focus is on artificial foreign invoicing structures and diversion of Canadian digital revenue. Consequences: (i) ETA s.280(1) materially false statements penalty - CAD 250 to CAD 25,000; (ii) ETA s.285 gross negligence penalty - 25% of net tax evaded or over-claimed; (iii) reassessment extended from the normal 4-year window to 7 years for negligence/misrepresentation and unlimited for fraud (ETA s.298); (iv) ITA s.163(2) false-statement penalty - 50% of tax understated for income tax filings; (v) criminal prosecution under ETA s.327.2 with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; (vi) daily compounding prescribed-rate interest. Clawback mechanics: CRA reverses the 0% classification, assesses 5%/HST on the full historical gross subscription/advertising revenues, denies associated ITCs, and stacks the above penalties. The taxpayer bears the burden of proving the non-resident status and foreign place of consumption. Note: the 180-day figure reflects the schema's treasury benchmark; Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for export receipts - revenues are recognized on an accrual basis under ITA s.9, and delayed repatriation does not defer Canadian tax, so the 180-day target serves as an internal fiscal discipline metric, not a CRA compliance deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; there are no exchange-control licensing regimes, no notification or approval requirements for inward foreign direct investment into a Limited Partnership, and no restriction on the repatriation of partnership profits or capital distributions to non-resident partners. The sole structural hurdle is Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), which requires non-resident partners to be allocated Canadian-source income only after satisfaction of withholding obligations (or PDE/waiver certification under s. 216 for discretionary allocations), administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) rather than by a foreign-exchange regulator. Digital cross-border subscription, advertising, and sponsored-media revenues are classified as current-account services income and are not subject to any exchange conversion requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds or foreign-currency receipts. Digital magazine publishing revenues earned in USD and settled via Stripe or AdSense may be held in foreign-currency corporate bank accounts at a Canadian chartered bank without any requirement to convert into CAD, subject only to reporting thresholds under the PCMLTFA (funds received in a single transaction or series of related transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC as a Large Cash Transaction Report or EFT Report, as applicable) and to the LP's partnership agreement regarding currency allocation methodology.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acting as the monetary authority, with foreign-exchange policy administered jointly with the Department of Finance Canada under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) and the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52); currency control coordination is executed through the Single Reporting Window of the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium under a two-tier settlement architecture. Canada does not license 'Electronic Money Institutions' as a distinct category; domestic fintechs (e.g., Wealthsimple, Koho, Nuvei, and payment processors) operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or as payments institutions holding exemptions under the Retail Payment Activities Act, 2021 (RPAA). Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may provide ancillary collection and FX conversion capacity but cannot serve as the primary depository for the LP because they are not Schedule I Canadian banks, are not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC), and may expose non-resident partners to unregulated payment-chain custody risk. Legal constraint: offshore EMIs must either obtain a Canadian MSB registration with FINTRAC (requiring a Canadian authorized agent and AML compliance officer) or operate in Canada solely through a Canadian chartered bank partner; the LP should therefore use a Tier-1 bank as the master sub-account holder and route Stripe/AdSense payouts through the bank's virtual-account or collection-aggregation product.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: All inbound capital contributions from non-resident partners must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the partner's home bank to the LP's CAD/USD operational account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, using the following field discipline: Field 50K (Ordering Customer - contributing partner), Field 59 (Beneficiary - LP legal name with CRA Business Number and province registration number), Field 70 (Remittance Information - mandatory reference 'CAPCON' plus the partnership registration number and a narrative identical to the capital-contribution schedule), and Field 72 (Sender-to-Receiver Information - bank-to-bank code, if applicable). Inward remittance purpose codes under the Bank of Canada's reported International Transfers: use code '851' (capital contribution to partnership) or '680' (foreign direct investment). The bank will verify the wire against the KYC file, apply a 24-hour hold for the first deposit, and issue an MT900 confirmation. Distribution out to non-resident partners within 90 days of contribution may trigger the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) or recharacterization as a loan; legal counsel should issue an opinion letter confirming capital permanence. For USD-denominated injections from the US, routing should use Fedwire or CHIPS via the bank's US intermediary (e.g., RBC Bank in Atlanta or TD Bank US), and the LP must not accept third-party same-day ACH credits from unverified digital-advertising partners because such credits trigger the bank's 'push payment' fraud protocol.Principal clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 for all non-Canadian trade and service disbursements to magazine contributors, licensing partners, and foreign editorial freelancers; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium under a two-tier settlement architecture. Canada does not license 'Electronic Money Institutions' as a distinct category; domestic fintechs (e.g., Wealthsimple, Koho, Nuvei, and payment processors) operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) or as payments institutions holding exemptions under the Retail Payment Activities Act, 2021 (RPAA). Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may provide ancillary collection and FX conversion capacity but cannot serve as the primary depository for the LP because they are not Schedule I Canadian banks, are not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC), and may expose non-resident partners to unregulated payment-chain custody risk. Legal constraint: offshore EMIs must either obtain a Canadian MSB registration with FINTRAC (requiring a Canadian authorized agent and AML compliance officer) or operate in Canada solely through a Canadian chartered bank partner; the LP should therefore use a Tier-1 bank as the master sub-account holder and route Stripe/AdSense payouts through the bank's virtual-account or collection-aggregation product.
