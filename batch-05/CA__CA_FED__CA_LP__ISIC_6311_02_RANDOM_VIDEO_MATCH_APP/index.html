Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada) for the federal general partner and Business Number; provincial companies registry (e.g., Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery, Companies and Personal Property Branch) for the Certificate of Limited Partnership.

Governing statute

Canada does not provide a federal limited partnership statute; the limited partnership is constituted under a provincial Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.7, ss. 2, 8, 9-10), while a corporate general partner is formed under the Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44, ss. 16, 21; federal tax identifiers (Business Number, GST/HST, and payroll) are governed by the Excise Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. E-15, s. 241, and the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.).

Formation / constitutional filings

Certificate of Limited Partnership (provincial filing), together with the federal Form RC1 - Request for a Business Number (CRA) and, where applicable, Articles of Incorporation (CBCA Form 1) for the corporate general partner.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Partners may contribute cash, property, or services as agreed in the partnership agreement; no escrow account is required before registration, provided that the limited partners' capital contributions are actually made or contractually undertaken to preserve limited liability.

Funding & unlock path: For the head-office tenancy, obtain fire safety clearance under the National Fire Code - 2020 (NFC) and the local fire marshal's inspection certificate; maintain worker health and safety board coverage (e.g., WSIB in Ontario) under the applicable provincial occupational health and safety act; structural load inspection is not applicable for a standard office lease, but leased premises must display a valid occupancy certificate from the local building authority.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Partners may contribute cash, property, or services as agreed in the partnership agreement; no escrow account is required before registration, provided that the limited partners' capital contributions are actually made or contractually undertaken to preserve limited liability.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; taxpayer portal: CRA My Business Account (MyBA) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtaining a statutory tax identity must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or using the 'Register a business' online service. For a Canadian Limited Partnership, the general partner files the application in the partnership's name, designates partnership capacity, and provides the registered address, jurisdiction of formation, list of partners, and anticipated taxable supplies paid. If GST/HST registration is required, the CRA will assign a dedicated GST/HST account number. A non-resident limited partner need not obtain a separate BN solely for the partnership, but the general partner must act as the agent of the partnership for all tax administration matters.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity obtaining a statutory tax identity must apply for a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or using the 'Register a business' online service. For a Canadian Limited Partnership, the general partner files the application in the partnership's name, designates partnership capacity, and provides the registered address, jurisdiction of formation, list of partners, and anticipated taxable supplies paid. If GST/HST registration is required, the CRA will assign a dedicated GST/HST account number. A non-resident limited partner need not obtain a separate BN solely for the partnership, but the general partner must act as the agent of the partnership for all tax administration matters.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election; a Limited Partnership is statutorily treated as a pass-through entity under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, with partners computing their own income shares. Available election mechanisms include: (i) the partnership admission election under subsection 97(2) of the Income Tax Act to deem a fair market value transfer to the partnership; (ii) the GST/HST joint venture election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act allowing a partner to be the operator for GST/HST purposes; and (iii) hybrid mismatch recharacterization adjustments under sections 93.5 and 93.6 of the Income Tax Act for cross-border payments, limiting deductible amounts where there is a hybrid payee or payment character mismatch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including taxable supplies made in Canada and by non-resident digital service providers) in any four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies. A smaller threshold applies to transporters and non-residents carrying on a commercial activity in Canada that supplies real property in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for zero-rating when they are exported by the supplier or a related intermediary and shipped directly to a destination outside Canada, with the supplier retaining continuous export evidence. Services qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is a non-resident person at the time the consideration becomes due and the service is not performed for an individual present in Canada, does not relate to real estate located in Canada, and is not a service of rendering tangible personal property situated in Canada. For the described video-match social app, a supply of online digital services to a non-resident user will generally be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the service is not delivered to a person in Canada and the supplier can demonstrate the non-resident status of the user through payment routing, IP address geolocation, and user subscription data.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency form B13A or authorized electronic export reporting declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (through bill of lading or multimodal transport document evidencing physical movement of goods from Canada to a foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank wire / SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds into a Canadian bank account)
  • Commercial invoice and purchase contract/purchase order from the non-resident buyer
  • Non-resident status evidence of the digital service recipient, including IP address logs, billing address, and subscription records

Canada does not mandate a national real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance system. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records under the GST/HST electronic record requirements, provided they are in an accessible format and permit accurate verification. There is no digital stamping or API-based pre-clearance for GST/HST returns; however, registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA's electronic services and maintain complete audit trails. For cross-border digital services, non-resident suppliers may be required to register under the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration framework and file returns through CRA's e-services.

CRA may audit exporters and deny zero-rating where documentation is incomplete. Retrospective reassessment within the normal four-year limitation period (or longer in cases of misrepresentation or fraud) will impose GST/HST at 5% on the previously zero-rated supply, together with compound interest on arrears. Civil penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act may reach the greater of CAD 500 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, with additional penalties for repeated false statements of up to CAD 25,000. Fraudulent export schemes may result in criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. All input tax credits originally claimed on underlying expenses for zero-rated exports are subject to clawback if the zero-rating is reversed.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current and capital accounts under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. CAD/USD and all major-currency pairs are unrestricted. Non-resident limited partners may remit profits and capital without foreign-exchange approval; however, distributions characterized as partnership income bear Part XIII withholding tax (treaty-reduced, typically 15% for US limited partners under the Canada-US Tax Convention), and all currency movements above CAD 10,000 require FINTRAC reporting. In-bound subscription and IAP receipts are freely remittable as business income, subject to normal Canadian income tax for the partnership and to transfer-pricing documentation where non-resident partners are involved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no conversion timeline exist in Canada. Exporters and cross-border service providers may retain foreign currency indefinitely. The only mandatory obligations are FINTRAC reports for cross-border currency transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, anti-money-laundering record-keeping under the PCMLTFA, and information returns (Form T2063/T106 as applicable) for partnership distributions to non-residents.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential banking supervision is shared with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and cross-border currency transaction reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No separate foreign-exchange control administration exists because Canada has maintained a freely floating, fully convertible currency since the abolition of exchange controls in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Canada does not recognize the UK/EU electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence as a deposit-taking authorization; an offshore EMI may be used as a secondary collection corridor but cannot serve as the entity's principal regulated bank account. For digital revenue settlement, the preferable structure is a federal Schedule I bank operating account supplemented by a FINTRAC-registered money-services-business (MSB) licence or a Payments-Canada-member payment facilitator. The target rails (Apple IAP and Paymentwall) function as merchants of record, so settlement flows arrive as depository payouts rather than direct card-acquiring; this reduces payment-licensing friction but adds a reconciliation layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: SWIFT MT103 with remittance information tagged 'Limited Partnership Capital Contribution – [Partner Legal Name]'. No Bank of Canada pre-approval or purpose-code registration is required; however, the receiving bank must hold the executed capital-call notice, the limited partnership agreement annexure, and a beneficial-ownership attestation. Non-resident CAD funds are cleared directly through Payments Canada's Lynx system; USD funds are routed via the bank's US correspondent (New York branch) using ABA and Fedwire or CHIPS.
