Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on Canadian limited partnerships; capital is fixed by the limited partnership agreement and may be contributed in cash, property, or services. No government-mandated escrow or trust capital account exists at formation; subscription proceeds must be credited to the LP capital ledger upon registration. The CBCA corporate general partner must maintain at least one issued share.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) under the Ministry of National Revenue; official self-service portal is 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses.html) and the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) service (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html). The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) separately administers customs export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Limited Partnership (LP) provincially under the applicable Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Ontario/Alberta/British Columbia) and obtain the provincial registration number; a valid LP declaration/registration is a precondition for federal tax identification. Step 2: Apply for a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1; the BN functions as the statutory TIN/GST identifier for all federal tax accounts. Step 3: Open program accounts linked to the BN, specifically the 'RP' GST/HST account; registration for GST/HST is mandatory for a non-resident LP as soon as its worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export sales of used machinery) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters, while voluntary registration may be elected at any time under ETA subsection 240(1). Step 4: Execute CRA Form RC59 or the online 'Represent a Client' authorization to appoint a Canadian tax agent for the non-resident partnership. Step 5: File the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return (Schedule 50 for foreign partners) to report allocated income to each partner, including non-resident limited partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; partnership tax status flows statutorily from provincial registration and ITA sections 96-103. Available elections are: (i) allocation of partnership income/loss to partners under the LP agreement, subject to ITA subsection 103.1 arm's-length reallocation for unreasonable allocations; (ii) a non-resident partner's election under ITA subsection 216(1) for taxation on net rental or timber income (not relevant to machinery trading but available if rental income arises in the LP); (iii) designation of partners as 'active' versus 'non-active' for loss restriction under ITA subsection 96(2.1); (iv) taxation year selection for the partnership under ITA subsection 96(3.2) to align with common partner year-ends; (v) anti-hybrid mismatch elections relating to Canadian limited partnerships with foreign transparent partners, including the look-through rules of Canada's tax treaties (e.g., Canada-US Treaty Article IV(7)) and the hybrid mismatch rules in ITA sections 94.1 through 94.4 governing FLLI status; (vi) for export wholesaling, the LP may elect to apply the net metering/alternative registration procedures of ETA section 239 to simplify rebate claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current and capital account purposes, consistent with Canada's IMF Article VIII obligations. No prior regulatory approval is required to receive, hold, convert, or remit CAD or foreign currency. Profit remittance hurdles are documentary only: non-resident limited partners must be tax-compliant on their allocated Canadian-source business income; the LP must hold CRS/FATCA self-certifications; management fees, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents may give rise to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, subsections 212(1)(b), (c), and (d). Post-tax partnership profit distributions are not subject to exchange-control or additional withholding impediments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender period and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts: 0% of export proceeds must be converted into CAD, and there is no deadline for holding, reinvesting, or repatriating funds. Exporters may retain CAD or foreign-currency balances indefinitely at a Canadian bank. The only binding obligations are bank-level FINTRAC reporting for electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more and balance-of-payments tagging under the Bank of Canada / Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting System.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting jointly with the Department of Finance Canada; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises all Schedule I banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces AML/CFT transaction reporting. Canada does not operate a centralised foreign-exchange-control administration equivalent to a dedicated FX regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a standalone CAD settlement rail; Medium as a supplementary inbound collection rail. A non-resident digital EMI cannot settle CAD directly in Canada unless it registers as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, is business-established in Canada, and uses an OSFI-regulated sponsor bank for access to Canadian clearing. Foreign EMIs are not eligible for direct membership in Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS or the ACSS retail clearing system. The used-equipment LP must therefore direct all capital contributions and trade proceeds through a Schedule I bank; offshore EMIs may be used only for small foreign buyer deposits or pay-in collection, not for sovereign capital or trade disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all capital contributions from non-resident limited partners via SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers into the LP's CAD-denominated Operational Account at the Canadian Schedule I bank. Incoming funds of CAD 10,000 or more are reported by the receiving bank to FINTRAC within one business day as international electronic fund transfers. The bank tags the remittance under the Statistics Canada International Transactions Reporting System using the BPM6 direct-investment equity-capital code, identifying the injection as equity contribution to a Canadian enterprise. The LP must provide a numbered capital-call notice, a GP resolution containing the exact remittance reference, the executed subscription agreement, partner bank confirmation, and CRS self-certification records in the same audit file. The bank performs enhanced verification of remitter identity and source of funds before crediting the account.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) domestic CAD payments to Canadian vendors, freight providers, and warehouse operators clear via Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS for same-day high-value disbursements, with ACSS batch rails available for smaller CAD payments; (2) international settlement to the foreign seller of used hydraulic earthmovers is executed via SWIFT MT103 under SWIFT gpi, with cover routed through MT202; (3) where the foreign seller requires payment security, the LP opens an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (MT700) governed by ICC UCP 600, with presentation of the commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, independent inspection certificate, and PPSA title search; the sight LC is settled through the issuing Canadian bank's correspondent network and the seller's confirming bank; outward remittances of CAD 10,000 or more are reported by the bank to FINTRAC as large outgoing electronic fund transfers.FX Forward (deliverable CAD/USD) under ISDA 1998 FX & Currency Derivatives Definitions; no exchange-control approval is required in Canada and execution is bilateral with the Canadian bank.FX Swap (CAD/USD principal exchange at two future value dates) for rolling over injected capital and trade disbursements while minimising funding cost, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with board-authorised hedging limits.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CAD fixed-for-USD floating) to align used-machinery purchase debt with CAD sale proceeds over a matched maturity.Non-Deliverable Forward (USD/CAD) is permissible in the OTC market for exceptional settlement-mismatch cases, although CAD is not a typical NDF reference currency; execution requires an OSFI-regulated dealer and explicit board approval.
