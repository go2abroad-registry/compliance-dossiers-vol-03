Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada, including a non-resident limited partner through a Canadian limited partnership, must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA. Registration for GST/HST, payroll, and corporate income tax accounts is integrated under the BN. Foreign entities may apply by mailing or faxing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), by telephone, or by registering online through CRA's Business Registration Online service. Where the limited partnership itself is required to file or to collect GST/HST, the partnership obtains its own BN and GST/HST account; non-resident partners should also obtain an individual or entity BN if filing Canadian returns. A non-resident without a permanent establishment in Canada may still be required to register for GST/HST if it makes taxable supplies in Canada that exceed the registration threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification system. A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent by default under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act; income, gains, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners who are subject to tax. Hybrid mismatch rules are embedded in sections 18.4, 18.5, and 18.6 of the Income Tax Act, but there is no elective mechanism to reclassify the entity. Certain partnership fiscal-period and international elections are available through prescribed CRA forms, but no election is available to treat the LP as a separate taxable entity at the federal level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) measured over any four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, the person must register for GST/HST

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the supplier are generally zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods or arranges shipment to a destination outside Canada, and the goods are exported in a continuous journey with commercial documentation. Supplies of services to non-residents may be zero-rated when the services are performed wholly outside Canada or are otherwise qualifying export services, provided the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not primarily for use in Canada. For a mobile utility app sold through Apple App Store and Google Play, revenue from non-resident end users may qualify as zero-rated export of services if the conditions in the Excise Tax Act, including the 'place of supply' rules, are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency export reporting or authorized exporter alternative)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill or other transport documentation
  • Proof of export (carrier confirmation, customs manifest)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign exchange receipts
  • Platform sales records for digital App Store / Google Play sales

No federal continuous transaction control (CTC) or real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists in Canada. The CRA does not require digital stamping, pre-clearance API filing, or mandatory e-invoicing. Taxpayers may issue electronic invoices, provided the required GST/HST invoice information is maintained and records are complete. Provincial sales tax regimes (e.g., QST, PST/RST) may impose separate invoicing or filing obligations but do not create federal e-invoicing clearance.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess uncollected GST/HST where export documentation is insufficient or where the supply is determined to be made in Canada. False statements or omissions can trigger penalties under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act, including penalties equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the understated tax, increased to 10% in cases of gross negligence. Fraudulent refund claims may attract interest and civil penalties; criminal prosecution is possible under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act for wilful evasion. Retrospective clawback periods of up to four years generally apply, extending indefinitely where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized, freely convertible currency regime with no exchange controls administered under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act. Both current account (goods/services, royalties, app-store remittances) and capital account (FDI, equity injections, loans, profit repatriation) transactions are unrestricted. There are no approvals required for dividend or partnership distribution remittances to non-resident partners, provided the partnership files required T2/T5013 information returns and any applicable Part XIII or Part XIV withholding tax is remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Conversion between CAD and USD/EUR/GBP occurs in a deep, liquid interbank market; no mandatory conversion channel exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Receipts from app store sales and cross-border trade settlements may be held in foreign currency accounts indefinitely or converted freely. Only FINTRAC large transaction and wire reporting rules apply (reporting of incoming/outgoing international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF and foreign exchange reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate. Foreign digital EMI/FX platform providers (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) are not chartered banks under the Bank Act, but are regulated as money services businesses (MSBs) or foreign money services businesses (FMSBs) requiring FINTRAC registration in Canada. They may be used as operational adjuncts for collection and payment routing, but cannot substitute for a Canadian Schedule I bank account for the treasury backbone, receipt of LP capital contributions, or audit-proof fund flows. OSFI maintains regulatory clarity that only federal or provincial credit union deposits are CDIC-insured; EMI balances are not. Legal constraint: EMIs must not serve as the primary clearing vehicle for capital account inflows without a registered Canadian MSB/FMSB (MSB registration under PCMLTFA section 11) and full transaction reporting adherence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity and partnership contribution wires must originate from the LP's non-resident partners' own regulated bank accounts and be routed via SWIFT MT103 with proper purpose codes. For capital injections, use ISO 20022 Transaction Type Code / purpose category 'CAPT' (Capital Transfer) or 3-letter code 'ITR' (Income from capital) where permissible; for loan or debt instruments, use 'STO' (Short-term loan) or 'LTC' (Long-term loan) with accompanying loan agreement. Bank of Canada real-time/ACH rails are not used for large cross-border capital; funds clear via SWIFT through correspondent banks. The beneficiary bank's reference field should include 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [LP NAME]' and each partner's source-of-funds attestation must be pre-staged for KYC review. For the $10,000+ transaction, FINTRAC EFT report will be filed by the receiving bank; the LP does not need a separate filing unless it acts as a money services business.Trade and vendor settlements are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103/PACS.008 wires for international disbursements and Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for domestic CAD payments. Commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) are available for physical-good transactions, but as the mobile utility app is a digital product, MT103 with beneficiary bank verification and ICC URDG/ISP-compliant demand guarantees serve as the principal clearing rail. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

N/A – Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; no Canadian federal corporate income tax at entity level. Underlying corporate partners are subject to the 15% federal general corporate rate; provincial rates apply separately.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident; not applicable to LP profit distributions. For a limited partnership, distribution of active business income is taxed as business income, not as a dividend.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a December 31 fiscal period-end, the T5013 is due March 31 of the immediately following calendar year. For non-calendar fiscal year-ends, the return is due within five months after fiscal period-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 – the aggregate annual value of reportable cross-border non-arm's length transactions involving amounts paid/payable to a non-resident partner or related person, triggering mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation obligations.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the Canada Revenue Agency may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s.245 ITA) to deny tax benefits where a Canadian LP lacks authentic economic substance or is established primarily to circumvent tax obligations. A GAAR application results in a penalty of 25% of the disallowed tax benefit. Treaty 'beneficial ownership' tests and PE attribution rules require genuine functions, assets, and business risks in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: On indictment, a fine between 50% and 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years; on summary conviction, imprisonment up to 2 years. Civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax plus arrears interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner and directing officers can be prosecuted under s.242 ITA; upon conviction, personal criminal restitution orders, travel bans via court restrictions, and imprisonment may be imposed.
Shell Company / Artificial Entity

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment disallowing the tax benefit, GAAR penalty equal to 25% of the disallowed amount, interest retroactive to the filing date, and potential loss of treaty relief.

Director personal liability: The general partner who caused the transaction is jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalty where partnership assets are insufficient; a court may impose disclosure orders or travel restrictions during assessment.
AML / PCMLTFA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for serious non-compliance; criminal prosecution under the PCMLTFA can lead to imprisonment up to 5 years and seizure of assets involved in the offense.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorized or acquiesced to the offending conduct face personal fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment; partnership liability does not shield individuals acting in a personal capacity.
Late or False Non-Resident Withholding and T5013 Filing

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty of CAD 25 per day up to CAD 2,500 per return; separate penalties up to CAD 100 per slip for failing to provide information on a slip; wilful omission can trigger gross negligence penalties up to 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: General partners may be held personally liable for the partnership's withholding tax debts if partnership assets are insufficient; CRA can assess directors of a corporate partner for unremitted source deductions under s.227.1 ITA.
