Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Canadian limited partnership; capital is fixed contractually in the Limited Partnership Agreement and must be recorded in each partner's capital account. No escrow is mandated by statute; escrow arises only if required by the agreement or by a lender or investor. General Partner and Limited Partners may contribute cash or property at arm's-length valuation.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety certificate or occupational clearance is mandatory absent premises and employees. If the SPV occupies a small administrative office, obtain a municipal occupancy permit, comply with the Ontario Fire Code (O. Reg. 213/07) for extinguishers and evacuation signage, retain commercial general liability and directors'/officers' insurance, and confirm structural load adequacy via the landlord's certificate of occupancy.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

None applicable — passive equity holding (ISIC 6420 / NAICS 551112) is not a regulated industry in Canada; general federal oversight of the holding structure rests with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED).

Category: None required; no business operating permit, manufacturing licence, or sectoral authorization applies to a pure equity holding SPV. Sectoral licences arise only if a future subsidiary conducts regulated activities (e.g., banking, insurance, securities).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business registration portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html); GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE; Partnership returns via T5013 filing; Provincial LP registries coexist (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act registry for creation of the LP).

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Limited Partnership under provincial LP legislation; no federal LP registry exists. 2) Obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA (via Business Registration Online (BRO)) in the name of the general partner acting as the partnership's representative; the BN with account type RP (business number for a partnership) serves as the tax identifier. 3) For GST/HST, register under ETA section 240 if the LP is not a 'small supplier' (taxable supplies > CA$30,000 per four-quarter period) OR voluntarily elect to register for input tax credit recovery (ETA s. 240(2), 241(1)) — the pure equity SPV may, however, remain unregistered since dividend income is an exempt financial service. 4) Open CRA My Business Account; submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) if CRA registration is by paper. 5) Non-resident partners: the LP must file T5013 Summary/Information Returns if it has a non-resident partner or otherwise meets the T5013 filing criteria; non-resident partners receive allocations via T5013 slips and file Canadian income tax returns (T1/T2 or Part XIII forms) as applicable. 6) A foreign entity acting as general partner must itself obtain/link its foreign registration to the LP BN and may require a CRA non-resident account (e.g., Non-Resident Withholding T4/T5 program) for withholding obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope: Canadian-source income allocated to partners under ITA s.96; the LP itself is transparent for Income Tax Act purposes (not a 'taxpayer' under s.2(1) for income tax; however, a partnership is a 'person' for certain procedural provisions). Publicly traded or 'SIFT' limited partnerships may be deemed to be a corporation/taxpayer under ITA s.197 (Specified Investment Flow-Through rules) — not applicable to a private pure-equity SPV. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable at federal level; the SPV is subject to Part I tax at the partner level.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' exists under Canadian law. Available elections: (a) ITA s.97(2) joint election to defer capital gain on transfer of property to the LP at agreed (rollover) amounts; (b) ITA s.96(1.1)/reg. s.1102 partnership fiscal-period election; (c) ETA s.156/157 election for closely related corporations to treat intercompany taxable supplies as made for nil consideration (GST/HST); (d) ETA s.150 election to treat holding corporation as a 'listed financial institution' where applicable; (e) non-resident landlord election under ITA s.216(1) (not typical for holding SPV); (f) hybrid-mismatch 'deemed deduction' elections/compliance: Canada's BEPS Action 2 rules (ITA s.18.4(1), s.18.4(2), s.18.5(1)) disallow deductions for payments subject to hybrid mismatch treatment — no affirmative election exists; taxpayers may file with the CRA under s.18.4(5) (designation of avoided jurisdictions). Note: CA LPs are not eligible for a US check-the-box by Canadian filing; a U.S. entity-level classification is separately driven by US Treasury Reg. §301.7701-3, which is outside CRA's jurisdiction.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating currency regime under a fully liberalized capital-account environment. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are generally free from exchange-control quotas or licensing requirements. Dividends, interest, royalties, and capital proceeds may be remitted cross-border without central-bank approval, subject to tax withholding and anti-money-laundering verification. Non-resident partners of a Canadian limited partnership remain eligible for treaty-reduced or treaty-exempt withholding on Canadian-source distributed income where applicable under the Income Tax Act and the relevant bilateral tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada maintains no statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds. There is no prescribed timeline for conversion of foreign-currency export receipts into Canadian dollars, and no forced central-bank settlement obligation. Exporters may retain foreign currency in corporate accounts at authorized Canadian financial institutions. Reporting obligations are limited to anti-money-laundering thresholds (FINTRAC large transaction and electronic funds transfer reports) and, for Canadian-controlled entities with foreign property, CRA Form T1135 may apply when aggregate foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary depository or escrow bank; Medium as an ancillary FX payments processor with a Canadian-registered payment service provider. Legal constraints: Under the Bank Act (Canada), only banks authorized under Schedule I, II, or III may accept deposits in Canada. Off-shore digital EMIs, including neobank or e-money institutions domiciled outside Canada, cannot operate a Canadian deposit-taking business without a federal banking license. Payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada as a retail payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) for payment-function activities. The pure equity holding SPV should therefore hold all formal cash balances in a Tier 1 Canadian chartered bank and, at most, use a licensed fintech payment platform for earmarked operational flows with full downstream reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections and shareholder loans must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the investing entity's bank to the Canadian bank account held in the name of the limited partnership. The payment message must carry an accurate narrative and purpose code describing the transaction as 'Capital Contribution - Holding of Subsidiary Shares' or 'Shareholder Loan Disbursement'; common ISO 20022 purpose codes such as 'EQUI' (equity, securities) or 'LOAN' (loan) should be used where the bank requires a structured purpose code. The originating bank must confirm the ultimate remitter and identify the final beneficiary to satisfy FINTRAC and bank-level sanctions screening. The Canadian receiving bank will apply an electronic funds transfer report for transactions at or above CAD 10,000 where required, and the limited partnership must maintain a board-authorized capital contribution ledger, unredacted SWIFT advices, source-of-wealth evidence, and completed beneficiary due diligence forms to withstand compliance look-back and regulatory review.For distributions, dividends, shareholder loan repayments, and trade settlement obligations, the clearing rail is typically the SWIFT network using MT103 for customer credit transfers; where a banks' upstream interbank offset is needed, MT202 COV may also be used to ensure end-to-end beneficiary transparency. For commercial trade transactions that involve actual goods or documentary credits, delivery versus payment is effected through an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Canadian bank and advised/confirmed by a counterparty bank; payment on negotiation is reconciled to the relevant bill of lading, invoice, and customs entry. Domestic CAD settlement, when the counterpart is inside Canada, should be cleared through Payments Canada's LVTS for same-day finality. Each outbound transfer should attach a purpose code such as ISIC-compatible 'Dividends', 'Interest', 'Shareholder Loan Repayment', or 'Payment for Goods/Services', and the treasury team must retain the underlying commercial or distribution resolution to substantiate the payment in any tax treaty or withholding review.Deliverable over-the-counter FX forwards for CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/GBP to lock in conversion rates on known future dividend or loan flowsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for non-CAD currencies where physical delivery is constrained by settlement infrastructure of the particular currency pairCross-currency interest rate swaps transferring principal and fixed or floating interest streams between CAD and foreign-denominated debt to hedge a foreign-currency balance sheet or intercompany loanVanilla FX options (put/call structures) when adopted under a documented treasury policy and approved by the board of the general partner, with ISDA documentation and collateral arrangements compliant with Canadian securities and banking regulations
