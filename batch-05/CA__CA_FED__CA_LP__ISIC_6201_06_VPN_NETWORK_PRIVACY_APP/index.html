Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — My Business Account portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html; GST/HST NETFILE and Business Registration Online (BRO)

E-government filing portal

The foreign entity must apply for a Business Number (BN) via Business Registration Online (BRO). For a Limited Partnership, the partnership itself requires a BN with a GST/HST program account (RT) if it makes taxable supplies in Canada. Non-resident partners not carrying on business in Canada may also require a BN for Part XIII withholding. The LP must appoint a representative and file GST/HST returns on a fiscal-year basis. The application requires the partnership agreement, general partner identity, and confirmation of place of business in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Election under subsection 186(1) not available to partnerships; partnerships may elect under section 21 of the Excise Tax Act to have the GST/HST net tax calculation on a cash (collection) basis versus accrual basis. Hybrid mismatch rules under BEPS Action 2 are limited in Canada; no check-the-box election exists — entity classification is determined by the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act (a limited partnership is a fiscally transparent flow-through vehicle; each partner's share of income is taxed at the partner level).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold; registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies from Canadian carrying-on exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of digital and VPN software services qualify under Section 7 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: a supply of a service made to a non-resident person is zero-rated, provided the service is not rendered to an individual present in Canada, does not relate to real property in Canada, and is not supplied to a person registered for GST/HST. The recipient must be a non-resident at the time the supply is made, and the supplier must maintain evidence of the recipient's non-resident status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (if goods physically cross the border — not applicable to pure digital SaaS)
  • Commercial invoice or contract with the non-resident recipient
  • Evidence of non-resident status (e.g., foreign address, foreign VAT registration)
  • Payment and bank transfer records confirming settlement in foreign currency
  • IP/software delivery logs and server records demonstrating offshore service performance

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate. Canada has adopted a voluntary Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework based on the PEPPOL/Peppol BIS 3.0 standard. CRA permits electronically stored invoices if the taxpayer retains a complete, legible record. No API-based clearance or digital stamping (contrast with LATAM RF/CFDI regimes). Mandatory data elements per subsection 169(1) and section 3 of the ETA include supplier name, recipient name, date, total amount, and applicable GST/HST rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both the current and capital account. Capital inflows, including equity contributions to a limited partnership, require no exchange-control permission. Profit remittances (distributions, interest, royalties, fees) are freely remittable in CAD or foreign currency; the only material hurdle is tax compliance: non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25% unless reduced under a tax treaty) on certain payments (dividends, interest, royalties). Allocations of active business income are not subject to withholding, but are taxable to non-residents only if they carry on business through a Canadian permanent establishment; therefore, remittance testing depends on the LP’s permanent establishment analysis and the partnership agreement’s distribution waterfall.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines for export receipts in Canada. The investment dealer and bank may retain foreign currency indefinitely. Only statistical reporting to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) or through the Bank of Canada’s International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) is required; FINTRAC reporting occurs when a financial exchange of CAD 10,000 or more takes place. VPN/encryption software revenues are treated as service exports and are export permitted/controlled under the Export Control List (ECL) Sub-Group 5D002, but no FX surrender applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada does not maintain a stand-alone Foreign Exchange Administration — exchange control power is effectively not exercised.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (qualified). A Canadian limited partnership may use a domestic MSB authorisation holder (e.g., Wise Canada / Stripe Treasury) for limited foreign-currency settlement, but an offshore digital EMI without an OSFI-regulated custody bank or FINTRAC MSB registration cannot act as the LP’s primary treasury rail. Under the Bank Act (Section 524) only chartered banks are permitted to accept deposit liabilities in Canada; offshore EMIs therefore must be contracted through a Canadian Schedule I bank or a designated clearing agent. VPN/encryption merchants are treated as Medium-Risk Enhanced by most EMIs, so additional due diligence (product documentation, no-anonymity policy, jurisdiction-of-use data) will be required; viability is High for CAD payments via Stripe/Paymentwall as sub-processors, but Low for using offshore EMIs (e.g., UK/Europe EMI) for primary treasury settlement due to CRS and FINTRAC reporting gaps.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

VPN / network privacy SaaS product perceived as enabling illegal anonymity, evasion of geo-restrictions, or sanctions circumvention.

Root cause: VPN / network privacy SaaS product perceived as enabling illegal anonymity, evasion of geo-restrictions, or sanctions circumvention.

Defense evidence: Provide corporate substance evidence: certificate of LP registration, partnership agreement, business plan with ISIC 6201 / NAICS 517919 classification, ECCN (5D002) export control self-classification, technical due diligence pack describing pure commercial use, license agreements, terms of service, and a legal opinion from a Canadian law firm confirming compliance with Canadian export controls, Criminal Code provisions, and anti-money-laundering regulations.
Disproportionate cross-border payment settlement through third-party rails (Stripe/Paymentwall) rather than direct bank wires.

Root cause: Disproportionate cross-border payment settlement through third-party rails (Stripe/Paymentwall) rather than direct bank wires.

Defense evidence: Provide executed payment processor agreements, PCI-DSS attestation of compliance, merchant identification and MCC codes, six months of aggregated settlement reports, backend KYC/KYB files, and a letter from a Canadian CPA confirming that processor receipts correspond to recognised SaaS revenue without high-risk un-invoiced flows.
Limited partnership structure with potentially opaque beneficial ownership of foreign limited partners.

Root cause: Limited partnership structure with potentially opaque beneficial ownership of foreign limited partners.

Defense evidence: Provide robust beneficial ownership schedule (to each Canadian bank’s Form 3841 equivalent), the limited partnership deed as registered, all partners’ certified identification documents, corporate books/resolutions, and a no-foreign-criminal-earnings representation from the general partner; if any owner is a non-resident entity, include a check-the-box/CRS self-certification and head-office/location proof.
Initial capital injection originates from an unadmitted investor or an offshore corporate service provider without verifiable source of funds.

Root cause: Initial capital injection originates from an unadmitted investor or an offshore corporate service provider without verifiable source of funds.

Defense evidence: Provide six months of bank statements from the partner/investor’s own account, notarised subscription agreement, capital contribution ledger, source-of-wealth questionnaire, and proof of Canadian CAD settlement; commit to using only the Canadian bank as the first funding destination.
Foreign exchange or IP-royalty flows absent underwriting backing for recurring trade settlements.

Root cause: Foreign exchange or IP-royalty flows absent underwriting backing for recurring trade settlements.

Defense evidence: Provide pro forma invoice template, a licensing/disbursement register, intercompany agreements, and a board-approved transfer pricing policy; for each counterparty, evidence of due diligence (sanctions screening, entity verification, beneficial ownership). Add a forward FX exposure waiver or documented hedge policy to reduce unexplained wire frequency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are routed as SWIFT MT103 / MT202 to the LP's segregated CAD account at a Schedule I regulated bank. The payment instruction must identify the beneficiary and the purpose using clear trade and treasury fields: 'CA_LP Paid-In Capital under Partnership Agreement', 'Capital Contribution' or 'Paiement de capital' (where applicable). The receiving bank will request a copy of the LP partnership agreement, general partner resolution, and a capital account ledger. Since Canada imposes no exchange-control or FDI approval for this scope (Investment Canada Act review only if the investor acquires control of a non-Canadian business or a sensitive sector; VPN/encryption might be sensitive if national-security nexus exists, but ordinary paid-in capital is not notifiable), the injection can clear within one business day. The bank must file a FINTRAC Suspicious/EFT Report for thresholds ≥ CAD $10,000, and the LP must maintain a 'Capital Injections Register' for audit trail and KYC refresh.Outbound trade disbursements (settlement of software licenses, infrastructure, hardware, and engineering services) are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 (single beneficiary) or MT202 COVER for USD via New York clearing (FDN/Fedwire). Commercial Letters of Credit are available for hardware-network acquisitions where a shipment performance condition is required; a typical irrevocable, confirmed LC is issued by the Canadian bank for USD/EUR settlement with a 30/60/90-day tenor. For digital subscription and server expenses, ACH/EFT pushes through the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) and continuous linked settlement (CLS) banks are used. Each outbound wire must include a 'purpose of payment' (e.g., 'ISIC 6201 network operation service') and counterparty name; the bank will require the payment compliance form and sanctions screening evidence. The LP should maintain separate sub-accounts for treasury operations and disbursements to enable clean transactional lineage and facilitate audits by FINTRAC, OSFI, and the CRA.Deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) with 1M–24M maturities, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and a Credit Support Annex (CSA) with a Canadian Schedule I bank.Cross-currency swaps (CCIRS) used for hedging foreign-currency debt-servicing / ongoing operational exposure; permitted only where the LP can evidence underlying current or anticipated foreign-currency revenue obligations.Vanilla FX options (call/put) on the Canadian dollar with the bank as dealer; no options with zero-cost collars except where the bank confirms no speculative mismatch.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in currencies such as CNY/INR/KRW for invoice-related exposures where no deliverable market exists; NDFs in CAD/USD are effectively unavailable and unnecessary because the CAD is fully deliverable.Plain-vanilla cross-currency basis swaps under the same ISDA/CSA regime if the LP enters a USD/EUR capital transaction.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate after federal abatement and general rate reduction (s.123(1)); Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPC) benefit from a 9% federal rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income. Provincial corporate income tax rates apply in addition.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII rate on dividends paid to a non-resident of Canada. Tax treaty relief may reduce this to 15% under the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty and often 5% for a U.S. corporation owning at least 10% of voting shares; other DTAs provide varying rates (0%, 10%, 15%). Limited partnership profit allocations to non-resident partners are not 'dividends' and are generally taxed under Part I on a net basis rather than Part XIII withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal period ending December 31, the T5013 must be filed no later than March 31 of the following calendar year. For a non-calendar fiscal period, the return is due no later than the last day of the third month after the partnership's fiscal period-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the end of each taxation year; for a corporation with a December 31 year-end, the due date is June 30 of the following calendar year, with payment of any balance due no later than March 31 for most corporate taxpayers (subject to instalment rules).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing is due the same day as the primary return: for a partnership, the T5013 due date; for a corporation, the T2 due date. Late filing penalties apply separately.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000: annual aggregate value of non-arm's length transactions with any single non-resident person that triggers the need for contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and a Form T106 filing. Canada does not impose a separate statutory Master/Local File threshold, but an OECD-aligned Local File/Master File set is recommended whenever the aggregate amount exceeds CAD 1,000,000, and is expected for multinational enterprises with large intercompany VPN licensing or network services flows.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to tax-haven jurisdictions. Instead, CRA relies on s.247 transfer-pricing rules, the s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), and common-law doctrines of substance-over-form and sham. A Canadian limited partnership claiming to pass through VPN revenue must demonstrate a real Canadian operation, actual employees, infrastructure, and risk-taking; a limited partner that participates in control of the partnership or withdraws funded capital within one year may be stripped of limited liability under provincial partnership statutes, and CRA can disregard a conduit LP if its principal purpose is to defer tax or obtain treaty benefits artificially.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239 of the Income Tax Act: on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 100% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Civil penalties under s.163(2) add the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax, plus arrears interest and erroneous-return interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner who participated in or directed the evasion can be personally prosecuted as parties to the offence, face personal fines and imprisonment, and may be subject to court-ordered restitution and travel restrictions during extradition or fraud proceedings; CRA may also pursue civil liability for funds diverted.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under s.247(2) restoring arm's-length prices; a penalty under s.247(3) equal to the greater of 10% of the transfer-pricing adjustment and CAD 5,000, plus daily interest; late T106 filing penalties of CAD 100 per day to a maximum of CAD 25,000; a gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) can be stacked for intentional TP misreporting.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner are generally not automatically liable for s.247 adjustments, but may be liable under s.227.1 for unremitted withholding taxes that arise from disguised payments; if a director knowingly signs a false T106 or instructs the falsification, he or she may be named in a s.239 prosecution and may face personal restitution orders.
Shell Company and Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply s.245 GAAR to deny all tax benefits and reassess the partners as if the arrangement had not been executed; alternatively the arrangement may be struck down as a sham at common law; penalties include s.163(2) gross-negligence penalties, loss of loss-carryovers for the LP, and disallowance of all intercompany deductions; criminal liability under s.239 may apply if misleading information has been filed.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner can be forced to restore distributions made to shareholders if the partnership is frustrated; a limited partner who takes part in management or acts with actual authority may be personally liable for partnership debts; courts have pierced the LP veil where the entity was a pure sham, exposing individual directors to unlimited personal liability.
AML and Proceeds of Crime Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations and up to CAD 500,000 per violation for individuals; CRA and FINTRAC may jointly impose asset forfeiture; criminal penalties for obstruction of a FINTRAC examination can include up to two years imprisonment; a temporary or permanent order barring the LP from financial services may also be issued.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers owe a statutory duty to ensure an AML compliance program exists; if they knowingly direct, authorise, or acquiesce to violations, they may be charged personally under s.74(1) of PCMLTFA, face up to 5 years imprisonment, personal fines, and an order to pay restitution; travel bans can accompany a criminal fraud/investigation arrest.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII and Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Withholding non-compliance triggers a 10% immediate penalty on unremitted amounts, plus daily compounding interest; where a payer deliberately fails to withhold, CRA may levy a 50% gross-negligence penalty; unpaid source deductions are trust-fund debts collectable from current and future assets; criminal prosecution under s.227(10) can result in fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years for refusal to remit.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for all unremitted source deductions and Part XIII amounts under s.227.1, even after the company is dissolved; the CRA can assess the director personally, issue garnishment, travel bans and seizure orders, and may bring a civil enforcement action for the full amount, without the usual bankruptcy discharge protection.
