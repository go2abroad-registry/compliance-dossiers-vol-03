Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); online portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency, CRA My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

The Canadian Limited Partnership, including one with non-resident partners, must first determine whether it is carrying on business in Canada or otherwise making taxable supplies in Canada. Where mandatory registration applies, the general partner or an authorized representative files CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and GST/HST Account) through Business Registration Online or by paper, identifying the entity as a Limited Partnership and providing its provincial limited-partnership registration number, general partner details, registered address, expected activity (NAICS 541810), and estimated Canadian taxable supplies. CRA then issues a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and a two-letter account identifier, normally ending in RT0001 for the GST/HST account. A non-resident owner must obtain or maintain a Canadian representative for service of documents and enter the representative through CRA's Represent a Client service. The partnership is treated as a deemed person for GST/HST purposes and should register separately from its partners; voluntary registration is available if the LP wants to claim input tax credits while making zero-rated exports. The LP must keep its BN-RT account active by filing nil returns when no tax is payable and by annually confirming partnership details with CRA and the relevant provincial registry.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act; partnership income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to the partners, and the LP itself generally does not pay federal income tax. A separate entity-level tax applies only to a specified investment flow-through partnership if its non-portfolio investment earnings are within the SIFT regime. There is no general hybrid-mismatch election available in federal domestic law; anti-hybrid outcomes are addressed through the Act, treaty rules, transfer pricing, and CRA administrative interpretation. For GST/HST, a registrant may use the closely related corporations election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act to treat qualifying supplies between registrants as nil, but this does not change the LP's income tax classification or its eligibility to zero-rate exported services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada over a four-calendar-quarter small supplier determination period. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST once its Canadian taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in that period; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. Non-residents making specified digital supplies to Canadian consumers may also be captured by the special registration rules under Canada's digital economy measures once the same threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act when sold by a registrant in the course of commercial activity, exported by the supplier or on its behalf in a continuous shipment, and delivered to a destination outside Canada before Canadian use. Services, including app-store optimization and multilingual organic search localization agency services supplied to a non-resident corporation, generally qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VII if the recipient is a non-resident, the service is not supplied to an individual physically in Canada, and the service is not primarily in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The agency should obtain and retain a non-residency attestation (W-8BEN-E, certificate of incorporation, or foreign tax residency certificate), invoice the client as 'Zero-rated export under Schedule VII / Excise Tax Act', and document offshore consumption of the service. If a portion of the SEO/ASO service is delivered for Canadian-market app listings or to a Canadian resident publisher, the claimant must use a reasonable documented allocation between taxable Canadian supplies and zero-rated export supplies.

  • CBSA export declaration (B13A) or electronic export report transmitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System for physical exports of goods
  • Commercial invoice and service-level invoice identifying the Canadian supplier, non-resident buyer, HS/NAICS classification, description, value, and the line 'Zero-rated export under Schedule VI / Schedule VII, Excise Tax Act'
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or carrier proof of export showing destination outside Canada and continuous movement beyond Canada
  • Foreign-exchange remittance record from the non-resident client, such as SWIFT MT103, Wise B2B statement, Payoneer payout record, or bank inward remittance certificate
  • Non-residency evidence of the service recipient, including foreign incorporation certificate, overseas tax registration, or W-8BEN-E

Canada does not impose a federal real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-API clearance mandate for commercial invoices. A registered supplier must issue a GST/HST invoice containing the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act, including the supplier's legal name and BN-RT account, invoice date, recipient identification for taxable supplies above CAD 150, description, consideration, and GST/HST paid or payable, or a signed tax-included notice. Electronic invoices in PDF, XML, or EDI formats are accepted if legible and complete. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or EDI where required by CRA filing rules.

CRA may audit export zero-rating claims and require contemporaneous evidence of export, non-residency, service delivery, and payment. If the taxpayer cannot prove the supply was exported or supplied to a non-resident, CRA may reassess the supply as taxable, deny the corresponding input tax credits and refunds, and impose arrears interest from the original due date. Gross negligence or false statements under the Excise Tax Act can attract penalties of 25% of the tax amount with a statutory minimum, in addition to late-filing penalties and compound interest. Misrepresentation or fraud removes the normal four-year limitation period, allowing retrospective clawback beyond that window. Canada does not impose an express federal statutory foreign-currency repatriation deadline, but documented receipt and settlement of the export sale within 180 days is the operating benchmark used to sustain commercial substance and payment traceability; prolonged outstanding foreign receivables should be supported by enforceable export contracts, aging schedules, and collection correspondence. Cases referred to the CRA Criminal Investigations Program may result in prosecution, fines, or imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current account and capital account transactions. There are no central bank approvals required for profit repatriation, dividend remittances, or withdrawal of partner capital. The Canadian dollar is a free-floating currency; cross-border payments are subject only to general tax withholding rules, Investment Canada Act review thresholds for controlling acquisitions, and FINTRAC reporting obligations for large electronic funds transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio exist under Canadian federal law. Exporters and service providers, including ASO and SEO localization agencies, may retain foreign currency earnings in any bank account and are free to convert, hold, or remit such proceeds at their own commercial discretion. Documentary records must nonetheless be retained for income tax and transfer-pricing compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering supervision and reporting are administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational liquidity and low-float cross-border fee disbursements; Low for capital escrow, commercial letters of credit, or regulated clearing. Offshore digital financial institutions and electronic money institutions are not authorized to carry on deposit-taking business in Canada. Wise B2B and Payoneer may operate in Canada only through FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) structures or their Canadian bank partners; they are acceptable as supplementary payment rails for small recurring vendor payments but must never replace a Tier 1 Canadian bank account for capital contributions, statutory escrow, or large trade settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credit transfers to the limited partnership's Canadian operational account with full remitter legal name and address, the limited partnership's exact bank account title, and a clear payment narrative stating 'Limited partnership capital contribution' or 'Partner capital injection'. Purpose codes are not centrally enumerated by Canada, but banks require an economic-purpose statement; the bank's compliance desk will record sector code and transaction purpose. For amounts above CAD 10,000, FINTRAC reporting obligations apply to electronic funds transfers initiated by financial institutions, and source-of-funds verification must be available in every case. The partnership may not use fintech EMIs or Payoneer as the final destination for statutory capital contributions unless the entity is a functioning chartered bank.Outbound trade settlement is executed principally by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers from the Tier 1 Canadian bank to the beneficiary bank. For high-value or multi-shipment localization vendor engagements, commercial letters of credit may be issued under UCP 600 to guarantee payment against presentation of conforming documents. Domestic Canadian-dollar clearing for payroll and operational expenses occurs through the Lynx high-value payment system for wire amounts and through the Automated Clearing Settlement System for batch payments.
