Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — administered via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at www.canada.ca and the My Business Account platform; federal GST/HST accounts are program account 'RT' appended to the 9-digit Business Number (BN).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA. The general partner (resident or non-resident) applies on behalf of the LP via BRO or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number); CRA assigns the 9-digit BN and GST/HST program account 'RT' within 1–5 business days for online applications. The LP is a separate registrable person for GST/HST purposes by reason of subsection 273(1) of the Excise Tax Act (ETA). The small-supplier threshold of CAD 30,000 is evaluated on the LP's worldwide taxable supplies (exclusive of zero-rated exports and financial services) over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters and the current quarter; once exceeded, registration is mandatory within 29 days. Voluntarily registration is available immediately, enabling input tax credit recovery.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The LP is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax: no federal income tax is imposed at the partnership level; each limited or general partner includes its respective share of the LP's income or loss computed under the Income Tax Act. A non-resident partner is taxable under Part I only where the partnership income is attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment or a business carried on in Canada; certain passive Canadian-source payments (e.g., dividends from Canadian corporations, rental or royalty income) paid through the partnership may attract Part XIII withholding tax. The LP must file an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return but generally pays no federal income tax itself.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' classification regime. Available statutory elections for a Canadian LP: (1) subsection 273(2) ETA joint election to deem a partner's interest in the partnership to be disposed of/re-acquired for GST/HST registrant-status purposes — rarely used and subject to CRA acceptance; (2) section 156 ETA election between a registrant and a partner to treat certain supplies as made for nil consideration where the recipient is a registrant; (3) the Quick Method election under section 198 ETA for eligible small registrants (annual taxable supplies ≤ CAD 400,000) simplifying output tax remittance; (4) section 55.1 Income Tax Act election to have a partner's share of Canadian-source income computed as if the partnership were a separate person for treaty-eligibility purposes; (5) joint election under subsection 150(3) ITA for non-calendar fiscal period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — gross worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies and financial services) in the last four consecutive calendar quarters and in the current calendar quarter; mandatory registration within 29 days of ceasing to be a small supplier, with special aggregation rules for associated persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Registered agent and offshore secretarial services supplied by the Canadian LP to a non-resident client are zero-rated under section 9 of Schedule VI to the ETA provided: (a) the recipient is a non-resident at the time the service is supplied; (b) the recipient is not registered for GST/HST; and (c) the service is not supplied to or for a Canadian permanent establishment of the recipient and is not in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Corporate secretarial, registered office, and regulatory filing services are intangible services that generally satisfy these conditions. The LP must maintain written evidence establishing the recipient's non-resident status; CRA policy P-051R2 requires a contractual nexus abroad and a documented attestation of non-residency.

  • Signed commercial services agreement / engagement letter identifying the non-resident client and jurisdiction of residence
  • GST/HST tax invoice (electronic or paper) indicating zero-rated status and the LP's BN/RT account number
  • Proof of payment and inward remittance confirmation (SWIFT/MT103, credit card processor settlement) demonstrating offshore settlement
  • Non-resident status attestation certificate executed by the client, valid for audit retention purposes

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control regime exists in Canada. The CRA does not require API-based pre-authorisation, digital stamping, or government-side invoice clearance. However, each GST/HST registrant must issue tax invoices (domestic or simplified for taxable supplies ≤ CAD 150) reflecting the prescribed information under section 3 of the ETA, including the BN/RT number, date, description, and tax amounts. Electronic invoicing is permitted with the recipient's consent; CRA is progressively adopting the Peppol/Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework on a voluntary basis, expected to become the standard for B2B interoperability by 2026–2028, but no statutory mandate has been enacted at the federal level.

Canada imposes no specific statutory FX repatriation deadline for service exporters; the 180-day benchmark is adopted as a prudent internal treasury protocol. Where zero-rating is claimed incorrectly, the CRA may reassess within the normal 4-year limitation period under subsection 298(1) ETA, or indefinitely where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud (subsection 298(4) ETA). A false statement in a GST/HST return made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence triggers a penalty under subsection 285(1) ETA equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the amount by which the tax was understated. Erroneous refunds are clawed back with compound daily interest at the prescribed rate (section 280 ETA). Criminal prosecution may be pursued under section 327 ETA for intentional evasion, exposing the partnership and its general partner to fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency regime consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. Current account and capital account transactions are generally free from exchange controls. Dividends, partnership distributions, management fees, and other profit remittances to non-resident partners may be made without prior exchange-control approval, subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the federal Income Tax Act and applicable double-taxation treaty reductions. No exchange-control barriers exist for the repatriation of capital; however, financial institutions must report any incoming or outgoing electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion timeline. Exporters may retain foreign currency in approved accounts, choose their settlement currency, and convert to Canadian dollars freely. Export documentation for reporting purposes must be filed with Statistics Canada under the Trade Reporting System for the compilation of national statistics; no repatriation or conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Department of Finance Canada (exchange rate policy); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium as a standalone clearing rail; Medium-to-High as a supplementary FX and international payments channel. Offshore electronic money institutions are not authorized deposit-taking institutions in Canada and cannot operate as the primary settlement account for a Canadian Limited Partnership. An offshore EMI engaging with Canadian clients must be registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC and may require provincial licensing where it holds or moves Canadian-dollar funds. CAD settlement must ultimately occur through a Canadian Schedule I bank or a bank authorized under Canadian clearing rules; EMI balances do not constitute Canadian payment-system clearing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax rate for ordinary active business income after the General Rate Reduction; the Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and is not an entity-level Part I taxpayer, so its income is allocated to and taxed in the hands of resident and non-resident partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents, subject to reduction under an applicable DTA. A Limited Partnership does not pay dividends; distributions to partners are allocations of income or return of capital and are not subject to separate dividend withholding tax, though partners with Canadian business income are taxed under Part I or Part XIII depending on the character of the payment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 - aggregate value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with a non-resident in a taxation year triggers mandatory Form T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation; CbC notification applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue equal to or above CAD 1.1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime applicable to all resident companies, but the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, the permanent-establishment concept in income tax treaties, and the OECD Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test. A Canadian Limited Partnership providing registered-agent and offshore-secretarial services must demonstrate genuine local decision-making, physical premises, qualified staff, books and records, and active client-service operations; a bare nominee arrangement or a flow-through conduit distributing profits to a low-tax treaty jurisdiction without economic substance invites GAAR recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and transfer-pricing adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment with interest; gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of understated tax; criminal tax evasion penalties of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment for up to five years on indictment; perjury sanctions for false statutory declarations.

Director personal liability: General partners, and directors of a corporate general partner, are personally assessed for unpaid taxes and source deductions, have corporate veils pierced where the LP is a sham or conduit, and may be subjected to civil restitution, asset seizure, court-imposed travel restrictions, and imprisonment for criminal evasion.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income to the arm's-length price under section 247; a 10% transfer-pricing penalty applies to net adjustments where adequate contemporaneous documentation is absent; 50% gross-negligence penalties, interest, and reassessment beyond the normal period are also available.

Director personal liability: Corporate general-partner directors may be assessed personally for the LP's unpaid Part I tax and penalties where they directed or knowingly participated in a sham TP arrangement; professional disciplinary action, personal bankruptcy exposure, criminal prosecution, and travel bans are possible in serious cases.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR disallowance under section 245; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; reassessment with arrears interest and penalties; administrative strike-off of the partnership registration; CRA could also require personal guarantees and post security for tax liabilities.

Director personal liability: Limited partners who take part in the control and management of the business lose limited-liability protection and become jointly and severally liable for partnership debts; directors may incur breach-of-duty claims and personal liability for taxes if the arrangement is a sham.
AML and Unlicensed Corporate Secretarial Activity

Statutory sanction: Provincial regulatory fines and licensing revocation; FINTRAC administrative penalties of up to CAD 2,000,000 for large businesses and CAD 500,000 for specified persons; conviction for money laundering under the Criminal Code carries imprisonment for up to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly direct or acquiesce in AML violations are personally chargeable with offenses; FINTRAC may name individuals in compliance orders, regulatory bodies may disbar professional agents, and personal property can be subject to restraint and confiscation.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Non-Resident Taxes

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full unremitted withholding tax plus compound daily interest; late-withholding penalties of 10-20% of the unremitted amount; Form T1042 filing penalties and criminal prosecution for deliberate non-remittance.

Director personal liability: Directors of a withholding corporate partner or corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted amounts under section 227.1; CRA can certify the debt, register liens on personal assets, impose travel restrictions through court processes, and pursue civil recovery against their estates.
