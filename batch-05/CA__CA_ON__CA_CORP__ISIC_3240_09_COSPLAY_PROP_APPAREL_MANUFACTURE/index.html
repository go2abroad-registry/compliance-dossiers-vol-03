Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO), GST/HST NETFILE, and T2 corporate income tax e-filing; Ontario Ministry of Finance for any provincial non-HST levies such as Ontario Employer Health Tax (EHT).

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian or foreign-owned Ontario business corporation: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA); (2) obtain a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1; (3) where the entity is a non-resident carrying on business in Canada, register for GST/HST using the non-resident registration route, with supporting documentation such as articles of incorporation, a Canadian permanent establishment address, a resident agent/attorney for service, and evidence of Canadian business activities; (4) if only making exports from Canada without a Canadian establishment, a non-resident may still register voluntarily to claim zero-rated treatment and input tax credits, using Form RC1 and CRA's International Tax Services Office; (5) Ontario corporate income tax is harmonously administered by CRA through the T2 corporate return, and no separate Ontario provincial tax account is required for income tax purposes except for EHT/payroll accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election. Corporate tax classification follows incorporation/residency rules under the Income Tax Act (ITA). A Canadian-resident corporation is taxable on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation is taxable only on Canadian-source business income and gains. Available statutory elections include the GST/HST election for closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; the ITA s.216 election for non-resident owners of Canadian real estate; and the simplified GST/HST filing option for qualifying non-resident registrants. Ontario corporate income tax is calculated on the federal T2 return through the Ontario lines, with no separate provincial entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies; this threshold applies to GST/HST registrants, including non-resident distance sellers and purveyors of imported digital services under Excise Tax Act s.240 and related provisions. Export supplies that are zero-rated do not count toward the threshold unless they are the only supplies and the registrant later seeks input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act s.6 and Schedule VI, supplies of tangible personal property (including cosplay costumes, theatrical robes, and props) are zero-rated if the supplier exports the goods from Canada, delivers them to a consignee outside Canada, or ships them by a common carrier or mail to an address outside Canada within the prescribed documentary timeframe. Services such as manufacturing or finishing of costumes are zero-rated where the service is performed wholly outside Canada; if partly performed inside Canada, the zero rate may still apply if the benefit of the service accrues exclusively to a non-resident client and the supply does not relate to real property in Canada. The exporter must hold contemporaneous commercial evidence that sufficient outbound movement occurred, and the export must be a genuine commercial disposition made to an arm's-length or documented non-resident party.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed with CBSA (ACI/eManifest or Form B13A where required)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / carrier waybill showing destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of receipt of funds from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract covering the exported goods
  • Packing list and weight/measurement certificate
  • Freight or courier manifest, with date of shipment and consignee address
  • Foreign customs entry or import documentation evidencing arrival abroad
  • Carrier proof of pick-up and any tracking records

Canada does not maintain a mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance regime or digital fiscal stamping for GST/HST. Registrants must retain electronic records and issue prescribed tax invoices. The CRA mandates electronic filing for GST/HST/HST returns for registrants with annual taxable revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million via GST/HST NETFILE, E-file GST/HST, or commercial accounting software APIs. For customs export reporting, CBSA requires electronic export declarations under the Customs Act through ACI eManifest or approved service providers; no fiscal stamp is affixed to exports.

The CRA may deny a zero-rated treatment and claw back any refund or input tax credit claimed on the export if the documentation is inadequate, the goods are subsequently returned to Canada without proper accounting, or the sale is found to be fictitious. Penalties include the reassessment of unpaid GST/HST plus interest, administrative penalties for false statements or omissions (potentially 50% of the net tax amount where gross negligence applies), and criminal penalties for knowing tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act, which may include fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Export documentation must be retained for at least six years, and CRA can pursue extended reassessment windows where misrepresentation is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada imposes no exchange controls or currency convertibility restrictions on any current or capital account transaction. The Canadian dollar floats freely, and non-resident shareholders may repatriate dividends, royalties, interest, and management fees without prior foreign-exchange validation. Statutory withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (25%, typically reduced to 5–15% under an applicable tax treaty) applies to outbound passive income, and payments must be reported through CRA Form NR4; however, no exchange control permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain receivables in any foreign currency. However, FINTRAC reporting applies to all international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and full transfer records must be retained for a minimum of five years under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Foreign Exchange Administration is not a distinct entity in Canada—foreign exchange regulatory oversight is shared by FINTRAC (anti-money laundering and terrorist financing intelligence) and OSFI (prudential supervision of banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (supplementary cross-border payments) / Low (primary operating bank). Legal Constraints: Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex are not Canadian chartered banks and cannot accept deposits or issue Canadian banking licences. To the extent they carry out payment services in Canada, they must register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, appoint a compliance officer, and report suspicious transactions. They may be used as auxiliary rails for low-value disbursements, employee reimbursements, or small trade settlements, but all principal liquidity must remain in a Canadian Schedule I bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is through SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the Ontario corporation's operational account. The wire must include the beneficiary legal name, the Canadian bank institution number (e.g., 0003 for RBC), transit/branch number, and account number. The narrative field must clearly state 'Capital Injection – Share Subscription' or 'Carried Interest / Working Capital Loan' plus the remitter's legal identity. For USD-to-CAD conversion, the receiving bank's FX desk executes the exchange on a spot basis using a rate locked by the corporate FX dealer. A board resolution authorising the share issuance and any shareholder loan agreement must be filed in the corporate records; no exchange-control approval is required, but FINTRAC records threshold of CAD 10,000 per international wire transfer applies.Trade settlements for raw materials and third-party manufacturing are cleared via (a) SWIFT MT103 open-account transfers for routine vendor payments and (b) commercial documentary letters of credit (sight or usance) for high-value purchases from overseas textile and wig-component suppliers. For e-commerce retail disbursements, funds are aggregated and settled by card acquirers (Stripe/Shopify Payments) through network rails onto the Canadian operating account.
