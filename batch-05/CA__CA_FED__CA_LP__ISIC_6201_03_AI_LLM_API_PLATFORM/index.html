Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permitted for the sole purpose of maintaining a registered address and receiving service of process in the province of registration (Ontario or Alberta), provided that a physical street address is held by the registered agent in the province and the agent is a licensed service corporation. For the AI LLM API platform, however, CRA and counterparty due diligence (Stripe, USDC exchange partners, and enterprise customers) will demand genuine substance: a Canadian bank account under the LP's own name, Canadian payroll and a resident operational manager, and a documented decision-making seat in Canada. Use of a pure virtual office without employees or physical presence risks: (i) denial of LP pass-through treatment for non-resident partners under the Income Tax Act and applicable DTAs; (ii) the 'anti-avoidance' rule under s 245 (GAAR); and (iii) classification as a 'managed services entity' with imputed GST/HST obligations. Recommendation: obtain a serviced-office lease (minimum 12 months) in Toronto or Calgary with dedicated meeting room and mail handling.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal GST/HST and Income Tax registration administered through Business Registration Online (BRO); portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign or Canadian Limited Partnership obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) by registering online through CRA Business Registration Online or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) to CRA. The LP must then add a GST/HST program account (RT prefix) if it is not a small supplier or chooses to register voluntarily. Non-resident registrants may use the simplified online GST/HST registration procedure and must file electronically. CRA issues the BN and RT account without requiring a business licence or minimum capital.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes; profits and losses flow through to partners. Non-resident partners are taxable only on allocated Canadian-source income, subject to applicable tax treaty relief. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is treated as a separate person and must register, charge, file, and remit GST/HST in its own BN.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime; LP pass-through treatment is automatic. A partnership may select a fiscal period subject to the Income Tax Act rules. A non-resident supplier of specified digital services (e.g., cloud-based AI/LLM API services) may elect to use the simplified GST/HST registration and remittance procedure. Hybrid mismatch rules apply as automatic anti-avoidance adjustments; there is no elective hybrid mismatch regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies in any four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). Once exceeded, the LP must register for GST/HST; non-resident suppliers of specified digital services may voluntarily register under a simplified registration procedure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered LP may zero-rate an export supply only if the supply is a zero-rated supply under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Goods must be exported from Canada and documentary proof of export must be retained. AI/LLM API services supplied to a non-resident recipient are generally zero-rated as exported services under Part V if the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is not an excluded service (e.g., services relating to Canadian real property or certain services performed in Canada). The LP must collect and retain evidence of the non-resident recipient's identity, residence, billing address, IP/usage location, payment source, and the commercial contract.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration / Canada Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (air waybill, sea waybill, or freight manifest)
  • Commercial invoice and supply contract evidencing non-resident recipient and agreed terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 payment confirmation evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • GST/HST zero-rated export substantiation file (non-resident certificate, IP/billing evidence for digital AI API services, technical usage logs)

No national real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoice API, or digital stamping regime in Canada. Registrants must issue tax invoices satisfying the Excise Tax Act. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for non-resident registrants and for registrants with annual taxable supplies above CRA thresholds. CRA does not operate a mandatory pre-clearance e-invoice API; tax software may file returns via GST/HST NETFILE or compatible EDI.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, extended to seven years in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. If export zero-rating is not substantiated, the claimed refund or credit is clawed back and net tax is reassessed with interest. Penalties include the 25% false statement or omission penalty, the 50% gross negligence penalty, late-filing/remittance penalties, and possible criminal prosecution for fraudulent export claims. Records substantiating zero-rated exports must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) operates under a free-floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility; the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act impose no exchange controls, prior approvals, or forced conversion obligations on cross-border capital movements. Partnership capital injections, profit distributions, shareholder loans, and trade remittances to non-residents are legally unrestricted. The sole tax friction is Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), which imposes a statutory 25% withholding tax on certain payments to non-residents (treaty-reduced, e.g., 5% portfolio dividend rate under the Canada-US Treaty); partnership distributions are generally non-withholdable when the LP is transparent. The Investment Canada Act applies only to acquisitions of control of Canadian businesses, not to ordinary treasury flows. All cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender or mandatory repatriation requirement for export proceeds. The CA_LP may freely retain, disburse, or convert export receipts denominated in USD, EUR, USDC, or other currencies without statutory timeline or conversion-ratio constraints; exporters may maintain proprietary foreign-currency accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole monetary and central clearing authority; prudential bank supervision is administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/CFT supervision is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and payment systems oversight is shared with Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Since September 8, 2024, payment service providers (PSPs) must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and the Retail Payment Activities Regulations; any offshore EMI with a Canadian nexus, solicitation of Canadian clients, or payment processing for Canadians is subject to PSP registration, safeguarding rules, and FINTRAC money services business (MSB) registration. A fully offshore reverse-solicitation model is legally possible but operationally fragile and generally rejected by better correspondent banks. Therefore, the CA_LP treasury spine must be a Schedule I Canadian bank; EMIs and merchant acquirers such as Stripe settle card payouts via affiliated Canadian PSP/EFT rails, while USDC conversions must run through a registered Canadian crypto dealer or restricted-access wallet policy before fiat entry.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank via Fedwire/CHIPS for USD or Target2/EBA for EUR, with OECD/ISO 20022 purpose codes ('EQUI' for equity injection, 'INTC' for intercompany loan) and beneficiary field 59 exactly matching the registered LP account name; incoming CAD instructions settle through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value payment system with final settlement at the Bank of Canada. Beneficiary-bank compliance verification requires the capital contribution to align with the signed partnership agreement, and mandatory FINTRAC reporting applies to any single wire of CAD 10,000 or more. USDC capital injections must be converted via a registered Canadian crypto dealer or an approved fiat off-ramp prior to domestic settlement; raw USDC transfers into the bank account are not accepted by Schedule I banks without an authorized dealer intermediary.Trade settlements and treasury disbursements use: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments, intercompany cash concentration, and licensing fees, with contract/invoice references in field 70; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) confirmed by the Canadian issuing bank for procurement of GPU clusters, colocation infrastructure, and bandwidth services; (3) Payments Canada rails for domestic flows — Lynx for high-value/time-critical CAD, Real-Time Rail (RTR) for instant low-value disbursements, and EFT/ACH for batch vendor and payroll runs; (4) USD outflows via CHIPS and Fedwire for North American settlements.
