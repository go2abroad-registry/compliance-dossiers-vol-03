Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — GST/HST administration, federal tax registrations, and filing at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; equivalent provincial competence retained by Revenu Québec for QST where Québec nexus exists.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Constitute the Limited Partnership under applicable provincial Limited Partnership legislation (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16) and obtain provincial registry acknowledgement — no federal partnership charter exists. Step 2: Apply to CRA for a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or the BRO portal; the LP is identified as the legal person for tax purposes, not the general partner. Step 3: Open the GST/HST program account (two-letter suffix 'RT') by filing Form RC1 or using BRO, electing the appropriate reporting period and fiscal year. Step 4: Where the LP is a non-resident supplier of specified digital services (e.g., MMORPG subscriptions, in-app virtual items) to Canadian recipients, register under the expanded July 1, 2021 digital services GST/HST regime (zero registration threshold, simplified non-resident registration path). Step 5: File periodic GST/HST returns (Form GST34) electronically via GST/HST NETFILE upon registration; remit net tax. Step 6: For any Quebec substantive presence, separately register with Revenu Québec for QST and obtain a Québec Enterprise Number (NEQ).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Income Tax Act (Canada): a Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; no partner-level election to tax the entity at partnership level exists (no Canadian check-the-box equivalent). The mandatory look-through rule (MLTR), introduced by S.C. 2017, c. 33 and codified in proposed amendments to ss. 96, 97.1, 124.2, and related hybrid mismatch provisions, overrides the separate-entity treatment for specified cross-border partnership arrangements, preventing double non-taxation. GST/HST: under Excise Tax Act, s. 156(2), a closely related corporation and partnership may jointly elect to have supplies deemed made for nil consideration, subject to the financial services carve-out. No provincial tax classification election is available for LPs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold applicable to a Canadian-resident partnership (mandatory registration once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters). Non-resident suppliers of specified digital services to Canadian customers face a zero threshold and must register effective July 1, 2021, irrespective of revenue volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 (services supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, excluding services in respect of Canadian real property, tangible personal property ordinarily situated in Canada, or services rendered to a person while physically present in Canada) and s. 23 (supply of intangible personal property, including software licenses and virtual in-game items, to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada). MMORPG digital subscriptions, downloadable content, and in-app purchases delivered to non-Canadian players therefore qualify for 0% GST/HST. Where Apple/Google act as merchant of record or payment facilitator, the LP must maintain platform payout statements and jurisdiction-of-sale evidence to substantiate the non-resident status of the underlying end consumer. Physical premium merchandise exported from Canada would also qualify as zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 1–5.

  • B13A Export Declaration / CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) report — mandatory for physical shipments valued above CAD 2,000; not applicable to cloud-delivered digital MMORPG services
  • Customs Commercial Invoice (consignor/consignee, HS code, value, Incoterms) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing movement of physical premium merchandise
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent Canadian bank FX credit statement evidencing cross-border settlement proceeds
  • Apple App Store Financial Reports and Google Play Payments Profile payout statements demonstrating non-Canadian customer jurisdiction of sale
  • Non-resident customer proof package (IP geolocation logs, billing address records, platform storefront jurisdiction, and end-user country attribution data)

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoice clearance mandates (no analogue of French E-invoicing, Italian SDI, or Polish KSeF). GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via CRA's GST/HST NETFILE if annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5M or where the registrant is non-resident. Use of PEPPOL, EDI, or custom XML e-invoices is voluntary; CRA mandates complete books and records retention for six years and accepts electronic substantiation provided a reliable audit trail exists.

CRA national verification teams scrutinize zero-rated digital supply claims for inadequate non-residency documentation; absent sufficient export evidence, the LP is reassessed at the applicable GST/HST rate (5% federal or up to 15% HST in participating provinces) on unreported revenue. Interest on assessed tax is charged under s. 280 of the Excise Tax Act; arrears penalties apply under s. 280(1); gross negligence penalties of 25% of the tax amount apply under s. 285 where false statements or omissions are made knowingly; criminal prosecution for evasion is available under s. 327 ETA. Input tax credit refunds claimed against development and operating costs require export documentation preserved for six years (indefinite in cases of misrepresentation). Retroactive clawback risk and administrative monetary penalties arise where zero-rating is disallowed and the platform reporting trails do not map cleanly to non-resident customers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no foreign-exchange controls. Limited partnership profits and capital distributions to non-resident limited partners may be freely remitted, subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) where applicable and to any applicable income tax treaty relief. Funds derived from global MMORPG in-app purchases are repatriable through normal banking channels without prior central bank approval, but source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership documentation must satisfy the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and FINTRAC regulatory requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists in Canada. Exporters are not required to repatriate, convert, or deposit foreign currency with a designated bank within any mandated timeline. The CA Limited Partnership may retain Apple App Store and Google Play settlement funds in USD or convert to CAD at commercial market rates. Transactions at or above C$10,000 and any suspicious cross-border financial movements are reportable to FINTRAC, and payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions or persons are prohibited under Canadian sanctions law.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Foreign exchange administration in Canada is decentralized; there is no separate exchange-control administration. Registered banks are prudentially supervised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/CFT obligations. Global Affairs Canada administers economic sanctions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability with legal constraints. Fintech electronic money institutions and virtual IBAN providers are useful for cross-border collections in non-CAD currencies but are not substitutes for a Canadian chartered bank account. They generally are not members of Payments Canada and cannot settle through the Large Value Transfer System or Real-Time Rail. For the CA Limited Partnership, an EMI may be used as a pre-funding or payout aggregation tool for European or Asia-Pacific app-store receipts, provided the EMI is licensed and regulated in its home jurisdiction and does not violate Canadian money-services business registration rules. The primary banking and liquidity backbone must remain a Canadian Schedule I bank to ensure FINTRAC-compliant reporting, CAD payment system access, and regulator confidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting investor's bank to the CA Limited Partnership's operational or capital escrow account at a Canadian chartered bank. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of reportable transactions with each non-resident related party (Form T106 trigger and Local File expectation); CAD 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers Form RC4649 (Country-by-Country Report). CRA contemporaneous documentation must be prepared, dated, and retained for at least 6 years. For a global MMORPG with IP-holding foreign parents, a Local File addressing royalty/IAP allocations is mandatory at the above threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance is a determinative factor of: (i) GAAR under s. 245 (penalty up to 25% of the denied benefit); (ii) treaty entitlement under the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test; (iii) the sham/association doctrine; and (iv) the deductibility and allocation of partnership losses (ss. 96(2.1)–(2.6) and 143.2 disallow losses beyond at-risk capital). A Canadian LP that only streamlines IAP revenues through Canada, without employees, servers, development activity, or decision-making functions, is vulnerable to recharacterization and treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: On indictment: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 2 years less a day (ITA s. 239(1)). The CRA also imposes civil gross-negligence penalties of 50% (s. 163(2)) and interest at the prescribed rate

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and senior employees who directed, authorized, or participated in the evasion are liable as parties to the offence. For a corporate general partner of the LP, directors are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted payroll/GST-HST source deductions; criminal conviction may trigger Canadian travel bans, arrest warrants, and extradition proceedings for cross-border principals
Gross Negligence / Careless Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax or the amount of any refund/credit claimed, under s. 163(2); in egregious cases the CRA may wire-tap under judicial authorization and pursue criminal tax evasion

Director personal liability: Though the s. 163(2) penalty is assessed on the taxpayer, directors who personally prepare or sign knowingly false schedules are exposed to director-level assessments, professional misconduct, and facilitation charges; corporate veil piercing is available where the LP corporate general partner is a mere puppet
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to the arm's-length amount plus a s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (no penalty is imposed if the taxpayer has made reasonable efforts to document the arm's-length results); gross adjustments also increase taxable income and foreign accrual property income exposure

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the T106/T2 or direct the pricing policy may be assessed for negligent misstatement if the CRA establishes personal involvement; repeated TP penalties increase the risk of criminal prosecution for conspiracy to evade taxes
Artificial Shell Company / Conduit LP

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) denial of the tax benefit, reduced treaty relief under the MLI Principal Purpose Test, and a 25% penalty on the denied benefit; the CRA may also apply the 'sham transaction' doctrine for retroactive allocations, voiding the LP's allocations; the provincial LP registrar may dissolve an offending limited partnership

Director personal liability: General partners (individual or failing corporate) are personally liable for the partnership's debts; directors who cause or authorize a shell arrangement may face joint and several liability for the assessment, professional disqualification, and cross-border enforcement of civil judgments
AML / FINTRAC Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) up to CAD 1,000,000 per contravention under the PCMLTFA; criminal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years for obstruction or unregistered MSB activity; designated business reviews can shut down payment channels

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally penalized where the LP is the beneficial owner of the MSB business; FINTRAC can levy AMPs on individuals responsible for the compliance program, and serious AML failures expose directors to criminal liability and travel restrictions while awaiting trial
Unremitted Withholding and Non-Resident Compliance

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable under s. 215 to pay the full amount of tax that should have been withheld, plus interest and penalties; repeated omissions trigger s. 227(8) prosecution; director liability attaches automatically for unremitted amounts

Director personal liability: s. 227.1 imposes strict (negligence-excused) personal liability on directors for unremitted payroll, GST/HST, and Part XIII withholding amounts of the corporation even if the LP itself is insolvent; failure to exercise due diligence is the only defense, leading to personal bank asset seizure and credit impairment
