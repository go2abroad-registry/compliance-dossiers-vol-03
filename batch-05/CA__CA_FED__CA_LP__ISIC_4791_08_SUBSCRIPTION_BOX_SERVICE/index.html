Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); operational registrations are processed via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and managed through My Business Account (MyBA) at www.canada.ca. Cross-border goods reporting is administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

The Canadian Limited Partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent for income tax, but must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) before commencing commercial activity. Procedure: (1) Complete provincial registration under the relevant Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Ontario or British Columbia) using the required provincial form and pay the statutory filing fee; the provincial registration certificate is a precondition for federal BN issuance. (2) Apply for the BN through the CRA Business Registration Online portal or by mailing Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number'; select the 'RP' program account (partnership) for T5013 Partnership Information Return filing and the 'RN' program account for GST/HST registration; if customs transactions are anticipated, also request the 'RD' import/export account. (3) Provide the LP's legal name, jurisdiction of formation, Canadian head office address, general partner legal identities and residential addresses, and particulars of non-resident limited partners; the CRA assigns the BN effective on the earliest date the LP commenced business activity. (4) The LP must file T5013 Summary and T5013 slips by March 31 following each calendar-year end, and remit any GST/HST collected via NETFILE/MyBA. (5) Non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII withholding at 25% on distributions attributable to Canadian-source business income, unless reduced by treaty; the LP must obtain and file the treaty-based exemption documentation (NR301/NR302) where applicable. (6) For payment-rail onboarding, the BN and GST/HST registration number must be furnished to Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net as the tax identifier for invoicing and recurring charges.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under ITA s.96, the LP is not a taxpayer for federal income tax purposes; income is allocated to partners according to the partnership agreement. Territorial scope: Canadian-source business income is allocated; non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on their share of Canadian-source partnership income only. For GST/HST, the LP is a separate 'person' under ETA s.123(1)(b) and must register in its own right once the registration threshold is exceeded. A non-resident corporate limited partner is statutorily deemed under ITA s.94.1 to carry on the partnership's Canadian business through a permanent establishment in Canada. No federal tax-exempt zone applies; however, the LP may benefit from provincial investment credits where applicable.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification regime; the LP is statutorily transparent with limited statutory elections. Available mechanisms: (1) Alternative fiscal period election under ITA s.249.1(4), available to a Canadian-resident partnership and subject to the income-testing conditions; (2) GST/HST 'Quick Method' accounting election under ETA s.227, available where annual worldwide taxable supplies do not exceed C$400,000, allowing simplified net-tax computation; (3) Annual GST/HST remittance election under ETA s.259 for qualifying small businesses with taxable supplies below C$1,500,000; (4) GST/HST rebate-streaming elections applicable where the LP has a corporate general partner and both are registrants, subject to the closely-related rules of ETA s.273 which generally apply only to corporations; (5) Hybrid-mismatch review under ITA s.18.4 where a foreign jurisdiction classifies the Canadian LP as opaque, potentially triggering the denial of deduction for hybrid dividends or requiring an ITA s.94.1 deemed-PE inclusion for a non-resident corporate limited partner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 (gross worldwide taxable supplies of the LP in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current quarter; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration applies. Non-resident suppliers of specified digital products or qualifying goods to Canadian recipients are required to register without regard to this threshold under ETA ss.240.1–242.1, and operators of distribution platforms are deemed to make the underlying supply and must collect GST/HST on qualifying sales made through the platform).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: A supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.1 if the supplier ships or delivers the property to a destination outside Canada and the property is exported as part of the transaction. For the subscription-box service, each box shipped from Canada to a foreign address qualifies for 0% GST/HST, provided export occurs shortly after the supply and the LP continuously maintains export evidence. Services: services supplied to a non-resident (not registered for GST/HST) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.7, unless the service relates to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance or is performed to an individual present in Canada; subscription-management and logistics services rendered to the foreign customer generally qualify. While Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark in this dossier is applied as the administrative reference for aligning remittance evidence with the export documentation bundle during CRA audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible without prior approvals on both current-account transactions (subscription receivables, supplier payments) and capital-account transactions (capital contributions, profit distributions, loan guarantees). No surrender, repatriation, or financing restriction applies to the subscription-box operating model. The only material regulatory encumbrances are tax-related: Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding at a statutory rate of 25% (treaty-reduced to 15% under most applicable DTCs) on distributions or deemed remittances to non-resident partners, and Investment Canada Act notification/review obligations for non-resident capital formation. Profit remittance is therefore an income-tax and Canada Revenue Agency compliance matter, not an FX-control hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Exchange Rate Regulations and the Bank of Canada Act impose no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts. Cross-border subscription-box export proceeds may be retained in USD or any freely convertible currency indefinitely. The only mandatory statutory reporting requirements are CBSA export declarations (B13A/CBER) for goods above the applicable threshold, FINTRAC large-transaction and electronic-funds-transfer reporting for amounts at or above CAD 10,000, and annual CRA information returns (e.g., T106, T1134) where triggered. There is no mandated conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; foreign-exchange policy is coordinated by the Department of Finance, prudential bank supervision is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/CFT compliance is supervised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada operates under the Currency Act and the Exchange Rate Regulations with no foreign-exchange licensing or approval body; the Bank of Canada additionally regulates retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High for ancillary channels only; not a substitute for a Schedule I bank account. Non-resident electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise for Business, Airwallex, CurrencyCloud, and Corpay can support supplier FX, USD-denominated virtual accounts, and high-volume micro-disbursements, but they are subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) if they execute payment functions or hold customer funds for Canadian merchants, requiring registration with the Bank of Canada. The Limited Partnership should use an EMI strictly as a sub-ledger for vendor payouts and maintain the primary treasury and settlement accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank to preserve OSFI/FINTRAC compliance and ensure clean integration with PSP rails (Stripe Billing, Authorize.Net).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital injections route by SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the Limited Partnership's Canadian Schedule I bank account using the institution/transit numbers and BIC, with the payment reference identifying the specific capital call number and the entity's CRA business number. Domestic CAD transfers above CAD 500,000 should clear through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS, Canada's RTGS); smaller amounts settle through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) via Payments Canada. Each deposit must be reconciled to a signed capital call notice, the notarised partnership agreement, and investor identification; FINTRAC requires the receiving bank to report any incoming wire of CAD 10,000 or more, and the treasury function must be able to deliver the underlying capital call and partner KYC pack within 24 hours of request. Domestic CAD vendor payments settle through ACSS or LVTS. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High for ancillary channels only; not a substitute for a Schedule I bank account.
