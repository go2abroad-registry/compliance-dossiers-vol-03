Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html, including the 'My Business Account' and 'GST/HST NETFILE' online channels. Customs administration is vested in the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), which operates the Single Window Initiative, marine eManifest, and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

There is no automatic TIN for foreign entities. A Canadian limited partnership and each non-resident corporate partner must apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). Since Canada has no federal limited partnership statute, the CA_LP is constituted under provincial partnership law; the general partner then files RC1 in the partnership's legal name and requests the GST/HST program account code 'RT'. Non-resident corporate limited partners must hold their own BN with program account code 'RC' for corporate income tax, and must file T2 returns and Part XIV branch tax returns under ITA s. 219 where they carry on business through a Canadian permanent establishment. The BN is issued by the CRA (typically within 5-10 business days) and must appear on all GST/HST invoices and on CBSA export declarations. An ocean Ro-Ro carrier must further register with CBSA as an importer/exporter to transmit marine eManifest outward cargo data and CERS export filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada operates no check-the-box election; ITA s. 96 makes the LP fiscally transparent by default. The LP files only the T5013 Partnership Information Return, and each partner is taxed on its allocation. Relevant federal elections and regimes: (i) an ITA s. 103(1) allocation agreement supporting reasonable non-pro-rata income allocations among partners; (ii) the ETA s. 156 closely-related-corporations election, available within a corporate group comprising the LP's corporate general partner and ship-management affiliates (the LP itself is ineligible as it is not a corporation); (iii) voluntary GST/HST registration election for a small-supplier LP to recover input tax credits; (iv) the ETA s. 273 joint-venture election, which permits a qualifying operator to account for GST/HST where the Ro-Ro operation is conducted through a joint venture involving the LP; (v) automatic Part XIV branch-tax compliance for non-resident corporate partners, which is not elective. Hybrid mismatch: draft federal hybrid-mismatch rules implementing BEPS Action 2 (2021-2022) neutralize deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes involving hybrid entities, including a foreign limited partner fiscally transparent elsewhere; these rules are self-assessing and do not permit the LP to elect entity-level taxation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. No exchange-control approval is required for inbound capital, profit repatriation, or loan servicing. Non-resident limited partners may remit capital freely; however, distributions to non-resident partners that carry Canadian-source income are subject to Part XIII withholding under the Income Tax Act (Canada), and all electronic fund transfers exceeding C$10,000 must be reported by the receiving Canadian bank to FINTRAC. No material profit-remittance hurdles exist other than the standard tax withholding and CRS/FATCA information reports.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements. Ro-Ro freight income may be retained in USD or any freely convertible currency. For Canadian tax purposes, foreign-currency amounts must be translated into CAD using the Bank of Canada daily noon exchange rate at each fiscal reporting date. FINTRAC requires a Cross-Border Currency or Monetary Instruments Report for physical movement of equivalent funds of C$10,000 or more, and ongoing electronic-fund-transfer reporting triggers at C$10,000 per transaction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with banking prudential oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and utility anti-money-laundering enforcement by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-to-Medium for primary treasury in this shipping LP. Canadian-regulated fintech payment firms (including subsidiaries of global EMIs) may operate as money-services businesses registered with FINTRAC and, as of 2024-2025, must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada. Such platforms can support ancillary FX conversion and pooled multi-currency collections, but they cannot issue or did not confirm documentary letters of credit, provide voyage finance, or act as the LP's primary clearing bank with a Canadian LVTS settlement account. The LP should use a Schedule I bank as the core treasury rail and may attach an EMI only for niche corridor collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners remit capital contributions via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's cash concentration account at a Schedule I Canadian bank. The legal purpose codes must be stated as 'G100 Capital Contribution' or, where capital is injected as a shareholder loan, 'G104 Cash Injection'. The Canadian receiving bank automatically files an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC for any single remittance equal to or exceeding C$10,000. The bank's FX desk will convert USD to CAD under a standing instruction only when the partnership agreement requires CAD funding; otherwise the LP retains a USD leg. The remittance must be matched against a capital-call notice and the limited partner's contribution schedule in the partnership agreement to avoid a statutory deemed-partnership issue. CRS/FATCA wrappers no further Treasury approval.Operating disbursements consisting of port fees, stevedoring, bunker purchases, canal tolls, agent commissions, and crew wages are cleared by corporate wire, i.e., SWIFT MT103 or, for large interbank settlements, MT202 through the Canadian bank's correspondent. Documentary trade settlements are supported by irrevocable confirmed Letters of Credit in SWIFT MT700/MT710 form, with the shipowner's bank as confirming bank. For charter performance and freight protection, the LP may issue or receive standby LCs using SWIFT MT760. CAD domestic clearing occurs through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); USD clearing runs over SWIFT/CHIPS. Box 2 – Business Income Active business income, including the corporate partner's share of LP income tax adjusted for items like CCA recapture and foreign exchange. Non-resident corporate partner must file if it carries on business in Canada through the LP (not merely passive investment). Late filing penalty on T2: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month to 12 months; repeat failure penalty; non-filing penalty of C$100 per month late up to C$5,000.
Box 368 – Foreign Tax Credit Claim credit for foreign source taxes paid by the partner's foreign jurisdictions. If the corporate partner is resident in a treaty country, claim the treaty rate and foreign tax credit; attach a completed Form T2SCH7. Late filing penalty on T2: 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per full month to 12 months; repeat failure penalty; non-filing penalty of C$100 per month late up to C$5,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the substance-over-form doctrine, GAAR under ITA s.245, and the concept of 'business of the partnership' under s.102 all require a genuine commercial purpose. A Ro-Ro LP must demonstrate real decision-making, crew management, vessel operation, and revenue accounting in Canada (or through a Canadian place of business) to avoid treaty shopping and sham characterization by the CRA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ITA s.238: fine of 50%-200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalty under s.163(2) up to 50% of the understated tax; indefinite reassessment period (no limitation) for wilful misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unpaid partnership taxes to the extent of assets; insurance indemnities do not protect against criminal fraud. Convicted officers face travel bans, extradition risk, and disqualification from serving as corporate directors.
Abusive Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: CRA will disregard the LP under substance-over-form and GAAR; all income reattributed directly to the foreign shareholder, with a 100% gross-up and Part XIII withholding if earlier distributions are re-characterized as deemed dividends. Penalty of 25% plus gross negligence may apply.

Director personal liability: Limited partners who participate in the scheme may lose limited liability protection and be personally assessed; officers may be subject to personal sanctions under CBCA for breach of fiduciary duty to maintain proper records and real operations.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA adjustment under s.247(2) to arm's length price; s.247(3) penalty equal to 10% of net upward adjustment if the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts. Gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) can add a further 50% if documentation is fabricated.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing documentation known to be false may face criminal charges for tax evasion; the corporate veil can be pierced in civil suits brought by CRA, leading to personal judgment equal to the tax deficiency plus interest and penalties.
AML / Reporting and Licensing Violation

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC penalties up to C$500,000 for each violation under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; Criminal Code money laundering offence up to 10 years' imprisonment; Transport Canada licence revocation plus C$100,000 daily fine for unlicensed operation.

Director personal liability: Senior officers of the corporate general partner may be charged personally for failing to establish required AML procedures; CBCA disqualification and personal fines may apply where a sanctioned entity has been captain/operator in breach.
