Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration responsible for income tax, GST/HST, Part XIII non-resident withholding, and T5013 partnership reporting; digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and CRA Represent a Client for tax professionals; export and customs interface: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Single Window Initiative (SWI) and the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) client portal.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership (including one wholly owned by non-residents) is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes but must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA to open its T5013 partnership information-return account (program-account suffix 'RP') and its GST/HST registrant account (suffix 'RM'). Application is made electronically through My Business Account, or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) and, where compulsory or voluntary registration is sought, Form GST1 (Application for GST/HST Registration). The general partner must upload proof of legal existence (certificate of limited partnership, partnership agreement, foreign registration extract for foreign corporate partners) and a Canadian address or authorized agent address; there is no minimum capital requirement and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted. Each non-resident individual partner must obtain an Individual Tax Number (ITN) via Form T1261; each foreign corporate partner must obtain its own BN through the same CRA gateway. The LP files the T5013 partnership information return annually within five months after fiscal year-end and issues T5013 and NR4 slips, with the general partner reporting every non-resident partner's allocated Canadian-source film-licensing income and remitting Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on that income (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced where applicable).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the Canadian LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (ITA) ss. 96–103: federal income tax is imposed at the partner level, not at the partnership level; the LP itself is nevertheless the statutory 'person' that registers for GST/HST under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 123(1) and files the T5013 return. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Canadian LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (ITA) ss. 96–103: federal income tax is imposed at the partner level, not at the partnership level; the LP itself is nevertheless the statutory 'person' that registers for GST/HST under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 123(1) and files the T5013 return. Territorial scope: Canadian-source film-licensing royalties and active distribution income are taxable in Canada in the hands of resident and non-resident partners; foreign-source film-licensing royalties are generally taxable only in the hands of resident partners and are not taxable to non-resident partners absent a Canadian permanent establishment; no federal tax-exempt free-zone or tax-holiday regime applies to film-licensing activities in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style 'check-the-box' or entity-classification election. Relevant statutory elections and compliance mechanisms for a film-licensing limited partnership include: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4)/(7) to adopt a non-calendar-year fiscal period; (ii) election by a non-resident partner, instead of remaining under Part XIII gross-basis withholding on Canadian-source film royalties, to file a Part I net-basis return under ITA s. 115(1) using the treaty application process and a resident representative, thereby claiming allocable deductions and treaty reductions; (iii) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240 by a small supplier or non-resident rights holder to obtain a BN and claim input tax credits on Canadian production and distribution inputs; (iv) hybrid-mismatch compliance documentation under ITA ss. 18.2–18.13 — a partner resident in a jurisdiction that treats the Canadian LP as opaque must maintain contemporaneous entity-classification evidence and allocation schedules so that a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch does not arise; no 'hybrid election' filing exists, only a documentation and disclosure obligation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 240; a person is a small supplier if the total value of its world-wide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies but excluding exempt supplies) does not exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter nor in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. Once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required within 29 days. A non-resident film licensor with no Canadian permanent establishment may register voluntarily and must register if it makes taxable supplies in Canada in the course of commercial activity. Registration entitles the LP to claim input tax credits on Canadian inputs and supports the evidentiary chain for zero-rated export supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for independent film licensing is claimed under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 10(1): a supply of intangible personal property (copyright and film distribution/licensing rights) to a non-resident person that is not registered for GST/HST and that does not acquire the property for use in Canada is taxable at 0%. Supplementary zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 to associated 'services' supplied to a non-resident (e.g., digital distribution, mastering, metadata, localization and delivery services) provided the non-resident is not a GST/HST registrant and the service is not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property in Canada. The LP must distinguish each royalty stream: royalties attributable to Canadian theatrical, television, VOD, or streaming exploitation attract 5% GST (or HST where the licensee takes the rights for use in an HST province), while royalties attributable to foreign-territory exploitation are zero-rated. A written international licensing agreement enumerating licensed foreign territories, a declaration or equivalent evidence that the licensee is a non-resident non-registrant, and, where physical deliverables such as DCP masters are shipped, a CBSA export declaration are the core substantiation. Zero-rated supplies preserve input tax credit entitlement, unlike exempt supplies.

  • CBSA G7 Canadian Export Declaration — electronically filed via CARM/Single Window Initiative for physically exported film deliverables or goods
  • Original or verified electronic Bill of Lading / Air Waybill naming the foreign destination and the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank SWIFT/MT103 foreign-exchange credit advice or other FCAC-eligible inward remittance evidence showing receipt of the export consideration in Canada
  • Signed international film-licensing agreement enumerating licensed territories, term, media rights, and royalty or sales schedule
  • Non-resident status and non-GST/HST-registration declaration or equivalent documentary evidence from the foreign licensee
  • DCP/exhibition-master delivery logs, encrypted streaming/download manifests, or other digital-evidence packs proving exploitation occurred outside Canada
  • Six-year record-retention compliance file maintained under ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230, including export ledger extracts in CSV/XML form

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API invoice-authorization mandate comparable to Latin American or EU clearance models. CRA requires most GST/HST registrants to file returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE/GST34 and to make payments electronically; there is no statutory digital-stamping duty. Every registrant must issue invoices containing prescribed information under ETA s. 223 and the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations — registrant name and Business Number, date of invoice or supply, consideration, and GST/HST charged — and electronic invoices are expressly permissible. On the export side, physical exports must be reported electronically to the CBSA through the G7 Canadian Export Declaration using the Single Window Initiative/CARM portal; shipments by courier or freight require ACI eManifest data. Failure to transmit export data at the time of export destroys the documentary foundation for zero-rating.

In a CRA audit, a claimed zero-rated export is rebuttably presumed taxable unless the LP maintains full contemporaneous documentation; absence of an outward customs declaration, foreign-use evidence, or an inward remittance within the 180-day documentary repatriation window leads to reassessment at 5% GST/HST on the full royalty, plus daily compound interest under ETA s. 280. Reassessment windows are four years generally (ETA s. 298 / ITA s. 152(4)), seven years in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default, and indefinite for fraud. Penalties include: failure-to-file or late-remittance penalty of 5% plus 5% per month up to 12 months (ETA s. 280); gross-negligence penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the understated net tax (ETA s. 285 and, for partners, ITA s. 163(2)); false-statement penalty of CAD 250 to CAD 10,000 (ETA s. 281); and criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239(1) attracting fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment. The general partner and, subject to provincial limited-partnership liability shields, partnership members are jointly and severally liable for the LP's GST/HST debts, interest, and penalties under ETA s. 272.1(2). Retrospective adjustments also claw back previously claimed input tax credits and Part XIII withheld amounts, with recaptured refund interest and compliance-cost charges; denial of export zero-rating is treated by CRA as a gross-negligence trigger when the export evidence chain is absent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency with full convertibility on both the current and capital accounts. There are no exchange-control restrictions on profit remittance for non-resident partners of a Canadian Limited Partnership. However, non-resident partners are subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on taxable partnership income allocated to them, unless an applicable tax treaty reduces the rate. All royalty and licensing payments to foreign rights holders are subject to a statutory 25% withholding tax, which can be reduced to a treaty-eligible rate upon submission of a valid Canada Revenue Agency Form NR301 and supporting certificate of residence. The Bank of Canada daily exchange rate governs the conversion of foreign-currency receipts for Canadian-dollar tax reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation requirement in Canada. A resident entity may retain foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts. For income tax purposes, export and licensing receipts must be reported in Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada exchange rate prevailing on the date of receipt or revenue recognition, and the Limited Partnership must comply with Canadian partnership reporting obligations, including the filing of T5013 partnership information returns.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with strict legal constraints. Foreign digital EMIs and cross-border payment fintech providers are not licensed as banks in Canada. They must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and if offering retail payment services to Canadian customers, they may also be subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act oversight. For a Canadian Limited Partnership engaged in film copyright licensing, an offshore EMI can serve only as an ancillary collection or disbursement channel for low-value transactions; it cannot replace a Schedule I Canadian bank for capital escrow, large-scale royalty settlement, or commercial-letter-of-credit facilitation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident partners should be routed exclusively through SWIFT MT103 wires using the ISO 20022 remittance field with the narrative 'CONTRIBUTION TO PARTNERSHIP CAPITAL – [PARTNERSHIP NAME]' and a specific reference to the applicable capital-account line item. For partner contributions in foreign currency exceeding CAD 10,000, the receiving Canadian bank will automatically file a Cross-Border Currency Transaction Report with FINTRAC. There is no exchange-control approval step; nevertheless, the bank will perform enhanced due diligence if the originating jurisdiction is not an FATF-compliant member. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal general corporate rate); provincial/territorial corporate rates range from 0% to 16%, producing a combined headline rate of approximately 15% to 31%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents; a Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent, so ordinary partnership distributions to partners are not dividends and bear no dividend withholding tax

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T5013 must be filed within five months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period under s. 229(1) of the Income Tax Regulations; for a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is May 31 of the following calendar year

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a Canadian-controlled private corporation, the T2 is due on the last day of the third month after the fiscal year-end; for a non-CCPC, the return is due two months after fiscal year-end

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions) is mandatory when the aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 5,000,000 in a tax year; a formal Local File and Master File must be prepared contemporaneously to secure the 'reasonable efforts' defence against transfer-pricing penalties; Country-by-Country Reporting applies where the consolidated group revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to those in the UAE; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ss. 245 and 246 of the Income Tax Act) and the transfer-pricing rules under s. 247 require that an interposed limited partnership formed for film licensing has genuine commercial activity, adequate personnel and premises, and arm's length substance. A purely conduit LP with no independently exercised management of copyright material will be vulnerable to GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits under the treaty-shopping provisions, and application of the Controlled Foreign Affiliate income rules where applicable

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s. 239(1) of the Income Tax Act imposes a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded (minimum CAD 5,000) and imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil penalty under s. 163(2) for false statements is 50% of the understated tax plus statutory interest

Director personal liability: Directors and partners are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, civil restitution orders, travel bans, and imprisonment where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud; CRA may also assess joint and several liability under s. 227.1 for unremitted amounts
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: s. 247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment unless the adjustment is excluded by the CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue de minimis threshold; separate information-return penalties and administrative fines may be imposed for failure to produce Master File and Local File documentation

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in fabricating TP documentation or directing the sham pricing can be assessed personally for any resulting withholding deficiencies and may face criminal charges under s. 239 for the resulting false returns
Withholding Tax / Part XIII Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA assesses the unremitted amount under s. 227(8) plus interest; s. 227(10) adds a penalty of 10% of the amount; deliberate non-remittance is an offence prosecutable under s. 227(9), with fines of up to CAD 5,000 and imprisonment; repeated failures expose the partnership to a s. 163(2) misrepresentation penalty

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted Part XIII withholding, interest, and penalties; liability is not insurable and follows the person even after resignation
Shell Company / GAAR / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s. 245(2) denies the tax benefit and may impose a penalty of up to 50% of the benefit under s. 245(4); FINTRAC reporting violations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act attract administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 500,000 for individuals and CAD 2,000,000 for corporations; laundering the proceeds of crime is punishable by imprisonment under Criminal Code s. 462.31

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the limited-partnership veil where the entity is used for fraudulent or criminal purposes; directors and officers face asset forfeiture, travel restrictions, extradition risk, and incarceration if they knowingly benefit from the laundering or sham arrangements
