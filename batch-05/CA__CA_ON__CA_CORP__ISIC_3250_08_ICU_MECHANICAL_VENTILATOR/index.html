Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada; a CBCA/OBCA business corporation is irrevocably an entity-level taxpayer and cannot elect pass-through status. Available elections relevant to this manufacturing group: (1) ITA s. 85(1)/85(2) rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property into CA_CORP; (2) ETA s. 156 election among closely related corporations to keep inter-company supplies outside GST/HST; (3) voluntary GST/HST registration election (Form RC1) once the CA$30,000 small-supplier threshold is crossed; (4) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s. 18.2 and s. 18.21 apply to disallow deductions in US LLC or hybrid financing structures - no election can render the Canadian corporation transparent for income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CA$30,000 - the ETA s. 148(1) small-supplier threshold: total consideration for worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated export sales, in the current and previous four consecutive calendar quarters must not exceed CA$30,000; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory before the next taxable supply is made. There is no export-only exemption. The higher CA$50,000 public service body threshold does not apply to CA_CORP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated exports under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA: (i) direct export - CA_CORP ships ICU ventilator hardware (HS Code 9019.20) out of Canada within the prescribed period, generally 60 days after delivery; (ii) indirect export - goods are delivered to a common carrier, customs bonded warehouse, or licensed export broker for export outside Canada; (iii) services performed outside Canada for non-resident purchasers may be zero-rated under ETA Chapter IV. The 0% rate is claimed on the GST/HST return; where input tax credits exceed output tax, a net refund is paid quarterly. Domestic sales to Canadian hospitals remain subject to 13% HST with the hospital claiming a public service body rebate for the provincial component.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Declaration / Form B13A filed through CERS for shipments exceeding CA$2,000, showing HS Code 9019.20, quantity, value and port of exit; US-bound shipments are reported where an export permit is required or where CBSA elects to request the report)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air or truck B/L evidencing that the ICU ventilators physically departed Canada within the prescribed 60-day export window, with consignee, port of loading, model description, serial numbers, and purchase order references)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT credit advice or foreign-currency repatriation certificate evidencing receipt of USD or other export proceeds within the 180-day benchmark)

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or per-invoice government authorization. CRA requires electronic GST/HST return filing (GST34 via NETFILE) for registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CA$1.5M. E-invoicing is voluntary; CRA supports the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / Universal Business Language standard and accepts XML, EDI and PDF invoices. A valid GST/HST invoice must show the supplier BN/GST/HST number, invoice date, recipient, goods/services description, consideration, and applicable tax rate. Export declarations are filed electronically through CBSA CERS for shipments valued over CA$2,000, with optional XML/API-based e-business transmission available but no per-invoice fiscal stamp.

The CRA Export Verification Center audits require the complete CERS declaration, bill of lading, and inward remittance bundle; missing or inconsistent evidence reverses zero-rating to 13% HST with compounded prescribed-rate interest and penalties. ETA s. 285.1 imposes a penalty equal to the greater of CA$250 and 25% of net tax understated for false or misleading statements; gross negligence attracts a 50% penalty under ITA s. 163.2. The normal reassessment window is four years under ETA s. 298, extended to ten years for misrepresentation or fraud. Deliberate evasion is indictable under ETA s. 327 with fines up to 200% of tax evaded on indictment and potential imprisonment of officers. Canada has no statutory FX-repatriation deadline, but failure to repatriate proceeds within the 180-day benchmark undermines the export-for-value evidentiary chain and may trigger clawback of all zero-rated amounts plus officer joint liability under ETA s. 296.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada imposes no general exchange-control restrictions on the Canadian dollar. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without central bank authorization. Profit remittance, dividends, interest, and management fees can be repatriated freely after paying applicable Part XIII withholding taxes, typically reduced by the Canada-United States tax treaty for US-resident shareholders. There are no restrictions on maintaining foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada. The only formalities are anti-money-laundering qualification reports under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and, in cross-border transfer cases, FINTRAC reporting equivalent to CAD 10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any surrender requirement or mandatory conversion timeline for export receipts. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency in Canadian bank accounts, convert at market rates, or use those funds for offshore operational costs. For statistical and customs compliance, exports must still be reported as required under the Customs Act and International Trade Transactions data reporting; however, no statutory repatriation or conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervising federally regulated deposit-taking institutions, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administering cross-border currency and wire transaction reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Ontario-incorporated manufacturing entities may engage licensed Canadian money-services businesses (MSBs) for smaller operational flows, but offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) have no direct Canadian settlement capability and cannot open credit facilities, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide material CAD liquidity clearing. Any offshore EMI must partner with a Canadian bank or MSB registered with FINTRAC. Given the need for standby letters of credit and Ministry-of-Health-backed payment instruments, a Tier 1 bank is the only practical liquidity rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Ontario entity should be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent's bank using the following protocol: (a) beneficiary name matching the exact registration certificate of the Ontario business corporation, (b) beneficiary account number prefixed with the bank's 3-digit Canadian institution code and 5-digit transit code, (c) SWIFT BIC of the receiving Canadian bank, (d) purpose of payment description 'Capital contribution for the issue of common shares in accordance with Ontario Business Corporations Act', and (e) reference to the corporate KYC case file. Banks will apply their own payment-screening rules; amounts exceeding CAD 10,000 in a single cross-border transaction are reportable to FINTRAC. No prior exchange-control approval is needed, but the bank may request a board resolution and the supporting financing agreement to finalize compliance clearance.Outbound trade disbursements for ventilator component purchases and factory outsourcing are executed through either SWIFT MT103 for open-account terms or commercial letters of credit issued by the Canadian bank under UCP600. Letters of credit use the bank's trade-finance desk and require presentation through a confirming bank in the seller's jurisdiction. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 500,000 for goods, CAD 250,000 for services, or CAD 250,000 for intangibles. Master File required if consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 500 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. Corporations must demonstrate real economic activity, including physical presence, employees, and decision-making in Canada, to avoid recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (Canada) Section 239: fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under Section 227.1 if they failed to withhold or remit. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization, disallowance of deductions, and penalty of 25% of tax benefit under Section 245. CRA may also impose a 10% penalty for failure to file TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for penalties if they authorized the sham arrangement. Travel bans and asset freezes possible under the Income Tax Act.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations, imprisonment up to 5 years for officers.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years. Civil forfeiture of assets and travel bans may be imposed.
