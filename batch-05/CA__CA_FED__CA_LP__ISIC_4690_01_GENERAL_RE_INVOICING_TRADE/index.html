Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

ServiceOntario – Companies and Personal Property Security Branch (Limited Partnership Registry), acting as the provincial registering authority, in coordination with Corporations Canada, Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, as the federal incorporating registry for the corporate general partner.

Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. Formation / constitutional filings

Ontario Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form 3) filed with ServiceOntario, together with CBCA Articles of Incorporation (Form 1) and Notices of Registered Office and Directors (CBCA Forms 3 and 4) filed with Corporations Canada.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum statutory paid-up capital is imposed on a limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act. Limited partners may contribute cash, property, or services as agreed in the partnership agreement; no escrow, trust, or holdback account is required by law for a general re-invoicing trade intermediary.

Funding & unlock path: For any physical premises, obtain a building occupancy permit under the Ontario Building Code Act, 1992, undertake a fire safety inspection under the Ontario Fire Code, O. Reg. 213/07, obtain approval of the fire safety plan, provide structural load certification for any warehouse racking, and post the occupational health and safety policy under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. O.1. Obtain a WSIB clearance certificate (Form CU-129) if employees are registered with the Workplace Safety and Insurance Board.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum statutory paid-up capital is imposed on a limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act. Limited partners may contribute cash, property, or services as agreed in the partnership agreement; no escrow, trust, or holdback account is required by law for a general re-invoicing trade intermediary.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sectoral industrial licensing authority applies to ISIC 4690 wholesale trade intermediaries and re-invoicing operators at the federal level. The relevant general authorities are the local municipal licensing and standards division for any physical premises, the Canada Revenue Agency for trade and tax registration, and Global Affairs Canada only if the entity later handles controlled or sanctioned goods requiring an export permit under the Export and Import Permits Act. FINTRAC registration would apply only if payment services or money services business activity is added.

Category: Not applicable – no operating or manufacturing permit is required for a general commercial re-invoicing and wholesale trade intermediary. An optional municipal business licence may be required only where a physical office is opened, and a CRA import-export program account would be needed only if goods physically move through Canada.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); GST/HST filings and administration via CRA My Business Account portal and Business Registration Online (BRO), with customs matters administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

Non-resident limited partnerships must first determine whether the partnership is carrying on business in Canada or is a non-resident supplier making taxable supplies in Canada. If registration is required, submit an application for a federal Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account through CRA's Business Registration Online service or by using Form RC1 / RC1A for non-residents. Provide the partnership agreement, proof of limited partnership existence from the foreign jurisdiction, a general partner declaration, and the appointed representative's/agent's contact details in Canada if a Canadian address is required. The BN program account issued by CRA serves as the statutory TIN and GST/HST registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no federal check-the-box entity classification election. A limited partnership is automatically fiscally transparent; it is neither taxed as a corporation nor eligible to elect corporate status. Relevant statutory elections available to the partners include: (i) selecting the partnership's fiscal period under subsection 249.1(1) of the Income Tax Act; (ii) entering into the section 216(4) election for taxation of non-resident partners on Canadian-source rental income where applicable; and (iii) allocating partnership income in accordance with the partnership agreement with substantial economic effect. Canada's anti-hybrid and anti-avoidance rules (including the excessive interest and financing expense rules) apply to re-characterize mismatches, but no direct hybrid mismatch election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

30,000 CAD in taxable worldwide supplies in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold). Registration is mandatory for any person, including a non-resident LP, carrying on business in Canada above this threshold or making taxable supplies of goods/services in Canada above this threshold; zero-rated exporters may elect to register voluntarily.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the goods are exported from Canada in the normal course of business and are delivered to a destination outside Canada, or are supplied to a non-resident and exported as verified by documentary evidence. Offshore services supplied by a commercial re-invoicing intermediary are zero-rated where the services are supplied to a non-resident and performed in Canada or are performed wholly/primarily outside Canada, subject to CRA administrative policy. The exporter must maintain contemporaneous documentation demonstrating exportation and foreign payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A form or approved electronic export declaration where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing shipment from Canada to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/credit transfer confirmation evidencing foreign currency receipt from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration and transportation documents

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or mandatory national e-invoicing regime currently applies in Canada. Electronic invoices are accepted provided they are issued in a legible, retrievable format that maintains the prescribed content and audit trail. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or E-filing via authorized tax software) in accordance with CRA's e-services mandate; larger registrants may be required to file electronically. No digital stamping or real-time CRA clearance is imposed.

CRA may deny the zero-rating and reassess GST at the statutory rate, with default interest and arrears interest, if the exporter fails to substantiate exportation. Penalties include the gross negligence penalty (25% of the federal tax portion in cases of gross negligence), false statements penalties, failure-to-file/registration penalties, and willful evasion prosecutions culminating in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and possible imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks apply against fraudulent export schemes, round-tripping of goods into Canada, and misuse of zero-rating on non-qualifying services.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. The Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, and the Income Tax Act do not restrict the conversion of Canadian dollars into foreign currency or the cross-border remittance of profits. A Limited Partnership may freely receive and disburse CAD and foreign currency; however, non-resident partners remain subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on Canadian-source partnership allocations, and FATCA/CRS reporting duties apply to financial institutions and beneficial owners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory repatriation, surrender, or mandatory conversion requirements for export proceeds in Canada. Receipts from offshore re-invoicing trade may be retained in foreign currency accounts without compelled conversion. Canadian tax residents reporting on an accrual basis are taxed on worldwide income, but no FX control mandate is engaged.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); residual foreign-exchange policy authority is vested in the Minister of Finance, and day-to-day AML/CFT oversight is delegated to FINTRAC. Canada does not maintain a separate Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange-control bureau.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for reliance as primary treasury rails. Canadian-chartered banks and OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institutions are the only entities that may offer full fiduciary and clearing services. Foreign digital EMIs (electronic money institutions) are not recognized as banks under the Bank Act (Canada); they are generally treated as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and must register with FINTRAC. Their utility is limited to ancillary payment aggregation, not core liquidity clearing or trade-credit facilitation, and they face restricted access to CAD settlement systems.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate (38% basic rate less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction); the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent, so corporate partners are taxed on allocated income; provincial corporate rates add approximately 0%–16% depending on province.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident, subject to treaty reduction. LP profit allocations are not dividends; they are taxed directly to the non-resident partner under Part I on the partner's share of Canadian-source business income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year partnership, T5013 is due March 31; for other fiscal periods, due no later than five months after the fiscal period-end. T2 corporate returns are due within six months after each taxation year-end; final balance-due day is two months after year-end for general corporate partners and three months for CCPCs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 is required where the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceeds C$1 million in a taxation year; CRA expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation comparable to OECD Local File documentation. Country-by-Country Report is required for MNE groups with annual consolidated revenue of at least C$1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada has not codified a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the CRA applies substance-over-form, the general anti-avoidance rule (s.245), and OECD/MLI treaty-abuse principles. A re-invoicing hub must have meaningful commercial functions, Canadian decision-making, employees, premises and risk in Canada. Lacking substance, CRA may recharacterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, impose GAAR penalties (potentially 50% of the tax benefit), and apply transfer pricing adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: on summary conviction, a fine equal to 50%–200% of the tax evaded and up to 2 years imprisonment; on indictment, fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment. CRA may also assess gross-negligence penalties of 50% of the misstatement.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners or officers who direct, assent to or participate in the evasion are personally liable for penalties and court-imposed restitution; criminal conviction can trigger travel limitations and imprisonment. Courts may pierce the LP veil on clear evidence of fraud or sham.
Aggressive Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: s.247(2) restates profits to arm's length amounts. A transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment applies where documentation is inadequate and the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5 million and 10% of gross revenue; interest and arrears compound until paid.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for corporate transfer-pricing reassessments, but directors who knowingly approved false pricing can be assessed as participants in a sham and held personally liable for resulting withholding obligations or penalty amounts.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke GAAR to deny the tax benefit and apply the new 50% GAAR penalty; the treaty may be denied under the principal purpose test; s.247 transfer pricing adjustments may disregard the entity entirely. Provincial securities/registry authorities may dissolve non-compliant LPs.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling partners who use a shell LP for abusive tax avoidance face personal liability for CRA collection if the entity is deemed a sham or if they guaranteed obligations; CRA may seek a compliance order and court-ordered restitution.
