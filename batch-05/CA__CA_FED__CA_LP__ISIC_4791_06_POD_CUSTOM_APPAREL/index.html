A commercial registered agent or virtual registered office service is generally permissible for a Limited Partnership in common-law provinces, provided the address is a physical street address in the province of registration, not a post office box, and the registered office is staffed during normal business hours to accept service of process. Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Provincial Registrar of Limited Partnerships (Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery, Ontario; BC Registry Services in British Columbia) and Corporations Canada, Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, for federal registration of the corporate general partner.

Governing statute

Limited partnerships in Canada are constituted under provincial limited partnership statutes, as no federal limited partnership statute exists. For this federal-jurisdiction footprint, the general partner may be formed under the Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44, ss. 3-21 and 102-105; the limited partnership itself is governed by the applicable provincial Limited Partnership Act (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.8, Part I; British Columbia Partnership Act, R.S.B.C. 1996, c. 348, ss. 3-20); income tax treatment of the partnership flows under the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), s. 96.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form 3 under the applicable provincial Limited Partnership Act), accompanied by the executed Limited Partnership Agreement and NUANS name search report; extra-provincial limited partnership registration form for cross-provincial operations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a limited partnership. Capital contributions are contractual and set out in the Limited Partnership Agreement; a limited partner must contribute cash, property, or services and may not withdraw capital if such withdrawal would render the partnership insolvent. No escrow account or paid-up capital deposit is required by the provincial registrar or under the CBCA for the corporate general partner.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Ontario Fire Code (O. Reg 213/07), including fire extinguisher inspections, fire escape plan, and annual fire marshal inspection; structural load verification for printing equipment and warehouse racking; municipal building code occupancy certificate; HVAC and make-up air compliance for print emissions; Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB) workplace registration and occupational health and safety clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. O.1.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a limited partnership. Capital contributions are contractual and set out in the Limited Partnership Agreement; a limited partner must contribute cash, property, or services and may not withdraw capital if such withdrawal would render the partnership insolvent. No escrow account or paid-up capital deposit is required by the provincial registrar or under the CBCA for the corporate general partner.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal tax administration for income tax, GST/HST, payroll and excise; principal portals are Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MBA), and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers export and import reporting.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Limited Partnership obtains its federal identity through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1, receiving a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (BN suffix 'RT'). Where the LP sells exclusively through e-commerce channels to Canadian consumers without a permanent establishment, the Simplified GST/HST Registration for Non-Residents can be used to obtain only the RT account without activating income tax accounts. Because GST/HST refunds are payable only to a Canadian bank account or an approved intermediary, the LP should appoint a Canadian agent or accountant to act as CRA contact and remittance agent (Form RC59). The standard sequence is: (1) establish a Canadian service address and fulfilment/tax agent; (2) file RC1 or the simplified non-resident enrolment; (3) receive the RT account effective from the first taxable supply in Canada (or the date of voluntary registration); (4) remit GST/HST under the quarterly or annual prescribed cycle; and (5) activate T5013 partnership information return filing obligations with the partnership's fiscal year-end.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under ITA ss. 96–98, the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax: income or loss is computed at the partnership level but taxed in the hands of each partner; no federal entity-level income tax applies. For GST/HST, the ETA s. 133 treats the partnership as a separate person, so the LP itself is the GST/HST registrant (RT account) on taxable supplies it makes through its Canadian fulfillment operations; the partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership net tax to the extent of partnership property under ETA s. 323. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under ITA ss. 96–98, the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax: income or loss is computed at the partnership level but taxed in the hands of each partner; no federal entity-level income tax applies. For GST/HST, the ETA s. 133 treats the partnership as a separate person, so the LP itself is the GST/HST registrant (RT account) on taxable supplies it makes through its Canadian fulfillment operations; the partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership net tax to the extent of partnership property under ETA s. 323. Territorial scope: supplies made in Canada by the Canadian establishment; Canada has no general tax-exempt zone for onshore e-commerce activities.

Available election forms

Canada has no federal check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections for a Canadian LP include: (1) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 96(3) aligning the partnership year-end with the majority-interest partners and deeming a year-end upon partner admission or withdrawal; (2) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 177(1) (Form GST31) permitting the LP to register before exceeding the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in order to claim input tax credits on POD fulfilment costs; (3) closely related persons election under ETA s. 273 (Form RC275 or equivalent) to deem inter-entity supplies made for nil consideration where the LP and its corporate general partner satisfy the 90% ownership linkage under ETA s. 123; and (4) hybrid mismatch neutrality rules under ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5, effective for taxation years beginning after 30 June 2022, which eliminate double deduction and foreign tax credit generator outcomes for a non-resident partner whose home jurisdiction treats the fiscally transparent LP as a taxable entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 240(1), measured as the preceding 12-month worldwide taxable supplies of the person and any associated persons. A non-resident LP carrying on business in Canada has no small-supplier exemption for supplies made through a Canadian establishment, and registration is required from the first dollar of Canadian taxable supply; non-resident e-commerce sellers using an online platform may be relieved by the platform's deemed-supplier collection responsibility under the 1 July 2021 GST/HST framework. The LP may elect to register voluntarily even when below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A POD merchandise retailer may claim 0% GST/HST on exports of custom apparel under ETA Schedule VI Part I, s. 10 where the supplier (i) ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, (ii) exports the goods in an essentially continuous and unbroken outbound movement, and (iii) can demonstrate, with evidence satisfactory to the Minister, that export occurred as soon as reasonably practicable. Associated design and fulfilment services rendered in Canada for a non-resident buyer are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V, s. 7(1), subject to the carve-outs for services relating to Canadian real property or to tangible property situated in Canada at the time of performance and supplied to a Canadian resident. The LP must maintain item-level proof of foreign destination (foreign shipping address in the e-commerce platform, courier waybill showing non-Canadian delivery address, and destination-country delivery confirmation) and must not zero-rate where the online platform is legally deemed to be the supplier of the goods. Export proceeds should normally be repatriated within 180 days of invoice settlement; Canada does not impose a fixed statutory repatriation deadline, but unexplained delay beyond 180 days invites CRA audit on the substance of the underlying export.

  • Validated CBSA export reporting record (Canadian Export Reporting System (CESR) declaration for commercial exports above CAD 2,000, or the courier's consolidated eManifest export summary for low-value POD parcels)
  • Commercial invoice with full exporter/importer identification and HS tariff classification, together with the carrier Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (DHL/FedEx/UPS) evidencing continuous outbound carriage to the foreign consignee address
  • Inward foreign exchange remittance or settlement evidence — Stripe/PayPal payout reports, bank FX credit advice, or other interbank remittance records tracing the foreign buyer's payment to the exported goods

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no government fiscal API obligation, and no digital stamping or invoice pre-clearance requirement. Mandatory electronic filings apply only to GST/HST returns: registrants with taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000 must file electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI (ETA s. 278 and regulations). Voluntary e-invoicing operates through the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing network aligned with Peppol BIS 3.0 / UBL 2.1 (launched 2024); CRA does not operate an invoice clearance portal. Commercial invoices, Stripe or PayPal payment accounts, and courier waybills are accepted documentation, provided the registered BN/RT number and the zero-rating basis are stated for export sales.

CRA e-commerce audit teams apply heightened scrutiny to registrants reporting a high ratio of zero-rated export supplies, especially where courier documentation and FX settlement records are incomplete. Normal GST/HST reassessment window is four years (ETA s. 298), extended without limitation for misrepresentation, neglect or fraud. Where zero-rating substantiation fails, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 5% GST (or up to 15% HST where provincial nexus applies), the net tax is reassessed, and compounded daily interest accrues at the prescribed rate. Civil penalties include a 6% late-filing penalty plus 1% per full month up to 24% (ETA s. 281), a gross-negligence false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the overstated refund or credit (ETA s. 281.1), and wilful-evasion criminal penalties of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment (ETA Part X, ss. 327–329). The LP's general partner and limited partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's net GST/HST under ETA s. 323(1), producing direct personal clawback exposure on failed export zero-rating claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account under IMF Article VIII. Canada imposes no exchange controls, no current-account conversion restrictions, and no capital repatriation ceilings. Profit remittance from a limited partnership is not FX-blocked; however, distributions to non-resident partners attract Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act at 25%, subject to treaty reduction, and must be reported on the relevant NR forms. The LP may maintain foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada without central bank authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or repatriation timeline exists. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely, voluntarily converted at commercial-bank spot rates, or used to settle trade obligations without mandatory conversion ratios under any Canadian federal statute.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, and no separate foreign-exchange control administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer can serve as auxiliary cross-border payout and collection channels for a Canadian LP, but legal constraints restrict direct substitution of a Canadian-chartered bank account. Any provider facilitating payment transactions in Canada is subject to registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act with Bank of Canada, and FINTRAC registration as a money services business applies to FX conversion or money transmission. The LP must retain a domestic operating account for CAD clearing, GST/HST obligations, and payroll. EMI settlements must be structured only through whitelisted bank counterparties to avoid AML breakdown.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident limited partners and opaque beneficial ownership structure

Root cause: Non-resident limited partners and opaque beneficial ownership structure

Defense evidence: Provide registered partnership agreement, all partner residency declarations, a complete beneficial ownership registry for every partner holding 10% or more, source of funds and source of wealth statements, notarized passports, and a legal opinion confirming no shell-company or nominee structure.
Heavy reliance on third-party online marketplaces and high refund/chargeback mobility in POD e-commerce

Root cause: Heavy reliance on third-party online marketplaces and high refund/chargeback mobility in POD e-commerce

Defense evidence: Supply merchant agreements with Stripe and PayPal, six months of processor settlement statements, refund and chargeback policy documentation, PCI-DSS compliance attestation, and export of transaction data showing expected revenue and refund patterns.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate on net income allocated to the partners; a Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and does not itself pay income tax. Individual partners pay graduated personal rates; corporate partners pay the 15% federal general rate plus provincial corporate rates (8%-16%). Part XIII withholding applies separately on certain outbound payments.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation. A Canadian LP is a pass-through entity; profit allocations to partners are not dividends and no withholding tax is imposed on those allocations. However, if the LP's profits are routed through a Canadian corporate general partner or a corporate manager that then pays dividends to non-resident shareholders, the 25% statutory rate applies, subject to treaty reduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 and the Local File are mandatory where the aggregate fair market value of controlled cross-border transactions with non-arm's length non-residents exceeds C$5,000,000 in the partnership's fiscal year (for fiscal years beginning after 2021). The Master File is required where the multinational enterprise group's consolidated revenue exceeds C$500,000,000 and the Canadian entity is a designated participant. For a typical POD retail partnership with third-party fulfilment and standard payment processors, these thresholds generally are not met; however, if the LP transacts with a related non-resident parent or supplier, contemporaneous comparability analysis must be prepared and the allocation must be documented.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations or the EU ATAD economic substance requirements. Instead, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in s. 245 of the Income Tax Act and the common-law 'sham' doctrine recharacterise arrangements that lack a genuine business purpose. An LP with no real commercial activity, no physical presence, or one created solely to shift e-commerce income to non-residents (e.g., a 'synthetic' partnership) may be disregarded, and the CRA may assess the beneficial owner directly. Thin capitalization (s. 18(4)) restricts interest deductions where the debt-to-equity ratio of a non-resident-controlled partnership exceeds 1.5:1; the partnership must also comply with the CRA's 'Partnership Audit Technique' requiring full disclosure of the business purpose of the partnership structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Non-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under Income Tax Act s. 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Civil penalty under s. 163(2): 50% of the understated tax for gross negligence. CRA will also apply prescribed-rate interest and may pursue third-party demand for unpaid amounts.

Director personal liability: General partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's tax debts. Limited partners who participate in the control or management of the POD business forfeit limited liability and become jointly liable for all partnership debts, including tax debts. The directors and officers of a corporate general partner may face personal liability and, for wilful evasion, imprisonment; the CRA may obtain a court order prohibiting departure from Canada under s. 226 of the Income Tax Act and may request refusal or revocation of Canadian passports for tax debts exceeding C$50,000.
Shell Company / Artificial Partnership Structure (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: GAAR under s. 245 allows the Minister to deny the tax benefit and recharacterise the arrangement. A sham partnership is wholly disregarded for tax purposes. Civil penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of the tax recharacterised; criminal referral where false statements are knowingly made; interest is compounded daily on the reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner who authorise the artificial structure can be assessed under s. 227.1 for unremitted withholdings and under the common-law duty of care for restitution. The CRA may apply the 'director's certificate' procedure to collect unpaid amounts from personal assets; travel bans and seizure of personal property are available when the director acts in bad faith.
Part XIII Withholding Non-Compliance (Non-Resident Payments)

Statutory sanction: Under s. 227(8), the partnership is liable for the full amount of tax not withheld plus a penalty of 10% of the amount (20% if the failure was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence). Interest accrues on the unpaid withheld amounts at prescribed rates. If the non-resident fails to file a Canadian return, an additional penalty of $100 to $2,500 may apply under s. 162.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII amounts under s. 227.1(1) if the partnership fails to remit and the director did not exercise 'reasonable care'. Liability is joint and several and survives distribution of partnership assets; directors may be pursued through civil proceedings, garnishment, and, in extreme cases, criminal prosecution.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under s. 247(3), a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment applies where the adjustment exceeds the greater of C$5,000,000 and 10% of the partnership's gross revenue for the year. The penalty is in addition to the tax on the adjustment plus interest. Failure to file Form T106 on time attracts a penalty of $100 per day, up to $10,000 (for fiscal years beginning after 2021).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in transfer-pricing mispricing may be held personally liable under the 'aiding and abetting' provisions of s. 227.1(5) and under the general civil liability framework; although TP penalties are not normally 'amounts' for s. 227.1 purposes, directors are exposed to corporate law claims for breach of fiduciary duty in connection with such mispricing.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): up to C$1,000,000 for an entity and C$500,000 for an individual for non-compliance; criminal offence for knowingly making false statements or providing false information to FINTRAC: imprisonment up to 5 years. The CRA may also cross-reference FINTRAC reports with tax returns, triggering a full income tax audit.

Director personal liability: The PCMLTFA expressly creates personal liability for officers, directors, and agents who 'direct, authorize, assent to, acquiesce in, or participate in' the commission of an offence. In addition, directors of the general partner may be subject to CRA director liability provisions if the underlying activity constitutes tax evasion; travel bans and seizure of assets may be ordered in a criminal money-laundering prosecution.
