Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). In Ontario, CRA administers the federal GST and the Ontario HST portion under the Ontario Comprehensive Integrated Tax Coordination Agreement; the Ontario Ministry of Finance administers Ontario-only tax programs.

E-government filing portal

A federal/provincial Ontario business corporation receives a 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation through Corporations Canada / ServiceOntario. The corporation must then activate CRA program accounts for corporate income tax, GST/HST, and any payroll source-deductions through Business Registration Online or Form RC1. For a foreign entity: apply to CRA for a BN and the appropriate program account using the same online registration route or RC1; CRA may require a Canadian agent for service and may require security under section 242 of the Excise Tax Act before GST/HST registration is granted for a non-resident. Registration must be completed before taxable supplies are invoiced in Canada unless the small supplier exception applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a check-the-box election; the corporation is a per se taxable corporation under the Income Tax Act and, for U.S. entity-classification purposes, is a per se corporation under Treas. Reg. section 301.7701-2(b)(8) and cannot be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership. Available elective mechanisms are limited to tax-return elections, not entity reclassification: voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier; election under ETA section 156 for nil consideration on GST/HST for qualifying supplies among closely related corporations; and, where eligibility criteria are met, election of annual GST/HST filing. Hybrid-mismatch rules are mandatory and non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies for GST/HST purposes measured over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a person becomes a GST/HST registrant after exceeding the small supplier threshold, but may voluntarily register before the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of FPV racing drones are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the goods are exported by the supplier or delivered to a non-resident recipient who exports them as soon as reasonably possible under a contract of sale. For retail sales made through PayPal or Stripe, zero rating requires shipment by a common carrier or courier from Canada to an address outside Canada and documentary evidence that the goods did not remain in Canada. Zero-rated exports preserve the manufacturer's input tax credits on carbon-fiber milling, brushless motor integration, packaging and freight costs, creating the ability to claim net GST/HST refunds when credits exceed tax charged.

  • Customs export declaration or CBSA cargo control/export manifest (CARM / ACI electronic declaration where required)
  • Commercial invoice with CRA Business Number, full product description, HS/customs tariff classification, FOB value, and foreign consignee address
  • Bill of lading / air waybill / courier receipt or tracking report evidencing physical export from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Proof of export clearance or foreign customs entry showing the overseas delivery
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or PayPal/Stripe settlement report demonstrating receipt of export proceeds in the exporter's Canadian account
  • Purchase order and non-resident customer identification/address evidencing that the sale is a true export and not a domestic supply

Canada currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate. CRA requires GST/HST registrants to keep invoices that contain prescribed information, including the recipient's Business Number, date, description, GST/HST rate and amount paid; electronic invoices are valid if they contain the same data and are accessible. Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing standards (Peppol / PDRI) are voluntary and not yet mandatory.

CRA audits cross-border drone sales carefully and may deny zero rating if the export documentation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent. If zero rating is denied, the registrant is assessed for uncollected 13% HST on the full selling price, input tax credits are clawed back, and interest and penalties accrue from the original filing date. Under ETA section 285(1), false statements or omissions in returns attract a civil penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; fraudulent conduct is subject to prosecution with fines and imprisonment. Directors and officers may be liable under ETA section 323 for unremitted GST/HST. CRA has four years to reassess absent misrepresentation, and record retention of at least six years is required.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada accepts IMF Article VIII obligations, providing unrestricted convertibility for trade, dividends, interest, and capital movements. Repatriation of profits by way of dividends is permitted without central bank approval, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, generally reduced to 15%/5% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty for a US parent). Domestic banks may operate CAD, USD, EUR, and offshore CNY accounts; no approval is needed to convert CAD or hold foreign currency balances. Investment Canada Act screening only applies to acquisitions above statutory monetary thresholds, which do not apply to this early-stage retail entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, forced conversion, or repatriation deadline on export proceeds. Documentary obligations are limited to: (i) retention of B13A/B3 Canada Border Services Agency declarations, commercial invoices, and courier/waybill evidence to substantiate GST/HST zero-rating of exported goods; (ii) FINTRAC reporting of incoming or outgoing electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 in a 24-hour window; and (iii) maintenance of underlying trade documents (purchase orders, shipping records, bank payment confirmations) to satisfy OSFI anti-money-laundering and know-your-client due-diligence expectations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary and currency issuance authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential regulator of federally licensed banks; FINTRAC as the AML/CFT supervisor and cross-border EFT reporting authority. Canada abolished its Foreign Exchange Control Board in 1951; no exchange-control commission or foreign-exchange licensing regime exists today.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general rate 15% plus Ontario general rate 11.5%, reduced to 10% for qualifying manufacturing and processing (M&P) profits; combined headline rate for an Ontario FPV drone manufacturer: 25%. A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) pays a combined 12.2% (federal 9% + Ontario 3.2%) on active business income up to CAD 500,000 before the federal and Ontario general rates apply to the excess.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory 25% withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (ITA) on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; rate reduced to 15% (portfolio) or 5% (10% or greater voting interest) where the applicable income tax treaty so provides.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 and all schedules must be filed within six months after the end of the taxation year. For a December 31 year-end, the filing and balance-due day is June 30 of the following year. Tax instalments are due quarterly for CCPCs and monthly for other corporations when estimated tax payable exceeds CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory for taxation years when total non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000. Exemption: a CCPC with gross revenue not exceeding CAD 50,000,000 and aggregate cross-border related-party transactions not exceeding CAD 5,000,000 is not required to prepare a Local File. Master File is required where the MNE group's consolidated revenue is at least CAD 100,000,000 (fiscal years beginning after December 31, 2022); Country-by-Country Report is required where consolidated group revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations registration regime; economic substance is tested through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) which denies tax benefits from avoidance transactions lacking genuine economic substance; treaty-shopping restrictions through beneficial ownership, the LOB clause in the Canada-US treaty, and the MLI principal-purpose test; foreign accrual property income rules for controlled foreign affiliates; and CRA audit assertions requiring real premises, employees, inventory, and milling and motor-integration equipment to substantiate the manufacturing business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the misstated amount (ITA s. 163(2)); on summary conviction fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or up to 2 years imprisonment; on indictment fine up to 200% of the evaded tax and/or up to 5 years imprisonment (ITA s. 239(1)); Criminal Code fraud over CAD 5,000 carries up to 14 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or authorize evasion are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for unremitted amounts; CRA asset freezes and garnishments; travel bans via court order in fraud prosecutions; civil restitution and compensation orders against directing minds.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under ITA s. 245 with full tax benefit denial and interest; 50% gross negligence penalty for misrepresentation; denial of treaty relief under beneficial ownership, LOB, or PPT; promoter penalties under ITA s. 163.2 up to CAD 100,000 plus 100% of gross compensation.

Director personal liability: Directors held personally liable for Part XIII withholding failures on stripped payments; assessments extended to officers who caused the corporation to sign false certificates; criminal exposure where the structure facilitates tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 247: upward income adjustment plus daily compound interest; 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a year and adequate documentation is not provided within 90 days of a written demand.

Director personal liability: Directors generally shielded from pure TP adjustments, but personal gross-negligence penalties apply where they sign false documentation; s. 163.2 counsellor penalties apply to advisers; prosecution under s. 239 for false records.
Part XIII Withholding and Non-Resident Distribution Failures

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 227(8)-(9): 10% penalty on the unremitted withholding amount, daily compound interest on the arrearage, NR4 late-filing penalties (CAD 25/day, minimum CAD 100, maximum CAD 2,500), and the grossed-up Part XIII amount remains collectible from the payer.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s. 227.1 for the full unremitted Part XIII amount, enforceable by federal tax lien and seizure; liability survives corporate bankruptcy; travel restrictions may apply in fraudulent non-remittance prosecutions.
AML and FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for a corporation per violation (CAD 100,000 for individuals); criminal prosecution for knowingly making false statements or obstructing a FINTRAC examination; asset seizure under the Criminal Code for proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Senior officers can be personally named in Notices of Violation and assessed penalties; deliberate non-compliance triggers criminal liability for directors and officers under PCMLTFA ss. 74-77, with fines and imprisonment up to 5 years.
