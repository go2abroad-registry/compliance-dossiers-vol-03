Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a limited partnership. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); official federal tax administration portal is CRA My Business Account, with entity registration conducted through Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html.

E-government filing portal

The Canadian limited partnership must first obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and open a federal GST/HST program account. For a non-resident LP, the prescribed sequence is: (1) determine whether GST/HST registration is mandatory as a non-resident registrant, noting that a non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada without carrying on business in Canada must register with no small-supplier exemption, whereas a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is subject to the ordinary CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; (2) apply online through BRO or file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number (BN) and Certain Program Accounts'; (3) register in the LP's own legal name, selecting the RT (Regular GST/HST) program account; (4) if the LP has no establishment in Canada, furnish acceptable security, such as cash, an irrevocable letter of credit, or a surety bond, under Excise Tax Act subsection 240(2) as a precondition to registration; (5) file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, using the LP's separate registration identity; and (6) simultaneously satisfy the T5013 Partnership Information Return obligation under Income Tax Act section 233.1, which applies because the LP has a corporate general partner or corporate limited partners, and prepare Schedule 50 allocations for each partner. No notarization or Canadian registered agent is statutorily required for GST/HST registration, but a Canadian representative with authority to receive service and manage the security arrangement is strongly recommended.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Canadian limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes: the LP files an annual partnership information return but does not itself pay income tax, and each limited and general partner is taxed on its allocated share of partnership income or loss computed at the partner level. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is a distinct 'person' under subsection 122(1) of the Excise Tax Act and is normally the registrant of record for all taxable supplies made by the partnership. The resulting dual identity is decisive for cross-border wholesale activities: transparent for income tax, substantive for indirect tax. Territorial scope requires particular attention because a non-resident limited partner is deemed to carry on business in Canada through a partnership that carries on business in Canada, rendering its Canadian-source share of partnership income taxable in Canada on a net basis absent a treaty allocation override.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box or formal entity-classification election; an LP cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation, and the fiscally transparent treatment is mandatory. The material elective mechanisms available to this structure are: (i) the closely-related persons election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act, which permits a qualifying Canadian partnership and its eligible closely-related corporate members to elect that taxable supplies made between them are deemed to be made for nil consideration, thereby deferring GST/HST cash tax on intercompany distributions of ophthalmic frames; (ii) voluntary early registration as a small supplier under ETA subsection 240(1), allowing the LP to register before reaching the CAD 30,000 threshold in order to claim input tax credits on start-up costs; and (iii) to the extent the LP or its partners hold passive real-property or rental income, the Income Tax Act section 216 election remains available for non-resident persons, although it is inapplicable to the active wholesale distribution of eyewear. A non-resident registrant may also rely on CRA administrative positions permitting simplified non-resident GST/HST return reporting, but that is a procedural concession rather than an entity election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada abolished comprehensive exchange controls in 1951 and no central-bank approval is required for capital inflows or outflows. Non-resident limited partners may freely subscribe capital and repatriate distributable profits, subject only to Canadian tax compliance: the partnership must withhold Part I tax on the non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source business income under the applicable treaty, and any passive payments attract Part XIII withholding (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced). Open-account trade, pre-payment, and deferred settlement are unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable under Canadian law. There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation obligation for export proceeds, which may be retained in foreign currency at any bank or financial institution. However, for income tax purposes export revenue is recognized in Canadian dollars under the Income Tax Act, and realized foreign-exchange gains or losses on settlement are computed in accordance with the realization rules, including subsection 39(1).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervising deposit-taking institutions and FINTRAC administering cross-border transaction reporting; Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange control administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium, with legal constraints. Global electronic money institutions such as Wise, Airwallex, or Corpay may provide supplementary multi-currency wallets and cost-effective cross-border disbursements, but any entity providing fund-transfer or payment services to Canadian residents must register as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC and obtain provincial money-services licences in applicable provinces such as Ontario or British Columbia. Such EMIs are not authorized under the Bank Act to accept deposits or provide credit in Canada and cannot directly access the Lynx/LVTS high-value Canadian-dollar clearing infrastructure. The limited partnership should maintain a Schedule I chartered bank as its principal clearing and settlement counterparty, using EMIs only for ancillary low-value international disbursements.
