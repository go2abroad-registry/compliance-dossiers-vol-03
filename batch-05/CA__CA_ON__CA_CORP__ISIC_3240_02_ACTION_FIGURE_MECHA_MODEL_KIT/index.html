Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no exchange control laws restricting conversion, transfer, or remittance of dividends, interest, management fees, or repatriated capital. Profit remittances from an Ontario corporation are subject to standard Part XIII Canadian withholding tax unless reduced by treaty, but are not blocked or deferred by any FX control mechanism. Currency risk is fully borne by the treasury and can be hedged in the onshore interbank market.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists under Canadian law. An Ontario exporter may retain 100% of foreign currency earned from sales in an onshore foreign currency account or offshore account. The sole statutory obligation is to file a B13A Export Declaration with CBSA when export values exceed CAD 2,000 or the goods are controlled, and to report trade data to Statistics Canada. No timeline or conversion ratio applies to such proceeds.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no dedicated foreign exchange control administration exists due to the liberalised exchange control regime, with OSFI as prudential banking supervisor, CBSA administering cross-border currency declaration enforcement, and FINTRAC as AML/CFT financial intelligence unit.

Defense evidence: Notarised certificate of corporate good standing, register of directors and officers, share register and UBO schedule, passport copies, proof of residential address, board resolution appointing bank signatories, and corporate organisational chart describing actual control.

Defense evidence: Share subscription agreement or shareholder loan agreement, board resolution authorising the funding, source-of-wealth statements of lenders, corporate registry extracts from lender jurisdiction, and transfer documentation proving the banking trail of funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory Local/Master File threshold in Canadian law; contemporaneous documentation under ITA s. 247(4) and CRA IC 87-2R applies when cross-border non-arm's-length transactions occur; small business documentation relief applies where gross revenue < CAD 50,000,000 and aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions < CAD 5,000,000 per year; Country-by-Country Report filing required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue ≥ CAD 750,000,000

      
Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulation in Canada; corporate residency is determined by central management and control (place of effective management), and the GAAR (ITA s. 245) denies benefits of avoidance transactions lacking a bona fide business purpose; a 'shell' Canadian entity with no office, employees, or operational activity may be ignored for tax purposes, its income attributed to the foreign parent, and the foreign parent deemed to carry on business in Canada (PE risk); loans to non-resident shareholders are deemed dividends under ITA s. 15(2)/212(2), exposing them to Part XIII WHT.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Criminal)
        
Statutory sanction: Civil penalty 50% of understated tax under ITA s. 163(2); criminal fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years (ITA s. 239(1)); CRA publishes taxpayer names in the Tax Evasion Conviction list

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or direct the evasion are personally liable for penalties; criminal convictions result in personal fines and jail terms (max 2 years); CRA may impose travel restrictions (e.g., court-ordered surrender of passports pending trial), freeze personal bank accounts, and issue garnishment against personal assets

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under ITA s. 247(2); penalty under s. 247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment amount where documentation is not prepared and the adjustment is material; interest compounded daily on both the tax and penalty

        
Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under ITA s. 227.1 (joint and several), but for TP penalties directors are generally not assessed unless they signed false returns (s. 163(2) applies to the corporation; officers directing the false filing can be convicted under s. 239)

      

      
      

        Surplus Stripping / Disguised Repatriation
        
Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as taxable dividends under ITA s. 84(2)/(3) and s. 245 GAAR; Part XIII WHT of 25% (or treaty rate) assessed on the deemed dividend plus arrears interest; GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit under the 2024 expanded GAAR framework

        
Director personal liability: Directors who authorize the stripping transaction may be assessed for the amount of Part XIII tax withheld that was not remitted (ITA s. 227.1); personal liability applies to the full amount of the unremitted WHT plus interest and penalties; travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed in CRA collection proceedings
