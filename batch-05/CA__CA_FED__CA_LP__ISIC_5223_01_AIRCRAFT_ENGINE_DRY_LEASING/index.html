Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

No federal registry has general authority to form or register a Canadian limited partnership. Registration must be made with the corporate or business registry of the selected province or territory, such as Corporations Canada only where a federal corporation is used as general partner, and the applicable provincial or territorial registry for the limited partnership itself.

Governing statute

No general federal limited-partnership incorporation statute applies. A Canadian limited partnership is formed under the applicable provincial or territorial Limited Partnerships Act or Partnership Act, typically through registration of a declaration or statement of limited partnership. The Canada Business Corporations Act does not govern limited partnerships. Federal tax treatment is principally addressed under the Income Tax Act, including partnership rules in sections 96 and related provisions.

Formation / constitutional filings

Provincial or territorial Declaration, Certificate, or Statement of Limited Partnership, together with any required extra-provincial registration and business-name filings. The exact instrument and terminology depend on the province or territory of formation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No general statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Canadian limited partnership. Capital commitments, contribution schedules, profit allocations, withdrawal rights, and default remedies should be specified in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account is generally required, although lender, investor, aircraft-owner, or transaction documents may require a segregated account.

Funding & unlock path: No special civil-defence or workplace clearance is required for a non-operational leasing office beyond ordinary municipal occupancy, fire-code, occupational-health-and-safety, and building requirements. If engines are stored, repaired, tested, or handled at a facility, obtain applicable municipal fire approvals, hazardous-material and dangerous-goods controls, structural and floor-load verification, emergency-response procedures, worker training, and provincial or territorial occupational-health-and-safety compliance. Transport Canada and airport-authority approvals may also apply to airport premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No general statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Canadian limited partnership. Capital commitments, contribution schedules, profit allocations, withdrawal rights, and default remedies should be specified in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account is generally required, although lender, investor, aircraft-owner, or transaction documents may require a segregated account.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No general industrial licensing board is required for aircraft-engine dry leasing. Transport Canada Civil Aviation may regulate aircraft registration, airworthiness, maintenance organizations, and aviation operations, but a passive lessor that does not operate aircraft or perform maintenance is generally not required to hold an air operator certificate or maintenance-organization approval.

Category: No general operating or manufacturing permit for passive dry leasing of aircraft engines. Transport Canada aircraft-registration, import, export, airworthiness, maintenance, or operational approvals may apply to the owner, operator, importer, exporter, or maintenance provider depending on the transaction and physical activities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online and My Business Account portal; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) – Canadian Export Reporting System for customs export declarations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity applies for a Business Number (BN) through the CRA Business Registration Online portal or by filing Form RC1. For a Canadian Limited Partnership, the partnership obtains its own BN and, if engaged in taxable supplies, an RT program account for GST/HST. Registration is mandatory when taxable supplies in Canada exceed the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits. Non-resident partners may require their own BN for Canadian withholding and reporting obligations but are not separately registered solely by virtue of being a partner.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – a Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under section 96 of the Income Tax Act; no federal entity-level income tax is imposed on the LP and income is allocated to partners. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is a separate 'person' capable of carrying on commercial activity and must register and account for GST/HST as a registrant to the extent it makes taxable supplies in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box or analogous entity-classification election. Applicable elections include: (i) Excise Tax Act section 156 election to treat taxable supplies between closely related registrants as having nil consideration for GST/HST purposes; (ii) Income Tax Act subsection 249.1(4) election to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period for a partnership; and (iii) Income Tax Act subsection 216(1) election by a non-resident partner to report Canadian-source rental or leasing income on a net Part I basis rather than a gross Part XIII withholding basis, subject to treaty and administrative conditions. Hybrid mismatch benefits are not elective and remain subject to the Canadian hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (C$30,000) in taxable supplies made in Canada during a calendar quarter or the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; a non-resident lessor whose Canadian taxable supplies exceed the threshold must register, subject to place-of-supply rules and tax treaty limitations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered supplier may claim zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act for a supply of goods, including a lease, made to a non-resident recipient where the goods are exported from Canada as part of the transaction and are not acquired for consumption, use or supply in Canada. For a dry lease of a turbofan aircraft engine, zero-rating is available if the engine is delivered to the foreign lessee outside Canada or shipped by common carrier to a destination outside Canada, and the lease documents demonstrate that the engine was placed at the disposal of the non-resident outside Canada. Services performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to a non-resident in direct relation to exported goods may also qualify. The supply is not automatically zero-rated merely because the lessee is non-resident; the engine must not remain in Canada or be first used in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing export from Canada to the foreign lessee's jurisdiction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX receipt evidencing repatriation of lease consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, digital stamping regime or government-mandated API invoice clearance exists at the federal level in Canada. GST/HST invoices must satisfy Excise Tax Act section 169(1) particulars, including supplier Business Number, date, description, consideration and tax charged; electronic records are accepted. CBSA export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System is a customs export control filing, not a fiscal e-invoice clearance.

Improper or unsupported export zero-rating may result in CRA reassessment of unremitted GST/HST, prescribed interest, and a gross negligence penalty under Excise Tax Act section 285.1 equal to the greater of C$100 and 25% of the understated tax. Wilful evasion or participation in a false export claim may lead to criminal prosecution, fines ranging up to 50%–200% of the evaded amount, and imprisonment; related refunds can be denied and clawed back within the normal reassessment period or longer where misrepresentation or neglect is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions; no exchange-control legislation restricts payments or profit remittances. Aircraft-engine dry-lease rentals and distributions from a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident limited partners are freely remittable in any convertible currency. The only structural frictions are: (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on partnership distributions to non-resident limited partners (statutory 25%, treaty-reducible under Canada's income tax conventions and subject to the partnership conduit rules of ITA 96(8)); (ii) FINTRAC reporting of cross-border electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more; and (iii) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments and FDI survey filings covering lease receivables and non-resident capital contributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no required conversion ratio for export or service receipts. Dry-lease rental income may be retained offshore in foreign currency, netted against lease obligations, or settled in US dollars without Bank of Canada or Canada Revenue Agency consent.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with exchange and balance-of-payments functions coordinated through the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF and cross-border EFT reporting, and Statistics Canada for international transactions and foreign direct investment surveys. Canada maintains no standalone Foreign Exchange Control Administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rated Medium-Low as a primary treasury rail and Medium as a contingency corridor. Canada does not issue EU-style electronic-money-institution licences; non-bank fintechs operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC. Offshore digital EMIs can offer float, multi-currency settlement, and rapid cross-border transfers, but they cannot hold notional Canadian-dollar demand-deposit accounts (deposit-taking is reserved to Schedule I banks), lack direct LVTS membership and CDIC deposit insurance, and are commonly disfavoured by aviation-title or leasing lenders as principal lease-settlement counterparties. The EMI should therefore be used only for pre-funding, payroll, or low-value operational flows, never as the sole clearing rail for engine-lease receivables or capital escrow.

Route capital injections exclusively via SWIFT MT103, Fedwire, or ACH in CAD/USD, using ISO 20022 purpose code TREA or CAPX, with the beneficiary name exactly matching the registered limited partnership name. The paying bank must attach the partnership capital-call notice, and the receiving Canadian bank will require source-of-wealth statements from each contributing limited partner, wire instructions matching the account mandate, and beneficial-owner confirmation.
