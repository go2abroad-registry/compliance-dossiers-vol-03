Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/corporate-services/business-registration-online.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must first confirm its requirement to register for a Business Number (BN) and GST/HST. If taxable Canadian-source supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single quarter or across four consecutive quarters (small-supplier threshold), registration is mandatory; otherwise voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits. Application is made via the CRA BRO portal, through an authorized representative (e.g., a Canadian accountant or law firm), or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The partnership is registered in its own name for GST/HST purposes; a T5013 income tax information return is filed separately for each fiscal period. Provinces with provincial VAT may require additional provincial registrations (e.g., QST in Quebec, PST in Manitoba/Saskatchewan/B.C. if applicable). The CRA issues the BN and GST/HST program account with a unique account number (e.g., 123456789RT0001). Non-residents without a Canadian physical presence may need to appoint a resident agent or use a representative contact within Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election. Limited partnerships are statutorily transparent under the Income Tax Act; allocations are made to partners who pay tax at their own rates. Certain statutory elections affect timing/reporting (e.g., partnership fiscal period election under s. 249.1, GST/HST s. 156 election for closely related persons, and s. 216 election for non-residents filing on net rental income). Hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.2–18.4) apply automatically to payments involving outbound/inbound hybrid structures; no elective mismatch election exists in Canadian domestic law. A limited partnership that is a specified investment flow-through (SIFT) may lose pass-through treatment and be taxed at the entity level on non-portfolio earnings, but this is a statutory status trigger, not an elective check-the-box regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada during any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold). Non-residents performing services outside Canada (offshore) are generally outside the Canadian GST/HST regime unless the services are in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For the on-site factory inspection/testing/QC service model, services performed outside Canada on tangible personal property situated outside Canada are zero-rated (Part V, s. 7). Supplies of services to a non-resident are also zero-rated provided the service is not in respect of real property in Canada, nor of tangible personal property located in Canada, and the benefit of the service is not enjoyed by a Canadian resident at the time the service is performed. Exported goods (e.g., sample merchandise) qualify for zero-rating when the supplier ships or delivers the goods out of Canada, retains proof of export, and satisfies all evidentiary rules. For zero-rated services, the supplier must be registered for GST/HST, but is eligible to claim input tax credits. If the LP is a non-resident without a Canadian permanent establishment, it may still claim GST/HST refunds for input tax credits attributable to zero-rated activities by filing a GST/HST rebate application (e.g., s. 262 for non-residents).

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (B13A / AER report for goods exported at CAD 2,000 or above, or as required by Statistics Canada)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing outward movement from Canada and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-exchange receipt substantiating the actual receipt of export proceeds in Canadian or convertible currency within 180 days

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or pre-approval of invoices exists in Canada. GST/HST is self-assessed by the registrant. Invoices must satisfy the Excise Tax Act (s. 223) recording requirements and be retained for audit. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory via GST/HST NETFILE, E-Services, or the Represent a Client portal when taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million in the qualifying threshold period; non-electronic filers may be subject to penalties. Registrants with annual taxable supplies of CAD 10 million or more are required to remit GST/HST electronically via CRA-approved systems. There is no invoice-level digital stamping or tax authority clearance requirement, but CRA has the legal power to demand real-time access to point-of-sale and electronic accounting records during an audit.

CRA audits zero-rating claims and denies the zero rate if the exporter fails to retain sufficient documentary proof of export or foreign recipient status. Reassessment authority extends 4 years from the date of the original assessment or notice of determination; this window is unlimited for misrepresentation (including fraud or negligent omission). Penalties may include recapture of input tax credits and reversed zero-rating (tax becomes collectible at 5%), plus compound interest under s. 280.1. Specific penalties under s. 285 for false statements range up to 25% of the net tax attributed to the false statement (and up to 100% for gross negligence). Criminal tax evasion penalties under s. 239 can be up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. A director/officer of the partnership may be personally liable for unpaid taxes under s. 323 of the Excise Tax Act. CRA routinely scrutinizes FPXR (Foreign Proof of Export) for fictitious exports and shell-company invoicing; documented inward remittance evidence within the 180-day statutory deadline is mandatory to maintain zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible with a fully open capital account. The Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act and the Income Tax Act impose no exchange-control permit, no prior Bank of Canada approval, and no restriction on current-account or capital-account transactions for a licensed Canadian Limited Partnership. Profit and capital remittances to non-resident limited partners are unrestricted; taxation is limited to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5–15% under Canada's double-tax treaties), and the remittance is subject solely to anti-avoidance rules (GAAR, foreign accrual property income rules) and to the LP's own Canadian source taxation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada mandates no surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline. The CA Federal LP may retain foreign-currency receivables (USD, CNY, EUR) in foreign-currency ledger accounts and convert or disburse them at its discretion. The only compliance overlay is FINTRAC's Large Cash Transaction reporting and Large International Electronic Funds Transfer reporting (threshold C$10,000) plus the record-keeping duty for every international electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and financial-stability authority; Canada operates no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration. Prudential supervision of Schedule I banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision and Money Service Business (MSB) registration is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Medium viability for auxiliary rails only. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital must arrive via SWIFT MT103 wire from the foreign GP or the limited partners' own corporate accounts into the LP's Schedule I account (or the CIBC capital escrow account). The wire must include: (i) beneficiary legal name and full account number with bank transit and institution numbers; (ii) the Canadian bank's SWIFT/BIC; (iii) purpose narrative 'Capital contribution to limited partnership — [capital call notice number]' with the ISO 20022 classification CONT (capital contribution); (iv) prior notice to the bank's KYC desk with the signed capital call notice and the LP agreement excerpt; (v) for contributions above C$100,000, the pre-clearance defense dossier described above. Clearing occurs via SWIFT to the Canadian receiving bank and then through Lynx (Canada's real-time gross settlement system) for same-day value. Amounts of C$10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC Large International Electronic Funds Transfer Reports at both remitting and receiving ends.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 wire transfer for supplier invoice settlements under UCP600 rules; documentary-trade settlements for factory quality-control retainers use an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the LP's bank via SWIFT MT700 with a sight draft and the inspection certificate presented as a required document. CAD and USD disbursements settle through Lynx; same-day FX conversion is executed through CLS Bank eligible market contracts; intra-Canada vendor payments clear through Lynx and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for low-value disbursements. An independent audit becomes mandatory when: (i) the LP is a reporting issuer under provincial securities legislation (annual audited financial statements required under NI 41-101); (ii) the LP is a federally regulated financial institution subject to OSFI supervision; or (iii) the partnership agreement, a lender, or a government contracting authority expressly requires audited financial statements. Absent such triggers, a review engagement or Notice to Reader compilation is sufficient.
