Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal: CRA Business Registration Online, My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 through Business Registration Online. The BN is the federal tax identifier and supports separate accounts for income tax (RC), GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM). The Canadian limited partnership must hold its own BN to file the T5013 partnership information return, and non-resident GPs must register for GST/HST once taxable supplies in Canada exceed the small-supplier threshold of C$30,000 in the preceding four calendar quarters, or immediately if the entity is a listed financial institution.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under ITA s.96, a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; the GP and limited partners separately report their shares of partnership income and loss, while the LP itself files only an information return (T5013). For GST/HST purposes the LP is a distinct registrant; sales of fund securities are exempt supplies and management fee income is generally taxable at 5% GST or the applicable HST rate. — A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 through Business Registration Online. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under ITA s.96, a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; the GP and limited partners separately report their shares of partnership income and loss, while the LP itself files only an information return (T5013). For GST/HST purposes the LP is a distinct registrant; sales of fund securities are exempt supplies and management fee income is generally taxable at 5% GST or the applicable HST rate.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; classification follows legal form under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act. Available elections include ITA s.102(2.2) to deem a non-Canadian partnership a Canadian partnership where it carries on business through a permanent establishment in Canada, ITA s.216 for non-residents to elect net-basis taxation of Canadian rental and timber income, ITA s.217 for non-resident employees, and ETA s.156 elections between closely related corporations or partnerships to treat inter-entity supplies as nil for GST/HST. Hybrid mismatch rules are implemented as mandatory anti-deferral measures rather than elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada during the immediately preceding four calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold under ETA s.148); a non-resident person that is a listed financial institution carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST without regard to the threshold; a VC GP supplying management services to a Canadian-resident fund will generally be required to register once fees exceed C$30,000 and must then collect 5% GST or applicable HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI for goods that are actually exported by the supplier or delivered to an export destination, and for services supplied to non-residents unless the service relates to Canadian real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or is rendered to an individual physically in Canada. For a VC GP, portfolio management and administrative services supplied to a non-resident fund are generally zero-rated where the fund does not hold Canadian real estate or Canadian-situated tangible assets; management services to a Canadian-resident fund remain taxable. Financial services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under ETA s.10(2) subject to carve-outs for services relating to Canadian resident financial instruments or Canadian establishments; registrants recover zero-rated export input tax credits through normal GST/HST net-filing.

  • CRA Business Number (BN) or exporter identification cited on the export declaration
  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration equivalent) with HS codes and value
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing shipment to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms 2020 and stated destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Forwarder’s certificate or customs broker confirmation of physical export

No federal real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate exists; CRA accepts electronic invoices and machine-readable records without digital stamping. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable sales exceeding C$1M, and payments remitted by wire transfer exceeding C$10,000 may trigger anti-money-laundering reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess GST at 5% (plus provincial components) where export documentation is incomplete, goods are not exported within a reasonable time, or services are not in fact supplied to a non-resident abroad; compound quarterly interest applies on reassessments. Penalties include ETA s.280 (greater of C$250 and 25% of the net tax sought to be evaded), ETA s.281 for participation in false statements, and ITA s.239 criminal penalties of up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years for fraudulent export schemes. The normal reassessment window is three years for corporations, extended to unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established; CRA record-retention requirements require export evidence to be kept for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely floating and fully convertible currency under the Currency Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52). Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted; no Bank of Canada approval is required for conversion, profit remittance, or repatriation of capital. For a Venture Capital General Partner, the only structural hurdles are: (i) the Investment Canada Act review threshold, applicable only where a non-Canadian acquires 'control' of a Canadian business — a passive LP capital call normally falls below the WTO-review threshold; (ii) Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on distributions of income to non-resident partners, reduced to 15% or less under applicable tax treaties (e.g., Canada–US, Canada–UK); and (iii) FINTRAC reporting of cross-border fund transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 per 24-hour period. Profit remittance is therefore unrestricted, subject only to treaty-based withholding documentation and CRS/FATCA self-certification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory regime: NONE. Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. The former Exchange Control restrictions were repealed and are no longer reflected in the Currency, Mint and Exchange Fund Act or the Bank of Canada Act. The only administrative duty for a Canadian VC GP is indirect: (i) CBSA export declaration for goods, not applicable to management services; and (ii) FINTRAC's cross-border currency/transfer reporting threshold of CAD 10,000. There is no statutory timeline for conversion of inbound or outbound proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) exercising prudential licensing and supervision of Canadian banks, the Department of Finance Canada administering foreign-exchange policy and the Investment Canada Act, and FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) as the national AML/ATF intelligence unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability: LOW for core treasury function; CONDITIONAL for ancillary flows. Canadian securities law (National Instrument 81-102 s.6 and the CSA 'qualified custodian' rule) requires investment fund assets to be held by a qualified custodian, namely a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank, a trust company regulated by OSFI, or a registered dealer. A foreign fintech EMI (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Qonto) as sole custodian would (i) trigger registration of the GP as a money services business under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, (ii) generate an automatic high-risk AML rating because EMI screening is not OSFI-regulated, and (iii) prevent execution of the LPA's segregated capital-call custody provision. Permitted use is limited to FX conversion tickets below CAD 5,000, travel-expense reimbursement, and small urgent payments; capital calls and portfolio disbursements must never traverse an EMI.
