Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for the limited partnership; the Limited Partnership Agreement fixes capital contributions, and the general partner's contribution must be made as agreed in cash or kind; no statutory escrow account is mandated for partnership capital, although LME warrant counterparties and prime brokers may require segregated margin/collateral accounts.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), administered through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-registration-online.html) and the CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) with the GST/HST program account (RT suffix) by filing Form RC340 (Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts) with the CRA or by applying electronically through BRO. No minimum statutory capital or Canadian physical presence is required; the LP must appoint an authorized representative using Form RC59 (Business Consent Form) with a RepID for digital CRA correspondence, and may be expected to designate a Canadian agent for service of process where it carries on business in Canada. Registration becomes mandatory once the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded; an exporter making only zero-rated supplies may register voluntarily at any time in order to recover input tax credits. The BN/RT account is the statutory VAT identifier used on invoices, CBSA export declarations (CERS), and periodic GST/HST returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Capital account transactions are generally liberalized but require post-transaction reporting to the Bank of Canada. Profit remittances and partnership distributions to foreign partners are permitted without prior approval when supported by audited financial statements and beneficial ownership verification. Hurdles include FINTRAC registration, tax withholding under the Income Tax Act, and source-of-funds documentation for large or unusual transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds required. Canadian exporters must report transactions via the Canadian International Merchandise Trade Statistics if values exceed thresholds but may convert freely to CAD or foreign currency at their discretion without surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada
