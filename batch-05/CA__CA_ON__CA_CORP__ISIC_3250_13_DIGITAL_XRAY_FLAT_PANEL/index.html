Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada - official portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). Ontario corporate income tax is co-administered with the Ontario Ministry of Finance under the federal-provincial Corporate Tax Administration Agreement (CTAA); all registrations, filings, and assessments flow through the CRA systems using the federal Business Number.

E-government filing portal

Canada does not issue a separate TIN. Every registered corporation is assigned a unique 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RM (import/export), and RZ (non-resident withholding). Upon incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Ontario Business Corporations Act the CRA automatically issues the BN. Step-wise procedure for a foreign-owned entity: (1) Incorporate the subsidiary and obtain the BN; (2) Activate the RC program account through My Business Account - mandatory for filing the annual T2 corporate income tax return; (3) Register the RT (GST/HST) account online via the CRA 'Register a GST/HST account' web service or Form RC1 - registration is compulsory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; voluntary registration is available to recover input tax credits on exported goods; (4) A non-resident vendor operating without a Canadian subsidiary must file the CRA non-resident registration package and identify a Canadian agent for service; no separate Ontario tax identifier is required because Ontario corporate income tax and the Ontario HST component are administered within the federal RC and RT accounts under the CTAA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Canadian-resident business corporation is taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act on its worldwide income. No pass-through or flow-through classification is available to an incorporated entity. The Ontario corporate income tax is assessed on the same taxable income through the federal T2 return pursuant to the CTAA; the entity is not a territorial or tax-exempt-zone classification.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; every corporation is axiomatically a taxable entity. Statutory elections available to this structure include: (i) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election among closely related corporations and partnerships to treat qualifying internal supplies as made for nil consideration for GST/HST neutrality; (ii) Income Tax Act s. 216(1) election by a non-resident recipient of Canadian rental income to be taxed on a net-income basis instead of gross Part XIII withholding; (iii) Income Tax Act s. 217 election by a non-resident employee to file a Canadian net-income return; (iv) Income Tax Act s. 83(2) capital-dividend election for a private corporation distributing tax-free capital dividend account balances; and (v) joint amalgamation and continuation elections. Hybrid-mismatch rules enacted under Income Tax Act ss. 18.7 and 18.8 are mandatory and grant no elective relief for cross-border payment mismatches or reverse-hybrid arrangements involving Canadian deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies by the person and associated persons over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, with a single-quarter containment test of CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold). Registration is mandatory within 29 days of crossing the test. A non-resident that solicits orders in Canada or carries on business in Canada is also subject to registration once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available for exporters with no Canadian nexus to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of digital X-ray flat-panel detectors (tangible personal property) is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act where: (a) the supplier ships the goods to a destination outside Canada under a contract of sale or consignment; (b) the goods are delivered to a common carrier, freight forwarder, or customs broker for immediate export; or (c) possession transfers in Canada to a non-resident purchaser who exports the goods without use, consumption, or substantial transformation in Canada. The goods must not be diverted back into the Canadian market. Consignments valued at or above CAD 2,000 must be reported through the Canada Border Services Agency Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED). Domestic sales of OEM detector components to Canadian radiology equipment manufacturers attract 13% HST unless the goods fall within Schedule VI, Part II zero-rated medical-device provisions - a classification unlikely for a non-consumer OEM component. Services physically performed wholly outside Canada and freight/packaging services ancillary to the export are similarly zero-rated.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A / CAED electronic export declaration with CBSA-assigned transaction number) for consignments of CAD 2,000 or more
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or alternative cargo-control document evidencing physical departure from Canada to the named foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice containing full technical description, unit value, Incoterm, and an explicit export-destination statement (or customs-broker export certificate for Canada-U.S. shipments)
  • Foreign-purchaser contract or purchase order confirming sale to a non-resident and transfer of risk of loss outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 confirmation, or equivalent proof of settlement in foreign currency from the non-resident customer
  • CBSA export accounting acknowledgement and freight forwarder/carrier cargo documents evidencing the outbound international movement

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, government API authorization, mandatory digital stamping, or pre-approved e-invoice regime comparable to Latin American systems. GST/HST registrants must issue commercial tax invoices containing the supplier's Business Number and registration status; simplified invoices are permitted for supplies under CAD 150. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million, and remittances exceeding CAD 10,000 must be transmitted electronically through CRA NETFILE or My Business Account. The Pan-Canadian e-invoice interoperability framework based on the Peppol standard is voluntary and does not condition the entitlement to zero-rated export treatment.

Export zero-rating claims are priority CRA audit targets. The CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period, extend indefinitely where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud is present, reverse the zero-rating, and re-assess 13% HST on the full consideration with prescribed arrears interest. Civil penalties include the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the misstated net tax or refund for false statements or omissions under Excise Tax Act s. 280, and CAD 200 plus 50% of the net tax misstatement for wilful or grossly negligent conduct under s. 285. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Excise Tax Act s. 327 or Criminal Code s. 380 may also be initiated in cases of documentary fabrication or diversion of exported goods back into Canada. Records must be retained for six years under Excise Tax Act s. 286. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day settlement window is the dossier benchmark for export collection discipline, and prolonged open receivables beyond that period will increase documentation scrutiny but do not by themselves trigger a statutory penalty.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating exchange rate regime with fully convertible current and capital accounts. Non-resident direct investment, equity injections, loan repayments, dividend repatriation, and profit remittances are unrestricted and require no central bank approval. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is globally fungible. Profit remittance is subject solely to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%–15% under the Canada-U.S. and similar income tax conventions) and, for business acquisitions by non-residents, to the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review thresholds (greenfield manufacturing projects are not reviewable). Cross-border transactions must be reported to Statistics Canada for balance-of-payments purposes under the International Transactions Reporting System, and cash/negotiable instruments equal to or exceeding C$10,000 crossing the border must be declared to CBSA/FINTRAC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may freely keep foreign currency offshore in non-resident bank accounts and may convert CAD to foreign currency or vice versa without restriction. Documentary compliance, however, requires retention of customs invoices, bills of lading, CBSA export declarations, and underlying commercial contracts for six years. For goods on Canada's Export Control List (including categories covering imaging/radiation-emitting medical devices), a Global Affairs Canada export permit must be obtained prior to shipment, and all transactions must screen against economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and Global Affairs Canada (sanctions and export controls); no dedicated foreign exchange control agency exists in Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a supplemental collection/disbursement rail; low viability as a primary or escrow account. Canada does not recognize the European electronic money institution (EMI) licence. Foreign digital payment providers (e.g., Wise for Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business) may operate only as money service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). They cannot take deposits, hold client funds in true escrow, or provide credit. Federally incorporated Schedule I banks remain mandatory for the capital escrow account, equity injection routing, and LC issuance. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident parent should remit equity capital via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) to the Ontario corporation's Schedule I bank account, using remittance information 'EQUITY-CONTRIBUTION-[CIN]' referencing the board resolution. CAD-denominated capital is credited through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system; USD-denominated capital clears over Fedwire/CHIPS through the Canadian bank's New York correspondent. No Bank of Canada authorization or exchange-control approval is required. If the capital injection results in acquisition of control of a Canadian business, an Investment Canada Act post-closing notification or application for review may be triggered based on the enterprise value threshold. Statistics Canada reporting of the inward direct-investment flow is mandatory. Payments in cash or bearer instruments exceeding C$10,000 crossing the border must be reported to CBSA/FINTRAC; the corresponding wire itself is reported by the bank under the PCMLTFA large-value transaction rules.Trade disbursements for component imports and equipment purchases should be executed on two rails: (i) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for high-volume low-value supplier payments where no documentary protection is required, and (ii) irrevocable commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) with CAD or USD tenor for capital equipment (vacuum deposition chambers, sputtering targets, a-Si TFT panels, cesium iodide scintillators) and for back-to-back OEM supply contracts. CAD payments clear over Lynx; USD payments clear via the bank's correspondent network through Fedwire/CHIPS. All disbursements are subject to FINTRAC reporting, Canadian autonomous sanction screening (Special Economic Measures Act), and, where the exported finished DR detector contains controlled imaging components, to a Global Affairs Canada export permit and documentary compliance under the CBSA export reporting regime. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility as a supplemental collection/disbursement rail; low viability as a primary or escrow account. Canada does not recognize the European electronic money institution (EMI) licence. Foreign digital payment providers (e.g., Wise for Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business) may operate only as money service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). They cannot take deposits, hold client funds in true escrow, or provide credit. Federally incorporated Schedule I banks remain mandatory for the capital escrow account, equity injection routing, and LC issuance. The fintech rail may be layered as a sub-account for recovery of low-value vendor invoices, provided the service provider holds a valid FINTRAC MSB registration and the treasury function maintains a consolidated cash-position report.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000 aggregate value per non-arm's-length related party for mandatory Form T106 and related Schedule 91 filing; contemporaneous Master File and Local File documentation expected for taxpayers with gross revenue exceeding CAD 50 million or cross-border transactions above CAD 5 million; country-by-country report required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue over CAD 750 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, economic substance is material through the general anti-avoidance rule (section 245 of the Income Tax Act), treaty-shopping and beneficial-ownership analysis, and the section 247(1) arm's-length requirement. Searches for functional, asset, and risk substance are used by the CRA when validating intercompany profit allocations for medical device OEM structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine from 50% to 200% of taxes evaded and imprisonment for up to two years; gross negligence civil penalty under section 163.2 equal to 50% of the understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes and unpaid GST/HST under section 227.1, with no due-diligence defense available for knowingly authorizing evasion; conviction can trigger civil restitution orders, travel bans under mutual legal assistance treaties, and extradition or imprisonment
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under section 245, adjustment of intercompany prices under section 247, denial of deductions, transfer pricing penalties up to 10% of the net adjustment for transfer pricing gross negligence, and the 50% gross negligence penalty under section 163.2; CRA may also impose a 25% Part XIII gross-up for recharacterized dividends

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize the sham structure can face director liability assessments for the corporation's tax debts, civil fraud claims by creditors or taxing authorities, and adverse personal assessments if the corporate veil is pierced; travel bans are possible where large outstanding tax liabilities are combined with asset departure risk
AML / Suspicious Transaction Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per serious violation for regulated entities; criminal money-laundering under section 462.31 of the Criminal Code carries up to ten years' imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be named in FINTRAC penalty assessments if they directed or acquiesced in AML non-compliance; criminal charges may lead to fines, restitution, travel restrictions imposed by bail or sentencing conditions, and imprisonment for responsible officers
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: 10% penalty on the amount of any transfer pricing adjustment arising from a gross or negligent failure to use arm's-length pricing; late-filing penalty for T106 of CAD 1,000 to CAD 8,000 per omission; additional interest and penalties on reassessed tax

Director personal liability: Director personal liability generally does not apply to pure transfer pricing penalties, but directors who sign false T106 returns or knowingly permit fraudulent documentation can be prosecuted under section 239, subject to civil restitution and imprisonment; indirect liability may arise through a director's breach of fiduciary duty in ignoring CRA settlement demands
