Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) administers federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, and Ontario corporate income tax (under the federal–provincial collection agreement) through the My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Ontario Ministry of Finance administers provincial revenue programs (e.g., Retail Sales Tax on specified supplies) via the Ontario Business Information System; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers import/export program accounts, CARM, and customs border enforcement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a statutory Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online or Form RC1. Procedure: (1) request the 9-digit BN and activate a GST/HST program account (RT) if the entity makes taxable supplies carrying on business in Canada, supplies digital/remote services in Canada, or exceeds the small-supplier threshold; non-resident vendors of remote/digital supplies may use CRA's simplified foreign supplier registration. (2) Activate a corporate income tax program account (RC) and file a T2 return under Part I if carrying on business in Canada, disposing of taxable Canadian property, making a s. 216 election, or otherwise having a Canadian permanent establishment. (3) Obtain a CBSA RM (import/export) program account and CARM credentials for export declarations. (4) For Ontario corporate tax, allocate taxable income to Ontario on T2 Schedule 501/504; an Ontario RST vendor permit is only required for RST-taxed activities. Processing is typically completed within 1–5 business days; a foreign entity with no Canadian location should appoint an agent for service of process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA). A resident corporation pays federal and Ontario corporate income tax on worldwide income; a non-resident corporation pays Part I tax on income from carrying on business in Canada and taxable Canadian property gains, plus Part XIII withholding on passive Canadian-source payments. No pass-through status is available under Canadian law; shareholders are not taxed on corporate income until dividends are paid.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a 'check-the-box' election for entity classification; classification follows corporate legal form. Relevant elections for a foreign-owned Canada-incorporated entity include: (i) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident corporation to file a Part I return and be taxed on a net basis on Canadian business or employment income rather than Part XIII gross withholding; (ii) ITA s. 219 election for taxation on gains from disposal of taxable Canadian property by a non-resident corporation; (iii) GST/HST s. 156 election enabling closely related corporations to treat certain intercompany supplies as not taxable; (iv) simplified accounting/period-end elections under the Income Tax Regulations; and (v) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s. 18.4/18.5 apply without election to deny double deductions inconsistent with BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General GST/HST small-supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (and in the current quarter when, combined with the preceding three, the total exceeds CAD 30,000). Registration becomes mandatory when the threshold is exceeded, with exceptions for small suppliers. Non-resident vendors of digital or remote services (including online aligner planning software services) are required to register once revenue from such taxable supplies to Canadian recipients exceeds CAD 30,000 in any 12-month rolling period; no small-supplier threshold applies to specially registered non-resident remote digital suppliers. Low-value imported physical goods (value CAD 150 or less) are subject to GST/HST collection at the border through platforms/carriers, while goods valued above CAD 150 are assessed at customs clearance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of dental clear-aligner thermoformed sheets are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (s. 20–22), provided the vendor exports the goods itself, or delivers them to a common carrier or into a bonded service for export, within a reasonable time and retains prescribed documentary evidence. Supplies of medical devices, including custom-fitted clear aligners prescribed by a dentist and supplied to a named patient, are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part II and the Medical Devices Regulations when the product is a licensed Class II dental device and the documentation identifies the patient. Services such as custom thermoforming, trimming, and digital aligner treatment planning supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Canada are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VII, Part V (s. 9) if not performed in respect of goods located in Canada or Canadian real property. Zero-rating is unavailable for domestic commercial sales to distributors without patient-specific documentation; those supplies attract 13% HST. Input tax credit (ITC) claims are dependent on the same substantiation documents.

  • CBSA electronic export declaration filed through CARM or the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) system
  • Commercial invoice with correct HS classification (e.g., HS 3920.61/3920.69 for plastic sheets) and a statement 'GST/HST zero-rated export'
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or terminal receipt evidencing international movement of the goods
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank FX confirmation, or payment order evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency
  • CRA proof-of-export documentation worksheet or equivalent retained under ETA s. 286 (e.g., Form GST190)
  • Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence / product licence documentation if the medical-device zero-rating is relied upon

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or a Peppol-type e-invoicing clearance regime for GST/HST. Registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information (ETA s. 221(1)) and retain them for six years to substantiate input tax credits; simplified invoices may be used for retail sales. Electronic filing of returns is mandatory for prescribed registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 10,000,000 and for all new GST/HST registrants, through GST/HST NETFILE or web services. Export documentation must be filed electronically through the CBSA CARM/CAED system; there is no government pre-approval of each commercial invoice.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within four years after the original notice of assessment; the limitation period is unlimited where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. If prescribed export documentation cannot be produced contemporaneously, the full 13% HST is assessed on the supply, with arrears interest and penalties. Specific penalties: late or missing GST/HST return penalties of 5% of the net tax remittance plus 1% per month (maximum 12 months); gross negligence penalty under ETA s. 285.1 of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the deficiency; intentional evasion may attract a fine of 50%–200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years under ETA s. 327. Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST. CBSA export-reporting failures are subject to AMPS penalties up to CAD 25,000 per contravention. Although Canadian income tax law does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for physical exports, maintaining a 180-day FX repatriation cycle and documentary consistency is prudent to avoid transfer-pricing adjustments, deemed benefits, or Part XIII withholding inquiries in audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating currency with full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There are no formal exchange-control restrictions, no requirement for central bank approval for foreign-exchange transactions, and no differentiated treatment between current-account and capital-account settlements. Profit remittances in the form of dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees are permitted without exchange-control authorization; the only frictions are tax-based, namely Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (s. 212) at a statutory rate of 25%, generally reduced by an applicable tax treaty, and the obligation to file Form T106 for reportable cross-border related-party transactions. Non-resident investment is further reviewed only for national-security or large-enterprise economic thresholds under the Investment Canada Act, which is not a currency-convertibility impediment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements in Canada. Exporters are not obliged to repatriate or convert foreign currency receipts into Canadian dollars, and no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory timeline applies. Export documentation is limited to customs and statistical reporting, such as the Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) under the Customs Act, required for shipments leaving Canada, and the reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for large cross-border wire transfers and virtual currency transactions. Voluntary conversion of export receipts into CAD is conducted exclusively at prevailing market rates through a domestic authorized bank or a registered money services business.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking regulation and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money laundering/anti-terrorist financing supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary collection or FX routing vehicle; Low as a primary treasury bank. Legal constraints: Canada does not recognize the European 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) passport. A foreign EMI wanting to operate in Canada must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) regime is fully enforced by the Bank of Canada, hold a retail payment activity licence. In practice, an offshore EMI (e.g., a UK FCA-authorised EMI) can open a USD or EUR collection account for the Canadian entity, but cannot legally serve as the main clearing bank for CAD-denominated payments unless licensed and locally established. Processing of platform receivables (e.g., credit-card settlements from Stripe) should be directed to a domestic operational account at a Tier 1 bank rather than an offshore EMI to avoid sanctions-screening gaps, CDIC deposit insurance loss, and FINTRAC registration friction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from a foreign parent or sole shareholder must be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the originating bank to the domestic beneficiary account held at a Tier 1 Canadian bank. The wire should identify the purpose of payment using the Canadian banking standard for direct investment, commonly coded as 'EQTY' or 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' in the beneficiary-to-originator information field, and include the narrative 'subscription for common shares of [Company]'. The receiving bank may hold funds in a suspense account pending completion of CDD, so the company must submit the board resolution approving the share issue, a subscription price breakdown, and the certificate of good standing of the investing entity. For USD capital injections, the intermediary clearing should be routed through the bank's US correspondent network via Fedwire or CHIPS; for EUR injections via TARGET2. All cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 in a single day are reported by the bank to FINTRAC under the large-transaction reporting rules, and the company may be required to file Form T106 for any non-arm's-length inflow.Outbound trade settlements are cleared using (1) open-account SWIFT MT103 transfers for recurring resin or consumable purchases, with the DEBT/PURP code indicating the goods class; (2) irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit issued under SWIFT MT700 following UCP 600 for high-value imports such as thermoplastic aligner sheets, with a required presentation comprising the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and quality-inspection certificate; and (3) intercompany treasury transfers using MT202 COV when bank-to-bank settlement is needed for liquidity discipline. CAD-denominated disbursements settle in real time through Payments Canada's Lynx system; USD disbursements settle through the US Federal Reserve network or ACH via the bank's US clearing correspondents. Legal constraints: Canada does not recognize the European 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) passport. A foreign EMI wanting to operate in Canada must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) regime is fully enforced by the Bank of Canada, hold a retail payment activity licence. In practice, an offshore EMI (e.g., a UK FCA-authorised EMI) can open a USD or EUR collection account for the Canadian entity, but cannot legally serve as the main clearing bank for CAD-denominated payments unless licensed and locally established. Processing of platform receivables (e.g., credit-card settlements from Stripe) should be directed to a domestic operational account at a Tier 1 bank rather than an offshore EMI to avoid sanctions-screening gaps, CDIC deposit insurance loss, and FINTRAC registration friction.
