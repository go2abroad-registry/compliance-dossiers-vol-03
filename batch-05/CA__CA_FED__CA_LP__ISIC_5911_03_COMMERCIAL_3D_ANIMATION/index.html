Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada) for the corporate general partner; provincial limited partnership registrar (e.g., Ontario Companies and Partnerships Directorate via ServiceOntario) for the limited partnership; Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal Business Number and GST/HST registration.

Governing statute

No single federal Companies Act governs limited partnerships: the limited partnership is created under the applicable provincial Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16, ss. 2-7); the corporate general partner is incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44, ss. 5-18; federal tax recognition and registration occur under the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), s. 96.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (CBCA Form 4001) for the corporate general partner; Declaration/Certificate of Limited Partnership accompanied by the Limited Partnership Agreement; CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Canadian limited partnership or its corporate general partner; capital contributions are contractually fixed in the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow or capital trust account is required by federal rule; contributed funds may be held in a Canadian-domiciled commercial bank account immediately upon registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and occupancy permit issued by the municipal fire marshal; occupational health and safety clearance under the Canada Labour Code, Part II, and applicable provincial occupational health legislation; WHMIS training and safety data sheets for any cleaning or printing chemicals; structural load inspection confirming standard office floor loading for computing racks and rendering hardware.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); primary online registration channel: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and the CRA My Business Account portal. Provincial and territorial tax authorities separately administer provincial corporate, sales, payroll, and other taxes where applicable.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership generally obtains a CRA Business Number (BN) and applicable program accounts through CRA Business Registration Online or by submitting Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts. A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada, earning income from Canada, importing commercial goods, or registering for GST/HST must provide legal name, jurisdiction of formation, partnership details, principal business activity, Canadian business address or authorized representative information, ownership or partner information, and effective dates. The GST/HST program account is added to the BN when registration is required or elected. A non-resident partnership may also be required to file a T5013 Partnership Information Return and obtain or use the applicable partnership-related CRA identifiers. Registration for payroll, corporate income tax, or information-reporting accounts is completed separately where relevant. A foreign entity does not receive a separate universal Canadian VAT number; its GST/HST registration number is generally the BN followed by the RT program identifier and account suffix. CRA registration does not replace provincial registration, extra-provincial registration, municipal licensing, or customs registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a general United States-style check-the-box election for Canadian limited partnerships. A Canadian limited partnership is ordinarily fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes, subject to partnership allocation, basis, at-risk, specified-member, and anti-avoidance rules. Income and loss are generally allocated to partners under the partnership agreement and applicable Canadian tax rules, while the partnership may have information-reporting obligations. A limited partnership cannot generally elect to become a Canadian corporation merely through a tax classification filing. Certain statutory elections may apply to specific transactions, including elections under the Income Tax Act for transfers of property to a partnership, partnership wind-ups, debt or capital transactions, and other prescribed events. Hybrid-entity treatment may arise under foreign law, but there is no Canadian domestic hybrid mismatch election that converts the entity's default classification. Cross-border arrangements remain subject to Canada's hybrid mismatch rules, treaty limitation provisions, partnership anti-avoidance rules, and transfer-pricing requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding supplies of financial services and certain other excluded amounts, measured under the small-supplier rules over the applicable preceding four-quarter period or in a single calendar quarter. Voluntary registration is generally available before the threshold is exceeded. HST applies instead of the federal GST in participating provinces at combined rates of 13% or 15%, depending on the province. Registration and collection obligations can also arise under specified non-resident digital-economy rules without relying solely on the ordinary small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are generally zero-rated where the statutory place-of-supply and export conditions are met, including exportation by or on behalf of the supplier or delivery to a recipient outside Canada under prescribed circumstances. The supplier must retain evidence linking the goods to exportation, such as customs records, commercial documentation, transport records, and proof of delivery outside Canada. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated only where the specific zero-rating provision applies. For commercial CGI, visual-effects, and 3D-animation services, eligibility depends on the statutory service category, recipient residence, place where the service is performed, exclusions for services relating to Canadian real property or tangible personal property, and whether the service is provided to a person in Canada or to another person in connection with a person in Canada. Remote performance or a non-resident customer alone does not automatically establish zero-rating. Exporters may claim input tax credits for eligible GST/HST incurred in making zero-rated supplies, subject to registration, valid documentary support, and ordinary input-tax-credit restrictions. Non-resident suppliers may qualify for a rebate only under the specific non-resident rebate rules; the general commercial export treatment is normally achieved through zero-rating and input-tax-credit recovery.

  • Canada Border Services Agency export declaration or applicable electronic export reporting record, including the Canadian Export Reporting System filing where required
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, purchaser or consignee, goods or deliverables, value, currency, and terms of sale
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier documentation evidencing shipment from Canada to a destination outside Canada
  • Packing list and proof of delivery or foreign importation where relevant
  • Customs accounting, export permit, certificate, or controlled-goods documentation where required by the goods or destination
  • For exported services: executed contract, purchase order, customer tax-residency or non-resident evidence, service description, delivery records, work-product transmission records, timesheets, and evidence that statutory Canadian-place-of-supply exclusions do not apply
  • GST/HST invoices, input-tax-credit support, payment records, and bank records linking the transaction to the export supply
  • Inward remittance or foreign-exchange evidence where commercially available; Canada generally does not prescribe an inward-remittance certificate as a universal GST/HST zero-rating document

Canada has no general nationwide real-time VAT invoice-clearance system, mandatory digital tax stamping regime, or universal business-to-business electronic invoicing mandate for ordinary GST/HST transactions. Registered suppliers must issue invoices or other prescribed documentary evidence containing required GST/HST information, including supplier identity, GST/HST registration number where applicable, date, consideration, tax rate, and tax amount, at the relevant invoice-value thresholds. Electronic invoices are generally acceptable if statutory record-integrity and accessibility requirements are satisfied. GST/HST returns and remittances are filed electronically when CRA electronic-filing rules apply, including mandatory electronic filing for specified registrants. CRA may require electronic books and records, and import transactions use the Canada Border Services Agency's customs systems. Digital invoices do not themselves constitute CRA tax clearance.

Canada has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated to Canada within 180 days. The specified 180-day parameter is therefore not a Canadian foreign-exchange deadline and should not be treated as a legal filing or repatriation rule. CRA may deny zero-rating or input tax credits where export conditions, recipient status, place-of-supply rules, or documentary requirements are not satisfied, resulting in assessed GST/HST, interest, and applicable penalties. A person who knowingly or recklessly makes a false statement or omission may face gross-negligence penalties, generally 50% of the understated tax or overstated credit or refund, subject to statutory calculation rules. False statements, fraud, or wilful evasion may trigger prosecution and criminal penalties under the Excise Tax Act and Criminal Code, including fines and imprisonment. CRA may reassess within the statutory assessment period and may reassess beyond the normal period in cases involving misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful blindness, fraud, or specified waivers. Incorrect export treatment may also require repayment of improperly claimed input tax credits or rebates, with applicable interest and penalties. Customs violations can separately result in seizure, monetary penalties, export-reporting penalties, permit consequences, and criminal liability under customs and export-control legislation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar trades freely on a floating basis with no central parity, no exchange-control permit regime, no currency rationing, and no repatriation restriction for dividends, partnership distributions, royalties, or service export proceeds. Profit remittance from a Canadian Limited Partnership to non-resident partners is unrestricted from an FX perspective; the sole statutory friction is withholding/compliance administration at the Canada Revenue Agency level — Part XIII withholding of 25% on distributions to non-residents, reducible under an applicable income tax treaty and evidenced by CRA T4A-NR, NR6/NR301, and the T5013 partnership-return filings. The only genuine convertibility blocker is the application of financial sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act, which require the treasury to screen all counterparties and their ultimate beneficial owners against the Canadian sanctions registry and OFAC lists before any CAD or USD clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters and cross-border service suppliers may retain foreign currency offshore indefinitely or convert at will. For the ISIC 5911 animation studio, service exports do not attract the Customs Act export declaration threshold (CAD 2,000) applicable to goods; reporting is statistical only, through the Bank of Canada's Balance of Payments and Statistics Canada service-survey questionnaires (BP/SRV forms), which carry no conversion obligation. Banking practice requires electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to be recorded for FINTRAC, and central-bank-type statement reporting applies to any single transaction above CAD 10,000 in cash, yet no surrender rule is ever triggered.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority responsible for monetary policy, financial-system stability, bank-note issuance, and lender-of-last-resort functions. Canada maintains no dedicated 'Foreign Exchange Administration' agency; foreign-exchange supervision is structurally embedded in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking conduct, FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering client due diligence and large-value monitoring, and the Department of Finance Canada for international financial policy and sanctions administration. Payment-service-provider oversight is being consolidated under the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act registration regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM — permissible only as a secondary, low-value disbursement rail. Canadian law does not recognise the European EMU 'Electronic Money Institution' licence; a non-resident EMI must become a registered Money Services Business with FINTRAC and register under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada before offering payment services. Wise Business is viable for small/mid-value B2B payments (freelancer artist payouts, cloud-render invoices, sub-consultants) and offers CAD/USD/EUR/GBP pooling with mid-market FX, but it cannot provide commercial documentary letters of credit, capital escrow, CDIC-insured deposits, or Treasury-grade liquidity. The treasury blueprint must therefore centralise all client milestone receipts and capital injections through a tier-1 chartered bank, while a FINTRAC-registered PSP rail is permitted solely as a non-core disbursement channel with a strict per-transfer control threshold (e.g., below CAD 25,000) and full transaction-ledger audit export.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM — permissible only as a secondary, low-value disbursement rail. Canadian law does not recognise the European EMU 'Electronic Money Institution' licence; a non-resident EMI must become a registered Money Services Business with FINTRAC and register under the Retail Payment Activities Act with the Bank of Canada before offering payment services. Wise Business is viable for small/mid-value B2B payments (freelancer artist payouts, cloud-render invoices, sub-consultants) and offers CAD/USD/EUR/GBP pooling with mid-market FX, but it cannot provide commercial documentary letters of credit, capital escrow, CDIC-insured deposits, or Treasury-grade liquidity. The treasury blueprint must therefore centralise all client milestone receipts and capital injections through a tier-1 chartered bank, while a FINTRAC-registered PSP rail is permitted solely as a non-core disbursement channel with a strict per-transfer control threshold (e.g., below CAD 25,000) and full transaction-ledger audit export.
