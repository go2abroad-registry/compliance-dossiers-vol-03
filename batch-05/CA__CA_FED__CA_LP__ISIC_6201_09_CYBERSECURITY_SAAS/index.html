Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Provincial Companies and Partnerships Division (e.g., ServiceOntario Registry under the Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery) for the organic LP declaration; Canada Revenue Agency, Business Registration Online, for the federal business number and tax accounts.

Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Act of the Province of Registration, e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16, ss. 2-17 (Ontario), or B.C. Partnership Act, RSBC 1996, c. 348, Part 3; federal overlay: Income Tax Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), ss. 96-103, and the Canada Revenue Agency Business Number Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. B-9, s. 3. Canada does not maintain a federal LP registry; provincial registration forms the organic statute, with federal tax administration via the CRA. Provincial filing fees vary from CAD 50 to CAD 205.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form 1 LP) under the applicable provincial Limited Partnership Act; federal CRA Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts.

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; capital is set exclusively by the Limited Partnership Agreement and recorded in capital accounts. No escrow account is required for a non-regulated SaaS limited partnership under federal law; however, if employees are added, source deductions remitted to the CRA are held in statutory trust under Income Tax Act s. 227.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); corporate business and GST/HST registration portal: CRA My Business Account / Represent a Client

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or operating through a Canadian limited partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) for the partnership, unless the partnership is a small supplier. The partnership, as a deemed person for GST/HST purposes, must apply separately for a GST/HST account via Form RC1 ‘Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts’ (or online through the CRA Business Registration Online portal), unless the LP is below the $30,000 small-supplier threshold on aggregate worldwide taxable supplies. The general/non-resident partner, if registering in its own capacity, may also require a BN; non-resident partners cannot use a SIN and must obtain a BN. CRA will issue a nine-digit BN plus a two-character program account suffix (e.g., RT for GST/HST) upon completion. No physical local presence or notarized power of attorney is required for registration, but non-resident individuals may need authorization via a Canadian representative for portal access.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity establishing or operating through a Canadian limited partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) for the partnership, unless the partnership is a small supplier. The partnership, as a deemed person for GST/HST purposes, must apply separately for a GST/HST account via Form RC1 ‘Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts’ (or online through the CRA Business Registration Online portal), unless the LP is below the $30,000 small-supplier threshold on aggregate worldwide taxable supplies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime comparable to the US IRS; a limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for federal income tax, with income/loss computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners. For GST/HST, a Canadian partnership is statutorily deemed a person and may register as a GST/HST registrant; no election for complete tax-transparency can be made for GST/HST. A partnership may elect to use the ‘partnership annual GST/HST return’ (election under section 30 of the Excise Tax Act and prescribed form) where applicable, and non-resident partners can file returns on a calendar-year basis. The only relevant hybrid-mismatch measure is the federal ‘hybrid mismatch arrangement’ rules under the Income Tax Act (section 18.1 and section 20.4) which can deny deductions or recharacterize payments involving hybrid entities; the LP itself cannot elect to become an opaque entity for income tax purposes in Canada.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (annual worldwide taxable supplies threshold for ‘small supplier’ status; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration with CRA; separate thresholds and rules may apply for provincial HST and QST in Quebec)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI (GST/HST) if tangible personal property is exported from Canada by the supplier or the supplier causes it to be exported in compliance with the Evidence Requirements (customs export documentation). For services: zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI if the service is supplied to a non-resident who is not registered under GST/HST and the service is neither under section 9 (services related to real/tangible property in Canada) nor certain excluded supplies; cybersecurity SaaS (cloud-based vulnerability scanning / penetration testing) supplied to a non-resident non-registrant customer, with no Canadian in-use or consumption, generally qualifies as zero-rated exported service. Zero-rating also may apply when the service is supplied to a non-resident and the ‘in-ordinary-course’ exclusion is met. IT and cyber services delivered electronically to non-resident clients are not per se exempt; the supplier must demonstrate the non-resident recipient, absence of Canadian consumption, and valid documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or equivalent ACI/export transmission) for physically exported goods
  • Commercial invoices and packing lists evidencing shipment to a non-resident buyer
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill or equivalent transportation document showing departure from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT / wire transfer record showing payment from the non-resident recipient to a Canadian account
  • Written contract or service order confirming the non-resident recipient and offshore delivery/consumption of the SaaS (where no physical goods are shipped)

No federal statutory mandate requiring real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing approval, or digital stamping by the CRA. GST/HST invoices (i.e., ‘tax invoices’) may be issued in paper or electronic format if they contain prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act (e.g., supplier name, BN, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, amount of tax). Canada participates in the Pan-European Public On-Line Procurement (PEPPOL) network on a voluntary basis for e-procurement, but CRA does not operate a pre-clearance API for invoice stamping. Registrants must retain books and records in Canada or provide them on request, and may use CRA’s electronic filing services; there is no mandatory API-based real-time clearance mandate.

CRA may deny zero-rating and assess GST/HST at 5% (plus provincial HST/QST where applicable) if documentation is incomplete or if the supply is found to be made in Canada or to a Canadian resident/registrant. Penalties include: (i) under Excise Tax Act subsection 280(1) for failure to register (USD 10,000 or 10% of tax not collected, whichever is greater, for non-registrants); (ii) 8% per year arrears interest on unreported GST/HST compounded quarterly; (iii) ‘gross negligence’ penalties under subsection 285.1(2) of 50% of the tax or rebate/refund claimed; (iv) fines and imprisonment under section 327.1 for knowingly making false statements on tax returns; (v) retrospective reassessments for up to four years (extended to a 7-year limitation period where negligence or fraud is established) and no limitation period in case of misrepresentation attributable to fraud. CRA routinely scrutinizes export zero-rating claims from SaaS providers for evidence of non-resident use, export contracts, IP transfer, and FX repatriation; aggressive zero-rating of ‘cyber services’ supplied through Canadian cloud infrastructure without export evidence subjects the taxpayer to clawback of input tax credits claimed on related costs plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canadian law imposes no general exchange control restrictions on inward or outward remittances, foreign currency accounts, or profit repatriation. Profit remittances by a foreign-owned Canadian Limited Partnership are permissible on a current account basis, subject to domestic partnership taxation allocation rules and withholding provisions where applicable. Capital account transactions are open, though certain foreign investments in Canada may require review under the Investment Canada Act on national security or economic grounds; such review is not an FX restriction. No separate capital or current account convertibility licensing regime exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada maintains no mandatory export proceeds surrender regime and no statutory timeline or minimum conversion ratio requiring the repatriation or conversion of export receipts into CAD. Exporters may hold receipts in any currency; however, the Bank of Canada and Statistics Canada rely on International Transactions reporting for balance-of-payments measurement. For the cybersecurity SaaS revenue context (subscription-based cross-border receipts), no surrender requirement applies, and all invoicing and settlement can be freely denominated in CAD, USD, or any other convertible currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); in tandem with the federal prudential supervisor Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking conduct, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money laundering and terrorist financing compliance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to medium feasibility for offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs). Canadian federal prudential regulation treats deposit-taking as restricted to bank institutions licensed under the Bank Act; a non-resident EMI is not recognised as a bank in Canada and cannot offer a Canadian deposit account without partnering with a Schedule I bank. Offshore EMIs may offer payment facilitation or currency conversion services, but such arrangements face elevated AML/KYC scrutiny and are not a substitute for a domestic Canadian banking relationship. Settlement in Canada should therefore use a Schedule I or II bank account, with EMI rails limited to ancillary payment acceptance for low-value SaaS subscriptions. Legal constraints include FINTRAC registration for money service businesses, prospectus and clearing restrictions, and limitations on bank-partnership sponsorship programs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions and intercompany subordinated loans to the Canadian LP must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the partner's bank to the Canadian bank's clearing SWIFT address, with a purpose code that identifies the transaction as a current or capital account remittance. For capital injections, use a detailed narration such as 'Limited partner capital contribution — investment in Canadian LP' together with the registry number of the LP and the escrow account reference. Canadian banks rely on the payment instructions, the underlying partnership agreement, and the beneficial ownership declaration to confirm that the injection is lawful and not subject to investment review. No CFIUS-type approval or FX surrender certificate is required in Canada.Statutory and cross-border trade disbursements may be cleared through the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier payments, licensing fees, and intercompany remittances, using the Canadian bank's high-value clearing system (the Large Value Transfer System — LVTS, operated by Payments Canada); (2) the Bank of Canada's settlement account system for real-time gross settlement of CAD payments; (3) commercial letters of credit (LC) issued by the Canadian LP's bank on behalf of the LP for large vendor or strategic supplier acquisitions, with the LC being routed via MT700 to a nominated negotiating or confirming bank; and (4) automated clearing settlement (via Payments Canada) for recurring CAD payroll and operating costs. For USD payments, the LP may use a USD correspondent banking account held at one of the Canadian Schedule I banks, which clears on the CHIPS or Fedwire rails in the United States through the bank's US correspondent network.
