Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The corporate registry of the selected province or territory; Corporations Canada does not register limited partnerships. The Canada Revenue Agency administers business-number and tax-account registrations.

Governing statute

A Canadian limited partnership is not formed under a federal limited partnership statute. It must be registered under the applicable provincial or territorial Partnership Act or Limited Partnerships Act, generally through a declaration or registration filing. If a federally incorporated general partner is used, the Canada Business Corporations Act, including sections 6, 19, 106, 115 and 121, applies to that corporation only.

Formation / constitutional filings

Provincial or territorial Limited Partnership Declaration, Certificate of Limited Partnership, or Registration Statement, as prescribed by the applicable jurisdiction.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 57 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No general Canadian statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a limited partnership. Capital contributions, allocation rights, withdrawal rights, and any escrow or trust arrangement must be documented in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory incorporation escrow account is generally required; banking, sanctions, source-of-funds, and beneficial-ownership controls remain applicable.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal building, occupancy, fire-prevention, and emergency-egress approvals before opening. Complete fire-alarm, sprinkler, extinguishing-equipment, electrical, refrigeration, structural-load, accessibility, and seismic inspections where applicable. Implement occupational-health-and-safety training, hazard assessments, workplace policies, incident reporting, lifting controls, and workers’ compensation registration under the applicable provincial or territorial regime.

Paid-in capital rule: No general Canadian statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a limited partnership. Capital contributions, allocation rights, withdrawal rights, and any escrow or trust arrangement must be documented in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory incorporation escrow account is generally required; banking, sanctions, source-of-funds, and beneficial-ownership controls remain applicable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Ottawa; primary self-service portal is CRA My Business Account and the GST/HST NETFILE gateway, with federal business registrations also accessible through Business Registration Online (BRO).

E-government filing portal

The limited partnership must first be registered under applicable provincial limited partnership law, then file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and, if taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold, an associated GST/HST program account (RT suffix). A foreign corporate or individual limited partner with a Canadian filing obligation must obtain its own BN by filing the same RC1 and executing the requisite CRA authorization forms; where the LP itself has no Canadian physical presence, it must appoint a representative for service under ETA section 267 before the CRA will issue the BN and RT account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Territorial_Scope: A Canadian-resident limited partnership is a flow-through entity under ITA section 96, computing income or loss on Form T5013 and allocating the amounts to resident and non-resident partners, while the LP itself pays no federal income tax. Resident partners are taxed on their allocated share of worldwide LP income; non-resident limited partners are taxable in Canada only on the portion of LP income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment or a business carried on in Canada, subject to the deemed-residency rules of ITA subsection 94(1). No Tax_Exempt_Zone status applies to the retail grocery activity, although basic grocery supplies may be zero-rated for GST/HST as an indirect-tax measure.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a check-the-box entity classification election for partnerships; classification is determined under ITA section 96 and partnership law. Available statutory elections relevant to a CA LP include: (i) ITA subsection 249.1(8), adoption of a non-calendar partnership fiscal period; (ii) ITA section 21, election to treat gains or losses on eligible foreign-currency debts and obligations as capital in nature; (iii) ETA section 177, election among closely related group members to deem taxable supplies made for nil consideration, which can affect provincial HST recovery; and (iv) the hybrid-mismatch ordering rules in ITA sections 18.1 to 18.4, which are mandatory but permit affected partners to apply the principal-purpose exception or dual-inclusion relief where the conditions are documented in the T5013 partnership return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in annual worldwide taxable supplies under the small-supplier threshold in ETA subsection 148(1). The LP becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when its total taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 either in a single calendar quarter or across the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters, calculated on the worldwide supplies of the LP itself rather than the individual partners. Registration is retroactive to the first supply that caused the threshold to be exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI, Part V requires verifiable proof that (a) goods were sold to a non-resident purchaser and exported by the supplier by common carrier outside Canada within 60 days of the invoice date, or (b) goods were shipped by the supplier to a destination address outside Canada. Ordinary over-the-counter sales to tourists are not zero-rated because the goods leave in the purchaser's luggage; only store-shipped exports qualify. Most basic grocery categories, such as vegetables, rice, and condiments sold as food, are already zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part III, so no additional export claim is needed for those items, whereas taxable non-grocery items exported by the LP must satisfy the documentary test shipment-by-shipment.

  • Validated Canada Export Declaration (B13A) or Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED/ATS) record when the goods are exported under the Customs Act
  • Customs export declaration and export movement reference number
  • Commercial invoice of the exporting LP showing the 0% GST/HST line, terms of sale, and foreign destination address
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or road-freight manifest in the LP's name showing dispatch from Canada
  • Proof of delivery at the foreign address or a signed carrier delivery receipt
  • Destination-country customs import entry evidencing that the goods cleared outside Canada
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or foreign-exchange receipt evidencing settlement by the non-resident purchaser within 180 days

Canada has no federal real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control mandate for GST/HST. Compliance is self-assessed, with periodic returns filed through GST/HST NETFILE or the CRA Represent a Client API, and invoices may be kept in paper or electronic form for six years without pre-clearance. Quebec's provincial EDI/XML electronic invoicing regime applies only where the LP has a Quebec establishment, which is not the case for a supermarket hub located in Ontario.

CRA GST/HST audits reconcile zero-rated export lines against the B13A/CAED database and shipping manifests; any invoice zero-rated without matching export evidence is reassessed at 5% GST or the applicable provincial HST rate, with interest and penalties calculated from the original filing date. A false B13A declaration or knowingly staged export triggers the ETA section 285 penalty of 25% of the net tax amount, additional fines of up to CAD 50,000 per false entry, criminal prosecution under the Customs Act with maximum imprisonment of two years and a fine of up to CAD 100,000, and the CRA may require the LP to post security, revoke its zero-rating privilege, and claw back input tax credits for the entire four-year reassessment period, or indefinitely in cases of fraudulent misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account, consistent with Canada's IMF Article VIII obligations. Residents and non-residents may freely remit capital contributions, loan proceeds, trade payments, dividends, and partnership distributions without Bank of Canada approval or conversion quotas. There are no statutory barriers to profit remittance; however, certain non-resident payments such as interest, dividends, rents, royalties, and management fees attract 25% federal Part XIII withholding tax unless a tax treaty reduces the rate. Treaty relief must be documented through CRA forms including NR301, and the limited partnership must maintain tax-residency self-certifications, CRS/FATCA forms, and partnership records to demonstrate entitlement to treaty relief. Related-party loans must be documented with arm's-length interest rates and repayment terms to avoid recharacterization under Canadian transfer-pricing and thin-capitalization rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory retention deadline. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency, deposited into USD or CNH trade accounts, or converted to Canadian dollars at market-determined rates. For income-tax and GST/HST purposes, records supporting export transactions must be retained for at least six years, and any international electronic funds transfer of C$10,000 or more within a 24-hour period must be reported to FINTRAC by the financial institution or money-services business.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority, but Canada does not operate a general exchange-control agency. Cross-border funds movement and anti-money-laundering surveillance are performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), while prudential supervision of banks' foreign-exchange risk is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). The Minister of Finance retains residual authority over international financial agreements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary operating account; Medium as a supplementary settlement rail for cross-border supplier payments and foreign-currency conversion. A foreign fintech EMI cannot provide Interac POS clearing for the grocery checkout lane, and if it serves Canadian residents it may be regarded as carrying on payment-services activity in Canada. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, market participants with a Canadian nexus must be registered with the Bank of Canada and comply with risk-management, incident-reporting, and safeguarding requirements. A licensed Canadian money-services business that is itself a bank client can be used regionally, but the core treasury capital and cleared grocery receipts should remain in Schedule I Canadian banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Preferred routing is an SWIFT MT103 or MT202 message from the non-resident partner through their banking institution to the Canadian Schedule I bank's corresponding international banking centre, credited to the LP's designated Capital Escrow Account in Canada. The remittance instruction must show the LP's exact legal name, full account number, Canadian financial-institution number, branch and transit number, and the receiving bank's BIC/SWIFT code. Canadian banks do not use product-specific fixed government purpose codes; instead, the remitter must provide a clear purpose narrative such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO LIMITED PARTNERSHIP' or 'LOAN TO LIMITED PARTNERSHIP'. FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting is mandatory for any international wire transfer of C$10,000 or more within a 24-hour period; in practice the bank files the report, but the LP must retain supporting source-of-funds and source-of-wealth documentation to answer enhanced due-diligence questions. All inbound wires must be matched to partner capital accounts, and loan-funded injections must be supported by a signed loan agreement, interest-rate calculation, repayment schedule, and CRS/FATCA self-certifications.Outbound trade settlements to Asian grocery suppliers will use the following clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for open-account and advance payments; commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 for high-value supply contracts, advised and optionally confirmed by a bank in the supplier's country; documentary collections for established repeat suppliers; and domestic Interac or electronic funds transfer for Canadian wholesalers. Major Canadian banks will clear CAD through domestic payment systems and USD/CNH through New York or Hong Kong correspondent nodes. The treasury file for each disbursement should contain the commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway waybill, packing list, HS code, certificate of origin, and any phytosanitary or food-safety certificate for imported vegetables and perishable condiments.
