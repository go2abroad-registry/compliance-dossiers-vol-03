Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office addresses are generally accepted as the registered office provided they constitute a physical street address in the province of registration (post office boxes are not permitted), a registered agent is appointed in that province, and the agent is available during normal business hours to accept legal process. The partnership records, minute books, and beneficial ownership information must be retained at an address accessible in Canada (ITA s. 230; CBCA s. 20 for a corporate general partner). For Canadian tax purposes, a virtual office alone does not create commercial substance or a permanent establishment; to qualify as a 'Canadian partnership' under ITA s. 102(2) all partners must be resident in Canada, so non-resident limited partners should be admitted through a Canadian-resident general partner or feeder structure, or the partnership must accept characterization as a non-resident partnership subject to Canadian-source remittance rules, Part I.13 tax on dispositions of Canadian resource property, and Part XIII withholding. Commercial substance planning is therefore mandatory before capital contributions are wired.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Provincial corporate and commercial registry where the LP is constituted (e.g., Ontario Business Registry, Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery), together with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for the federal Business Number and tax program account registrations. No separate federal limited partnership register exists; federal interface is effected through CRA registration and via Corporations Canada only for availability of the definitive name search of existing CBCA entities.

Governing statute

Limited partnership formation is governed by the applicable provincial Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Ontario: Limited Partnerships Act, RSO 1990, c. L.7, ss. 2-20) and, for residual default rules, the provincial Partnerships Act. Federal tax treatment follows the Income Tax Act, RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), Part I, s. 96, including the at-risk loss limitation rules in s. 96(2.1)-(2.4) applicable to limited partners. Extra-provincial registration is governed by the applicable provincial Business Information Act or analogous statute of each jurisdiction where the LP carries on business or holds assets.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form 1) filed with the provincial registry; where operations, assets, or a place of business exist outside the province of formation, the applicable Statement of Registration / Extra-Provincial Limited Partnership Registration; federal Business Number program account registration via CRA Form RC1 or Business Registration Online (BRO).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a limited partnership under Canadian provincial law. Contributions are fixed by the Partnership Agreement and may be made in cash or property; limited partners may not contribute services. No regulated escrow account or capital deposit is required for formation. Deductibility of a limited partner's share of losses is statutorily capped at the partner's at-risk amount pursuant to ITA s. 96(2.1), and the partnership records must set out capital accounts accurately from inception.

Reg. 213/07), covering segregation of chemical commodities, fire separation, and extinguishing-system certification. Structural: building and occupancy permits issued by the municipal authority having jurisdiction, based on floor-load design and seismic compliance under the applicable provincial Building Code. Occupational: workplace-clearance inspection and conformity with the provincial Occupational Health and Safety Act (e.g., OHSA, RSO 1990, c. O.1 in Ontario), including WHMIS training, SDS availability, dust-exposure controls, housekeeping, and material-handling operator certification.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No federal sector-wide operating license applies to the wholesale charter of prilled urea and potassium chloride. Where product compliance is engaged, the competent authority is the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) under the Fertilizers Act, RSC 1985, c. F-10 and the Fertilizers Regulations, CRC c. 666, which govern the registration of fertilizers imported or sold in Canada. The provincial Ministry of Agriculture and local municipal authorities may impose storage, zoning, and handling requirements on bulk fertilizer warehouses.

Category: Not required for the wholesale activity itself. However, imported or sold prilled urea and potassium chloride products must be covered by a valid CFIA Fertilizer Registration held by the manufacturer or importer; the LP, as distributor or import-agent, must verify registration status and maintain lot and sales records in conformity with the Fertilizers Regulations, ss. 18-21. No separate federal or provincial occupational permit is needed for pure distribution without on-site formulation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services/cra-biz-login-services.html) and CRA GST/HST online registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA. For GST/HST registration, file Form RC1, Request for a GST/HST Business Number Registration, together with supporting business details; alternatively use the CRA Non-Resident Business Registration online service. Upon approval, the entity receives a BN with a distinct RT (GST/HST) program account. This BN serves as the Canadian tax identification number for GST/HST filing, T5013 partnership information returns, and other program accounts such as payroll where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A limited partnership may elect a fiscal period end under Income Tax Act s. 249.1(b) with partner concurrence; a non-resident partner may elect under ITA s. 216(1) for certain Canadian-source income; and hybrid mismatch ordering rules under ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6 apply without elective characterization. General anti-avoidance rules under s. 245 may challenge artificial elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada during any calendar quarter or the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); non-residents making or intending to make taxable supplies above this threshold must register, including where the supplies are zero-rated exports, in order to claim input tax credits and lawfully apply the 0% rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies when a registrant sells goods in Canada and, as part of that supply, the goods are exported from Canada by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier, customs broker, or forwarding agent for export outside Canada (Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V). For prilled urea and potassium chloride, the wholesaler must obtain and retain commercial evidence proving the fertilizer physically left Canada within a reasonable period. Services supplied to a non-resident person are generally zero-rated unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of performance.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Canada does not currently mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Registrants may issue paper or electronic invoices, subject to CRA record-keeping and invoice information requirements. CRA may, on reasonable notice, require accounting records to be made available in a readable electronic format.

CRA may deny zero-rating, reverse input tax credits, and assess GST/HST plus interest where export documentation or currency repatriation evidence is deficient within the normal four-year reassessment window, with no time limit in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. False export zero-rating claims attract a penalty under Excise Tax Act s. 285 of no less than CAD 100 and up to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus fines and imprisonment on conviction; disputed refunds may be retrospectively clawed back and, where fraudulent, expose the taxpayer to substantial non-deductible penalties and reputational harm.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with unrestricted convertibility for both capital and current account transactions. Profit remittances to foreign investors are permitted without regulatory hurdles, subject only to standard Canadian tax filing obligations and beneficial ownership verification under the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Bank of Canada is required. Exporters must report all foreign exchange earnings in Canadian tax filings, with repatriation encouraged within 30 days for compliance with anti-money laundering and foreign asset reporting obligations under T1134.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax rate on taxable income; provincial/territorial corporate rates are additional (e.g., 0% to 16% depending on province, with a combined federal-provincial rate typically ranging from 23% to 27% for general active business income; for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) the small business deduction may reduce the effective federal rate to 9% and provincial small business rates may apply)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII Canadian withholding tax is 25% on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident person; however, for distributions by a Limited Partnership, active business income allocations are not subject to dividend withholding, but deemed dividends on certain partnership distributions (e.g., where the partnership has SBC – specified business income or where payments are recharacterized by the Canada Revenue Agency as a benefit) may be withheld at 25%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Income Tax Act, transfer pricing documentation must be prepared contemporaneously for non-arm's-length cross-border transactions, but there is no fixed monetary threshold for documentation; however, transactions exceeding CAD 1 million for tangible goods and services (or CAD 250,000 for intangible-related transactions) in a fiscal year are typically subject to heightened CRA audit scrutiny per the CRA's internal transfer pricing thresholds. For a wholesale commodity trading operation buying urea and potash from foreign related suppliers or selling to foreign related buyers, in any non-arm's-length transaction above CAD 1 million, a contemporaneous Local File / Transfer pricing policy must be maintained and a CRA information request may be issued; Master File is recommended when worldwide related party revenue exceeds CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the CRA may apply the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, section 245 of the Income Tax Act), the business purpose test, and the OECD Multilateral Convention (MLI) principal purpose test (PPT) to deny treaty benefits where a Canadian or non-resident entity lacks economic substance. A limited partnership trading fertilizer commodities is expected to show real physical presence (warehouse, logistics, staff, sourcing contracts, invoices, bills of lading, freight manifests, and bank flows) to avoid recharacterization of sales as agency, artificial conduits, or sham transactions. Canada also applies a 'meaning of carrying on business' in Canada test under subsection 253(1) that includes a non-resident selling goods in Canada through a dependent agent or soliciting orders; a limited partnership may itself be considered a person under section 238 and liable on any tax obligation only to the extent of the partnership's assets, but the general partner is liable for the partnership's obligations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: on indictment, imprisonment up to five years and a monetary fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded; on summary conviction, imprisonment up to two years and a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded. Civil penalties under subsection 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax where false statements or omissions are attributable to culpable neglect, gross negligence, or willful default; CRA may also seize records and demand penalties under section 231.1, and publication of taxpayer name under federal 'name-by-name' disclosure if convicted.

Director personal liability:
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Adjustments under subsection 247(2) reallocating gross revenue, expenses, or allowances between related persons; penalties under subsection 247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment (excess of the adjusted transfer price over the arm's-length amount), with a minimum CAD 5,000 penalty, reduced to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment if the taxpayer fails to make 'reasonable efforts' and unless the taxpayer provides documentation within 30 days of a request; there is a corresponding reduction in penalties where the taxpayer holds complete documentation. For egregious conduits, GAAR may apply to deny the economic substance and retroactively tax deemed income, plus 50% gross-negligence penalty if reclassification is based on misrepresentation.

Director personal liability:
Shell Company / GAAR – Economic Substance Deficiency and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR under section 245 disallows the tax benefit and reassesses fair market value consideration; penalties of up to 50% under subsection 163(2); treaty relief denied under PPT, resulting in full 25% Part XIII withholding, arrears interest and penalty, and repricing of all transactions under section 247. The CRA may also issue an information circular to the director of a corporation that is a general partner of the limited partnership and assess penalties against the general partner for failure to file the T5013 and for the unauthorized claim of deductions. A court may impose a fine under section 239 and order restitution of all unpaid taxes.

Director personal liability:
AML and Commodity Trade Finance Infraction (Professional Money Laundering Risk in Agrochemical Commodities)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, penalties for failing to report a suspicious transaction or large cash transaction: up to CAD 500,000 for a first administrative violation and CAD 1,000,000 for a subsequent administrative violation; after 2023, CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and up to CAD 2,000,000 for entities for non-compliance; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code section 462.31 is indictable, with imprisonment up to 14 years. Directors and senior officers of the general partner may be prosecuted if they directed the laundering, and the CRA can freeze partnership bank accounts, impose civil penalties, and refer for prosecution.

Director personal liability:
Unregistered Non-Resident Permanent Establishment / Failure to File Part XIV Branch Tax

Statutory sanction: CRA can issue a deemed assessment based on the income attributable to the permanent establishment, plus interest and late-filing penalties; under subsection 152(8), the taxpayer remains liable even if no assessment is made; branch tax under section 219 is 25% of after-tax taxable income earned in Canada not re-invested; failure to pay Part XIV tax triggers daily interest at prescribed rate (currently roughly 10% annually, compounded daily) and 5% late-filing penalties; criminal referral for continuous refusal to file.

Director personal liability:
Directors' Liability and Piercing the Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 227.1 of the Income Tax Act, directors of a corporation are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unremitted GST/HST and income tax withholdings (source deductions) only, but not for Part I tax; this liability is limited to the amount unremitted plus interest and penalties. The CRA may issue a director's liability assessment under subsection 227.1(3), and liability may include penalties of up to 100% of unremitted amounts if the director was a 'deemed agent'. For the general partner of an LP, the general partner is liable without limitation for all debts of the limited partnership, including tax debts; a non-resident limited partner who participates in control of the partnership loses limited liability under provincial limited partnership Acts (e.g., Alberta Partnership Act, Ontario Limited Partnerships Act) and becomes jointly and severally liable for all debts to the CRA. For criminal tax evasion by the general partner's directors, imprisonment of up to five years and personal fines up to 200% of the evaded tax; court can impose a restitution order; a travel restraint can be imposed under the Criminal Code if fugitive flight risk is shown.
