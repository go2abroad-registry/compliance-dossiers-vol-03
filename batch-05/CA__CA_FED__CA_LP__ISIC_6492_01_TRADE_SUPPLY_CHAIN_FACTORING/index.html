Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); portal: My Business Account at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services.html

E-government filing portal

For the Canadian Limited Partnership, apply for a Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account (RT) using Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts') or online through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO). A non-resident partner must identify itself and appoint a Canadian agent for service where the partnership is carrying on business in Canada. The BN is issued centrally by CRA; the same BN is used for income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export (RM) accounts. The LP is not itself a taxpayer but must be registered to file T5013 partnership information returns and to collect GST/HST where taxable supplies are made. Registration is generally required within 30 days of beginning an activity subject to mandatory registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Canadian federal tax law; a Limited Partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent and is not an entity subject to income tax. Partners may elect to change the partnership's fiscal period under subsection 249.1(4) of the Income Tax Act. For hybrid mismatch arrangements, Canada applies the excessive interest and financial arrangement rules in Part XVI.I, and the participants may make timing or designation elections where expressly permitted by those provisions, but no entity-level classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — generally the threshold for small supplier registration is taxable revenues in Canada not exceeding C$30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters; non-residents supplying taxable services in Canada must register once the threshold is crossed. A factoring entity is generally supplying financial services, which are exempt, and may therefore have no mandatory registration unless making other taxable supplies; voluntary registration may be available in limited circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may claim 0% GST/HST on exported goods when the goods are exported in the course of the supplier's commercial activity and the supplier obtains documentary evidence of export within the prescribed time (normally two years from the transaction date). Services (including trade finance support services, if structured as a service for GST/HST purposes) may be zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident who is not a consumer and the service is not for consumption in Canada or is otherwise described in Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Fees on true factoring of accounts receivable are generally exempt financial services under Part VII of Schedule V rather than zero-rated; zero-rating for factoring can arise only where the supply is a financial service supplied to a non-resident in respect of a financial instrument and is expressly listed as zero-rated under Part IX of Schedule V to the Excise Tax Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration made electronically through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canada Duty Free/Canadian Automated Export System (CDS/CADEX), with ZES or QP proof of export
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill evidencing movement from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice or Canada Customs Invoice with HS classification, value, and consignee details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 foreign-currency payment evidence satisfying repatriation and proof-of-consideration requirements
  • Factoring assignment notice and purchase agreement linking the financed account receivable to the underlying exported goods or qualifying offshore service

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance for GST/HST invoices and does not operate a mandatory e-invoice clearance API. Electronically filed GST/HST returns are mandatory for registrants with taxable supplies over C$1,500,000 under NETFILE/XML. Voluntary B2B e-invoicing may use the Pan-Canadian Peppol network (PINT format). Books and records, including invoices and export evidence, must be retained for six years.

CRA has a general reassessment period of four years after a notice of assessment, extended to 10 years in cases of neglect, misrepresentation or fraud. If export documentation is not retained for six years or does not substantiate zero-rating, CRA may claw back the zero-rated input tax credits and refunds, reassess the income and GST/HST accounts, apply arrears interest and a 6% penalty under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act, and impose false-statement penalties under section 285.1 (generally 25% of the net tax evaded, 50% for knowing false statements). Fraudulent claims also expose the taxpayer and senior officers to criminal tax fraud penalties, including imprisonment, under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and section 380 of the Criminal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; there is no general exchange-control or FX-licensing regime. The Canadian dollar floats freely, and funds may be converted into any foreign currency without prior central-bank approval. Profit remittance and partnership distributions to non-resident limited partners are freely permitted subject to Part XIII withholding tax of the Income Tax Act (Canada) (default 25% for non-residents, often treaty-reduced, e.g. to 15% under the Canada-US treaty for distributions), but no FX-control or capital-control permit is required. Practical convertibility hurdles are limited to sanctions screening (OSFI-sanction guidance, Global Affairs Canada measures under the Special Economic Measures Act and United Nations Act) and FINTRAC reporting thresholds; Canada has no blocked-currency, dual-rate, or surrender mechanism.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export receivables. Canadian exporters and factoring vehicles may retain export proceeds in foreign currency, hold foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada or abroad, and convert freely. Cross-border currency transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC, and the underlying export invoices must be reconciled for Canadian Revenue Agency (CRA) tax reporting and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) trade-data purposes. Factoring entities must document export-account assignment notices and maintain an audit trail to demonstrate the trade receivable underlying each transaction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with foreign-exchange administration functions coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking supervision, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CTF enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low viability. Canada does not recognize a distinct EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence. Payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) for retail payment functions, while true e-money issuance and deposit-taking are not authorized under the Bank Act. Offshore digital EMIs cannot open a 'bank account' in the Canadian legal sense and cannot act as the primary escrow or operational account for the Canadian LP. Their permissible role is limited to cross-border payment settlement and collection pass-through; this may require FINTRAC Money Service Business (MSB) registration if FX dealing or remittance services are provided to Canadian residents. For a cross-border reverse-factoring platform, a non-resident EMI may be used only as a supplementary collection channel, with the core clearing and custody functions residing at a Canadian Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for investor capital is a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the Canadian LP's Schedule I bank account, using the receiving bank's SWIFT BIC together with the Canadian 5-digit institution number, 7-digit transit number, and account number; Canada does not use IBAN. The payment purpose code must be stated as 'capital subscription', 'equity contribution', or 'shareholder loan', and the receiving bank will require underlying support documentation before crediting, including the partnership agreement, capital call notice, investor subscription letter, and sponsor due-diligence pack. Foreign-currency funds are credited through the bank's nostro account and may be converted into CAD via a spot FX trade executed with the bank's treasury desk. All inbound payments undergo sanctions and AML screening at the wire-entry point; amounts exceeding CAD 10,000 are captured for FINTRAC reporting, and amounts exceeding CAD 100,000 may trigger enhanced due diligence, requiring additional source-of-funds evidence for the LP's investors.Outbound trade settlements are executed through the following clearing rails: (a) domestic CAD disbursements are settled through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for lower-value payments; (b) cross-border disbursements are settled via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with complete beneficiary details, invoice references, and underlying trade-contract references; (c) commercial letters of credit (LC) are issued by the Canadian bank and transmitted via the SWIFT MT700 series, with advising, confirmation, and negotiation handled through MT710/MT720 messages; and (d) documentary collections are processed through the MT400 series. All outbound instructions must include precise remittance information (invoice number, purchase order, and contract identifier) to satisfy the bank's AML, sanctions-screening, and trade-compliance controls. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general corporate income tax rate: 15%; Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) small business rate: 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income. Provincial corporate rates range from 8% to 16% (e.g., Ontario 11.5%), producing a combined federal-provincial headline rate of approximately 23% to 31%. The Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under Part I of the Income Tax Act; the statutory headline rate applies to corporate partners, not to the LP itself.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII statutory rate. Partnership distributions are not dividends; the non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source business income allocated by the LP is subject to Part XIII WHT at 25% unless a treaty rate applies. Under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention, dividends to a US corporate partner owning 10% or more of the entity are limited to 5% (15% for portfolio); however, LP income allocations generally fall under the Business Profits article (0% absent a Canadian permanent establishment) rather than the dividends article.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes no statutory Local File/Master File monetary threshold under s.247; CRA administrative guidance (Transfer Pricing Memorandum TPM-09 and Information Circular IC 87-2R) requires complete and accurate contemporaneous documentation for all non-arm's-length cross-border transactions, with administrative focus where aggregate transaction value exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting applies where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent equals or exceeds CAD 1,000,000,000 (the EUR 750,000,000 threshold as adopted).

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations in the UAE/offshore sense do not apply in Canada; instead, Canada applies: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) with the economic substance doctrine affirmed in Copthorne Holdings (SCC 2011), where a transaction lacking economic substance and defeating the purpose of the provisions may be recharacterized; and (ii) the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test adopted by Canada, which denies treaty benefits (including reduced WHT rates) unless obtaining the benefit was not a principal purpose of the arrangement. An LP carrying on cross-border factoring must maintain premises, employees, decision-making and risk assumption in Canada, and avoid circular financing/back-to-back structures that strip Canadian profits to related non-residents.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239(1) ITA: summary conviction fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: s.163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax attributable to the misrepresentation; s.163(1) 10% penalty for failure to report income on a return. The Crown may register judgment and seize the LP's bank accounts and receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of the corporate general partner who directed the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted Part XIII WHT amounts under s.227.1. Criminal conviction carries imprisonment and, for non-resident directors, a Canada-wide travel ban, deportation, and extradition exposure under tax mutual legal assistance treaties.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse (GAAR / PPT)

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under s.245(2): the tax benefit is denied, the transaction is reconstructed, and reassessments are extended beyond the normal three-year period. Treaty benefits are denied under the MLI Principal Purpose Test, exposing the full 25% Part XIII WHT on all distributions plus arrears interest. Mandatory disclosure non-compliance for notifiable/reportable transactions attracts a 25% penalty on the tax benefit (s.237.4). Transfer pricing mispricing adds the s.247(3) penalty of 10% of the net adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner face s.227.1 personal liability for the assessed WHT; the corporate veil is pierced where the dominance and fraud are proven (common-law fraudulent conveyance principles), permitting recovery against the directors' personal assets, freezing orders, and worldwide asset seizure under Canadian enforcement of foreign judgments.
