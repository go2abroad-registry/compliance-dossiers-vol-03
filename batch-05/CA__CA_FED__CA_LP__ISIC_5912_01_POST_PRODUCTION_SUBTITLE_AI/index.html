Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Primary registration and compliance portal: CRA My Business Account; online business registration at Business Registration Online (BRO).

E-government filing portal

Register the limited partnership provincially/territorially or extra-provincially to obtain the governing certificate of registration. File CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number (BN) and Program Accounts) via BRO or mail to obtain the nine-digit BN with the GST/HST program account (RT) and, if physical exports are made, the customs import/export account (RM). The BN also anchors the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return; each partner (including non-resident members) reports its distributive share of Canadian-source income under the Income Tax Act (ITA) Part I, subject to the non-Canadian-partnership rules where non-resident members exist. For GST/HST, the partnership must register once it ceases to be a small supplier (CAD 30,000 threshold) or may elect voluntary registration before that threshold; non-resident partners should designate an authorized Canadian representative/agent for service of notices and records inspection, and the LP must maintain a Canadian books-and-records address for CRA audit purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes: it computes income and files the T5013 information return, and partners are taxed on their shares; non-resident partners are taxed on their Canadian-source business income (subject to treaty relief). However, for GST/HST the LP is a separate 'person' required to register and remit, and partners are jointly and severally liable for the LP's net tax (ETA s. 272.1).

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification regime; classifications are rules-based. Available elections and mechanisms include: (i) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident to file a net-basis Canadian return on Canadian-source business income (relevant to LP partners); (ii) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(2.1) permitting a non-resident LP to register before the CAD 30,000 threshold to recover input tax credits on Canadian launch costs; (iii) the Canada-US Tax Treaty fiscally-transparent-entity rule allowing look-through to LP members for treaty benefits; and (iv) domestic BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch rules and OECD MLI provisions, which must be assessed where the LP or a foreign investor entity is differently classified in Canada and the home jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold under the Excise Tax Act: total worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters and the current quarter, excluding supplies of capital property and financial services; non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada measure the same threshold on their Canadian traceable revenue).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating eligibility: supplies of automated subtitle transcription, AI dubbing/voice-cloning services, and related intangible property (licences, masters, deliverables) to non-resident clients are zero-rated at 0% GST/HST where the customer is a non-resident, is not registered for GST/HST, and the supply is not in respect of Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. Physical exports (hard drives, masters) are zero-rated where the goods are exported by the supplier under the Schedule VI exported-goods rules. The supplier must hold robust documentary evidence of the customer's non-resident status, the digital or physical delivery point, the contract/SOW, and the absence of Canadian situs of the underlying media assets. CRA GST/HST Memoranda Series 4.5 (Exports) and the Schedule VI zero-rated service rules govern substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration: electronic Canada Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) / Canadian International Trade Single Window for any physical export of masters, drives or equipment.
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill, commercial invoice and signed contract/SOW evidencing shipment or digital delivery to the non-resident client outside Canada.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or equivalent payment-provider settlement report (e.g., Wise, Stripe payout advice) evidencing foreign-currency receipt and conversion into CAD.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax clearance, or mandatory digital stamping regime applies in Canada. Registrants must issue prescribed GST/HST invoices or simplified receipts within legislated timeframes (ETA ss. 221-223) containing the supplier's BN, invoice date, description, consideration and tax charged, and must retain electronic records in a readable format. Filing is effected electronically through GST/HST NETFILE and remittances through CRA My Business Account. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework aligned with the PEPPOL standard is emerging for B2B interoperability, but it is not a CRA clearance mandate and carries no digital stamp or pre-clearance step.

CRA audit practice subjects zero-rated export claims to strict evidentiary review; where documentation is deficient or the non-resident status is unproven, the supply is reassessed as taxable at 5% GST plus interest. Civil penalties include the false-statement/omission penalty (greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the net tax understated) and gross-negligence penalties where the taxpayer or agent knowingly misstates export eligibility; criminal tax-evasion fines can reach 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment (ETA s. 285). Retrospective clawbacks may extend beyond the standard four-year limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud is established, and zero-rating claims made in error also expose the partnership to recovery of any input tax credits wrongly claimed in respect of the export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions in accordance with IMF Article VIII. The Canadian dollar is freely floating and there are no exchange control approvals required for dividend distributions, capital repatriation, or profit remittances by non-residents. Remittances are subject only to applicable withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (typically 25% for non-treaty jurisdictions, reduced under treaties) and FINTRAC reporting thresholds for large international electronic fund transfers (CAD 10,000 or more). A Canadian Limited Partnership may freely remit distributable income to foreign partners in CAD or any convertible foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines on export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts at their discretion. Obligations are confined to statistical reporting when selected by Statistics Canada for the Balance of Payments survey and standard income tax reporting on worldwide income. An automated subtitle transcription and AI voice cloning studio export service invoices may be settled directly via wire transfer or payment service provider (Stripe, Wise) without any FX conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); no separate Foreign Exchange Administration is established as Canada operates under a non-restrictive, liberalized foreign exchange regime

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for payment facilitation, Low for primary banking. Offshore digital EMIs are not permitted to accept CAD deposits or provide Canadian operating accounts unless duly licensed as a bank under the Bank Act (Canada) or authorized as a foreign bank subsidiary. Canadian-registered EMIs may operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and are appropriate for cross-border settlements, API vendor disbursements, or low-value working capital flows. Legal constraints include mandatory registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), robust KYC/beneficial ownership identification, and restrictions on deposit-taking activities. For the purposes of a Low_Risk_Standard banking tier, a conventional Schedule I bank account remains essential for escrow and committed liquidity.
