Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Primary federal portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (MyBA); registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO); GST/HST filing and remittance via GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

The Canadian Limited Partnership (LP) obtains a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) through the CRA registration process. Step 1: file the limited partnership declaration with the applicable provincial registry and obtain the provincial registration number. Step 2: the general partner on behalf of the LP applies via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number (BN)', opening the LP's BN with a dedicated GST/HST (RT) account, corporate income tax (RC) account, and payroll (RP) account where applicable. Step 3: non-resident partners are not required to obtain a Canadian BN solely by reason of passive LP investment, but the general partner must provide the relevant foreign tax residence documentation; a non-resident who carries on business in Canada through the LP must assess its own ETA s. 240 registration duty. Step 4: the LP, as a 'person' under ETA s. 248(1), must register for GST/HST once its total taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold of CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is available. Step 5: maintain all registration documents, partner allocation schedules, and provincial extra-provincial registration evidence for CRA audit purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act s. 96, the LP is fiscally transparent: no entity-level Part I tax is imposed; each partner computes its allocable share of the LP's income, loss, and tax attributes on a fiscal-period basis. Under the ETA the LP is itself a 'person' and may be a GST/HST registrant in respect of its commercial activities. Royalty or other amounts distributed to non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax or treaty relief depending on source characterization.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime; federal classification of an LP is statutory and non-elective. Relevant elections and designations include: (i) an election under ITA s. 96(3) to adopt a non-calendar partnership fiscal period where a bona fide business purpose exists; (ii) an election under ITA s. 216 available to a non-resident partner in respect of Canadian-source rental or royalty income allocated through the LP; (iii) an election under ITA s. 18.4(3) to apply or opt into the hybrid mismatch limitation rules for cross-border arrangements involving hybrid or reverse-hybrid entities; and (iv) for U.S. partners, an IRC § 7701 check-the-box analysis is separate and defaults the Canadian LP to partnership status for U.S. federal tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold. Mandatory GST/HST registration arises when the LP's total worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the aggregate over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register at the same threshold; voluntary registration is available to any person engaged in commercial activity in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: tangible personal property exported by the LP or by or on behalf of the recipient is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 1, provided export occurs within the statutory time frame (generally by the time consideration becomes due, with documented continuous export arrangements permitted up to 60 days or two years in specific contract situations). Intangible personal property / patent licences: a supply of a patent licence or other intangible personal property to a non-resident person is zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V s. 7 if the non-resident is not registered under ETA s. 240(1) and the supply is not an exempt supply; the LP must obtain contemporaneous evidence that the licensed rights are used and enforceable outside Canada, such as foreign patent registrations, non-resident declarations, and usage records. Ancillary technical or support services performed wholly outside Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V ss. 6 to 8. Royalties received from non-resident licensees for use of a patent outside Canada are generally not within the GST/HST tax base because no supply is made in Canada; where GST/HST is erroneously charged, the LP may issue a credit note under ETA s. 232 and adjust net tax, or the non-resident may pursue a rebate where applicable. The LP remains entitled to input tax credits on acquisitions attributable to its zero-rated exported supplies, and any resulting refund is payable through the normal GST/HST net-filing system.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Automated Export Declaration or ACI eManifest) — required only where tangible goods embodying the licensed intellectual property are physically exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — transportation evidence for physical exports; not applicable to a pure intangible patent licence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange transaction records evidencing receipt of the royalty consideration from the non-resident licensee
  • Executed patent licence and sub-licence agreements identifying territory, scope of rights, and consideration
  • Non-resident status and non-registration declaration of the licensee under ETA s. 240(1), together with evidence of foreign patent registration and demonstrated use of the licensed rights outside Canada

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax stamping, or a central clearance requirement for invoices. GST/HST registrants must issue, retain, and produce invoices in readable form under ETA s. 169(4) and s. 286, including electronic records where legible. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing framework, based on the PEPPOL/BIS 3.0 standard, is optional for B2B transactions as of the current federal framework; no legislative mandate is in force for B2B clearance, and the CRA accepts PDF, EDI, XML, and OCR-compatible formats. All records supporting GST/HST returns must be retained for a minimum of six years.

CRA may reassess within the normal period of four years from the end of the relevant assessment or return period under ITA s. 152(4) and ETA s. 296, and may retrospectively claw back zero-rating and disallow refunds where the export substantiation bundle is inadequate. Penalties include: (i) ETA s. 285(1) — the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax for false, incomplete, or misleading statements; (ii) ITA s. 163(2) — gross-negligence penalty of up to 50% of the understated Part I tax; (iii) prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239(1) for fraudulent or wilful evasion, exposing the LP and its partners to penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment. Arrears interest and instalment interest accrue from the original due date. The CRA Voluntary Disclosures Program may waive penalties and some interest only where disclosure is filed before enforcement action is initiated and is both voluntary and complete; legal and tax advice should be obtained before relying on it. Although a 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is often used as an evidentiary convention, Canadian tax law does not impose a general statutory 180-day repatriation deadline for export royalties; the LP must nevertheless maintain continuous documentary proof that the licensed patents are exploited outside Canada, since CRA may otherwise recharacterize the royalties as Canadian-source and subject them to full GST/HST and Part I tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada maintains no exchange control regime, no central bank approval for outward remittances, and no restriction on capital flows or profit repatriation. Non-resident royalty or dividend payments are subject only to withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act and, where applicable, a tax treaty rate reduction. Banking receipt of inbound capital is subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds, but no prior central bank authorization is required. For a Canadian limited partnership engaged in cross-border IP sub-licensing, conversion of foreign currency receipts is unrestricted, and repatriation of royalties to foreign licensors is constrained solely by transfer-pricing documentation and Canadian withholding tax obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no surrender requirement for export proceeds or cross-border royalty receipts. There is no statutory timeline for conversion of foreign currency earnings into CAD, and no mandatory conversion ratio. A Canadian LP may retain, convert, or disburse foreign currency freely through a Canadian Schedule I bank treasury account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — Canada's monetary authority and operator of the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and Real-Time Rail (RTR) under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act. It does not administer foreign exchange controls; the relevant legal framework is the Currency Act and the Bank Act, with prudential supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium, legally constrained for primary treasury functions. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorized to take deposits in Canada under the Bank Act unless chartered as a bank or authorized as a foreign bank branch. Such entities may operate as money service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and can provide ancillary cross-border payout or multi-currency card services, but they cannot provide CDIC-insured CAD-denominated operating accounts or access to Payments Canada clearing rails. For a Canadian LP holding global IP and collecting intercompany royalties, an offshore EMI is viable only as a secondary collection/disbursement tool; the primary liquidity rail must reside with a regulated Schedule I bank or a regulated Canadian payment service provider partnered with a bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be effected via SWIFT MT103 from the contributing foreign partner or shareholder, quoting the LP's name, account number, and bank identifier. Canadian banks require an explicit payment narrative; use standardized wording such as 'Initial capital contribution — Limited Partnership Agreement dated [date]' or 'Equity injection pursuant to partnership resolution'. No official Canadian purpose code exists, but the bank's foreign-exchange desk will map the remittance to the IMF Balance of Payments category 'Direct investment in reporting economy'. Payments exceeding CAD 10,000 are automatically reported to FINTRAC. To avoid quarantine pending AML review, submit in advance a board or partnership resolution, the executed subscription document, a source-of-funds declaration, and, where the transfer is from an affiliate, the signed intercompany loan or IP purchase agreement.Use SWIFT MT103 for direct royalty disbursements from the LP's operating account to foreign licensors or sub-licensees. For equipment or services embedded in IP commercialization, use commercial letters of credit (UCP600) issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank, payable at sight or deferred basis. Domestic same-day CAD clearing is achieved through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), while cross-border USD flows rely on correspondent banks (e.g., JPMorgan Chase or Bank of America) integrated to the Canadian bank. All outbound payments to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax of the Income Tax Act; the bank may require an NR8/NR83 waiver or a treaty eligibility certificate before processing. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium, legally constrained for primary treasury functions. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorized to take deposits in Canada under the Bank Act unless chartered as a bank or authorized as a foreign bank branch. Such entities may operate as money service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and can provide ancillary cross-border payout or multi-currency card services, but they cannot provide CDIC-insured CAD-denominated operating accounts or access to Payments Canada clearing rails. For a Canadian LP holding global IP and collecting intercompany royalties, an offshore EMI is viable only as a secondary collection/disbursement tool; the primary liquidity rail must reside with a regulated Schedule I bank or a regulated Canadian payment service provider partnered with a bank.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate; combined federal plus provincial rates generally range from approximately 23% (e.g., Alberta at 8%) to 26.5% (e.g., Ontario at 11.5%). The 9% federal small-business rate is unavailable to an IP HoldCo limited partnership deriving primarily passive royalty income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under ITA Part XIII (s.212(2)) for dividends paid or deemed paid to non-resident shareholders, including a non-resident partner's allocated share of dividends flowing through the LP; the Canada–U.S. Treaty reduces the rate to 15% generally and 5% where the beneficial owner is a corporation owning at least 10% of the voting shares; other DTAs typically provide 5% to 15%.
