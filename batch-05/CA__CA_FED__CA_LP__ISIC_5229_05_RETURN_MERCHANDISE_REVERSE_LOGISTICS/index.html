Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO); taxpayer portal: My Business Account. Registration for the Business Number (BN) plus the RT program account (GST/HST) is effected online via BRO, by mail (Form RC1), or by telephone.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a 9-digit BN and open a GST/HST program account (RT) before making taxable supplies above the small-supplier threshold (CAD 30,000 in four consecutive quarters) or immediately when commercial activity in Canada commences. Application is submitted through BRO or Form RC1 with partnership details (name, jurisdiction of formation, Canadian agent for service), mailing address, fiscal year-end, and estimated Canadian taxable supplies. A GST/HST account is activated upon BN issuance; the partnership must file returns electronically and may elect monthly/quarterly/annual reporting. For income tax, the partnership itself is generally not a taxable person but must file partnership information returns (Form T5013) and a T5013 Summary if it carries on business in Canada or has Canadian-source income, even if all partners are non-resident.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' system. An LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA s.96): income/loss is computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners. Elections relevant to cross-border reverse logistics: (i) ITA s.216/216.1 elections for non-resident partners on Canadian-source service/rental income (gross-basis taxation); (ii) GST/HST s.156 election for closely related corporations (may not apply to partnerships); (iii) GST/HST joint election under Schedule VI Part V to zero-rate certain services supplied to a non-resident where the supplier and recipient are deemed not associated in prescribed circumstances; (iv) hybrid mismatch elections/ordering rules under ITA ss.18.2-18.4 (BEPS Action 2) to align deduction/non-inclusion outcomes for partnership-financed structures. No entity-level C-corporation default is available for a Canadian LP; partners are directly taxable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (other than financial services and zero-rated supplies) measured over the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters; the threshold is reduced to CAD 0 for non-residents carrying on a 'taxi' or 'commercial accommodation' business and for businesses that are not resident in Canada but supply goods in Canada if the supply is made in the course of a business in Canada. Reverse logistics inbound processing services performed for non-residents are evaluated under the general CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold unless the services fall under zero-rating provisions that exclude them from 'taxable supply' counting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a supply of goods is zero-rated where the supplier ships/transports the goods to a destination outside Canada (ETA ss.142(1)-(2), 143) or the goods are exported by the recipient within 60 days in prescribed circumstances; documentary transfer of title to a non-resident without export does not qualify. Services: supplies to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V s.9 only if the service is not in connection with real property situated in Canada and not in connection with tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. CAUTION: reverse logistics services performed on returned goods physically located in Canada (inspection, grading, refurbishment, warehousing in transit) generally do NOT qualify for zero-rating under s.9 because the service relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada; such services are taxable at 5% GST even when supplied to a non-resident principal. Quality-grading and advisory components may qualify under s.7 (professional services) only when separately contracted and not ancillary to the handling of goods in Canada. The LP should therefore structure service contracts segregating offshore advisory services (zero-rated) from in-Canada handling (GST-taxable) and claim ITCs for input GST. Non-resident principals that are not registered and receive taxable in-Canada services may add the tax to cost or, where eligible, apply for the limited rebates prescribed under the Excise Tax Act; the primary recovery route is ITC claiming through voluntary registration.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls are currently imposed at the federal level. E-invoicing is voluntary; the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (Peppol/PINT) was adopted in 2024 to enable B2G and interoperable B2B electronic exchange, but the CRA does not require prior clearance of invoices. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no requirement for central bank approval on any payment. Exchange controls were abolished in 1951 and have not been reintroduced; the Canadian dollar is freely floating. A non-resident-owned Limited Partnership may receive capital contributions, earn service revenue, and remit distributions without restriction. The only statutory friction is Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) on distributions to non-resident partners: the statutory rate is 25%, reducible to 15% or lower under an applicable treaty such as Article XI of the Canada–United States Tax Convention. Withholding amounts must be remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency before the balance is paid; the remittance itself is unrestricted. Foreign-currency denominated accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks are freely permitted without Bank of Canada or OSFI authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no compulsory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or statutory timeline for export proceeds. A reverse-logistics service provider may retain foreign-currency receipts indefinitely in domestic or offshore bank accounts. The sole compliance requirement is statistical: international individual payments and receipts of C$100,000 or more (or the foreign-currency equivalent) must be reported to Statistics Canada on ITRS Form A (receipts) and Form B (payments) quarterly; the Bank of Canada shares the data for balance-of-payments compilation. Penalties for non-reporting apply under the Statistics Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. S-19). No conversion ratio exists for any export or service category.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy coordination under the Department of Finance Canada; prudential supervision of chartered banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing enforcement by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and its regulations (SOR/2002-421).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a payment-rails complement; Low as the sole custodian of capital escrow or statutory funds. Airwallex (Airwallex Canada ULC) operates as a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) and can lawfully provide the CA_LP with multi-currency transaction accounts, mid-market FX conversion, and cross-border wire/ACH disbursement routing. Legal constraints are material: (1) an MSB is not a bank, cannot accept full-scope Canadian-dollar deposits, and is not CDIC-insured; client funds must be safeguarded under PCMLTFA and provincial MSB licensing (Quebec and Manitoba regimes); (2) the prudent architecture is a Schedule I bank as the escrow and capital repository, with Airwallex streaming only operational refunds and vendor payouts; (3) contractual AML/CFT record-keeping must be delegated explicitly to Airwallex, including FINTRAC ETR reporting for international EFTs of C$10,000 or more and the 7-year documentation retention obligation; (4) Airwallex's settlement layer supports SWIFT, Payments Canada ACSS Local ACH, and its global liquidity network, which is suitable for reverse-logistics refund tickets and overseas quality-grading vendor disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for routing a non-resident partner's capital contribution to the CA_LP: instruct the remitting bank to issue an SWIFT MT103 with narrative 'Contribution to Limited Partnership — CA_LP Capital Escrow Account' and, where the remitter specifies, SWIFT Category 8 (SUPP) or OTHR correspondent instructions; the receiving Canadian Schedule I bank settles Canadian-dollar items above C$25,000 through Payments Canada's Lynx real-time gross settlement system and lower-value items via the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); a United States-dollar leg clears through the bank's New York correspondent network. FINTRAC receives an Electronic Funds Transfer (ETR) report automatically from the receiving bank for every international EFT of C$10,000 or more; the client is not required to file ETRs. The LP must separately report aggregate international receipts of C$100,000-plus to Statistics Canada on ITRS Form A each quarter. Verification is achieved via the MT910 credit confirmation matched against the issued capital-call notice; no Bank of Canada or OSFI approval is required for the injection.Settlement rails for outbound trade and operating disbursements: (1) high-value vendor and refund payments execute via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) through the Canadian bank's correspondent network; domestic CAD payments settle via Lynx (>C$25,000) or ACSS batch ACH (low value); (2) large, contractually committed quality-grading services may be secured by Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the Canadian Schedule I bank and governed by UCP 600, advisable where the grading counterparty requires documentary assurance; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High as a payment-rails complement; Low as the sole custodian of capital escrow or statutory funds. Airwallex (Airwallex Canada ULC) operates as a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) and can lawfully provide the CA_LP with multi-currency transaction accounts, mid-market FX conversion, and cross-border wire/ACH disbursement routing. Legal constraints are material: (1) an MSB is not a bank, cannot accept full-scope Canadian-dollar deposits, and is not CDIC-insured; client funds must be safeguarded under PCMLTFA and provincial MSB licensing (Quebec and Manitoba regimes); (2) the prudent architecture is a Schedule I bank as the escrow and capital repository, with Airwallex streaming only operational refunds and vendor payouts; (3) contractual AML/CFT record-keeping must be delegated explicitly to Airwallex, including FINTRAC ETR reporting for international EFTs of C$10,000 or more and the 7-year documentation retention obligation; (4) Airwallex's settlement layer supports SWIFT, Payments Canada ACSS Local ACH, and its global liquidity network, which is suitable for reverse-logistics refund tickets and overseas quality-grading vendor disbursements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million (gross revenue) for Local File; CAD 50 million for Master File; CAD 5 million for intercompany transactions with non-residents (per CRA guidance).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act may apply to transactions lacking economic substance. For partnerships, the 'reasonable expectation of profit' test and anti-avoidance rules for loss allocation apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act section 239.

Director personal liability: For limited partners, liability limited to capital contribution; general partners are personally liable for partnership debts and penalties. Directors of corporate general partner may face personal liability for unremitted withholding taxes (section 227.1).
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of income, penalties up to 10% of net adjustment (if not reasonable efforts), plus interest.

Director personal liability: General partners may be liable for penalties; corporate general partner's directors can be liable if they authorized the non-compliant pricing.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, penalties for false statements.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of corporate general partner may be jointly and severally liable for reassessed taxes and penalties.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals, CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporate general partner can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly fail to comply.
