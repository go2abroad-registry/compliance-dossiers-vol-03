Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), with the principal business portal 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/services/business.html), supplemented by GST/HST NETFILE and 'Represent a Client' for authorized representatives.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' via the CRA online business registration tool or mailed application. The BN is the statutory base; a GST/HST program account (prefix RT) is added where taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold, while an income tax program account (RC) is opened if the LP has Canadian-source income and no other CRA account. For a foreign LP without a Canadian physical address, the non-resident must appoint a fiscal representative acceptable to the CRA under subsection 273(1) of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) for GST/HST purposes, and must execute the CRA 'Non-Resident Business Number Registration' procedures, proving the authority of a Canadian general partner or fiscal agent. Identity verification is conducted through the Canadian resident partner's CRA credential or via a notarized Form RC1A and proof of partnership registration in the foreign jurisdiction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; a limited partnership is fiscally transparent by default under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (ITA), under which income is computed at the partnership level and allocated to each partner. Key statutory elections applicable in this structure include: (i) ITA subsection 96(3) election respecting a deemed partnership year-end and the continuation of the partnership on admission of new partners; (ii) ITA subsection 143.2(1) election to re-characterize a substituted partnership interest on the admission of a new partner; (iii) ETA subsection 156(1) election to treat a supply between closely related persons (corporations or partnerships) as not being a taxable supply, where the LP and a Canadian operating affiliate satisfy the close-relationship tests; (iv) ETA subsection 273(1) election/designation of a fiscal resident representative for the non-resident LP; and (v) elections respecting the GST/HST quick method of accounting. There is no elective commodity-tax hybrid mismatch election; the mandatory hybrid mismatch rules introduced by the 2023 Federal Budget (ITA subsection 96(5.1) and section 247.1) neutralize certain deductible-imbalance outcomes without elective action.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000, being the threshold of worldwide taxable supplies (excluding GST/HST zero-rated supplies) measured over any preceding four consecutive calendar quarters under ETA subsection 148(1). For the webtoon syndication model, zero-rated digital supplies to non-resident readers do not count toward the threshold, but digital supplies made to Canadian consumers through Apple/Google marketplace platforms are attributed by the deemed-supplier regime (ETA subsection 242.1(2)), and the licensed platforms themselves cross the CAD 30,000 threshold and collect the applicable HST at checkout.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for digital webtoon exports is available under ETA Schedule VI section 23, which zero-rates a supply of intangible personal property (including a digital product) made to a non-resident recipient where the recipient is not resident in Canada, is not registered for GST/HST, and the supply is not 'received in Canada'; alternative relief may arise under Schedule VI section 13 for services exported to non-residents. The supplier must maintain reliable transaction-based place-of-supply evidence, including IP geolocation, billing country, storefront country, and Apple/Google subscriber data, showing delivery into the foreign jurisdiction. There is no customs export declaration requirement for electronically delivered intangibles; the digital delivery and settlement trail constitutes the export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration / N/A for intangible electronic services, replaced by the platform's digital delivery logs and the LP's geolocated distribution record
  • Bill of Lading / N/A for digital goods, substituted by the Apple IAP and Google Play Billing weekly/monthly sales extraction reports evidencing the non-Canadian storefront territories
  • Webtoon syndication and reseller agreements with each non-resident digital distributor, evidencing that title, delivery, and control of the digital content occur outside Canada
  • Zero-rated tax invoices meeting ETA section 223 invoice content requirements, annotated with the applicable Schedule VI zero-rating provision
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / FX settlement reports from Apple and Google generated in settlement currency, reconciled monthly to CAD using CRA-acceptable exchange rates (Bank of Canada noon rates or CRA-approvable methodologies)
  • General ledger export reconciliation and retained e-audit files demonstrating the exclusion of zero-rated export turnover from the Canadian HST base

Canada has no mandatory real-time e-invoice clearance (CTC) system, no fiscal stamping, and no API-based real-time fiscal reporting to CRA. E-invoicing is voluntary, with the Canadian Digital Service (CDS) and PEPPOL BIS 3.0 as accepted interoperability standards for B2B electronic exchange. GST/HST returns are filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or certified third-party software, on a monthly/quarterly/annual basis depending on taxable supply volume. Statutory invoicing content is prescribed by ETA subsection 223(1) and must show the supplier's BN and program account, the date of supply, the tax rate, and the net tax payable; for zero-rated exports, invoices must be annotated with a zero-rating mechanism. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing act as deemed suppliers/collectors for domestic Canadian supplies, and the LP must retain the platforms' gross-revenue statements, tax remittance files, and settlement reports as complete fiscal records.

CRA audit scrutiny of export zero-rating concentrates on place-of-supply documentation, the non-resident status of the recipient, and platform integrity. False or fraudulent zero-rating, or arrangements that mischaracterize Canadian-dimensioned digital sales as foreign exports, trigger: (i) reassessment beyond the normal four-year window under ETA subsection 298(3) for misrepresentation committed with gross negligence or wilful default (extended seven-year window); (ii) the 25% penalty imposed by ETA subsection 285(1) on the fraudulent tax amount; (iii) daily compounding interest under ETA subsection 280(1) at the CRA-prescribed rate plus a provincial supplementary rate; (iv) prosecution and fines under ETA section 327.1 and Income Tax Act section 239, reaching CAD 1,000,000 on indictment and up to five years' imprisonment for officers/partners; (v) joint and several liability of all Canadian and non-resident partners under ETA subsection 272.1(1); and (vi) retroactive HST clawback on every previously zero-rated sale, causing irrecoverable consumer-borne HST because end-user invoices cannot be reissued after the fact, plus potential cancellation of the LP's GST/HST registration and BN.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully liberalized capital and current account convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating currency with no exchange control legislation, no prior approval requirements for cross-border remittances, and no restrictions on dividend or profit repatriation to non-resident partners. Remittances are subject to Part XIII withholding tax (generally 25%, reduced by treaty) and to normal anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, are permitted without currency conversion approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency bank accounts in Canada or offshore. The only compliance obligations relate to accurate bookkeeping, tax reporting of foreign-source income, and mandatory reporting of cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) as the monetary authority; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the federal banking prudential regulator; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the financial intelligence unit; Department of Finance Canada as the policy lead for foreign exchange and payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for wholly digital cross-border financial technology EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) as primary banking rails. Such entities are not banks in Canada and cannot hold 'deposits' without a license; they typically operate as payment service providers or as agents of regulated Canadian banks. Legal constraints under the Bank Act and the PCMLTFA require MSB registration with FINTRAC and, for certain activities, provincial money-services licensing. An LP may use these rails for payment disbursement, but the capital escrow account must reside at a scheduled bank or an authorized foreign bank branch with OSFI approval. Full treasury duties, including FX execution, should be centralized at a tier-1 Canadian bank for cost efficiency and settlement certainty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be transferred via SWIFT MT103 using the partnership's CAD operating or escrow account. Purpose code must be classified as 'Capital contributions' under the Canadian Balance of Payments reporting framework (or bank-specific code for direct investment/equity). No separate exchange-control approval is required. The following protocol applies: (a) the remitting bank must use the exact legal name as registered on the Canadian LP certificate, including the GP's name; (b) the LP's full address and the Canadian bank account number (5/7/12 digit format) must be accurately stated; (c) accompanying memo should reference the definitive LP agreement and partnership capital call; (d) if the transfer exceeds CAD 10,000, the remitter must complete the standard financial institution inquiry; (e) for amounts exceeding CAD 1 million, the bank may request a capital call notice and a board/GP resolution authorizing the injection. No exchange conversion is required if monies are held in USD; conversion to CAD may be executed at the LP's discretion on the local account without reporting to the central bank.Trade settlements (license payments to content creators, translators, and rights holders) will be cleared through: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers to non-Canadian payees, using 'Business/presentment' purpose code (e.g., '7210 - Rights to books, manuscripts and other informational content' or a code suggested by the payee's bank); (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) may be used for larger contracts requiring documentary compliance; however, for digital content the normative rail is wire transfer. Direct disbursements to individuals should follow the EFT protocol for payroll/contractors, while payments to corporate counterparts should use ACH via the Canadian domestic clearing system (Quebec-ACH or LVTS) for CAD payments, and the Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS) or SWIFT for USD cross-border disbursements. Each payment invoice must include a cross-reference to the specific chapter-unlocking sales report to support the transaction description for AML review, but no statutory FX approval is required.Forward foreign exchange contracts (plain-vanilla deliverable forwards) with a Canadian Schedule I bank or authorized derivatives dealer, permitted only for hedging the LP's known USD/CAD receipt exposures derived from Google and Apple revenue flows; physical delivery of CAD is customary on maturity.FX swaps (currency swaps) executed bilaterally with an OSFI-regulated Canadian bank under an ISDA master agreement, subject to mandatory central clearing and trade-reporting requirements if the LP exceeds applicable derivatives transaction thresholds (typically derivatives net notional > CAD 5 billion).NDFs (Non-Deliverable Forwards) are not applicable in the CAD/USD relationship because the CAD is freely deliverable; they would be non-permissible for speculative intent and would offer no compliance advantage.Options on USD/CAD (call and put) may be used for risk-management purchases covering revenue shortfall scenarios, provided the option underlying is a physical FX transaction on the LP's account and the instrument is booked with a Canadian financial institution.Cross-currency swaps are allowed for long-term currency liability management, but must be limited to genuine economic hedges of revenue streams under the digital content licensing agreements; speculative hedging is restricted by OSFI guidance and the partnership's investment mandate.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium feasibility for wholly digital cross-border financial technology EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) as primary banking rails. Such entities are not banks in Canada and cannot hold 'deposits' without a license; they typically operate as payment service providers or as agents of regulated Canadian banks. Legal constraints under the Bank Act and the PCMLTFA require MSB registration with FINTRAC and, for certain activities, provincial money-services licensing. An LP may use these rails for payment disbursement, but the capital escrow account must reside at a scheduled bank or an authorized foreign bank branch with OSFI approval. Full treasury duties, including FX execution, should be centralized at a tier-1 Canadian bank for cost efficiency and settlement certainty.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate under Part I of the Income Tax Act, after application of the 13% general tax reduction; the Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent and is not a taxpayer, and each corporate partner is taxed on its allocated share of Canadian-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII statutory rate applies to dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident. For the Canadian limited partnership, cash distributions to partners are not dividends; the LP is fiscally transparent and no Part XIII withholding applies to profit distributions. The non-resident corporate partner is instead subject to Part I tax on its allocated share of the LP's Canadian-source income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate arm's length value of reportable non-arm's length cross-border transactions triggers the Local File and Form T106 information return; a Master File is required where the consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000; a Country-by-Country Report is due at group turnover of CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; the CRA instead enforces economic substance through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, s. 245 of the Income Tax Act), the reasonable-expectation-of-profit rule, and the anti-hybrid and anti-conduit provisions (ss. 18(4), 94, 95). These jointly require the LP to execute genuine Canadian commercial activity — content production, local licensing, platform management, and active decision-making — to avoid recharacterization as a treaty-shopping shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under s. 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: a fine ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; civil reassessment plus a s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted Part I instalments; upon conviction, courts may order restitution, passport seizure, and travel bans through the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act enforcement framework.
Shell Company / GAAR Treaty-Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s. 245) disallows the entirety of the tax benefit; a 25% GAAR penalty applies under s. 163(2.2); the CRA recharacterizes the arrangement and reassesses the non-resident partner directly under Part I, retroactively denying treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified sham partnership agreements are personally exposed to the 25% GAAR penalty and, for willful participation, prosecution under s. 239; the CRA may obtain a civil court order preventing departure from Canada.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment under s. 247(2) with no correlative relief; a s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment applies where the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts to price at arm's length or fails to maintain required documentation; a T106 late-filing penalty of CA$100 per day, up to CA$2,500 per failure, is also imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be assessed personally for the adjusted tax and penalties as related parties under s. 247 if they signed the transfer-pricing certificates; the CRA may also freeze the LP's merchant payment rails and issue a requirement to pay against director bank accounts.
Part XIII Withholding and Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: Section 227(8) makes the LP liable for the full unreported withholding plus interest; s. 227(9) imposes a penalty of 10% of the amount that should have been withheld, up to CA$10,000; the non-resident recipient may also be reassessed under Part XIII with a gross-up.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are absolutely and personally liable under s. 227.1 for the unremitted amounts, regardless of the LP's solvency; the CRA can freeze personal bank accounts, place holds on cross-border payments, and obtain a departure-ban court order until the liability is fully satisfied.
