Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) under the Canada Revenue Agency Act; principal federal tax portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency. The CRA administers Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST), Part I of the Income Tax Act, and the federal Business Number (BN) registry; the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs export reporting (eManifest/CERS) that substantiates GST/HST zero-rating.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian Limited Partnership (LP) is not a taxpayer for federal income tax purposes but is a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes under subsection 123(1) of the Excise Tax Act and must register through a Business Number (BN). SOP: (1) Apply via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Accounts'; (2) CRA issues a 9-digit BN with program-account suffix 'RT' for GST/HST, and the LP opens a separate program account for T5013 partnership information-return filing; (3) the application must disclose each general and limited partner, including non-resident partners and their foreign addresses; (4) a non-resident partner or a foreign entity without a Canadian establishment may file the non-resident GST/HST registration variant of Form RC1 and must appoint a Canadian tax representative (Form RC59) where CRA requires a domestic agent for service and notices; (5) the same BN serves as the customs business identifier for CBSA export declarations; registration becomes effective on the day the small-supplier threshold is exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, the LP is fiscally transparent: income, capital gains, losses and tax attributes are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners according to the partnership agreement; each partner, including a non-resident partner, reports its allocated share in its own tax return, and the LP files only an information return (T5013). For GST/HST the same LP is a distinct registrant: it collects and remits GST/HST on its taxable supplies, files periodic GST/HST returns, and claims input tax credits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, the LP is fiscally transparent: income, capital gains, losses and tax attributes are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners according to the partnership agreement; each partner, including a non-resident partner, reports its allocated share in its own tax return, and the LP files only an information return (T5013). For GST/HST the same LP is a distinct registrant: it collects and remits GST/HST on its taxable supplies, files periodic GST/HST returns, and claims input tax credits. Non-resident partners carrying on business in Canada through the LP are deemed by section 253 of the Income Tax Act to be carrying on business in Canada and may incur Part I filing obligations, Part XIII withholding, or both.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a U.S.-style check-the-box election; the LP flow-through classification is statutory and non-elective. Relevant elections and mandatory regimes in this NVOCC structure: (i) Excise Tax Act section 156 related-party election, which may treat otherwise taxable supplies between closely related registrants, including partnerships owned by the same members, as made for no consideration; (ii) Income Tax Act subsection 96(1)(b) election permitting the partnership to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period; (iii) the GST/HST Quick Method and nil-remittance elections available to qualifying registrants, subject to eligibility thresholds; (iv) the mandatory hybrid mismatch rules in Income Tax Act sections 18.4 to 18.6, which automatically deny deductions for duplicate deduction, deduction/non-inclusion and reverse-hybrid outcomes and cannot be overridden by election or contract; and (v) treaty-based Part XIII withholding arrangements requiring CRA approval where non-resident partners receive management fees, interest, rents or royalties from the LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold measured on total taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, made over the current quarter and the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. Exceeding CAD 30,000 triggers mandatory registration under section 240(1) of the Excise Tax Act; the LP must register effective the day the threshold is exceeded. A non-resident freight-forwarding business that solicits orders in Canada, maintains employees or agents in Canada, or performs services in Canada may also be required to register even where its invoicing is issued offshore.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An NVOCC ocean freight forwarder may zero-rate (0% GST/HST) supplies under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act: (i) Part I section 16 for goods exported by the supplier; (ii) Part V section 22 for a freight transportation service, or a service in respect of goods transported under a contract for freight transportation, where the goods are shipped from a place in Canada to a place outside Canada or from a place outside Canada to a place in Canada; and (iii) Part V section 16 for a service supplied to a non-resident that acquires the service for consumption entirely outside Canada. Containerized ocean carriage handled by the NVOCC therefore qualifies where the bill-of-lading route crosses the Canadian border or is performed wholly outside Canada; a purely domestic Canadian inland trucking or warehousing leg that is not part of an international through-shipment remains taxable at the headline GST/HST rate. Zero-rating is conditional on the supplier holding contemporaneous documentary evidence of the non-resident recipient and the physical export; CRA applies the evidentiary tests set out in GST/HST Memoranda 4-7 and 17-5.

  • CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration / eManifest filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods physically exported from Canada
  • House Bill of Lading issued by the NVOCC and the underlying ocean carrier Master Bill of Lading / cargo manifest showing the foreign port of discharge
  • Commercial invoice and written freight-forwarding contract identifying the non-resident customer, the shipping route, and the zero-rated supply description
  • Proof of export: ocean terminal receipt, container gate-out/gate-in record, vessel sailing report, dock receipt, or foreign customs entry evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / ACH transaction record evidencing collection of the freight consideration from the non-resident within the 180-day benchmark
  • GST/HST zero-rated supply register, input tax credit (ITC) allocation file, and T5013 partnership schedule supporting the LP's export transactions

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, pre-authorization, digital stamping, or continuous-transaction-control requirement. Registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or the XML/large-business e-file protocol and must retain invoices satisfying the content requirements of GST/HST Memorandum 16.2 (supplier name and BN, recipient, description, amount and tax charged). Since 2024, a voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service using the Peppol PINT BIS 3.0 / EN16931 standard exists for structured B2B e-invoices; issuance remains elective and does not replace CRA statutory books-and-records obligations.

CRA applies enhanced audit scrutiny to NVOCC and freight-forwarding zero-rating claims because fabricated or 'ghost' export schemes are a recognized fraud typology. Under section 298 of the Excise Tax Act, the CRA may normally reassess within four years of the required filing date; where the LP or its partners commit misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default, the four-year cap is removed and retrospective clawbacks may reach all open years. On adverse findings, the CRA assesses uncollected GST/HST on the supply, denies associated input tax credits, compounds daily prescribed-rate interest, and imposes: (a) the gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the misstated tax (minimum CAD 100) under ETA section 280.1; (b) failure-to-file and failure-to-remit penalties under ETA section 280; and (c) criminal fines up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years for knowing evasion under ETA section 327. False export declarations independently trigger CBSA administrative monetary penalties under the Customs Act, and partnership members remain jointly and severally liable to the extent provided by applicable partnership law and CRA collection provisions for any GST/HST liability assessed on amounts later reversed from the zero-rated export claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no residual exchange controls; the Canadian dollar (CAD) trades freely under a floating-rate regime, and the BoC does not ration FX or restrict repatriation. Profit distribution and capital repatriation to non-resident limited partners of an NVOCC Limited Partnership (LP) are legally unrestricted, provided the partnership files Part XIII non-resident withholding (25%, reduced under the Canada-United States Tax Convention to 0% for active business profits not attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment, or 5%/15% for passive payments) and complies with FATCA/CRS reporting. Escrow and operational foreign-currency balances may be held indefinitely offshore or onshore without conversion mandates; statutory friction is limited to FINTRAC reporting for incoming/outgoing international electronic funds transfers (EFTs) over CAD 10,000 within a 24-hour window, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) trade documentation interface, and Investment Canada Act (ICA) net-benefit review only when a non-Canadian acquires control of an LP's business above the annually adjusted WTO-investor monetary threshold (typically not triggered for an NVOCC asset value below CAD 1 billion).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory percentage surrender, and no conversion ratio for export proceeds; NVOCC freight-forwarder receivables denominated in USD, EUR, or CNY may remain in foreign currency in Canadian or offshore accounts without FX conversion. The sole obligations are: (i) FINTRAC EFT reporting for cross-border transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, and (ii) income recognition in CAD for tax purposes under section 261 of the Income Tax Act using the Bank of Canada noon or daily average exchange rate (or a CRA-approved quarterly rate).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (BoC) is the central monetary and lender-of-last-resort authority; foreign exchange administration is not centralized in the BoC — the Department of Finance Canada holds the statutory mandate for exchange control policy (the Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951), while the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates chartered banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) enforces AML/CFT surveillance of cross-border funds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, S.C. 2000, c. 17 (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) cannot act as the primary treasury rail for a Canadian LP unless they hold a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business (MSB) licence in Canada and partner with a Schedule I bank for CAD settlement; no offshore EMI may issue a Canadian clearing-code account (institution/transit/account number), originate BCD or ACH debits in Canada, or provide CDIC-insured deposit custody. An offshore EMI is legally viable only as a secondary collection/payment concentration node for outbound disbursements in hard-currency jurisdictions, contingent on the LP's own FINTRAC MSB registration if it offers payment services to third parties (not the case for internal NVOCC treasury operations). For the strategic objective of escrow integrity (pass-through partner capital), the mandatory rail is a Schedule I chartered bank account with CDIC coverage and OSFI custody standards.

Compliant routing for foreign capital contributions from non-resident limited partners: SWIFT MT103 (single client credit transfer) to the LP's CAD operational account or USD escrow subledger at a Schedule I bank; mandatory remittance field 70 should read 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — [LP NAME] PARTNERSHIP ACCOUNT' with the partner's ledger reference; field 52/56/57 must show the originating bank as an OSFI-recognized entity. All inbound EFTs over CAD 10,000 trigger automatic FINTRAC reporting by the receiving bank; no pre-approval is required. CAD credits settle irrevocably in real time through the Bank of Canada's Lynx high-value payment system (ISO 20022, pacs.008); USD credits settle via SWIFT/CHIPS through the bank's U.S. correspondent (e.g., BMO Harris, TD Bank, N.A.) with same-day finality. If the inbound contribution exceeds the ICA notification threshold (annual WTO-investor adjusted amount, approximately CAD 1.24–1.37 billion in 2024–2025), a Form IS-1 notification must be filed with Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED); an NVOCC capital injection of operational scale will not trigger this, but counsel should confirm.Clearing rails for the NVOCC operational cycle: (1) SWIFT MT103 for all direct ocean-carrier payments (Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, Hapag-Lloyd), terminal handling charges (THC), and foreign customs duties — beneficiary BIC, invoice number, and container reference must appear in field 70 for audit traceability; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600 by a Schedule I Canadian bank for vessel sub-charter or container depot contracts exceeding USD 250,000, with negotiation through the U.S. correspondent and SWIFT MT700/MT202 flow; (3) US ACH Direct (via FedACH or The Clearing House EPN) for U.S.-based drayage, warehouse, and intermodal partners, requiring the LP to hold a U.S. dollar account and valid US routing number obtained through the parent or bank arrangement; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) cannot act as the primary treasury rail for a Canadian LP unless they hold a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business (MSB) licence in Canada and partner with a Schedule I bank for CAD settlement; no offshore EMI may issue a Canadian clearing-code account (institution/transit/account number), originate BCD or ACH debits in Canada, or provide CDIC-insured deposit custody. An offshore EMI is legally viable only as a secondary collection/payment concentration node for outbound disbursements in hard-currency jurisdictions, contingent on the LP's own FINTRAC MSB registration if it offers payment services to third parties (not the case for internal NVOCC treasury operations). For the strategic objective of escrow integrity (pass-through partner capital), the mandatory rail is a Schedule I chartered bank account with CDIC coverage and OSFI custody standards.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the limited partnership entity level (fiscally transparent flow-through); each partner is taxed on its allocated share of Canadian active business income, with federal corporate partners at 15% general rate (or 9% on the first C$500,000 of CCPC active income under the Small Business Deduction) and individual partners at progressive federal rates up to 33% plus provincial tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on dividend payments from a Canadian resident corporation; standard profit distributions by a limited partnership to non-resident partners are not dividends and are not subject to Part XIII withholding, but are taxable under Part I as Canadian-source business income of the partner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate non-arm's length cross-border transactions with non-residents triggers the T106 filing requirement; if the LP is part of a multinational group with C$750,000,000 or more consolidated revenue, Country-by-Country reporting and a Master File are mandatory. Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is expected for all material intercompany NVOCC service, container arrangement and financing transactions regardless of amount.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s.245, the sham doctrine, and the treaty-shopping/substance-over-form principles to disregard artificial NVOCC structures. The LP must maintain real commercial substance in Canada — physical premises, Canadian employees, actual container booking and carrier contracting activity, and valid licences — to support Canadian-source income characterization and to preserve treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: fine not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA may also impose a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the unreported amount under s.163(1), plus compound interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner who authorize or acquiesce in the misstatement can be personally liable for unpaid source deductions or Part XIII amounts; courts may pierce the limited-partner liability shield when the partner participates in control, allowing execution on personal assets, asset-freezing orders and travel restrictions pending collection or prosecution.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can apply GAAR to recharacterize the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, and reassess Part XIII withholding at 25%; a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax and interest may be assessed.

Director personal liability: If the corporate general partner is a shell, directors and controlling limited partners who exercise management authority may be treated as carrying on business jointly and lose the limited-partner shield; travel bans, passport surrender orders and civil restitution can attach where CRA obtains a court order in serious collection or evasion cases.
AML / Proceeds of Crime Exposure

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties can reach C$1,000,000 per violation; Criminal Code money-laundering offences carry up to 10 years imprisonment and unlimited fines; civil forfeiture and asset seizure of freight proceeds may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners and compliance officers may be prosecuted personally if they knowingly facilitate money laundering; courts commonly impose travel bans and passport surrender as pre-trial conditions in significant financial-crime prosecutions; responsible persons may be disqualified from financial or freight-payment service roles.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Incomplete Documentation

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the transfer price under ITA s.247 and reassess income; a 10% penalty is imposed on the gross adjustment amount where reasonable efforts were not made to determine the arm's-length price; T106 late-filing penalties of C$100 per day, up to C$2,500, may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly liable for unremitted source deductions and specified GST/HST; CRA may recharacterize excessive distributions to directors or controlling limited partners as fraudulent conveyances; senior executives approving false TP documentation may face criminal tax-evasion charges with personal imprisonment exposure.
