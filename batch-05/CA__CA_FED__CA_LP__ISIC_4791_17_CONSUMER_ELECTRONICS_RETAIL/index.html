Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Confirm Canadian 'carrying on business' nexus (Canadian fulfillment inventory, employees, or permanent establishment). Step 2: Apply for a federal Business Number (BN) for the Limited Partnership via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' with the partnership agreement and, where an agent acts, Form RC59 'Business Consent'. Step 3: Activate the GST/HST account (program code RT, resulting in a BN such as 123456789 RT 0001); non-resident suppliers of digital/remote services may use the CRA's simplified non-resident GST/HST registration. Step 4: Register for payroll (RP) if the LP has Canadian employees. Step 5: The LP must then file the annual T5013 Partnership Information Return where it carries on business in Canada even if all partners are non-residents. No minimum capital contribution is statutorily required for the LP; foreign ownership is permitted. Identification for partners is reported separately under their own BN/SIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box elective classification; the LP is statutorily fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (Canada) subdivision j (ss. 96-98), and no domestic election converts it into a taxable entity. Relevant statutory elections and mechanisms include: (i) the s. 96(3) election in prescribed form filed with the Minister, which deems the partnership not to be a partnership where all members consent (used for co-ownership/joint-venture structures); (ii) the s. 249.1(4) election to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period where all partners are non-residents; (iii) the s. 261 functional-currency election permitting income computation in USD or another eligible currency; and (iv) for US federal tax purposes only, an IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election may reclassify the LP as an association or disregarded entity, but the CRA ignores such foreign classification and applies Canadian domestic transparency and treaty-based rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (total worldwide taxable supplies of the supplier and associates in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; for non-resident suppliers of digital or remote services under the July 1, 2021 simplified regime, CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies to Canadian consumers in a 12-month period triggers registration)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, an export supply of tangible personal property is zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods by or on behalf of the supplier and delivery occurs outside Canada. For online retail of consumer electronics and smart gadgets shipped from Canadian inventory to foreign end-customers, 0% GST/HST applies provided the supplier obtains contemporaneous documentary proof of export (carrier waybill, customs export declaration, and foreign customs entry) and retains it for six years. Ancillary freight and logistics services supplied to non-residents in connection with exported goods may also be zero-rated. Supplies delivered in Canada, or released from a Canadian fulfillment centre to a Canadian address, remain taxable at 5% GST (or the applicable HST rate in a participating province). The claimant must hold the export evidence at the time of filing the GST/HST return to support net-tax zero-rating.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (formerly B13A) filed electronically via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for shipments valued over CAD 2,000; alternative exporter records for low-value postal or courier shipments
  • Commercial invoice / sales contract identifying the foreign buyer and Canadian seller
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by a common carrier demonstrating outbound physical movement from Canada
  • Bank inward remittance statement / FX transfer record (equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Carrier delivery confirmation or foreign customs entry documentation evidencing delivery outside Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance or pre-invoice stamping mandate exists in Canada; the CRA operates a self-assessment model. Registrants must issue an invoice or other supporting document containing vendor and recipient names, the vendor's BN, the date, the consideration, and the GST/HST charged (ETA s. 223). Electronic filing of returns is mandatory for all registrants via GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary, aligned with the Pan-Canadian Electronic Business Document Standard (EN 16931); payment gateways such as Stripe and PayPal have no CRA clearance integration.

CRA audit programs (GST/HST Memorandum 4-5-2) require contemporaneous and coherent export documentation. Deficient records may cause reversal of the 0% rate, reassessment at the standard 5% GST/HST, denial of associated input tax credits, and retrospective clawback of refunds. The general reassessment window is four years (ETA s. 298); the period extends to seven years for negligence and is unlimited for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. Interest accrues daily at the CRA quarterly-adjusted prescribed rate. Penalties include: (i) failure-to-file penalties under ETA s. 280, equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the net tax understated; (ii) false-statement penalties under ETA s. 285(1), equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the misstated amount where the person acted knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence; and (iii) on indictment for wilful evasion under ETA s. 327, imprisonment of up to five years and a fine ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded. The CRA may also revoke the GST/HST registration and pursue designated members of the partnership for amounts owing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized current and capital account under the Currency Act and the Investment Canada Act. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible; there are no currency exchange controls, no prior approval requirements for foreign direct investment remittances, and no restrictions on profit repatriation, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees payable to non-resident partners, subject to normal tax withholding and anti-money-laundering verification. All transfers must comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) reporting thresholds for incoming and outgoing electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements in Canada. Exporters and online retailers may retain proceeds in foreign currency, convert freely at negotiated market rates, or repatriate at their discretion. No statutorily imposed timeline or ratio exists. Banking practice generally requires the underlying commercial invoice and shipping documentation for the credit of trade proceeds, and for income tax purposes, revenue recognition follows the accrual accounting method under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — Foreign digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) providers are not chartered banks in Canada and cannot accept deposits or provide deposit-like products. They may operate as money services businesses (MSBs) only if registered with FINTRAC and subject to provincial licensing. In practice, a foreign EMI may be used for limited payment facilitation, but full treasury clearing through a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank is highly recommended. Some Canadian banks are reluctant to open accounts if the primary operating account is held with an unregulated offshore EMI; thus an EMI should be supplementary, not core.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident partners to the Canadian Limited Partnership should be routed via SWIFT MT103 using a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank. The remitter must include full legal entity name, address, account number, and a clear payment reference: 'Capital Contribution — [Partner Name] to [LP Name]'. Under Canadian practice, no mandatory 'purpose code' is used, but the bank will require a narrative purpose statement such as 'Limited partnership capital contribution under the Limited Partnerships Act (Ontario)' and supporting documentation evidencing the transaction, including the partnership agreement, capital commitment schedule, and KYC forms. For amounts equal to or greater than CAD 10,000, the bank must file a Large Cash or Electronic Funds Transaction Report with FINTRAC. There is no exchange-control approval requirement for inbound capital; however, the transaction must not breach sanctions or terrorist-financing controls. The funds should be cleared to the LP's Capital Escrow Account before conversion into CAD for operational use.Outbound trade settlements for consumer electronics retail should use SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments and Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) for high-value or milestone-based purchases. MT103 payments to foreign suppliers require precise SWIFT BIC, IBAN/account, beneficiary name, and a clear invoice reference. Commercial LC terms should be irrevocable and confirmed by a prime Canadian bank where the beneficiary is in a higher-risk jurisdiction. For CAD-chargeable payments to foreign beneficiaries, the LP's bank will execute conversion at the spot rate; for USD-dominated trade flows, operate a USD clearing account to reduce conversion costs. All outgoing wires exceeding CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC reporting.
