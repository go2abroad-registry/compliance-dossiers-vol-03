Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); GST/HST program administered via the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and My Business Account (canada.ca); ministerial authority: the Minister of National Revenue under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. E-15.

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Appoint the general partner and identify the partnership's place of business. Step 3: Determine mandatory registrant status under ETA s. 240: a partnership carrying on a commercial activity in Canada is required to register unless it qualifies as a small supplier; voluntary registration is available on application. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope: the LP is a fiscally transparent flow-through entity for federal income tax (income and losses are allocated to partners under ITA s. 96) but is a separate person for GST/HST purposes and must register in its own right as a registrant; no federal tax-exempt zone applies to the franchise commercial activity, and GST/HST applies to taxable supplies made in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a U.S.-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows legal form under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act. Principal statutory elections: (i) ETA s. 273 joint election designating a registrant member (typically the general partner) as agent to account for and remit the partnership's GST/HST; (ii) the partnership rollover election under ITA s. 97(2) allowing a partner to transfer property to the LP on a tax-deferred basis; (iii) the ETA s. 156 annual nil-consideration election between the LP and a closely related registrant corporation, available only in narrow corporate-shareholding configurations; and (iv) hybrid mismatch compliance: ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 require that cross-border franchise royalty and financing arrangements be structured so that a payment deductible in Canada is concurrently included in the income of the non-resident recipient in a treaty state, or the deduction is denied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible floating currency under IMF Article VIII. Canada does not maintain capital or current account exchange controls; dividends, partnership allocations, interest, royalties and repatriated capital can be freely remitted through chartered banks without central bank approval. Cross-border wires and conversions are conducted by authorized institutions under standard market rules; the only hurdle for a limited partnership is withholding tax compliance under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for non-resident payees and the filing of a T5/T5013 or Part XIII return, not governmental FX approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency receipts, and no obligation to repatriate offshore earnings into CAD. Export and service receipts may be held indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banking institutions; if the LP receives royalties in USD from a US master licensee, the funds can be converted at market rate when repatriated, with reporting to Statistics Canada for balance-of-payments purposes and to the Canada Revenue Agency for annual tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in conjunction with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML oversight; no dedicated foreign exchange administration for approving convertibility.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary banking; offshore digital EMI accounts are not recognized as permissible substitutes for a Canadian corporate operating account. An EMI without FINTRAC MSB registration cannot provide regulated payment services into or within Canada; the Canadian LP may use such an EMI only as a payment collection gateway in jurisdictions where the EMI is duly licensed, with all funds consolidated daily into a tier-1 Canadian bank account. To be viable, the EMI must be registered as a money services business with FINTRAC (if operating here) or act solely as a foreign payment intermediary, never as the deposit-holding or clearing institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate for Canadian corporations after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general reduction; reduced federal rate of 9% for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) on the first C$500,000 of active business income. A limited partnership is fiscally transparent; it is not a taxpayer and the rate applies only to corporate partners on their allocable share of Canadian-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Distributions from a limited partnership are not dividends and are treated as partner-level income allocations; non-resident corporate partners are subject to Part I income tax on their Canadian-source business income allocation. If the LP is a “SIFT partnership” (publicly traded) or a non-resident investor receives management fees, royalties or interest, Part XIII rates apply separately.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1 million aggregate arm’s-length threshold for mandatory Form T106 filing; contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation required for any transaction above C$1 million. Enhanced Master File and Local File documentation is expected where Canadian revenues exceed C$50 million and related-party cross-border transactions exceed C$5 million; Country-by-Country report compulsory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above C$750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under ITA s.245 to deny tax benefits from artificial transactions lacking economic substance. The LP must maintain real substance in Canada: bank accounts, employees, premises, board decisions and actual management control. A “letterbox” limited partnership with no Canadian operational substance is at high risk of GAAR reassessment and potential imposition of the 50% penalty for misrepresentation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal prosecution under ITA s.239: summary conviction fine of 50%–200% of the tax evaded and up to 2 years imprisonment; indictment up to 5 years imprisonment. CRA may also assess interest and revoke business privileges.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unpaid source deductions (ITA s.227.1). Where the director knowingly participated in evasion, CRA can pursue civil restitution, asset freezing, travel restraint and personal bankruptcy proceedings.
Abusive Avoidance / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of the tax benefit; reassessment of taxes and interest; penalty of up to 50% under s.163(2); in severe cases fine up to C$1,000,000 and imprisonment under offshore compliance rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed under the ITA s.160 transfer rules for assets transferred to avoid tax; a director who manages a shell LP can be personally liable for the partnership’s unpaid Canadian tax to the extent of the transferred assets. CRA can issue travel bans to prevent departure from Canada pending audit.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: 10% transfer-pricing penalty under ITA s.247(3) on the net adjustment, plus full reassessed tax and interest. CRA can also disregard the transaction under s.247(2) and recharacterise it.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner that authorises false transfer-pricing documentation face personal exposure under wilful blind-eye rules for penalty assessments and prosecution for accessory to tax evasion. CRA may require director attendance for cross-examination and obtain a court order for restitution.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: up to C$500,000 for a corporation, C$100,000 for individuals, plus criminal money-laundering offence under Criminal Code s.462—up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally liable for failing to implement a compliance program, for knowingly causing signature cash split transactions, and for approving payments to non-resident trusts without beneficial-ownership identification. Criminal money-laundering convictions carry director imprisonment up to 10 years and civil forfeiture orders.
Failure to File / Instalment Violation

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% of tax payable plus 1% per month up to 12 months; source-deduction failure penalty of 10% of amounts not withheld or remitted plus interest and consequential gross negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for all payroll source deductions withheld but not remitted to the Receiver General; CRA can issue a director’s liability assessment and obtain a court judgment, garnishment, and director passport denial in serious cases.
