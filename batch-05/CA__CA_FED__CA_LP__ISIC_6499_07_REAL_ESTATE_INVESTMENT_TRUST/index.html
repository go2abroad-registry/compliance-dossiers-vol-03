Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); primary digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses.html); entity registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned or non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online, by phone, or by mail using Form RC1. The BN serves as the statutory tax identifier for the partnership and is used to open a GST/HST account, payroll account, and customs/import-export account. For a non-resident LP earning Canadian-source income, CRA requires a Canadian representative or agent, the issuance of Form T5013/T5013A slips, and the filing of Form T5013 partnership information return; non-resident partners must disclose their foreign tax identification numbers, and the LP must implement Part XIII and Part XIV withholding procedures on allocations and distributions. If the limited partnership is a specified investment flow-through (SIFT) partnership, CRA requires it to obtain a corporate-style BN and file a T2 corporate return.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-owned or non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online, by phone, or by mail using Form RC1. The BN serves as the statutory tax identifier for the partnership and is used to open a GST/HST account, payroll account, and customs/import-export account. If the limited partnership is a specified investment flow-through (SIFT) partnership, CRA requires it to obtain a corporate-style BN and file a T2 corporate return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election; the limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act section 96. Available statutory mechanisms include: the GST/HST closely related persons/partnerships election under Excise Tax Act section 156 for nil-consideration transfers; partnership fiscal-year election; and for the REIT business model, the real estate investment trust designation/election under ITA subsection 122.1(3), which permits a qualifying trust to maintain conduit treatment for property income. The SIFT deeming rules in ITA subsection 122.1(2) and related provisions apply compulsorily to publicly traded or otherwise-captured property LPs and generally cannot be displaced by unilateral election. Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA sections 18.2 and 18.3, with amendments to section 12.2) are non-elective and impose deduction disallowances; relief is available only through factual structuring and contemporaneous documentation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (C$30,000) of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold), unless the non-resident supplier is carrying on business in Canada; non-resident digital-economy suppliers making specified supplies to Canadian consumers may be required to register regardless of the threshold. The LP REIT must register once taxable supplies exceed C$30,000; zero-rated export supplies count for threshold purposes but do not attract output tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the supplier (or delivered outside Canada to a non-resident recipient) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V if export occurs as soon as practical after supply and the supplier retains prescribed evidence. Services supplied to a non-resident and performed entirely outside Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part VI; services in respect of Canadian real property, including property management, leasing, and development services, are generally taxable. The REIT LP may claim input tax credits (ITCs) on expenses attributable to zero-rated export supplies in the same GST/HST return and must support the zero-rating with export documentation.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration filed through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or equivalent paper Form B13A
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing shipment to the non-resident
  • Commercial invoice identifying the non-resident purchaser and the goods/services
  • Packing list
  • Purchase order, contract, or signed sale agreement showing transfer of title, risk, and possession
  • Carrier's receipt or customs terminal proof of export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire / FX receipt evidencing payment or repatriation of export proceeds
  • T5013/T5013A schedules confirming the flow-through of export income to resident and non-resident partners

Canada does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or government pre-clearance of invoices. Electronic invoices (EDI, XML, PDF) are valid under the Excise Tax Act if legible, complete, and auditable. CRA supports GST/HST electronic filing through My Business Account, Netfile, and machine-to-machine APIs. The Government of Canada requires business-to-government (B2G) invoices to be transmitted electronically using the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing infrastructure based on the PEPPOL/CIUS interoperability standard; no e-invoicing clearance mandate applies to private B2B or B2C transactions.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess uncollected GST/HST, disallow input tax credits, and impose compound daily interest if the documentary bundle is incomplete or the export timing tests are not met. False statements or omissions expose the LP to gross-negligence civil penalties under ETA section 289.2 and ITA section 163.2, typically the greater of C$100 and 25% to 50% of the understated tax. Willful evasion is a criminal offence with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks are generally limited to the applicable statutory reassessment window, or are unlimited in cases of fraud, neglect, or wilful misrepresentation. In the REIT context, recharacterization of export zero-rating transactions can also trigger Part XIII or Part XIV dividend/withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners and a SIFT-tax clawback on the partnership.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions. The Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act establish the Canadian dollar as legal tender, while Canada maintains an open capital account with no general restrictions on inbound or outbound transfers, profit remittances, dividend distributions, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. Non-resident investors in a Canadian limited partnership may remit distributable income and capital freely subject only to tax withholding under the Income Tax Act and federally enacted anti-money laundering/reporting thresholds. No advance regulatory approval, surrender requirement, or differentiated conversion rate applies to real estate investment trust distributions or capital events.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement for export receipts. Residents and non-resident controlled partnerships may retain, hold, and disburse foreign currency earnings in any currency, and no central bank directive requires conversion to Canadian dollars. Reporting obligations are limited to FINTRAC’s large virtual currency transaction reports, terrorist property reports, and suspicious transaction reporting thresholds; customary banking documentation must evidence the commercial or investment purpose and beneficial ownership of any wire transfer.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, operating under the Bank of Canada Act, as the federal monetary authority; foreign exchange regulatory oversight for cross-border payment integrity is shared with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision. Canada imposes no centralized exchange-control licensing authority comparable to an FX administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for foreign digital EMI-only structures and Medium for regulated Canadian Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC. A non-resident electronic money institution with no Canadian registration cannot act as the primary operational clearing bank for a Canadian limited partnership holding real estate assets, as such entities are not chartered banks under the Bank Act and may not take deposits in Canada. Canadian-licensed MSBs and provincially regulated fintech entities may facilitate FX, remittance, and payment services only as supplementary clearing rails, subject to FINTRAC MSB registration, or a restrictive remittance licence in Quebec, and to partnership account KYC requirements. The statutory deposit-taking monopoly of Canadian Schedule I banks makes a chartered bank account mandatory for principal capital custody, mortgage disbursement, and investor distribution operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for capital injections into the Canadian limited partnership’s capital escrow account uses SWIFT MT103 through a Canadian chartered bank correspondent, with detailed remittance information including a clear purpose of payment code such as ISIC/NAICS reference for real estate acquisition capital, the partnership’s legal name, the general partner or authorized signatory, and a supporting reference to the subscription agreement or capital call notice. The Canadian bank will require pre-notification of any wire exceeding CAD 10,000 and will file a large cash transaction report or large virtual currency report only where necessary; for corporate capital calls, notification of expected value, source jurisdiction, and beneficial ownership is required. Inbound investments should be denominated in CAD or USD; if received in a third currency, the receiving bank’s automated conversion desk will apply a customary retail or corporate rate unless a standing forward contract is pre-agreed. For non-resident funded partnerships, FINTRAC-compliant beneficial ownership evidence must accompany the capital call instruction, and the bank will verify the remitter against Canadian economic sanctions lists prior to crediting the account.Outbound trade and investment disbursements for the real estate trust, including acquisition deposits, construction draws, property manager payments, and investor distributions, are settled via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wires, domestic large-value transfers through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or the modern Real-Time Rail for expedited payments, and Commercial Letters of Credit for third-party construction or supply contracts. All outbound disbursements require an authorized signatory mandate from the general partner and are subject to bank monitoring thresholds; payments to foreign beneficiaries require the beneficiary’s full legal name, account number or IBAN, SWIFT BIC, and a purpose code or unrestricted remittance explanation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations code. Instead, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and treaty-shopping limitations apply. A REIT/LP must demonstrate genuine management, staff, business premises, and real economic risk in Canada to access treaty benefits and avoid recharacterization as a conduit arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of the misstated amount under ITA s.163(2); fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years under ITA s.238 and Criminal Code s.380

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and executive trustees who participate or acquiesce are jointly and severally liable for penalties; criminal conviction can trigger travel bans, extradition orders, and restitution orders.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR applies to deny the tax benefit under ITA s.245; penalties may be imposed under s.163(2) for false statements; CRA may recharacterize distributions and assess Part XIII withholding tax with interest

Director personal liability: Limited partners lose statutory limited liability if they exercise control over the partnership; directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable for jeopardized tax amounts under ITA s.227.1; treaty benefits may be retroactively denied.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income to the arm's length amount under ITA s.247(2); a penalty of 10% of the gross adjustment applies under ITA s.247(3); interest and late-filing penalties accrue on any resulting deficiency

Director personal liability: Directors of the Canadian contracting entity may be assessed personally for any trust or withholding amounts not remitted; wilful participation in false documentation may result in criminal liability.
Non-Resident Withholding and Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: The payer is immediately assessable for the unremitted withholding tax, plus penalties of 10% of the amount not withheld and 20% of the amount not remitted; interest compounds monthly

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the full amount of unremitted withholding tax under ITA s.227.1 without a due diligence defence; CRA may freeze bank accounts, garnish receivables, and obtain court compliance orders.
