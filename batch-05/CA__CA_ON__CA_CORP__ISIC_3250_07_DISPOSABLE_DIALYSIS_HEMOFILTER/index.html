Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box flow-through election exists under Canadian Federal/Provincial corporate law; a Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (ITA). Available elections include: Section 216 ITA election for non-residents' rental income; Section 219 ITA election for non-resident corporations to file as a foreign business corporation (branch tax regime); Small-supplier optional GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(1); Quick Method of Accounting election under ETA s. 147 (Form GST74); Inter-company transfer pricing documentation election for contemporaneous documentation under ITA s. 247(4); Canada has no hybrid mismatch election, but ITA ss. 18.2/18.3 apply to hybrid mismatch arrangements in line with BEPS Action 2

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1): global taxable supplies below CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter and below CAD 30,000 in the last four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident suppliers carrying on a business in Canada that exceed this threshold must register; once registered, generally no derecognition of registration for 12 months)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1, a supply of tangible personal property (including disposable dialysis hemofilters) is zero-rated if the supplier (a) ships the property to a non-resident purchaser at a location outside Canada, or (b) delivers the property to a common carrier or consignee for export to a non-resident; goods must be exported as soon after the sale as is reasonable (CRA administrative practice generally expects export within 60 days of delivery); the supplier must retain proof of export for audit; supporting evidence normally requires at least two of the following: a customs export declaration, a bill of lading/air waybill, a commercial invoice, a freight forwarder or carrier certificate, or a copy of the CBSA cargo control document; for drop-shipment scenarios, the Canadian supplier may also rely on section 1 of Schedule VI with a written certificate from the non-resident recipient confirming export outside Canada

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form A8/A6) or automated export reporting through the Canada Border Services Agency single-window (GCA/CCL) confirming the shipment left Canada
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean or Air Waybill) showing the non-resident purchaser as consignee and final foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List issued by the Canadian exporter describing the medical device, quantity, and value
  • Carrier certificate / freight forwarder certificate of export (when used to satisfy the two-document rule)
  • CBSA stamped export certificate or provincial export permit if the goods are declared under controlled goods regimes

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API stamping, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada at federal or Ontario level; CRA accepts paper, PDF, EDI, or XML invoices provided they meet the prescribed information requirements under ETA ss. 169(1) and 169(4), including supplier name, BN, invoice date, and GST/HST amount; the federal government has announced a voluntary PEPPOL e-invoicing framework and is piloting adoption with the CRA 'E-Invoicing' initiative (expected phased launch from 2025); exporters should nonetheless maintain machine-readable records and a full audit trail of all zero-rated export claims

If export proof is insufficient, CRA will disallow the zero-rating and re-assess unreported GST/HST at 13% on the full sale price, plus arrears interest accruing from the date the tax would otherwise have been remitted (s. 280 ETA); gross negligence penalties under ETA s. 285 of up to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; false statements or omissions under ETA s. 285.1 subject to penalties of CAD 100 to CAD 25,000 plus criminal prosecution (up to CAD 25,000 fine and/or 12 months' imprisonment) for knowing false declarations; additional ITA transfer-pricing penalties under s. 247(3) may apply if the export pricing to a non-resident related party is mispriced (penalty up to 10% of the gross adjustment amount), and general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) may claw back tax benefits from abusive round-tripping or fictitious export arrangements; CRA has flagged medical device exporters as a high-risk category for fictitious export and shell-company audit examination

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible regime for both current and capital accounts with no restrictions on profit remittances or capital repatriation. Current account transactions including export proceeds are freely convertible. Large foreign exchange transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC for AML compliance. No mandatory surrender ratios or forced conversion requirements apply; repatriation timelines are governed by commercial contracts and standard 90-day guidelines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be repatriated to Canada within 90 days of receipt or as stipulated in supply contracts. No statutory mandatory conversion ratios or surrender obligations exist. All FX transactions above CAD 10,000 require FINTRAC declaration. Proper documentation including invoices, shipping records, and bank confirmations must be retained for audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs due to FINTRAC and OSFI oversight. Legal constraints include mandatory AML/KYC compliance, potential licensing for cross-border payment services, and restrictions on non-Canadian correspondent banking arrangements without proper verification.
