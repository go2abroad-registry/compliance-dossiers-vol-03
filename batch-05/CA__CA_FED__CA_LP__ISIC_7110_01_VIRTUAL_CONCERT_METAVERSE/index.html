Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The corporate registry of the selected province or territory; for Ontario, ServiceOntario through the Ontario Business Registry. Corporations Canada is not the competent registry for a limited partnership.

Governing statute

A limited partnership is not incorporated under a federal Canadian statute. Limited partnerships are formed under the limited partnership statute of the selected province or territory, such as the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.16, including sections 2, 3, 9 and 10. The Canada Business Corporations Act does not provide a federal limited partnership formation procedure.

Formation / constitutional filings

Provincial or territorial limited partnership declaration, registration statement, or certificate of limited partnership, depending on the selected jurisdiction. For Ontario, the filing is a declaration of limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Act.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 45 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital or mandatory escrow account generally applies to a Canadian limited partnership. Capital commitments, contributions, withdrawal rights, priority returns, and funding conditions must be stated in the limited partnership agreement. Any funds held pending formation should be maintained in a segregated client or subscription account under the applicable engagement and banking arrangements.

Funding & unlock path: No site-specific fire, structural, or occupational clearance is required for a remote software operation without controlled premises. Any leased studio, office, warehouse, data-centre, or event venue must satisfy municipal building and occupancy permits, fire-code inspection, emergency planning, electrical and structural requirements, accessibility rules, occupational health and safety legislation, worker training, and applicable workplace insurance registration. Public events may require venue approval, crowd-management plans, security arrangements, and emergency medical provisions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portal: My Business Account and the CRA Non-Resident Simplified GST/HST Registration portal; customs administration for export documentation: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A Canadian Limited Partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes but is nonetheless required to hold a federal Business Number (BN) issued by the CRA. The non-resident LP or its Canadian resident agent files Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) specifying the partnership's legal name, registered address, general partner details, and the ISIC 7110 virtual performance hosting and digital ticket tokenization activity; the CRA then issues a nine-digit BN with program account suffixes (e.g., RC for GST/HST, RP for payroll/remuneration, and RT for corporation income if applicable). For GST/HST, the LP must register as a supplier under the Excise Tax Act through My Business Account or the simplified non-resident digital economy registration channel, disclosing estimated Canadian and worldwide digital supply revenues; a non-resident LP may also be required to appoint a Canadian representative for service of documents and, where appropriate, post security required by the Minister. The partnership must file the annual T5013 partnership information return within five months after fiscal year-end and must ensure that foreign limited partners are disclosed; Canadian-source income allocations to non-resident partners may trigger Part XIII or Part I withholding and reporting requirements. The BN must be quoted on all GST/HST invoices, customs declarations, and CRA correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; partnership classification is statutory under Income Tax Act section 96. A limited partnership may, with all partners' concurrence, file an election under subsection 96(3) to compute income, taxable capital gains, and net capital losses at the partnership level before allocating those amounts to partners, although the entity remains fiscally transparent and does not become a taxpayer. For GST/HST, the Excise Tax Act provides elective grouping and closely related corporation/branch elections, but these do not change entity-level tax classification. Hybrid mismatch elections are not separately available; anti-avoidance provisions, including general anti-avoidance rules under ITA section 247 and the foreign entity characterization rules in sections 94.1 and 94.2, may recharacterize cross-border hybrid arrangements involving the LP and its non-resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. A supplier is a small supplier if its worldwide taxable supplies, including supplies made in Canada, are CAD 30,000 or less in a calendar quarter and in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters. For non-resident digital-economy suppliers of metaverse concert access and digital ticket tokens, mandatory GST/HST registration arises under the simplified regime when aggregate worldwide taxable digital supply revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 and the supplier makes taxable supplies to Canadian-resident consumers; the threshold is measured on a global revenue basis with Canadian place-of-supply attribution.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, supplies of intangible personal property and services are zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident of Canada who is not registered for GST/HST and the supply is not solely in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Digital concert access, virtual venue effects, and non-fungible ticket tokens supplied through a metaverse platform to a non-resident consumer generally qualify for zero-rating where the place of supply rules attribute the supply to Canada but the benefit is enjoyed mainly outside Canada. The zero-rating does not apply if the recipient is a Canadian resident, the non-resident is physically present in Canada when the service is performed, or the supply is a 'specially defined service' linked to Canadian property. The taxpayer must demonstrate through geolocation data, user account residency, payment rail settlement location, and contract terms that the recipient and the place of enjoyment are foreign.

  • CBSA export declaration and Canadian Export Declaration (CAED) filing log where tangible media or hardware is shipped
  • Commercial invoice with ISIC 7110 / NAICS 711310 classification and platform transaction identifier
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for any tangible component; service agreement for purely digital supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign-payment ledger from Stripe and USDC blockchain settlement showing receipt from non-resident payor
  • Signed terms of access and metaverse platform service agreement specifying the non-resident recipient and foreign enjoyment
  • IP geolocation, device, and consumer residency evidence extracted from the virtual event platform
  • GST/HST zero-rated supply substantiation record retained under ETA section 286

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API stamping, digital stamp, or invoice-clearance regime is in force in Canada. Electronic invoicing is permitted but voluntary; CRA supports the Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard built on Peppol interoperability. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing the supplier's BN, the recipient's name, the date, a description of the supply, and the tax amount under Excise Tax Act section 223. Non-resident digital suppliers using the simplified framework may issue electronic invoices and file GST/HST returns in Canadian dollars but are not subject to any CRA clearance API, unique invoice code, or real-time validation requirement. Books and records, including metaverse transaction logs, invoice files, and payment rail ledgers (Stripe and USDC), must be retained per ETA section 286.

CRA may reassess GST/HST returns within the normal four-year reassessment period, with no limitation period where the return is filed with a false statement, fraud, or gross negligence, and may claw back net tax reductions or input tax credits erroneously attributed to zero-rated export supplies. Penalties under ETA section 285(1) include a minimum of CAD 250 and up to 25% of the unreported or overstated net tax amount, plus interest under ETA section 280. For income tax, a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the additional tax payable may apply to partners under ITA section 163(2) where export invoices are fabricated or zero-rating claims are made without substantiation. Deliberately structured non-resident arrangements designed to manufacture zero-rated output or otherwise avoid Canadian tax on Canadian-source revenue can be challenged under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in ITA section 247, resulting in denial of the tax advantage, reassessment, interest, and penalties, as well as potential wilful-evasion prosecution under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility under IMF Article VIII for both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittances, dividends, or capital repatriation for resident or non-resident investors. Nevertheless, international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and physical import/export of currency or bearer monetary instruments exceeding CAD 10,000 must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose statutory surrender, compulsory conversion, or a repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Receipts may be retained in any designated foreign-currency account. The entity must retain underlying transaction records in accordance with PCMLTFA and the Income Tax Act (including Form T657 for transfer pricing, if applicable), but no central bank forced conversion applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — supervisory for monetary stability; FINTRAC administers cross-border fund transfer reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate-to-High. Canadian law permits electronic money institutions (EMIs) to operate as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) after FINTRAC registration and provincial licensing where applicable. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) cannot independently act as the primary settlement bank for a CAD-denominated escrow account; they may act as ancillary payment facilitators if they partner with a Schedule I Canadian bank and maintain segregated funds in a trust account at a regulated Canadian financial institution. A standalone offshore EMI without Canadian registration is not viable for CAD settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at LP level (fiscally transparent); Canadian federal Part I general corporate rate is 15%; CCPC partners may claim the 9% small business rate on active business income up to CAD 500,000 annually.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII gross withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident; a CA_LP profit distribution is not itself a dividend and is instead taxed as allocated partnership income. If the general partner is a corporation and later declares a dividend out of those profits, 25% statutory withholding applies, subject to treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013 must be filed no later than five months after the partnership's fiscal period end; for a calendar-year virtual concert enterprise, the deadline is May 31 of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed within six months of the corporate partner's taxation year-end; any corporate general partner of CA_LP must report its allocated Canadian-source partnership income on this return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation are mandatory when the fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with specified non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the fiscal period.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations registration regime, but CRA applies common-law substance-over-form, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, and treaty anti-abuse rules. A virtual-concert LP must have genuine Canadian operational substance, real commercial management by the general partner, actual employees or contracted operators, Canadian bank accounts, and authentic commercial risk in order to preserve pass-through tax treatment and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239 offences: summary conviction a fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; indictment a fine of up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Reassessment outside the normal limitation period is permitted where wilful misrepresentation is established.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any director of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under ITA s.227.1; CRA may impose director liability, asset liens, garnishment, and restrict travel where collection is at risk.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the LP as a sham, reallocate all business income to the controlling non-resident entity, deny treaty benefits under the GAAR, and impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty plus daily interest on corrected Part I tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling general partners may face civil restitution, joint and several liability for taxes assessed on the corporate entity, and piercing of the corporate veil where the LP is found to be a mere facade; serious cases may trigger travel bans and criminal fraud charges.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: PCMLTFA administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for corporations and CAD 100,000 for individuals per violation, plus criminal fines and stop-activity orders; unregistered crypto transmission is a separate criminal offence.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors can be prosecuted where the offence was committed with their consent or negligence; personal fines up to CAD 500,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, disqualification from operating regulated money services business, and restrictive travel conditions are available sanctions.
