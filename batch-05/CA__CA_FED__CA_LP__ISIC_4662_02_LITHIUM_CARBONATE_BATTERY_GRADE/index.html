Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); GST/HST and income tax administration via 'My Business Account' portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html); customs and export declarations administered by Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS); Business Registration Online (BRO) for federal program accounts.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Limited Partnership provincially under the applicable Partnership Act (e.g., Alberta, Ontario, or British Columbia) and obtain the provincial certificate of limited partnership. (2) Obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online or Form RC1; the BN is issued with a default RL (business) program account. (3) Open a GST/HST program account (RT suffix) effective from the first day of commercial supply; registration is mandatory under ETA s. 148 once annual taxable supplies made in Canada (including zero-rated exports) exceed C$30,000. (4) Open an Import/Export program account (RM suffix) with CRA/CBSA; enroll in CERS for electronic export declarations on shipments exceeding C$2,000 or controlled goods. (5) Non-resident partners must each obtain a Canadian tax identification number (SIN, ITN, or non-resident BN) and, where applicable, file Form NR301/NR302 to substantiate treaty-reduced withholding on passive Canadian-source payments; treaty exemption for active business profits is claimed via the partner's Canadian Part I return. (6) If the LP is non-resident, it must appoint a Canadian representative acceptable to CRA for GST/HST administration.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. For income tax, a Canadian LP is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96: income and losses are computed at partnership level and allocated to partners; no federal entity-level income tax applies. For GST/HST, the LP is a separate 'person' under ETA s. 123(1) and is the registrant responsible for returns, ITCs, and export zero-rating. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on their allocable share of the LP's Canadian-source business income (ITA s. 2(3)(b) and s. 115), generally where the LP carries on business through a permanent establishment in Canada (ITA s. 253). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For income tax, a Canadian LP is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96: income and losses are computed at partnership level and allocated to partners; no federal entity-level income tax applies. For GST/HST, the LP is a separate 'person' under ETA s. 123(1) and is the registrant responsible for returns, ITCs, and export zero-rating. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on their allocable share of the LP's Canadian-source business income (ITA s. 2(3)(b) and s. 115), generally where the LP carries on business through a permanent establishment in Canada (ITA s. 253). Export arbitrage arrangements under which title to battery-grade lithium carbonate passes outside Canada and no Canadian PE exists will not attract Canadian business income tax for foreign partners. Territorial scope: Canadian tax reaches the LP's income attributable to Canadian PE activities; foreign partners remain taxable in their own residence jurisdiction. Budget 2024 proposed ITA s. 96(2.1) would deem certain LPs with 90%+ non-resident partners and greater than 95% arm's-length marketed capital to be corporations; operating commodity-trading LPs with substantive Canadian activity are not within the proposed target.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style 'check-the-box' election; classification follows domestic statutes and facts. Relevant elections and mechanisms: (1) ITA s. 249.1(2) — election for a non-calendar partnership fiscal period; (2) ITA s. 97(2) — tax-deferred rollover election on property contributed to the LP; (3) ITA s. 216 — net-basis election on Canadian rental income allocable to non-resident partners; (4) Form NR301/NR302 — treaty-based reduction or waiver of Part XIII withholding; (5) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.2-18.4) — mandatory application where a foreign jurisdiction treats the LP as opaque while Canada treats it as transparent, disallowing double deduction/no-inclusion outcomes; no elective opt-out; (6) ETA — GST/HST grouping and nil-consideration elections for closely related registrants; (7) competent-authority mutual agreement under the applicable income tax treaty (e.g., Article XXVI of the Canada-US Convention) to resolve entity-classification conflicts for non-resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1); mandatory GST/HST registration applies once the LP's annual worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada, inclusive of zero-rated export supplies, exceed C$30,000 in any four-consecutive-quarter window; registration is effective from the first day of the calendar month in which the threshold is exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ETA s. 25(1) and Schedule VI, Part V: (a) Goods exported by the supplier — the LP, as exporter, must deliver the battery-grade lithium carbonate to a common carrier, mail or ship it, or cause it to be exported within 60 days after the consideration becomes due (Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 9-23); export must be carried out 'in a manner satisfactory to the Minister' per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4-2.1. (b) Services supplied to non-residents — zero-rated where the service is not in respect of, or incident to, real property in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply (Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7). For cross-border procurement arbitrage, the supply qualifies for 0% only where title to the lithium carbonate powder passes to the non-resident buyer outside Canada, physical export is completed, and documentary evidence is retained. If the LP merely brokers a sale between foreign parties without goods being located in Canada at the time of supply, the supply is outside the scope of Canadian GST/HST entirely; any Canadian agency or logistics service component is taxed or zero-rated depending on recipient and place of supply. All zero-rated sales must be reported on the GST/HST return and corresponding ITCs on procurement inputs are fully recoverable.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) — mandatory for shipments exceeding C$2,000 or controlled/regulated goods
  • Bill of Lading (original ocean bill or air waybill) evidencing receipt of goods by carrier outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank evidence (Swift MT103/wire transfer record) confirming FX proceeds received from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Freight-forwarder or cargo-agent certificate confirming physical export and destination delivery

Canada does not impose a statutory real-time fiscal clearance regime, live API tax authority validation, or digital stamping of invoices. CRA mandates electronic GST/HST return filing (NETFILE/XML) where annual taxable supplies exceed C$1,500,000 and electronic remittance for amounts above C$10,000. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoice Standard (PEPPOL/UBL 2.1) is voluntary for B2B transactions; no e-invoicing penalty exists. Export declarations are mandatory electronically via CERS for commercial exports valued above C$2,000, with statistical reporting under the Canadian International Trade Database. CRA may require SAF-T-style extraction of general ledger, invoice, and export records upon audit; records must be retained for six years (ETA s. 286).

CRA treats export zero-rating claims as a high-risk audit area. The LP must retain all export, transport, and FX documentation for six years (ETA s. 286). Normal reassessment window is four years from the date of filing (ETA s. 298(1)); the limitation period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation, negligence, or willful default is established (ETA s. 298(2); ITA s. 152(4)(b)). Penalties include: ETA s. 280(1) false statements — greater of C$4,000 and 25% of net tax; ETA s. 282(1) willful evasion — 50% of net tax per occurrence plus criminal liability; ETA s. 329(3) false refund or rebate claims — greater of C$100 and 25% of the excess claimed; ITA s. 163(2) gross negligence — 50% of understated income tax; ITA s. 239 offences — fines up to C$25,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Retrospective clawback of ITCs claimed on lithium carbonate procurement will follow any failure of export substantiation; prescribed-rate interest compounds daily on reassessed net tax, and the ITA s. 163.1 large-corporation penalty applies where applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada maintains no general exchange-control regulations, no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital, and no prior authorization requirement for cross-border transfers. Non-resident limited partners may contribute capital and receive distributions freely, subject to Canadian tax compliance, FATCA/CRS reporting, and the withholding tax rules for distributions of Canadian-source income under Income Tax Act (Canada) Part XIII. Payments to non-residents for goods are likewise unrestricted; any source payments that may be deemed dividends, interest, rent, royalties, or management fees are subject to applicable withholding rates under the Canada-US tax treaty (or other relevant treaty) if the recipient qualifies. The only structural monitoring is imposed for terrorism-financing and money-laundering reporting thresholds; banks will document the purpose of every wire transfer exceeding CAD 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no designated timeline for export proceeds in Canada. Exporters may maintain export receipts in foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts, use hedging instruments freely, and convert to Canadian dollars at their own discretion. For Canadian Income Tax purposes, accounts receivable and realized exchange gains or losses must be reported in functional currency, but no central bank repatriation or conversion deadline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); with market conduct oversight for deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and anti-money-laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability with regulatory constraints. Canada has no dedicated 'EMI' charter; an offshore electronic money institution is not permitted to operate as a bank or hold Canadian deposits without a federal deposit-taking license and CDIC membership. An offshore EMI can support the treasury workflow only as an unlicensed payment facilitator or Money Service Business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC, operating through a correspondent Canadian bank account. Any such structure requires transparent KYC to avoid being treated as a 'shell' conduit; for a lithium-carbonate import-export arbitrage LP, reliance on an offshore EMI as the primary operating account is not advisable due to heightened correspondent-bank due-diligence and potential beneficiary-bank rejection of MT103 payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for limited partner capital contributions into Canada is a SWIFT MT103 wire to the Canadian bank-designated capital escrow or operational account, using the bank's correspondent ABA/CNAPS/SWIFT-BIC chain. The sender must include in Field 70 (Remittance Information) the purpose code 'CB' or the bank's specific language referencing 'Capital Contribution to Canadian Limited Partnership' and the LP registration number. Canada's purpose-code regime is largely risk-based rather than exchange-control-driven; banks will map the transfer to the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) category 'Inward Investment - Equity/LLP/LP capital'. Supporting verification must be delivered for each inbound transfer: executed capital commitment schedule, partnership agreement excerpt naming the contributor, limited partner KYC pack, signed representation that funds are not derived from proceeds of crime and are not subject to Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) red flags, and a Canadian dealer/investment counsel opinion if the good-faith capital characterization is questioned. Transfers exceeding CAD 100,000 may also require confirmation of the transaction's economic substance and the sender's bank compliance certification to avoid rejection under FINTRAC's due-diligence expectations.Trade settlements for battery-grade lithium carbonate purchases must be executed through SWIFT MT103 for open-account settlements, or through SWIFT MT700 (Issuance of Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit), MT707 (Amendment), MT710 (Advice of Third Bank's LC), and MT720/MT721 for transferable LCs when the LP acts as a cross-border procurement intermediary. For dollar-heavy jurisdictions, an alternative is a sight or usance documentary LC payable via a Canadian bank's Trade Finance Desk with a draft drawn at the discretion of the seller. Where LC is not involved, commercial invoices and packing lists must align with payment so that the bank's trade-surveillance team can match goods description, Incoterms, and country of origin. For payments to suppliers in Canada or US-dollar clearing, use SWIFT MT103 directly. For non-US dollar corridors with limited liquidity, the preferred rail is an MT103 with a Canadian-bank correspondent providing American Depository Receipt-style custody settlement; for high-value transactions, dual-currency MT103 with direct correspondent debit is acceptable. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium viability with regulatory constraints. Canada has no dedicated 'EMI' charter; an offshore electronic money institution is not permitted to operate as a bank or hold Canadian deposits without a federal deposit-taking license and CDIC membership. An offshore EMI can support the treasury workflow only as an unlicensed payment facilitator or Money Service Business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC, operating through a correspondent Canadian bank account. Any such structure requires transparent KYC to avoid being treated as a 'shell' conduit; for a lithium-carbonate import-export arbitrage LP, reliance on an offshore EMI as the primary operating account is not advisable due to heightened correspondent-bank due-diligence and potential beneficiary-bank rejection of MT103 payments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 200% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution with fines up to $100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings
Shell Company / Sham Partnership

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of tax benefits, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; possible criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; personal assets may be seized
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to $2 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to $500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined and imprisoned; travel bans and prohibition from serving as directors may be imposed
