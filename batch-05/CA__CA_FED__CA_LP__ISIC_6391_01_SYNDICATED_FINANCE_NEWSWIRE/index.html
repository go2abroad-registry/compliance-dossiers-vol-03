Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery — Companies and Provincial Entities; Corporations Canada (ISED) and Canada Revenue Agency are relevant only for federal tax/Business Number and non-registry compliance windows.

Governing statute

Canada has no federal limited partnership registry; limited partnerships are constituted under provincial/territorial Limited Partnerships Acts. For a federal-jurisdiction composite, the LP is registered under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.8, s. 2 (formation) and s. 4 (declaration), and is treated as a fiscally transparent partnership under Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), s. 96. The Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44, is not applicable to limited partnerships except for any associated corporate general partner.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form 1 under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act); Extra-Provincial Limited Partnership Registration Form if carrying on business in other provinces or territories.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 55 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership under the Ontario Limited Partnerships Act. Capital contributions are fixed by the Limited Partnership Agreement and must be deposited into the general partner's segregated escrow/trust account; release to the partnership is permitted only after issuance of the Certificate of Limited Partnership and confirmation of the registered office.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office and data operations facility must obtain a standard commercial fire safety certificate from the local fire marshal, including smoke alarm and emergency exit inspection. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; portal: CRA My Business Account (canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business.html)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or registering online through the My Business Account portal. The application must identify the partnership's domestic and foreign partners, the Canadian agent or tax representative if one is appointed, and the start date of Canadian GST/HST obligations. A separate GST/HST account is activated under the BN when taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold or when voluntary registration is elected. For income tax purposes, the BN is also used for the T5013 partnership information return filing requirements.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA by completing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or registering online through the My Business Account portal. The application must identify the partnership's domestic and foreign partners, the Canadian agent or tax representative if one is appointed, and the start date of Canadian GST/HST obligations. A separate GST/HST account is activated under the BN when taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold or when voluntary registration is elected. For income tax purposes, the BN is also used for the T5013 partnership information return filing requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists in Canada. A limited partnership is income-tax transparent; each partner reports its allocable share. For GST/HST, the partnership may be a separate registrant, and partners may elect under the Excise Tax Act to have the partnership be the registrant with a single GST/HST account. Certain fiscal-period and allocation elections are available under sections 96 and 249.1 of the Income Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.2 to 18.8 operate independently and do not require taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of a financial newswire/API subscription service is zero-rated under the Excise Tax Act if made to a non-resident person and the service is not performed on land or goods situated in Canada, is not a personal service rendered in Canada, and is not otherwise excluded by Schedule VI, Part V. The supplier must maintain contemporaneous evidence that the recipient is a non-resident and that the service is for use outside Canada.

  • Export Services Agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and jurisdiction of consumption
  • GST/HST zero-rated commercial invoice showing 0% rate
  • Evidence of non-resident status (foreign registration certificate, foreign tax ID, or comparable proof)
  • Inward remittance certificate and bank wire transfer records evidencing payment from the foreign counterparty
  • If tangible goods are supplied incidentally: B13A Customs Export Declaration and Bill of Lading or rail/air waybill

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance regime is in force at the federal level. CRA accepts electronic invoices and API-based submission is available but voluntary. Registered persons with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically. There is no digital stamping or government pre-clearance of invoices.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess at 5% GST/HST where the exporter fails to retain sufficient documentary evidence. Penalties and interest apply under sections 280 to 285 of the Excise Tax Act, including gross negligence penalties up to 250% of the tax evaded and clawback of any input tax credits or refunds paid. Canada does not impose a statutory foreign-currency repatriation deadline for export proceeds; CRA instead assesses the commercial substance of the transaction and requires remittance terms that are consistent with normal arm's-length trade practice.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime, with unrestricted current-account and capital-account transactions. No central-bank approval, exchange-control permit, or prior authorization is required for profit remittances, capital distributions, loan repayments, or dissolution proceeds payable by a Canadian Limited Partnership (CA_LP) to non-resident limited partners. The practical remittance hurdles are exclusively administrative and fiscal: (i) Part XIII withholding tax on actual distributions of Canadian-source income to non-resident partners, reduced under applicable income tax treaties; (ii) FINTRAC cross-border EFT and large-cash transaction reporting at CAD 10,000; (iii) CRS/FATCA reporting obligations triggered by non-resident account holders; and (iv) Investment Canada Act notification/review thresholds, with a Canadian newswire service statutorily classified as a cultural business, subject to lower asset-based review thresholds for non-Canadian acquirers. No foreign-exchange restriction, surrender, or licensing is imposed on inbound capital contributions or outbound dividends/distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no statutory repatriation timelines. Export receipts from financial newswire aggregation, syndication feeds, and digital news-licensing services may be retained indefinitely in any foreign currency, held in foreign or domestic bank accounts, repatriated freely, or converted at the prevailing market rate. The only binding obligations are (i) FINTRAC reporting for every international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more, (ii) Income Tax Act section 39 treatment of foreign-exchange gains or losses on a look-through basis to the partners of the pass-through LP, and (iii) zero-rated GST/HST treatment for exported digital news services under Schedule VI, Part V, section 23, with no foreign-exchange reporting requirement to the CRA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) administers monetary policy, operates the Large Value Transfer System, and supervises retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act; foreign-exchange convertibility is governed by the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act; prudential supervision of domestic commercial banks is vested in OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions); cross-border AML/CFT transactional reporting is administered by FINTRAC; Canada maintains no dedicated central foreign-exchange control board and imposes no exchange-control licensing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH, subject to structural constraints. Offshore digital EMIs and payment platforms (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, Stripe) may be used as ancillary treasury sub-rails because the Retail Payment Activities Act regulates payment-service providers rather than merchants; the CA_LP, acting solely as a merchant receiving subscription and syndication revenue, is not required to register with the Bank of Canada as a PSP. However: (i) the primary capital escrow and operational clearing accounts must remain with a Schedule I domestic bank to preserve CDIC deposit insurance and core banking relationships; (ii) any arrangement in which the EMI holds Canadian client funds or executes payment functions for third parties on behalf of the LP would trigger RPAA registration; (iii) Canadian banks treat offshore EMI balances as elevated-risk and may require full disclosure of all foreign accounts during onboarding; (iv) FATCA/CRS reporting applies because an offshore EMI is a foreign financial institution; (v) EMI balances are not CDIC-insured. The permissible architecture is to use an EMI (including Stripe Treasury) solely for automated content-royalty payouts to foreign news agencies and partner journalists, mirror-funded from the domestic operational account, with daily auto-sweep reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the limited partner's own bank account with the beneficiary name matching the registered LP exactly, the LP operational or escrow account number quoted, and a clear payment narrative reading 'Capital Contribution - Subscription Agreement [date] - LP Unit Class [ ]'. The remitter should use SWIFT purpose code CAPI (Capital Injection) or EQUI (Equity-related) and provide a pre-advice to the receiving Canadian bank for injections exceeding CAD 1,000,000. Funds are credited to the Capital Escrow Account and released to the Operational Clearing Account only upon satisfaction of closing conditions under the LP agreement. The Canadian receiving bank will file a FINTRAC report for any international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more; for USD-denominated injections the bank must be instructed to credit the USD sub-ledger, and conversion to CAD is not mandatory. Prior to closing, the non-resident partners must confirm whether the investment triggers an Investment Canada Act filing: a new Canadian business notification is required, and a review may be required because a financial newswire operation is statutorily classified as a cultural business with lower asset-based thresholds. Commercial letters of credit (UCP600) are legally permissible but commercially unnecessary for low-value SaaS-type financial data subscriptions; an LC should be issued only when a counterparty contractually requires a bank-backed guarantee. All outbound payments must pass OFAC, SEMA, and UN Act sanctions screening, and each international disbursement equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 is subject to FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH, subject to structural constraints. Offshore digital EMIs and payment platforms (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, Stripe) may be used as ancillary treasury sub-rails because the Retail Payment Activities Act regulates payment-service providers rather than merchants; the CA_LP, acting solely as a merchant receiving subscription and syndication revenue, is not required to register with the Bank of Canada as a PSP. However: (i) the primary capital escrow and operational clearing accounts must remain with a Schedule I domestic bank to preserve CDIC deposit insurance and core banking relationships; (ii) any arrangement in which the EMI holds Canadian client funds or executes payment functions for third parties on behalf of the LP would trigger RPAA registration; (iii) Canadian banks treat offshore EMI balances as elevated-risk and may require full disclosure of all foreign accounts during onboarding; (iv) FATCA/CRS reporting applies because an offshore EMI is a foreign financial institution; (v) EMI balances are not CDIC-insured. The permissible architecture is to use an EMI (including Stripe Treasury) solely for automated content-royalty payouts to foreign news agencies and partner journalists, mirror-funded from the domestic operational account, with daily auto-sweep reconciliation.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% entity-level for the Limited Partnership (fiscally transparent); 15% federal general corporate rate (post-13% General Rate Reduction) applied only at the level of corporate partners on their allocable share

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on amounts paid or credited to non-resident partners that are deemed Canadian-source dividends or guaranteed partnership income; a pure return of capital and a properly reported allocable share of Canadian business income are instead taxed under Part I at treaty-protected rates

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$5,000,000 aggregate fair market value of cross-border non-arm's-length transactions in the year triggers Form TPD-II (Local File); Form TPD-I (Master File) applies when the multinational group's consolidated revenue reaches C$1,000,000,000; documentation must exist before the applicable filing deadline to preserve the s. 247(3) penalty-relief safe harbour

Economic substance scope: Canada imposes no standalone UAE-style ESR statute; substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), the common-law sham doctrine, anti-hybrid rules (ss. 212.3, 18.2, 18.3), and OECD BEPS substance requirements for treaty benefits. The LP must demonstrate real commercial activity — a staffed Canadian newsroom, editorial decision-making, physical servers or colocation agreements, API infrastructure, and arm's-length contracts in the partners' own names — otherwise the CRA may reallocate income under s. 103, disallow losses, or recharacterize the structure as a conduit subject to s. 212.3 deemed-dividend withholding

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — s. 239(1) Income Tax Act

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years less a day; indictment: fine of 100% to 200% and/or imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted withholdings; criminal conviction triggers disqualification from board service (CBCA s. 105), civil restitution, asset-seizure certificates under s. 223, and examination under oath
Gross Negligence / False Statements — s. 163(2)

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax on each false statement or omission, plus prescribed-rate interest; no cap on the cumulative assessment

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised or knowingly acquiesced in the misstatement face director-liability assessments, CRA third-party demand letters under s. 231.2, and professional-indemnity exposure; egregious cases escalate to s. 239 criminal referral
GAAR / Conduit Shell Company — s. 245

Statutory sanction: Full recharacterization of the abusive transaction under s. 245(2), a 25% penalty under s. 245(4), plus interest; loss of treaty relief; reclassification of the LP as a foreign-controlled taxable entity, exposing deemed-dividend withholding under s. 212.3

Director personal liability: CRA may pierce the limited-liability shield and assess the general partner's board; limited partners participating in management of the newsroom trigger the statutory 'control rule', voiding limited liability and exposing them personally to the LP's debts and tax obligations under provincial Limited Partnership Acts
AML / Unregistered Money Services Business — PCMLTFA

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty up to C$1,000,000 per violation for the entity and C$500,000 for individuals; conviction for money laundering under Criminal Code s. 462.31 carries imprisonment up to 10 years

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner face personal fines, disgorgement, and a five-year prohibition on serving as directors; CBSA travel-restriction orders, asset-freezing orders, and cross-border seizure powers apply under the Criminal Code and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act
Part XIII Withholding / Remittance Failure — ss. 212, 227

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the unremitted amount plus prescribed-rate interest under ss. 227(8)–(9); the CRA assesses the LP for the full amount that should have been withheld; repeated failures can trigger a ministerial compliance order

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the entire unremitted amount under s. 227.1; a due-diligence defence requires proof of reasonable care and a compliance system; the Minister may impose international travel bans under s. 226 to compel collection and examinations
Transfer-Pricing Mispricing — s. 247

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to the arm's-length value under s. 247(2); a 10% penalty on the net transfer-pricing adjustment under s. 247(3) whenever the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue; documentation deficiencies forfeit penalty relief

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany newswire-licence agreements without supporting comparables may be assessed for negligence; s. 227.1 director liability is engaged if the TP adjustment renders the LP insolvent and unpaid taxes result; criminal referral is possible for fabricated contracts
