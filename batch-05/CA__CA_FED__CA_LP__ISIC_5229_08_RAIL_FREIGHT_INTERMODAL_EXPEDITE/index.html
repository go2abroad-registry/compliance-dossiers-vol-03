Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The applicable provincial or territorial corporate and partnership registry; Corporations Canada does not register domestic limited partnerships.

Governing statute

Canada has no federal limited partnership statute or federal limited partnership registry. A Canadian limited partnership must be constituted under the Partnership Act or Limited Partnerships Act of the selected province or territory, together with the applicable provincial registration provisions. The province or territory was not specified in the payload.

Formation / constitutional filings

Provincial or territorial Declaration, Certificate, or Registration of Limited Partnership, using the terminology prescribed by the selected jurisdiction.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No Canadian statutory minimum capital requirement generally applies to a limited partnership. Capital contributions, capital accounts, profit allocations, withdrawal rights, and any escrow or subscription mechanics must be established in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account is generally required, although regulated financing, contractual, or banking arrangements may impose separate requirements.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal building, zoning, occupancy, fire-prevention, and emergency-planning approvals for any warehouse, yard, or terminal. Complete structural, racking, loading, electrical, fire-alarm, sprinkler, and emergency-egress inspections under applicable provincial or territorial building and fire codes. Comply with the occupational health and safety legislation of the worksite jurisdiction, including worker training, hazard assessments, personal protective equipment, incident reporting, and workplace clearance requirements. Dangerous goods handling must comply with the federal Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act and applicable regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: No Canadian statutory minimum capital requirement generally applies to a limited partnership. Capital contributions, capital accounts, profit allocations, withdrawal rights, and any escrow or subscription mechanics must be established in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account is generally required, although regulated financing, contractual, or banking arrangements may impose separate requirements.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The general partner, acting for and on behalf of the proposed limited partnership, resolves that: (1) the limited partnership agreement is approved and the partnership interests described therein are accepted; (2) the registered office and records office shall be maintained at the address approved by the general partner; (3) the identified general and limited partners are admitted upon execution and contribution of their agreed commitments; (4) authorized officers of the general partner may sign and file all registration documents, tax elections, banking mandates, and commercial contracts; and (5) the partnership’s books, statutory records, and compliance calendar shall be maintained in accordance with applicable provincial law. A limited partnership has no separate statutory board unless contractually created.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No single federal industrial licensing board applies to ordinary freight forwarding. Transport Canada and the applicable provincial, territorial, municipal, port, terminal, customs, and railway authorities regulate particular activities and facilities.

Category: No general federal manufacturing or freight-forwarding operating licence is required for an intermediary arranging rail freight. A railway operating certificate, railway safety authorization, customs authorization, bonded-warehouse approval, port permit, dangerous-goods approval, or municipal business and occupancy permit may be required if the LP performs those regulated activities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Fraser Building, 555 Mackenzie Avenue, Ottawa. Digital portal: 'My Business Account' via canada.ca, with GST/HST NETFILE and Represent a Client. The CRA administers the federal Income Tax Act, Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST), and issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Confirm whether the foreign limited partnership 'carries on business in Canada' within ITA s. 253 and ETA s. 123(1). Step 2: Complete Form RC1 (or RC1A for non-resident applicants) with the LP's legal name in its home jurisdiction, partnership agreement, full address of the foreign head office, Canadian agent, name and nationality of every general partner, and date of first Canadian taxable supply. Step 3: Submit to the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre by mail, fax, or via a registered tax agent or the 'Non-Resident Business' channel; CRA issues a standard 9-digit BN plus a 2-letter program account code, with 'RT' indicating the GST/HST account. Step 4: For non-resident LPs not otherwise required to file under Part I, update the account with a fiscal representative's contact details (a resident Canadian agent is recommended but not statutorily mandatory). Step 5: Add the 'RP' payroll account if the LP employs Canadian staff and 'RM' account if the LP imports/exports goods commercially. Issuance standard: 3-6 weeks by mail; faster if filed electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax: the LP itself is not a taxpayer; income, gains, and losses are computed at the partnership level under Part I and allocated to partners under the partnership agreement; each non-resident partner is taxable on Canadian-source business income attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment (Part I) or subject to Part XIII withholding on certain passive allocations. For GST/HST the LP is treated as a separate 'person' and must register if it makes taxable supplies in Canada exceeding the small supplier threshold.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available elections include: (i) ITA s. 96 partnership fiscal-period election (aligning the LP's fiscal period with a fiscal year end), (ii) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident partners to file Part I returns instead of Part XIII on rental income, (iii) ITA s. 216.1 election for non-resident service providers, (iv) ITA s. 217 election for non-resident employment income, and (v) ETA elections: Quick Method (same line 227), GST/HST annual filing election (s. 238), and election under ETA s. 237 for a prescribed fiscal year. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 163.41-163.48, effective 1 July 2022) may deny income tax deductions to an LP where a partner is resident in a jurisdiction treating the LP as opaque; no elective carve-out exists, but a taxpayer may rely on a bilateral treaty tie-breaker or apply for confirmation via CRA advance income tax ruling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including taxable supplies made in Canada) computed on the basis of quarterly calendar-year cumulative amounts under ETA s. 148; for a non-resident LP the threshold is reached when total taxable supplies made in the course of carrying on business in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or in the prior four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration becomes mandatory on the date of the transaction that crosses the threshold; voluntary registration is available below it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part I (s. 1) of the ETA, goods are zero-rated when the supplier delivers them by way of sale to a common carrier for direct export outside Canada and the goods are continuously exported as soon as reasonably possible after delivery. Under Schedule VI, Part V (ss. 7, 8), a freight transportation service, including rail carriage, is zero-rated where the transportation is to or from a point outside Canada, or where the service is supplied to a nonresident person who does not carry on business in Canada and the transportation is to/from a point outside Canada. An intermodal rail freight forwarding LP is therefore eligible to claim 0% GST on cross-border export shipments even where the ultimate destination is a US or offshore port. Claiming zero-rating does not depend on CRA pre-approval, but the exporter must retain a 'qualifying shipping document' as defined in ETA s. 1(1) of Schedule VI and applicable regulations; the carrier's through bill of lading, rail waybill, or CERS declaration suffices. A registered LP that makes only zero-rated supplies is generally entitled to full ITC recovery, eliminating federal GST on its operating costs.

  • CERS (Canadian Export Reporting System) / Form B13A export declaration through the CARM portal, filed electronically via the CBSA-Accredited Customs Broker
  • Rail bill of lading or through rail-ocean bill of lading naming the consignee outside Canada and evidencing the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice issued by the LP with the sales terms (e.g., CIP/CPT INCOTERMS) and an explicit '0% GST – Export Supply under Schedule VI, ETA' indication
  • Intermodal container interchange receipt and container seal number tying the shipment to the Canadian railhead
  • Customs broker written confirmation of export disposition, AES/CAERS confirmation number if applicable, and CBSA exit transaction printout
  • Packing list, weight manifest, and container yard gate pass at the export border crossing
  • Inward remittance certificate issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank or authorized foreign correspondent evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency export proceeds into the LP's Canadian account
  • Partner allocation schedule recording the export profit allocation among the LP partners (supporting partnership pass-through compliance)

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time e-invoicing fiscal clearance regime, API-based CRA clearance, or digital stamping. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE) and corporate income tax returns (CORPORATE INTERNET FILING). The CRA, together with provincial bodies, has launched a voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework aligned with the Peppol/UBL 2.1 XML standard, with phased adoption expected from 2024 onward; rail freight B2B invoices remain paper or PDF unless a counterparty subscribes to the voluntary network. Records must be retained in machine-readable form where the CRA makes an electronic request.

The CBSA and CRA jointly audit export transactions through the CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) platform. Failure to produce the required export documentation within the statutory 6-year record retention period results in disallowance of the zero-rating, immediate assessment of 5% GST on the full invoice value, interest under ETA s. 280.1, and arrears interest from the original filing due date. Gross-negligence false statements trigger a penalty equal to 25% of the net tax sought under ETA s. 285.1(1). Failure to register when required leads to a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of the uncollected tax (s. 280). Willful evasion, including knowingly false documentation of exports, is prosecutable under ETA s. 327 with fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years. The CRA's normal reassessment window is 4 years; it is extended to 7 years for misrepresentation, neglect, or carelessness, and indefinitely where fraud or misrepresentation attributable to the taxpayer is established. Retrospective clawbacks are not limited to a single fiscal year and cross-year trailing assessments are common in contemporaneous intermodal freight audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a freely floating exchange rate and full convertibility of the Canadian dollar for both capital and current account transactions. There is no exchange control legislation restricting the conversion, transfer, or remittance of funds. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to non-resident partners of a Canadian limited partnership are generally unrestricted, subject solely to withholding tax obligations and treaty-based rate reductions. No administrative authorization from the Bank of Canada or the Minister of Finance is required for ordinary profit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Canada requiring exporters to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or to exchange them into Canadian dollars within any timeline. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency bank accounts or converted through authorized financial institutions at commercial rates. However, all cross-border wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and records of export payments should be retained for compliance verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) overseeing prudential banking conduct and FINTRAC administering anti-money laundering and cross-border wire reporting obligations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, with important legal constraints. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) cannot act as primary deposit-taking banks in Canada without a bank or money-services-business license. A Canadian-limited-partnership treasury may use a foreign EMI for non-resident payment facilitation only if the EMI is registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC where Canadian activity occurs. No EMI can provide equivalent of a domestic lien position or settlement finality, so the LP should maintain a tier-1 Canadian-dollar operating account for all regulatory and tax settlement flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed through SWIFT MT103 or equivalent international wire transfer protocols with explicit purpose codes aligned to the ISIC 5229 freight forwarding classification. For a Canadian limited partnership receiving capital from a non-resident partner, use payment purpose code 'N706' (Intercompany Loans) or 'N600' (Capital Injection into a Non-Financial Canadian Entity) as accepted by Canadian clearing institutions. The remitter must include the entity legal name, CA-FED jurisdiction identifier, and partnership registration number in the reference field. Verification requires the receiving bank to match the remitter to the registered partner list; funds will be accepted into a Canadian dollar or multi-currency operating account without exchange control approval, but FINTRAC reporting and bank-specific source-of-funds questionnaires apply.Outbound trade payments to rail carriers, drayage providers, and intermodal terminals must settle via SWIFT MT103 across the global clearing network or by commercial Letter of Credit (documentary collection or irrevocable LC) where counterparty risk mitigation is required. For urgent freight disbursements, use high-value clearing system wire transfers through the Canadian LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) for domestic leg trimming and SWIFT MT103 for cross-border legs. LC issuance should be handled by the tier-1 Canadian bank through a confirmed, irrevocable LC advising the counterparty bank; standard UCP600 indemnity and presentation terms apply.
