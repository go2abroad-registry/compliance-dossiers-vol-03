Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Canadian LP must maintain the registered office or registered address required by the governing provincial or territorial statute and must be able to receive legal process and official notices. A commercial virtual office may be acceptable for registration if it is a genuine service address, the provider is authorized to receive service, and the relevant registry permits it. A mailbox, accommodation address, or purely nominal address is not sufficient. For online sales of maternity and infant-care commodities, a virtual registered office may be acceptable for formation, but payment processors, banks, tax authorities, consumer regulators, customs authorities, and product-safety authorities may require a real operating address, fulfillment location, records location, and evidence of management and inventory activity. Retail sales of ordinary goods generally do not require a sector-specific federal licence, but product safety, labeling, import, privacy, tax, consumer-protection, and provincial business-registration rules remain applicable. Products making health, therapeutic, or medical claims may trigger additional Health Canada regulation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes and no check-the-box election exists analogous to US Subchapter K; active business income is allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level. For GST/HST purposes the partnership is a separate 'person' and may elect under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act to have a partner, member, or appointed representative act as the partnership representative. No hybrid mismatch or entity classification election is available for income tax purposes.

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada (small supplier threshold); non-resident digital platforms and online vendors selling to Canadian consumers are also governed by the simplified GST/HST registration regime effective July 1, 2021 and expanded July 1, 2024, triggering registration at CAD 30,000 of Canadian-source digital sales

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada when the exporter is registered and obtains acceptable documentary proof of export under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Services supplied to a non-resident may also be zero-rated under section 5(a) of Part V when performed partly outside Canada and for the direct benefit of the non-resident, or when the service is not in relation to real property situate in Canada or tangible personal property located in Canada; qualifying digital and support services supplied to non-resident customers are eligible if they meet the statutory offshore-service conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill (showing shipper/consignee identity and Canadian point of origin)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/financial institution confirmation showing settlement of export proceeds in the exporter's name

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or government digital stamping applies; CRA does not require pre-approved e-invoicing APIs. Registered businesses must issue GST/HST invoices containing the business number, date of supply, description, GST/HST paid or payable, and applicable rate. Electronic invoices and records must be retained in an accessible, readable format and synchronized with GST/HST returns filed through CRA's electronic filing services if the filing threshold requires it.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export if the exporter cannot produce satisfactory export evidence within the statutory assessment period; repatriation of proceeds is expected within the contractual payment terms, which CRA tests for artificial financing arrangements. Failure to substantiate zero-rating results in reassessment at 5% GST or the applicable HST rate, plus statutory interest and late-filing penalties. Knowingly making a false statement to claim nil or zero GST/HST attracts a gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax avoided; fraudulent export schemes may be prosecuted criminally with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to two years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully liberalised, free-floating currency regime for current and capital account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible without administrative approval, and non-resident investors and limited partners may freely convert CAD to any foreign currency other than sanctioned jurisdictions. Profit remittances, including distributions from a Canadian limited partnership to foreign partners, are unrestricted under exchange control legislation; however, such remittances may be subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, typically 25% unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty, and must be reported in the partnership's information return (T2046/T5013) and, where applicable, under Form T106. There are no exchange rate pegging mechanisms, no capital repatriation quotas, and no quantitative currency convertibility hurdles, but all wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are subject to FINTRAC reporting and banks may refuse transactions inconsistent with the client's known business profile or without adequate purpose code information.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion timeline, or prescribed exchange repatriation ratio on resident or non-resident exporters. E-commerce proceeds from maternity and baby supply sales earned through Stripe/PayPal may be retained in foreign currency, provided they are accurately booked under the partnership's Canadian financial records and properly reported to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) in accordance with the Income Tax Act and the excise tax on imported goods. Nonetheless, for anti-money-laundering compliance, any substantial or recurring foreign currency credit to a Canadian bank account must be supported by underlying e-commerce transaction records, including platform settlement reports, sales invoices, refund chargebacks, and shipping documentation, and must be traceable through the Schedule I bank's currency reporting infrastructure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada, in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control agency; the monetary framework and currency issuance reside with the Bank of Canada, while foreign currency transactions and international payments are governed by the Currency Act, the Payment Card Networks Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for payment-service integration, Low for primary treasury and capital-escrow purposes. Canada does not grant federal 'electronic money institution' licences in the EU/UK sense. Foreign EMIs (e.g. UK FCA-authorised or European EEA-registered) may lawfully provide payment gateway services to Canadian merchants through their existing licences, but such entities are not permitted to accept deposits or hold CAD settlement balances unless they obtain a Canadian Money Services Business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC and, depending on province, a money-lender or money-transfer licence. Practical legal constraints require a minimum deposit-taking relationship with a Schedule I bank for CAD clearing and settlement; Stripe and PayPal as target payment rails are permissible third-party PSPs but do not obviate the need for a Canadian OSFI-regulated account. For this baby/maternity e-commerce LP, Stripe/PayPal should feed a Canadian operating account, and the fintech EMI cannot act as a standalone treasury or FX hedging counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital injections into the Canadian LP must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 cover where an intermediate bank is used) to the receiving Schedule I bank account. The wire mandate requires the exact beneficiary legal name 'CA LP [Entity Name]' and the USD/CAD account number; the introductory reference line should contain the attribute 'CA_LP_CAPITAL_ESCROW' together with the LP registration number and the document reference of the partnership resolution. A standard purpose code, such as 'P251' (capital contribution) or ISO 20022 equivalent 'ACCT' / 'CRED', should be included to satisfy the Canadian bank's regulatory classification. Canadian banks are obliged to validate the originator's full name, address and account number against the payer's bank; where a foreign financial institution (APX) is an intermediate, all SWIFT 'Ordering Customer' and 'Beneficiary Customer' fields must be fully populated under the Canadian Payment Transparency Rule. On receipt, the bank will expect a copy of the capital introduction letter, the executed LP agreement, and evidence of the wire purpose. Funds are then credited to the Capital Escrow sub-account, and may be swept to the operating account only after the KYC clearance and the CRA partnership profile are verified.The preferred clearing rail for outbound trade settlements is SWIFT MT103, sending direct USD or CAD beneficiary instructions to the exporter's bank. For high-value or recurring MOL business, a commercial letter of credit (L/C), subject to the ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600) and ISBP 821, is the recommended rail, with the Canadian issuing bank handling the LC fully. Trade disbursements may also be executed in the following modes: (i) direct telegraphic transfer against commercial invoice in conjunction with a Canadian bank's TreasuryPlus platform; (ii) CAD wire to local Canadian suppliers via the Real-Time Rail or the large-value transfer system (LVTS) as applicable; (iii) USD clearing through the bank's US-dollar clearing account for USD settlement with US suppliers; and (iv) for transactions involving Chinese RMB, settlement through the offshore CIPS-capable correspondent network or an SWIFT CNY channel provided the payment is consistent with sanctions and trade-controls due diligence. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for payment-service integration, Low for primary treasury and capital-escrow purposes. Canada does not grant federal 'electronic money institution' licences in the EU/UK sense. Foreign EMIs (e.g. UK FCA-authorised or European EEA-registered) may lawfully provide payment gateway services to Canadian merchants through their existing licences, but such entities are not permitted to accept deposits or hold CAD settlement balances unless they obtain a Canadian Money Services Business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC and, depending on province, a money-lender or money-transfer licence. Practical legal constraints require a minimum deposit-taking relationship with a Schedule I bank for CAD clearing and settlement; Stripe and PayPal as target payment rails are permissible third-party PSPs but do not obviate the need for a Canadian OSFI-regulated account. For this baby/maternity e-commerce LP, Stripe/PayPal should feed a Canadian operating account, and the fintech EMI cannot act as a standalone treasury or FX hedging counterparty.
