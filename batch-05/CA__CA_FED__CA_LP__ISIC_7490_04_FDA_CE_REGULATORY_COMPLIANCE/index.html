Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Federal administration of the Income Tax Act and Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST). Registration and filing portal: 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO); GST/HST returns filed via GST/HST NETFILE or EDIfile.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership that carries on business in Canada and is not a 'small supplier' must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account before making taxable supplies. Procedure: (1) appoint a Canadian agent or provide a Canadian address for service; (2) register through the Business Registration Online (BRO) service or file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'; (3) submit the partnership agreement, partner particulars (SIN, ITN, or entity BN), fiscal period, Canadian business address and annual revenue estimate; (4) CRA issues the BN with program accounts, notably the 'RT' GST/HST account and the 'RP' payroll account where applicable; (5) the LP remains fiscally transparent for income tax: it files a T5013 partnership information return and each partner reports its allocable Canadian-source income; non-resident partners are taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their Canadian-source share, subject to any applicable Canada tax treaty.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96; income, losses and taxable capital gains are computed at partnership level and allocated to partners; the LP itself pays no federal income tax but is a 'person' for GST/HST purposes and may be a registered supplier or registrant.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election for limited partnerships. The available federal elections and structural mechanisms include: (i) ITA s. 97(2) rollover election on a partner's contribution of property to the LP; (ii) ITA s. 98(3)/(5) elections on partnership cessation and distribution of partnership property; (iii) designation of a 'designated member' responsible for preparing and filing the T5013 partnership information return; (iv) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) for eligible registrants with annual taxable supplies not exceeding C$400,000; and (v) default application of hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2/18.3 to foreign disregarded entities used by the LP, which cannot be overridden by election and require contemporaneous documentation of the Canadian-law classification of each entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating, fully convertible currency under IMF Article VIII obligations with no separate treatment of current-account versus capital-account transactions. Both accounts are open, and no licensing, registration, or prior approval is required to convert, hold, or remit CAD. Profit distributions from the Limited Partnership are legally unrestricted and may be paid in CAD, USD, or EUR; the only frictions are Part XIII tax withholding (including Income Tax Regulations section 105 at 15% for service fees paid to non-residents) and FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting at C$10,000. A non-resident partner treaty claim is documented on CRA forms rather than via FX approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist in Canadian federal law. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, no surrender certificate, and no export-proceeds retention ceiling. FDA 510(k) and CE Mark consulting receipts may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency, and the LP may elect to compute Canadian taxable income in U.S. dollars under Income Tax Act section 261, subject only to normal anti-money-laundering record keeping.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and sole issuer of Canadian banknotes; however, no exchange-control agency exists. Prudential regulation of banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while cross-border currency-movement oversight is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium - legally available but operationally constrained. Non-resident digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar) cannot operate as deposit-taking banks in Canada; they must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and are not CDIC-insured. They are acceptable for low-balance expense disbursements and auxiliary receivables collection, but are not viable for capital escrow, retainer trust, or FX collateral margins, which require a Schedule I bank deposit base and Canadian standing. Recommendation: use EMIs only as a secondary rail, never as the liquidity anchor.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at entity level (limited partnership is pass-through; income taxed in partners' hands; federal general corporate rate of 15% applies to corporate partners on their allocable share

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (Part XIII) on distributions of Canadian-source partnership income to non-resident partners; reduced to treaty rate for applicable partners (typically 0% for active business income with no Canadian PE under treaty; otherwise 5-15% for non-active investment income)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due within 5 months after the partnership's fiscal year-end (e.g., May 31 for a December 31 year-end). T5013 slips to partners due by the last day of February (or last day of the month following fiscal year-end for non-calendar periods).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Elect to file before or with the T5013; form must be filed within 3 years after the year to which it applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA generally expects contemporaneous transfer pricing records for any non-arm's length cross-border transaction where the aggregate value exceeds CAD 1 million (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services). A full Master File and Local File are expected for transactions exceeding CAD 1 million with related parties unless the taxpayer qualifies as a smaller entity.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone statutory ESR regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act recharacterizes transactions lacking bona fide economic substance. The LP must maintain real operational substance—a substantive general partner, active management, and a Canadian office—else the CRA may deny treaty benefits and income allocations. For limited partners, limited liability protection is contingent on the partner not taking part in control of the partnership.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of evaded tax under subsection 163(2); daily interest on unpaid amounts; criminal prosecution with fines of up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years (Income Tax Act section 239).

Director personal liability: General partner and any limited partner participating in management can face joint and several liability for unpaid partnership taxes, penalties, and interest; egregious cases trigger travel bans, restitution orders, and criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Partnerships

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize the entire partnership structure as a sham or conduit, disallow loss deductions, deny treaty protection, and assess a penalty of 25% of the tax benefit under GAAR; civil monetary penalties up to CAD 25,000 per year.

Director personal liability: Officers of the general partner are personally liable for overdue withholding taxes (subsection 160(2)), civil restitution for mistaken allocations, and potential criminal fines or imprisonment up to 2 years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the transaction under section 247 and impose a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (abusive avoidance). Interest accrues at 5% annually on the reassessed tax; double taxation risk if no MAP (mutual agreement procedure) is pursued.

Director personal liability: Directors of the LP (if it is a legal person) can be held personally liable for gross negligence penalties under subsection 163(2) if they authorized inaccurate documentation; personal net worth is subject to attachment.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: civil penalties up to CAD 250,000 for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal charges for wilfully not registering or reporting carry imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly fail to establish and maintain an AML program are personally subject to criminal penalties including heavy fines and imprisonment (up to 5 years per count).
