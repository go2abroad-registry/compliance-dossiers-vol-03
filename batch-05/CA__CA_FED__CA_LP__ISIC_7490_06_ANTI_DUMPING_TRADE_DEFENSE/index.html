Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC); primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), GST/HST NETFILE, and My Business Account (MyBA); customs-side authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership must obtain a nine-digit federal Business Number (BN) before commencing commercial activity. Registration is effected through Business Registration Online, by filing paper Form RC1, or by telephone. The partnership must open a separate GST/HST program account (RT) once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 (or immediately for voluntary registration). Non-resident partners without a Canadian establishment must provide CRA with a Canadian agent or representative for service of notices. Where the partnership supplies legal/defense consulting services to non-resident clients, the same BN registration applies, and CRA Form RC459 (GST/HST non-resident registration) is the relevant instrument for offshore service providers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act (ITA) section 96; the partnership computes and allocates income, but each partner bears tax. Non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income allocated to them, subject to treaty relief, and may be liable to Part XIII withholding on passive amounts. Territorial scope applies only to non-residents; Canadian-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income. No federal tax-exempt zone applies to this trade-remedy defense services activity.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A Canadian partnership carrying on business in Canada may elect under ITA subsection 96(3) to be treated as a corporation for taxable purposes. For GST/HST purposes, partnerships may elect under ETA section 273 to have a supply deemed made by a partner rather than the partnership, and may use the partnership-level registration with a single RT account. Hybrid mismatch positions are constrained by ITA sections 18.4 to 18.6, which disallow deductions for imported mismatches; administrative elections can be made to align tax character of payments and neutralize double non-taxation in cross-border fee arrangements. All elections require CRA filing forms and annual disclosure in prescribed tax filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of total worldwide taxable supplies in any single calendar quarter or over any four consecutive calendar quarters; no threshold relief for non-resident suppliers of digital services or legal/consulting services to Canadian recipients that must register under the simplified non-resident GST/HST regime; registrants under the normal regime must also register for payroll and import/export accounts as applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods physically exported by the supplier is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA, provided the supplier retains proof of export. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient qualify for 0% GST/HST under section 7 of Schedule VII when (a) the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant in Canada, (b) the service is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, and (c) the service is acquired for consumption outside Canada. Anti-dumping trade-defense consulting and legal representation services rendered to foreign importers, exporters, or foreign government tribunals are zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident acting in a commercial or administrative capacity and the benefit is realized offshore. The registrant may claim full input tax credits (ITCs) for related Canadian expenses and may reflect excess ITCs quarterly or annually.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration) for physical goods
  • Commercial invoice and written consulting/legal services agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and the nature of the trade-remedy defense services
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or freight-forwarder certificate evidencing export (for goods-based zero-rating)
  • Inward Remittance evidence: SWIFT/wire-transfer receipts or banking statements proving settlement of fees from abroad
  • Non-resident status supporting documents (foreign registration certificate, passport or tax-residence certificate, and OECD/CRA Form for treaty residency, where relevant)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, or digital stamping of invoices. GST/HST registrants with annual revenue above CAD 1 million must file returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or via electronic interchange (EDI); the CBSA CARM system requires electronic submission of customs export and import declarations. The CRA participates in the Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing network on a voluntary basis. Large registrants must retain ERP-readable electronic records and, during audit, transmit data via secure web-service or API-compatible extracts upon CRA request.

The CRA may reassess a registrant within four years of the filing date when zero-rated export claims lack documentary support, disallow ITCs, and impose arrears interest at the prescribed rate. Civil penalties include (i) an ETA section 285.1 false-statement penalty of 50% of the tax evaded, (ii) an ITA section 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated income tax for partners, and (iii) daily penalties of CAD 1 to CAD 25 per return or filing. Wilful evasion is a criminal offence attracting fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment under the ETA and Criminal Code, in addition to revocation of the GST/HST registration and the automatic imposition of a 200% penalty on evaded net tax. Exporters must preserve all substantiation for six years under ETA section 286, and failure to repatriate or document foreign receipts may also trigger Canada's foreign-income verification and anti-avoidance rules under Part XVIII and GAAR.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control laws, no prior approvals, and no restrictions on the repatriation of capital, payment of dividends, profit distributions, or the remittance of service fees to a non-resident limited partner. The operative frictions are tax and reporting frictions only: allocable Canadian-source partnership income is taxable to non-resident partners under Part I of the Income Tax Act via Section 115; Canadian-source royalties or interest paid to non-residents attract Part XIII withholding; CRS and FATCA self-certifications must be collected from each investor; and every cross-border wire above CAD 10,000 is reportable to FINTRAC as a large-value EFT. Convertibility risk therefore lies in the client jurisdictions from which anti-dumping defense fees are collected, not in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no statutory timeline for repatriating or converting foreign-currency receipts, either under the Currency Act or the Customs Act. A trade-remedy defense consultancy may retain USD, EUR, or any other freely convertible currency in operating or sweep accounts at a Canadian bank. Compliance duties are archival: books and records must be retained for at least five years under PCMLTFA; the functional-currency election and exchange gain or loss recognition are governed by CRA administrative positions; and CBSA export declarations are not triggered where the ISIC 7490 services are intangible legal advisory outputs.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act, is the sole federal monetary authority; prudential licensing and supervision of all Schedule I banks and foreign bank branches rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); transaction-level AML/CTF supervision and cross-border EFT reporting are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility, strictly supplementary. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer are not Schedule I Canadian banks, do not enjoy CDIC deposit insurance on their e-money floats, and have no direct access to Lynx, the Canadian high-value payments and settlement system. A UK FCA EMI authorization or EU EMI Directive license confers no Canadian payment-system membership, and any such provider operating in Canada must itself be registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC. The LP should treat an offshore EMI as a low-risk collection aggregator for small fee payments only; all funds above operational minimums and all capital injections must clear through the Tier-1 bank to preserve audit trails and treasury security. Given the target payment rails of Swift_Wire and Wire_Transfer, EMIs meet the need only as remittance-side front-ends, not as the sanctioned clearing rail.

Non-resident partners remit capital through a single SWIFT MT103 wire in USD or EUR to the LP's CAD/USD operating account at the Tier-1 bank. Canada imposes no FX license and Bank of Canada or OSFI approval is never required; the remittance instruction should nevertheless carry the bank's mandatory compliance narrative in Field 70, for example 'Partnership capital contribution - CA__CA_FED__CA_LP__ISIC_7490 trade-defense consultancy', because FINTRAC requires large-value EFT reporting within 24 hours for any wire above CAD 10,000. Funds are either retained in USD, converted at the bank's intraday spot, or swapped under the permitted hedge instruments below. For tax integrity, each inbound transfer must be booked against a capital subscription schedule, reflected in the general partner's capital ledger, and traceable to the T5013 partnership information return filed with the CRA.International disbursements for expert witnesses, foreign counsel, documentary data providers, and CBSA or CITT procedural payments use SWIFT MT103 under SWIFT gpi with full remittance narratives; no prior central-bank approval is needed in Canada. Domestic CAD disbursements settle through Lynx, the successor to LVTS, and EFT under Payments Canada rules, or through the emerging Real-Time Rail for sub-threshold advisory fee payments. If the trade-defense file requires the acquisition of physical samples under investigation or laboratory test materials, a sight commercial letter of credit can be issued by the Tier-1 bank on a cash-secured basis under UCP 600, although for a pure ISIC 7490 services mandate the documentary LC is a contingent and rarely used tool.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards in CAD/USD and CAD/EUR, maturities generally under 12 months, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Canadian Credit Support AnnexNon-deliverable forwards for restricted or non-convertible emerging-market currencies in which client exporters pay defense fees, documented under ISDA 1998 FX and Currency Options DefinitionsCross-currency swaps to match non-resident partner capital contributions, fee receipts, and repatriation dates, relying on the non-financial-counterparty end-user hedge exemption from Canadian OTC clearing rulesExchange-traded CME USD/CAD futures as a margin-efficient alternative to OTC forwards for shorter-tenor receipts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility, strictly supplementary. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer are not Schedule I Canadian banks, do not enjoy CDIC deposit insurance on their e-money floats, and have no direct access to Lynx, the Canadian high-value payments and settlement system. A UK FCA EMI authorization or EU EMI Directive license confers no Canadian payment-system membership, and any such provider operating in Canada must itself be registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC. The LP should treat an offshore EMI as a low-risk collection aggregator for small fee payments only; all funds above operational minimums and all capital injections must clear through the Tier-1 bank to preserve audit trails and treasury security. Given the target payment rails of Swift_Wire and Wire_Transfer, EMIs meet the need only as remittance-side front-ends, not as the sanctioned clearing rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate; the Canadian limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level federal income tax. Corporate partners are taxed at 15% (or 9% for Canadian-controlled private corporations on eligible active business income up to C$500,000).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident. The Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent, so its ordinary profit distributions to partners are not subject to dividend withholding tax; a non-resident partner is instead taxed under Part I on Canadian-source allocable income, while passive payments made by the LP such as interest, rents, or royalties may attract Part XIII withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 in aggregate annual reportable transactions with any single non-resident related party triggers the Form T106 information return. OECD-aligned Master File and Local File documentation, together with Country-by-Country reporting, is required where the consolidated group revenue exceeds C$750,000,000. For all non-arm's-length cross-border transactions, contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation must exist before the filing due date to avoid the 10% transfer-pricing adjustment penalty.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations filing regime; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA section 245 and transfer-pricing rules under section 247 permit the CRA to disregard or recharacterize transactions lacking real economic substance. The Canadian limited partnership engaged in anti-dumping trade-defense consulting must demonstrate substantive decision-making, qualified Canadian professionals, physical premises, written contracts, actual invoicing, and genuine cash flows. Without such substance, the CRA may recharacterize Canadian-source income, deny treaty benefits, or assert a permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Information Return

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act subsection 239(1): on conviction, a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, or imprisonment for up to 5 years, or both. False statements in a return attract additional penalties under subsection 162(7) and gross-negligence penalties under subsection 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who authorize false filings are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and can be assessed personally under ITA section 227.1. Criminal conviction can expose individual partners or directors to imprisonment, civil restitution orders, and travel restrictions where risk of flight is established.
Transfer Pricing / Non-Arm's-Length Misallocation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act subsection 247(2) authorizes upward income adjustments and recharacterization; subsection 247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of the taxpayer's gross revenue. Interest accrues on reassessed amounts, and GAAR may separately deny tax benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors generally avoid strict liability for corporate-level transfer-pricing adjustments, but directors who sign false or incomplete T106 forms or documentation are subject to fines and potential negligence-based liability. In sham or conduit arrangements, the limited liability shield may be pierced, exposing directors to personal assessment and criminal penalties.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA section 245 permits the CRA to disregard abusive tax avoidance transactions, deny treaty benefits, and recharacterize income under Part I or Part XIII. Multiple reassessments may carry 10% gross-negligence penalties plus interest. The CRA may also cancel business registration and impose penalties for failure to file annual information returns.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate general partner is a bare shell with no separate identity, courts may pierce the corporate veil under the fraud or serious-misconduct exception. Directors can face personal tax liability, non-resident withholding obligations, criminal fines, imprisonment, asset freezes, and travel bans in serious economic-substance misuse cases.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act violations expose a corporation to administrative monetary penalties up to C$250,000 per violation and an individual to penalties up to C$100,000; criminal proceedings can result in imprisonment for up to 5 years. The CRA may also impose penalties for unreported international transfers.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize, or knowingly participate in AML breaches are personally liable and may be subject to imprisonment, fines, asset freezes, and travel restrictions. FINTRAC may name non-compliant entities publicly, harming cross-border operations and licensing.
