Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for CBCA corporations or provincial limited partnerships; partner contributions are contractual and set out in the partnership agreement. Escrow accounts are not mandated by federal or provincial law, but contributed capital must be evidenced and adequately funded to avoid undercapitalization liability exposure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – registration and administration via My Business Account portal; GST/HST filings via GST/HST NETFILE; customs export documentation via Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned limited partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the online 'Register for a Business Number' service. The application must disclose all general and limited partners (including non-residents), the partnership agreement, and the principal business activity. The BN is also the GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) account identifier. Non-resident partners must also provide their own TIN / foreign tax identification number where applicable; CRA may require proof of agent for service (Form RC59). The registration must be completed before any taxable supply is made in Canada if the partnership is not a small supplier.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-owned limited partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the online 'Register for a Business Number' service. The application must disclose all general and limited partners (including non-residents), the partnership agreement, and the principal business activity. The BN is also the GST/HST (RT), payroll (RP), and import/export (RM) account identifier. Non-resident partners must also provide their own TIN / foreign tax identification number where applicable; CRA may require proof of agent for service (Form RC59). The registration must be completed before any taxable supply is made in Canada if the partnership is not a small supplier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes; no 'check-the-box' entity election exists under the Canadian Income Tax Act (ITA). The partnership must adopt a fiscal period (generally non-calendar-year with CRA concurrence or a deemed year-end under ITA s. 99(2) after cessation). For GST/HST purposes, the partnership may elect under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 to have a 'qualifying partner' report GST/HST, or use the 'special election for closely related persons' under ETA s. 156 to treat supplies as having no consideration. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA s. 18.2/18.3 may apply to cross-border partnership structures but are not elective in the sense of US-style check-the-box; relevant elections include IT-179 fiscal period election and ETA s. 156 joint elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (worldwide taxable revenues of the partnership; non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada must register once this threshold is exceeded, unless the only taxable supplies made in Canada are zero-rated; small supplier exception applies to non-residents carrying on business in Canada only if they are not required to register and total worldwide taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 30,000).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (TPP) are zero-rated under Sched. VI, Part V, s. 1 of the ETA if the supplier exports the property 'in accordance with the conditions and regulations prescribed' (generally, the property must be exported by the supplier, delivered outside Canada, or exported by a common carrier, and the supplier must maintain proof of export as required by ETA s. 169 and section 3.2 of the Proof of Export Regulations). Offshore services to non-residents are zero-rated under Sched. VI, Part V, s. 7 provided the service is supplied to a non-resident and is not performed primarily in connection with real property or tangible personal property in Canada, and is not of a type where the recipient is a registrant acquiring for commercial use (in which case the recipient self-assesses). A bakery master franchise exporting pastry ingredients or finished goods, or providing franchising royalties to a foreign master franchisee, must document the service as performed outside Canada / delivered to a non-resident and retain foreign customer identity evidence.

  • Canadian Export Declaration transmitted via CERS (CBSA Form B13A equivalent) or a Canada Customs Export Declaration where applicable
  • Commercial invoice / proforma invoice identifying the buyer, destination, goods or service description, and export consideration
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or other carrier receipt evidencing shipment outside Canada
  • Proof of payment / Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation demonstrating foreign currency repatriation
  • Master franchise agreement or royalty schedule substantiating the zero-rated offshore service supply

Canada does not operate a government-mandated real-time clearance or digital stamping regime for GST/HST invoices. Vendors must issue prescribed GST/HST invoices (ETA s. 221 and Sched. V) containing the supplier's BN, date, description, amount, and tax rate when the total consideration is over CAD 30. Electronic invoices (EDI, PDF, XML) are acceptable if the format is legible and contains all prescribed data. For customs exports, CERS requires electronic transmission of export declarations to CBSA in real time for controlled or regulated goods; no API clearance is required for standard commercial exports. XML direct filing of GST/HST returns is available through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE XML standard.

CRA will reassess and disallow zero-rating (i.e., recapture 5% GST) where the required export documentation is absent, incomplete, or fabricated. Penalties include: (i) a penalty under ETA s. 280(1) of CAD 250 for failure to provide information; (ii) a penalty of 25% of the net tax amount where a person knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence makes a false or omitted statement (ETA s. 285.1); (iii) interest on unreported GST from the due date; (iv) criminal fines and imprisonment for fraudulent evasion under ETA s. 327; and (v) joint and several liability for all partners in the limited partnership for any assessed GST/HST and penalties attributable to the partnership. In master-franchise cross-border structures, CRA scrutiny focuses on whether the supply was made in Canada, whether the royalty is attributable to goodwill or marketing intangibles ultimately located in Canada, and whether the payment is repatriated within commercially reasonable and documented terms.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account convertibility and liberal capital-account treatment. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible. Profit remittances and partnership distributions are not subject to central-bank conversion approvals. Cross-border transfers carry FINTRAC reporting thresholds, foreign-investment review under the Investment Canada Act, and Part XIII withholding tax on non-resident distributions, but no ex-ante exchange-control permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not require export proceeds to be surrendered to the central bank and imposes no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may hold foreign currency in Canadian or offshore bank accounts. Export documentation must still comply with Customs Tariff, CBSA, KYC, and trade-sanctions screening obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act; no dedicated foreign-exchange-control authority exists. Trade sanctions and anti-money-laundering are administered by Finance Canada, FINTRAC, and the Department of Foreign Affairs, Trade and Development.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMIs cannot take deposits in Canada as banks. A foreign EMI may operate as a money services business with FINTRAC registration and, if providing retail payment services, must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. CAD clearing will generally require a domestic banking partner or payment service provider. Fully unlicensed open-banking style EMI access is not available.
