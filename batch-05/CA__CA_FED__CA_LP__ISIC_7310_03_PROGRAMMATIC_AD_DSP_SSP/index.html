Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is statutorily permissible as the registered office address in most provinces and for federal corporate registrations, provided the registered agent maintains a real street address in the jurisdiction for service of process. However, for a Canadian limited partnership operating a programmatic AdTech exchange and SSP platform with Swift wire and ACH payment rails, Canadian financial institutions and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) substance review require a de facto physical place of business in Canada. A pure virtual office may jeopardize banking onboarding, GST/HST registration treatment, and the pass-through eligibility of the limited partnership under Income Tax Act s 96. Recommended structure: a physical operational office in the governing province plus a registered agent's address for service of process, with books and records maintained in Canada.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO); customs counterpart: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN), which is the federal tax identifier and the base for all program accounts. Application is made by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) via BRO or by paper, or through Form GST20 for non-resident simplified GST/HST registration where applicable. Required particulars for the LP include: executed limited partnership agreement; legal name and date of formation; each general partner's SIN, BN, or foreign tax identifier; the Canadian agent's physical address for service; and a description of the ISIC 7310 programmatic advertising and SSP activities. Upon issuance, the CRA assigns program accounts with two-letter suffixes and a four-digit account number, e.g., RT for GST/HST, RM for import/export, RP for payroll, and RC where a corporate general partner is separately registered. The same BN is used for the T5013 partnership information return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes: the LP computes income and loss at the partnership level under ITA section 96, but no Part I tax is levied on the partnership itself; each partner is separately liable on their share of income. Limited partners enjoy liability limited to contributed capital and unpaid commitments. For GST/HST purposes, however, the partnership is a separate 'person' and is the registrant (ETA section 272.1), distinct from the partners. Territorial scope: a Canadian-resident partnership is taxed worldwide through its partners; non-resident partners deriving Canadian business income through the LP are taxed on a net basis under Part I, with no Part XIII withholding on ordinary business allocations, provided timely returns are filed.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' election; entity classification follows strict legal form. Available elections material to a Canadian LP include: (i) ITA section 249.1(4) election to adopt a non-calendar partnership fiscal period; (ii) ITA section 216 election by a non-resident partner to be taxed on a net Part I basis for Canadian rental or timber royalty income instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (iii) ITA section 150(2) election to file a partnership information return where not otherwise mandatory; (iv) ETA section 177 agency/principal election to fix which party is the supplier of advertising services for GST/HST; and (v) for hybrid mismatch arrangements, compliance with ITA sections 18.4 to 18.6 (effective for arrangements entered into or substantially modified after the applicable 2022 implementation date) through documented commercial substance. A foreign partner's home-jurisdiction classification election (e.g., U.S. check-the-box) does not alter Canadian domestic classification of the LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small supplier threshold; registration becomes mandatory in the calendar quarter in which the LP's taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 either in that single quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. A non-resident LP supplying specified digital goods or services to Canadian consumers is subject to the same CAD 30,000 threshold under the simplified GST/HST regime; once registration becomes obligatory, the registrant must remain registered even if revenue subsequently falls below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for services, including programmatic ad-exchange, DSP/SSP connectivity, and digital ad-placement services, is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA when the Canadian LP supplies the service to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and who is not present in Canada at the time of supply (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7). The supply is not zero-rated if it relates to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or a service performed while the recipient is physically present in Canada. The residence of the commercial recipient (the non-resident DSP, agency, or advertiser) governs for B2B supplies; the fact that an advertisement may target Canadian audiences does not, by itself, defeat zero-rating. Physical goods exported by the LP, if any, are zero-rated under sections 5 and 6 of Part V, Schedule VI, subject to CBSA export reporting. Input tax credits on Canadian overhead attributable to zero-rated exports are recoverable through the LP's ordinary GST/HST return; refunds are subject to CRA proof of export and records audit.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration / e-Export report filed through CARM (required only if physical goods are exported; pure digital advertising services require no customs declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical media or ad-tech hardware (servers, terminals); for pure services, retain the executed DSP/SSP service agreement, insertion orders, and dated technical service logs
  • Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent - SWIFT MT103 or ACH bank confirmation evidencing remittance of export proceeds from the non-resident DSP counterpart, together with the contract, invoices, and a certification of the recipient's non-resident status (e.g., certificate of residency or foreign incorporation document)

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no government-operated digital stamping, and no mandatory API-based clearance loop exists in Canada. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices satisfying ETA section 221(2), containing the supplier's BN and GST/HST account number, the recipient's name, the date, a description of the supply, the consideration, and the tax amount. Electronic invoices and books are valid if maintained in a readable, printable, and accessible format at a CRA-accessible location (ETA section 286; CRA Electronic Records policy). PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is voluntary in Canada; there is no mandatory fiscal-data interchange with CRA for B2B invoices.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export and claw back unremitted GST/HST plus associated input tax credits within 4 years of the applicable assessment date (ETA section 298), extended to 7 years for negligence or misrepresentation, and without limitation for fraud. If zero-rating is denied, the LP must remit 5% GST on the full supply value plus interest at prescribed rates, and may be liable under ETA section 285 for false statements or omissions to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the unreported tax. Failure to collect or remit tax attracts penalties under ETA section 280, and deliberate evasion is indictable under ETA section 327 and ITA section 239, with fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years. Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is an internal compliance control, and significant delay in remittance of export proceeds may trigger CRA scrutiny of the transaction's authenticity, potential application of the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA section 245), and evidentiary challenges to the zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account; Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and applies a free-floating CAD exchange rate with no capital-control licensing, no profit-remittance permit, and no conversion approval for partnership distributions. Profit remittance note: allocations of Canadian-source partnership income to non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1, 5th Supp.) at 25% unless a tax treaty reduces the rate; distributions characterized as return of capital are generally non-taxable. An NR6 undertaking with matching T5013 reporting should be established in the bank's cash management profile to avoid trapped withholding on a US LP/LLC partner structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender ratio and no mandatory conversion applies in Canada. Export and ad-tech service proceeds may be retained in USD or any convertible currency. Mandatory administrative obligations: cross-border electronic funds transfers equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC within 5 business days, and physical transportation of currency or monetary instruments equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000 must be declared at the border under the Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and regulator of retail payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23, supervisory regime in force November 2024). Foreign exchange administration is shared: the Department of Finance Canada holds statutory powers under the Currency Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52) and the Bretton Woods Agreements Act, while FINTRAC administers cross-border funds reporting obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA, S.C. 2000, c. 17).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Canada has no 'EMI' licence equivalent to the EEA. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) offering CAD wallets must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC under PCMLTFA and, for payment services to Canadian users, register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Legal constraint: an unregistered offshore EMI servicing a Canadian entity would breach RPAA obligations and FINTRAC registration under PCMLTFA s. 11, and deposits would not be CDIC-insured. Recommended use: collect-only virtual IBAN for low-value SSP receivables, with weekly sweeps to the Schedule I operating account; do not use an offshore EMI as the primary clearing or disbursement rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal general corporate tax rate after 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction; the limited partnership is fiscally transparent and Canadian-source income is taxed in the hands of its partners rather than at the LP level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII for Canadian-source dividends paid to non-resident persons. For the Canadian limited partnership, ordinary profit distributions are not dividends and do not trigger Part XIII dividend withholding; rather, non-resident partners are subject to Part I tax on their allocated Canadian-source income and, for corporate partners, the 25% branch tax under Part XIV may apply to unremitted after-tax earnings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No explicit statutory threshold creates a personal filing trigger for a standalone Master File/Local File in Canada. Under subsection 247(4), contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and maintained when the aggregate amount of controlled transactions involving non-arm's length non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year. Consolidated groups with revenue of at least CAD 2,500,000,000 in a preceding year are subject to Country-by-Country reporting and are expected to maintain OECD-aligned Master File and Local File documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a territorial Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to offshore financial centres. Substance requirements are imposed through the general anti-avoidance rule in section 245, the sham doctrine, and the OECD permanent establishment tests. An AdTech LP claiming Canadian treaty benefits or deducting intra-group expenses must maintain real decision-making, actual employees, office presence, server assets, risk-bearing capacity, and substantive commercial contracts in Canada. Artificial interposition between Canadian advertisers and foreign publishers will be disregarded and reassessed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment with back taxes and compounded interest; gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated amount under subsection 163(2). Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act exposes penalties ranging from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years. CRA can also obtain jeopardy orders freezing bank accounts and pending assets.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner and managers who authorize false filings or diversion schemes can be held personally liable under joint and several liability. Civil restitution, confiscation orders, pre-trial travel restrictions, restraint orders, and imprisonment are all available. The corporate veil is pierced where a limited partner participates in control or where the entity is a total sham.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the transfer price to the arm's length result under subsection 247(2), deny deduction for excessive payments, and charge interest on the reassessed amount. A gross benefit adjustment also triggers a penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment where reasonable efforts have not been made. Country-by-Country non-compliance can result in additional audit consequences and administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation or sign corporate tax returns containing inflated deductions can be assessed for civil penalties if they knowingly participated. The limited partner veil can be pierced for active participation in the mispricing; extreme cases expose directors to regulatory investigation and criminal liability for fraud.
Shell Company and General Anti-Avoidance Rule

Statutory sanction: The GAAR permits CRA to deny the resulting tax benefit, reassess partners at applicable Part I and Part XIII rates, impose interest, and apply gross negligence penalties. CRA may also disregard the existence of the shell and treat the foreign parent as directly carrying on business in Canada. Withholding obligations may be re-characterized and assessed against the Canadian payer.

Director personal liability: Directors of the LP's general partner may be personally liable for unremitted withholding tax under subsection 227.1 where the entity is stripped of assets. Civil avoidance remedies and fraudulent conveyance actions can remove the protection of limited liability. Senior managers involved in a sham arrangement are exposed to criminal prosecution, bail restrictions including travel bans, and imprisonment.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation are routinely issued to non-registered payment intermediaries. Criminal prosecution for money laundering under the Criminal Code, specifically section 462.31, carries imprisonment up to 10 years and fines at the court's discretion. Asset forfeiture orders can attach profits from unlawful transactions.

Director personal liability: Officers, directors, and partners who knowingly allow unregistered money laundering activity lose limited-liability protection and can be individually prosecuted. Sanctions can include civil restitution, surrender of passports and travel bans as bail conditions, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment. Compliance officers and directors are personally accountable for failure to file Suspicious Transaction Reports where knowledge can be established.
