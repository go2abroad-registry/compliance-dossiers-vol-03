Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal administrator of income tax, GST/HST and other excise taxes; primary online portal is 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO), both accessible at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Customs and export reporting for zero-rated export supplies is shared with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). Provincial tax authorities (e.g., Revenu Québec for QST, Ontario Ministry of Finance for provincial HST component) are separate but outside the scope of this federal jurisdiction record.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine registration obligation — a non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada, including soliciting orders in Canada, must register for GST/HST once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 per calendar year (Excise Tax Act s. 240); voluntary registration is available under ETA s. 240(2). Step 2: Obtain the Business Number (BN) — file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and CRA Program Accounts) via Business Registration Online (BRO) or by mail to the Sudbury Tax Centre, adding the RT (GST/HST) program account; the BN is the federal tax identification number used on all CRA filings. Step 3: For a Canadian limited partnership, no separate entity-level corporate income tax account is required because the LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96; each partner reports its distributive share. Step 4: For online compliance, the non-resident LP must enrol in CRA 'My Business Account' and can appoint a Canadian representative through the 'Represent a Client' portal. Step 5: If the e-commerce operation hires Canadian employees, open an RP payroll account and complete provincial registrations as applicable. Step 6: Retain the partnership agreement, partner ownership registry, non-resident declarations, and BN confirmation as the permanent tax identity file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes: income, losses, and credits flow through to partners under Income Tax Act s. 96, and the LP itself is not subject to entity-level federal income tax. For GST/HST, by contrast, the LP is itself a distinct 'person' eligible to register under ETA s. 122(1), creating a hybrid profile: transparent for income tax, separate registrant for indirect tax. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source income; GST/HST applies to supplies made in Canada regardless of the LP's country of formation.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a U.S.-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows legal form and the Income Tax Act. Relevant elections for a Canadian LP in cross-border MRO e-commerce: (i) partnership fiscal-period election to adopt a non-calendar year-end where all partners have concurring year-ends (ITA s. 249.1(4)/(5)); (ii) partnership income-allocation election under ITA s. 103(1) for allocating income other than per the partnership agreement, subject to CRA concurrence and reasonableness; (iii) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) and election to file annual GST/HST returns for eligible small suppliers; (iv) joint election available under ETA s. 273(1) to treat a partnership and qualifying members as closely related for inter-entity GST/HST non-application where the ETA s. 156/157 conditions are met. Hybrid mismatch: the OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules (deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction measures) have been proposed in Canada (draft legislation released 2022–2023) but are not yet in force; until enactment, the CRA may apply the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s. 245) and the foreign accrual property income rules (ITA ss. 91–95) to hybrid financing or partnership structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies — the 'small supplier' threshold under ETA s. 148(1); once exceeded by the non-resident LP, GST/HST registration is mandatory. A non-resident soliciting orders in Canada may be deemed to be carrying on business in Canada (ETA s. 142(1)) and can become a registrant regardless of revenue level. Voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits (ITCs) on Canadian MRO procurement and e-commerce operating costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian limited partnership may zero-rate (0% GST/HST) supplies of MRO goods under Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 of the ETA where the supplier exports the goods from Canada and retains documentary proof of export; supplies made to non-resident customers for export are zero-rated under Part V, section 10; services performed wholly outside Canada for non-resident B2B MRO customers qualify under Part V, section 7; and B2B intangible or digital services sold to non-residents can qualify under Part V, section 23. All zero-rating claims require contemporaneous commercial documentation demonstrating that the goods physically left Canada within the applicable time frame, the customer was a non-resident, and payment terms were arm's length. The LP may claim refunds of GST/HST paid on Canadian inputs via its GST/HST NETFILE return or, where applicable, Form GST189 for non-resident rebates, and must maintain sufficient export evidence to support the resulting net-refund position.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / CERS electronic export report, including e-manifest) filed for all prescribed shipments leaving Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of MRO goods from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or bank credit advice evidencing receipt and currency conversion of export sale proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and MRO catalogue/order documentation matching the declared goods and values
  • Proof of foreign destination and non-resident status of the buyer (international master client file, US Customs entry or foreign customs import record, forwarder's certificate of export)
  • Partnership records retained for at least six years under ETA s. 286, including export shipment logs and zero-rating reconciliation workpapers

Canada currently imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax authority clearance, or digital stamping of invoices. GST/HST invoices may be issued in paper, PDF, EDI, or e-invoice form and must contain the prescribed information under ETA ss. 169–221, including the supplier's BN/RT registration number, date of supply, description, taxable consideration, and GST charged. In December 2024, the federal and provincial governments announced the pan-Canadian adoption of the Peppol e-invoicing framework, to be phased in initially as voluntary business-to-government e-invoicing with mandatory phases expected in subsequent years. GST/HST returns themselves must be filed electronically via CRA GST/HST NETFILE or approved XML/API channels, but there is no government pre-clearance of individual invoices.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST within the normal four-year period under ETA s. 298(2), extended to eight years where misrepresentation, fraud, neglect, or wilful default is shown (ETA s. 298(3)). Export zero-rating claims are a priority audit focus: if the documentation bundle is incomplete or shows no physical export, the CRA will claw back the zero rate retroactively with GST/HST, interest, and penalties. Applicable penalties include failure-to-file and monthly arrears penalties under ETA s. 280, false statement or omission penalties under ETA s. 281, gross negligence penalties under ETA s. 285.1, and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ETA s. 239, which can reach 50% of the tax sought to be evaded or imprisonment on conviction. Although Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, export receivables outstanding materially beyond 180 days attract transfer-pricing scrutiny under ITA s. 247, source-document inquiry, and potential denial of zero-rating where the transaction is recharacterized as non-export financing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. No regulatory approvals are required for foreign currency purchase, holding, or repatriation, and profit distributions attributable to non-resident partners of a Canadian limited partnership may be freely remitted in CAD or USD after compliance with Part XIII non-resident withholding and Canadian income tax filing obligations. Restricted measures do not exist; the only compliance gate is FINTRAC reporting on monetary transfers, which is an AML regime rather than an exchange-control mechanism.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts. A Canadian limited partnership may retain export proceeds in any currency, hold them offshore or domestically, use them directly for settling foreign trade payables, and conduct FX hedging at its discretion, provided transfer-pricing documentation supports intercompany transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act as the sole monetary and currency authority; Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administration or exchange-control office, and the Department of Finance Canada retains policy oversight for currency and export/import permit matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms are viable only as a complementary settlement rail, rated Medium-to-Low standalone. Canada does not recognise a distinct EMI licence class; a non-resident payment provider directing services into Canada must register as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, observe CAD 10,000 record-keeping and reporting thresholds, and will frequently face correspondent de-risking by Schedule I Canadian banks. A resilient structure combines a FINTRAC-registered MSB layer for card and cross-border payment settlement with a Tier-1 domestic operating account for core CAD clearing.
