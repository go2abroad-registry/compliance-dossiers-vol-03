Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official e-services portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) via canada.ca and CRA 'My Business Account' (Mon dossier d'entreprise). CRA administers the federal GST/HST system nationally; provincial sales tax (PST/QST) obligations remain separate where applicable.

E-government filing portal

Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) free of charge from CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or paper Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'. A Limited Partnership must register in the partnership's legal name, supply the partnership agreement, and identify all general and limited partners; non-resident partners must provide contact details and may be required to appoint a Canadian agent for service. To collect GST/HST, request program account 'RT' at registration; the GST/HST account number is BN + 'RT' + 4-digit identifier. Registration is effective no later than the day on which a taxable supply is made; it may be backdated to the first day of commercial activity. Separately, register with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) import–export ledger (RM account) if the LP acts as importer of record for goods entering Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax under s. 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (ITA); income, losses, and tax credits are computed at partnership level and allocated to partners. However, for GST/HST purposes a partnership is a separate 'person' and can itself be a registrant under the Excise Tax Act (ETA), including joint and several liability of partners.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election; entity classification follows legal form. Available elections include: (i) a joint election under s. 97(2) ITA to defer recognition of capital gains on property contributed to the LP; and (ii) partnership-level filings and elections under the ETA respecting the partnership as a registrant. A Canadian LP cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation. Cross-border hybrid loan/entity arrangements are addressed by Canada's hybrid mismatch rules enacted in 2022 (effective 1 July 2022), which may defer, deny, or recharacterize deductions in deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction mismatches; no private election can override these rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold is exceeded once worldwide taxable supplies (other than financial services and certain supplies) in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters exceed CAD 30,000; registration becomes mandatory on the day the threshold is exceeded. Voluntary registration is available to small suppliers and is commonly used to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part I of the ETA applies only where a registrant supplies goods to a non-resident and the goods are physically exported from Canada as part of that supply, with proof of export retained. CRA generally requires export to occur within a commercially reasonable time (close to 60 days) and goods not to be diverted back into Canada. In an automated dropshipping model where goods move directly from foreign warehouses to end customers, no Canadian export occurs: supplies to foreign destinations are outside the GST/HST net, while supplies delivered to Canadian consumers are fully taxable and cannot be zero-rated.

  • CBSA export declaration — electronic export report filed via CERS/CADEX (Customs Export Declaration) evidencing removal of goods from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the Canadian exporter/shipper and foreign consignee with proof of foreign destination delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank statement evidencing actual receipt of foreign export proceeds within the 180-day repatriation window

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate for invoices. Peppol-compliant e-invoicing is voluntary and promoted through the Pan-Canadian Business-to-Business E-Invoicing framework. GST/HST registrants with annual revenue exceeding CAD 1,500,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically; all registrants must remit any positive net tax and may be required to use electronic payment for large remittances.

CRA may disallow zero-rating and reassess federal GST plus applicable HST with interest at prescribed rates. Ordinary reassessments are limited to four years; the window extends to six years for neglect and is unlimited for fraud. Specific penalties include ETA s. 327.1 false-statement/omission penalties of 5% of net tax (10% for repeat contravention) and ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalties of 50% of tax at partner level. Abusive export and fake-shipping schemes may trigger joint and several liability, criminal prosecution, forfeiture of refund entitlements, and retrospective clawback of all claimed input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies on both current and capital accounts. The Canadian dollar floats freely, with no parity bands, no capital-account approval gates, and no central bank permit requirement for profit remittances, partnership distributions, or liquidation proceeds. The only statutory frictions are Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act, CRS/FATCA reporting, and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting; a non-resident LP making remittances must apply the applicable treaty-reduced rate and obtain the relevant CRA Form NR301 certification where needed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligations exist. Canada imposes no repatriation timeline and no minimum conversion ratio on export receipts; exporters and e-commerce sellers may hold foreign currency balances indefinitely in domestic and offshore accounts. Firms must observe CBSA export declaration requirements, Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys, and FINTRAC electronic fund transfer reporting for transactions of CAD $10,000 or more. For income tax purposes, foreign-currency revenue is converted to Canadian dollars at Bank of Canada exchange rates, or under a prescribed functional-currency reporting basis.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; exchange-control administration was abolished with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951. Oversight of the banking and treasury system is performed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential matters, the Department of Finance Canada for foreign exchange policy, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for transaction surveillance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 equivalent) to the account held in the LP's exact registered legal name at a Schedule I bank. The wire narrative must identify each contributing partner and state 'capital contribution' and 'limited partnership equity injection'; descriptive purpose code 'EQUI' is recommended. Receiving banks will apply FINTRAC electronic transfer reporting at CAD $10,000 and will require the partnership agreement for any single injection over CAD $50,000; no Bank of Canada or OSFI approval is required because Canada operates a fully liberalised capital account. FX conversion, if needed, is executed at the Bank of Canada daily rate, with an option to credit a linked USD-denominated operating subsidiary account to manage currency risk.Automated dropshipping payouts are cleared through SWIFT MT103 correspondent banking for foreign suppliers, with USD denominated flows optimally routed via Fedwire or CHIPS through the Canadian bank's US node to avoid double conversion. Commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) are permissible for bulk replenishment and are issued by the Schedule I relationship bank against collateral or approved receivables; standby letters of credit may be required by certain marketplaces. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate (for corporate partners); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and has no entity-level federal corporate tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% non-resident withholding under Part XIII on dividends paid by Canadian corporations; for a Limited Partnership, distributions are not dividends, but non-resident partners are liable to Part XIII withholding on their allocable share of Canadian-source business income at 25%, subject to treaty relief

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory revenue threshold for preparing documentation; a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of net adjustment applies where the adjustment exceeds CAD 10,000 and the taxpayer has not made reasonable documented efforts to establish arm's length terms by the filing deadline. CRA expects contemporary Local File and Master File for cross-border related-party transactions involving the dropshipping supply chain.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not employ a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime analogous to the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in section 245 denies tax benefits arising from transactions lacking economic substance. For limited partnerships, the CRA may disregard or recharacterize a partnership where the arrangement is a sham, lacks real business purpose, or uses contractual allocations solely to obtain tax advantages. Cross-border dropshipping hubs must maintain operational substance in Canada, including local personnel, physical inventory management, or algorithmic decision-making functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Unreported Cross-Border Income)

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act, a fine of between 50% and 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest; if prosecuted by indictment, imprisonment for up to 2 years and an unlimited discretionary fine; civil tax reassessment including gross negligence penalties of 50% of the understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors or general partner are jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted source debts, GST/HST, and Part XIII amounts; may face travel bans, seizure of personal assets, restitution orders, and in extreme cases extradition or penal measures
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment under subsection 247(4); the tax adjudication may deny business expense deductions, set deemed arm's length amounts, and apply late-payment interest; penalties are not deductible

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be held personally liable for corporate or partnership penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer-pricing misallocation or failed to oversee the filing of required T5013 schedules and Part XIII remittances; court orders may include disgorgement of profits and prohibition on leaving Canada
Artificial Shell Arrangement / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: The GAAR under section 245 denies the tax benefit and reassesses the arrangement on a reasonable alternative basis; additional penalties and interest accrue, and the transaction may be recharacterized as a Canadian permanent establishment attracting full income tax plus late-filing penalties

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are liable for the tax reassessment up to the distributed amounts received from the LP; in flagrant cases, they can be charged with fraud under the Criminal Code, receive lifetime director bans, and be subject to asset forfeiture
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Unlicensed Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 1 million per violation for entities, and CAD 500,000 for individuals; operating without registration can result in a fine of up to CAD 1 million and imprisonment for up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner are personally liable for AML compliance failures if they authorized, directed, or influenced the conduct; potential consequences include criminal conviction, confiscation of all payment rails, a ban from operating financial services, and imprisonment
