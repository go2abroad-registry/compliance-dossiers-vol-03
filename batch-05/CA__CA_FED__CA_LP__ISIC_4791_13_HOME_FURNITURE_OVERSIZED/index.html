Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Federal portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). All federal tax registrations are managed under the 9-digit Business Number (BN) system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or Canadian Limited Partnership obtains a BN from the CRA as follows: (1) assemble the partnership agreement, legal name, registered office address, principal business activity, and expected date of first taxable supply; (2) file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online via Business Registration Online or by paper using the non-resident business onboarding process; (3) CRA issues a 9-digit BN plus program accounts, including RT (GST/HST), RC (corporate income tax, where applicable), and RP (payroll if Canadian employees exist); (4) for GST/HST registration the applicant must state whether it is a resident or non-resident and provide a Canadian address for service or appoint a representative for service; (5) the LP files GST/HST returns under the RT program account, files Form T5013 (Partnership Information Return), and issues T5013 slips to partners; each partner reports its share of partnership income on its own return using its own SIN or BN.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - The LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96: the partnership itself is not taxable for income tax purposes; each partner includes its allocated share of income or loss on its own return. Limited partners' deductible losses are limited to their at-risk amount under ITA s. 96(2.1)-(2.4). Non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source income through Part I filing or Part XIII withholding, subject to treaty relief. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The LP is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96: the partnership itself is not taxable for income tax purposes; each partner includes its allocated share of income or loss on its own return. Limited partners' deductible losses are limited to their at-risk amount under ITA s. 96(2.1)-(2.4). Non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source income through Part I filing or Part XIII withholding, subject to treaty relief. For GST/HST, the partnership is a separate person and must register if it carries on a commercial activity in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Applicable elections and rules include: (a) ITA s. 97(2) tax-deferred rollover election for property transferred into the partnership; (b) ITA s. 98(3)/(5) elective winding-up or disposition of a partnership interest; (c) ITA s. 216 net-basis election for non-residents earning rental or timber-royalty income; (d) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related registrants to be treated as one person for GST/HST; (e) ETA s. 241.1 simplified GST/HST registration for non-resident suppliers; (f) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2-18.5 ordering and denying deductions for cross-border deduction/inclusion mismatches, with no elective deferral available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the current calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148). A person whose only taxable supplies are zero-rated exports is generally not required to register but may voluntarily register; a non-resident supplying goods into Canada may be deemed to be carrying on a commercial activity in Canada for GST/HST purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A sale of oversized furniture by the LP qualifies for 0% GST/HST as an export of goods under Part V of Schedule VI when: (i) the goods are supplied by way of sale; (ii) the goods are delivered or shipped to a destination outside Canada under a contract of carriage; (iii) the goods are exported within 60 days after the supply (unless a longer period is accepted by the CRA due to circumstances beyond the supplier's control); and (iv) the supplier retains prescribed export documentation. Physical movement out of Canada is required; electronic transfer of title alone is insufficient.

  • CBSA export declaration (B13A or EDI equivalent under the Canadian Export Reporting System) where export reporting is mandatory; otherwise a complete commercial export record
  • Commercial invoice and packing list naming the foreign destination, HS codes (e.g., HS Chapter 94 for furniture), and Incoterms
  • Proof of export: original bill of lading, air waybill, or cross-border truck e-manifest showing delivery outside Canada
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, or banking records evidencing FX repatriation of sale proceeds from the foreign buyer

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime exists for GST/HST. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework (PCEI, Peppol-based) is voluntary. Suppliers must issue prescribed sales invoices containing the supplier's name and BN, the receiver's name, the date, the consideration, the GST/HST rate, and the tax amount (ETA s. 223(2)); invoices and supporting records must be kept for six years (ETA s. 286) and produced to the CRA on audit.

No GST/HST statutory FX-repatriation deadline exists; CRA auditors use a 180-day benchmark to assess whether an export sale is genuine and at arm's length. If export evidence is deficient or proceeds are not repatriated, CRA may treat the supply as taxable in Canada, deny zero-rating and input tax credits, and reassess within 4 years (extended to 7 years for misrepresentation, ETA s. 298). Penalties include: 25% gross-negligence penalty for false statements (ETA s. 285); failure-to-file penalty of 1% of unremitted tax plus monthly increments; evasion fines of 50%-200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment of up to 5 years (ETA ss. 280-282). Retroactive clawbacks carry prescribed interest compounded daily.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully freely convertible. Canada maintains an open capital account with no exchange control legislation under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act; current and capital account transactions are unrestricted. Profit remittances by a limited partnership to non-resident partners are not FX-restricted but are subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory, reducible by treaty, e.g., 15% general dividend rate or 5% for substantial holdings under the Canada-US treaty); partnership distributions of Canadian-source income to treaty-protected partners require a NR3016 waiver or treaty-based relief claim. No prior regulatory approval is required for cross-border capital movements; banks merely report transactions under FINTRAC threshold reporting (C$10,000) and the Bank of Canada does not vet inflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Canada. Exporters may hold foreign currency accounts without restriction and may settle export receivables in any currency agreed pursuant to Section 12 of the Currency Act. A Canadian limited partnership engaged in online retail may invoice in USD or CAD and repatriate proceeds freely; corporate banking agreements may, however, impose internal liquidity covenants to reduce FX conversion risk.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); banking prudential supervision is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF oversight for money services businesses rests with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); no separate foreign exchange administration agency exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may open sub-accounts for a Canadian LP, but any entity 'dealing in payment' or performing retail payment functions in Canada must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada; FINTRAC MSB registration (C$10,000 threshold) is also mandatory. Unregistered offshore EMIs cannot lawfully provide payment services to Canadian residents. For treasury-grade custody, escrow of partner capital, and settlement of oversized-good trade flows, a Schedule I bank is required; EMIs should be limited to payroll, minor supplier payouts, or multicurrency float accounts, not core liquidity. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Routing protocol: (1) Remitter bank initiates SWIFT MT103 with ordering customer (field 50) and beneficiary being the LP's operating or escrow account at a Canadian Schedule I bank; (2) CAD settlement of the domestic leg occurs through Payments Canada Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); (3) no Bank of Canada approval is required for inward capital injections, but the receiving bank requires a transaction narrative (field 70) specifying 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – LIMITED PARTNER' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN / QUASI-EQUITY (LP INTEREST)'; (4) where the remitter is a non-financial entity, the beneficiary bank requires a copy of the LP's subscription agreement, the LP certificate, and a KYC assurance letter from the remitting bank; (5) payments above C$10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC; non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally unavailable onshore for USD/CAD because CAD is fully convertible, and any NDF referencing restricted currencies (e.g., CNY) would require an offshore bank desk and a separate ISDA schedule with close-out netting enforceability opinion

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may open sub-accounts for a Canadian LP, but any entity 'dealing in payment' or performing retail payment functions in Canada must register under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada; FINTRAC MSB registration (C$10,000 threshold) is also mandatory. Unregistered offshore EMIs cannot lawfully provide payment services to Canadian residents. For treasury-grade custody, escrow of partner capital, and settlement of oversized-good trade flows, a Schedule I bank is required; EMIs should be limited to payroll, minor supplier payouts, or multicurrency float accounts, not core liquidity. Settlement concentration risk and absence of CDIC deposit insurance further cap their treasury role.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (ITA s.123); 9% federal small business deduction rate for Canadian-Controlled Private Corporations (CCPCs) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income. The CA_LP is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96; the partnership itself is not a taxable entity, and each partner is taxed on the allocable share of partnership income. Provincial/territorial corporate taxes are additional for entities with a permanent establishment outside the federal schedule.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. A Canadian limited partnership does not pay dividends; profits are allocated and taxed under Part I. Where a corporate conversion or a deemed dividend under ITA s.84 occurs, the 25% rate applies. Treaty relief examples (Canada-U.S. Convention): 15% portfolio rate, 5% for corporate beneficial owners with at least 10% voting control, and 0% for pension-type investors. For a non-resident corporate limited partner, the undeployed after-tax earnings allocated through the LP attract Part XIV branch tax at 25% (reduced to 5% for U.S. corporations under the Canada-U.S. treaty).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate cross-border non-arm's-length transaction value in a taxation year requires mandatory Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents). The CRA expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (OECD-aligned Local File standard per IC 87-2R) for all reportable transactions. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) applies to multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million (approx. CAD 1.1 billion). A Master File is required for large groups with Canadian entities, while a limited partnership above the T106 threshold must prepare a Local File documenting product flows, freight, marketing, and payment-processor service fees.
