Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; sole national administrator for federal income tax (Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1, 5th Supp.) and Part IX GST/HST (Excise Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. E-15). Business registration portal: Business Registration Online at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; enterprise number issuance (Business Number, BN) is centralized in the CRA Business Registry. For Quebec-situs digital customers, Revenu Quebec administers the parallel QST regime; for customs export procedures, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers the Customs Act and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED/ERS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Canadian Limited Partnership (LP) with the applicable provincial registry under the relevant Limited Partnership Act (e.g., Ontario: Ministry of Government and Consumer Services; British Columbia: BC Registry) and obtain the provincial LP registration number. A federal extra-provincial registration via Corporations Canada is required only if the LP is registered in Ontario/Quebec with a head office outside the province. Step 2: Obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or by using the instantaneous Business Registration Online (BRO) service; the LP must state its legal name, provincial registration number, fiscal period end (if elected under ITA s. 249.1), principal business activity (NAICS 512110 Motion Picture and Video Production), and the first reporting period. CRA issues the 15-character BN in the format 123456789RT0001, where RT is the GST/HST program account; separate program accounts (RC payroll, RP corporate income tax) are opened only if applicable. Step 3: For a foreign-owned LP, each non-resident general or limited partner must file the prescribed non-resident identification forms: Form RC59 (Business Consent Form) to appoint a Canadian representative for communication and service of process, together with notarized constitutional documents and, for corporate partners, proof of foreign corporate existence (English/French translation required). Step 4: Apply for the GST/HST registration program account using the same BN (RT prefix) either online or by paper Form RC1; for a non-resident supplier of specified digital services that is not carrying on business in Canada, the simplified GST/HST registration under ETA s. 242.2 applies and can be completed without a permanent establishment (PE). Step 5: If the LP carries on business in Canada but has no place of business and the CRA demonstrates risk, CRA may require security under ETA s. 307 (cash or irrevocable letter of credit generally set at 50-100% of estimated net tax); a non-resident LP should include this security in its working-capital model. Step 6: Open a Canadian corporate bank account and have the CRA BN printed on bank instructions, platform payment-rail registrations (Apple Developer / Google Play / Stripe merchant account), and the streaming app's terms of service. Step 7: File an annual partnership information return (T5013 Summary and T5013 slips) within five months after the LP's fiscal year-end; the CRA BN is the key reference for all filings. Step 8: If QST registration is triggered (Quebec residential consumers exceed CAD 30,000 of specified digital supplies), file a separate QST application with Revenu Quebec and obtain a QST number (effective combined rate 14.975%).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For federal income tax purposes, the Canadian LP is a fiscally transparent partnership: it computes income at the partnership level (ITA s. 96) but is not a taxable entity, and each partner (resident or non-resident; general or limited) is allocated its agreed share of income, loss, and credits for inclusion in the partner's own return. Non-resident partners are taxable under Part I on the LP's Canadian-source business income attributable to the partner (s. 2(3) and s. 212(13) ITA), subject to any income tax treaty limitation; withholding under Part XIII applies to certain passive distributions and management fees. Territorial scope: the LP is a Canadian resident partnership only to the extent it is formed under provincial law and either carries on business in Canada or is controlled from Canada; for a vertical micro-drama export business, Canadian-source income is limited to income from Canadian customers and Canadian production activity. Tax exemption zone: not applicable; there are no federal tax-free zones for streaming studios (Atlantic Investment Tax Credit and Ontario Interactive Digital Media Tax Credit are claimable incentives, not tax holidays). For GST/HST, the LP is a separate 'person' deemed distinct from its partners under ETA s. 272.1 and must register and account for GST/HST in its own name if its taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold. The LP may not be consolidated with a corporate general partner for GST/HST purposes; each person is tested separately.

Available election forms

Available elections: (1) Partnership agreement allocation election under ITA s. 96(3): the partners may jointly elect in the LP agreement to allocate partnership income/loss in a proportion that differs from the legal sharing ratios, provided the allocation is supported by the agreement and is not a sham; (2) GST/HST closely-related election under ETA s. 150: the LP and a related registrant corporate general partner may jointly elect to treat taxable supplies between them as made without GST/HST (no tax effect on zero-rated exports but simplifies intercompany cost recharges); (3) Fiscal period election under ITA s. 249.1(5): a Canadian-resident partnership may elect a non-calendar fiscal period; for a partnership with a non-resident partner the deemed year-end rules in s. 249.1(3) require prorated allocations in transition years; (4) At-risk rules election: limited partners are subject to the 'at-risk amount' limitation in s. 96(2.1)-(2.3); the general partner may elect to have the LP track adjusted cost base and at-risk amounts at the partner level in the T5013; (5) Hybrid mismatch and interest limitation election: Canada has no separate 'check-the-box' partition for an LP, but the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) rules in s. 18.2 and s. 18.4 ITA (effective for taxation years beginning on or after 1 October 2023) permit an affiliated-group election to calculate the 30-40% of EBITDA interest cap on a consolidated Canadian group basis, and permit a transitional election to use EBITDA of the preceding three years; the LP should enter the EIFEL election if it will hold Canadian production debt; (6) Non-resident security election: under ETA s. 307(1.1), a non-resident registrant may elect to provide an irrevocable letter of credit rather than cash security (practical treasury election); (7) Section 216 election: generally United States/Canadian cross-border rental election, irrelevant to a streaming and production LP and expressly not elected; (8) US Federal check-the-box: for US partners holding the LP interest, the LP is a default foreign partnership for US federal tax purposes (no Form 8832 needed); the US general partner may instead elect to treat the Canadian LP as a disregarded entity only if the LP is wholly owned by one US corporate member (allowed under Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3); this election is not a Canadian election and must be reconciled with Canadian transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies (exclusive of GST/HST) made worldwide in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or, if that test is not met, in the current calendar quarter (the 'small supplier' test, ETA s. 148(1)). Once the LP's quarterly cumulative taxable revenue exceeds CAD 30,000, registration is mandatory and becomes effective prospectively from the date the threshold is exceeded (long-form registration) or on the day the obligation arises. For non-resident suppliers of specified digital services (streaming, downloadable content, online entertainment) under the digital-economy rules effective 1 July 2021, the same CAD 30,000 threshold applies but is measured solely on supplies to Canadian-resident consumers, and the supplier may use simplified registration under ETA s. 242.2 without a Canadian PE; a non-resident supplier that voluntarily registers remains registered for at least one year (s. 242.1). The LP, as a Canadian-resident partnership selling directly to Canadian consumers, is subject to the general threshold; the corporate general partner's own supplies do not aggregate with the LP's for small-supplier testing. Provincial note: a separate QST registration with Revenu Quebec is required if Quebec-tied specified digital supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP may zero-rate (0% GST/HST) the following export revenue streams under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act: (1) Supply of a service to a non-resident person who is not registered under the ETA, where the service is not performed in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or a service to be performed wholly in Canada (s. 7); this covers B2B licensing and delivery of vertical micro-drama content to foreign streaming platforms, foreign broadcasters, and overseas distributors. (2) Supply of a right, licence, or privilege to use intellectual property (copyright, format rights, script rights) to a non-resident who is not registered, where the right is not for use in Canada (s. 10). (3) B2C zero-rating limited to non-resident individuals: the supply of a digital download or streaming service to a consumer outside Canada, determined by the place-of-supply rules in s. 142.1(4) using the recipient's ordinary residence; the LP must demonstrate foreign residence through reliable indicators (Apple/Google account storefront, billing country code, IP address geo-location, mobile country code, bank card issuing country). Critically, zero-rating is denied for (a) services physically performed in Canada (e.g., Canadian set production or Canadian post-production for a non-resident producer), which are taxable at the rate applicable where the service is performed; (b) content licensed for exploitation in Canada; and (c) any supply to a Canadian-resident customer or to a non-resident who is registered for GST/HST. For goods (physical merchandise), zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 23 requires the goods to be exported by the supplier and documentary proof of export retained. The LP may also claim input tax credits (ITCs) on Canadian inputs (studio rentals, Canadian labour, royalties paid to Canadian creators, platform commissions charged by Apple/Google Canadian entities) in proportion to its taxable/zero-rated supplies; the simplified ITC method for non-residents is not available to a Canadian-resident LP, so full registrant status and ordinary ITC rules apply.

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance e-invoicing, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime comparable to Mexico's CFDI or Brazil's NF-e. The CRA accepts both paper and electronic invoices, provided the supplier retains durable, legible, and readable records (ETA s. 286 and GST/HST Memorandum 12-10). Mandatory invoice content for a GST/HST-registered supplier: the supplier's name or trading name and Business Number; the date of invoice; the recipient's name; a description sufficient to identify the supply (e.g., 'vertical micro-drama video streaming subscription, ISIC 5911'); and either the amount of GST/HST charged or a clear statement that the supply is zero-rated (with the applicable ETA Schedule VI provision). Electronic filing: registrants with annual taxable revenue above CAD 1.5 million must remit and file GST/HST returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE), and payments over CAD 10,000 must be made by electronic funds transfer; no API-based fiscal clearance is required. For exports of physical media (promotional merchandise or hard-copy deliverables), the CBSA requires an electronic export declaration (Canadian Export Reporting System, CAED) for shipments valued above CAD 2,000 per transaction; for pure digital export of streaming services, no CBSA clearance or customs declaration is required. Apple IAP / Google Play Billing / Stripe reporting: the LP must ensure its CRA BN is displayed on invoices generated by the app stores where considered a tax invoice, and must obtain from the platform operators the transaction-level tax reports (country, billing address, IP geolocation) necessary to apply the correct place-of-supply and rate; the platform operators act as payment facilitators and are not the deemed supplier for GST/HST in the typical developer-controlled pricing model.

CRA audit focus: (1) strict verification that the non-resident customer is not a GST/HST registrant; (2) verification that digital streaming services are consumed outside Canada and that the place-of-supply indicators (ordinary residence) are not manipulated through VPNs or foreign app-store accounts; (3) review of intercompany licensing to foreign affiliates to ensure the royalty is not attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment; (4) matching of zero-rated revenue with inward remittances (bank SWIFT records) and reconciliation of the 180-day repatriation covenant; (5) review of Apple IAP / Google Play settlement files to ensure Canadian transactions are not misclassified as export sales; (6) transfer pricing audit of the apple-to-apple margins between the LP and any foreign distribution affiliate. Legal consequences: (a) Reassessment — CRA may reassess GST/HST net tax within four years of the reporting period, or indefinitely where the return is false or incomplete due to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default (ETA s. 298(2)); (b) Interest — daily compounded prescribed interest (approximately 9% per annum in 2025) from the date the net tax was due; (c) Civil penalties under ETA s. 285.1 — for knowingly or under gross negligence making a false statement or omission, a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax that would have been payable; for a corporation the penalty range is CAD 1,000 to CAD 25,000 in addition to the 25% amount; (d) Gross negligence under ITA s. 163(2) — a 50% penalty on the understated income tax of the partners where the LP allocation is artificially structured to defeat Canadian tax; (e) ITC clawback — if the zero-rating is overturned on audit, all input tax credits attributable to the export revenue are disallowed and become payable with interest, creating a double-cost exposure; (f) Director/general partner liability under ETA s. 323 — the general partner is jointly and severally liable for the LP's unremitted GST/HST (including penalties and interest) where the LP fails to remit and ceases to carry on business; (g) Criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 — fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years for wilful evasion; (h) CBSA/customs penalties — for false customs export declarations involving physical goods, penalties under the Customs Act up to CAD 25,000 plus seizure of goods; (i) Recharacterization of B2C app-store sales — if the CRA determines the LP used Apple/Google Canadian distribution entities as agents, the app-store commissions become taxable Canadian-source supply subject to Part XIII withholding and the zero-rating claim collapses; (j) Late or missing FX repatriation — the 180-day covenant is an internal treasury control; Canadian law does not prescribe a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds, but an unexplained receivable outstanding beyond 180 days from the invoice date will trigger a CRA transfer-pricing adjustment under ITA s. 247(2), imputed interest under s. 17(1) for shareholder/affiliate loans, and may be treated as evidence that the supply was not an arm's-length export, allowing the CRA to reverse the zero-rating with interest and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Canadian dollar trades freely and there are no exchange controls on capital injections, profit repatriation, loan repayment, or investment disposition. Non-resident profit remittances are permitted without exchange approval, but Canadian-source payments of dividends, royalties, interest, rents, or management fees to non-residents remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25% unless reduced by an applicable income tax treaty). FINTRAC reporting thresholds apply to wire transfers and currency movements of C$10,000 or more, and CBSA declaration rules apply to physical cross-border currency only. The ISIC 5911 vertical micro-drama business is treated as an ordinary commercial activity; no industry-specific licensing requirement blocks conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or prescribed timeline for export receipts. Export proceeds derived from the global distribution of vertical micro-dramas may be held indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at a Schedule I or Schedule II bank, converted at market rates, or applied directly to trade disbursements. For digital delivery of content, CBSA export declaration requirements are not triggered unless physical media is exported; FINTRAC wire-transfer and large-cash-transaction reporting continue to apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is market-based under the Currency Act, with regulatory oversight of financial institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and financial intelligence reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with material legal constraints. Canada does not recognize a standalone 'electronic money institution' in the same manner as the EU; non-bank fintechs must be registered as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, maintain a partnership with an OSFI-regulated bank to hold funds, and cannot offer Canadian-dollar deposits without banking authorization. Offshore digital EMIs lack a Canadian passport and may not open accounts for the LP as a full treasury host. Their plausible role is limited to payment facilitation for app-store proceeds, provided they are licensed in their home jurisdiction and are contractually integrated with a Canadian operating account. Full treasury and liquidity management should be conducted through a tier-1 Canadian bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions for general documentation; CRA expects contemporaneous Local File and Master File when gross revenue exceeds CAD 5,000,000 and controlled transaction value exceeds CAD 1,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate economic substance code; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, section 245) can recharacterise transactions lacking economic substance. For film tax credits, a Canadian-controlled production corporation with a significant Canadian labour presence and permanent establishment in Canada is required by the CPTC rules (section 125.4 and Regulations)

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine up to $10,000 or 10-100% of the tax evaded; jail up to 5 years; application of gross-negligence penalties under section 163(2) at 50% of understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under section 227.1 for unremitted Part XIII withholding taxes and penalties, and can face travel bans, asset seizure and imprisonment
Shell Company / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR applies to cancel the tax benefit and impose interest; penalties under section 245(3) are 25% of the denied tax benefit over certain thresholds

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed for unpaid Canadian-source withholding taxes under section 160 (transferee liability) if property was transferred to non-arm's length parties
Anti-Money Laundering / Unlicensed Payments

Statutory sanction: Under FINTRAC (SDNA): administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for individuals and CAD 10,000,000 for entities; criminal fines up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to implement Canada's AML compliance requirements can be personally fined and disqualified from acting as directors
Non-Resident Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 215(1), the payer must withhold; failure attracts the full 25% withholding amount plus interest and penalties; a 50% gross-negligence penalty may be applied under subsection 163(2)

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of the LP are personally liable under subsection 224(1.2) for the entire unremitted amount, including interest and penalties
