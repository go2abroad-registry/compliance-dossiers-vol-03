Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Canadian limited partnership. Capital contributions are fixed solely by the limited partnership agreement and may be made in cash, property, or services. The federal corporate general partner must satisfy the CBCA s. 34 solvency test after giving effect to any redemption and must maintain assets sufficient to cover partnership liabilities. No escrow, guarantee, or government deposit account is required for federal or Ontario LP formation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) — federal tax and GST/HST administrator; business registration is performed through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and ongoing administration through My Business Account (MyBA) at canada.ca.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Canadian limited partnership must procure from CRA a federal Business Number (BN), which functions as the federal TIN and GST/HST registration identifier. The non-resident applicant should submit Form RC1 ('Application for a Business Number') together with the RC1-WS worksheet through CRA's Non-Resident Registration service or by mail, and must apply for a GST/HST Program Account (RT) if the LP will make taxable supplies in Canada or wishes to voluntarily register. There is no minimum capital requirement. CRA will issue a 9-digit BN and a 2-letter program account suffix (e.g., RT0001). The LP must then file annual T5013 partnership information returns and issue T5013 slips to both resident and non-resident partners.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity establishing a Canadian limited partnership must procure from CRA a federal Business Number (BN), which functions as the federal TIN and GST/HST registration identifier. The non-resident applicant should submit Form RC1 ('Application for a Business Number') together with the RC1-WS worksheet through CRA's Non-Resident Registration service or by mail, and must apply for a GST/HST Program Account (RT) if the LP will make taxable supplies in Canada or wishes to voluntarily register. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists. A limited partnership is a mandatory pass-through entity under Income Tax Act (Canada) s.96 and cannot elect entity-level taxation for federal income tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.2, 18.6 and related anti-hybrid provisions) apply mandatorily and provide no elective override. Administrative elections relevant to cross-border LPs include voluntary GST/HST registration under Form RC1 and the use of CRA Form NR6/NR7 procedures by non-resident partners where treaty relief or deferred Canadian-source income remittance is sought.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — registration is mandatory where a person, including a non-resident carrying on business in Canada or a non-resident digital platform/service provider, makes taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) in Canada and exceeds C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Excise Tax Act and Schedule VI where (i) tangible goods are exported from Canada by the supplier in accordance with prescribed documentary conditions, generally within 60 days; or (ii) a service, including SEO/analytics SaaS subscriptions and content analytics software, is supplied to a non-resident person that is not registered under Part IX, the service is not performed in Canada in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property, and the non-resident is not present in Canada at the time of supply. For the CA_LP exporting MarTech analytics services, zero-rated treatment is available on invoices to non-resident commercial clients where the recipient's non-resident status and foreign commercial destination are documented. Supplies to Canadian residents through digital platforms may instead be subject to the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration rules.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) / Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) for physical goods exports
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or freight forwarder evidence showing final export destination (for goods; not required for pure SaaS/digital services)
  • Commercial invoice and signed SaaS/content analytics subscription agreement identifying the non-resident customer and foreign place of use
  • Inward remittance certificate / foreign exchange bank statement or payment gateway transaction report evidencing collection of foreign currency proceeds
  • Payment reconciliation report demonstrating invoice-to-receipt matching and FX conversion/custody trail

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping regime currently exists at the federal level. CRA accepts electronic invoices and records provided they are complete, accurate, and available on audit. Canada is implementing a voluntary PEPPOL-compliant pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework; CRA does not operate a centralized fiscal clearance gateway for GST/HST invoices.

CRA requires contemporaneous documentary substantiation for every zero-rated export supply. If zero-rating is not proven, CRA recharacterizes the supply as taxable at the 5% federal GST rate plus any applicable provincial HST, assesses unremitted output tax, compounds interest daily, and may impose penalties under Excise Tax Act s.280 (failure to collect/remit) and s.285 (gross negligence or false statements, generally up to 50% of the additional tax sought). Deliberate falsification of export documentation can lead to criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines up to 200% of the tax attempted to evade and, in the case of a Canadian limited partnership, partner-level reassessments of resident and non-resident partners under Part I or Part XIII of the Income Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains free convertibility for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, under the Bank of Canada Act. Capital account transactions, such as non-resident equity investments, generally require prior approval from the Minister of Finance if exceeding CAD 100 million in aggregate value. Profit remittance from a Canadian Limited Partnership to foreign partners is permitted without restriction for operational distributions, subject to Income Tax Act withholding obligations, beneficial ownership declarations, and anti-money laundering verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose mandatory surrender of export proceeds for most transactions. Export receipts are freely convertible and remittable on sight. Surrender requirements apply solely to strategic goods exported under the Export and Import Permits Act, typically mandating surrender within 30 days of permit issuance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs due to stringent requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Corporate entities must utilize regulated banking channels for cross-border transfers; digital wallets and EMIs are not permitted without specific exemptions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% net federal corporate rate for non-resident-owned corporations (39% statutory rate less 10% provincial abatement, 13% general rate reduction and 1% other adjustments); limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and not a separate taxpayer.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners to the extent the distribution is from Canadian-source income. This is the default statutory rate; the limited partnership is not a taxable corporation and no separate dividend withholding tax applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate controlled cross-border transactions OR CAD 5,000,000 gross revenue triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File/Master File documentation for penalty protection under section 247 of the Income Tax Act.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations in Canada. CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, source allocation rules, and substance-over-form doctrines to challenge transactions lacking economic reality. A limited partnership must maintain genuine commercial purpose, substantive business activity, and arm's length contracts to be respected.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act: fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil reassessment with compound interest and gross negligence penalty of up to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Officers and general partners may be personally liable if they sign false returns or direct concealment; civil restitution, travel bans, extradition risk, and imprisonment are possible. Corporate veil will be pierced for fraudulent conduct.
Unpaid Non-Resident Withholding

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is liable for the entire unremitted amount, plus interest and penalties. CRA may assess the partnership as agent for the non-resident partner under section 227.

Director personal liability: General partners (and in certain cases limited partners acting as managers) are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes. Directors and officers may face personal assessments and travel bans if CRA issues a 'danger for departure' order.
Artificial Partnership / Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard the partnership under GAAR and reassess the ultimate economic owner. GAAR penalty equals 50% of tax benefit, plus interest; potential criminal charges for wilful blindness.

Director personal liability: The promoter and principal participants may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax benefit; sanctions include fines, imprisonment, asset freezing, and loss of corporate benefits of the partnership structure.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust income under section 247, impose a 10% transfer pricing penalty on the adjusted amount in excess of CAD 5,000,000, and assess foreign reporting penalties of CAD 250 to CAD 2,500 per month for incomplete Form T1134.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-credible transfer pricing policies may be assessed for penalties if they fail to exercise reasonable care; personal liability attaches to unpaid taxes and penalties, with travel bans and extradition exposure in egregious cases.
