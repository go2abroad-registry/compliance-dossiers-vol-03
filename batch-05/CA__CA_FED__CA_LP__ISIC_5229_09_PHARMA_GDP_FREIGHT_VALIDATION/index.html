Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portals at www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; GST/HST returns filed via CRA GST/HST NETFILE; customs enforcement by Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada: (1) confirm GST/HST registration duty under ETA s. 240 against the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (ETA s. 148); (2) obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with the GST/HST program account (RT) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or online through Business Registration Online using the partnership's foreign tax identification number and a Canadian contact; (3) attach the provincial extra-provincial registration certificate of the LP, the general partner's governing resolutions, and proof of Canadian nexus (freight brokerage contracts, cold-chain warehouse leases, Canadian bank account); (4) use the RT account for GST/HST collection, input tax credit (ITC) claims, and periodic returns; (5) if no permanent establishment exists but taxable supplies are made in Canada, register voluntarily to zero-rate exports and recover ITCs on Canadian inputs; (6) maintain books and records in Canada or accessible electronically per ETA s. 286, appointing a Canadian representative where necessary (Form RC59).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax under ITA s. 96 (income and loss flow to limited and general partners); the LP is, however, a separate mandatory registrant 'person' for GST/HST purposes (ETA s. 123(1)); territorial scope is not available to the LP itself, but non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only through a permanent establishment attributable to the LP's Canadian business.

Available election forms

No elective 'check-the-box' mechanism exists for Canadian LP tax classification; pass-through treatment is statutory. Available elections: (i) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4)-(6); (ii) GST/HST annual filing election under ETA s. 238(1) if annual taxable supplies are within the prescribed limit; (iii) Quick-Method accounting election under ETA s. 227; (iv) nil-consideration election for closely related registrants under ETA s. 156; (v) U.S. members may file U.S. Form 8832, but a resulting classification mismatch is neutralized by Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6; (vi) special allocations of partnership income/loss are respected only with substantive economic effect and must satisfy the reasonable allocation standard of ITA s. 103(1).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters (excluding zero-rated financial services, capital property, and goodwill), ETA s. 148 small-supplier threshold; non-resident freight arrangement businesses with Canadian nexus are subject to the same threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Goods: zero-rated when sold and exported to a destination outside Canada, or delivered to a common carrier for export, with departure 'as soon as is reasonably possible' (ETA Sch. VI, Pt. V, s. 1); (2) Services: freight-arranging and common-carrier transport supplies are zero-rated when the goods are transported to a destination outside Canada (ETA Sch. VI, Pt. V, s. 8); (3) ancillary logistics services supplied to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST are zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI, Pt. V, s. 7 unless the service relates to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance; (4) cold-chain storage, kitting, and handling services performed on goods while in Canada remain taxable — the LP must separate zero-rated freight arrangement consideration from taxable ancillary consideration and substantiate per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4.5.1.

  • Customs Export Declaration — B13A Export Declaration or CBSA ACI electronic manifest (required for exports over CAD 2,000 and controlled pharma products) with HS codes and declared value
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — issued by the common carrier showing Canadian port of exit, foreign destination, and pharmaceutical consignment details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank or foreign-exchange settlement record evidencing receipt of proceeds from the non-resident principal
  • Commercial invoice and packing list — correlating consignment value, ISIC 5229 service fees, and temperature-controlled GDP packaging statement
  • Pharma export permits — Health Canada Drug Establishment Licence and, where applicable, Controlled Drugs and Substances Act export permit
  • Freight forwarder's certificate of export — destination-agent delivery confirmation evidencing physical departure from Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance in Canada. CRA does not pre-clear, digitally stamp, or receive transaction-level invoice data. A voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework aligned with the Peppol PINT standard is in place; federal procurement may require Peppol e-invoices to the Government of Canada, but this is not an indirect-tax precondition. Registrants must retain full invoice and transportation records (ETA ss. 169-171, 286) and file GST/HST returns electronically.

CRA may reassess within 4 years of the filing date (ETA s. 298); the limitation period is unlimited for fraud or misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. Where zero-rating is unsubstantiated, the LP is assessed uncollected GST/HST on the full consideration with arrears interest (ETA ss. 280-283), and related input tax credits claimed on zero-rated freight inputs are clawed back. Civil penalties: ETA s. 285.1 gross-negligence penalty of up to 50% of the net tax for false statements; ETA s. 280.1 fines for knowingly false reporting; criminal prosecution for evasion under ETA s. 327 with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; the general partner remains jointly liable for the LP's tax debts, with director/partner liability under ETA s. 326(1). In practice, export proceeds settled later than 180 days after shipment trigger heightened document review and reclassification risk from zero-rated to taxable supplies.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member; the Canadian dollar is freely convertible in both current-account and capital-account transactions with no exchange-control permits, quotas, profit-remittance restrictions, or prior-approval regimes. Substantive remittance hurdles are limited to (i) Part XIII withholding tax on partnership distributions to non-resident investors (subject to treaty relief) and (ii) declarative reporting obligations: FINTRAC EFTR for cross-border EFTs of CAD $10,000 or more, Statistics Canada balance-of-payments questionnaires for transactions above CAD $30,000, T1135 foreign-property reporting where aggregate non-registered foreign assets exceed CAD $100,000, T1158/T106 filings for reportable non-resident transactions, and CRS/FATCA self-certification. None of these constitute convertibility impediments; they are compliance steps.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no percentage-of-export-receipts conversion requirement. A Canadian-domiciled GDP freight entity may retain export and logistics receivables indefinitely in USD, EUR, GBP, or any permitted currency at Schedule I banks, may convert at arm's-length negotiated rates, or may deploy the funds for offshore working capital. The only statutory controls are FINTRAC EFTR reporting for electronic transfers at or above CAD $10,000, T1135 foreign-property reporting where applicable, and general client due-diligence obligations under the PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC), with the Department of Finance Canada administering the floating-exchange-rate framework and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulating deposit-taking institutions; no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration exists because Canada maintains full liberalisation of current and capital account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for disbursement-only rails; Low for capital-escrow or deposit-taking. Canada has no European-style EMI licence; a foreign digital EMI must either register as a FINTRAC money services business (MSB) if it only transmits or remits funds, or obtain a Schedule I/II bank licence from OSFI to hold client deposits or escrow floats. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which came into force in 2024 with a transitional registration window, payment service providers that hold, clear, or settle funds must register with the Bank of Canada. Consequently, an offshore EMI cannot lawfully escrow LP capital contributions without becoming a Canadian regulated entity. For GDP freight payouts to counterparties, an RPAA-registered PSP with FINTRAC MSB registration can act as a payment corridor, subject to the Canadian bank's principal-agency due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors route contributions by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for nested interbank instructions) to the LP's CAD or USD demand-deposit account at the selected Canadian Schedule I bank. The sending bank must populate full originator and beneficiary fields (legal name, address, account number, LEI if held) and enter a clear purpose narrative in field 72 / Remittance Information, e.g., 'CAPC - Capital contribution to [LP name] per capital call dated [date]'. The receiving Canadian bank will: (i) file a FINTRAC EFTR if the injection equals or exceeds CAD $10,000; (ii) verify the LP charter, good-standing certificate, and general-partner resolution opening the account; (iii) map the transaction to CRS/FATCA attribution and to any T1158/T106 reporting obligation of the LP; and (iv) retain the MT103 record within the statutory PCMLTFA record-keeping period. USD flows clear via the Canadian bank's US correspondent (Fedwire or CHIPS) into its US nostro and post to the LP's USD sub-ledger; CAD flows settle through Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS system. Conversion to CAD is executed by the bank's wholesale FX desk or under a separate FX forward contract, never embedded in the incoming transfer.Outbound freight disbursements are cleared by SWIFT MT103 for individual invoices normally below USD 500,000, and by commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700 issuance, MT707 amendments, MT734/MT756 presentation and negotiation) for milestone-based pharmaceutical freight purchase orders above that threshold. USD payments route via the Canadian bank's US clearing correspondents (Fedwire/CHIPS); EUR via RTGS Target2 through the bank's euro clearing member; GBP via CHAPS in London; and other currencies via the global nostro network. For USD batch disbursements below USD 1 million, Direct ACH through the US clearing correspondents is supported, matching the entity's target rails of Swift_Wire and Direct_ACH. Canadian domestic supplier payments settle over Lynx (real-time, irrevocable) or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batch EFT. The LP should hold a USD sub-ledger to minimise conversion spreads. Turnover: No federal statutory audit is imposed on a limited partnership solely because of its pass-through status. A mandatory independent audit is triggered where: (i) partnership gross revenues exceed CAD 5,000,000; (ii) total assets exceed CAD 1,000,000; (iii) the partnership is a reporting issuer or is required by a lender, insurance covenant, or Health Canada Drug Establishment Licence/GDP customer condition; or (iv) the corporate general partner is a CBCA corporation that is not exempt because it fails the small-company audit waiver threshold (assets below CAD 1,000,000 and revenue below CAD 5,000,000).

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal general corporate rate; provincial rates additional, e.g., Ontario 11.5%, combined ~26.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (for dividends paid by Canadian corporations to non-residents; partnership distributions are not dividends but income allocations, subject to Part XIII withholding on certain types of income)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million gross revenue and CAD 500,000 in cross-border transactions (for Local File); Master File required if total revenue exceeds CAD 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific ESR, but applies GAAR (Section 245) and substance-over-form principles. For treaty benefits, the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the MLI may deny benefits if the main purpose is to obtain treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 200% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution with fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (Section 227.1) and for penalties if they participated in or acquiesced to the evasion.
Failure to File / Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Penalty of CAD 25 per partner per day (max CAD 2,500 per partner); additional penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month for up to 12 months.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unremitted withholding taxes and penalties if they failed to ensure filing.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (if adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue); interest on reassessments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing mispricing.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under GAAR; reassess income to the actual economic owner; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they used the entity to evade tax.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for fines if they knowingly failed to implement AML policies.
