Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — My Business Account (CRA's secure online portal). Ontario provincial corporate tax is administered by the CRA under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act for corporations with permanent establishment in Ontario.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a 15-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA. Registration may be completed online through CRA Business Registration Online, by mail, by telephone, or by fax. For an Ontario-resident Business Corporation, the same BN is activated for GST/HST, payroll, federal income tax, and Ontario Corporate Tax by selecting the corresponding program accounts (RP, RT, RC, plus Ontario corporately administered account RI-ON). Supporting documents typically include the certificate of incorporation, registered office address, Ontario business address, full identity of directors and any Canadian agent, and descriptions of expected Canadian business activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a 15-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA. Registration may be completed online through CRA Business Registration Online, by mail, by telephone, or by fax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada. Entity classification follows domestic Income Tax Act rules. Canadian corporations may make specific tax elections such as Income Tax Act section 85 rollover elections for property transfers, section 85.1 share-for-share exchanges, and discretionary fiscal-year elections. Hybrid mismatch integrity rules (BEPS Action 2) may deny deductions for certain arrangements involving no-tax or dual-entity statuses; Canada has no general disregarded-entity election for domestic corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada per calendar year or over a single quarter for GST/HST registration; non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada are generally required to register unless they qualify as non-resident small suppliers, with special rules where the non-resident solicits business in Canada through Canadian agents or promotional activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property (e.g., wooden Montessori toys) exported outside Canada qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act provided the supplier exports the goods by a common carrier or by mail, delivers the goods to a Canadian exporter, or exports them itself as a licensee. Offshore services supplied to non-resident persons are zero-rated when the services are acquired for consumption outside Canada. Substantiation requires evidence of prompt export and that the goods did not undergo further processing in Canada.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A form or electronic Canada Export Reporting System submission when applicable)
  • Transport document (bill of lading, air waybill, or CRA-recognized proof of shipment)
  • Commercial invoice issued to the foreign purchaser
  • Packing list / purchase order
  • Foreign customs entry or border crossing evidence
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank settlement record, or payment confirmation evidencing export consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice stamping, or digital fiscal vault mandate is currently in force for GST/HST. CRA requires electronic filing for GST/HST registrants, and electronic payment/remittance is encouraged. Taxpayers must retain invoices, receipts, and source records in machine-readable format for audit purposes; export-related evidence and accounting records must be kept for at least six years.

CRA strictly audits export zero-rating claims. Missing or contradictory export documentation causes denial of zero-rating, resulting in GST/HST reassessment plus compound daily interest. Normal reassessment limits are four years for corporations, extended to ten years or indefinitely where fraud, wilful default, neglect, or misrepresentation is established. Civil penalties can reach 50% of the refund or credit knowingly claimed without acceptable substantiation; criminal prosecution for tax evasion may also apply under the Excise Tax Act and Income Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar on both current-account and capital-account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange controls, no quantitative restrictions on currency purchase, no discriminatory currency arrangements, and no express licensing gate for the repatriation of dividends, interest, or royalties. Profit remittance by an Ontario business corporation is subject solely to Canadian corporate income tax compliance, non-resident withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (base rate 25%, typically reduced to 10% or 0% under bilateral tax treaties), and the filing of T106/T2 information returns where applicable. Funds may be held in CAD or foreign currency in Canadian domestic bank accounts, and CAD is freely tradable for trade settlement, debt service, and equity investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender mandate, no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed conversion timeline, and no requirement to route receipts through a licensed dealer. Exporters are free to retain foreign-currency balances in Canadian bank accounts and convert to CAD when commercially convenient. Compliance obligations are limited to CBSA export declarations (for statistical and trade-control purposes), CRA income recognition in Canadian-dollar equivalent terms under applicable accounting standards, and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting where a single transaction exceeds CAD 10,000 in a regulated entity's view.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) acts as the monetary authority; foreign exchange administration responsibilities are allocated between the Minister of Finance (under the Currency Act and the Export and Import Permits Act), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for regulated deposit-taking institutions, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering transaction reporting, and Canada Border Services Agency for trade-related export declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low–Medium. A foreign fintech or EMI (electronic money institution) cannot lawfully operate as a bank, hold deposits, or clear CAD in Canada unless it is a Bank Act–authorized bank or a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) and, if operating under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), is licensed or recognized by the Bank of Canada as a payment service provider. Digital-first providers domiciled offshore and lacking a Canadian MSB/RPAA registration can only act as unlicensed payment facilitators, not as settlement banks. Amazon Pay and Stripe are permissible as payment facilitation rails but require a Canadian funding account at a Tier-1 bank for final CAD settlement. Regulatory constraint: all non-bank PSPs collecting funds must maintain segregation from proprietary funds and observe the Bank of Canada's sound payment-systems oversight.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital should be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's foreign bank to a Tier-1 Canadian bank account in Toronto or Ontario, using the bank's Canadian institution number, transit number, account number, and SWIFT/BIC code. The wire must include a transparent purpose-of-remittance message, e.g., 'Equity-Shareholder Capital Contribution to CA Corp' or 'Subscription Price for Common Shares'. For USD-denominated injections, the receiving bank can credit the account in USD or convert to CAD using a disclosed spot rate; for CAD injections, use the local large-value transfer system (LVTS) or Real-Time Rail (RTR) after the bank has opened the operational account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined federal-Ontario general corporate rate (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident shareholder, reduced to 15% under most tax treaties for portfolio dividends and to 5% for dividends beneficially owned by a corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the voting shares (e.g., Canada-US Treaty).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end; the balance-due day is 3 months after year-end for a CCPC eligible for the small business deduction and 2 months for all other corporations.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Part A – Corporate Identification Legal name, Ontario Business Information Act identification number, federal Business Number (BN), registered office, mailing address, director names, and tax year reference dates. T106 – Information Return for Non-Arm's-Length Transactions Detailed disclosure of every controlled cross-border transaction with non-residents (sales, purchases, royalties, interest, management fees) and a copy of the transfer pricing documentation. Non-resident-controlled entities must report all cross-border payments to related parties; the CRA may disallow deductions when T106 is late or incomplete. Late filing penalty under s.162(2) of the Income Tax Act: 5% of the unpaid tax balance plus 1% per full month of delinquency (maximum 12 months), with a 10% supplementary penalty for second-and-subsequent late filings; late instalment penalties under s.157 and s.163.1; failure to file T106 attracts CAD 100/day penalty up to CAD 2,500, plus additional penalties for false statements or gross negligence under s.163.
Ontario Corporate Tax Calculation (Provincial Schedule) Computation of Ontario tax payable, application of Ontario's small business deduction, the Ontario Innovation Tax Credit, and permanent establishment allocation factors. Foreign corporations with a permanent establishment in Ontario must allocate income to Ontario under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act Rules and file this schedule. Late filing penalty under s.162(2) of the Income Tax Act: 5% of the unpaid tax balance plus 1% per full month of delinquency (maximum 12 months), with a 10% supplementary penalty for second-and-subsequent late filings; late instalment penalties under s.157 and s.163.1; failure to file T106 attracts CAD 100/day penalty up to CAD 2,500, plus additional penalties for false statements or gross negligence under s.163.
Loss Amounts Schedules Statement of net capital losses and non-capital losses, including carry-back and carry-forward claims (3-year carry-back, 20-year carry-forward for non-capital losses). Non-residents may only use Canadian-source losses that arose while carrying on business in Canada; foreign losses cannot reduce Canadian taxable income. Late filing penalty under s.162(2) of the Income Tax Act: 5% of the unpaid tax balance plus 1% per full month of delinquency (maximum 12 months), with a 10% supplementary penalty for second-and-subsequent late filings; late instalment penalties under s.157 and s.163.1; failure to file T106 attracts CAD 100/day penalty up to CAD 2,500, plus additional penalties for false statements or gross negligence under s.163.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 100,000 aggregate controlled transaction threshold triggers mandatory filing of Form T106; CRA expects full OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation for any material cross-border transaction, commonly benchmarked at CAD 1 million or above, and will impose penalties if a taxpayer has not made reasonable documentation efforts.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the Income Tax Act's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) and transfer-pricing substance-over-form principles (s.247) require real business activity, actual management and control, and an arm's-length allocation of risks and assets. A Canadian entity serving only as a shell with no Ontario mind-and-management or book-and-record presence will be recharacterized and cross-border arrangements may be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s.239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under s.323 of the Excise Tax Act and s.227.1 of the Income Tax Act; criminal restitution orders, court-imposed travel bans as bail conditions, and imprisonment are real possibilities.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may pierce the corporate veil, deem the entity a conduit or agent, apply s.245 GAAR to nullify the tax benefit, and reassess the controlling shareholders personally for the undeclared income; administrative penalties and interest accrue without limit.

Director personal liability: Directors who know the company is a shell and allow tax avoidance face personal assessments for collected but unremitted payroll/GST amounts, civil penalties for false statements, and potential criminal liability under the Criminal Code where the arrangement defrauds the Crown.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, an unregistered MSB can be fined up to CAD 500,000 (first offence) and CAD 1,000,000 (repeat violation); FINTRAC can issue administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 100,000 per violation and criminal prosecution under s.462.31 of the Criminal Code carries up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Senior officers and directors can be personally assessed for corporate AML penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in the non-compliance; FINTRAC can freeze corporate and personal bank accounts and a conviction may lead to extradition or travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA will adjust the consideration to the arm's-length price under s.247(2); a separate 10% penalty is imposed on the gross understatement where the taxpayer has not made reasonable documentation efforts; interest is charged on the reassessed balance.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation or deliberately omit required T106 disclosures face gross-negligence penalties up to 50% of the understated income, personal civil liability for the penalty if it is levied on the corporation, and criminal prosecution for intentionally misleading the CRA.
