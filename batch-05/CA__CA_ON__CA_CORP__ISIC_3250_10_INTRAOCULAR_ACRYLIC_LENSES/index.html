Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); operational portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), CRA My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE, and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA); Ontario corporate income tax (John 4/CT23) is administered jointly by the CRA under a federal-provincial tax administration agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance, which separately administers the Employer Health Tax (EHT); all GST/HST in Ontario, including the 8% provincial component, is administered by the CRA.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (Corporations Canada) or provincially under the Ontario Business Corporations Act (ServiceOntario); online federal incorporation triggers automatic CRA Business Number (BN) issuance when elected during filing, otherwise submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) electronically, by fax, or by mail to the CRA Business Registration Unit. (2) The BN is a 9-digit federal identifier with two-letter program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RD (customs/export importer-exporter code). (3) Non-resident foreign corporations must register the RT account no later than the day on which the first taxable supply in Canada is made; ETA s. 240(3) permits the Minister to require security from non-resident registrants without a Canadian permanent establishment, and ETA s. 246 requires appointment of a Canadian agent for service. (4) Ontario EHT registration is separate and becomes mandatory once Ontario payroll exceeds C$1,000,000 (2024 threshold), filed through Ontario Business Central / the Ontario Ministry of Finance portal. (5) Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) under the Medical Devices Regulations is required before intraocular lenses are imported, exported, or sold, and must be referenced in the CRA registration profile for zero-rating substantiation; the Canadian customs client identifier and importer-exporter code are obtained concurrently with the RD account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a taxpayer distinct from its shareholders for federal and Ontario income tax; no pass-through eligibility for corporate structures. Territorial_Scope: a Canadian-resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income (ITA s. 2(1)); a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada through a permanent establishment is taxed on Canadian-source business income (ITA s. 2(3)); export business income remains subject to Canadian corporate income tax despite GST/HST zero-rating (zero-rating is an indirect tax relief only). Tax_Exempt_Zone: none — Canada and Ontario provide no general tax-free or special economic zone for intraocular lens manufacturing; medical-device producers rely on statutory zero-rating, input tax credits (ITCs), and the Scientific Research and Experimental Development (SR&ED) investment tax credit, not on territorial exemptions.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election; all corporations are taxed at the entity level. Available statutory elections relevant to a manufacturer: (i) ETA s. 273 election between closely related corporations to treat otherwise taxable supplies as made for nil consideration, preserving GST/HST efficiency within a corporate group; (ii) ETA s. 273.1 joint venture election for GST/HST; (iii) ITA s. 85(1) rollover election for tax-deferred transfer of intellectual property, moulds, and manufacturing assets into the corporation at incorporation; (iv) ITA s. 216(1) election for non-resident rental or timber income (not applicable to manufacturing); (v) hybrid mismatch arrangements are not elective — ITA ss. 18.4, 18.5, and 95.2 apply mandatorily to deny deductions for certain hybrid mismatch payments where Canada is the payer jurisdiction under BEPS Action 2, and the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) obligations are relevant to cross-border royalty structures for surgical lens patents; (vi) Ontario does not grant separate entity classification elections; EHT filing group election is available for related Ontario employers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in total worldwide consideration for taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, per ETA s. 148(1) 'small supplier' definition; once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required within 30 days. For the intraocular lens manufacturer, exports of lenses and contract polishing/finishing services are zero-rated taxable supplies and therefore count toward the C$30,000 threshold. Voluntary registration is available immediately to recover ITCs on cleanroom equipment, hydrophobic acrylic resin inputs, coatings, sterilization, R&D, and capital construction; for non-resident registrants, the Minister may impose security under ETA s. 240(3).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported intraocular acrylic lenses is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V (Exports): (i) goods sold by the Canadian manufacturer and delivered by the manufacturer to a non-resident consignee, or (ii) goods shipped by the manufacturer outside Canada pursuant to a contract of sale, or (iii) goods supplied by a non-resident acting as agent, are zero-rated provided the manufacturer retains documentary proof of export within a reasonable time (CRA administrative guideline: physical export within 60 days of the time of supply; extension available for manufacturing lead times and large capital goods). In addition, domestic supplies of intraocular lenses to hospitals, licensed ophthalmologists, and ambulatory surgical centres are zero-rated as medical devices under Schedule VI, Part II, provided the lens holds a Health Canada medical device licence under the Medical Devices Regulations (Class II/III/IV) — this preserves ITCs while eliminating output tax on cataract lens sales within Ontario. Contract manufacturing, lens polishing, and sterilization services performed in Canada on goods destined for non-resident principals may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 when the recipient is a non-resident and the tangible personal property is situated in Canada solely for processing. The manufacturer must record the export in CERS (B13A electronic declaration) and reconcile the zero-rated supply with the CRA GST/HST return line for zero-rated exports; the exporter must verify the non-resident status of the buyer (no GST/HST registration number required for export sale) to avoid misclassification.

  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill naming the Canadian exporter as shipper and the non-resident purchaser as consignee, with a foreign port/airport of discharge

  • Cargo Control Document or freight forwarder certificate of physical departure proving the goods left Canada
  • Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) and any CRA GST/HST ruling letter confirming zero-rating classification of the intraocular lens

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices exists in Canada. The CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns by registrants with annual taxable sales exceeding C$1,500,000 (GST/HST NETFILE or TELEFILE) and mandates that all records, including supplier invoices, purchase orders, and customs documentation, be retained in electronic machine-readable format acceptable to the CRA (ETA s. 286; CRA Information Circular IC-04-03). Canada has endorsed the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing framework on a voluntary basis (operational 2024-2025); adoption is not yet a compliance mandate for intraocular lens manufacturers. Ontario imposes no supplementary electronic invoicing clearance; CRA audits may request a complete digital audit file (CTF/DAF) for large GST/HST registrants.

Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is the operative internal-control window for complete export file closure (order-to-payment traceability), while the CRA requires the physical export to occur within a reasonable time (generally 60 days) and documentation to exist at the time of the GST/HST return. On audit, CRA tax services offices and the International Large Business Directorate scrutinize B13A/CERS filings against bills of lading, freight forwarding files, and bank remittance evidence; where zero-rating is unsupported, the CRA reassesses the supply as taxable at 13% HST, retroactively to the original reporting period, denies the associated ITCs, and issues interest (daily compound at the applicable prescribed rate, currently approximately 9% for large corporate arrears) plus penalties. Specific penalty provisions: ETA s. 280 late-filing penalty of 5% of net tax plus 5% per month of continued default (maximum 12 months); ETA s. 285 false statement or omission penalty of the greater of C$250 and 5% of the tax that would have been payable; ETA s. 285.1 gross negligence misrepresentation penalty of 25% of the tax avoided; ETA s. 296 normal reassessment window is four years, with no limitation period where a taxpayer has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, or committed fraud; aggravated conduct may be prosecuted as tax evasion under Criminal Code s. 380 (fine up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years). For Ontario, false EHT returns attract penalties under the Employer Health Tax Act; and the Ontario Securities Commission or Health Canada may add regulatory sanctions if export declarations are falsified. The clawback risk is thus full exposure to 13% HST on aggregate export revenue, reversal of all export-related ITCs, interest, civil penalties, and potential criminal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. Canada has no foreign-exchange control legislation and maintains a free-floating CAD with market-clearing rates. Profit remittance (dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees) is unrestricted and requires no FX permit; however, a resident payer in Ontario must apply Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at the 25% statutory rate, reduced under the applicable Canada income tax convention, and dividend payments require a solvency-certified board resolution under the Canada Business Corporations Act or Ontario Business Corporations Act. Inbound equity and intercompany debt funding are subject to Investment Canada Act notification or national security review for the medical-device manufacturing sector, but not to FX licensing. Exchange rates for income tax translation are sourced from the Bank of Canada daily noon and closing rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished foreign-exchange controls in 1951; there is no statutory timeline requiring surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio applicable to export receipts. The Ontario corporate exporter may retain USD, EUR, or other foreign currency proceeds in domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. For Canadian income tax purposes, accounts receivable and FX gains and losses must be translated into CAD at the Bank of Canada noon rate on the applicable recognition date. Customs export documentation under the Canada Border Services Agency and bank settlement statements must be retained to substantiate revenue recognition, trade receivables, and hedging positions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - central monetary authority, sole issuer of Canadian bank notes, and official foreign exchange agent for the Government of Canada. Prudential banking regulation is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision of banks and money services businesses is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Minister of Finance retains statutory policy authority under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Canada does not recognize the European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence class; deposit-taking and core operating-account services for commercial entities are reserved to Schedule I banks under the Bank Act, federal trust and loan companies, and provincially regulated credit unions. An offshore digital EMI may onboard the Ontario entity only as a narrow, non-deposit-taking payments facilitator for card-linked expenses and low-value disbursements. Any EMI acting in Canada as a money services business must register with FINTRAC as an MSB, appoint a Canadian compliance agent, and enforce the Travel Rule for transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. Because the intraocular-lens manufacturer requires bank-grade liquidity segregation, commercial LC issuance, SWIFT MT103 settlement, and payroll banking, the offshore EMI is an auxiliary agency channel and cannot serve as the primary treasury rail.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Canada does not recognize the European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence class; deposit-taking and core operating-account services for commercial entities are reserved to Schedule I banks under the Bank Act, federal trust and loan companies, and provincially regulated credit unions. An offshore digital EMI may onboard the Ontario entity only as a narrow, non-deposit-taking payments facilitator for card-linked expenses and low-value disbursements. Any EMI acting in Canada as a money services business must register with FINTRAC as an MSB, appoint a Canadian compliance agent, and enforce the Travel Rule for transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. Because the intraocular-lens manufacturer requires bank-grade liquidity segregation, commercial LC issuance, SWIFT MT103 settlement, and payroll banking, the offshore EMI is an auxiliary agency channel and cannot serve as the primary treasury rail.
