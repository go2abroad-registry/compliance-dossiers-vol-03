Governing statute

Canada has no federal limited partnership statute; the Limited Partnership is formed under an applicable provincial Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Limited Partnerships Act, RSO 1990, c. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The Canadian Dollar (CAD) floats freely with no surrender, allocation, or prior-approval requirements for cross-border capital flows. Profit remittances, interest payments, dividends, and management fees from a Canadian Limited Partnership to non-resident partners are unrestricted, subject only to standard withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and applicable treaty relief (typically 15% or lower on dividends/royalties; interest generally 25% absent treaty reduction). No Bank of Canada authorization is required for any class of cross-border payment, and there are no documentation requirements for remittance other than those imposed by the reporting bank's own AML/KYC and transaction-monitoring policies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada prescribes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion percentage, or surrender protocol for export proceeds. A Canadian exporter, forwarder, or fulfillment entity may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in non-resident or foreign-currency bank accounts indefinitely. The only obligations are transactional reporting to FINTRAC for large cash transactions above CAD 10,000 (inapplicable to wire-based B2B proceeds) and standard financial statement recognition under IFRS/ASPE; no repatriation deadline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as anti-money laundering supervisor; Canada maintains no central foreign-exchange control agency equivalent to SAFE or the Central Bank's Capital Administration.

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High for supplementary treasury rails but Low/Medium as a sole primary settlement bank. Legal constraints: (i) these platforms are not OSFI-regulated Schedule I banks and therefore cannot provide deposit insurance (CDIC) coverage nor originate commercial Letters of Credit; (ii) FINTRAC MSB registration requires the LP's beneficial-owner disclosure and appointing a Canadian Compliance Officer (anti-money-laundering/AML); (iii) funds held at non-Canadian EMI subsidiaries are subject to foreign insolvency and ring-fencing regimes; and (iv) some EMIs refuse Limited Partnerships with non-resident general partners due to characterization complexity, so a fully compliant LP package (below) must be pre-vetted with each platform's underwriting desk. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For injections from a US or offshore parent into the Canadian LP, the compliant routing is: (i) SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank with field 50K (ordering customer) naming the contributing entity, field 59 (beneficiary) naming the Canadian LP, and field 70 (remittance information) stating 'Treasury capital contribution — inter-company working capital, non-acquisition'; (ii) settlement via Scotiabank/RBC's New York branch in USD (Fedwire/CHIPS clearing) with onward CAD conversion through the bank's FX desk at the daily market rate plus standard interbank spread; or (iii) direct CAD settlement via SWIFT to the Canadian bank's main branch utilizing the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Lynx replacement rail for same-day finality. Canada does not mandate purpose codes; however, for internal risk classification, banks apply ISO 20022 remittance and purpose sub-category 'TREA'/'CASH' style narratives, and the LP should simultaneously submit a Statutory Declaration under the Income Tax Act that no proceeds constitute taxable income of a non-resident carrying on business in Canada at this preliminary capital stage. If the injection represents a loan, a cross-border loan agreement with arm's-length interest rate must be maintained and interest reported on NR4 if paid. No Bank of Canada approval or Investment Canada Act notification is required for a passive treasury injection, but a Form ISED voluntary notice is recommended to eliminate any ambiguity regarding the non-reviewable nature of the transaction.For settlement of outbound trade flows — ocean-freight bookings, overseas-warehouse storage, local line-haul carrier payments, and US/UK/EU partner fees — the LP should deploy three rails in order of preference: (1) SWIFT MT103 for vendor invoices exceeding USD 25,000, using the 'Recipient ID' and 'Remittance Information' fields to embed the vendor payee account at their local bank (settlement via the Canadian bank's correspondent network, e.g., RBC via Wells Fargo in the US, and via Scotiabank's correspondent in the UK/EU); (2) Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C confirmation) via either RBC or TD Trade Finance desk for high-value warehouse expansion contracts or capital equipment, with shipment draft and ocean bill of lading as performance triggers — however, an e-commerce fulfillment hub rarely requires L/C, so the facility should remain undrawn and standby-only; and (3) local-currency clearing through the EMI rail (Airwallex Business API), which executes ACH/Wire locally in USD to US vendors, Faster Payments/BACS to UK providers, and SEPA Instant for EU partners, at rates 30–80 bps better than the bank's cross-currency spread. All outbound wires must be matched to a PO in the warehouse management system and periodically reconciled through the LP's banking portal using MT940/MT942 statement standards; provided no cash transactions occur, no FINTRAC large-cash form is triggered.Deliverable Forward FX Contracts (USD/CAD and CAD/EUR) — plain-vanilla forwards executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a bank FX Master Agreement with RBC/TD/Scotia, allowing the LP to lock in conversion rates for known monthly warehouse and carrier disbursement cycles; full legal delivery capacity exists and no regulatory restriction applies.Cross-Currency Swaps (FX Swaps, USD/CAD) — permitted for the LP to swap fixed USD notional into CAD floating to cover CAD-denominated operating expenses (rent, payroll, GST) where the maturity profile exceeds 12 months; requires ISDA CSA collateral arrangement and internal treasury approval.European-Style Currency Options on USD/CAD — permissible and available over-the-counter from Canadian Schedule I banks to cap downside on a large anticipated repatriation of US revenues; premium payable in CAD, no leverage restriction under Canadian law (subject to prudent board policy).Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — technically unavailable for CAD because CAD is fully deliverable in the offshore market; accordingly, NDFs are removed from the hedging toolkit and replaced exclusively by deliverable instruments to avoid basis-risk confusion.Centrally Cleared FX Futures (CME USD/CAD) — permitted to hedge near-dated inventory purchase exposure on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange through a futures-clearing broker; not normally needed given the low-risk profile but available as a benchmark rate reference.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (net federal corporate income tax rate on general business income; basic 38% less 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction). Limited partnerships are pass-through entities and do not pay federal income tax at the partnership level; each partner is taxed on his or her allocable share.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders. For a limited partnership, business profit distributions are generally NOT treated as dividends; a non-resident partner carrying on business in Canada through the LP is taxed on its allocable share of net income at graduated rates, and no dividend withholding tax applies to ordinary profit distributions. However, if the LP is held by a corporate non-resident partner and deemed to operate through a Canadian permanent establishment, additional branch tax (25%) may apply under Part I on after-tax earnings repatriated.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transactions with non-residents exceeding CAD 1,000,000 per annum (or CAD 5,000,000 in aggregate) trigger mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation expectations, including a Local File and, where applicable, a Master File. The threshold applies to the total value of non-arm's length cross-border dealings in a taxation year, and documentation must be prepared before filing (contemporaneous requirement).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under subsection 245(2) applies to disallow tax benefits arising from transactions lacking genuine economic substance or realistic possibility of profit. Canadian transfer pricing rules treat the substance and functions performed as decisive, attributing income to a Canadian permanent establishment commensurate with actual value-adding activities. A pure conduit or shell arrangement used for treaty shopping may be recharacterized under GAAR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act, s. 239)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: fine up to 200% of the understated tax and/or imprisonment up to 2 years. On indictment: fine up to 200% of the understated tax and/or imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who participated in or authorized the evasion can be criminally prosecuted. Civil liability may apply under the joint and several liability rules for non-remitted amounts. Travel bans and asset freezes may be ordered under criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Income Tax Act, s. 247)

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 10% of the amount of the transfer pricing adjustment (understated income), unless the taxpayer has made reasonable efforts to document and support the pricing. CRA may also assess additional interest on the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for the TP penalty; however, if the corporation fails to remit the penalty, CRA can pursue directors for the unremitted source deductions or withholdings, but not the TP penalty itself. Directors who knowingly create false documentation may be prosecuted under the tax evasion provisions.
Failure to Withhold/Remit Part XIII Tax (Income Tax Act, s. 215/227)

Statutory sanction: Late withholding penalty: 10% of the amount withheld; on second offence within 4 years, 20%. Interest accrues at the statutory prescribed rate on the unremitted amount. The underlying withholding remains collectible from the payer.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding under subsection 227.1(1), unless the director exercised reasonable care and diligence. CRA may issue a director liability assessment and seek collection from personal assets, and enforce asset freezes or travel restrictions in serious cases.
Money Laundering / AML Non-Compliance (Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, SC 2000, c. 17)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations (per violation); criminal sanctions for individuals up to 5 years’ imprisonment. Repeat violations expose the entity to cancellation of registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal liability if they authorized or acquiesced to the violations. The AML Act imposes personal liability for fines if the company cannot pay, and senior officers may be subject to prosecution for knowing participation in transactions designed to evade the Act.
