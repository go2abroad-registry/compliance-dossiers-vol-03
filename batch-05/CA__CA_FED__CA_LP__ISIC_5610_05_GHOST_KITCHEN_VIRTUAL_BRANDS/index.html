Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permissible with qualification. CBCA s. 19 requires the registered office of the general partner corporation to be located at a specified address in Canada (a physical street address) where documents and process may be served. A virtual office used exclusively for mail forwarding is acceptable only if it corresponds to a lawful commercial address where the general partner's records and minute book are physically maintained and where an agent authorized to accept service is present during ordinary business hours. For the limited partnership itself, most provincial LP registries (Ontario, BC, Alberta) permit registration with a virtual agent's address provided the agent consents and service capacity exists. For a ghost-kitchen operation, the physical prepared-food production site (commissary kitchen facility) must be separately identified for municipal food-service licensing and CFIA/Public Health inspection – the virtual office will not satisfy operational-substance requirements for CFIA licensing under the Safe Food for Canadians Act when the kitchen is a food-manufacturing premise. The entity will need: (i) registered office agent, (ii) the operational kitchen facility, and (iii) an accounting/tax records office in Canada to maintain commercial substance under the Canada-foreign tax treaties.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: My Business Account - https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html

E-government filing portal

Because Canada has no federal limited partnership statute, the LP is first registered under a provincial Limited Partnerships Act. The federal tax identity is the CRA Business Number (BN): a foreign or domestic limited partnership applies online via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, or for non-resident businesses via Form RC172 (Application for a Business Number and GST/HST Account for Non-Resident Businesses). The application must identify the partnership and each general partner; a BN with a GST/HST program account (suffix 'RT') is issued separately from any income tax or payroll accounts. Online registration is generally effective immediately; paper applications are processed by the CRA within approximately 4 to 6 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a Canadian LP is mandatorily pass-through for Income Tax Act purposes, while the partnership itself is a registrant under the Excise Tax Act. Available elections include: (i) non-calendar fiscal period election under ITA subsection 249.1(7); (ii) voluntary GST/HST registration for a small supplier under ETA subsection 240(4); (iii) the ETA section 211 election for deemed supplies in jointly owned or unincorporated ventures; and (iv) the ordering rules for hybrid mismatch arrangements under ITA sections 18.4 and 18.5, which apply automatically without a formal check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - mandatory GST/HST registration is triggered when the LP's worldwide taxable supplies in Canada exceed $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters; supplies of restaurant meals made through an online distribution platform are included in this threshold computation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Prepared meals and food products physically exported by the LP from Canada qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, and certain services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated where performance is not primarily performed in Canada or use is overseas. Domestic ghost-kitchen sales through delivery platforms are taxable supplies: under the GST/HST online distribution platform deeming rules effective July 1, 2021, the platform operator is deemed to make the taxable supply to the end consumer, and the LP is deemed to make a separate taxable B2B supply to the platform, thereby entitling the LP to input tax credits on its operating expenditures.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) confirmation
  • Commercial invoice specifying the goods, quantity, sale terms, and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued to the non-resident buyer or consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment record evidencing settlement in foreign currency from the non-resident customer
  • Supporting evidence of delivery to a non-resident address, including courier tracking or carrier trip documentation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework. The CRA accepts electronic records and invoices provided they contain the GST/HST information required by ETA sections 169 and 286 (e.g., supplier BN, date of invoice, consideration, and tax amount). B2B invoices issued by the ghost kitchen to platform operators such as UberEats or DoorDash are standard commercial electronic documents with no government pre-approval.

Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline for export zero-rating; the 180-day figure is applied by this dossier as an internal documentation benchmark. The CRA may reassess within the normal four-year period, or without limitation in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default, and may reverse zero-rating with interest. Penalties include: a late-filing penalty equal to 1% of the unremitted net tax plus 25% per complete month (to a maximum of 12 months); a false statement or omission penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax; and a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated. Criminal tax evasion is prosecutable under ETA sections 327 and 329, with the general partner and partnership representatives jointly liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible and freely floating; Canada maintains no exchange control regime. Current-account transactions (imported food ingredients, delivery platform fees, franchise royalties) and capital-account transactions (non-resident partner capital contributions, profit remittances, partnership distributions) are unrestricted and require no Bank of Canada permit. Remittance friction arises only indirectly through Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory on distributions absent treaty relief) and transfer-pricing rules under the Income Tax Act (Canada); no FX approval or conversion licence is required for any payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratios. Proceeds from delivery-platform sales of ghost-kitchen virtual brands and cross-border food-service consulting may be retained in USD or EUR current accounts without forced CAD conversion. The only applicable obligations are FINTRAC reporting thresholds (currency transaction reports and electronic funds transfer reports above CAD 10,000), which are filed by the financial institution.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), in coordination with OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) and FINTRAC under the Bank of Canada Act, Currency Act, and Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for auxiliary pay-in/pay-out rails (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer) but Low for primary operating treasury. Offshore digital EMIs and e-money institutions can hold pre-funded payment balances and provide excellent MT103-style cross-border transfers, yet they are not Canadian deposit-taking institutions without OSFI registration or a Schedule I bank partnership; their balances are not CDIC-insured and lack direct access to LYNX RTGS settlement in native CAD. Recommended configuration is a Schedule I bank as core liquidity host with a fintech EMI used only for small USD/EUR vendor disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

LP pass-through: no federal entity-level income tax; each corporate partner pays federal Part I tax at 15% (general, ITA s. 123) plus provincial corporate rates of 8%–16% (e.g., ON 10%, AB 8%, BC 12%); individual partners pay progressive marginal rates up to 33% federal plus provincial top-up. Determinable federal headline for a corporate partner: 15%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding on any partnership distribution of Canadian-source income to a non-resident partner where the non-resident has not filed a Canadian Part I return; treaty-reduced rates: 15% portfolio / 5% on dividends paid to a US corporate partner owning at least 10% voting power (Canada–US DTA Art. X); LP repayment of contributed capital is not subject to Part XIII.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T5013 return due within five (5) months after fiscal period-end (s. 204.2? no, the administrative practice: May 31 for a December 31 year-end). Partner T5013 slips must be issued to partners by the last day of February and filed with CRA by the end of March; if the due date falls on a weekend or holiday, the deadline shifts to the next business day.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous TP documentation (IC 87-2R / s. 247(4)) must be prepared, and the risk exists even where no formal Local File obligation is triggered. Mandatory Local File where the entity's gross revenue ≥ CA$50 million AND cross-border non-arm's-length transactions exceed CA$5 million (for taxation years beginning after 2022); Master File where the MNE group's consolidated revenue ≥ CA$1 billion; country-by-country report trigger: consolidated group revenue ≥ CA$750 million. Below thresholds, a 'reasonable efforts' TP analysis of every non-arm's-length cross-border transaction (e.g., fee of the virtual-brand licensor) is still required.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no UAE-style dedicated ESR law; substance is enforced through (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), broadened by the June 2024 amendments so that a tax benefit tainted by an avoidance purpose is denied where the transaction lacks real economic substance, (ii) the specified-member/SBD rules and limited-partner at-risk rules (s. 96(2.1)-(2.4)), and (iii) treaty-LOB rules under the Canada–US Fifth Protocol. A delivery-only virtual-brand LP must demonstrate a Canadian physical kitchen footprint, employed personnel, genuine order-fulfilment activity, and real risk-bearing to withstand a sham/GAAR challenge; the CRA will attack a 'warehoused IP' structure if the Canadian LP's functions are performed without substance through a zero-employee foreign brand owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements (delivery-revenue suppression)

Statutory sanction: Civil: gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (ITA s. 163(2)); interest accrual at the CRA prescribed rate (compound daily). Criminal (ITA s. 239(1)): summary conviction — fine from 50% to 200% of evaded tax and/or up to 2 years' imprisonment; indictment — fine from 100% to 200% and/or up to 5 years' imprisonment plus forfeiture of proceeds (Criminal Code s. 462.37).

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source and Part XIII withholdings (ITA s. 227.1; ETA s. 323 for GST/HST). On criminal conviction, directors face personal fines, confiscation orders, seizure of personal assets, travel-ban/restraint orders pending trial (Criminal Code s. 462.33), and imprisonment; the CRA also publishes the names of convicted tax evaders and can hold the GP personally liable for partnership debts through sham/agency veil-piercing.
Shell Entity / Absence of Economic Substance / GAAR

Statutory sanction: Full reassessment under GAAR (s. 245): 100% of the tax benefit denied, interest and (where culpable conduct) 50% gross-negligence penalties; double-count penalties for failure to file documentation; denial of limited-partner losses and at-risk amount. For cross-border cases, a 10% transfer-pricing penalty is added where the adjustment exceeds CA$5 million or 10% of gross revenue (s. 247(9)).

Director personal liability: The corporate GP's directors may be assessed personally on the partnership's non-deductible/recharacterized income if distributions to the GP were made in lieu of a lawful return; veil-piercing applies where the corporate GP is a façade or the LP agreement creates an agency; directors can be named in CRA collection actions for withholding debts and barred from cross-border travel while tax debts remain certified.
Withholding non-compliance on profit repatriation (Part XIII)

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the un-withheld amount (ITA s. 227(8)) plus daily compound interest; the LP's GP becomes a debtor of the Crown for the full un-withheld tax (s. 227(1)); the non-resident partner also remains separately assessable; repeated conduct triggers criminal prosecution under s. 239 and gross-negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate GP are personally joint and severally liable for every unremitted Part XIII amount (ITA s. 227.1) — a pure no-fault liability where the GP's corporation is the responsible payer; directors are exposed to asset seizure, travel prohibitions, and bankruptcy discharge denial where the Crown proves willful blindness.
AML / FINTRAC compliance failure (low-risk but fatal)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CA$500,000 for corporations under the PCMLTFA (2024 tiering); criminal offence under Criminal Code s. 462.31 (money laundering) with imprisonment of up to 10 years; FINTRAC can compel statutory audits and freeze accounts; the CRA coordinates with FINTRAC on undisclosed foreign-source funds.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be charged personally for failure to report a suspicious transaction where they directed or acquiesced in the activity; convictions carry imprisonment up to 5 years (summary) or 10 years (indictment), personal fines up to CA$500,000, and travel-ban/restraint orders while proceedings are pending.
