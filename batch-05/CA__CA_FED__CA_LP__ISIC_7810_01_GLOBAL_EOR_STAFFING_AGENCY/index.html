Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility (IMF Article VIII). The Canadian dollar floats freely and there are no exchange controls, approval requirements, or surrender obligations on capital inflows or outflows. The Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) fixes CAD as legal tender. Profit repatriation to non-resident limited partners is unrestricted at the FX level but Part XIII withholding tax of 25% (treaty-reducible) applies to income distributions; a return of capital is not subject to Part XIII withholding. Inbound capital subscriptions require no tax or exchange-control pre-clearance; an Investment Canada Act notification may be triggered where the subscription results in acquisition of control of a Canadian business (not applicable to a newly formed LP unless thresholds are met).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender and no mandatory CAD conversion timeline; receipts may be held in foreign currency. However, under the PCMLTFA, physical cross-border movements of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must be declared to CBSA, and financial institutions must file EFT reports to FINTRAC on wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. EOR service receipts must be documented under the Income Tax Act and supported by OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation for deductible cross-border fees.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central bank and monetary authority; prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT transaction reporting to FINTRAC; cross-border currency declaration to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a secondary disbursement rail; not viable as the primary treasury/escrow bank. Canada does not recognize the EU EMI licence; non-resident digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and, under the Retail Payment Activities Act (effective 2024, supervised by the Bank of Canada), obtain a retail payment licence. MSBs may not take deposits, issue guarantees, or settle like a bank, and client funds must be safeguarded at a Schedule I bank. Wise is practical for low-value global contractor payroll payouts at mid-market FX; capital injections, CRA statutory source deductions, and institutional escrow must clear through a Canadian chartered bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal Part I general corporate income tax rate; the Canada limited partnership is fiscally transparent and the 15% rate is applied at the partner level on allocable Canadian-source business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents; treaty rates under the Canada-US treaty are 5% for affiliated companies and 15% for portfolio dividends; distributions from a Canadian limited partnership are generally taxed as Part I income at the partner level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate value of cross-border non-arm's length transactions with a related foreign party per taxation year triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation and the 10% transfer pricing penalty exposure; Country-by-Country Reporting is required for multinational groups with C$750,000,000 or more in consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate offshore Economic Substance Regulations; economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, the common law sham doctrine, and Income Tax Act s. 247(2) recharacterization powers requiring the EOR partnership to have genuine employees, premises, bank accounts, and non-resident decision-making.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Wilful False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239(1): fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; gross negligence penalty of 50% under s. 163(2); full reassessment of tax, interest, and restitution orders.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and senior officers who direct or acquiesce in the evasion are exposed to criminal prosecution as parties under Criminal Code ss. 21-22, personal fines, imprisonment, asset seizure, travel bans, and civil restitution.
Unremitted Payroll Source Deductions

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 227.1 director liability certificates for unremitted amounts; 10% penalty for failure to remit; interest at the prescribed rate compounded daily; CRA garnishment and security demands.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for all unremitted source deductions, interest, and penalties; CRA may enforce without a court order, impose asset freezes, and issue travel bans preventing departure from Canada.
Sham / Treaty-Shopping Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Invalidation of the arrangement under the sham doctrine; GAAR penalty of 25% of the tax benefit; transfer pricing reallocation under s. 247(2); reassessment at the statutory 25% withholding rate plus arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable under s. 160 for transfers to non-arm's length persons and receive court orders to repatriate funds; criminal fraud prosecution is possible when the shell is used to deceive CRA.
Anti-Money Laundering Deficiencies (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for individuals and C$1,000,000 for corporations per violation; criminal liability for money laundering offences carries imprisonment up to 10 years and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may be fined personally, banned from operating financial businesses, and subjected to civil forfeiture; willful blindness to laundering is a predicate offence.
Limited Partner Control / Loss of Limited Liability

Statutory sanction: Under each Canadian provincial Limited Partnership Act, the limited partner forfeits the limited liability shield and becomes jointly and severally liable for all partnership debts and tax obligations, including withheld payroll taxes.

Director personal liability: The offending limited partner and their corporate directors become personally liable for EOR obligations; CRA and creditors may pursue personal bank accounts, residences, and investments without the normal corporate veil protection.
