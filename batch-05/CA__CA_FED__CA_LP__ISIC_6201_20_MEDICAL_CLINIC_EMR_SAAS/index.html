Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada); national portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; digital services: My Business Account portal and GST/HST NETFILE at file.gst-hst.cra-arc.gc.ca (French equivalent: canada.ca/fr/agence-revenu).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — determine whether the non-resident LP carries on business in Canada under ETA s.123(1); SaaS B2B distributions to Canadian clinics normally constitute taxable supplies made in Canada, requiring registration. Step 2 — appoint an authorized representative/general partner with a Canadian contact address for CRA correspondence. Step 3 — obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus program accounts via the Business Registration Online (BRO) service (where the representative holds a SIN) or by submitting Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts; mark the entity type as a partnership and request the GST/HST program account (RT prefix); add payroll (RP) only if Canadian employees are hired and import/export (RM) only if physical goods cross the border. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; the Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96 — the LP is not an income tax payer; income/loss flows through to partners (residents taxed on worldwide shares; non-residents taxed on Canadian-source shares allocated to a Canadian permanent establishment); the LP is nonetheless a separate person for GST/HST under ETA s.123(1) and must register, collect, remit and claim input tax credits in its own name; no Canadian tax-exempt zone or offshore regime is available to the LP.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; transparency is statutory. Elective reliefs available: (1) ITA s.97(2) joint election by all partners for cost-basis roll-over contribution of Canadian capital property to the LP, deferring recapture and capital gains; (2) ITA s.249.1(7) election to adopt a non-calendar partnership fiscal period in prescribed circumstances; (3) ETA s.225 Quick Method election enabling the LP to remit GST/HST on a simplified rate once taxable supplies (exclusive of GST/HST) are not greater than $400,000 CAD; (4) ETA s.240(2.1) voluntary GST/HST registration election to recover input tax credits on Canadian start-up costs before revenue begins; hybrid-mismatch deductions are denied under ITA s.18.4-18.6 without any elective relief and require an annual factual self-review of the LP's foreign tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada either in a single calendar quarter or accumulated over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; the threshold applies to the LP as the recipient-facing supplier of cloud EMR subscriptions; below-threshold foreign LPs may nevertheless register voluntarily under ETA s.240(2.1) to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LP's cloud EMR SaaS export may be zero-rated: (i) when characterized as a service supplied to a non-resident recipient under Sch. VI, Part V, s.7 of the ETA, provided the supply is not made to an individual present in Canada and is not for use in Canada; or (ii) when characterized as intangible personal property under Sch. VI, Part V, s.10, provided the intangible personal property reasonably relates to goods situated outside Canada or to services performed wholly outside Canada. Practical preconditions: the SaaS agreement must be executed with a foreign parent/entity having no Canadian establishment in the contract; billing and payment must be in foreign currency from the non-resident; the LP must not permit download or installation of licensed software code in Canada; Canadian-domiciled clinics must be billed as separate taxable supplies at 5% GST (or HST where applicable). Substantiation records must be retained in the LP's CRA books and records.

  • Commercial invoice containing a zero-rated export indicator and description of the cloud service
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or Stripe settlement statement evidencing receipt of export consideration in a foreign currency within the credit period
  • Copy of the recipient's foreign tax registration identifier (e.g., US EIN, EU VAT ID) confirming non-residence

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping; e-invoices are valid GST/HST documents if retained per ETA s.286(1) in the supplier's normal electronic format; the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and the Peppol BIS are the voluntary B2B e-invoicing channel; mandatory electronic filing (NETFILE or GST/HST Returns Digital service) applies to registrants above the CRA prescribed revenue threshold; no invoice pre-authorization exists. The LP must issue full GST/HST invoices (ETA s.169(2): supplier name and BN, recipient, date, description, tax charged) to Canadian clinics; simplified invoicing is not available for taxable supplies.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated export retroactively within the normal 4-year limitation window, with no limitation period for fraud or misrepresentation; a failed zero-rating triggers net tax recomputation at 5% GST (or HST) with statutory interest and penalties - ETA s.285(1) gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of $250 CAD and 25% of the net tax misstated, plus ITA s.163(2) penalty for a false statement on a T5013; the LP's claimed input tax credit refund can be clawed back to nil under ETA s.296; criminal evasion under ETA s.327 carries fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; for repeat offenders, CRA applies the repeat gross negligence penalty and may publish the taxpayer's name under the CRA Name Disclosure Program.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains no statutory exchange controls, no prior approval for inward direct investment, no profit-repatriation licensing, and no ceiling on partnership distributions to non-resident limited partners. Current-account software receipts and capital-account subscriptions may be settled in CAD, USD, or any freely trading currency without Bank of Canada authorization. The only compliance encumbrances are tax-treaty withholding at source (Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, generally 25% where no treaty applies, reduced to 15% or less under the Canada-US Tax Treaty), FATCA/CRS reporting by the receiving financial institution, and FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports (EFTR) on every cross-border wire equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000. For a medical-clinic EMR SaaS exporter, neither the federal government nor the Bank of Canada imposes liquidity, convertibility, or export-license conditions on the revenue stream.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion ratio, and no time-bound repatriation timeline. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in the original foreign currency, held in multi-currency operating accounts at a Schedule I bank, or converted to CAD at market rates without reporting to the central bank. Canadian-resident taxpayers and partnerships must nevertheless observe CRA foreign-reporting thresholds: Form T1135 must be filed where the total cost of specified foreign property, including unrestricted foreign-currency bank accounts, exceeds CAD $100,000; and cross-border intercompany software-licence payments must respect the arm's-length transfer-pricing rules under sections 247 and 247.1 of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border funds movement reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High with a structural caveat: Canada does not recognize the UK/EU Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence. Cross-border digital payment platforms such as Wise Platform, Airwallex, and Payoneer may operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) and, where applicable, as Retail Payment Activity Providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with oversight by the Bank of Canada. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No exchange-control approval is required under Canadian law. All capital contributions from non-resident Limited Partners should be routed by SWIFT MT103 into the Schedule I bank's CAD or USD clearing account, using the receiving bank's purpose code for partnership capital contribution or direct investment (e.g., DI code or standard description 'Capital subscription to Limited Partnership'), with a copy of the executed subscription agreement, the LP investor's KYC pack, and the General Partner's resolution attached to the bank. The remitting investor must be named exactly as the wire originator; any third-party funded wire will trigger automatic enhanced due diligence and likely delay settlement. Every inbound wire at or above CAD $10,000 is reported by the receiving institution to FINTRAC as an EFTR. For aggregate funded capital that meets the applicable review threshold under the Investment Canada Act (2025: approximately CAD $1.363 billion enterprise value for direct acquisitions by WTO investors; CAD $5 million to CAD $50 million asset-based bands for non-WTO investors), a post-closing notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada is required; a portfolio-limited-partnership exemption applies where no control of a Canadian business is acquired.The principal clearing rail for international vendor and licensor payments is SWIFT MT103 through the bank's correspondent network, with USD settlements cleared via a New York Fed/CHIPS correspondent and EUR settlements via Frankfurt or London clearers. Domestic high-value CAD disbursements settle in real time on Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS), while recurring domestic supplier and clinic-partner payouts below CAD $1 million settle via EFT credits on the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). Commercial letters of credit (UCP600) are limited to material hardware or sub-licensing arrangements; for standard EMR SaaS expenditures (cloud hosting, AI subprocessors, telecom, and clinical support), direct book transfers and Stripe Billing inward-collection API settlement into the operating account are fully compliant. All outbound wires at or above CAD $10,000 require FINTRAC EFTR and must reference the underlying invoice or contract number in the SWIFT remittance-information (RFB) field.Plain-vanilla deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (USD/CAD, EUR/CAD) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the Canadian FX Master Agreement with the Schedule I dealing bankFX swaps and cross-currency swaps used to align USD-denominated EMR subscription receivables with CAD operating liabilities and periodic GP/LP cash distributionsVanilla FX options (calls and puts) for optional USD/CAD hedging of material committed overseas collections without locking in adverse forward ratesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) legally permissible for exotic or restricted-deliverability currency exposures (rarely required for the freely convertible USD/CAD pair)Natural-currency offset via multi-currency operating accounts, whereby USD inbound subscription fees are retained to pay USD cloud-infrastructure and international subcontractor payables, reducing gross FX conversion volume

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High with a structural caveat: Canada does not recognize the UK/EU Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence. Cross-border digital payment platforms such as Wise Platform, Airwallex, and Payoneer may operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) and, where applicable, as Retail Payment Activity Providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with oversight by the Bank of Canada. They are viable as overlay rails for low-value clinic subscription collections and international vendor payouts, but they cannot lawfully accept cad deposits in escrow, issue commercial bank Account-level guarantees, or provide credit. The Limited Partnership must maintain its true capital-escrow vault and principal clearing relationship at an OSFI-regulated Schedule I bank, with the fintech layer engaged solely as a payment facilitator. Stripe Billing is accepted by Canadian banks as an approved payment aggregator, provided the operating account receives settlement funds net of processor fees.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for transactions with non-residents (Local File required); Master File required if total revenue exceeds CAD 50 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAP) and substance-over-form principles apply. Partnerships must demonstrate genuine economic substance to avoid recharacterization of transactions.

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 50% of the tax evaded, plus interest; criminal fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to withhold or remit; travel bans and asset freezes may apply.
