Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada) for the CBCA corporate general partner and the federal Business Number; Companies and Personal Property Security Branch of ServiceOntario for the provincial Limited Partnership registry; Canada Revenue Agency for tax enrolments.

Governing statute

Canada has no federal limited partnership statute; the Limited Partnership is formed under the representative Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.8, ss. 2, 3, 9, 13, 25 ('OLPA'). The corporate general partner is governed by the Canada Business Corporations Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-44, ss. 5, 6, 21, 106 ('CBCA'). Pass-through taxation is sourced to the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985 (5th Supp.), c. 1, s. 96, and the limited partners' liability shield to OLPA s. 9.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation under CBCA s. 6 for the general partner; Limited Partnership Declaration (Form 3) under OLPA s. 3 for the Limited Partnership; Business Registration (BN, GST/HST, Import/Export Account) through the CRA Business Registration Online portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 44 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under either the CBCA or OLPA; limited partners may contribute cash or property and must not withdraw a contribution if it would render the partnership unable to pay its debts (OLPA s. 13). No escrow account is statutorily required at formation, but prudent practice holds initial capital contributions in a Canadian operating account until the Certificate of Limited Partnership is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire clearance under the National Fire Code of Canada and the applicable provincial fire code is required for any warehouse or office premises: fire extinguisher placement, exit signage, emergency lighting, and an approved fire safety plan. Where mechanical handling or racking is used, a structural load inspection and a workplace OHSA inspection at the provincial occupational health authority are completed before occupation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under either the CBCA or OLPA; limited partners may contribute cash or property and must not withdraw a contribution if it would render the partnership unable to pay its debts (OLPA s. 13). No escrow account is statutorily required at formation, but prudent practice holds initial capital contributions in a Canadian operating account until the Certificate of Limited Partnership is issued.

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

No check-the-box elections; Limited Partnerships are statutorily classified as pass-through entities. Hybrid mismatch election rules apply under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and transfer pricing provisions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods where the goods are exported from Canada and not consumed therein. Eligibility requires proof of export and compliance with the Excise Tax Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Registrants must issue tax invoices and file GST/HST returns electronically through the CRA's My Business Account. Real-time digital stamping is not required; however, electronic filing of returns is mandatory.

Non-compliance may lead to GST reassessment, penalties of up to 50% of the unreported tax, and interest charges. Fraudulent claims result in criminal prosecution and full clawback of zero-rated amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no Canadian exchange controls restricting the conversion of CAD into foreign currency, nor any requirement for central-bank approval of remittances. Dividend, partnership-distribution, interest, royalty and trade payments may be remitted freely, subject only to normal Income Tax Act withholding taxes (e.g., Part XIII non-resident withholding) and anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds. The Department of Finance does not operate a surrender or conversion regime affecting a Canadian Limited Partnership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts into CAD. A Canadian Limited Partnership selling electronic components abroad may retain foreign-currency balances in Canadian Schedule I bank accounts and may convert such balances at commercial exchange rates, provided the underlying transactions are accurately recorded for customs, GST/HST and income tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); general foreign exchange policy resides with the Department of Finance Canada, while OSFI and FINTRAC supervise banking and anti-money laundering compliance. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration comparable to exchange-control authorities in emerging markets.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium as a primary or sole banking rail. A foreign electronic-money institution or digital EMI is generally not permitted to carry on deposit-taking business in Canada without an order under the Bank Act or a foreign bank branch authorization. A Canadian Limited Partnership may use a domestically regulated fintech or payment service provider for segregated funds and payment initiation, provided the provider holds a money-services-business registration with FINTRAC and maintains accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank, but the DSP (delivery of payment services) does not replace a full Canadian corporate banking relationship for capital escrow, credit lines or cross-border LC issuance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound contribution wires must be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian LP’s operating or escrow account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) should clearly state 'Capital contribution to Canadian Limited Partnership — CA__CA_FED__CA_LP' together with the capital-call reference and the contributing partner’s name and entity identifier. Field 71A should be 'OUR' where fee transparency is required. For CAD-denominated injections, the receiving Canadian bank may clear over Payments Canada’s Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); for USD injections, the clearing rail will typically be the receiving bank’s American correspondent network. The transaction must not be labelled as a trade settlement when in substance it is an equity or partnership capital injection. Large contributions exceeding CAD 10,000 are subject to normal FINTRAC reporting, and Canadian banks may request copies of the capital call and the underlying partnership agreement.Outbound trade disbursements for electronic component purchases are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 messages for commercial value transfers, sent from the Canadian Schedule I bank to the beneficiary’s bank in the supplier jurisdiction. For large or recurring letter-of-credit transactions, the Canadian bank may issue a commercial documentary LC (UCP600) or a standby LC, ensuring compliant presentation through a confirming foreign bank. CAD-denominated payments can use Payments Canada LVTS wires for same-day finality; USD-denominated payments may use correspondent clearing, ABP (Automated Clearing Payments) or Fedwire depending on the Canadian bank’s correspondent.
