Virtual office addresses are not generally permissible as a registered office for a Canadian federal corporation or a provincial limited partnership. The registered office must be a physical address in Canada where the partnership records are maintained and where legal process can be served. A virtual office service that provides a physical street address, receives mail, and permits personal service during business hours may satisfy the requirement in most provinces, but a generic virtual mailbox or non-physical address will be rejected. The registered office must be within the province of registration and must be accessible to the registrar.

Competent corporate registry

Provincial Limited Partnership Registrar of the constituent province (e.g., Ontario Companies and Personal Property Branch, Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery; British Columbia Corporate Registry); federal counterpart for tax registration and Business Number issuance: Canada Revenue Agency, with Corporations Canada/ISED providing the integrated federal registration window.

Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Act of the constituent province (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.2, ss. 2(1), 9; British Columbia Partnership Act, RSBC 1996, c. 348, Part 4). Canada maintains no federal limited partnership registry; the federal dimension is administered through the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), s. 96 (pass-through taxation) and the Impact Assessment Act, S.C. 2019, c. 28 (EIA screening not triggered for digital platforms).

Formation / constitutional filings

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); national portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency (My Business Account: canada.ca/my-cra-account)

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 - Determine residence: a Canadian limited partnership registered in a province (e.g., Ontario) is generally considered resident in Canada for GST/HST and income tax purposes. STEP 2 - Apply online through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) platform (canada.ca/business-registration-online) to obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts. STEP 3 - Register the partnership as a single GST/HST registrant in the partnership's name (BN + 'RT' program account); the partnership is treated as one GST/HST registrant and is not required to be registered separately from its partners. STEP 4 - If the LP has no permanent establishment in Canada but carries on business through a partnership, the non-resident partners' allocable income is reported via the partnership's T5013 Schedule 50 and the partnership must provide Form T5013. STEP 5 - Register for CRA's payroll program account (BN + 'RP') if the LP has employees, and for the Export Reporting System (via the Canadian Export Reporting System - CERS portal) for mandatory customs reporting. STEP 6 - Document the authorized signatory with a completed Form RC59 (Business Consent) or authorization via Represent a Client, with supporting notarized resolutions for non-resident general partners. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under section 96(1) of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c.1, 5th Supp.), an LP is not a taxpayer; all income, losses, and capital items are computed at the partnership level as if the LP were a distinct person, then allocated to each partner (including non-resident partners) in accordance with the limited partnership agreement; the LP itself is not liable for Part I income tax, and residency of the LP for classification purposes is determined by the 'management and control' test applied to the entity; a Canadian LP with provincial registration is treated as a Canadian partnership for the domestic GAAR and hybrid mismatch rules.

Available election forms

1) No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Canada; partnership status is determined by law and fact, and an LP is mandatorily pass-through. 2) Hybrid mismatch rules: sections 18.2, 18.3, 18.4 and 18.6 of the Income Tax Act (applicable from taxation years beginning after December 31, 2022) require certain hybrid-entity and hybrid-instrument payments to be re-characterized; a Canadian LP should file a 'Hybrid Mismatch Designation' (Form T1130 - no separate CRA form; include a written designation with the T5013 return) where the LP has non-resident partners classified as disregarded for foreign tax purposes. 3) Section 96(3) - election for discretionary allocation of income to non-resident partners of an 'electing Canadian partnership' (where one or more partners are non-residents) - available to avoid the default pro-rata allocation. 4) Section 261 functional currency election - permits the LP to compute income in USD (relevant given USD-denominated platform fees), subject to CRA approval of the requested tax year. 5) GST/HST election under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act - permits the LP and any incorporated seller-distributor in the same group to treat inter-party supplies as made for nil consideration, reducing compliance friction. 6) Section 216(1) election for non-resident partners receiving Canadian-source rental or timber income (not applicable to software platform fees).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the 'small supplier' threshold in section 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act is exceeded when global taxable supplies in either a single calendar quarter exceed CAD 30,000 or the sum over the prior 4 consecutive calendar quarters exceeds CAD 30,000; once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory within 29 days under subsection 240(1); for a B2B marketplace platform billing an established stream of subscription and commission fees to sellers, registration is effectively mandatory from the first month of operating revenues; note that the platform is a separate registrant from its seller-users, and the LP's supplies of platform services to Canadian sellers are subject to GST/HST even if the underlying goods cross borders

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies: (1) for goods - Part V, section 1 zero-rates goods exported directly by the supplier, requiring evidence of exportation within prescribed CRA timeframes; (2) for services - Part V, section 7 zero-rates a supply of a service made to a non-resident, unless the service is supplied to an agent/employee of the non-resident in Canada, relates to real property situated in Canada, relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is a telecommunication service; (3) the B2B marketplace platform's commission, listing, and payment-processing facilitation services to non-resident sellers qualify for zero-rating under section 7 where the seller's business is outside Canada and the service is not tied to Canadian-located inventory; (4) platform services supplied to Canadian-registered sellers are taxable at the prevailing HST/GST rate and cannot be zero-rated merely because the sellers sell to foreign buyers; (5) for B2B cross-border goods facilitation, CRA administrative policy (GST/HST Memorandum 4.5.1) requires the LP to maintain 'contemporaneous export evidence': a shipping document from the carrier, customs export declaration, and proof of payment in foreign currency; the documentary retention period is 6 years under subsection 286(1) of the Excise Tax Act.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (filed through the Canada Export Reporting System (CERS) portal) for goods valued above CAD 2,000 or controlled/prohibited goods; for goods below CAD 2,000, an NDA or commercial invoice suffices; must include the specifics of the destination country, HS classification, value (CAD equivalent), and shipper/exporter BN
  • Commercial Invoice (platform-generated, showing seller identity, buyer identity, HS code, Incoterms, unit values, and total consideration) which serves as the primary evidence of the export sale for both customs and CRA
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (original or electronic) evidencing shipment from Canada to the foreign destination; includes ocean bill of lading with port of loading/discharge and the house bill for consolidators
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or equivalent SWIFT MT103 / MT999 proof issued by the Canadian clearing bank (e.g., RBC, TD) evidencing receipt of USD or foreign currency proceeds into the LP's Canadian USD current account, mapped to the specific invoice; required by the CRA in the course of an export-verification audit to substantiate the commercial reality of the foreign sale
  • Non-resident documentation pack: executed seller onboarding contracts, W1/Ben103 forms (non-resident seller information), KYB records confirming the seller's permanent establishment outside Canada, and any Incoterms confirmation for determining the place of title transfer
  • GST/HST zero-rated election statement: internal worksheet per subsection 169(4) and section 7 of Schedule VI documenting the basis for zero-rating each invoice (service-to-non-resident test or goods-export test)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada for B2B transactions. The CRA accepts electronic invoices and EDI records as 'books and records' under section 286 of the Excise Tax Act provided they are in an auditable, retrievable format. Canada has adopted the PEPPOL-based Pan-Canadian B2G API e-invoicing (voluntary, effective 2023-2025 for federal procurement), and the Ontario/Quebec regimes are B2G voluntary. No CRA API submission is required for individual invoices; tax returns are filed through GST/HST NETFILE or TELEFILE. Practical mandate: the LP should ensure its platform portal generates GST/HST-compliant invoices showing the BN/RT registration number, the recipient's BN, the date of supply, the nature of the supply, and the applicable HST/GST rate (5%, 13%, or 15%) based on the place-of-supply rules in sections 136.1 and 142.5-142.9 of the Excise Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility with no exchange-control licensing, approval, or repatriation restrictions. Canadian-dollar profit and distribution remittances from a Canadian Limited Partnership to non-resident partners are freely transferable without central-bank approval; the sole statutory hurdle is Part XIII withholding tax (default 25% of amounts deemed paid or credited to non-resident partners) under the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), as reduced by applicable tax treaties. Practical hurdles are limited to bank-level KYC/AML due diligence, SWIFT wire-format accuracy, and documentary evidence of source of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; foreign-currency receipts may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency deposit accounts at Schedule I banks without conversion. The Bank of Canada intervenes in markets only for its own Exchange Fund Account and never on behalf of private exporters.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with exchange administration functions vested in the Department of Finance Canada through the Exchange Fund Account under the Currency Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-52, and the Bank of Canada Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. B-9; AML/ATF supervisory authority rests with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a supplementary payout rail, not as a primary treasury anchor. Legal constraints: offshore EMI/e-money firms are not chartered Canadian banks and may not take deposits in Canada under the Bank Act, S.C. 1991, c. 46, s. 540; any entity dealing in foreign exchange or money transfer in Canada must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Offshore EMIs without a Canadian MSB registration cannot lawfully market cross-border payout services to a Canadian-resident LP. Recommended construct: Schedule I bank operating account as settlement anchor, with an MSB-registered cross-border payment platform used solely for vendor payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections to the Canadian LP must arrive via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers using the receiving bank's BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC, BOFCATTTND for BMO, NOSCCATT for Scotiabank), the LP's CAD/USD account number, and the LP's legal name exactly as registered. Remittance Information (field 70) should state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / EQUITY INJECTION' and reference the registered capital account ledger entry of the LP. No central-bank approval is required, but the receiving bank may request a capital-contribution resolution of the General Partner, updated LP register, and source-of-funds evidence for amounts above approximately CAD 100,000. Wires are verified under the bank's large-transaction monitoring and reconciled to the LP's capital accounts; funds routed through Alibaba_Pay affiliates must be pre-cleared with the Relationship Manager to avoid intermediated-rail flags.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for cross-border vendor and supplier payments, with SWIFT GPI tracking for end-to-end transparency; interbank funding legs use MT202/MT205. Domestic high-value CAD settlements clear through Payments Canada Lynx (RTGS); low-value batch payments use ACSS/ACH. USD disbursements settle via the Canadian bank's US correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS). Commercial Letters of Credit are issued by the Canadian bank via SWIFT MT700 and advised/confirmed under UCP600 rules, with MT707 amendments; sight drafts under 'payment at sight' LC terms constitute a compliant trade settlement rail. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium as a supplementary payout rail, not as a primary treasury anchor. Legal constraints: offshore EMI/e-money firms are not chartered Canadian banks and may not take deposits in Canada under the Bank Act, S.C. 1991, c. 46, s. 540; any entity dealing in foreign exchange or money transfer in Canada must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Offshore EMIs without a Canadian MSB registration cannot lawfully market cross-border payout services to a Canadian-resident LP. Recommended construct: Schedule I bank operating account as settlement anchor, with an MSB-registered cross-border payment platform used solely for vendor payouts.
