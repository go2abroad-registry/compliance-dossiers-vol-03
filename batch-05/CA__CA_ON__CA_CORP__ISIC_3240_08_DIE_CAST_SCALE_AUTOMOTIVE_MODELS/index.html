Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax authority; My Business Account portal at canada.ca/cra-business. Ontario corporate income tax and Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) are administered by CRA under a single administration agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance; no separate Ontario provincial tax identity is issued for income tax or HST.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada registers through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or on paper Form RC1, obtaining a 9-digit Business Number (BN). Program accounts are activated under the BN: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll withholding). Ontario requires no separate provincial TIN; Ontario corporate tax and HST are administered and collected by CRA. A non-resident corporation with a physical establishment in Ontario must also complete extra-provincial registration under the Ontario Business Corporations Act, and must register for GST/HST once it makes more than CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in Canada in a trailing four-calendar-quarter period, or earlier if it solicits orders in Canada. A federally incorporated entity is separately registered with Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada).

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' pass-through election exists under the Income Tax Act; Canadian corporate classification follows legal form and residence. Available elections include: ITA s. 85(1) tax-deferred rollover election on incorporation or transfer of property; ITA s. 186 election mechanisms regarding intercorporate dividends; ITA s. 256 associated-corporation status adjustments; GST/HST s. 156 election between closely related corporations; and hybrid mismatch restrictions under ITA s. 18.4 and s. 18.5, which limit deduction/non-inclusion outcomes in cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada during the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148). Registration becomes mandatory once exceeded, including for non-resident corporations that solicit orders in Canada through a Canadian establishment. Non-resident providers of digital services are subject to a separate mandatory simplified registration regime effective July 1, 2021, which can override the small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property (die-cast scale models) exported by the supplier, or delivered to a common carrier for shipment to a destination outside Canada, is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1, provided the supplier obtains and retains satisfactory documentary evidence of export. Services rendered to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 unless the service falls within an excluded category (e.g., services performed in relation to tangible personal property located in Canada or services supplied to a person while present in Canada). Export revenues are excluded from the small-supplier threshold calculation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA) — Form B13A or electronic export declaration containing the Business Number and HS classification; generally required where the export value exceeds CAD 2,000 or for controlled goods
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, or road) or courier consignment waybill evidencing physical movement of the goods to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice or equivalent evidence of receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice containing CRA-prescribed elements (supplier's BN, transaction description, quantities, and values)
  • Packing list and certificate of origin supporting the export declaration

Canada has no national real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate for GST/HST. Invoices must meet the ETA s. 223 content requirements (supplier's BN, date, description of supply, amount of GST/HST charged or payable), and taxable suppliers must issue invoices no later than 15 days after the end of the reporting period in which the supply is made. Electronic invoices are valid if the content and legibility satisfy CRA requirements; books and records must be retained for six years. CRA supports voluntary Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoice exchange for federal procurement, but no API clearance gateway is imposed on taxpayers. Customs exports are reported through CBSA eManifest/Single Window, not through an invoicing clearance system.

CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period where misrepresentation or fraud is established, retroactively denying zero-rating treatment and recovering unremitted GST/HST together with prescribed interest. Civil penalties include 8% of net tax under ETA s. 285 and a gross negligence penalty of 25% of the amount assessed under ETA s. 285.1. Criminal tax evasion carry fines from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years (ETA s. 327; ITA s. 239). The General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245; ETA s. 274) may also apply to artificial export arrangements. CRA export verification programs require full documentary substantiation at the time of audit; absent adequate evidence, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 13% HST with penalties and interest accrued from the original filing date.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII; there are no capital controls, no profit-remittance approval, and no repatriation restriction for dividends, royalties, interest, or share-capital reductions. Current-account and capital-account transactions are both liberalized; CAD-to-foreign-currency conversion is unrestricted and requires no central-bank certificate or purpose code. The sole operative friction is tax-side: remittances of dividends, interest, and royalties to non-residents attract Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US Treaty and other applicable DTCs). AML-side frictions: FINTRAC international EFT reporting at CAD 10,000+, record-keeping, and correspondent-bank sanctions screening for USD wires cleared through the United States (OFAC).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists for Canadian exporters. There is no mandatory conversion timeline, no minimum conversion ratio (ratio = 0%), and exporters may freely hold CAD or foreign currency in domestic or overseas accounts. Applicable residual obligations only: (i) FINTRAC reporting of any international EFT at or above CAD 10,000; (ii) GST/HST zero-rating of exported die-cast scale models (Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act) with supporting commercial invoices and bills of lading retained; (iii) CBSA export declaration and trade-data reporting under the Customs Act where thresholds apply; (iv) corporate income-tax recognition of receipts under normal tax-accounting rules. No timeline-based conversion or repatriation is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central bank and operator of the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Real-Time Rail (RTR) framework; policy supervision by Department of Finance Canada; cross-border funds monitoring authority: Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); prudential regulation of clearing banks: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada has no separate Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange-control statute; the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) govern the regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High as an ancillary collection rail but insufficient as the treasury spine. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity or shareholder-loan capital is injected via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent/shareholder bank (Hong Kong or US) to the Canadian operating CAD account. Canada maintains no BOP purpose-code regime; the wire field 70 should state Equity Subscription or Shareholder Loan for bank file documentation. USD-denominated inflows route through the Canadian bank's US correspondent (RBC New York, TD Bank US, BMO Harris) via Fedwire/CHIPS, followed by a spot USD/CAD conversion credited through Payments Canada LVTS; CAD-denominated inflows credit directly via LVTS. FINTRAC international EFT reporting applies at CAD 10,000 or more; OFAC and consolidated sanctions screening is automatic at the receiving bank. For shareholder loans, an executed arm's-length promissory note is required to sustain CRA interest deductibility, observing the thin-capitalization debt-to-equity limitation of 1.5:1 under Income Tax Act s. 18(4) relative to non-resident shareholdings. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High as an ancillary collection rail but insufficient as the treasury spine. PayPal (an explicit target payment rail), Wise, and Stripe operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) and may lawfully collect card and e-commerce receipts, convert FX, and remit CAD to the operating account; however, under the Bank Act only Schedule I/II banks and authorized foreign banks may take deposits. Consequently, EMI/MSB balances are not CDIC-insured deposits, are typically pooled at third-party banks, and may be subject to merchant holdback or reserve cycles. For SWIFT MT103 issuance, commercial LCs, payroll, import financing, and sanctions-graded USD clearing, a Schedule I commercial-bank relationship is mandatory. Recommended architecture: Schedule I bank as sole clearing and treasury spine; PayPal as the customer-facing collection node with nightly sweep to the operating account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File required if total value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million in a fiscal year; Master File also required if consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions (e.g., OECD BEPS) require genuine economic substance for transactions to be respected; shell companies without real activities risk recharacterization and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties up to 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes withheld (e.g., payroll) under s.227.1; travel bans and asset freezes possible under criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard entity under GAAR; treaty benefits denied; reassessment with penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in sham arrangements; travel bans and extradition risk.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income; penalty of 10% of net adjustment (if understatement exceeds CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue); interest on reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors liable for penalties if they directed or acquiesced to TP non-compliance; potential criminal liability for gross negligence.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations; criminal penalties for money laundering up to CAD 2 million and 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned for up to 5 years if they knowingly fail to comply with Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.
