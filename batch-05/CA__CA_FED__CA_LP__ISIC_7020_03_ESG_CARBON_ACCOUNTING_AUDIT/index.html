Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), administering the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act; registration and filing portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration, with GST/HST NETFILE for indirect tax returns; provincial extra-provincial registration may also be required by the applicable provincial registry (e.g., Ontario Business Registry).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian Limited Partnership must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO), by telephone, or by mailing Form RC1; open a GST/HST (RT) program account where taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, or voluntarily to recover input tax credits; open a payroll (RP) program account if employees are engaged; file an annual T5013 Partnership Information Return; and non-resident partners lacking a Canadian SIN/BN must apply for an Individual Tax Number (ITN) via Form T1261 in order to file Canadian returns, claim treaty benefits, or remit Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source allocations. No statutory occupational license is required for ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541620 carbon footprint auditing or offset brokerage.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - fiscally transparent for Canadian federal income tax: the Limited Partnership is not a taxpayer for Part I tax purposes and its computed income or loss is allocated to, and taxed in the hands of, resident and non-resident partners; the partnership is, however, a separate person for GST/HST purposes and must itself register and account for tax on taxable supplies; provincial flow-through treatment generally follows the federal allocation but may be adjusted by provincial partnership and notional allocation rules.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists in Canada. Material elections relevant to this LP include: (i) subsection 216(1) Income Tax Act election for a non-resident partner to file a net-basis Part I return instead of bearing Part XIII gross-basis withholding on Canadian-source allocations; (ii) section 156 Excise Tax Act election between closely related persons to treat eligible taxable supplies as being made for nil consideration; and (iii) mandatory self-designations and disclosure elections under Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4, 18.5 and 18.6) for foreign entity and instrument mismatches consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

30,000 CAD (small-supplier exemption threshold based on annual worldwide taxable supplies; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available for non-residents to claim input tax credits; carbon accounting and offset brokerage services are generally subject to 5% GST or participation-province HST at the applicable rate and may be zero-rated for non-resident recipients).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated as tangible personal property exported from Canada (Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V). Exported services, including carbon footprint auditing, ESG advisory, and offset brokerage supplied to a non-resident, are zero-rated under section 7 of Schedule VI, Part V where the service is not, in substance, performed in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property and the recipient is a non-resident; the supplier must demonstrate that the benefit of the service accrues outside Canada. A complete paper trail must evidence the non-resident status of the foreign client and the foreign place of benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Coding Form B13A or automated export declaration through the Canadian Export Reporting System/CAED)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document issued by the carrier
  • Commercial Invoice and, where applicable, export permit or certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment confirmation or SWIFT wire record evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Non-resident status evidence for the recipient (certificate of residency, foreign TIN/VAT registration, or signed declaration)
  • Signed contract or statement of work identifying the place of supply and place of benefit for the exported service

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or government pre-clearance of invoices exists in Canada. CRA requires registrants above prescribed thresholds to file GST/HST returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE); invoices and underlying records must be retained in electronic or paper form and produced to the CRA on demand.

CRA may audit export evidence and, if zero-rating is denied, reassess GST/HST at the applicable 5% or HST rate with arrears interest and penalties. Misstatements, gross negligence, or fraudulent charges carry a 50% gross-negligence penalty (Excise Tax Act s. 285.1 and Income Tax Act s. 163.2, as applicable), and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Criminal Code is possible. The standard GST/HST reassessment period for a registrant is four years from the filing due date, which is extended in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud; Canada does not impose a statutory 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline, but the 180-day benchmark is used in practice for FX repatriation substantiation and transfer-pricing documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency under a floating exchange rate regime. No distinction is made between capital and current account convertibility for most purposes; however, transactions are subject to reporting thresholds and sanctions screening under the United Nations Act and the Special Economic Measures Act. Profit remittances by a limited partnership to non-resident partners are generally free from withholding tax if the partners are residents of a treaty country and the partnership is treated as fiscally transparent, but must be supported by a valid Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Form NR301 or similar treaty-based exemption documentation. In-bound and out-bound capital movements are unrestricted except for transactions with sanctioned jurisdictions or entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Canada or any designated agent. Exporters may retain foreign currency in Canadian dollar or foreign currency bank accounts at domestic or authorized foreign banks. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline for repatriation. However, cross-border movements of currency or monetary instruments including wire transfers exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, and trade-based money laundering risk must be addressed through appropriate transaction monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML surveillance; the Bank of Canada does not administer exchange controls, while the Minister of Finance and the Canada Border Services Agency oversee certain cross-border reporting requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium with significant legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI or electronic money institutions are not licensed by the Bank of Canada or the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and cannot provide deposit-taking services in Canada without a federal bank license or being registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC. They may provide cross-border payment facilitation or virtual payment card services through a Canadian-registered MSB arrangement, but custody of client funds and clearing through the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) is not directly accessible. Use of unregulated offshore EMIs will trigger enhanced due diligence and may be rejected by audit clients as an unacceptable cash management rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident partners into the Canadian limited partnership must be received by wire transfer using the society for worldwide interbank financial telecommunication (SWIFT) MT103 message. The beneficiary bank account must be a Tier-1 Canadian bank account opened in the partnership's legal name, with the CBCA or provincial extra-provincial registration number and CRA business number included in the beneficiary field. The remitter should use payment purpose code 'GSTR' (general transfer of capital) or, depending on bank instruction, the ISO 20022 purpose code 'TRAN' (transfer of cash collateral) or 'ADVD' (advance). If a partner is repatriating previous profits or a loan, the code should be 'LOAN' or 'INTR'. The bank will require the remitter to provide a 'wire transfer instruction letter' signed by the general partner, documenting the economic substance and expected source of funds. All inbound wires exceeding CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting; the bank automatically generates the report, but the partnership must maintain the supporting documentary evidence. A receipt confirmation and the bank's credit advice should be filed with the corporate ledger.For trade settlements related to carbon credit purchases or brokerage disbursements, the preferred clearing rail is an international wire transfer in Canadian dollars or US dollars both executed through SWIFT MT103, but where the counterparty requires documentary mitigation, a commercial letter of credit (LC) may be used with the Canadian bank as issuing bank. The outbound instruction must include the CRA business number, the tax registration number or VAT/GST number of the counterparty, and an explicit transaction description matching the underlying carbon registry reference. The payment should be cleared via the Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for domestic same-day settlement, or via SWIFT with a network correspondent for foreign-currency payments. The partnership should not use personal accounts for trade disbursements; all payments must originate from the partnership's operational account and be governed by a dual-signatory authorization matrix. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax rate for general corporations; 9% for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPC) on active business income up to CAD 500,000; a limited partnership is flow-through and generally not a taxable entity at the federal level

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII for dividends paid to non-resident shareholders of a Canadian corporation. A Limited Partnership is flow-through: non-resident partners are taxed under Part I on allocated Canadian business income and distributions of capital are generally not subject to Part XIII withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File/Master File threshold is imposed by Canada for all taxpayers; however, Form T106 (Schedule 89) must be filed when aggregate non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1 million. Detailed contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected under CRA guidance IC 87-2R where any such transactions occur, and the OECD country-by-country reporting threshold applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million (approximately CAD 1.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no general Economic Substance Regulations like offshore jurisdictions, but substance is central to the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245) and to treaty entitlement under the OECD BEPS Principal Purpose Test. A limited partnership must demonstrate real personnel, decision-making, expenditure, and risk in Canada; hollow shell approaches are reassessed as GST/HST fraud, sham arrangements, or GAAR abuse.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Section 239(1) of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to two years on first offence; gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and partnership managers can be jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for unremitted withholdings and other statutory amounts; criminal conviction may trigger civil restitution, travel bans, restrictions on serving as a director, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under the sham doctrine and GAAR (s. 245), denial of all tax benefits, recapture of losses, gross negligence penalties equal to 50% of the understated tax, and potentially proceedings under s. 163.1 for indexing to the tax amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve distributions while tax liabilities are outstanding become personally liable for the taxes withheld that were not remitted; injunctive relief and asset freezes are available to the CRA, and directors may be barred from leaving Canada after a criminal indictment.
AML/ATF Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations, CAD 500,000 for individuals; Criminal Code s. 462.31 money laundering conviction carries imprisonment up to 10 years and heavy fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face personal fines and imprisonment if they authorised or knowingly allowed the AML breaches; FINTRAC can issue compliance orders identifying responsible individuals, and travel suspensions or accommodations may be imposed as part of criminal bail or extradition procedures.
