Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal GST/HST is administered under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (ETA). Primary registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) via canada.ca/business-registration; non-resident digital suppliers use the CRA simplified non-resident GST/HST registration procedure. Provincial tax authorities (e.g., Revenu Quebec for QST and BC PST authorities) are separate and do not govern federal GST/HST registration.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership obtains one Business Number (BN) from the CRA as its statutory tax registry identifier. The general partner or authorized agent files Form RC1 or registers through Business Registration Online; the partnership must open a GST/HST program account (RT suffix) when its worldwide taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, or when, as a non-resident digital supplier, it makes taxable supplies of digital goods or services to Canadian-resident consumers under the July 2021 digital economy measures. Because the LP has no employees initially, no payroll (RP) account is needed unless it becomes an employer. The LP must file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually using the BN. A foreign limited partnership with no Canadian physical presence may still register directly with CRA using the simplified non-resident GST/HST procedure (Form GST134/RC1-VS), receiving a BN and RT account within one to five business days; CRA issues tax IDs at no fee.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under the Income Tax Act, a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent: the partnership computes income/loss at the partnership level and each partner is separately taxable on its allocated share, including non-resident partners subject to Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source allocations unless treaty relief applies. For GST/HST purposes, however, the ETA treats the partnership as a distinct 'person' and the partnership itself is the registrant and collector of GST/HST. The voice-streaming app is supplied by the partnership, not by the individual partners, for indirect tax purposes.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; entity classification follows legal form and CRA administrative positions. Relevant mechanisms include: ITA s. 102 (deemed Canadian partnership and 'Canadian partnership' status for non-resident partners); ITA s. 216 election by a non-resident partner to file on a net basis; ITA s. 96(3) partnership year-end election; ETA s. 86(2) designation of a partner as partnership representative for GST/HST; ETA s. 92 election for financial institution supplies; and hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 18.2/18.21 which operate by self-executing denial without elective opt-out. A non-resident GST/HST registrant may elect simplified registration (Form GST134) rather than appointing a Canadian agent under ETA s. 267. No additional DOTAS-style disclosure regime applies beyond the mandatory tax-planning disclosure rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000: a person is a small supplier and exempt from mandatory GST/HST registration if its worldwide taxable supplies do not exceed C$30,000 in a calendar quarter and C$30,000 over the four preceding calendar quarters. Non-resident digital-economy suppliers supplying digital services or intangible property to Canadian-resident consumers are subject to the same small-supplier threshold; once exceeded, registration and GST/HST collection are mandatory from the first supply.

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no government API filing of invoices, and no digital stamping or XML clearance akin to the SdI/CFDI regimes. Registrants must issue and retain valid GST/HST invoices containing the supplier's BN/GST/HST registration number, invoice date, recipient name and address (for invoices over C$150), description of the supply, total consideration, and GST/HST rate and amount. Electronic invoices and digital records are accepted if complete, legible, and retained within Canada or electronically accessible from Canada under ETA s. 286. Mandatory electronic return filing applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies above C$1.5M (GST/HST NETFILE or GST34 for non-residents), and large businesses with taxable supplies above C$10M must remit electronically.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada maintains IMF Article VIII status and imposes no foreign exchange control system restricting the purchase, sale, or repatriation of funds. Profit remittance for a limited partnership is treated as partnership distribution and, absent a rule of substance that recharacterizes the payment as fixed rate interest or royalty, is not subject to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII for a non-resident partner. Banks will nonetheless require a completed Canada Non-Resident Declaration and a partnership allocation statement evidencing the distributable profit share.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or to convert receipts into CAD within a prescribed timeline. Exporters and platform treasury operators must, however, comply with the FINTRAC Cross-Border Currency Flow Transparency Program: every electronic fund transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, whether inbound or outbound, must be reported to FINTRAC, and sanctions screening obligations apply separately under Canada's Economic Sanctions Administration rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); with foreign exchange oversight coordination by the Department of Finance Canada and cross-border transaction monitoring by FINTRAC

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. An offshore electronic money institution (EMI) cannot carry on deposit-taking retail business in Canada without a federal banking licence, but it may operate as a money services business (MSB) after FINTRAC registration and may use BaaS partnership with a licensed Canadian clearing bank to route API-initiated payments. The viability increases when the EMI provides only payment initiation, wallet settlement, or treasury visibility services while the actual float remains in a segregated Canadian bank account.
