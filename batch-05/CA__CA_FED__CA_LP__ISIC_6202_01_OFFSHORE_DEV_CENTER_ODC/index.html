Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - Business Registration Online (BRO) portal, 'Register my business' service, and GST/HST NETFILE electronic filing system; GST/HST account administered under the federal Business Number (BN) program.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Apply for a federal Business Number (BN) via CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or the Business Registration Online portal; foreign applicants must designate a Canadian contact/agent and submit a certified copy of the foreign entity's certificate of status, the complete limited partnership agreement, identification of the general partner(s), and the foreign tax identification number. Step 2 - Open an RT (GST/HST) program account under the partnership's own BN; under the Excise Tax Act a partnership is a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes, but each partner (including non-resident limited partners) is jointly and severally liable for the partnership's GST/HST obligations. Step 3 - Open an RP (payroll) account only if the ODC employs Canadian resident staff, and an RM (import/export) account only if physical goods will be imported/exported. Step 4 - Non-resident partners deriving Canadian-source business income through the LP must obtain their own BN (individual or corporate) and file Part I income tax returns (Form T2121 for individuals / T2 for corporations) reporting their allocable share of LP income; treaty-based relief is claimed through the applicable Canada income tax treaty. Step 5 - Submit the completed registration package to the CRA Non-Resident Registration unit (fax or mail); processing normally takes 4 to 8 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes under Income Tax Act s. 96; the LP itself does not pay Part I tax, and each partner is taxed on its share of Canadian-source partnership income. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is deemed a separate person and must register for, file, and remit GST/HST in its own name; the partners remain jointly and severally liable for the partnership's indirect tax obligations.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Canada; the pass-through status of an LP is statutory. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST s. 156 election for closely related registrants (90% or greater voting control) to treat taxable supplies between the corporation and a related Canadian partnership as outside the scope of GST/HST; (ii) partnership fiscal-period alignment under ITA s. 249.1 requiring a calendar-year fiscal period unless all partners are corporations, in which case a corporate 'reference partner' year may be elected; (iii) election under ETA s. 231 to claim a refund of GST/HST paid on uncollectible accounts; and (iv) non-resident partner elections under certain income tax treaties to file on a net basis. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 and 20.2) and the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL, Part VI.1) operate on a mandatory, non-electable basis; no elective hybrid check-the-box or branch-exemption mechanism is available. Cross-border related-party ODC transactions additionally require Form T106 and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s. 247(4).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small supplier threshold. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada, or making taxable supplies in Canada, must register for GST/HST once the value of its taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) made in Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or over the preceding four calendar quarters. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada that makes only zero-rated supplies (e.g., software services exported to non-residents) is not required to register but may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits (ITCs) and recover refunds. A non-resident not carrying on business in Canada that makes taxable supplies of services in Canada must register if those supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold. There is no separate provincial registration threshold at the federal level; however, in HST-participating provinces the federal 5% rate combines with the provincial component (e.g., 13% Ontario, 15% Nova Scotia/New Brunswick/Newfoundland and Labrador/Prince Edward Island) on taxable supplies made in those provinces.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of software engineering, application development, maintenance, and related IT consultancy services made to a non-resident person qualifies as a zero-rated export of services under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7, provided that: (i) the service is not supplied in respect of, or in connection with, real property situated in Canada; (ii) the service is not performed in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; and (iii) the service is not rendered to an individual in Canada for personal consumption. Services performed entirely outside Canada by an offshore development centre for non-resident clients fall outside the Canadian place-of-supply rule (ETA s. 142(1)) and are not subject to GST/HST at all. Services performed in whole or in part from a Canadian ODC for non-resident clients are zero-rated at 0%, and the registrant remains entitled to claim input tax credits on Canadian overheads (rent, cloud infrastructure, equipment, subcontractors), thereby generating periodic net GST/HST refunds. The zero-rating does not apply to services supplied to a Canadian-resident customer or to services rendered to a non-resident where the service is directed at Canadian-payable property or Canadian-based consumption.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government pre-clearance e-invoicing regime exists in Canada at the federal level. The CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE) for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million, including zero-rated exporters. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework is voluntary and Peppol-based (BIS 3.0); there is no requirement for an API-based tax clearance or XML fiscal signature prior to invoice issuance. Registrants must, however, retain complete source documentation (invoices, contracts, bank records) in machine-readable or PDF format for at least six years, and the CRA may demand electronic transmission of invoice-level data during an audit.

CRA audit focus for zero-rated exporters: residency of the recipient, the place of performance, and the commercial substance of the offshore arrangements. Adverse findings trigger: (i) reassessment removing the zero-rating and denying input tax credits attributable to the export, with retrospective clawback of all refunds plus arrears interest at the prescribed CRA rate compounded daily; (ii) gross-negligence penalty under ETA s. 280(1) equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the refund falsely claimed or obtained, and under ITA s. 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated income tax where the exporter knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence made a false statement; (iii) false-statement and failure-to-file penalties under ETA s. 285(1) of 5% of the unreported net tax plus 1% per month for up to 12 months; (iv) transfer-pricing penalty under ITA s. 247(3) of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million and no reasonable-effort documentation exists; (v) criminal prosecution for tax evasion under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239, carrying fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years; and (vi) extension of the normal 4-year reassessment window (ETA s. 298 / ITA s. 152(4)) to 7 years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, and indefinitely in cases of fraud. The exporter must retain the complete substantiation bundle for a minimum of six years, and the CRA may apply the 'direct cause' doctrine to disallow ITCs where the exported service is not supported by contemporaneous, documented commercial activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency on both current and capital accounts. No exchange controls, no requirement for central bank authorization for inbound or outbound wire transfers, profit remittances, dividends, or liquidation proceeds. The only impediments arise from compliance with the Criminal Code, Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, sanctions administered by Global Affairs Canada, and the Investment Canada Act notification/review thresholds for non-Canadian investors establishing or controlling a Canadian business, which may condition but never block convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines, no mandatory export proceeds surrender, and no compulsory conversion ratio to Canadian dollars. Export receipts may be retained in any eligible foreign currency, subject to enterprise internal treasury policy and FINTRAC transaction reporting thresholds. For corporations, foreign exchange gains and losses are taxable revenue/capital items; for a Canadian limited partnership, existence of such gains is allocated to partners under the partnership agreement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating within the framework of the Currency Act and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision; foreign exchange administration is liberalized with no central bank approval requirement, while FINTRAC enforces anti-money laundering and terrorist financing reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Legal feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, etc.) may be used for secondary disbursement rails, but they are not a substitute for a Canadian bank account for statutory treasury functions. Under the Proceeds of Crime Act and Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act, foreign EMIs offering payment services to Canadian entities must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and maintain a physical compliance presence if deemed to be carrying on business in Canada. Reliance on a non-resident EMI for primary liquidity clearing raises counterparty conduct risk, lack of CDIC coverage, and limited SWIFT direct membership. Recommend domestic tier-1 bank as primary, EMI only for branchless low-value vendor payments.
