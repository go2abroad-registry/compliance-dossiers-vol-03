Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). GST/HST portal: My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. Federal GST/HST is administered by the CRA; customs and TIR transit formalities are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on a commercial activity in Canada must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account with the RT suffix. Registration is effected online through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1, accompanied by the partnership agreement and identification of the general partner. Non-resident registrants must appoint a Canadian representative for service and records where directed by the CRA. A CBSA importer/exporter account (RM suffix) should be obtained concurrently to enable CERS electronic export reporting and TIR Carnet transit operations. Online BN/RT issuance is essentially immediate; paper RC1 processing generally takes four to eight weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (Canadian limited partnerships are fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s.96, with income and losses flowing to partners; for GST/HST purposes the partnership itself is a person and the statutory registrant under Excise Tax Act s.123(1)). — A non-resident limited partnership carrying on a commercial activity in Canada must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account with the RT suffix. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Canadian limited partnerships are fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s.96, with income and losses flowing to partners; for GST/HST purposes the partnership itself is a person and the statutory registrant under Excise Tax Act s.123(1)).

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada. The functional equivalents are the Income Tax Act s.96(5) election to deem a Canadian partnership to be taxed as a corporation where every member is a Canadian-resident corporation or another qualifying partnership; ITA s.216 net-basis election for non-resident partners; the GST/HST nil-consideration election under ETA s.156 between the partnership and closely related registrants; and the ETA s.150 election to waive zero-rating of financial services. Hybrid-mismatch constraints are imposed by ITA ss.18.2 and 18.3, which disallow deduction and inclusion mismatches relevant where the LP or its non-resident partners are fiscally transparent or opaque in foreign jurisdictions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a floating exchange rate regime with full current account convertibility for resident entities. Capital account transactions for residents are unrestricted, permitting free repatriation of profits, dividends, and capital contributions. Non-resident investors face Investment Canada Act screening for acquisitions exceeding CAD 5 million. Profit remittance is straightforward with no exchange controls for Canadian residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Bank of Canada. Canadian exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in offshore accounts or convert them at market rates. All FX transactions require compliance with FINTRAC reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, with no statutory timeline for conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMIs registered with FINTRAC, such as Wise Business, are viable for cross-border remittances but face limitations in corporate treasury integration and clearing. Offshore digital EMIs must comply with Canadian MSB regulations; serving Canadian LPs requires local licensing or partnerships, with higher compliance costs and restricted use for large-scale trucking disbursements.
