Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST Registration Portal via My Business Account; Corporations Canada for federal business registration and corporate identity matters

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA, generally by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through the CRA's online registration portal. For GST/HST purposes, the registrant must complete Form RC1 and indicate the required GST/HST account. A Canadian Limited Partnership (LP) must also ensure registration with the relevant provincial corporate registry where it carries on business; however, for federal tax identity, the CRA issues a single BN with program accounts (e.g., RT for GST/HST, RC for payroll, RP for corporation income tax only where applicable). Non-resident partners and the partnership itself should obtain a BN if the LP is required to register for GST/HST and has a permanent establishment in Canada. If the LP has no Canadian presence but makes taxable supplies in Canada through an online marketplace, the CRA may require registration under the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration regime. The LP must provide its legal name, partnership registration documents, names of all general and limited partners, and a Canadian or foreign address for service.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is not a taxpayer for federal income tax purposes; income or loss is allocated to partners who report their respective shares. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is treated as a separate person and must register separately if it makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier. The partnership may elect under subsection 156(2) of the Excise Tax Act to have qualifying members (general partners or limited partners that are registrants) appoint a representative member to account for GST/HST on behalf of the partners for supplies made by the partnership. No check-the-box election exists under Canadian federal law comparable to the U.S. entity classification election; however, a non-resident corporation may rely on treaty tie-breaker rules or Canadian domestic deeming rules to determine its residence status. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada has enacted hybrid mismatch rules (section 18.4 of the Income Tax Act) that may deny deductions or impose inclusions with respect to payments subject to hybrid treatment, but these rules do not alter the default pass-through classification of an LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters, measured on the preceding four quarters; once exceeded, the person must register for GST/HST. A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada through a physical presence, employees, or an online platform must monitor this threshold globally, not just Canadian supplies, except where special rules apply for non-resident suppliers of digital supplies or marketplace operators.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property exported from Canada by a registrant may be zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports the property and satisfies the documentary requirements, or if the property is supplied to a non-resident who exports it as part of the sale, subject to section 1.2 restrictions for goods left in Canada for more than 60 days. Smart home IoT hardware physically delivered to a foreign address or shipped via a common carrier to an export destination qualifies for zero-rating if the supplier keeps the export documents. Offshore services and intangible personal property may be zero-rated under sections 3 to 8 of Part V of Schedule VI when supplied to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant and the service or IP is performed or used wholly outside Canada. Sales to non-registered non-residents via online platforms may also qualify if the CRA's place-of-supply rules attribute the supply to a foreign jurisdiction. Zero-rating does not apply to goods delivered in Canada to the buyer's Canadian premises or to goods that will be installed, erected, or assembled in Canada.

Canada does not currently mandate real-time electronic invoicing or continuous transaction controls (CTC) at the federal level. GST/HST-registered suppliers must issue sales invoices containing prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act, including supplier name, BN, date of supply, description, GST/HST paid or payable, and the rate applied. Electronic invoices are permitted if they are readable and contain the required particulars. No mandatory CRA clearance, digital stamping, or fiscal API pre-clearance exists for standard retail invoices. Provincial sales tax (PST) in participating non-HST provinces (e.g., British Columbia, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and Quebec's QST) may impose separate invoicing and filing obligations. For smart home IoT hardware sold via e-commerce platforms, the marketplace facilitator rules apply to the extent the platform collects and remits GST/HST on behalf of the LP, and the LP must ensure its selling agreements clearly allocate tax compliance duties.

The CRA may reassess any GST/HST return within four years from the date of the assessment (extended to seven years for certain non-arm's length transactions or where the taxpayer failed to report required information). A false zero-rating claim results in the reversal of input tax credits and the assessment of GST/HST at the applicable rate, plus interest compounding daily under the Excise Tax Act and section 280 of the Income Tax Regulations (or similar provisions). Civil penalties apply under section 285.1 of the Excise Tax Act for false statements and omissions, equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the amount of tax sought to be evaded, plus potential gross negligence penalties up to 50% of the tax for wilful misrepresentation. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act carries fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to two years. The burden of proof lies on the registrant to substantiate the export, including the requirement to obtain and retain the export documents showing shipment within a reasonable time and payment from the foreign buyer within the prescribed period (generally 180 days for foreign exchange repatriation to evidence a true export sale; failure to receive proceeds within this period requires full re-characterization as a domestic supply). The CRA may also apply the international financial reporting and section 17(1) of the Customs Act penalties where the exporter fails to report customs transactions accurately. A clawback of input tax credits previously claimed on inventory purchased for export may be issued, with interest backdated to the date the credit was originally claimed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) on both current and capital accounts. The Currency Act and the federal exchange rate regime impose no general authorization, license, or surrender requirement for cross-border transfers. Profit remittances, including dividends, interest, royalties, and partnership distributions, are freely repatriable subject to standard withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act (Part XIII) and applicable tax treaties. Any limitation arises only from anti-money laundering (AML) reporting thresholds, sanction screening, and requirements to evidence source of funds and beneficial ownership, not from exchange control quotas. Non-resident investors may bring in capital, operate accounts, and exit funds without Bank of Canada pre-approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists. Canadian exporters are not obligated to convert foreign currency receipts into CAD, nor are they subject to remittance timelines. Export proceeds may be held in foreign currency accounts, used directly to settle foreign trade obligations, or converted through any licensed Canadian financial institution. Nonetheless, all electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more (or equivalent in foreign currency) are reportable to FINTRAC; the reporting burden falls primarily on financial institutions conducting SWIFT or correspondent banking transactions. For export-specific proceeds, banks may request underlying trade documentation (commercial invoice, customs entry, bill of lading) for their internal risk and FATCA/CRS screening.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority) in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) have only limited viability for a Canadian Limited Partnership. Canada does not issue an 'EMI' licence; any non-bank that offers payment accounts, remittances, or payment processing to Canadian clients is typically required to register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and maintain a Canadian compliance program. An offshore EMI without a Canadian MSB registration may not lawfully provide services to a Canadian resident LP or its Canadian principals. Major payment fintechs (e.g., Stripe, Shopify Payments, Payoneer, Wise Business) are viable for retail e-commerce settlement and low-value disbursements, but they generally do not replace a commercial bank for capital escrow, trade settlement via SWIFT, or committed credit facilities. Feasibility rating: Medium for operating payments; Low for core treasury, escrow, and foreign exchange hedging. The LP should maintain a primary tier-1 bank account and use fintech rails solely for merchant card acquiring and API-driven payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections for the Canadian Limited Partnership are routed as SWIFT International Transfer (MT103/SWIFT) from the Australian limited partner's bank to the LP's Canadian operating account. For each wire, the sender must include the required purpose code and descriptor, such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - LTD PARTNER - [NAME]' and, for CAD conversion, the receiving bank will apply a spot FX rate. Standard documentation required by Canadian banks includes a copy of the LP agreement, the board/GP resolution authorizing the capital call, an invoice/capital call notice, and a breakdown of applicable source-of-funds ('SAF'). Since no exchange-control authorization is required, the only limitation is the Canadian bank's single-transaction threshold for enhanced due diligence (typically CAD 100,000 or more). For amounts above CAD 250,000, the bank may request a Foreign Exchange Conversion Agreement and advance notice of at least three business days.Outbound trade disbursements for the IoT hardware retail business are executed over multiple clearing rails: (i) for direct supplier settlements of manufactured smart devices, SWIFT MT103 wires are the primary rail, with same-day or next-day USD/CAD settlement through the Canadian bank's correspondent network; (ii) for import shipments requiring documentary security, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) or Documentary Collection (CAD) can be established, especially with Asian suppliers; (iii) for retail customer refunds and payout to logistics providers, API-driven Payment Service Provider rails (Stripe, Shopify Payments) move local CAD funds; and (iv) for inter-company loans or management fees to the Australian entity, SWIFT MT103 wires with clear purpose codes are required, supported by an inter-company loan agreement to prevent misclassification under the Income Tax Act. All outbound transactions above CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting by the bank, and the LP must maintain an audit trail of trade documents (purchase orders, bills of lading, customs entries, and invoices) to clear AML review.Outright Foreign Exchange Forward Contracts — deliverable CAD/AUD, CAD/USD, and CAD/EUR forwards with maturities up to 24 months, available under a standard master agreement with a Canadian Schedule I bankNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — permitted for hedging CAD exposure against illiquid emerging-market currencies (if any) where the underlying is not readily deliverable or where settlement is restricted by the counterparty's jurisdictionCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) — permitted for hedging long-term capital contributions denominated in AUD/USD against floating-rate CAD obligationsFX Options (European-style, vanilla) — permitted for hedging contingent cash flows, subject to bank credit line approval and suitability assessmentForeign Exchange Rollovers and Swap Contracts (FX swaps) — permitted for short-term liquidity smoothing between CAD and foreign-currency operating accounts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) have only limited viability for a Canadian Limited Partnership. Canada does not issue an 'EMI' licence; any non-bank that offers payment accounts, remittances, or payment processing to Canadian clients is typically required to register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and maintain a Canadian compliance program. An offshore EMI without a Canadian MSB registration may not lawfully provide services to a Canadian resident LP or its Canadian principals. Major payment fintechs (e.g., Stripe, Shopify Payments, Payoneer, Wise Business) are viable for retail e-commerce settlement and low-value disbursements, but they generally do not replace a commercial bank for capital escrow, trade settlement via SWIFT, or committed credit facilities. Feasibility rating: Medium for operating payments; Low for core treasury, escrow, and foreign exchange hedging. The LP should maintain a primary tier-1 bank account and use fintech rails solely for merchant card acquiring and API-driven payouts.

Turnover: No statutory audit is mandated for a commercial limited partnership under Canadian federal law. Compulsory audit arises only where (i) the LP is an investment fund or reporting issuer under National Instrument 81-106, requiring annual independent audited financial statements without regard to revenue; (ii) the LP holds a securities registration (exempt market dealer, investment fund manager) under National Instrument 31-103, requiring audited financial statements and capital-adequacy verification; (iii) the partnership agreement, lender, or franchisor covenant imposes an audit; or (iv) partners holding at least 25% of partnership interests demand an audit.
