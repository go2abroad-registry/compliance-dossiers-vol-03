Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); CRA portals: 'My Business Account', 'Business Registration Online (BRO)' and 'GST/HST NETFILE'; customs and border authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) (import/export accounts and CARM portal); federal corporate registry: Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Limited Partnership engaged in cross-border e-commerce: (1) determine residence: a Canadian LP is resident where its majority/controlling general partner is Canadian-resident and management is exercised in Canada; (2) apply for the CRA Business Number (9-digit BN plus program accounts) using Form RC1 or BRO, identifying each partner's SIN/BN for disposition; the BN serves as the TIN for the GST/HST, import/export (RM), payroll, corporation and T5013 partnership information-return accounts; (3) assess GST/HST registration: registration is compulsory once worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies and supplies deemed made by a distribution platform) exceed CAD 30,000 in one calendar quarter or in the aggregate over four consecutive calendar quarters; marketplace FBA sellers may rely on the ETA s. 240.1 deeming rule (distribution platform operator such as Amazon collects the GST/HST), but direct B2B or private sales by the LP remain within the LP's own registration threshold; (4) open a CBSA import/export (RM) account and, where goods are landed for FBA inventory, obtain the importer-of-record declaration and pay GST/HST at customs release; (5) submit the BN/GST number to Amazon Seller Central for the FBA listing and to the target payment rails (Amazon Pay / Stripe / Airwallex) for KYC/AML validation; and (6) where the LP is a non-resident entity, appoint a Canadian resident agent (e.g., the Canadian general partner) for service and to administer GST/HST and CBSA obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; classification follows statutory and jurisprudential criteria. A Limited Partnership is a pass-through entity - income/loss is computed under ITA s. 96 and allocated to partners, with the partnership filing T5013 and each partner reporting allocated amounts. Available elections/mechanisms relevant to this structure: (a) election under ITA s. 96(3) to compute partnership income at the partnership level (an optional aggregation method); (b) statutory deemed-continuity rule in ITA s. 102(2) where a member becomes non-resident, preserving 'Canadian partnership' status if the departing member is a specified (non-active limited) partner - no election required; (c) ETA s. 271 deeming of the partnership as a separate person for GST/HST, allowing a partnership-level BN and a single GST/HST account; (d) ETA s. 240.1 statutory treatment appointing the distribution platform (Amazon FBA) as the GST/HST supplier of the underlying FBA marketplace sales; and (e) on hybrid mismatches, Canada's BEPS Action 2 proposals remain unenacted - the LP should nonetheless document partner tax residence and entity classification to prevent double-dip deductions or mismatched inclusion outcomes in cross-border flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility without a general exchange control regime. The Currency Act and Canada's IMF Article VIII observance impose no ministerial approval for capital inflows, profit remittances, or debt-service payments. A Canadian limited partnership may freely receive foreign capital contributions and remit distributable income; however, distributions of income to non-resident limited partners are generally subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (treaty-reduced, commonly 15% or 0% under the Canada-US Treaty where a US partner has no Canadian permanent establishment, subject to Form NR301/NR302, NR6 prepayment, and supporting treaty documentation). No exchange-control permit is required for CAD/USD conversion, which executes at market rates through Schedule I banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion timeline exists; Canada abolished statutory exchange controls in 1951. Amazon FBA export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD or any foreign-currency account. Reporting obligations are statistical only: exporters must file a Statistics Canada Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 under the Canadian International Merchandise Trade program and retain commercial support (Amazon settlement reports, invoices, bills of lading).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary and currency authority; prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction reporting supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); payment service provider registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility as ancillary clearing rails, Low as a substitute for a Schedule I bank mandate. Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise Platform, and Stripe operate in Canada as money services businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and are subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA registration mandatory from November 2024, with transitional compliance through 2025-2026). They provide multi-currency virtual IBANs, Amazon payout collection, and vendor disbursement, but they cannot take deposits or extend credit and must safeguard funds in trust with a Schedule I Canadian bank. Legal constraint: the LP master treasury account, statutory AML connection, and FX facility must remain with a chartered bank; EMIs serve only as payment rails and settlement utilities.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax rate (general rate); provincial rates vary from 1% to 12% (e.g., Ontario 11.5%, British Columbia 12%). Limited Partnership is a flow-through entity; income is taxed at the partner level, not at the entity level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For non-resident partners, distributions of Canadian-source income are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% (unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty). This is not a dividend withholding tax but a withholding on partnership distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services transactions; CAD 5 million for other transactions (per CRA guidance). However, contemporaneous documentation is required for all cross-border transactions with non-residents, regardless of threshold, to avoid penalties.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. The CRA may challenge transactions that lack economic substance or are structured primarily to avoid tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239). Penalties: 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be held personally liable for corporate tax debts if they fail to withhold or remit source deductions (s. 227.1). Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment may apply.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny tax benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit (s. 245).

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be jointly and severally liable for taxes and penalties if they participated in the scheme. Travel bans may be imposed for serious non-compliance.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (s. 247(3)), and interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing mispricing. Civil restitution and potential criminal charges for gross negligence.
AML/Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 5 million for entities, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for fines and imprisonment if they knowingly fail to comply. Travel bans may be imposed as part of criminal proceedings.
