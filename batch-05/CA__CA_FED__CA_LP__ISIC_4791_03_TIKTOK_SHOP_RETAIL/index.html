Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account-business.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) electronically through the Business Registration Online portal, by mail, or by fax. Identification, partnership agreement, certificate of existence, and general partner details are required. If only indirect tax compliance is triggered, the BN can be limited to a GST/HST account. Non-resident registrants may be required to post security up to 50% of the estimated annual GST/HST amount under the Excise Tax Act. CRA typically issues the BN/GST/HST account within 20 business days of a complete application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists. Under the Income Tax Act, a limited partnership is fiscally transparent unless an election is made to be treated as a separate taxpayer for specific purposes. Under section 96, partners compute their respective shares. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership itself is deemed a person under subsection 272.1(2) of the Excise Tax Act. Elections include voluntary registration under subsection 240(1), small supplier waiver under subsection 240(2), and designation of a non-resident partner as the partnership's representative under subsection 273.1(2).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in the current calendar quarter and in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, computed without regard to associated persons (except where applicable) and excluding exempt supplies, financial services, and zero-rated supplies made by small suppliers. Once exceeded, mandatory registration applies for GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods qualify for zero-rating when the supplier establishes that the goods are exported in accordance with Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act and the Export of Goods (Value of Cause) Regulations. The supplier must prove that the goods were delivered to an international destination and that possession was transferred outside Canada. For services, zero-rating applies under Part V of Schedule VI when the service is supplied to a non-resident and the service is not performed in Canada or relates to goods delivered outside Canada. For TikTok Shop retail sales, drop-shipping arrangements require documented evidence that each item physically left Canada, typically within 60 days of the sale, including e-commerce transaction logs and carriage records.

  • CBSA Canada Customs Export Declaration / ACI Export Report (Form B13A in respect of high-value or controlled goods)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean or Air Waybill) evidencing dispatch from Canada to the foreign buyer
  • Exporter's Commercial Invoice and buyer's Purchase Order indicating the foreign destination
  • Packing list and cargo control document bearing tracking/entry reference numbers
  • Bank inward remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103) or other verifiable payment settlement documentation from a non-resident buyer

No federal real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory electronic-invoicing stamping regime currently operates in Canada. CRA does not require API-based continuous transaction controls. Businesses must generate sales invoices containing prescribed information under section 3 of the Excise Tax Act and retain them either in paper or electronic format. For digital platform retail on TikTok Shop, the platform is required to report certain deemed supply activities under the digital services GST/HST framework, but the Canadian LP remains the registered supplier responsible for its own invoicing and remittance records.

CRA will scrutinize zero-rated export claims under a supply-proven audit framework. Incomplete documentation causes denial of zero-rating, resulting in reassessment of 5% GST plus interest and penalties. Willful false statements or fraudulent zero-rating attempts attract penalties ranging from 25% of the net tax assessed, increased penalties for repeat violations, and potential criminal liability under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and Criminal Code fraud provisions. The normal four-year reassessment period extends indefinitely for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible regime for both capital and current accounts under the Foreign Exchange Act. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation are unrestricted for Canadian entities subject only to standard tax filings and FINTRAC reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or central bank conversion required for export or trade receipts classified as current account transactions. All foreign currency receipts must be converted at market rates; transactions above CAD 10,000 trigger mandatory FINTRAC declaration for AML purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore EMIs face strict prohibition under Canadian Payment Services Act and FINTRAC oversight; only licensed domestic or approved cross-border platforms (e.g., Wise B2B) permitted for corporate treasury with full KYC and transaction monitoring.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the style of Caribbean tax havens; however, economic substance is enforced through judicial and administrative doctrines: the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), the treaty 'beneficial ownership' requirement, the permanent establishment threshold under s. 215 and relevant DTAs, and the 'thin capitalization' rules in s. 212(3) and 212(7)(d) which limit interest deductibility on Norwegian-style back-to-back financing. A TikTok Shop LP that lacks office premises, employees, inventory control, and decision-making in Canada risks being treated as a mere shell allowing the CRA to reattribute income under the GAAR or under the transfer-pricing rules in s. 247.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s. 239, a person who evades tax or makes a false statement is liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. The CRA also assesses civil penalties under s. 163(2) of up to 50% of the understated tax, in addition to the full tax reassessment, daily arrears interest, and a 10% late-instatement penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or direct the concealment may be charged personally under s. 239, exposed to imprisonment of up to 5 years, travel bans upon arrest, and civil restitution for the unpaid tax plus interest and penalties. The CRA can issue a director's liability assessment under s. 227.1 for any unremitted withholding, which is jointly and severally enforceable against each director through garnishments and personal bankruptcy action.
Shell Company and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s. 245 to recompute the tax consequences, collapse the offshore arrangement, reattribute the entire profit to the Canadian LP, and disallow deductions claimed for intercompany fees. The reassessment may carry a net-file-interest penalty of up to 50% under s. 163(2), and if the arrangement involves a sham, criminal penalties identical to those under s. 239 apply. In addition, the CRA may invoke transfer-pricing adjustments under s. 247(2), including an upward adjustment of 10% on the gross income attributable to lack of documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the sham structure and sign false returns risk a s. 227.1 director's liability order for all Part XIII and instalment amounts, a personal gross-negligence assessment under s. 163(2), and disqualification from serving as directors under the Canada Business Corporations Act. The CRA may seek pre-assessment injunctions or prohibition orders preventing travel, and on conviction, custodial sentences of up to 5 years for abetting tax evasion.
Withholding Tax and Part XIII Failures

Statutory sanction: The CRA assesses the full unremitted withholding amount as a debt of the LP and every partner, plus a 10% late-filing penalty and 5% failure-to-remit penalty under s. 227.9(1) and (2), and daily interest at the prescribed rate plus 4%. Non-deductible fines of up to CAD 25,000 per instance can be imposed under s. 162(4.1) for failure to remit, and a 10-year Crown priority lien attaches to all assets in Canada.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 for the full unremitted trust amounts, including penalties and interest, provided that the CRA has issued a demand letter under s. 227.2. A director who was also the 'influencing mind' of the non-remission faces criminal dishonesty charges under s. 239, travel restrictions pending trial, and potential imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Anti-Money Laundering and Unregistered MSB Exposure

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC may impose administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation on a corporate entity and up to CAD 100,000 per violation on individuals; for a first-tier violation, CAD 50,000 per entity. The CRA and FINTRAC share disclosures under s. 253.1, so any AML red flag automatically triggers a tax audit. Willful money laundering attracts criminal penalties under the Criminal Code (s. 462.31) including imprisonment up to 10 years, asset freezing, and forfeiture of the TikTok Shop proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of the corporate general partner who knowingly fail to implement a compliance program are subject to FINTRAC administrative penalties, interim underseizure monitoring orders, and, on conviction, imprisonment for up to 10 years for money laundering. They also remain personally liable for the restored director's withholding debts, and may face travel bans while on bail or under criminal investigation.
