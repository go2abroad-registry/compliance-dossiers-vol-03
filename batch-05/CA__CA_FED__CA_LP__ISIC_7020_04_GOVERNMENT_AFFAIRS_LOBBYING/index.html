Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax authority; administrative portal is CRA My Business Account and the Non-Resident Registration service via https://www.canada.ca/taxes.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and, if taxable supplies exceed the threshold, a GST/HST account (RT0001). Procedure: (1) appoint a named representative or contact in Canada; (2) file Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or use the CRA non-resident online registration module; (3) provide the partnership agreement, foreign registration certificate, and the Canadian representative's name and address; (4) receive the 9-digit BN with applicable program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RC for payroll if applicable); (5) alternatively, a foreign LP may file Form RC459 'GST/HST Registration for Non-Residents'. CRA processing generally completes within 15-30 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — limited partnerships are fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96 (income and loss flow through to partners), but for GST/HST purposes the partnership is treated as a distinct 'person' under Excise Tax Act s. 123(1) and must file its own GST/HST returns; territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income under ITA ss. 2(3) and 115; no tax-exempt zone treatment applies. — A non-resident limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and, if taxable supplies exceed the threshold, a GST/HST account (RT0001). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — limited partnerships are fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act s. 96 (income and loss flow through to partners), but for GST/HST purposes the partnership is treated as a distinct 'person' under Excise Tax Act s. 123(1) and must file its own GST/HST returns; territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source income under ITA ss. 2(3) and 115; no tax-exempt zone treatment applies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Canada; partnership transparency is mandatory. Available elections: fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(3) to adopt a non-calendar partnership taxation year; voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(1) where the partnership is a small supplier; ETA s. 156 election to zero-rate certain supplies between related participating registrants; hybrid-mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act implementing OECD BEPS Action 2 apply to deny double-non-taxation where a Canadian LP is also treated as opaque in a foreign jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — mandatory registration is triggered once the partnership's worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the four most recent consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); supplies of services to non-residents are included in the threshold calculation unless zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Government-affairs advisory and lobbying services qualify for 0% GST/HST under ETA s. 23(1) where the supply is made to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST, the service is not rendered while the non-resident is physically present in Canada, the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, and the service is not in respect of a business carried on by the non-resident in Canada. Goods physically exported by the partnership may be zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI (ETA s. 1) where export occurs within prescribed timeframes and evidence of export is retained.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) or G7 Export Reporting System record for goods shipped to the United States
  • Commercial invoice, bill of lading / airway bill, and customs cargo control document proving physical export
  • For services: signed advisory/retainer contract, contemporaneous narrative and time records, and foreign-client payment evidence (bank credit advice, SWIFT advice, or inward remittance certificate)
  • GST/HST return and working papers identifying zero-rated supplies (Line 103) and citing the applicable section of the Excise Tax Act

CRA does not require real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval, or digital stamping of invoices. E-invoicing is mandated only for federal B2G suppliers who must transmit invoices through the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL network when contractually required. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework. A GST/HST registrant must issue prescribed invoices showing the business number, GST/HST registration number, and supply description; books and records must be retained for 6 years.

Canada imposes no explicit statutory FX-repatriation deadline; however, CRA auditors will deny zero-rating where payment is absent or unduly delayed beyond reasonable commercial terms, with a 180-day benchmark applied in this dossier. Consequences of a failed zero-rating claim: GST/HST reassessment with interest; penalty under ETA s. 280 equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the overstated net tax (50% for knowing participation); s. 285.01 false-statement penalties; and the normal 4-year reassessment period is extended up to 10 years where misrepresentation or neglect is established. Criminal prosecution may arise for wilful evasion or fraud under the Excise Tax Act and Criminal Code, and lobbying compliance failures can additionally trigger disqualification or penalties under the federal Lobbying Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully free-floating and fully convertible currency regime with no exchange-control legislation in force; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and Canada accepted IMF Article VIII obligations in 1952. Current-account and capital-account transactions, including partnership capital contributions, accumulated income repatriation, and profit distributions to non-resident limited partners, are unrestricted and require no central-bank approval or conversion licence. The only structural constraint is the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review threshold applicable to large acquisitions of control by non-Canadians, which does not apply to treasury transfers, retained-earnings remittances, or advisory retainer settlements for the lobbying partnership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline on export receipts. The Limited Partnership may retain proceeds in CAD or any foreign currency freely, subject only to general FATF-compliant record-keeping and FINTRAC electronic funds transfer (EFT) reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 or equivalent).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising issuance, monetary policy, and financial-stability mandates, with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as ancillary authorities for currency policy, financial sanctions, and AML/CFT oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a supplementary corridor, low viability as a primary relationship. FINTRAC-registered payment service providers and EMIs (e.g., Wise Edge Canada, Payoneer, and other MSB-licensed platforms) may be used for expedited low-value cross-border retainer inflows and contractor disbursements; however, they are not chartered banks, cannot access Payments Canada large-value clearing (LVTS/Real-Time Rail) as direct participants, and will not support structured trade finance or documentary letters of credit. FINTRAC EFT record-keeping and MSB registration must be verified before routing any inbound partnership capital, and the LP must maintain a Tier 1 domestic deposit account as the treasury anchor.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Limited-partnership beneficial-ownership opacity, including silent limited partners holding an indirect economic interest.

Defense evidence: Current limited-partnership registration summary from the applicable provincial registry (Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, or Quebec), executed deed of limited partnership enumerating all partners, a 25%-and-greater ownership attestation, a FINTRAC beneficial-ownership information (BOI) declaration signed by the general partner, and notarized passport copies of ultimate natural-person principals.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partnership capital injections from non-resident limited partners must be routed via SWIFT MT103 through the LP's Tier 1 Canadian correspondent banking network, using the partnership's designated USD or CAD account at its primary bank; recommended intermediary routing is via the bank's US correspondent (e.g., JPMorgan Chase or Wells Fargo) through Fedwire or CHIPS, with conversion into CAD executed at the bank's commercial spot rate. The wire narrative must state 'LP capital contribution — investment', include a unique partnership identifier, and be accompanied by a source-of-funds letter from the remitting partner to satisfy FINTRAC EFT record-keeping (mandatory for transactions at or above CAD 10,000).
