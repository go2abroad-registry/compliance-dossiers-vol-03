Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); official business registration portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), CRA My Business Account (MyBA), and GST/HST NETFILE/TEI for electronic filing.

E-government filing portal

Canadian Limited Partnerships (LPs) are registered as a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes but remain transparent for income tax. Procedure: (1) Obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) by filing online via BRO, or by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; the CRA will enroll the LP as the taxpayer and issue a separate GST/HST program account (RT prefix) where the LP makes taxable supplies. (2) Mandatory GST/HST registration applies if the LP's worldwide gross taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or over consecutive four-quarter periods; e-commerce DTC retail is a commercial activity requiring registration once that threshold is crossed. (3) Provide the LP's registered office address, general partner identification, and projected Canadian revenue; the LP may also register non-resident partners' details for T5013 informational reporting. (4) For foreign partners, a Canadian agent or representative acceptable to the CRA must ordinarily be designated for service of process.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: For federal income tax purposes a Canadian LP is fiscally transparent; income and losses flow through to partners under Income Tax Act (ITA) subsection 96(1). For GST/HST purposes, the partnership itself is deemed a person under ETA subsection 221(1) and registers, collects, and remits HST/GST on taxable supplies. Residents of Canada are taxable on worldwide income; the LP itself has no federal entity-level income tax absent partnership-level excise items. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: For federal income tax purposes a Canadian LP is fiscally transparent; income and losses flow through to partners under Income Tax Act (ITA) subsection 96(1). For GST/HST purposes, the partnership itself is deemed a person under ETA subsection 221(1) and registers, collects, and remits HST/GST on taxable supplies. Residents of Canada are taxable on worldwide income; the LP itself has no federal entity-level income tax absent partnership-level excise items. No federal tax-exempt zone applies to standard DTC e-commerce retail.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box regimes; classification follows the legal form of the entity. Available elections include: (1) annual GST/HST filing (EDAT) for qualifying small suppliers; (2) designation of a partnership's fiscal period under ITA 249.1(4); (3) election under ITA 21.2 in respect of FX gains attributable to foreign accrual property income, as applicable; (4) non-resident supplier simplified GST/HST registration election under the July 2021 e-commerce framework (available only to non-residents); (5) ETA joint election for closely-related corporations to treat taxable supplies as exempt; and (6) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA 12.6, which may disallow deductions to the extent a foreign country classifies the LP as a taxable entity while Canada treats it as transparent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 annual worldwide taxable supplies (federal small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148). Registration becomes mandatory on the day the LP exceeds CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or in the preceding four calendar quarters. For non-resident vendors of digital products and services to Canadian consumers under the July 2021 e-commerce rules, the small supplier threshold is eliminated, but a resident CA_LP selling goods through a Shopify DTC webstore is subject to the CAD 30,000 general threshold for GST/HST purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Canadian LP DTC brand may claim the 0% rate on goods where: (a) title passes to a non-resident recipient; (b) the goods are physically exported from Canada as part of the supply; and (c) the goods are not acquired for consumption, use, or supply in Canada prior to export. For zero-rating to apply, the export must be completed before the last day of the calendar month following the month in which the consideration becomes due, unless a longer period is evidenced through the ordinary course of shipping. Exported goods may qualify even where the Shopify platform processes payment in a foreign currency, provided the CRA's documentary evidence requirements are met. Offshore services rendered to non-residents, if not connected with Canadian real or tangible personal property, are likewise zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V.

  • B13A Export Declaration (CBSA), or courier/Purolator/USPS manifest or postal export record for low-value parcels
  • Commercial invoice or sales contract clearly referencing destination outside Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill indicating Canadian origin and foreign destination of the goods
  • Proof of foreign consideration received (inward remittance certificate, Shopify Payments settlement summaries, PayPal/Stripe payout records with FX conversion reports)
  • Canadian customs or freight forwarder certification confirming the goods left Canada
  • Order-level traceability documentation linking the Shopify order ID to the carrier tracking number and the payment settlement

Canada does not currently mandate real-time tax authority clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls. B2B and B2G e-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Standard (PEPPOL BIS 3.0), announced in 2023 and adopted by the CRA for procurement. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically where revenue exceeds CAD 1.5 million. The CBSA operates the Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) digital platform for import/export declarations. No state-run API pre-clearance of invoices is required.

The CRA may reassess a previously zero-rated export and deny refunds/input tax credits within the normal four-year reassessment period (or indefinitely where misrepresentation is fraudulent). A clawback will include the GST/HST not collected, arrears interest, and penalties. Knowingly making a false or misleading statement in an export claim triggers a gross negligence penalty of 50% of the net tax understated under ETA s. 285 and ITA s. 163.2. Criminal evasion under ETA s. 327.1 carries fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. The CRA also cross-examines import records exchanged via the CBSA against export declarations to deter false zero-rating; a 180-day benchmark is recognized as a commercial settlement norm but not a statutory deadline in Canadian tax law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange rate with unrestricted current and capital account convertibility, having accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. There is no general foreign exchange control law, no quantitative limits on conversion, and no prior regulatory approval for foreign currency conversion or remittance. Profit distributions from a Canadian Limited Partnership to non-resident limited partners are freely remittable in CAD or foreign currency; the sole obstacles are statutory withholding tax obligations under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (default 25%, generally reduced to 15% or less under an applicable tax treaty) and standard domestic anti-money laundering compliance. Any contractual restriction on convertibility arises only from the individual banking institution's internal risk policy, not from the sovereign FX regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no Canadian statutory obligation to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within any prescribed timeline or ratio. A resident exporter or a Canadian-registered Limited Partnership may retain foreign currency earnings offshore or onshore indefinitely. Only transparency reporting applies: cross-border currency movements in or out of Canada of CAD $10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and the business must maintain records of the international electronic funds transfer (IEFT). For continuity, the LP should nevertheless require that Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe settle in USD/CAD wire transfers with a statement-level legend identifying the merchant's BN/Incorporation Number to avoid ACH mischaracterization of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; the Minister of Finance, with the Department of Finance Canada, oversees the exchange rate regime and the Currency Act; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally regulated financial institutions, and FINTRAC enforces cross-border currency transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High feasibility for a low-risk DTC webstore, provided the provider is a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) or an authorized foreign bank branch with a valid retail payments license under the Retail Payment Activities Act (effective September 2022, operative phases through 2024-2025). Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing into the Canadian LP's operational account: (i) wire denomination in CAD or USD via SWIFT MT103 using the bank's substantive interbank network; (ii) beneficiary reference must include the LP's legal name, the Canadian registered address, and the stated purpose code as 'Capital Contribution / Equity Injection (CFIA 2001/01 Purpose Code, Category A.1)' to avoid misclassification as trade revenue; (iii) for each transfer equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000, the depository bank files an electronic FINTRAC IEFT report without requiring the LP itself to file, but the LP must preserve the source-of-funds evidence (subscription agreement, foreign bank statement, board resolution) on file for six years; and (iv) if the injection flows through a payment services provider rather than a direct SWIFT sender, the LP must require the intermediary to include the routing bank's legal entity identifier (LEI) and ensure the beneficiary's account is tagged as a 'capital escrow' classification, thus preventing the bank's automated AML algorithm from earmarking the funds as unexplained electronic-commerce receipts.The outbound treasury rails for trade settlement are (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border supplier wires, employing a Tier 1 Schedule I bank's correspondent network to the supplier's overseas account with the purpose code 'Trade Settlement: Import of Goods (ISIC 4791 DTC inventory)' and an attached pro-forma invoice number to maintain an audit-visible invoice-to-payment trace; (ii) Commercial Standby or Documentary Letters of Credit (LC) for high-value inventory orders, opened via a Canadian bank's trade finance desk and uncirculated under UCP600 with the LP as applicant and the overseas supplier as beneficiary; (iii) domestic purchasing through Payments Canada's Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) and Lynx real-time rail for Canadian fulfillment centers; and (iv) controlled use of Shopify Payments' automated payout rail, which settles USD sales into the designated USD Foreign Currency Account within T+2, thereby permitting netting of FX exposure rather than daily micro-conversion at retail rates. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High feasibility for a low-risk DTC webstore, provided the provider is a FINTRAC-registered Money Service Business (MSB) or an authorized foreign bank branch with a valid retail payments license under the Retail Payment Activities Act (effective September 2022, operative phases through 2024-2025). Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, and PayPal Business Canada meet baseline viability for settlement velocity but carry legal constraints that preclude sole reliance: (i) funds held with such providers are generally not 'deposits' protected by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC); (ii) corporate account opening may be denied if the LP cannot evidence the General Partner's capacity to bind the partnership; and (iii) certain offshore digital EMIs without a Canadian presence cannot legally open a payment account for a Canadian LP without registering as an MSB in Canada, thereby triggering a physical representative and agent mandate. The internal rating for the recommended architecture is Medium, meaning a Tier 1 bank primary account must remain the liquidity pivot while a FINTRAC-compliant EMI is used solely as a collection funnel.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate (0% at LP level; fiscally transparent entity; income flows through to partners)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders; for a limited partnership, partnership profit allocations are not dividends and are not subject to Part XIII dividend WHT — the non-resident partner is taxed under Part I on Canadian-source active business income

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate gross annual specified cross-border non-arm's length transactions; triggers mandatory T106 filing and requires contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation under CRA administrative guidance

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, ITA Section 247 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Section 245) require genuine commercial and economic substance; a limited partnership that is a shell, without real Canadian business operations, employees, or decision-making, is exposed to reassessment, recharacterization of the arrangement, and gross-negligence penalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under ITA Section 239(1), summary conviction: fine of 50% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 18 months; conviction on indictment: fine up to 200% of tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalty under Section 163(2) of 50% for gross negligence

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be prosecuted personally; the CRA may impose director liability under Section 317.1 for unpaid penalties and interest where the entity cannot pay; travel bars and asset seizure under the Income Tax Act collection provisions; criminal conviction may lead to travel restrictions and cross-border enforcement
Shell Company / Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under GAAR Section 245, denial of treaty benefits, application of 15% federal penalty under Section 245(5) on the amount of the tax benefit, plus interest; large corporation penalties and transfer-pricing penalties under Section 247(3) up to 10% of adjusted net transaction amount

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns or direct partner distributions may be held jointly and severally liable for the tax benefit recharacterized, plus interest and penalties; CRA can issue a director liability assessment under Section 227.1 for unremitted withholdings if the partnership is a bare trust or conduit
AML Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, penalties for failing to report suspicious transactions or unauthorized payment processing can reach C$2 million per violation for an entity; criminal liability for directors and officers extends to imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined up to C$500,000 or imprisoned for up to 5 years for knowingly participating in AML failures; regulatory orders can also impose travel bans, threaten the renewal or suspension of payment provider agreements, and create a record that bars future Canadian entry
