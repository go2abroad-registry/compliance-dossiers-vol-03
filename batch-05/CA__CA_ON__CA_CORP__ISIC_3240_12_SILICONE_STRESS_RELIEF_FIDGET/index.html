Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and federal corporate income tax administration; CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE portals

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident corporation carrying on taxable activity in Canada must apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for a federal Business Number (BN) and GST/HST registration. For GST/HST, use Form RC4458 (Non-Resident GST/HST Registration) or request registration through CRA My Business Account. Required submissions include entity formation documents, registered office details, director/officer information, class of business, and expected Canadian taxable supplies. The BN serves as the statutory tax identifier; Ontario HST is administered through the CRA BN program and no separate provincial HST registration number is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under the Canadian Income Tax Act; a business corporation is taxed at the entity level. Elective mechanisms are limited to statutory rollover elections (ss. 85 and 73), CCPC status elections (s. 89), and treaty-based tie-breaker determinations for non-residents. Hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act ss. 18.2 and 18.3 may apply to deductible payments creating hybrid mismatches with foreign jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 of GST/HST taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or one calendar quarter; non-residents supplying or causing supplies to be made in Canada are subject to the same small-supplier threshold and must register once exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a supply of tangible personal property, including silicone compression-molded fidgets, where the goods are delivered or made available outside Canada by the supplier or transferred to a common carrier for export. Certain services supplied to non-residents are also zero-rated when the supply is not performed in Canada or the benefit is not consumed in Canada. The supplier must retain contemporaneous export evidence and, if GST/HST was charged in error, must adjust invoices or refund the tax within the applicable CRA timeframes.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13 / Canadian Export Reporting programme declaration)
  • Commercial invoice referencing the 0% export rate of GST/HST
  • Bill of lading or air waybill and freight forwarder delivery evidence
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT or banking records showing payment from the non-resident buyer
  • CBSA export records, customs broker reports, or carrier documentation evidencing that the goods left Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls. GST/HST registrants must issue and retain valid invoices and maintain prescribed books and records in Canada. Electronic GST/HST filing is mandatory for many registrants through GST/HST NETFILE or EDI/API-based submissions. Voluntary e-invoicing interoperability follows Peppol standards but is not legally required.

CRA may reassess export zero-rating claims within the normal four-year limitation period, and without limitation where misrepresentation, forgetfulness, or fraud is established. Unsupported export claims may be reversed, input tax credits denied, and penalties assessed under the Excise Tax Act, including gross-negligence penalties of up to 25% of the net tax amount, substantial late-filing penalties, and criminal liability for knowingly making false statements or evading tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada maintains a flexible exchange rate regime with no general restrictions on payments or transfers for international transactions, including dividend remittances, capital repatriation, and trade settlements. Profit remittance by foreign-owned Canadian corporations is permitted without prior regulatory approval, subject to corporate solvency tests, distribution documentation, and applicable withholding tax compliance under the Income Tax Act (Canada).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose mandatory surrender or conversion requirements for export proceeds, nor does it enforce statutory timelines for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters maintain unrestricted ownership of foreign currency earnings and may retain, convert, or dispose of them at commercial bank rates. Reporting obligations apply under the Customs Act and Statistics Act for balance-of-payments compilation, but no conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with legislative oversight under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; exchange control administration is jointly supported by the Department of Finance Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for trade reporting purposes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Canadian-regulated fintechs holding a Restricted or Full Bank Charter under the Bank Act may provide cross-border payment services. Offshore digital EMI entities (e.g., UK- or EU-authorised e-money institutions) cannot accept deposits in Canada and are restricted from operating core payment accounts without Canadian MSB registration with FINTRAC and provincial money services licensing. Such offshore EMIs may serve as ancillary disbursement rails, subject to KYC outsourcing risk and FINTRAC reporting thresholds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from foreign shareholders are routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the Canadian entity's Commercial Bank account. The originating bank must apply reason for payment code such as 'CAPT' (Capital Contribution) or 'LTCF' (Loans to Corporate Family). The receiving Canadian bank will require a completed Funds Source Declaration, corporate borrowing resolution, and shareholder subscription agreement. Contributions above CAD 10,000 that meet FINTRAC prescribed characteristics require CTR filing; no FINTRAC MSB registration applies to the corporate recipient acting on its own behalf. Funds should be denominated in CAD or USD, and the Canadian bank may request a bank confirmation letter from the remitting institution to verify source of funds.Payments to overseas suppliers of silicone raw materials, moulds, and packaging are executed through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 cover orders, supported by commercial invoices, packing lists, and contingent shipping documents. For high-value orders, the preferred rail is a commercially confirmed irrevocable letter of credit (LC) opened with a Tier 1 Canadian bank, advising through a reputable overseas confirming bank. CAD payouts are settled through Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS system; cross-currency payouts settle through SWIFT correspondent banking corridors. Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end. For a December 31 year-end, deadline is June 30 of the following year. Installment payments are required quarterly for corporations with taxable income exceeding CAD 3,000 (federal) and CAD 5,000 (Ontario).

Allocate costs based on Canadian operations; maintain separate books for Canadian branch. Late filing penalty: 5% of balance owing plus 1% per full month of delay (max 12 months). Repeated failure: additional 10% penalty. Interest on unpaid taxes at prescribed rate (currently 8% for corporations). Failure to file electronically may incur penalty of CAD 1,000.
Line 3 - Deductible Expenses Operating expenses such as salaries, rent, utilities, marketing, and depreciation (capital cost allowance). Expenses must be incurred for the purpose of earning Canadian income; cross-border allocations must be arm's length. Late filing penalty: 5% of balance owing plus 1% per full month of delay (max 12 months). Repeated failure: additional 10% penalty. Interest on unpaid taxes at prescribed rate (currently 8% for corporations). Failure to file electronically may incur penalty of CAD 1,000.
Line 4 - Taxable Income Net income after adjustments, including capital gains, dividends, and other income, minus deductions (e.g., loss carryforwards, SBD). Non-residents must compute taxable income under Part I rules, including treaty provisions for permanent establishment. Late filing penalty: 5% of balance owing plus 1% per full month of delay (max 12 months). Repeated failure: additional 10% penalty. Interest on unpaid taxes at prescribed rate (currently 8% for corporations). Failure to file electronically may incur penalty of CAD 1,000.
Line 5 - Federal and Provincial Tax Payable Calculate federal tax at 15% (or 9% if SBD applies) and Ontario tax at 11.5% (or 3.2% if SBD applies), after tax credits. Non-residents are subject to federal tax only; provincial tax may apply if permanent establishment exists in Ontario. Late filing penalty: 5% of balance owing plus 1% per full month of delay (max 12 months). Repeated failure: additional 10% penalty. Interest on unpaid taxes at prescribed rate (currently 8% for corporations). Failure to file electronically may incur penalty of CAD 1,000.
Schedule 1 - SBD Calculation Determine eligibility for small business deduction based on active business income and associated corporations. Non-resident corporations generally do not qualify for SBD unless they are CCPC (rare). Late filing penalty: 5% of balance owing plus 1% per full month of delay (max 12 months). Repeated failure: additional 10% penalty. Interest on unpaid taxes at prescribed rate (currently 8% for corporations). Failure to file electronically may incur penalty of CAD 1,000.

Losses must be from Canadian business; foreign losses are not deductible. Late filing penalty: 5% of balance owing plus 1% per full month of delay (max 12 months). Repeated failure: additional 10% penalty. Interest on unpaid taxes at prescribed rate (currently 8% for corporations). Failure to file electronically may incur penalty of CAD 1,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million (total value of cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties in a fiscal year) triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation (Local File and Master File). For services, threshold is CAD 500,000. Documentation must be prepared before filing deadline and provided to CRA within 3 months of request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act denies tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance. Additionally, CRA may challenge shell companies with no real business activity, employees, or assets in Canada. For cross-border transactions, substance is required to support transfer pricing positions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239: fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: gross negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (including withholding) under s.227.1 if they failed to exercise due diligence. Civil restitution, travel bans (via court order), and imprisonment for criminal evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the entity under GAAR, reallocate income, and impose penalties (up to 50% of tax benefit). For cross-border, transfer pricing adjustments with 10% penalty on net adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for taxes owed if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement; potential criminal charges for conspiracy to evade taxes.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative penalties up to CAD 1 million for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to comply. Civil liability for damages arising from money laundering activities.
