Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada) - Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership is a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes and must obtain its own 9-digit Business Number (BN) plus a program account (e.g., 'RT' for GST/HST). Non-resident partnerships and partners apply using Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' through Business Registration Online (BRO), or by paper to the CRA Non-Resident Income Tax Section. The application must identify the partnership name, all general and limited partners, the carrying-on-business address in Canada, the date of formation, and a designated contact or authorized representative; engaging a Canadian agent for service of process and notice is strongly recommended. No minimum capital is required. The BN is issued at CRA discretion after validation of the limited partnership's foundational registration in the relevant provincial registry. The partnership must open the GST/HST account separately when it makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier. Foreign entities may also require CRA payroll (RP), corporate income tax (RC), or import/export (RM) program accounts when applicable. For non-resident suppliers of specified digital services to Canadian consumers, the simplified GST/HST registration regime effective July 1, 2021 requires registration once Canadian gross revenue exceeds CAD $30,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' system; a limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act (ITA). Relevant elections and mechanisms: (i) no partnership-level income tax; each partner reports its share of Canadian-source income or loss in its own jurisdiction; (ii) the s. 216 ITA election allowing a non-resident partner with Canadian rental or timber income to file a Part I return on net taxable income instead of the gross Part XIII withholding tax; (iii) the s. 273 Excise Tax Act election permitting a 'qualifying partnership' to use a designated member as its GST/HST registrant; (iv) the optional joint GST/HST election under ETA s. 156 with an associated registrant; and (v) limited-scope elections under the hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) that do not change entity classification but may recharacterize certain payments for deductibility. A publicly traded Canadian limited partnership is subject to the SIFT partnership rules and cannot elect out of deemed corporation status.

5%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports by a Canadian limited partnership are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For goods, ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 1 applies where possession of the goods transfers to the recipient and the goods are exported by the supplier directly, or by the recipient as soon as reasonably possible, with final delivery outside Canada; documentary proof of physical export must be retained. For services and intangibles (the relevant LegalTech-SaaS export), ETA Schedule VI Part V s. 7 zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident person unless the service is performed in relation to real property in Canada, or in relation to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or where the supply is made to a consumer resident in Canada under the digital-supply attribution rules. Exported intangible personal property, including cloud-based document automation, software access rights, and e-signature authentication services, may be zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident and acquires the property for no more than minimal use or consumption in Canada. Practical conditions: obtain a written contract with a non-resident counterparty naming the offshore recipient; invoice the foreign buyer separately; evidence the buyer's non-resident status through a foreign corporate registry extract, certificate of residence, or equivalent; and demonstrate electronic provisioning of the SaaS platform and e-signature services from/of Canada. Zero-rating is denied where the recipient is a Canadian establishment, the customer is a Canadian consumer for GST/HST purposes, or the digital service has a Canadian nexus such as authenticating contracts executed in Canada.

  • Customs export declaration for goods (Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) or carrier's ACI eManifest for goods exceeding CAD $2,000; for pure SaaS services, commercial proof of export suffices and no customs declaration is required)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or carrier certificate evidencing physical shipment for goods exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, FX bank credit advice, or bank statement evidencing payment received from the non-resident buyer
  • Written contract or signed SaaS / e-signature service agreement with the non-resident customer, including governing-law and place-of-use clauses
  • Commercial invoice in English or French showing the zero-rated supply and the exemption citation (Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act)
  • Certificate of non-resident status of the buyer (e.g., foreign corporate registry extract or W-8BEN-E type evidence for US counterparties)
  • Delivery and access logs evidencing electronic provision of cloud document automation and e-signature authentication services outside Canada (server access logs, IP and geolocation records)

No federal real-time continuous-transaction-control (CTC) clearance, API fiscal stamping, or pre-clearance e-invoicing mandate currently exists under CRA. GST/HST invoices may be issued electronically (ETA s. 223(1)) and must contain the prescribed information: supplier name, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, vendor and purchaser identification, and the total tax paid or payable. Invoices and supporting records must be in English or French, retained for six years, and produced on CRA demand. The Canadian federal e-invoicing standard is based on the PEPPOL network (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) and is voluntary for commercial suppliers; the Government of Canada has mandated recipient-side acceptance of e-invoices for federal procurement on a phased basis beginning in 2024. Quebec has separately adopted a mandatory B2B electronic-invoicing regime for registrants with a Quebec establishment, phased in starting 2026-2027, which operates alongside the federal framework.

CRA audits place heavy scrutiny on export zero-rating claims, requiring the exporter to retain compelling documentation of the non-resident status of the customer, offshore delivery and use, and FX remittance, for the six-year statutory records period. Where a zero-rating claim is unsustainable, CRA reassesses the supply at the full reverse-of-threshold rate (5% GST or applicable HST) plus interest from the date the return was due, and may impose: (i) a false-statement or misrepresentation penalty of 25% of the tax evaded or understated under ETA s. 280; (ii) a gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) at the partner level of up to 50% of the understated income where the misrepresentation was knowing or wilfully blind; (iii) revocation of any input tax credits and GST/HST refunds previously paid; (iv) criminal prosecution for tax fraud under ETA ss. 276-287 and ITA s. 239, with fines and imprisonment of up to five years; and (v) extension of the normal four-year reassessment window to eight years in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. CRA may also claw back refunds retroactively where the invoice was not supported by the mandatory export documentation or where the e-signature authentication service is determined to have been performed in relation to Canadian contracts. Canada imposes no statutory CRA FX repatriation deadline for export zero-rating; the 180-day parameter operates as an internal treasury and transfer-pricing currency-control benchmark and should be documented in contemporaneous records to avoid audit challenge.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Canada maintains no exchange controls, no requirement for prior regulatory approval on cross-border transfers, and no restrictions on dividend or profit remittance. Non-resident investors may freely repatriate capital and earnings, subject to standard tax reporting and withholding obligations under the Income Tax Act (Canada).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion requirements for export proceeds. All exports may be settled in any freely traded currency, and receivables can be kept in foreign currency at any Canadian authorized financial institution.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); no separate exchange control agency; foreign exchange policy falls within the Department of Finance Canada and OSFI supervisory perimeter.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate to low. Canada does not recognize the EU-style EMI licence; non-resident fintech/EMI providers must register as money service businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and may require provincial money-lending or payment-licensing approvals. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident limited partners must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the LP's designated CAD or USD clearing account. For amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, the LP or its bank must report to FINTRAC under the EFT reporting rules. The wire instruction must clearly state the beneficiary LP name, the account number, SWIFT/BIC of the Canadian bank, and a purpose code aligned with Canada's balance-of-payments classification (e.g., 'S010' for equity investment, or 'C010' for intercompany financing). Supporting evidence (subscription agreement, capital call notice, and LP resolution) must be retained for AML and anti-avoidance review. There is no mandatory repatriation timeline; capital may remain in foreign currency if the account is maintained as a foreign-currency account with a Canadian bank.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct operational payments (vendor SaaS licensing, cloud hosting, professional fees) with explicit invoice references; (2) Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) via a Canadian chartered bank for procurement of hardware or enterprise software licences where a deferred payment security is required; (3) SWIFT MT202 for interbank transfers between the LP's own accounts at different financial institutions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate to low. Canada does not recognize the EU-style EMI licence; non-resident fintech/EMI providers must register as money service businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and may require provincial money-lending or payment-licensing approvals. The LP's low-risk SaaS model can use a domestic bank account linked to Stripe, but offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) are viable only as payment collection agents, not as primary treasury clearing institutions. Legal restrictions include FINTRAC MSB registration if settlement in fiat; otherwise, white-label processing through a licensed Canadian payment processor is recommended.
