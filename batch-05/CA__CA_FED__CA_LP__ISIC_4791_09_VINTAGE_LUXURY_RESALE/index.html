Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - as the national tax authority administering the Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) and federal income tax; primary online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by registering through CRA's Business Registration Online portal or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) through the general partner or authorized agent. The BN serves as the statutory tax identifier and is then activated for the required GST/HST program account (RT), payroll program account (RP), and, where applicable, Customs import/export program account (RM). The limited partnership is fiscally transparent; therefore, the BN is registered in the partnership name for GST/HST and information return purposes, while each partner's share of income is reported through T5013 partnership information returns and the partners' own tax returns. Mandatory GST/HST registration applies where annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; exporters with zero-rated export supplies may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a 'check-the-box' entity classification regime. A limited partnership is pass-through by operation of law under the Income Tax Act; no election can convert the LP to a taxable corporation. Under the Excise Tax Act, subsection 273(1), a partner may elect (using Form GST25) to have a supply made by the partnership deemed to be made by a specific partner for GST/HST purposes. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are governed by specific federal anti-avoidance and deduction-timing rules, with no affirmative entity classification election available; treaty-based hybrid entity status is determined by facts and applicable double-tax treaty provisions.

Standard VAT / GST rate

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export supplies under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V. Tangible personal property is zero-rated where the supplier or the exporter delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada or supplies them to a common carrier for immediate export, and the supplier obtains documentary evidence of exportation within the required time frame. For vintage luxury goods sold online and shipped from Canada to foreign buyers, the sale is zero-rated if the goods physically leave Canada and no use is made in Canada; title and risk of loss typically pass before or upon export. Services supplied to a non-resident may qualify as zero-rated exported services where the service is not performed in respect of Canadian real property or goods and is received exclusively outside Canada. The seller must establish the export nexus through contemporaneous shipping, customs, and payment records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or electronic CERS transmission through the Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/air waybill) or courier consignment note evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank/foreign-exchange confirmation evidencing settlement of export proceeds in convertible currency)

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or a government-controlled e-invoicing clearance mandate. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,500,000. CRA accepts electronic records, EDI, XML, and standard commercial invoices; exporters must retain transactional and export documents in accessible electronic format for CRA audit review, but no pre-clearance of individual invoices is required.

CRA export audits require substantive validation of each zero-rated sale. Where adequate export documentation is absent, CRA may reverse the zero-rating and reassess the supply at 5% GST, with statutory interest accruing from the original filing date. Penalties may be imposed under the Excise Tax Act for false statements, omissions, and gross negligence, including penalties of up to 25% or 50% of the tax sought to be evaded. Input tax credits attributed to export activities may be clawed back within the normal reassessment window, and fraudulent export-credit arrangements are subject to criminal prosecution under section 380 of the Criminal Code, public disclosure under the Income Tax Act, and applicable civil penalties for participation in a false or misleading scheme.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains IMF Article VIII status with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no surrender or repatriation requirements, and no prohibition on holding, transferring, or converting Canadian dollars or foreign currency. Non-resident partnerships and limited partners may freely inject capital into and receive distributions from a Canadian Limited Partnership subject only to: (i) Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act on distributions of income or profit-remittance amounts to non-residents (generally 25%, mitigated under an applicable income tax treaty, e.g., Canada-U.S. Treaty Article on partnership distributions); (ii) the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review for controlling acquisition of Canadian businesses above economic thresholds (2023 WTO investor enterprise-value threshold CAD $1.09B; transitional threshold CAD $415M reverting upward to CAD $1.09B by 2025; higher thresholds may apply for trade agreement investors); (iii) FINTRAC mandatory reporting of electronic fund transfers of CAD $10,000 or more in a single transaction or 24-hour period; and (iv) compliance with economic sanctions, frozen-assets restrictions, and the U.S. comprehensive sanctions patterns that Canadian banks voluntarily apply to avoid correspondent de-risking. Profit remittance to non-resident partners through normal bank wires is unrestricted once the entity has obtained a CRA business number and partnership registration, and a valid NR4/NR6 filing stream is in place.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule or mandatory currency conversion ratio exists in Canada, whether for goods, services, or intangible digital-commerce sales. Export proceeds of the vintage luxury resale entity may be retained in the invoicing currency, sold forward, or converted to CAD at the entity's discretion. Obligations are limited to: (i) customs export declarations (including Electronic Commerce Export Reporting via the CBSA) for goods physically crossing the Canadian border; (ii) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reports for cross-border payments of CAD $10,000 or more where the LP or its financial institution acts as an EFT reporting entity; (iii) GST/HST zero-rating compliance under ETA Section 21 for exported goods and services, with the requirement to maintain shipping documents, customs declarations, and authentication certificates in the LP's books; (iv) standard record-keeping under sections 13 to 15 of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act requiring five years of records for any EFT sent or received on behalf of partners or sellers; and (v) agriculture-level transfer-pricing documentation, if applicable, for cross-border intercompany transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential oversight coordinated with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated financial institutions; anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing supervision and reporting administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); economic and trade sanctions administered by Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act; Luxembourg-style exchange licensing is not applicable, and no central bank approval is required for foreign exchange, capital movements, or domestic cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Canada has no European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license regime; foreign EMIs and digital payment platforms (Wise Platform, Payoneer, OFX, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex) operate in Canada as (a) unregulated or lightly regulated payment-facilitation intermediaries, or (b) Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and, since November 11, 2024, may be required to register as Payment Service Providers (PSPs) under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. The LP may lawfully open a treasury account with a foreign EMI, but funds held with such entity are not insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC), and the entity cannot provide true trust or escrow protection in Canada unless it is a regulated trust company or chartered bank. A foreign EMI is also not a qualifying deposit institution for the LP's GST/HST credit or PST/Ontario retail-sale filings. Recommended structure is to use fintech EMI rails for merchant settlement and small-vendor payouts (e.g., Stripe Connect, Escrow Pay) while maintaining the primary settlement ledger, capital, and all material liquidity in a Schedule I chartered bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident limited partners should be executed using the standard SWIFT MT103 single-credit transfer (or Fedwire for USD-denominated contributions) with the following protocols: (i) Field 50K must include the remitter's full legal name and address exactly as shown on the LP register; (ii) Field 57D/57A must identify the receiving branch of the Canadian Schedule I bank; (iii) Field 59 must include the beneficiary account name 'CA_LP' and full account number; (iv) Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state verbatim 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO CANADIAN LIMITED PARTNERSHIP UNDER LP AGREEMENT DATED [DATE]', the total contribution amount, and the relevant limited partner name; (v) if the contribution is in a third-country currency, the Canadian bank will convert at its prevailing wholesale rate with an explicit FX-conversion SLA to be signed; there is no Canadian statutory purpose-code classification system, but banks internally tag the transaction as 'S-Services / C-Capital / T-Trade' and will apply the bank's EDD classification for 'Capital Injection — Partnership'; (vi) all inbound transfers of CAD $10,000 or more trigger the bank's internal reporting and may be subject to FINTRAC EFT report; (vii) for contributions exceeding CAD $100,000, provide a bank-drafted capital-account statement to permit multi-currency funding in USD, EUR, GBP, or JPY without forced conversion; post-receipt, the bank must issue a Partnership Capital Account Notice to enable the LP's accountant to update the capital account of each partner for Canadian tax and foreign reporting purposes.Primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for: (i) vendor/supplier payouts for acquisition of vintage luxury inventory; (ii) authentication-house and grading fees; (iii) customs duty, brokerage, and freight charges for goods crossing the Canadian border; and (iv) distribution of partnership profits to limited partners as approved by the General Partner. MT103 F-70 remittance fields must include clear trade descriptors, e.g., 'INVOICE XX / AUTHENTICATION FEE / DUTY AND FREIGHT', and banking instructions must identify the beneficiary bank with BIC/IBAN where applicable; for intra-U.S. disbursements, use ABA/Fedwire routing to the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent to avoid MT103 intermediary delays. Secondary clearing rail for high-value inventory purchases is an irrevocable documentary Commercial Letter of Credit (at-sight or deferred), issued under the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP600) Art. 14; this is recommended only when acquiring from institutional European or Japanese resale houses requiring bank-to-bank settlement and title documents; the applicable documents will include commercial invoice, insurance certificate, packing list, and third-party authentication certificate appended as a non-negotiable document. Tertiary rails include the merchant settlement aggregates from Stripe (CAD settlement to the LP's bank account on T+2) and Escrow Pay (AHT+10 release after buyer confirmation) which should be remitted in batch via the bank's treasury workstation (EBICS/API) without individual MT103s; the LP must implement a segregation protocol so that platform settlement credits land on the designated settlement sub-account and are swept into the operating account only after reconciliation. All outbound disbursements above CAD $10,000 are subject to FINTRAC EFT reporting, and all disbursements to outside Canada must be screened against OFAC, Canadian SEMA, and EU/UK consolidated sanction lists by the remitting bank.Outright plain-vanilla FX forward contracts in CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP, and CAD/JPY (tenors 1-12 months) offered by Canadian Schedule I banks; executed under a cross-border master FX agreement or ISDA Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex; no central-bank bilateral approval required; must be economically linked to the LP's purchase, sale, or capital transactions to satisfy bank suitability and IFRS hedge documentation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in restricted or low-liquidity currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, IDR, TRY) for hedging emerging-market cleaning/repair and authentication costs; permitted by Canadian law without exchange-control authorization, but require ISDA 2002 and standard NDF confirmation; bank trading desk will apply credit limits and require daily mark-to-market collateral in CAD.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) and forward-rate agreements (FRAs) under ISDA 2002 with Credit Support Annex, used to swap partner capital contributions from USD/EUR/GBP into CAD or to hedge multi-currency cash flows of the LP; permitted so long as they are of a hedging nature and the LP does not hold a dealer banking license; IFRS 9 hedge accounting may be elected if the instrument is designated and documented.Market-traded FX options (European-style calls/puts) and zero-premium structures (collar or participating forward) in eligible pairings; permitted without regulatory license; the LP Agreement must expressly authorize derivative instruments; options premiums and settlement are generally collateralized under a Canadian Master Agreement or ISDA.Currency-neutral multicurrency deposit sub-accounts and treasury pooling lanes at the Canadian bank, allowing booking of incoming foreign-currency settled inventory proceeds without conversion, thereby naturally offsetting FX exposure without engaging derivative instruments; this is a cash-mobilization technique, not a derivative, and is permissible up to the bank's product-line jurisdiction.Same-day FX swap (FX Swap / spot-forward swap) to manage temporary CAD cash shortfalls around escrow release dates; permissible under OSFI bank margin-and-collateral rules as a non-cleared derivative; documented under the Canadian FX master agreement and subject to standard monthly settlement netting.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Canada has no European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license regime; foreign EMIs and digital payment platforms (Wise Platform, Payoneer, OFX, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex) operate in Canada as (a) unregulated or lightly regulated payment-facilitation intermediaries, or (b) Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC, and, since November 11, 2024, may be required to register as Payment Service Providers (PSPs) under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada. The LP may lawfully open a treasury account with a foreign EMI, but funds held with such entity are not insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC), and the entity cannot provide true trust or escrow protection in Canada unless it is a regulated trust company or chartered bank. A foreign EMI is also not a qualifying deposit institution for the LP's GST/HST credit or PST/Ontario retail-sale filings. Recommended structure is to use fintech EMI rails for merchant settlement and small-vendor payouts (e.g., Stripe Connect, Escrow Pay) while maintaining the primary settlement ledger, capital, and all material liquidity in a Schedule I chartered bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general corporate income tax rate of 15%; the Limited Partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent (flow-through), so the statutory rate applies at the partner level. Non-resident partners are taxed in Canada only on Canadian-source income allocated to the LP (Part I tax on ABI or Part XIII withholding on non-business allocations).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation; for LP distributions to non-resident partners which represent business income, treaty-dependent withholding of 15% (US) or 0% (treaty business profits where no permanent establishment) is applied via a Form NR301/NR303 reduction request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File required where the aggregate value of non-arm's-length transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year (services and intangibles) or CAD 5,000,000 (goods and financing); Master File and Country-by-Country Report required where the MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000 (inflation-adjusted to CAD 880,000,000 for 2023).

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations; instead the Income Tax Act applies the economic substance doctrine through GAAR (s. 245), the sham doctrine at common law, and specific anti-deferral rules for non-resident beneficiaries. An LP that is a mere shell with intangible mobile income can be recharacterized: the CRA will attribute Canadian-source business income back to a foreign parent if the Canadian LP lacks real business purpose, staff, or Canadian management control. The 2024 GAAR amendments (s. 245(4)) codify the 'economic substance' test to deny avoidance transactions carried out mainly to obtain a tax benefit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s. 239(1) ITA: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; GAAR denial of the tax benefit with a 50% penalty for misrepresentations to avoid shell recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner face personal liability under s. 227.1 for unremitted Part XIII withholdings; a court may pierce the partnership veil for fraud, authorize travel bans, freezing orders on personal assets, and up to 5 years imprisonment; personal restitution of the 50% gross negligence penalty is non-dischargeable for the corporation/GP.
Unregistered / Dormant Limited Partnership (Shell Company)

Statutory sanction: Dissolution of the LP plus fines up to CAD 10,000 per year under provincial partnership laws; federal CRA summary consequence: mandatory T5013 filing is denied, and deeming of the LP as a 'specified foreign partnership' triggers 33% penalty and loss of treaty eligibility for the partners.

Director personal liability: The general partner's directors are jointly and severally liable for all undistributed obligations to the CRA and third-party luxury consignors; provincial authorities can impose personal liability for unpaid provincial taxes and penalties.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 500,000 per violation for corporations; for individuals up to CAD 100,000 per violation; criminal conviction under the Criminal Code for money laundering proceeds (including the resale of luxury goods) carries imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or fail to prevent non-compliance are personally subject to the same criminal sanctions; FINTRAC can freeze personal bank accounts; director bans from financial services for 10 years under the Proceeds of Crime regulations.
Transfer Pricing Failure / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of the transaction to arm's length with interest at the prescribed CRA rate (currently 8%?), a 10% transfer pricing penalty under s. 247(3) up to 10% of the adjustment (not 50%?) — actually Canada's TP penalty is 10% of the adjustment amount for netting overstatement; the penalty applies when documentation was not contemporaneous.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unremitted penalty and interest under s. 227.1 if the penalty was caused by willful default; CRA can add director letters and a 'director's certificate' to the personal credit file following a 30-day failure to cure.
