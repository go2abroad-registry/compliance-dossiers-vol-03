Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); portal: 'My Business Account' (CRA BizWeb) and Business Registration Online (BRO). CBSA administers the RM Import/Export account.

E-government filing portal

The Limited Partnership (LP) must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA via Business Registration Online. SOP: (1) Register BN with program accounts RT (GST/HST), RM (import/export, via CBSA), RP (payroll if employees), RC (corporate income tax if a corporate general partner; LP itself is not a taxpayer). (2) GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or cumulatively over four consecutive quarters (small supplier threshold). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For Income Tax Act purposes, an LP is fiscally transparent: income/losses flow through to partners (ITA s.96) and the LP files T5013. For GST/HST (Excise Tax Act), a 'selected limited partnership' (all members limited partners plus qualifying corporate general partner) is statutorily deemed a separate person (ETA s.272.1(2)) and must register in its own right. Territorial scope: GST applies to supplies made in Canada; export of tangible personal property is zero-rated (0%). No tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election under Canadian tax law; classification follows legal form. Available elections/statuses: (a) ETA s.272.1(2) allows an LP meeting the 'selected limited partnership' criteria to be treated as a separate GST/HST registrant; ETA s.272.1(5) provides an election to deem a partnership not to be a selected partnership in specified circumstances; (b) ITA s.96(3) election to compute partnership income at the partnership level; (c) for non-resident partners, an election to file a Part I return under ITA s.216 to report net rental/timber income (not applicable to trading income) and the option to pay Part XIII tax under s.96(3.1) as a final withholding; (d) hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.94B-94D) may recharacterize or deny deductions for certain payments through hybrid arrangements/instruments involving the LP or its non-resident partners.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible personal property (dehydrated garlic flakes, crushed chili pepper) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.1 (goods exported directly or as part of a continuous journey out of Canada) and s.5 (goods exported by the supplier for the purchase consideration) provided: title to goods passes to a non-resident purchaser, the goods are exported as soon as reasonably practicable, and the LP retains proof of export (ETA s.169(1) for ITC entitlement). Directly exported goods must leave Canada within a reasonable time and not be consumed or used in Canada before export. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Canada maintains no exchange control legislation, no prior approval regime for inward or outward remittances, and no restrictions on profit repatriation. A Canadian Limited Partnership may receive capital contributions from non-resident partners and remit distributions freely. The only frictions are fiscal: Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, reduced to applicable treaty rates) on distributions of taxable income to non-resident partners, and timely filing of the partnership information return (T5013) with allocation schedules. Statutory reporting obligations to FINTRAC apply for large cash transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 and electronic fund transfers exceeding CAD 10,000, but such reports are not controls on convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender requirements and no mandatory conversion ratios. Exporters of bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper may invoice and retain proceeds in USD, EUR, CNY, or any freely convertible currency without Central Bank permission. The only administrative requirement is customs statistical reporting: the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and Statistics Canada require a B13A Export Declaration for commercial shipments valued above CAD 2,000 per shipment, filed electronically via the Canadian Online Export Reporting System (GCR), for statistical purposes only.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with complementary oversight by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) over federally chartered deposit-taking institutions, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/CFT supervisory authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with material constraints. Foreign digital electronic money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Currenxie, and comparable EMIs) may provide CAD-denominated virtual accounts to a Canadian Limited Partnership, but only if the provider holds a restricted registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), supervised by the Bank of Canada. As of the current supervisory cycle, non-domestic EMIs must register with the Bank of Canada to provide payment services in Canada or to Canadian customers. Critical limitations: EMIs cannot clear directly through the Lynx or ACSS settlement systems and must sponsor through a Schedule I chartered bank; they cannot issue or advise commercial letters of credit; and they generally cap monthly throughput, rendering them unsuitable for bulk dehydrated vegetable trade settlements above approximately CAD 1 million annually. Conclusion: acceptable for supplier test payments and small-value disbursements, but a Schedule I bank operating account is mandatory as the primary liquidity spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital contributions are executed via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident partner's bank to the partnership's CAD or USD operating account at the tier-1 Canadian bank. The payment narrative must state 'LP Capital Contribution - Partnership Account Reference [number]' and the remitter should include the partner identification code; the receiving Canadian bank will code the transaction as 'capital transfer' and, where the remitter is a non-resident entity, the bank may request the partnership to provide a copy of the executed capital call notice and the partnership agreement for FATCA/CRS classification. There is no prior Bank of Canada approval, no exchange control permit, and no mandatory conversion: USD-denominated contributions may be held in a USD sub-account to avoid FX conversion in the inbound leg. For CAD funding, the bank will clear large values via Lynx (real-time gross settlement) and the partnership must transmit a FINTRAC EFT report if the transaction exceeds CAD 10,000. Documentation retained for clearing verification: capital call notice, partner remittance instruction, bank confirmation of value date, and CRS/FATCA self-certification forms for each non-resident partner.Outbound supplier and logistics disbursements use two rails: (a) Telegraphic Transfer via SWIFT MT103 for low-value replenishment purchases of dehydrated garlic flakes and chili pepper, with the CAD or USD funds cleared via the Canadian bank's correspondent network (Fedwire and CHIPS in the United States; compliance screening against OFAC, UN, and Canadian sanctions lists at both the originating and correspondent banks); and (b) Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary credit) via SWIFT MT700 for bulk orders exceeding USD 50,000, where the Canadian bank issues an irrevocable LC in favor of the overseas supplier, with settlement upon presentation of commercial invoice, packing list, full set of ocean or air waybill bills of lading, sanitary/phytosanitary certificate, and certificate of origin. The partnership should maintain a USD-denominated operating account to pay LC beneficiaries in USD without double conversion. Documentary collection (D/P) via SWIFT MT422 is a permissible fallback for established counterparties. Foreign digital electronic money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Currenxie, and comparable EMIs) may provide CAD-denominated virtual accounts to a Canadian Limited Partnership, but only if the provider holds a restricted registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), supervised by the Bank of Canada. Critical limitations: EMIs cannot clear directly through the Lynx or ACSS settlement systems and must sponsor through a Schedule I chartered bank; they cannot issue or advise commercial letters of credit; and they generally cap monthly throughput, rendering them unsuitable for bulk dehydrated vegetable trade settlements above approximately CAD 1 million annually. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

The Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96: no federal corporate income tax is payable at the LP level; each general and limited partner is taxed on its allocated share of partnership income. Reference federal headline rates for partners who are corporations: 15% general corporate rate, reduced to 9% for the first CAD 500,000 of active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) partner. For individual partners, federal top marginal rate is 33%, producing a combined federal-provincial rate of approximately 44% to 54% depending on province of residence. Non-resident partners are taxable under Part I of the ITA on their share of Canadian-source business income, subject to treaty relief.
