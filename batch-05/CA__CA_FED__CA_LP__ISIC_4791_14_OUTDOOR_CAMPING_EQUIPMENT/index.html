Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); official registration portal is CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and the GST/HST account dashboard is accessed through the My Business Account portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-business-portal.html.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned Canadian limited partnership must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA, which serves as both the income tax and GST/HST identifier; registration is completed via Business Registration Online or Form RC1, with the partnership itself (not the individual partners) entered as the registrant. The BN is used as-is for income tax and gains the 'RT' program/account suffix once the partnership registers for GST/HST under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act. Mandatory GST/HST registration applies once worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 over any four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration below the threshold is available. A partnership with non-resident partners that carries on business in Canada must also file T5013 partnership information returns and designate a resident Canadian representative for service of process and tax administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box entity classification election; a limited partnership is fiscally transparent by default under the Income Tax Act, with each partner reporting his or her allocated share of partnership income. Relevant statutory elections include the Excise Tax Act section 273 election to have a closely related corporation or partnership treated as a single registrant for GST/HST; Income Tax Act section 97(2) rollover election on contribution of property to the partnership; subsection 96(3) election respecting partnership fiscal year; and the mandatory hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act sections 12.7 and 18.4, which neutralize deduction and non-inclusion outcomes arising from fiscally transparent or hybrid partnership structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada during any four consecutive calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory on the day the threshold is exceeded and the GST/HST applies from that day forward. In harmonized provinces the federal registration automatically covers the provincial HST component; separate provincial sales tax accounts may be required in BC, SK, MB, and QC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 1, Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act for a supply of goods by a registrant (the Canadian LP) where the goods are exported from Canada, or delivered outside Canada, by the supplier or the non-resident purchaser within 60 days of the supply (or within a longer period accepted by the CRA), and the supplier retains documentary proof of export. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI where the services are performed in Canada for a non-resident and are not otherwise excepted; for internet retailing of outdoor camping equipment, goods export zero-rating is the dominant regime, including distance sales to US and other non-resident consumers where the goods are physically exported and the supplier maintains carrier and customs evidence. Fulfilment models using Canadian warehouses with direct-to-consumer cross-border shipment satisfy the export test; exceptions apply when goods are supplied to a non-resident purchaser within Canada before export.

  • B13A Canada Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or the Canadian International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or carrier cargo-control document evidencing physical movement from Canada
  • Proof of export report (carrier export manifest, customs release/exit certificate, or bonded warehouse removal document)
  • Sales invoice, purchase order, and delivery evidence identifying the non-resident purchaser and the exported outdoor camping goods
  • Bank remittance, credit card settlement, or foreign-exchange conversion records evidencing collection of the export proceeds

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for e-invoices. The CRA mandates that every sales invoice issued by a GST/HST registrant (paper or electronic) display the supplier's name, the GST/HST registration number (BN with RT suffix), the invoice date, the total consideration, and the amount of tax, with electronic records required to be retained in an intelligible, machine-readable format upon audit. The federal government supports voluntary e-invoicing under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing framework (Peppol BIS 3.0-based), but participation is not statutory and no API pre-clearance or security-stamp requirement applies.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply as taxable if the exporter fails to provide prescribed proof of export within the 60-day window or the documentation is insufficient; the LP becomes liable for the uncollected GST/HST, arrears interest, and penalties from the original due date. Civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 1,500 and 20% of the tax sought to be evaded (s. 285.1), as well as per-occurrence penalties for false statements or returns. Criminal prosecution for wilful evasion can lead to fines of up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment, in addition to full clawback of the zero-rating benefit. The normal four-year reassessment window is extended without limitation where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default is established, allowing retrospective clawback of export zero-rating benefits for prior open years; for income-tax purposes, non-arm's length export pricing with delayed repatriation beyond 180 days may further trigger transfer-pricing adjustments under section 247 of the Income Tax Act.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating currency; both current-account and capital-account items are unrestricted under the Currency Act. Profit remittance and partner distributions to non-residents are permitted without central-bank approval or fiscal consent; however, cross-border transfers are subject to FINTRAC reporting (CAD 10,000 threshold per incoming or outgoing transfer) and transaction-level reporting under Statistics Canada's International Transactions in Services and Balance of Payments framework. No FX clearing licence is required for a limited partnership (LP) to receive or disburse CAD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export receipts. E-commerce and retail export proceeds (ISIC 4791) may be retained in foreign currency, converted, or reinvested freely. Export entries must still be filed under the Customs Act (B13 declaration) when goods cross the frontier, and financial institutions retain record-keeping duties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for cross-border transactions above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign-exchange administrative oversight is shared with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential licensing and with FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing surveillance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Registered payment-service providers (Stripe, PayPal, Wise, Payoneer) are legally viable as settlement rails under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which mandates registration with the Bank of Canada for any PSP serving Canadian end-users. Foreign digital EMIs without RPAA registration and OSFI-supervised clearing access cannot function as a sole treasury anchor; they should be operated as payout and collection sub-rails over a Schedule I bank primary account. A Canadian LP may open EMI wrappers only after the merchant-agreement chain, PCI-DSS attestation, and fund-flow mapping are documented.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partner capital injections are remitted by SWIFT MT103 / ISO 20022 credit transfer to the beneficiary account at a Schedule I bank, with the remittance field specifying 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - LIMITED PARTNERSHIP' and the LP's provincial registry number to prevent mischaracterization as income. There is no pre-clearance by the Bank of Canada; however, the funds must correspond to the partnership agreement's contributed capital schedules for tax and securities-law consistency. Any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more into or out of Canada is reportable to FINTRAC. CAD legs clear in real time on Lynx (the Bank of Canada's high-value payment system, successor to LVTS); USD legs clear via the correspondent network (RBC New York, BMO Harris, or Scotiabank's US network). Inbound verification package includes the contribution schedule, partner instruction letter, and source-of-funds documentation retained for five years.Trade disbursements for retail inventory and fulfilment are executed via (i) SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments up to USD 50,000 equivalent; (ii) documentary or Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 (MT700 issuance) for first-run suppliers or commitments above USD 250,000; (iii) Lynx real-time gross settlement for CAD-denominated domestic vendor disbursements; and (iv) Stripe and PayPal standard payouts for customer refunds and micro-scale disbursements. A multicurrency operating ledger with CAD, USD, EUR, and CNH sub-accounts and standing settlement instructions (SSI) minimizes conversion costs.
