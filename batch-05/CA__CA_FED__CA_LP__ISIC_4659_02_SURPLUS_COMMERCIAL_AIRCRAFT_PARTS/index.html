Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST Rulings Directorate; Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration-online

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or using the Business Registration Online portal. The partnership must open a GST/HST program account if it is a registrant and may also need an Import/Export (RM) program account for customs purposes. For the BN application, identify the limited partnership itself as the person, provide the partnership agreement details, the Canadian representative or designated partner's name and address, and the expected date of first taxable supply in Canada. Non-resident applicants without a domestic address must provide a Canadian contact address or authorize a representative. The CRA may issue the BN immediately upon online registration or within 10 to 15 business days for paper filings. The GST/HST registration, where mandatory, commences on the earlier of the date the partnership first makes a taxable supply in Canada and the date it ceases to qualify as a small supplier. CRA program accounts updated by filing Form RC151 or via My Business Account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; income tax classification of a limited partnership is statutory under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act, making the partnership fiscally transparent unless an election is made for a taxable Canadian corporation or other special vehicle. Relevant elections include: (1) election under subsection 96(3) of the Income Tax Act to have a shared fiscal period for all members; (2) joint venture or partnership GST/HST elections under section 273 of the Excise Tax Act; (3) the section 156(2) election for closely related corporations; (4) the domestic reverse-charge election under section 178.8 for non-resident suppliers in certain service and intangible supplies; and (5) options under the Canada–US tax treaty to treat a Canadian limited partnership as a hybrid entity, with corresponding reporting through a CRA Form 52 or partnership fiscal transparency statement. No election exists to treat the LP as an entity taxable at the partnership level for Canadian income tax; each partner is taxed on allocated income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies over any trailing 12-month period; however, a non-resident partnership making taxable supplies in Canada is generally required to register for GST/HST as soon as it 'carries on business' in Canada and ceases to be a small supplier. Once registered, the registrant must collect GST/HST on supplies made in Canada unless the supply is zero-rated or exempt. Export supplies are disregarded when determining the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold if proper evidence of export is maintained.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Tangible personal property qualifies for zero-rating only if the supplier exports the goods directly or causes them to be shipped or delivered to a destination outside Canada, and the supplier retains documentary proof of export; property sold to a non-resident who resells inside Canada does not qualify. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person, provided the supply is not made in respect of real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of the supply, or a service the value of which is attributable primarily to goods or services consumed or used in Canada, subject to specific exceptions for advisory, professional and similar services to non-residents. Charitable and non-profit export supply exclusions do not apply to commercial aircraft parts trading. For integrated aircraft parts, the zero-rating of parts that are installed on civil aircraft may also be available under Schedule VI, Part I, section 3 or similar removals/exports provisions, provided the parts are shipped permanently out of Canada.

No mandatory electronic invoicing or government real-time fiscal clearance system exists at the federal level. Canada does not require digital stamping or API-based invoice clearance. The CRA requires registrants to issue invoices containing the supplier's name, Business Number and GST/HST registration number, the invoice date, a description of the goods/service, the amount of consideration, and the tax rate and amount. Electronic invoices and records are accepted if they are complete, unalterable and available to the CRA on demand; non-resident suppliers may satisfy invoicing requirements with electronic data interchange and digital record-keeping, but must preserve all source documents for six years.

CRA audits apply a heightened evidentiary standard for export zero-rating claims because the data relied upon is typically foreign-origin. Where a zero-rated supply is reassessed as taxable, the partnership is liable for previously uncollected GST/HST, interest under section 128 of the Excise Tax Act, and penalties. Reassessment beyond the normal four-year limitation period is permitted for misrepresentation attributable to negligence, fraud, carelessness or wilful default, allowing CRA to claw back refunds indefinitely in cases of fraud or wilful misstatement. A person convicted of tax evasion under section 239 of the Excise Tax Act is liable to a fine of up to 200% of tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to five years. In addition, the penalty under section 285 applies to knowingly making a false statement: a fine equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the tax, and any refund claimed may be denied, requiring the taxpayer to seek judicial review before the Tax Court of Canada. False export documentation can also trigger liability under the Customs Act for making false statements, with penalties up to CAD 25,000 per declaration and, in severe cases, seizure of aircraft parts or alternation of the exporter's bonded carrier status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime. Both current-account trade settlements and capital-account transactions, including equity contributions, partnership distributions, and repatriation of surplus, are unrestricted and require no Bank of Canada or Department of Finance approval. The only material statutory frictions are Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory rate 25%, generally reduced under applicable tax treaties such as the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention) on partnership distributions and certain interest payments, plus the requirement that all transactions comply with Canadian economic sanctions and anti-money-laundering law. Profit remittances and capital flows may be freely converted through any Schedule I Canadian bank, and no prior convertibility approval is required for ISIC 4659 aircraft-parts import, export, or distribution flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters and limited partnerships may retain foreign currency in Canadian foreign-currency accounts or offshore. The applicable compliance gates are: (i) CBSA G13 or equivalent export reporting under the Customs Act for physical goods; (ii) the Export and Import Permits Act and Controlled Goods Regulations, which may require an export permit for controlled, dual-use, or defence-related aviation technology; and (iii) FINTRAC reporting obligations for international electronic fund transfers or actual cross-border currency movements of CAD 10,000 or more. Export receipts may be held in USD and converted at the point of disbursement to achieve a natural hedge.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating with the Department of Finance Canada for sanctions and macro-financial policy and with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border anti-money-laundering surveillance. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange administration; the Bank of Canada administers monetary policy and oversees national high-value settlement through Lynx, which has replaced the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners inject capital by SWIFT MT103 credit transfer to the partnerships named Canadian capital-escrow account using the receiving Schedule I bank's SWIFT BIC, with the free-format message field referencing 'LP capital contribution, capital account subscription, no goods'. No Canadian exchange-control approval or certificate is required. The receiving bank performs source-of-funds verification, sanctions screening, and FINTRAC reporting for any international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. For contributions above CAD 100,000, the limited partnership should pre-submit a contribution notice, capital-account instruction, and evidence of the underlying limited-partnership agreement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for escrow, Medium for low-value operational disbursements. A non-resident or unlicensed electronic money institution cannot maintain deposit accounts equivalent to a Canadian bank account, cannot settle through Lynx or the Canadian cheque-clearing system, and is not OSFI-prudentially supervised. An EU- or UK-licensed EMI may operate in Canada only as a registered money service business under FINTRAC and applicable provincial MSB rules; even then, it cannot lawfully provide escrow services or documentary collection facilities for the aviation-parts industry. Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut-type rails are acceptable for minor administrative disbursements, but the partnerships primary treasury, FX, and high-value settlement functions must rest with a Schedule I bank. Escrow of aviation transaction proceeds requires a Canadian chartered bank, a licensed trust company, or a regulated escrow agent.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate; for the Limited Partnership the rate applies only at the partner level on Canadian-source business income, with combined federal/provincial rates ranging from 23% to 31%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII statutory rate is 25% on dividend payments to non-resident persons; tax treaties generally reduce this to 15% for portfolio dividends and to 5% for direct corporate participation of at least 10% of the voting shares. For a limited partnership, an ordinary distribution of allocable active business income to a non-resident partner is not subject to dividend withholding tax under domestic law; it is taxed on a net basis under Part I if the partner carries on business through a Canadian permanent establishment, unless treaty recharacterization applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of all cross-border non-arm's-length transactions in a taxation year triggers the CRA's expectation of comprehensive contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, to be retained for six years. For MNE groups with consolidated revenue above C$750 million, Country-by-Country reporting and master-file/local-file-style documentation are required for Canadian filing.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not issue a standalone ESR code, but the economic substance doctrine is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under ITA s.245, the business purpose test, and treaty limitation-on-benefits or principal-purpose tests. The CRA will deny partnership status to a limited partnership that lacks genuine Canadian commercial activity, real local assets or staff, and a profit-seeking joint venture relationship; a letter-box partnership with only bare ownership of aircraft part contracts will be treated as a taxable Canadian business or as an agency arrangement lacking treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax; Criminal Code s.380: up to 10 years imprisonment and an unlimited fine. Civil assessments also include gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under ITA s.163(2), plus accrued daily interest.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors who authorized, participated, or acquiesced in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for CRA assessments, with civil execution against personal assets, CRA-issued travel bans on tax debts, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Sham / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under ITA s.245; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; CRA may deem a permanent establishment in Canada and assess Canadian-source income in the hands of the non-resident beneficiary. A gross negligence penalty of 50% applies for false statements; Transport Canada may suspend or revoke the aviation spares distribution licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and promoters who established the sham can be personally liable for the full tax and penalties; CRA may register a certificate of personal liability in Federal Court and attach assets. Travel restrictions and refusal of CRA clearance certificates are common enforcement measures.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to C$1,000,000 for the entity and up to C$500,000 for individuals; failure to report is a criminal offence punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment; the aviation parts and proceeds are subject to forfeiture as offence-related property.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly participated or were negligent in implementing the prescribed compliance regime; travel bans may be imposed under criminal process, and disqualification from serving as an aviation goods broker may follow.
Withholding Tax / Part XIII Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally liable under ITA s.227(1) for the unremitted amounts and for penalties under s.227(8): 10% of the amount not deducted, increased to 20% for a second violation; interest compounds daily on the assessed arrearage.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under ITA s.227.1 for all withholding amounts that were required to be withheld; CRA may issue a security certificate against the director's property and use travel inhibition procedures where the amount remains unpaid.
