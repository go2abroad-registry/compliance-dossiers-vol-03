Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Federal tax administration; portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html) and GST/HST NETFILE; federal business registration via Canada Business Registration (https://www.canada.ca/business-registration).

E-government filing portal

A Limited Partnership (in Canada, governed by provincial LP statutes but federally registered) obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from CRA using Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number') or the online Canada Business Registration service. The LP then opens a GST/HST account (program account 'RT') once taxable worldwide sales equal or exceed C$30,000 in a calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident partners without a Canadian permanent establishment must file Form RC59 (Business Number Request — Non-Resident) and may be required to file a T5013 Partnership Information Return. The BN serves as the federal tax identification number for income tax, GST/HST, customs, and payroll accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes: the partnership itself is generally not subject to federal income tax, and income/losses are allocated to, and taxed at the partner level; the LP files an information return (T5013) and non-resident partners may be subject to Canadian tax under Part I (s.2(3) ITA) on Canadian-source income or Part XIII withholding on certain payments.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' entity classification election exists in Canada. Available statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s.103 allocation election to vary partnership income/loss sharing ratios (with CRA concurrence); (ii) s.152(4) waiver of reassessment limitation period; (iii) Excise Tax Act s.156/156.2 elections for closely related corporations/partnerships for nil-consideration GST/HST supplies; (iv) s.150(2) ITA election for non-resident partners to file taxable Canadian income on a net basis; (v) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss.18.2/18.4 which are mandatory — no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in worldwide gross annual revenue from taxable supplies (including 'cross-border digital supplies' such as EdTech subscriptions made to persons resident in Canada); non-resident suppliers above this threshold (measured on a quarterly basis over four consecutive calendar quarters) are required to register for GST/HST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated exports under the Excise Tax Act Schedule VI (Part V for goods; s.23 for services) and Part IX (s.21/22 transitional rules): the supplier must demonstrate that (a) the supply is made to a non-resident recipient, (b) the supply is not acquired for consumption/use in Canada, and (c) for digital EdTech subscription services (SaaS/automated tutoring), the recipient's ordinary place of residence and payment jurisdiction establish a non-Canadian place of supply; payments routed via Apple IAP / Google Play Billing to Canadian platform providers must segregate non-resident buyers and retain geo-location/payment evidence to sustain zero-rating.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration (G13/G13A) or e-manifest via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / electronic delivery proof for physical goods (not applicable to digital-only EdTech supplies)
  • Commercial Invoice and underlying subscription/software license agreement
  • Non-resident recipient evidence: IP address, billing country, bank/payment processor reports (Apple IAP / Google Play settlement statements)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign currency receipt evidence from the recipient

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is imposed at the federal level in Canada. Taxpayers who exceed C$1.5 million in annual taxable revenue must file GST/HST returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE); retention of electronic records is mandatory. Participation in the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing standard (PEPPOL-based, interoperable with EU regimes) is voluntary, not a clearance mandate.

CRA actively scrutinizes zero-rating claims through the GST/HST Export Verification and Refund Integrity programs. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims trigger: (i) full assessment of unreported GST/HST plus compound interest under ETA; (ii) penalties under ETA s.285(1) for false statements — the greater of C$250 and 10% of the tax sought to be avoided; (iii) gross negligence penalties up to 50% of the deficiency where the claim was made knowingly or with willful blindness; (iv) retrospective reassessment beyond the normal four-year statutory period where misrepresentation or fraud is established; and (v) potential criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code for fraudulent refund claims, with clawbacks of input tax credits and denial of future zero-rating.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and OECD Liberalisation Codes; no statutory exchange controls restrict conversion of CAD, inbound capital, or outward profit repatriation. A Canadian Limited Partnership may remit profits and capital to non-resident partners without FX authorisation; however, Part I tax on Canadian-source allocated income, Part XIV branch tax on allocated income of non-resident corporate partners, and Part XIII withholding on certain passive payments may apply, with domestic rates commonly reduced by applicable tax treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding-period restriction on export receipts or foreign-currency revenues. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts without reporting to the Bank of Canada; only statistical reporting to Statistics Canada under Balance of Payments or Foreign Trade surveys may apply above prescribed thresholds, and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting applies to CAD 10,000-or-more wire transfers.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with Canadian foreign exchange policy jointly administered by the Department of Finance; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) supervises AML/ATF obligations, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates federally chartered banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. Canada does not issue an 'electronic money institution' licence, but non-bank payment service providers must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) before performing retail payment clearing, settlement, or payment-account functions for Canadians. A foreign digital EMI may offer cross-border collections and disbursements as a FINTRAC-registered money service business, yet such an account does not qualify as a Canadian deposit account and is unsuitable for capital escrow, debt-covenant cash balances, or long-term treasury concentration. Use of licensed payment corridors (e.g., Wise, Convera) is operationally viable for small-value subscription disbursements but must not replace a Schedule I banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions must be received exclusively through identified bank-channel transfers: SWIFT MT103 wires from the contributing partner's account to the LP's segregated operational or escrow account at a Schedule I Canadian bank, using Field 70 narrative 'LP Capital Contribution' and an OECD/SWIFT purpose description consistent with direct investment (e.g., EQUI). The receiving bank applies PCMLTFA know-your-client checks and must file a Large Transaction Record with FINTRAC for any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; the compliance file should include the partnership agreement, the capital-call notice, and partner source-of-wealth confirmation. CAD/USD conversion of inbound funds is executed at the clearing bank's intraday market rate or a pre-negotiated corporate conversion rate.Standard supplier and trade payments are cleared via SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers; documentary trade transactions use commercial letters of credit issued through MT700 (with MT720 and MT740 for amendments and reimbursement as applicable). CAD settlement clears through Lynx using CDSX, while USD-denominated disbursements clear via the U.S. Fedwire or CHIPS corridors at Canadian bank U.S. correspondents.
