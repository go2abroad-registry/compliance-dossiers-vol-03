Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) - CRA My Business Account online portal; provincial tax authority for Ontario is the Ontario Ministry of Finance with respect to employer health tax and other provincial levies, though Ontario's HST is federally administered

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and register for GST/HST and corporate income tax. Step 1: Submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or register online through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO); Step 2: Provide constituent documents (e.g., articles of incorporation, foreign registration certificate), proof of identity of directors/officers, and a Canadian address for service or appoint a representative; Step 3: CRA issues a 9-digit BN with RZ (GST/HST) and RC (corporate income tax) program account numbers; Step 4: If the entity is a non-resident corporation, file Form RC4647 or indicate non-resident status to establish branch reporting and payment obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canada Business Corporation is a separate taxable entity subject to federal and provincial corporate income tax, with no check-the-box pass-through treatment for Canadian tax purposes; resident corporations are taxable on worldwide income while non-resident corporations are taxable on Canadian-source income only

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: Income Tax Act s.85 (tax-deferred transfer of property to a corporation), s.216(1) (election for non-resident corporations to compute tax on Canadian rental income on a net basis), and Excise Tax Act s.156 / Form GST20 for closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as non-supplies; hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s.18.4-18.6 limit certain excessive interest or royalty deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: persons making taxable supplies in Canada exceeding CAD 30,000 over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters must register for GST/HST; non-resident entities must register if they make taxable supplies in Canada and are not small suppliers; a non-resident that does not carry on business in Canada may still elect to register voluntarily under the simplified non-resident registration procedure

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of tangible personal property exported from Canada when the supplier obtains evidence establishing the goods were exported as part of the supply; the supply must be to an export of goods within the meaning of section 1 of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated only if the service is performed wholly or partly outside Canada and is exported as defined in section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI; manufacturing and packaging of instant rice noodles for export qualifies if documentation and title transfer support the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Export Reporting System / Canada Customs Coding Form B13A) and the exporter's proof of export
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill or equivalent carrier document showing the goods removed from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire / settlement evidence proving payment from the foreign purchaser
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase order matching the exported goods

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is imposed for GST/HST. Electronic invoicing is acceptable if records are legible and retainable; CRA accepts PEPPOL standards for voluntary e-invoicing involving public entities. Taxpayers must preserve source documents, accounting records, and electronic data in a readable/auditable format for CRA inspection. Ontario has no additional e-invoice clearance mandate.

CRA may reassess a zero-rated supply within the normal 4-year limitation period; where misrepresentation or fraud is involved, reassessment is open-ended and no limitation applies. Penalties under Excise Tax Act sections 280 and 285 include a general penalty of 5% of the net tax plus interest, repeated violations may attract 10%; false statements or evasive export documentation can result in gross negligence penalties, fines up to CAD 25,000 or 200% of the tax evaded, and imprisonment for up to 5 years. A taxpayer claiming zero-rating without substantiation is liable for uncollected HST, arrears interest, and potential revocation of registration privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account, with full acceptance of IMF Article VIII obligations and no licensing, approval, or repatriation restriction on profit remittances, intercompany loans, dividends, or trade settlements. Dividend remittances to a PRC beneficial shareholder are free of exchange-control interference; the only financial friction is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, set at a default 25% and generally reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement, deducted by the paying bank as tax-withholding agent. There is no minimum stay period, no forced conversion, and no requirement to monetize retained earnings in CAD before overseas disbursement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation quota, or surrender obligation on export proceeds. A Canadian exporter of retort rice-noodle products may hold foreign currency indefinitely in domestic foreign-currency bank accounts, may convert freely at market rates, and is subject only to standard FINTRAC record-keeping for cross-border transfers above CAD 10,000 and to statistical Balance of Payments reporting to Statistics Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Ottawa; prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/ATF oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange administration body; exchange-control functions were abolished and replaced by a floating-currency framework under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. Payments clearing infrastructure (LVTS, ACSS, Real-Time Rail) is governed by Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary cross-border payment corridors and Low for primary treasury operations. Offshore digital banks and electronic-money institutions are not licensed under the Bank Act (Canada), hold no deposit-taking authorization in Ontario, are not CDIC-insured, and cannot originate Canadian ACH or LVTS settlements. They may lawfully operate as foreign money-services businesses only if registered with FINTRAC, and their use must be limited to vendor disbursements or collections where the counterparty insists on non-bank rails. The Ontario corporation must maintain its capital escrow, payroll, GST/HST remittance, and principal CAD liquidity with a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank.
