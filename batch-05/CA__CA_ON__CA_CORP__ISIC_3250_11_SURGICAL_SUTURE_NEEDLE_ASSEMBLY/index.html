Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); operational portals: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-services.html) and the Canada Business Registration BizPortal (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes.html). Ontario corporate income tax is administered federally by the CRA under the Ontario Corporate Tax (OCT) administration agreement; the Ontario Ministry of Finance publishes the Ontario Corporations Tax Act, but assessment and collection are handled by the CRA.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity registering to carry on business in Canada must obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) signed by an authorized officer; no notarization or physical Canadian presence is mandatory, but a Canadian registered agent or representative is recommended. The applicant activates the following program accounts: Corporation Income Tax (RC0001), GST/HST (RT0001), Payroll (RP0001) if hiring Ontario employees, and Import/Export Account (RM0001) with the CBSA. CRA issues BN Confirmation Form RC2 (Logo and Business Number); non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada, importing goods, or carrying on business in Ontario are required to hold a GST/HST registration under ETA s. 240; failure to register triggers mandatory registration once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded. The Ontario facility additionally requires registration with the Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB) and an Ontario Employer Health Tax (EHT) account, both linked to the BN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian business corporation (federal or Ontario CA_CORP) is resident in Canada under ITA s. 89 and s. 250 where incorporated federally or provincially, and is subject to Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; no check-the-box pass-through election is available for corporate taxpayers. Income attributable to the permanent establishment in Ontario is allocated to Ontario and taxed under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act, administered through the CRA OCT system. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Canadian business corporation (federal or Ontario CA_CORP) is resident in Canada under ITA s. 89 and s. 250 where incorporated federally or provincially, and is subject to Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income; no check-the-box pass-through election is available for corporate taxpayers. Income attributable to the permanent establishment in Ontario is allocated to Ontario and taxed under the Ontario Corporations Tax Act, administered through the CRA OCT system. Non-resident shareholders face Part XIII withholding tax or Part XIV branch tax, and the entity must file a T2 Corporation Income Tax Return on a full accrual basis. No statutory tax-exempt zone applies to the manufacturing facility; Ontario generally imposes 13% HST on taxable supplies, and available incentives such as the federal SR&ED investment tax credit and Ontario regional development credits are statutory credits, not exemptions.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; available statutory elections for this entity include: (1) private corporation status election under ITA s. 89(11) with dividend refund implications; (2) GST/HST closely related corporations election under ETA ss. 156(1)-(2) to treat taxable supplies between a parent and its 90%-or-more-owned subsidiaries as made for no consideration; (3) joint venture election under ETA ss. 273(1) and 273(3); (4) quick method election for GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000; (5) election under ETA s. 211.1 for exported third-party goods; (6) capital cost allowance and recapture elections under ITA s. 13 and Regulations Schedule II, including a subsection 85(1) rollover election for property transfers among related corporations; (7) section 216(1) election for non-resident investment income; (8) transfer pricing contemporaneous documentation election under ITA s. 247 for intercompany pricing of the suture needle exports; and (9) hybrid mismatch reporting and ordering adjustment elections under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.21, subject to specific statutory conditions and filed on prescribed CRA forms within the applicable due dates.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains fully convertible current and capital accounts for the Canadian dollar with no exchange control licensing regime. Cross-border profit repatriation is freely permissible subject to corporate solvency and compliance with non-resident withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.)). There are no restrictions on foreign currency account holdings, and Canadian-domiciled corporations may denominate internal treasury balances in USD, CAD, or any other selected currency. Required reporting and documentary formalities are driven by anti-money-laundering regulations and tax transparency rather than exchange control authority.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained in the invoicing currency or repatriated as business judgment dictates. However, any cross-border wire transfer or related transaction involving CAD $10,000 or more, including foreign currency equivalents, must be reported to FINTRAC as part of the Large Cash Transaction and Cross-Border Wire Transfer reporting framework. Additionally, Section 235.2 reporting under the Income Tax Act mandates disclosure of reportable transactions, but not the surrender or conversion of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary banking rail. Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) generally cannot obtain direct clearing and settlement accounts with Canadian payments infrastructure under OSFI and the Retail Payment Activities Act (Canada). They may be used as supplementary treasury tools only if they operate through a Canadian Schedule I bank as the correspondent holding institution. Direct account opening with a non-resident EMI for the operational treasury of a Canadian manufacturing corporation introduces unnecessary transaction reporting opacity and is not recommended for statutory compliance purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use correspondent bank wire transfer protocols FILTERED through trusted Schedule I Canadian banks as the receiving institution. The optimal rail is SWIFT MT103 over the Fedwire or CHIPS corridor when sourcing USD from abroad, with the Canadian operating account denominated in CAD or USD. The wire instruction must disclose the Canadian corporation name and business number, the domestic bank's institutional identifier, and the purpose code consistent with equity contribution or shareholder loan. For tax-treaty-transparent capital injections, the sender should reference 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION' or 'NON-RESIDENT PARENT LOAN' in the narrative field. The receiving Canadian bank will perform sanctions screening and beneficial ownership cross-checking. Where the injection is material, a pre-approval letter from the Canadian bank treasury desk, a completed commercial banking facility application, and evidence of previously filed corporate tax balance sheet confirmations should be supplied.Settlement for purchase of raw materials and export-related services should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 for non-documentary trade payments, and SWIFT MT700/MT707 for documentary letters of credit used with large surgical material suppliers. The commercial LC rail is preferred for high-volume fabric and packaging purchases due to the embedding of negotiable bills of lading and inspection certificates into the clearing cycle. CAD payments must be cleared over the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) / Lynx, while USD payments may be cleared through the Continuous Linked Settlement accounts by the Canadian clearing bank or routed via Chase clearing at the New York level.
