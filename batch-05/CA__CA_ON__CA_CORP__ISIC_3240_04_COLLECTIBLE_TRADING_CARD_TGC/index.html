Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal tax administration and GST/HST authority; portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration. For Ontario provincial corporate income tax, the CRA administers the Ontario Corporate Tax (OCT) program on behalf of the Ontario Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident corporations must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) plus a GST/HST program account (RT suffix) prior to carrying on commercial activity in Canada. Procedure: (1) Submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or register through Business Registration Online (BRO) with a Canadian address for service or a Canadian representative; (2) Provide certified certificate of incorporation or equivalent foreign corporate registry proof; (3) Register separately for the GST/HST program account if taxable supplies made in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, or voluntarily to recover input tax credits (ITCs); (4) Ontario corporate income tax account (RC program) is automatically created via the OCT federal/provincial integration; foreign entities without a permanent establishment in Canada generally file a Part I non-resident corporate return (T2) only if Canadian-source taxable income exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian resident corporations (and Ontario-registered extra-provincial corporations) are taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income, plus Ontario corporate income tax (10% general rate; 3.2% reduced rate on first CAD 500,000 of active business income for Canadian-controlled private corporations). Non-resident corporations are taxable on Canadian-source income and may be subject to Part XIV branch tax. No pass-through entitlement. Territorial scope: resident = worldwide; non-resident = Canadian-source only. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Canadian resident corporations (and Ontario-registered extra-provincial corporations) are taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income, plus Ontario corporate income tax (10% general rate; 3.2% reduced rate on first CAD 500,000 of active business income for Canadian-controlled private corporations). Non-resident corporations are taxable on Canadian-source income and may be subject to Part XIV branch tax. No pass-through entitlement. Territorial scope: resident = worldwide; non-resident = Canadian-source only. Tax-exempt zones do not apply in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory under the Income Tax Act (ITA) and the Excise Tax Act (ETA). Available elections include: (1) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident corporations to report net rental/income from Canadian real property or timber limits instead of gross Part XIII withholding; (2) ITA s. 219 election for a non-resident corporation's taxable Canadian earnings to be subject to a 25% branch tax (reduced by treaty); (3) ITA s. 261 functional currency election to compute Canadian tax in a qualifying designated currency (e.g., USD); (4) ETA s. 156 election for closely related corporations to treat supplies as not being made for GST/HST purposes; (5) ETA Quick Method election for smaller registrants. Hybrid mismatch rules under the ITA (effective July 1, 2022) automatically deny deductions or require inclusions for arrangements producing deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; no elective hybrid mismatch election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold) in taxable supplies made in Canada in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four calendar quarters. Mandatory registration for any person (including a non-resident) who makes taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a commercial activity and exceeds the threshold. A non-resident supplier must also register if it carries on business in Canada and makes taxable supplies, subject to the same small-supplier threshold. Zero-rated export supplies count as taxable supplies for threshold measurement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies under ETA s. 16(1) where the supplier sells and ships tangible personal property (collectible trading cards) to a destination outside Canada specified in the contract, or delivers the goods to a common carrier for export, or exports the goods on the supplier's own behalf, provided the supplier retains evidence satisfactory to the Minister that the goods were exported. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident not registered for GST/HST are zero-rated under Schedule VI, s. 9, unless the services relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property ordinarily situated in Canada. A GST/HST-registered exporter may claim a refund (net payment) of input tax credits attributable to zero-rated exports via its periodic GST/HST return; non-resident registrants may also claim the ETA s. 252 rebate for supplies made outside Canada.

  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air waybill, or truck carrier)

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance mandate, or digital stamping regime exists for GST/HST or Ontario HST. E-invoicing is voluntary, with the Government of Canada implementing a PEPPOL-based national e-invoice interoperability framework (Canadian E-Invoicing Service) for B2B transactions; Ontario has not legislated mandatory e-invoicing. Registrants may issue electronic invoices, but every invoice must contain the supplier's BN, transaction date, brief description of goods/services, tax amount, and the total consideration paid/payable, per ETA s. 223. Accounting records and third-party substantiation must be retained for six years for CRA audit.

CRA scrutiny focuses on export substantiation, periodic filing consistency, and proof that goods left Canada within a reasonable time (typically within 60 days of sale). If export documentation is incomplete, CRA may reassess to tax the supply at 13% HST, deny ITC offsets, and impose interest. Civil penalties: ETA s. 280 penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax; ETA s. 285.1 false-statement penalty of 25% of the tax alleged to be understated; ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated income tax. Criminal exposure: ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 provide fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years (summary conviction) or eight years (indictable). The normal four-year reassessment period is extended where there is misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect, permitting retrospective clawback of zero-rating benefits for prior tax periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, consistent with Canada's IMF Article VIII status. There are no exchange-control approvals required for inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, outbound dividend remittances, royalty payments, or liquidation proceeds payable by an Ontario business corporation. Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding applies on outbound dividends (domestic statutory rate 25%, generally reduced to 5% for treaty-qualified corporate shareholders and 15% for portfolio shareholders under the Canada-US and Canada-UK treaties), interest, rents, and royalties; the repatriation of stated capital via share redemption is not subject to withholding but requires corporate minutes and a share-capital ledger. Thin capitalization rules under ITA Section 18(4) impose a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio for loans from specified non-resident shareholders. Canada maintains no restriction on maintaining multi-currency bank accounts either domestically or abroad, and no pre-approval is required for trade-related payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no fixed repatriation timeline for export proceeds. A federally or provincially incorporated Ontario exporter of collectible trading cards may lawfully retain invoice proceeds in any currency with a foreign bank or a Canadian chartered bank's nostro/vostro arrangement. FINTRAC reporting obligations apply to electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and Canadian exporters must record accrued export revenue for income tax purposes and for Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys, but no converted amount must be deposited with the Bank of Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the sole currency-issuing authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the prudential supervisor of federally regulated deposit-taking institutions; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/ATF supervisor and regulator of money services businesses; the Department of Finance Canada retains residual policy authority over any emergency exchange restrictions. No dedicated foreign exchange control administration currently exists in Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for a non-resident shareholder capital contribution: the remitting bank must send a SWIFT MT103 with Field 50K identifying the exact ordering customer (name, address, account number), Field 59 identifying the Ontario corporation beneficiary, and Field 70/72 explicitly stating 'Equity capital contribution to Ontario business corporation' or 'Shareholder loan to Ontario business corporation'. No Bank of Canada pre-approval is required. The receiving Schedule I bank will apply standard PCMLTFA verification, including FINTRAC EFT reporting if the remittance is CAD 10,000 or more; enhanced due diligence is typically triggered at CAD 100,000. The Dossier must include: Certificate of Incorporation, Articles of Amendment, unanimous shareholder agreement (if any), director resolution authorizing account opening and share issuance, subscribed capital ledger, source-of-funds declaration, and pass-through documentation for any intermediary account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility. A foreign digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie) may be used as a supplementary disbursement treasury rail, but it cannot lawfully serve as the primary merchant settlement account for CAD-denominated card transactions unless the provider is registered as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and, when the Retail Payment Activities Act comes into full force, registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities regime. Stripe and Authorize.Net operate through Canadian processing partners and Canadian acquiring banks; the legal constraint is that the merchant of record for the Ontario TGC publisher must settle to a Canadian bank account, not a foreign EMI account. The recommended structure is: Stripe/Authorize.Net settle in CAD to a Schedule I bank operating account, and the foreign EMI is limited to multi-currency vendor payments with no merchant settlement from card brands.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% (combined Federal 15% and Ontario general corporate rate 11.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII for dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 must be filed within 6 months after the corporation’s fiscal period end (e.g., a December 31 year-end requires filing by June 30). Instalments are due on the 15th day of the last month of each quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (CRA practical materiality threshold for contemporaneous TP documentation; no statutory minimum).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate overnight ESR legislation for Ontario, but economic substance remains the cornerstone: the GAAR under ITA s. 245 disregards avoidance transactions lacking reasonable commercial substance; the CRA uses the ‘beneficial owner’ and ‘substance-over-form’ doctrines to deny treaty benefits to shell entities; Ontario’s Business Corporations Act imposes registered office and records substance requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under ITA s. 239; on indictment: fine up to CAD 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: fine up to CAD 1,000,000 and/or up to 18 months; gross negligence penalty 25%.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or acquiesced in the evasion are liable for civil restitution; the corporate veil may be pierced; criminal fines and imprisonment apply personally; travel can be restricted by court orders pending trial.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under ITA s. 247; transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (s. 247(3)); interest on the deficiency; thin-cap limitation of interest on debt from specified non-resident shareholders exceeding 1.5:1 debt-to-equity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed inaccurate T2 schedules are exposed to criminal tax charges; personal assessment under s. 227.1 for unpaid source withholdings (but not TP itself); creditor actions may target directors for fraudulent conveyance.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment of the tax benefit; Part I penalty up to 25% of the benefit; interest; Canada's CFC income may be attributed to the Canadian shareholder under ITA s. 91; potential requirement to file Form T1134 and civil penalties.

Director personal liability: If the corporation is a shell, directors may be personally liable for all tax liabilities, as a court may disregard separate corporate personality; travel bans may be imposed for tax debts under the Canada Border Services Agency collection process.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the PCMLTFA: administrative fines up to CAD 100,000 per violation for entities; criminal penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for directors for failure to comply; potential freeze of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit money laundering are subject to liability under Criminal Code ss. 462.31; imprisonment up to 10 years; travel restrictions at border crossings; personal fines.
