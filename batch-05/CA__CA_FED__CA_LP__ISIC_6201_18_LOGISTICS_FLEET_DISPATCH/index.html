Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Administrator of federal income tax, GST/HST, and business registries. Official portal: canada.ca/en/revenue-agency and My Business Account (canada.ca/taxes/business-taxes).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a federal Business Number (BN) and corresponding GST/HST program account by filing Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number', with the CRA or by using the CRA's Business Registration Online service. A non-resident must first determine whether it has a permanent establishment or is carrying on business in Canada. For a Canadian limited partnership, registration is made in the partnership's legal name under its own BN, and each partner reports its allocated share of income. Voluntary GST/HST registration is available even where the small-supplier threshold is not met.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity obtains a federal Business Number (BN) and corresponding GST/HST program account by filing Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number', with the CRA or by using the CRA's Business Registration Online service. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election system. A limited partnership is automatically a flow-through 'Canadian partnership' for federal income tax purposes; the partnership itself is not a taxpayer. Partners may use the federal partnership fiscal-period election under section 249.1 of the Income Tax Act, elect to allocate GST/HST rebates among partners, and designate a partner as the GST/HST registrant for administration of the partnership's GST/HST account. Hybrid mismatch treatment is governed by the OECD Multilateral Instrument and domestic hybrid mismatch rules rather than entity-level check-the-box elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in worldwide taxable supplies over any 12 consecutive months; GST/HST registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded or where the non-resident carries on business in Canada. Voluntary registration is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies to goods that are exported from Canada, supported by customs evidence. For the SaaS fleet route optimization and telematics service supplied to a non-resident, zero-rating is available under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the service is supplied to a non-resident and is not for consumption in Canada, does not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is not rendered to persons present in Canada at the time the service is performed. The vendor must retain documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and the offshore use or benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or electronic CBSA export report) where goods are exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and/or Service Agreement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment/consideration evidence
  • Non-resident status evidence and supporting records demonstrating offshore consumption

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists at the federal level. The CRA does not require continuous API pre-approval of invoices. Taxpayers must issue valid GST/HST invoices and retain electronic records; CRA accepts electronic invoices and EDI formats for audit. B2G e-invoicing is increasingly Peppol-based under the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework, but participation is not mandatory for all private-sector taxable entities.

CRA verifies zero-rating claims against customs filings, payment routing, and non-resident documentation. If documentation is inadequate, zero-rating is denied and GST/HST is reassessed with statutory interest. False or negligent statements attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including up to 25% of the net tax understated for gross negligence and higher prosecution penalties for willful evasion. The normal four-year reassessment window is extended indefinitely where there is misrepresentation or fraud, and clawback may include the full GST/HST amount plus interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada abolished formal exchange controls in 1951; no central-bank approval is required for foreign direct investment, equity injections, current-account service receipts, or repatriation of profits. For ISIC 6201 logistics-fleet software exports, payments received from foreign clients are unrestricted and may be retained in USD, CAD, or any other currency. Profit remittance from a Canadian limited partnership to a non-resident partner is subject to normal Canadian tax compliance — Part I tax on Canadian-source business income allocated to the non-resident partner, or Part XIII withholding on treaty-protected passive amounts — but there are no FX-conversion, liquidity, or exchange-surrender restrictions. Reduced treaty withholding rates (commonly 5-15%) apply only if the non-resident partner provides a CRA Form NR301 or NR302 and satisfies substance requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export receipts or cross-border service revenue. The only relevant obligations are reporting-focused: (i) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer (EFT) reports for every incoming or outgoing wire equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000; (ii) cross-border currency and monetary-instrument reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for physical movements of CAD 10,000 or more; and (iii) Canadian transfer-pricing and record-keeping rules under subsection 247(4) of the Income Tax Act for intercompany export invoicing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); exchange-control and foreign-currency matters administered by the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No dedicated foreign-exchange licensing authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low-Medium as a primary anchor; Medium as an auxiliary treasury rail. Canadian law does not recognize an 'EMI' passport; offshore digital financial platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar) are treated as foreign money-services businesses and must, if soliciting Canadian clients, register as money services businesses with FINTRAC and operate via Canadian payment-service-provider arrangements. Such platforms cannot offer CDIC-insured Canadian-dollar deposit accounts and do not have direct access to Payments Canada clearing infrastructure. Recommended structure: maintain the OSFI-regulated tier-1 bank account as the principal treasury anchor and funds-holding entity; use EMIs solely as auxiliary collection gateways, FX intermediation, and multi-currency vendor-payout rails, with the tier-1 account receiving daily sweeps.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners are routed via SWIFT MT103 (or the corresponding bank's equivalent) to the LP's operational or capital-escrow account at the Canadian tier-1 bank. The beneficiary line must show the registered legal name of the limited partnership and its general partner; the payment reference must read 'Capital contribution to [CA_LP legal name] by [Contributing Partner].' Banks will apply the internal purpose code for a direct investment or capital-account contribution and will examine source-of-funds evidence for any injection at or above CAD 10,000 for FINTRAC EFT reporting and at or above USD 100,000 for enhanced due diligence. Capital contributions are not taxable receipts and do not attract GST/HST. For US-dollar partners, using the bank's US-dollar clearing correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS) into a USD sub-account, followed by an internal CAD conversion at the commercial FX desk, yields efficient settlement; no central-bank approval, OSFI permit, or CRA clearance is required for the conversion itself.Outbound disbursements — cloud-infrastructure fees, fleet-hardware vendor payments, licence fees, and logistics-carrier settlements — are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 and MT202 wires from the LP's CAD/USD treasury accounts. CAD same-day clearing uses Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System through the tier-1 bank; USD clearing uses Fedwire/CHIPS via the bank's US correspondent banking unit. For large imports of telematics hardware (ELD devices, in-cab sensors), a commercial letter of credit is permissible under UCP600, with a standby letter of credit available to secure software-delivery milestones. Any outbound wire equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 is a FINTRAC-reportable EFT. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal general corporate rate); 9% for Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) active business income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents; for an LP, this rate applies to income allocations or deemed distributions to non-resident partners. Treaty rates may reduce to 15% (generally) and 5% (substantial intercorporate shareholdings) under the Canada-US DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation (Local File) required when aggregate annual fair market value of transactions with a non-arm’s length non-resident exceeds CAD 5,000,000. A Master File is required when consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 50,000,000; a Country-by-Country Report is required when consolidated group revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in offshore financial centres, but CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and the OECD principal purpose test under the MLI. An LP must evidence genuine business activity (employees, assets, decision-making in Canada) to preserve treaty access and avoid being treated as a shell or conduit arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s. 238/239: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded; civil gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; criminal conviction may result in a fine of up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment not exceeding 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding tax under s. 227.1; civil restitution, asset attachment, travel bans, and imprisonment are available when directors participate in the evasion.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the LP as a sham, deny treaty benefits, apply GAAR to re-characterize income, impose gross negligence penalties of 50% of understated tax, and reassess with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face potential foreign prosecutions, mutual legal assistance, freezing orders, travel bans, and imprisonment in the partner’s home jurisdiction for fraudulent conduit arrangements.
Money Laundering / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative fines up to CAD 1,000,000 for non-compliance; criminal penalties of up to CAD 500,000 fine and 10 years’ imprisonment for laundering the proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally charged if they knowingly instruct or facilitate concealment; personal assets may be confiscated; arrest, extradition, and travel restrictions apply.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices under s. 247 and impose a penalty under s. 247(3) equal to 10% of gross revenue (less than CAD 5,000,000) where reasonable efforts to document arm’s length terms are absent; reassessment interest and compound interest accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or cause the mispricing may be held personally liable for penalties; court orders may prohibit management, impose restitution, and allow criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
