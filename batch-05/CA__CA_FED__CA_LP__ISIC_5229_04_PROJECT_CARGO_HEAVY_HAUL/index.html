Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a CBCA general partner or for the limited partnership itself. Capital contributions are fixed in the limited partnership agreement and may be cash or property. No escrow account is mandated for non-financial logistics entities, and capital is available for use immediately upon execution of the limited partnership agreement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal income tax and GST/HST administration; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — export customs control and export declarations. Registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) at canada.ca.

E-government filing portal

The Limited Partnership (LP) obtains a nine-digit Business Number (BN) — the federal tax identification number — by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) via CRA Business Registration Online or by mail. A non-resident LP must appoint a Canadian agent/representative, complete the non-resident portions of RC1, and attach the executed partnership agreement and partner schedule. Once the BN is issued, the LP activates program accounts: RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll, if the LP has Canadian employees) and, where applicable, RZ (income tax account; optional because the LP is fiscally transparent). Electronic registration is confirmed within five business days; a compliance certificate (Full/Regular) is issued automatically by MyBA.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the LP is a 'Canadian partnership' under section 96 of the Income Tax Act: it is a computation vehicle, not a taxpayer; income, gains, losses and ITCs flow through pro rata to partners. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the LP is a 'Canadian partnership' under section 96 of the Income Tax Act: it is a computation vehicle, not a taxpayer; income, gains, losses and ITCs flow through pro rata to partners. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only to the extent the LP carries on business in Canada through a permanent establishment and the allocable share is attributable to that PE; no federal entity-level capital tax or branch tax applies to the partnership itself.

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' regime; partnership transparency is statutory and non-elective. Available and relevant elections for an LP engaged in project cargo: (i) section 216 election allowing a non-resident partner to file a Part I return and pay tax on net Canadian-source business/rental income instead of gross withholding; (ii) partnership fiscal-period election under subsection 249.1(7) with unanimous partner consent; (iii) GST/HST election under section 273 (Exchange of Services) treating certain supplies with closely related persons as nil-consideration supplies; and (iv) conforming elections under the hybrid-mismatch rules in sections 93.4-93.5 of the ITA (enacted by S.C. 2024) that will coordinate the Canadian transparent treatment of the LP with any foreign opaque characterization of the same entity, effective for fiscal periods beginning after June 30, 2026.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold measured on the LP's worldwide taxable supplies (GST/HST) in the four preceding calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory in the quarter in which the threshold is exceeded. Non-resident suppliers carrying on no business in Canada may instead use the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration where annual Canadian taxable supplies do not exceed CAD 100,000 (Form GST391 regime). Voluntarily registered heavy-haul logistics operators may recover ITCs, including on the importation of out-of-gauge trailers and lifting equipment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export rate is claimable under the following heads in Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act: (i) section 1 — direct export of goods where the LP transfers title outside Canada and the goods are exported without further transformation; (ii) sections 7, 7.1 and 8 — freight transportation services (road, rail, sea or air) forming part of continuous international transport of goods from Canada to a place outside Canada, including OOG heavy-haul drayage and terminal handling where the goods are ultimately exported; (iii) section 9 and item 16, Part V — loading and handling services performed in Canada in respect of goods to be exported if the services are rendered to a non-resident and the supply is in respect of the exporter's goods; and (iv) section 23 — services supplied to a non-resident person that are performed wholly outside Canada. Milestone-wire and letter-of-credit payments do not alter zero-rating; the LP must retain documented evidence of exportation within the statutory two-year retention period to sustain the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration — electronic B13A/Canada Export Declaration filed through the CBSA G7/CARM single-window platform, including the approved eexport data record and let-export authorization
  • Bill of Lading — original ocean or multimodal B/L evidencing the heavy-haul cargo's movement from the Canadian inland origin to a foreign destination port, with consignee and port-of-discharge identified outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank-generated foreign-currency remittance confirmation and wire-transfer receipt demonstrating receipt of payment from the non-resident counterparty and full cash repatriation to the LP's Canadian accounts
  • Commercial invoice and equipment-specific packing list (OOG dimensions, axle loads, gross mass of the project cargo)
  • CBSA export cut-of-list/verification report, freight forwarder's receipt or terminal export certificate, if the consignment is selected for physical inspection

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping exists in Canada. Mandatory electronic filing (GST/HST NETFILE) applies to registrants with annual revenues exceeding CAD 1,000,000 and to all simplified non-resident filers. E-invoicing (including PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is permitted and recommended for B2B project-cargo contracts but is not legally validated or cleared by the CRA; invoice data exchange occurs bilaterally between the LP and its counterparties. The CRA provides optional API access (MyBA, Represent a Client, GST/HST registry look-up) for verification only, not for continuous transaction control.

The CRA and CBSA jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Failure to produce the export documentation bundle within two years of the supply results in reassessment of the 5% GST on the full contract value plus interest under sections 296 and 222 of the Excise Tax Act. False or negligent statements expose the LP to: (i) a 25% gross-negligence penalty on the net tax sought to be avoided; (ii) a false-statement penalty of CAD 500 to CAD 25,000 under section 285; (iii) denial of every associated input tax credit with retroactive clawback of previously refunded amounts and interest; and (iv) for fraudulent export schemes, criminal sanctions under sections 327-333.1 (evasion, suppression of documents, false declarations) with fines of up to 200% of the tax and imprisonment. The CRA also applies the general anti-avoidance rule to artificial circular-export or shell-consignee arrangements, and foreign-currency repatriation delays beyond 180 days after export attract heightened transfer-pricing and thin-capitalization scrutiny, including potential additional tax of 10% on outstanding export balances under section 17 of the Income Tax Act where the LP is a controlled foreign affiliate of a Canadian shareholder.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account under a floating exchange-rate regime; there are no exchange-control approvals, transfer restrictions, or quotas applicable to CAD or any foreign currency. For a Canadian-resident limited partnership with non-resident partners, profit remittance is governed solely by tax compliance (Part I Income Tax Act at partner level, Part XIII withholding on specified passive payments, and applicable treaty rates such as 15% under the Canada-US Tax Treaty for eligible distributions), with no central-bank or FX-control hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian law imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act and the Income Tax Act contain no surrender or forced-conversion obligation. Exporters may hold foreign currency indefinitely in domestic or nostro accounts, subject only to FINTRAC reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 for cash, CAD 30,000 for international electronic funds transfers), and foreign-exchange gains on revenue account are taxed as ordinary income for an accrual-basis partnership.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC), in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) serves as the national financial intelligence unit for wire-payment AML surveillance; no dedicated foreign-exchange administration exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary disbursements and payroll-style payments, Low for primary escrow and trade settlement. Canada has no statutory 'EMI' licence; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., segment fintechs) must register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA to lawfully hold or transmit CAD for Canadian clients. Legal constraints include no Bank Act deposit-taking privileges, no CDIC deposit insurance, and inability to issue or confirm UCP600 letters of credit; all L/C and capital-escrow activity must therefore remain with Schedule I chartered banks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% net federal corporate income tax rate for non-CCPCs carrying on business in Canada (38% basic rate less 10% federal abatement less 13% General Rate Reduction); provincial/territorial corporate rates apply separately

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder under ITA s.212(2); distributions by the limited partnership are not dividends and should not be treated as dividend repatriation

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CRA contemporaneous documentation and Form T106 are required where reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 in respect of a particular related non-resident; where aggregate related-party transactions exceed CAD 5,000,000, participants should maintain a complete Master File / Local File package accepted by CRA and OECD standards.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not impose a separate UAE-style Economic Substances Regulation; however, the CRA will apply the income tax general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under ITA s.245 and common-law substance-over-form to deny benefits to a limited partnership that lacks real employees, physical premises, or operational substance and has no bona fide business rationale for the heavy-haul logistics structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to the greater of C$100 and 50% of the tax understated; criminal conviction under ITA s.239(1) exposes fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who signed or directed false returns are jointly and severally liable for tax, penalties, and interest; personal asset seizure, travel bans, and incarceration are possible in wilful evasion prosecutions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and T106 Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess income under s.247(2); a 10% transfer-pricing penalty applies under s.247(3) if the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts to price arm's-length; T106 late filing penalties up to C$2,500 and gross negligence penalties under s.163(2) can also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors generally are not automatically personally liable for TP adjustments, but they risk clawback liability if they approved excessive fees that constitute fraudulent preferences or breach of fiduciary duties; in criminal tax audits, directors can be indicted for aiding false returns.
Artificial 'Flow-Through' / Shell Company Structure

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s.245 denies the tax benefit; the CRA may recharacterize the entire revenue as taxable directly to the corporate partner or general partner, with recalculated interest and penalties; provincial law may also strike the LP as a sham.

Director personal liability: General partner and controlling directors may be personally liable for the full tax debt and for creditor restitution once the corporate veil is pierced; travel bans and director prohibition orders can be imposed under tax enforcement proceedings.
Unpaid Withholding and Part XIII Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The payer remains assessable for the full withholding amount under ITA s.227, plus compound daily interest and penalties; culpable failure to withhold can also result in a fine for each omission.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner or payer are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholdings under ITA s.227.1; willful evasion triggers criminal liability and travel restrictions.
Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code money-laundering provisions (s.462.31) carry imprisonment up to 10 years and fines; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties may reach C$1,000,000 per violation where the business is a reporting entity under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who orchestrate trade-based laundering face criminal forfeiture of proceeds, restitution orders, asset freezes, and personal travel bans; liability cannot be shielded by the LP corporate form in laundering prosecutions.
