Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account is prescribed for a Canadian limited partnership. The initial capital position must reflect each limited partner's agreed contribution (Schedule A); the General Partner maintains a nominal capital account of at least CAD 1.00 to evidence good-faith organization, and contributions are recorded in the LP books but need not be deposited into a segregated escrow account.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification per NFPA 96 for commercial kitchen exhaust hoods, duct cleaning log and automatic suppression systems; structural load inspection certificate for production floors and cold-storage racking; provincial Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB) coverage and Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA) compliance clearance; Transport Canada airside security clearance and RAIC issuance for delivery drivers and ramp staff.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Federal tax administration; registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), accessible via the 'My Business Account' portal at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses.html.

E-government filing portal

For a Canadian Limited Partnership, the fiscal registration sequence is: (1) obtain a federal Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1, Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts, either online via BRO, by mail, or by telephone (1-800-959-5525); (2) open a GST/HST program account using the same application process; the BN forms the primary tax identifier for federal tax filings, the T5013 Partnership Information Return, and GST/HST remittances; (3) if the general partner or any partner is a non-resident, the partnership must apply for a BN and GST/HST registration in its own capacity, and the non-resident partner should file the applicable Part I waiver or election to avoid the default 45% withholding under subsection 194(1) of the Income Tax Act (the 'non-resident partner election' is submitted on the form prescribed by CRA, generally accompanied by a guarantee or security acceptable to CRA); (4) the partnership should also enroll in CRA's online services (My Business Account) for electronic correspondence and to file GST/HST returns via GST/HST NETFILE. A non-resident general partner may be required to obtain a Canadian BN and pay any applicable Part XIII withholding taxes on Canadian-source amounts paid by the partnership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: For income tax purposes, the Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; the partners are subject to Part I tax on their allocated Canadian-source income. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is a separate 'person' under the Excise Tax Act and must register for GST/HST in its own right if it is not a small supplier. Territorial scope: Canada imposes federal income tax on the worldwide income of residents; non-resident partners are taxable only on Canadian-source partnership income.

Available election forms

(a) Check-the-box: Not available; CRA does not recognise the US check-the-box classification and instead applies the entity's legal form under its jurisdiction of creation; a Canadian LP is fiscally transparent for income tax while being a separate 'person' for GST/HST. (b) Subsection 194(2) election / section 211 waiver: A non-resident partner may elect to be taxed under Part I on the partnership's Canadian business income in lieu of the default 45% withholding tax on distributions. (c) Small supplier election: A partnership that is not a small supplier may nevertheless voluntarily register for GST/HST (subsection 240(2) election). (d) The LP's status as a 'Canadian partnership' under subsection 102(2) of the Income Tax Act should be confirmed; CRA may deny 'Canadian partnership' status where all partners are not residents of Canada, triggering different compliance and withholding obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD of worldwide taxable supplies in the current and four preceding calendar quarters; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada who exceeds this 'small supplier' threshold is required to register for GST/HST. For the inflight catering operation, registration is mandatory notwithstanding the participation of non-resident partners, provided the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, Section 15 of the Excise Tax Act when the supplier (i) supplies tangible personal property to a non-resident, and (ii) proves exportation from Canada within a reasonable time via prescribed documentary evidence. For the inflight catering kitchen, the head supply (prepared inflight meals delivered to the airline's ramp at a Canadian airport) is a supply of prepared food made in Canada and is taxable at 5% GST (or applicable HST) unless the supplier can contemporaneously prove that the meals were shipped to a non-resident common carrier and exported on an international flight. The zero-rating is available in the latter case, provided the invoice states that the supply is zero-rated and the caterer obtains air waybills, flight manifests, or export declarations evidencing departure from Canada. Supplies of food for consumption in Canada (including domestic flights within Canada) are taxable. Note: basic groceries are zero-rated, but prepared meals are not, per Air Canada v. The Queen.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (Form B13A, filed through the Canadian Integrated International Trade System (CERS) for goods valued above CAD 2,000 or controlled goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank receipt evidencing receipt of payment from the foreign airline
  • Commercial invoice reflecting the supplier's GST/HST registration number and indicating the zero-rated supply
  • Flight manifest / ramp delivery record demonstrating goods were loaded on an international conveyance

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is currently in force at the federal level. Canada is transitioning toward PEPPOL-compliant B2B e-invoicing under the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing interoperability roadmap; the CRA accepts electronic invoices and records, allows GST/HST returns to be filed online via NETFILE, and requires large businesses to remit electronically when their annual net tax exceeds CAD 10,000. There is no API-based pre-clearance of individual invoices; audit validation is retrospective through CRA review of books and records.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated export within four years (or without limitation in the case of misrepresentation or fraud). If the supporting export documentary bundle is not timely collected, CRA denounces the zero-rating and assesses GST/HST at 5% (or the applicable HST rate), plus statutory interest and penalties: (i) 50% of the net tax under subsection 281.1(1) for false statements; (ii) 50% under section 285 for gross negligence; (iii) personal liability of directors or partners for unremitted amounts; and (iv) criminal sanctions for evasion under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act (up to CAD 100,000 fine and up to five years' imprisonment). Separately, the CBSA imposes administrative monetary penalties (AMPS) for inaccurate export declarations, and repeated non-compliance may lead to denial of zero-rating status and placement in CRA's high-risk exporter audit stream.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. The Bank of Canada does not administer exchange controls or require approvals for currency conversion. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, and loan repayments are freely transferable to non-resident limited partners and shareholders, subject only to Canadian withholding tax and income tax reporting requirements. The Canadian dollar trades under a floating exchange rate regime. There are no restrictions on maintaining foreign currency bank accounts, and no quantitative limits on cross-border capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law does not impose any statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency receipts into Canadian dollars within a specified timeline. Airline in-flight catering service receipts denominated in foreign currency may be retained in foreign currency accounts, converted to CAD at market rates, or used directly for cross-border procurement, subject to recordkeeping under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). No central bank approval or registration is required for such retention or conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (conditional). A foreign or offshore fintech EMI without Canadian registration cannot directly clear CAD or access Payments Canada settlement systems such as Lynx or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, payment service providers must be registered with the Bank of Canada; however, an offshore EMI license alone does not confer access to Canadian banking infrastructure. EMIs may be used for multi-currency wallets, payment initiation, and expense cards, but final treasury settlement for a Canadian limited partnership must be routed through a Canadian Schedule I chartered bank. Use of a registered fintech EMI is viable as an ancillary rail, not as a substitute for a tier-1 corporate bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate annual non-resident controlled transactions, or CAD 250,000 for taxpayers with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 10,000,000, triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under subsection 245(2) and the CRA's substance-over-form interpretation, but there is no standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime; nonetheless, an invoicing entity must demonstrate actual personnel, premises, and operational decision-making in the inflight catering chain to support its arm's-length remuneration

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years under sections 238 and 239 of the Income Tax Act; civil penalties may add an additional 50% of the understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unremitted payroll and GST/HST withholdings under section 227.1; the CRA may certify the debt and pursue travel bans, liens, and collection against personal assets
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess taxable income under subsection 247(2) and impose a 10% penalty on any net transfer-pricing adjustment where required contemporaneous documentation has not been prepared; prescribed interest and instalment penalties also apply

Director personal liability: A director or officer who knowingly signs or directs the use of transfer-pricing documentation that is false or misleading can be joined to the penalty as a co-conspirator and may be liable for the unpaid corporate balance through negligent misstatement and equitable fraudulent conveyance proceedings
Anti-Money Laundering / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act can reach CAD 2,000,000 per violation; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code section 462.31 carries a maximum imprisonment term of ten years where proceeds exceed CAD 5,000

Director personal liability: A director or general partner of a sham partnership can be subject to criminal prosecution for money laundering, ordered to repatriate corporate assets, and prohibited from leaving Canada during asset-investigation proceedings; the corporate veil is pierced whenever the partnership is found to be a fiction
Unlicensed Food Safety Operations

Statutory sanction: Licensing and enforcement penalties up to CAD 5,000,000, administrative monetary penalties, licence revocation, and immediate shutdown of the catering kitchen until compliance is restored

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who authorize, instruct, or acquiesce in unlicensed food production may be held personally liable for penalties after receiving written demand; repeat offenses can result in travel restrictions and extradition proceedings in cross-border safety-commerce cases
