Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a federal-stage Canadian limited partnership, a registered office address is mandatory in the province of registration. A virtual office or a commercial registered agent with a verifiable physical address is generally accepted for registration, provided real human presence and mail handling exist. Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The corporate registry of the province or territory where the limited partnership is formed and carries on business. Corporations Canada is not the formation registry for a Canadian limited partnership. Provincial examples include the Ontario Business Registry, the British Columbia Corporate Online system, and the Alberta Corporate Registry.

Governing statute

No federal Companies Act creates or registers a Canadian limited partnership. A limited partnership must be constituted under the Partnership Act or Limited Partnerships Act of the selected province or territory, including the applicable registration, limited-partner liability, filing, amendment, and dissolution provisions. The Canada Business Corporations Act does not apply to limited partnerships.

Formation / constitutional filings

Provincial or territorial limited partnership declaration, registration statement, or certificate of limited partnership, using the statutory form prescribed by the selected jurisdiction.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 45 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No federally prescribed minimum capital or paid-up capital applies. The applicable provincial or territorial statute normally requires disclosure of the general partner and limited partners, but does not require a statutory minimum contribution. Contributions, profit allocations, withdrawal rights, and capital calls should be documented in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account is generally required; any client-money or trust-account requirement must be assessed separately under applicable insurance, professional, or provincial rules.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal zoning and business-occupancy approval for the office or inspection facility. Obtain fire-code compliance, fire inspection, and occupancy certification where required by the municipality or building authority. Structural load inspections are required if equipment, storage, lifting systems, or a workshop changes imposed loads. Comply with provincial occupational health and safety legislation, WHMIS, personal protective equipment, confined-space, vehicle, marine-access, fall-protection, and incident-reporting requirements. Obtain port, terminal, vessel-owner, and site-access clearances before attending controlled facilities.

Paid-in capital rule: No federally prescribed minimum capital or paid-up capital applies. The applicable provincial or territorial statute normally requires disclosure of the general partner and limited partners, but does not require a statutory minimum contribution. Contributions, profit allocations, withdrawal rights, and capital calls should be documented in the limited partnership agreement. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

No single Canadian federal industrial licensing board licenses ordinary marine insurance surveyors. Transport Canada administers federal marine safety legislation and related vessel or marine-safety approvals. Provincial insurance regulators license insurance adjusters and other insurance intermediaries where the service involves claims adjustment, settlement, or representation. Municipal authorities regulate premises, occupancy, zoning, and business licensing.

Category: No general federal manufacturing or industrial operating permit is ordinarily required for independent vessel hull and cargo damage inspection. A provincial insurance adjuster licence, commonly titled an Adjuster Licence or Insurance Adjuster Licence, is required where the activities legally constitute insurance adjusting. Separate client, port, vessel, occupational, privacy, and municipal permissions may apply.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) - registration is completed through the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca; ongoing administration is handled in the My Business Account portal; non-resident and international matters are processed through the CRA International Tax Services Office (ITSO).

E-government filing portal

The limited partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent for income tax (Income Tax Act s. 96) but constitutes a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes (Excise Tax Act s. 123(1), s. 238(1)). Procedure: (1) complete extra-provincial registration of the LP in each province where the partnership carries on business, or form the LP domestically; (2) apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 or the BRO online service, opening the GST/HST 'RT' program account in the LP name; (3) open payroll 'RP' program accounts if the LP employs surveyors or administrative staff; (4) file the T5013 Partnership Information Return annually, with T5013SUM schedules allocating income, losses and flow-through amounts to partners per the partnership agreement; (5) remit Part XIII withholding on Canadian-source amounts paid to non-resident partners unless a net-basis Part I election applies; (6) retain the LP registry extract, certificate of limited partnership, partnership agreement and service contracts as the identity and authorization file supporting the BN registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the LP is transparent for Income Tax Act purposes: income, gains, losses and tax credits flow through to partners under s. 96; the LP itself is generally not subject to income tax, but it is a mandatory GST/HST registrant once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed the small supplier threshold. Territorial scope: Canadian-source business income allocated to non-resident partners is taxable on a net basis under Part I (ITA s. 115), while passive allocations to non-resident partners may attract Part XIII gross-basis withholding.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box election regime; flow-through treatment is statutory. Available elections include: (a) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(9); (b) hybrid mismatch joint elections under ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.3 permitting partners to recharacterize structured arrangement payments and align deduction timing; (c) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election between closely related registrants for supplies at nil consideration; (d) election for non-resident partners to file Part I returns on a net basis (ITA s. 216 with s. 115) instead of suffering Part XIII withholding on active business allocations; (e) agreed allocation election under ITA s. 96(3) documenting disproportionate sharing of income and losses among general and limited partners, subject to the limited-partner at-risk rules in ITA s. 96(2.5).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 - the small supplier threshold is exceeded when the LP's taxable supplies made in Canada exceed C$30,000 in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, or where the LP makes taxable supplies in Canada in a single calendar quarter and the threshold will be exceeded in that quarter. Non-resident LPs carrying on business in Canada may also be required to register under ETA s. 240(1) if they solicit orders, maintain an establishment, or perform services in Canada through an employee or agent.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act: (i) Part I s. 1 for goods physically exported from Canada, subject to documentary proof of export; (ii) Part V s. 7 for a supply of a service made to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant, unless the service is in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is a personal service rendered by an individual present in Canada; (iii) Part VII ss. 16-18 for international transportation services. For a marine insurance surveyor (ISIC 5222 / NAICS 488390), hull and cargo damage inspection performed at a Canadian port or drydock is a service performed in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, and is therefore taxable at 5% GST or the applicable HST, even where the client is a non-resident underwriter. Desk-based remote survey work regarding property situated outside Canada, and survey attendance performed outside Canadian territory, qualify as zero-rated exports under Part V s. 7 provided the non-resident client is not registered for GST/HST and the service is not for consumption in Canada.

  • CBSA/Statistics Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A or Global Case Management System export submission) evidencing outward movement of goods where cargo is exported
  • Bill of Lading / ocean waybill showing the insured cargo or vessel movement to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank wire advice (Underwriter_Wire / Wise) evidencing settlement received from the non-resident underwriter
  • Marine Survey Report and claim file referencing the policy slip, vessel/cargo identification, casualty event date and place, and scope of loss
  • Signed service contract with the non-resident insurer identifying the supplier, place of performance, compensation and agreed GST/HST treatment
  • Non-resident declaration (for Part V s. 7 claims) confirming the client is not a GST/HST registrant and that the service is not acquired for consumption in Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in Canada. E-invoicing remains voluntary; the federal government announced in the 2023 Fall Economic Statement a Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard based on the Peppol framework, with CRA pilot integration anticipated in 2025-2026. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies in Canada exceeding C$1.5M (ETA s. 273.1); returns are filed via NETFILE or GST/HST EDI. Statutory tax invoice requirements under ETA s. 169 and Part VI of the GST/HST Regulations require the supplier's BN/RT program account, transaction date, description of the supply, consideration, GST/HST rate and amount, and supplier/buyer identity; simplified invoices and annual invoicing agreements remain permitted.

CRA may reassess within four years from the date of filing (ETA s. 298), and without limitation where the filing reflects misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default. Where export zero-rating is reversed, the LP must remit the uncollected 5% GST or applicable HST, plus compound daily interest at CRA prescribed rates. The gross negligence penalty under ETA s. 285.1 is the greater of C$250 and 10% of the net tax amount. False statements, fictitious export schedules and deliberate misuse of export zero-rating expose the LP, the general partner and participating partners to criminal prosecution under ETA ss. 326-327, with fines up to double the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to two years on summary conviction or five years on indictment. Failure to repatriate foreign consideration within the 180-day window does not by itself invalidate zero-rating, but it materially increases audit scrutiny and requires documented commercial justification; the foreign insurer payment flow may also be recharacterized for Part XIII withholding if the fee stream is treated as a management fee or royalty-type payment rather than active business income.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized, free-floating exchange-rate regime and adheres to IMF Article VIII obligations; both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible into CAD and foreign currencies. There are no general exchange controls, no pre-approval for ordinary profit remittance, and no restriction on maintaining foreign-currency bank accounts. Inward equity contributions and outbound dividends, loan interest, or partnership profit distributions are permitted, subject to Canadian tax reporting and, where applicable, Part XIII withholding tax or treaty rate reduction. Inward direct investment may trigger reporting requirements under the Investment Canada Act only for acquisitions above statutory thresholds; the operation of a marine insurance surveying office is normally exempt review as a new non-controlling business unless sectoral rules apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion applies in Canada. Exporters and service providers, including marine insurance surveyors, may retain export proceeds in CAD or foreign currency without any prescribed remittance timeline. FINTRAC reporting obligations rest on financial institutions for international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; the entity itself is not required to surrender foreign currency. For Canadian income tax purposes, the partnership must report revenues and expenditures in Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or a consistent recognized rate, with unrealized FX gains/losses tracked on account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) is the central monetary authority; prudential supervision of federally regulated financial institutions is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money laundering/counter-terrorist financing supervision is administered by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).
