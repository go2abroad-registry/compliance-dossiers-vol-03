Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with full current-account and capital-account convertibility; the Canadian dollar floats freely and there are no exchange-control authorizations, no prior approvals for profit remittance, and no restrictions on holding foreign currency accounts onshore or offshore. The operational hurdles for remitting profits from a Canadian limited partnership are indirect: (i) FINTRAC mandates record-keeping and reporting of every international electronic funds transfer equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000; (ii) Statistics Canada collects cross-border balance-of-payments data under the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS); and (iii) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes withholding tax on distributions to non-resident limited partners at the 25% statutory rate, potentially reduced to 15% or lower under an applicable treaty such as the Canada-US Tax Convention. As a pass-through vehicle, the LP itself is not taxed at entity level but must file a T5013 Partnership Information Return allocating income, gains, and foreign exchange results to its partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada abolished foreign-exchange surrender requirements in December 1951; there is no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no central bank directive over the currency composition of export receipts. A Canadian LP may retain export-of-services proceeds in CAD, USD, or any freely convertible currency without ministerial consent. The only compliance duties are: (i) maintaining complete books and records to support ITRS balance-of-payments classification and GST/HST filings; (ii) reporting cross-border electronic funds transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 to FINTRAC; and (iii) filing the annual T5013 information return with foreign-exchange gain or loss allocations to resident and non-resident partners.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole central bank and monetary authority; the Department of Finance Canada administers the Exchange Fund Account; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) provides prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions; FINTRAC enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for AML/ATF and international electronic funds transfer reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viability rating: Medium for inbound disbursement-only flows and Low for capital escrow. Canada does not operate a standalone electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime; a foreign digital bank or EMI cannot accept Canadian deposits or clear CAD through the domestic payment system without holding a bank licence under the Bank Act. Wise B2B and comparable payment service providers may lawfully operate only as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) and are becoming subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) once fully in force. Such providers are viable for low-value operational disbursements but cannot serve as the LP's primary treasury account, and capital injections routed through an unregulated EMI will be declined by Tier-1 Canadian clearing banks owing to provenance concerns.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate rate; not levied at the limited partnership entity level because a Canadian LP is fiscally transparent under ITA s.96.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII ITA for dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. A limited partnership does not pay dividends; partner profit drawings are not subject to Part XIII withholding. However, a non-resident partner's share of Canadian-source partnership business income is taxable under Part I, and certain payments of interest, rent, or royalties to non-resident partners are subject to Part XIII WHT under ITA s.212 as if made directly by the partnership.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within five (5) months after the partnership's fiscal year-end; for a December 31 year-end, the return must be filed by May 31 of the following calendar year. Individual partners file T1 returns by April 30; corporate partners file T2 returns by six (6) months after their taxation year-end.

T5013 Summary – Schedule 50 (Partner Allocation) Allocation of each partner's share of income/loss, capital gains, charitable donations, and other tax attributes, including the partner's name and tax identification number. For each non-resident partner, mark 'N/R' and provide the partner's country of residence and foreign tax identification number. Attach a completed NR301 or NR302 if treaty relief is claimed. Late filing of T5013 triggers a penalty of CAD $25 per day, minimum CAD $100 and maximum CAD $2,500 (ITA s.162(7)); failure to comply with a CRA demand for information or documents may incur CAD $100 per day up to CAD $5,000 for a partnership, plus possible 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3).
T5013 Slip – Box 20 (Share of Partnership Net Income) Each partner's share of the LP's net income for Canadian income tax purposes, used by the partner in completing their T1/T2 return. A non-resident partner must file a Canadian return if this income is taxable in Canada; treaty protection can be invoked only by filing a return and establishing absence of a Canadian permanent establishment. Late filing of T5013 triggers a penalty of CAD $25 per day, minimum CAD $100 and maximum CAD $2,500 (ITA s.162(7)); failure to comply with a CRA demand for information or documents may incur CAD $100 per day up to CAD $5,000 for a partnership, plus possible 10% transfer-pricing penalty under s.247(3).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 – Form T106 must be filed when the aggregate consideration for reportable cross-border transactions with any single non-resident non-arm's-length person exceeds CAD $1 million. For master file/local file, the CRA applies an OECD-aligned contemporaneous documentation standard without a fixed monetary threshold, but transaction values above CAD 5 million create a strong expectation of complete TP documentation to avoid s.247 penalties.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not issue Economic Substance Regulations of the Gulf/offshore type. Instead, the GAAR (s.245 ITA) invalidates tax benefits from arrangements where economic substance is minimal or absent and tax avoidance is an objective. For limited partnerships, the CRA examines the existence of a real Canadian business operation, meaningful decision-making, business premises, and commercial risk. Treaty benefits are denied where the partner lacks substance in its residence jurisdiction or the partnership is a round-trip financing conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties: gross negligence penalty of 50% of taxes avoided (s.163(2)); repeated failure to report income triggers additional flat penalties under s.162(5)-(6). Criminal conviction under ITA s.239(1) for evasion: fine of 50% to 200% of evaded tax, imprisonment up to five years, plus interest and costs.

Director personal liability: A general partner or director who knowingly participates may be assessed personally for the LP's unremitted withholding and payroll taxes under s.227.1; the court may issue travel bans and asset freezes, and the individual faces imprisonment in the case of conviction.
