Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — official online portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account; Ontario corporate tax is administered by the CRA with the Ontario Ministry of Finance under the Canada–Ontario cooperative tax administration arrangement.

E-government filing portal

For an Ontario business corporation, after federal (CBCA) or provincial (OBCA) incorporation and any required Ontario extra-provincial registration, the corporation must register with CRA by filing Form RC1 or using Business Registration Online (BRO). CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and activates separate program accounts for corporate income tax (RC) and GST/HST (RT); no separate national VAT ID exists. Required documentary support normally includes articles of incorporation, director identity details, Canadian registered-office address, and the expected date of first taxable activity. Registration should occur before the first reporting period; the GST/HST account must be active before invoicing export supplies at 0%, and the corporation should maintain its CRA My Business Account and authorized representative mandates for ongoing filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box election for corporations. A resident corporation is automatically a separate taxpayer for federal and Ontario corporate income tax. Available statutory elections relevant to this structure include Income Tax Act s.85/88 rollover elections for corporate reorganizations, s.83(2) capital dividend election, s.216/217 elections for non-resident net-basis taxation, and Excise Tax Act s.156 closely related corporation election for GST/HST. Hybrid mismatch and interest-stripping rules operate automatically with limited elective scope.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies, excluding financial services) in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; suppliers below the threshold may voluntarily register, which is normally advisable to claim input tax credits and support zero-rated exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the sale and export of tangible personal property (epoxy-resin polyhedral dice and accessories) if the property is exported by the supplier or by a common carrier, by mail/courier to an outside-Canada destination, or is shipped as part of a continuous journey to a destination outside Canada and proper evidence of export is retained. Services to non-residents may also be zero-rated when the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST and the service is not supplied to or for the benefit of a person present in Canada and is not in respect of Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada at the time the service is performed; the registrant should document the foreign destination, foreign counterparty, and payment flow.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canadian Automated Export Declaration or current CBSA electronic export reporting) required for shipments valued above CAD 2,000 or controlled/regulated goods
  • Bill of Lading / airway waybill / postal customs declaration evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and contract/sales order with ISIC 3240 and NAICS 339930 product description, export destination, and express 'zero-rated export supply' notation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment confirmation proving receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident buyer
  • Export packing list and carrier tracking history to corroborate the transportation route

None. Canada has no national real-time fiscal clearance regime, no mandatory e-invoice clearance network, and no digital stamping requirement. CRA requires registrants to file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or approved accounting software; e-invoice exchange remains voluntary, but all invoice and export substantiation records must be retained in a form acceptable to the CRA.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year limitation period, or longer where misrepresentation or fraud is established. If an export zero-rating claim fails substantiation, the CRA will reassess the supply at 13%, disallow input tax credits/refunds, claw back paid refunds, and charge compounded daily arrears interest. Civil penalties may be assessed under Excise Tax Act s.280 for false statements, and intentional evasion can lead to criminal prosecution under s.327 with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment; directors/owners may also become jointly and severally liable for unremitted HST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no statutory restrictions on profit remittances, dividend distributions, or repatriation of capital to non-residents, provided applicable Canadian withholding taxes (Part XIII tax on dividends and interest, Part XIV branch tax) are met, transfer pricing rules are complied with, and tax information reporting (Form T1135 for specified foreign assets) is current. Cross-border payments require no central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement nor mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts and use export receipts directly for offshore procurement. Reporting obligations are limited to monthly sales tax/GST-HST returns, export declaration statistics via the CBSA, and residual foreign asset/tax disclosures; no repatriation deadline exists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (central monetary authority); no separate foreign exchange control administration; OSFI regulates banks, and FINTRAC, under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, oversees AML compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (viable as a supplemental settlement and currency-conversion rail, not as a primary treasury vehicle). Legal constraints: Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorized deposit-taking institutions in Canada and are not eligible for Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protection. If an EMI collects payments from Canadian customers it must comply with Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act and obtain registration under applicable Canadian payment regulation. The EMI must also support FATCA/CRS reporting and sanctions screening. The Canadian entity should use EMIs for last-mile payouts and small-value crowdfunding settlements while concentrating the majority of liquidity in a CDIC-insured Schedule I bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital injections must arrive via a recognized financial institution using SWIFT MT103 messaging. Each transfer should include the corporate name, Ontario company number, and the unrestricted reference 'CAPITAL INJECTION'. The receiving bank will require a board resolution authorizing the share issuance, executed subscription agreement, share certificate, and proof of funds from the shareholder's institution for source-of-wealth verification. Where a non-resident investor subscribes for equity, an Investment Canada Act review threshold may apply only when percentage of control reaches specified monetary bands; otherwise a notification to Statistics Canada under the Balance of Payments survey program is filed. Avoid routing through crypto-assets, unlicensed remitters, or same-day split wires, which trigger Reg R clearance and possible return of funds.Operational supplier payments are executed using SWIFT MT103 Corporate Payments via the corporate bank's treasury portal, with the beneficiary's IBAN/SWIFT address and full commercial-invoice matching mandated. For letters of credit, the Canadian bank issues a documentary Commercial LC under SWIFT MT700, typically irrevocable and confirmed by the exporter's bank, covering shipments of epoxy resins, molds, pigments, or finished dice; the instrument must include ICC UCP 600 terms and documentary requirements (invoice, packing list, bill of lading, quality inspection certificate). All outbound beneficiary countries are screened against Global Affairs Canada sanctions lists and OFAC screening.
