Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Business Corporation is an autonomous taxable entity: a Canadian-resident corporation pays Part I tax on worldwide income, while a non-resident corporation is taxable only on Canadian-source business income and capital gains, subject to Part I tax, Part XIII withholding and Part XIV branch tax (25% before treaty reduction). Shipbuilding income qualifies as manufacturing and processing (M&P) income, attracting reduced federal (13%) and Ontario (10%) M&P corporate rates. Canada has no pass-through or tax-exempt-zone classification for a commercial shipyard; Ontario corporate income tax is allocated to permanent establishments situated in Ontario.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available elections relevant to an ocean-vessel shipyard: (a) functional currency reporting election for a USD-denominated shipyard (ITA s. 261); (b) deemed-residence and treaty tie-breaker application for an offshore parent holdco (ITA s. 250(5)); (c) non-resident property-income election (ITA s. 216(1)); (d) zero-rating attestation that a vessel is supplied for use as a means of transportation for at least one year (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1) and proof-of-export retention under s. 6; (e) capital cost allowance, loss-carryover and associated-corporation elections (ITA ss. 111 and 256; Income Tax Regulations s. 1100); (f) hybrid-mismatch and interest-stripping outcomes are governed by ITA ss. 18.2, 18.4–18.6 (excessive interest and financing expenses limitation and hybrid mismatch rules) applicable to large cross-border groups.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold applies where the shipyard's worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, do not exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter and in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148(1)). Registration is compulsory once the threshold is exceeded; non-residents supplying specified digital or telecommunication services must register regardless of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The shipyard may zero-rate the sale of an ocean container vessel where: (i) the recipient acquires the vessel for use as a means of transportation for at least one year (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1); (ii) the vessel is exported by the supplier or delivered to a non-resident recipient for export within 60 days of delivery (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, ss. 6–7); or (iii) services are rendered to a non-resident in respect of the vessel and are performed outside Canada. Export zero-rating preserves full input-tax-credit refunds on the shipyard's inputs, including steel, marine equipment and subcontract labour. CRA requires contemporaneous documentary proof of export and may deny zero-rating where the 60-day export period is missed absent a ministerial waiver.

  • CERS (Canada Export Reporting System) electronic customs export declaration with Export Declaration Number — filed with the Canada Border Services Agency before departure for all commercial exports valued over CAD 2,000; vessel export must be reported through CERS
  • Bill of Lading (through bill of lading or maritime waybill) issued by the ocean carrier evidencing carriage from a Canadian port of loading to a named foreign destination and consigned to the non-resident purchaser
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — for Canadian settlement, a Foreign Exchange Certificate or bank settlement record issued by a Schedule I Canadian bank evidencing receipt of the purchase price under the maritime installment letter of credit or escrow arrangement
  • Supporting bundle retained for six years: signed sale and purchase contract, commercial invoice, packing list, vessel title and IMO registry documentation in the foreign owner's name, foreign customs or port-of-destination entry record, and any additional CRA-requested export evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or mandatory tax-authority API validation exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-Canadian Peppol-based standard (Canadiana framework, roll-out 2024–2026). Mandatory obligations: GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, net tax must be remitted electronically, and all source documents (invoices, bills of lading, CERS export declarations) must be retained in readable or machine-readable form for at least six years (ETA s. 286(2)).

CRA export audits test whether the vessel was in fact exported to a non-resident: denial of zero-rating imposes HST at 13% on the full sale price plus interest; the normal four-year reassessment window is extended indefinitely when a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default or fraud (ETA s. 298(5)). Penalties: s. 281.1 false-statement penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the amount claimed; s. 285.1 penalty of 25% of net tax sought to be evaded; criminal proceedings under ETA ss. 327–328 with fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to five years. Canada imposes no statutory 180-day export FX repatriation deadline, but export proceeds not repatriated within the 180-day commercial benchmark will attract transfer-pricing review under ITA s. 247 and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines for related-party maritime sales.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Capital account transactions are generally unrestricted. There are no current or capital account exchange controls, no prior exchange approvals for profit remittance, and no Canadian foreign exchange surrender requirements. Profit repatriation is subject to ordinary domestic tax withholding (Part XIII or treaty-reduced rates on interest and dividends) and to compliance with anti-money laundering reporting, but is not subject to exchange control permission. For a shipyard in Ontario, a non-resident shareholder may upstream dividends or redeem capital freely via CAD or foreign currency. Where foreign currency is held, banks permit termination into CAD at the prevailing market rate without central bank notification below FINTRAC reporting thresholds (CAN 10,000 or more in one transaction).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no deadweight loss from export receipt repatriation in Canada. Exporters may retain, hold, and convert export proceeds in any currency as a commercial matter. A shipyard receiving deep-sea container vessel milestone payments in USD, EUR, or other currency may maintain a foreign currency denominated account with a Canadian Schedule I bank, or convert via spot contract. All cash transactions of C$10,000 or greater and certain FINTRAC-specified virtual currency/reporting thresholds trigger record-keeping and reporting obligations, but no surrender duty is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange policymaking and oversight are also coordinated with the Department of Finance Canada. No separate foreign exchange administration authority exists because Canada maintains a freely floating exchange rate regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with important caveats. Offshore digital EMI providers that are not Canadian-licensed must register with FINTRAC as a money services business if they provide payment services to Canadian entities, and must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (as in force and with applicable thresholds). An offshore EMI may provide a multi-currency virtual account and facilitate low-value cross-border payments to the shipyard's subcontractors or suppliers. However, it cannot as a practical substitute for the shipyard's domestic Schedule I bank accounts because Ontario payroll, OSST/GSST filings, and the commercial letters of credit required for hull construction are not ordinarily supported by unlicensed EMIs. A local Canadian EMI or Canadian-incorporated payment service provider with a Schedule I correspondent account is more viable. The portfolio is classified as viable for payments below $1 million per transaction and only for non-documentary, non-LC obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI for lockbox and equity injection into a Canadian business corporation is routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the beneficiaries operating account. The routing should clearly identify the company legal name, corporate registry number, address, and a purpose description such as 'Foreign Direct Investment — shipyard capital investment, ISIC 3011//NAICS 336611'. Canada does not require special central bank approval; the receiving Canadian bank may treat the wire as an 'inward remittance' and request the source of funds. For a single transaction above CAD 100,000, standard FINTRAC reporting is required by the sender or Canadian bank, and the beneficial ownership source evidence must be made available. The Bank of Canada publishes no purpose code list; however, a commercial bank asks for ISO 20022 purpose code FIELD purposes such as 'IDCP' or 'TRFD' for investment or transfer. It is common practice to use 'GFUNC' for financing-related transfers when the bank provides the SWIFT categories. To avoid delay, include the ultimate beneficial owner passport or corporate ID, the Ontario registry business identification number, and an attestation of non-blocked-trafficking.The primary clearing rails for trade settlements are (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct cash payment under contract, (ii) documentary letters of credit issued by a Canadian bank under UCP600 (or ISP98 for standby LCs), and (iii) conditioned payment orders to suppliers/customhouse broker accounts. Canadian payment infrastructure uses the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) and its modern successor—Payments Canada Real-Time Rail—for CAD-denominated domestic settlements. An ocean container vessel yard will typically receive an irrevocable Commercial LC from the shipowner as the outer structural payment rail. Funds are paid against presentation of commercial invoice, inspection certificate, builder's certificate, bill of lading, and any agreed warehouse/hull insurance certificates. For supplier disbursements abroad, the yard issues SWIFT MT103 with a 'REMITTANCE INFORMATION' field referencing the purchase order and, where applicable, a bond/letter of credit number. Canada participates in CLS settlement for foreign exchange; however, CAD cross-border clearing is via SWIFT and correspondent banks. Offshore digital EMI providers that are not Canadian-licensed must register with FINTRAC as a money services business if they provide payment services to Canadian entities, and must comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (as in force and with applicable thresholds). An offshore EMI may provide a multi-currency virtual account and facilitate low-value cross-border payments to the shipyard's subcontractors or suppliers. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% aggregate (15% federal Part I general corporate rate plus 11.5% Ontario general corporate rate) for a non-Canadian-controlled private corporation operating in Ontario; shipbuilding is eligible for the Ontario Manufacturing and Processing (M&P) reduced rate of 10%, yielding 25.0% combined on qualifying manufacturing profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident (ITA s.212(2)); treaty relief may reduce the rate to 5% for a corporate beneficial owner holding at least 10% of voting shares, or to 15% for portfolio investors.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required when corporate gross revenue equals or exceeds CAD 50 million; Local File required where aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with a single non-resident exceed CAD 5 million (goods or intangibles) or CAD 1 million (services); Form T611, T617 and T618 must accompany T2 Schedule 32 to secure s.247 'reasonable efforts' penalty protection; penalty exposure begins when the transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds the greater of CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue for the year.

Economic substance scope: Canada administers no formalized 'Economic Substance Regulations' registration of the type used in some offshore financial centres; economic substance is enforced through ITA s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) Principal Purpose Test (PPT) embedded in Canadian treaties, the sham doctrine at common law, and CRA anti-avoidance audit doctrine; an Ontario shell entity with no physical shipyard, no employees, and no operational decision-making autonomy will fail substance review, trigger GAAR penalty of 50% of the tax benefit, and may be denied treaty benefits with source-state recharacterization of dividends, interest, and royalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Under-Reporting of Shipyard Revenue

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239: fines of 50% to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; Criminal Code s.380 fraud: imprisonment up to 14 years; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of understated tax; CRA reassessment window extended to 12 years under s.152(4) for misrepresentation attributable to neglect or fraud; daily compound interest applies to all arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize or participate in evasion are jointly exposed to civil restitution orders, personal fines, and imprisonment; CRA may assess directors personally for unremitted source and withholding amounts under s.227.1; courts may impose travel bans and CBSA removal proceedings on non-resident directors convicted of Canadian tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(4) penalty of 10% of the transfer-pricing adjustment; s.18(4) thin-cap denial of interest deduction causing full tax reassessment; GAAR penalty of 50% of the tax benefit; s.247(3) recharacterization of transactions may produce deemed dividends and Part XIII WHT exposure; CRA may apply the misrepresentation window of 12 years.

Director personal liability: Directors approving knowingly false T2 transfer-pricing disclosures face s.239 criminal exposure, personal fines equal to 200% of the evaded tax, and imprisonment; civilly, s.227.1 director liability attaches to unremitted withholding; professional disqualification and cross-border bench warrants are plausible for deliberate TP fraud.
