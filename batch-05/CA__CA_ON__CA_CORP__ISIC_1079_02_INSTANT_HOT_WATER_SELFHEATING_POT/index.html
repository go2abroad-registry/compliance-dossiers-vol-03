Digital access via CRA 'My Business Account' portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or provincially under the Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA). Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN), the universal tax identifier (TIN/VAT ID equivalent), by registering online via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) service; the BN auto-generates program accounts (GST/HST 'RT', corporate income tax 'RC', payroll 'RP'). Step 3: Open the GST/HST program account and register for the Ontario 13% HST rate; registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed CAD $30,000 in any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s.148). Step 4: File T2 Corporation Income Tax Return through CRA on behalf of both federal and Ontario corporate income tax; no separate Ontario corporate tax return exists. Step 5: For a non-resident-controlled corporation, appoint a Canadian agent for service of process (Form RC467) and note that CCPC status (and the small business deduction) is denied. Step 6: Register with the Ontario Ministry of Finance for Employer Health Tax if Ontario payroll exceeds CAD $1,000,000 annually, and obtain any required Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) licence for the manufacture of self-heating food products.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA); profits are taxed at corporate level (federal 15% general / 9% small business; Ontario 10% general / 3.2% small business on active business income up to CAD $500,000), and shareholders are separately taxed on dividends (Part XIII withholding applies to non-residents). It is NOT a pass-through entity. Territorial scope: resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations with a permanent establishment in Ontario are taxed on Canadian-source business income under ITA s.115. No tax-exempt zones exist in Ontario.

Available election forms

Canada has no 'check-the-box' entity classification regime. Available statutory elections include: (a) ITA s.216 election for non-residents with net Canadian rental income; (b) ITA s.219 election for non-resident corporations to file a Part I branch tax return instead of Part XIII withholding; (c) ITA s.85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property to the corporation; (d) functional currency election under ITA s.261 (Schedule 89); (e) hybrid-mismatch elections under ITA ss.18.2/18.3 recharacterizing deemed deductions; (f) GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA s.226.2); (g) GST/HST related-party simplified accounting elections under ETA s.227.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 — small supplier threshold under ETA s.148(1); registration is mandatory when worldwide taxable supplies exceed $30,000 in any single calendar quarter or across the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. For non-resident exporters of physical goods, only Canadian-source taxable supplies count toward the threshold; the payment rails (SWIFT Wire / TT) do not alter the nexus analysis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible manufactured goods (instant self-heating hot water pots and associated food/chemical heating packs) are zero-rated under s.1, Part V, Schedule VI to the ETA provided: (i) the purchaser is a non-resident, (ii) the goods are exported 'as soon as is reasonable' after the sale, and (iii) no further processing occurs in Canada. Zero-rating also extends to related services performed outside Canada (ss.6-9, Part V, Schedule VI). The manufacturer claims full input tax credits (ITCs) on all production inputs on the same GST/HST return reporting zero-rated exports. Domestic sales of such prepared self-heating products would be taxable at 13% HST (not basic groceries under Part III, Schedule VI), requiring careful ITC apportionment.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA B13A form or CADEX electronic equivalent
  • Commercial invoice evidencing sale to the non-resident buyer and stating the vendor's GST/HST BN
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (with port of exit, container number, consignee identifiers)
  • Packing list proving physical movement of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — Canadian banking equivalent: bank wire transfer / foreign exchange confirmation evidencing settlement of export proceeds
  • GST/HST return workpapers mapping zero-rated export lines to supporting customs and freight records
  • Six-year export substantiation register (CRA/GST Form 137 or internal equivalent)

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government invoice-validation API regime. Requirements: (a) invoices must contain prescribed particulars per ETA s.223 (seller name, BN, date, description, tax rate, tax amount); (b) electronic invoicing (PDF/XML/EDI) is permitted without prior CRA approval, but records must be retained for six years; (c) electronic GST/HST filing via GST/HST NETFILE is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD $1.5 million and for all non-resident registrants; (d) remittances over CAD $10,000 to CRA must be made electronically; (e) customs export declarations must be transmitted electronically to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CADEX) or equivalent prior to export.

CRA may reassess within four years of the original assessment (unlimited for misrepresentation or fraud). An exporter failing audit substantiation has zero-rating reversed, becomes liable for 13% HST on the full invoice value plus arrears interest (prescribed rate currently approximately 8%–10% per annum), and loses related ITCs. Penalties: (i) false or deceptive claim penalty under ETA s.282 — 50% of the amount improperly claimed; (ii) gross negligence penalty under ETA s.281 — 25%; (iii) failure to keep records under ETA s.285.1 — CAD $500 per day (minimum $1,000, maximum $25,000); (iv) criminal tax evasion under ETA s.327 — fines scaled to the tax/rebate falsely sought (summary conviction up to 2 years imprisonment; indictment up to 5 years); (v) directors and officers jointly and severally liable for unremitted GST/HST under ETA s.323(1); (vi) falsified export remittance records may trigger PCMLTFA anti-money-laundering audits and reputational sanctions for financial institutions processing the SWIFT/TT payments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account; there are no exchange-control restrictions on inbound equity subscriptions, trade payments, or outbound profit remittances. Profits/dividends may be repatriated without Bank of Canada approval provided the corporation passes the solvency test under the Canada Business Corporations Act and applicable withholding taxes (Part XIII Income Tax Act, treaty-reduced) are remitted. The only friction is bank KYC and FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting for movements of CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour period.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline on export receipts. Exporters may hold, convert, or use foreign currency freely; tax and customs records must still support the commercial substance of ISIC 1079 self-heating food and hot-water-pot export sales.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) is the central bank; no separate foreign-exchange administration exists. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially supervises federal banks, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/ATF obligations for cross-border funds. Currency matters are governed by the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate. Canada has no EU-style EMI license; an offshore digital EMI must either register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC, use an OSFI-regulated sponsor bank, or operate only as a payment facilitator. A pure offshore EMI cannot solely provide settlement liquidity. It is viable for collections from foreign distributors, but core treasury clearing must remain with a tier-1 bank to preserve credit and regulatory certainty.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general (15% federal + 11.5% Ontario); 25% combined for qualifying manufacturing and processing profits (15% federal + 10% Ontario M&P); 12.2% small business combined (9% federal + 3.2% Ontario) only for qualifying CCPC

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on gross Canadian dividends paid or credited to a non-resident shareholder; branch profits of a non-resident corporation may be subject to the separately imposed 25% branch tax under Part XIV

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due six months after fiscal year-end; tax balance due two months after fiscal year-end (three months for CCPCs claiming the SBD); non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada file under the same due date rules

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory CAD threshold for ordinary Local File/Master File: CRA requires contemporaneous and reasonable TP documentation as facts warrant. Mandatory Country-by-Country Report if the multinational group’s consolidated revenue is at least CAD 750,000,000. As a prudent benchmark, full Local File documentation is expected for controlled cross-border transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 annually, and T106 reporting applies to reportable non-arm’s length transactions above normal commercial thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate OFS-style Economic Substance Regulation, but economic substance is enforced through: (i) central management and control residence doctrine; (ii) GAAR under ITA s.245; (iii) treaty limitation-on-benefits and beneficial-ownership analysis; and (iv) CRA transfer-pricing substance tests. A Canadian manufacturing entity is expected to maintain real employees, premises, board records, bank accounts, inventory, and operational decision-making in Ontario; an artificial conduit may be recharacterized and denied treaty or Part XIII relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty under ITA s.163(2) of up to 50% of the understated tax; criminal tax evasion under ITA s.239(1) punishable on indictment by a fine of 50% to 200% of tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment for up to 5 years, and by lower summary conviction penalties for indictable-only conduct; CRA can reassess beyond the normal three-year period where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect or fraud

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct the evasion may be charged personally; veil-piercing under common-law fraud, sham, and oppression remedies; upon criminal conviction the director may face imprisonment, passport impoundment/travel ban, and restitution orders for taxes, penalties, and interest
Transfer Pricing Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may reprice transactions under ITA s.247; imposition of a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the statutory threshold and reasonable efforts are absent; denial of deduction is automatic to the extent of non-arm’s length amount; unpaid amounts attract interest and can be recharacterized as shareholder distributions under s.15(1), triggering Part XIII withholding

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement objectively unreasonable intercompany pricing without commercial substance can be named as participants in a sham; if the corporation is an alter ego for the foreign parent, courts may lift the corporate veil; directors may owe fiduciary restitution to the corporation and CRA may pursue them for source deductions or fraudulent conveyance proceeds
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR under ITA s.245 may nullify the abusive avoidance transaction and impose tax on the income as if the transaction had not occurred, preserving penalties and interest; CRA may deny treaty benefits for lack of beneficial ownership/LOB; Part XIII tax can be reassessed on the entire dividend or deemed distribution; the corporation may also lose the Ontario M&P and small-business preferences

Director personal liability: A knowing director of a shell can be personally liable for abetting a sham; criminal prosecution under s.239(1) if false information was filed; travel bans and bail conditions are possible while charges are pending, and equitable remedies allow restitution of dissipated corporate assets
AML / Cross-Border Payment Red Flags

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) can impose administrative monetary penalties (entities up to CAD 1,000,000 per contravention for certain violations, with larger sanctions for repeat failure to maintain AML program); criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31 is indictable with imprisonment up to 10 years; unknown-source corporate assets can be seized/forfeited and the corporation can be struck off for non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute or benefit from a laundering scheme can be prosecuted and sentenced to imprisonment; the court may impose a departure ban/travel prohibition, mandatory surrender of documents, and orders of forfeiture and restitution; directors cannot use the corporate veil to shield personal liability for deliberate AML violations
