Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Canadian limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed by the limited partnership agreement and may be in cash or in kind. No escrow account is statutorily mandated for formation, but client funds must be held in a segregated trust account at a Canadian Schedule I bank or other permitted financial institution in accordance with the applicable patent agent trust rules.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the municipal fire department under the National Fire Code of Canada; structural load and occupancy permit from the municipal building department; workplace health and safety clearance under the Canada Labour Code, Part II for employees of the general partner; workers' compensation registration and first aid certification are required before commencing operations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Canadian limited partnership; capital contributions are fixed by the limited partnership agreement and may be in cash or in kind. Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — GST/HST Program, Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at ca.businessregistration.gc.ca, and CRA 'My Business Account' (mybusinessaccount-cra; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Customs matters, including export declarations for physical deliverables, are administered separately by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 – Determine registration obligation: the Limited Partnership (LP) is a separate 'person' for GST/HST purposes (ETA s. 123(1) includes a partnership). The LP must obtain a Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account ('RT') if it is not a small supplier (worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters), if it makes taxable supplies in Canada in the ordinary course of a commercial activity, or if it voluntarily registers. STEP 2 – Application: Canadian-resident filers apply electronically through BRO; non-resident partners or non-resident owner-applicants apply by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' (supplemented by Form RC1A for non-resident accounts), the executed LP agreement, a register of partners, and proof of the managing partner's authority to the CRA Non-Resident GST/HST Program office. STEP 3 – Issuance: the CRA issues a 9-digit BN plus a program-account suffix, e.g., '123456789 RT0001' for GST/HST; payroll (RP), corporate income tax (RC) and customs (RM) accounts can be added under the same BN. STEP 4 – Post-registration compliance: the LP must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE when annual gross revenue exceeds CAD $1 million, remit net tax, or remit refunds for zero- rated exports; it must retain books and records for six years (ETA s. 286) and may maintain an online CRA business account. STEP 5 – Non-resident partner nexus: the LP must file Form T5013 partnership information returns and remit Part I or Part XIII withholding under ITA s. 219 on income allocable to non-resident limited partners, regardless of the GST/HST account status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' election; the Income Tax Act statutorily treats the LP as fiscally transparent (ITA ss. 96-103) and the Excise Tax Act treats the partnership as a registrant separate from its partners for GST/HST. Available statutory elections and designations include: (1) joint election under ITA s. 97(2) to transfer eligible property into the LP at agreed tax values at formation; (2) election under ITA s. 249.1(4) to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period for the partnership with concurrence of all partners; (3) designation of the managing partner as the partnership representative for CRA GST/HST correspondence and service of notices; (4) the GST/HST grouping rules under ETA s. 156 and s. 273 are generally unavailable to a bare partnership but a closely-related corporate group may elect to treat taxable supplies as made by a single registrant; and (5) hybrid-mismatch constraints: Canada's OECD BEPS Action 2 rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.3 and related provisions) deny deductions or withholding relief for hybrid arrangements involving offshore limited partners, and no elective 'reverse hybrid' classification exists domestically; a Canadian LP is always transparent for Canadian income tax, while foreign jurisdictions' classifications are disregarded to the extent they create hybrid mismatches.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no foreign exchange control laws requiring central bank approval or special licensing for current account payments, profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, or capital repatriation. Dividends and service fees paid by a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident partners or foreign service providers are subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, if applicable, and standard anti-money laundering monitoring under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Cross-border wire transfers equal to or greater than CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC. FATCA and CRS reporting obligations apply to relevant financial accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules apply in Canada. Export receipts and all other foreign currency revenue may be retained indefinitely in any currency, with no mandatory conversion ratio, timeline, or repatriation obligation. For physical currency exports or imports, any person carrying cash or equivalent monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more per crossing must declare it to the Canada Border Services Agency. For electronic transfers, the reporting trigger is solely the FINTRAC CAD 10,000 threshold.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority; Canada maintains no separate foreign exchange administration and no general exchange control legislation)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Moderate. Offshore digital payment institutions holding an EU EMI licence or similar foreign authorization cannot operate directly as a Canadian bank under the Bank Act. They may provide payment facilitation through a Canadian bank partner, but they are not eligible to open a native Canadian settlement account. To operate as a money services business in Canada, a fintech EMI must register with FINTRAC as an MSB, maintain a Canadian presence, and typically establish a commercial relationship with a tier-1 Canadian chartered bank. As a result, reliance solely on an offshore EMI for core treasury clearing is not recommended; viable only as a supplementary low-value disbursement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign partners should be effected via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers into a dedicated Capital Escrow Account. The wire reference must identify 'Capital Contribution' or 'Limited Partner Contribution', and the remitter must provide a source-of-funds statement, supporting bank reference, and a copy of the executed capital call agreement. Incoming trade payments for patent prosecution services must be routed to the Operational Account and coded as commercial payment for professional services, not as capital, to avoid mischaracterization. Under PCMLTFA, the receiving Canadian bank must record the transaction if it is CAD 10,000 or above and report to FINTRAC. Canada has no mandatory central bank purpose code, but banks may require the ISO 20022 purpose code 'GDSV' for professional legal services or 'NTRD' for other trade receipts depending on the specific correspondent routing.Outbound disbursements to foreign patent offices and client-associated entities are cleared through SWIFT MT103 wires or commercial letters of credit under UCP600 when a documentary transaction is required. For Canadian domestic payments, small-denomination claim-payment fees are cleared through Payments Canada's Real-Time Rail or older automated clearing settlement system (ACSS). For foreign patent office annuity payments and PCT fees, MT103 wires remain the standard clearing rail, with payment references containing the application number and WIPO/patent office account codes. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$5,000,000 (combined value of controlled cross-border transactions for transfer-pricing penalty shield purposes; CRA does not impose a formal local-file filing threshold, but contemporaneous documentation is expected for all material controlled transactions). Master File-style group documentation is expected when the multinational group has significant cross-border intercompany arrangements, and Country-by-Country filing applies to groups with global consolidated revenue above CA$700 million or the OECD EUR 750 million threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the CRA enforces substance-based taxation through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), the transfer-pricing reasonable-consideration standard (s. 247), and the 'carrying on business in Canada' test (s. 253). A patent prosecution agency must maintain real Canadian office premises, local professional staff, client files, contractual data and decision-making in Canada; paper-only non-resident structures will be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2) ITA of 50% of the net understated tax plus applicable interest. Criminal tax evasion under s. 239(1) ITA: on summary conviction, fines up to 100% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to two years; on indictment, fines up to 200% and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under s. 227.1 ITA for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST. CRA may assess them personally; criminal convictions can lead to travel restraints, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from acting as a director.
Artificial Shell Company / GAAR

Statutory sanction: CRA recharacterization under s. 245(2) GAAR denies any tax benefit; s. 245(5) imposes a penalty of 10% of the denied tax benefit. The CRA may also apply the sham doctrine at common law, making the return invalid and all allocations attributable to the actual Canadian taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Where a corporate general partner is found to be a facade, the corporate veil may be pierced, exposing directors to personal liability for unpaid Canadian taxes, interest, penalties and civil restitution. Criminal fraud prosecutions may also be brought under s. 380(1) of the Criminal Code, punishable by imprisonment up to 14 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under s. 247(2) ITA, the CRA will adjust the transaction to an arm’s-length amount. The penalty under s. 247(4) is 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue. Interest accrues from the original assessment date, and penalties may be stacked with gross-negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for TP adjustments, but if they authorize false invoices or withhold Part XIII tax on deemed royalties, s. 227.1 personal liability may apply to the full unremitted amount. Directors could also face negligent misrepresentation claims by other partners and CRA criminal referral for false accounting.
AML / FINTRAC Compliance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties may reach C$2,000,000 for a body corporate and C$500,000 for individuals per violation. Knowingly laundering proceeds of crime under s. 462.31 of the Criminal Code is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment up to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of the corporate general partner may be named personally in FINTRAC penalty proceedings for failure to exercise due diligence. Criminal money-laundering charges can result in personal fines, confiscation orders, travel bans, and imprisonment; the CRA also retains power to add directors to the 'director liability' list for failing to register and report under the Excise Tax Act.
