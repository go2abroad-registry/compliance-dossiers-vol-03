Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Tax registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA Business Account (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html). Limited partnership extra-provincial/domestic registration is administered by provincial corporate registries (e.g., Ontario Companies and Personal Property Security Branch), not by CRA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign (non-resident) limited partnership must: (1) apply for a Business Number and GST/HST account via Business Registration Online or Form RC1, selecting the non-resident partnership stream; (2) submit the foreign registration certificate, partnership agreement, and the full name and address of every general and limited partner for entity classification; (3) obtain the nine-digit Business Number with the 'RT' GST/HST program account; the same BN supports other CRA accounts. Where Paddle or Lemon Squeezy acts as merchant of record, the platform is the legal supplier to consumers for Canadian GST/HST purposes and the LP's direct registration exposure is limited to its own supplies in Canada. Online registration is immediate; paper RC1 processing typically takes two to six weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act (ITA) section 96 the LP is fiscally transparent: it is not a taxpayer; income and loss are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners. For GST/HST the partnership is a separate 'person' under Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 123 and may itself be a registrant. Non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source income only, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding and the limited-partner at-risk rules in ITA ss. 96(2.1)–96(2.5). — A foreign (non-resident) limited partnership must: (1) apply for a Business Number and GST/HST account via Business Registration Online or Form RC1, selecting the non-resident partnership stream; (2) submit the foreign registration certificate, partnership agreement, and the full name and address of every general and limited partner for entity classification; (3) obtain the nine-digit Business Number with the 'RT' GST/HST program account; the same BN supports other CRA accounts. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Income Tax Act (ITA) section 96 the LP is fiscally transparent: it is not a taxpayer; income and loss are computed at the partnership level and allocated to partners. For GST/HST the partnership is a separate 'person' under Excise Tax Act (ETA) section 123 and may itself be a registrant. Non-resident partners are taxed on Canadian-source income only, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding and the limited-partner at-risk rules in ITA ss. 96(2.1)–96(2.5).

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; LP transparency is automatic under ITA s.96. Available elections: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration by a small supplier under ETA s.240(3); once registered, the small supplier must remain a registrant for at least one year; (ii) ETA s.273 partnership election designating one partner as the partnership's GST/HST representative or agent; (iii) hybrid mismatch rules — ITA Part VI.1 (ss. 221.21–221.42, enacted by Bill C-59) apply automatically (payments under structured arrangements after June 30, 2023, and other mismatches for taxation years starting after December 15, 2023) with no taxpayer election to avoid deduction denial or recharacterization; (iv) the specified investment flow-through (SIFT) regime applies only to publicly traded partnerships and is irrelevant to a private micro-SaaS LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold in ETA s.123 and s.240(3), tested against worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) that the LP, and any associated persons, made in the current calendar quarter or in the preceding four calendar quarters. A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada must register upon exceeding this threshold or immediately when it has a permanent establishment or physical presence in Canada, whichever is earlier, subject to the special registration rules for digital-economy suppliers and distribution platforms.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% GST/HST applies as follows: (i) goods exported from Canada — ETA s.21 and Schedule VI, Part V (supplies to be exported), with proof of export retained; (ii) services supplied to non-residents — Schedule VI, Part V, s.7, zero-rated unless the service is performed in whole or in part in Canada, relates to real property situated in Canada, relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or is rendered to a person in Canada; (iii) intangible personal property (including hosted software accessed by download or via cloud, as characterized by CRA) — Schedule VI, Part II, zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident if the right or property is not to be used in Canada and the supply is not connected with Canadian-situated property. A micro-SaaS LP must be the legal supplier of record to claim zero-rating; when selling via Paddle or Lemon Squeezy as merchant of record, the platform is the taxable supplier to the consumer, the platform collects GST/HST on Canadian sales, and the LP's own zero-rating claim attaches only to its separate licence supply to the non-resident platform.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency export reporting via CARM/Form B13A) — mandatory for physical exports; not applicable to software delivered electronically
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (physical exports only)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment-platform foreign-currency settlement statements evidencing receipt of consideration from non-residents
  • Commercial invoice or SaaS subscription record expressly stating '0% GST/HST — zero-rated supply to a non-resident' and identifying the non-resident recipient's foreign address
  • Merchant-of-record payout reports from Paddle or Lemon Squeezy showing net remittances and end-customer geographic data
  • Executed software licence/subscription agreement and the non-resident customer's foreign VAT/GST registration number, where obtained

No federal requirement exists for real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls. CRA does not operate a VAT e-invoicing clearance API. GST/HST returns are filed electronically (NETFILE/EFILE or CRA Business Account) and tax is remitted with the return. Canada has adopted the Pan-European Public Procurement On-Line (PEPPOL) standard for B2G e-invoicing through the 'Polaris' network (Interac), but this is a procurement payment-format mandate for federal suppliers, not a tax clearance mechanism. Registrants must retain books and records for six years. In the merchant-of-record model, Paddle or Lemon Squeezy invoices the Canadian consumer and remits GST/HST at checkout under its own registration.

An exporter that claims 0% GST/HST without meeting the statutory conditions faces: (i) reassessment at 5% on the full consideration with statutory interest (ETA ss. 280/280.1; ITA s.161 for income-tax shortfalls); (ii) civil penalties — false statements or omissions under ETA s.285 at the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax understated, plus failure-to-register and failure-to-file penalties under ETA ss. 280 and 281; (iii) income-tax gross-negligence penalties under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of any income tax understated if the zero-rating claim is part of a scheme to underreport income; (iv) criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under ITA s.239(1) and ETA s.327, with fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years; and (v) retrospective clawback — the statute-barred period is extended indefinitely where the return was filed with misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default (ITA s.152(4); ETA s.298(3)), allowing full unwinding of prior refunds, denial of pending refunds, and revocation or restriction of the registration and zero-rating status for future periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime under the Bank of Canada Act. Current account transactions are freely convertible with minimal restrictions. Capital account transactions require prior approval for outflows exceeding CAD 1 million per transaction or involving non-residents. Profit remittances to non-resident limited partners are permissible following tax withholding at source and exchange control reporting. Key hurdles include mandatory Bank of Canada filings for capital repatriation, anti-money laundering screening, and compliance with the Foreign Exchange Act for any structuring that circumvents reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canadian exporters, including micro SaaS providers receiving foreign subscription payments, must surrender all foreign exchange proceeds to an authorized foreign exchange dealer within 90 days of receipt. Conversion to CAD is mandatory at prevailing market rates with no exceptions for low-volume operations. Statutory timeline is fixed at 90 days under Bank of Canada regulations; failure to comply triggers administrative penalties and potential license suspension.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax rate (plus provincial rates ranging from 0% to 16%; limited partnership itself is fiscally transparent and not subject to corporate income tax, but partners are taxed on their share of income)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents; for a limited partnership, distributions to non-resident partners are not dividends but are treated as a return of capital or income. Withholding tax under Part XIII applies to the partner's share of Canadian-source income (e.g., business income, interest, royalties) at a rate of 25% unless reduced by treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For fiscal years beginning after 2022, the CRA requires a Local File and Master File for transactions exceeding CAD 5 million (gross revenue) or CAD 1 million for certain transactions (e.g., intercompany services). For a micro-SaaS entity, the threshold is likely not met, but contemporaneous documentation is still recommended if cross-border transactions with non-arm's length parties exceed CAD 1 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act. The partnership must have genuine commercial substance; a shell partnership with no real business activity may be challenged. The CRA also applies the 'substance over form' doctrine and may recharacterize transactions. For a micro-SaaS, the partnership must demonstrate that it has the necessary personnel, assets, and functions to support its income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239) and Criminal Code (s. 380). Penalties: fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under the Income Tax Act (s. 227.1) if they fail to withhold and remit amounts. For tax evasion, individuals may face personal criminal liability, travel bans, and seizure of personal assets.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny tax benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties of 25% of the tax benefit (s. 245). The partnership may be deemed a sham, leading to full recharacterization of transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be jointly and severally liable for the tax liability of the partnership if they participated in the sham. Travel bans may be imposed under the Income Tax Act (s. 226) for unpaid taxes.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly failed to implement compliance programs. Travel bans may be imposed under the PCMLTFA for serious non-compliance.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust the transfer price, impose a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (s. 247(3)), and apply interest on the additional tax. For gross negligence, the penalty may be 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be liable for the penalty if they participated in the transfer pricing mispricing. Travel bans may be imposed for unpaid assessments.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: The partnership is liable for the full amount of unwithheld tax, plus interest and penalties (s. 227(8)). Penalties range from 10% to 20% of the amount not withheld, plus daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners are personally liable for the unwithheld tax under s. 227.1 if they failed to exercise due diligence. Travel bans may be imposed for unpaid amounts.
