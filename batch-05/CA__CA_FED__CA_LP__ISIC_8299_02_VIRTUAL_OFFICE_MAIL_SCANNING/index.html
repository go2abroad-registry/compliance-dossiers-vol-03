Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA), Business Registration Online (BRO) portal and CRA My Business Account portal; customs administration is vested in the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A Canadian Limited Partnership, including one formed with non-resident partners, must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and, where applicable, a GST/HST Program Account (RT) before making taxable supplies in Canada. Procedure: (1) apply through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) using the partnership pathway, or file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) providing legal name, Canadian registered or principal office address, mailing address, principal business activity code (NAICS 561431 - Commercial Mail Receiving Agency / Virtual Office), date of first business activity in Canada, full names and countries of residence of all general and limited partners, and remittance banking details; (2) the GST/HST RT account is opened within the same BN application where taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in Canada or where voluntary registration is elected; (3) CRA issues the BN and RT account confirmation in writing, ordinarily within 5 to 15 business days; (4) the LP must file the T5013 partnership information return annually and GST/HST returns in accordance with its class of registrant.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - a Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under Income Tax Act section 96, with income, losses, and tax credits flowing through to general and limited partners; the LP itself is not subject to federal income tax. For GST/HST purposes, however, the LP is a separate 'person' and independent registrant under the Excise Tax Act section 123, so indirect tax liabilities are computed at the partnership level.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box election system; entity classification is determined by statute. Available elections and adjustments include: (i) partnership fiscal period election under ITA section 249.1(4); (ii) non-resident owner election under ITA section 216(1) for Canadian rental property income; (iii) ITA section 85 rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of partnership property to a corporation; (iv) GST/HST closely related election under Excise Tax Act section 156 for nil-value supplies among closely related entities; (v) GST/HST Quick Method election under ETA sections 227 to 230 for simplified remittance; and (vi) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA sections 18.4 to 18.6, which apply mandatorily (no elective override) to neutralize foreign tax credit generator and hybrid partnership arrangements involving non-resident partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small supplier threshold under ETA sections 123(1) and 148(1); registration becomes mandatory once the value of taxable supplies made in or imported into Canada exceeds CAD 30,000 in any calendar quarter or in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters, with the LP eligible for voluntary registration below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported directly by the supplier or exported by a non-resident recipient within statutory time limits under ETA Schedule VI, Part I. For services, including virtual office and mail-scanning services supplied to non-residents, zero-rating is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V where the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the service is not supplied to an individual present in Canada; zero-rating is denied where the service relates to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada (including physical mail handling or storage in Canada), or Canadian intellectual property, or where the non-resident acquires the service for consumption, use, or supply in Canada. The LP must obtain per-client documentary evidence of non-resident status and confirm the place of supply analysis before reporting 0% GST/HST.

  • Customs Export Declaration - CBSA export report (B13 form or CARM e-manifest) with the applicable zero-rated export code for tangible goods shipped abroad
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill naming the non-resident consignee and the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank receipt (SWIFT MT103, FX confirmation, or remittance advice) evidencing receipt of export proceeds or offshore settlement
  • Commercial invoice and executed CMRA service agreement identifying the non-resident client, the place of supply, and the zero-rating basis

Canada imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API-based government clearance, or digital stamping for invoices. Mandatory invoice content is prescribed by ETA section 223: supplier name and Business Number, invoice date, supply date, description of the CMRA/mail-scanning service, consideration, and GST/HST shown separately. Electronic invoices are permissible under CRA Information Circular IC60-01; voluntary interoperability is available through the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PCTF) and the PEPPOL network. Records and e-invoices must be retained in electronically readable format for six years and produced to the CRA on demand.

Improper or fraudulent export zero-rating claims expose the LP to: (i) gross negligence penalties of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the GST/HST net tax in issue under ETA section 280, and income tax gross negligence penalties of 50% of understated tax under ITA section 163(2); (ii) false statement and failure to file penalties under ETA sections 285 and 285.1; (iii) criminal prosecution for evasion or wilful misrepresentation under ETA sections 282 to 283, with fines up to CAD 20,000 and imprisonment; and (iv) retrospective clawback of zero-rated refunds with daily compounding prescribed-rate interest (CRA prescribed rates currently ranging from approximately 7% to 9% per annum). The CRA may assess within four years in ordinary cases, seven years for misrepresentation or neglect, and without limitation for fraud or false fundamental statements under ETA section 298 and ITA section 152(4). Under the CBSA CARM automated risk-flagging system, export records lacking the complete substantiation bundle are subject to enhanced verification and hold-and-verify procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is freely convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada has an open capital account with no current-account/capital-account distinction for convertibility purposes. Profit remittances and LP distributions to non-resident partners are not blocked by FX controls, but are subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax, source-of-funds due diligence, and tax-residency documentation. A CMRA virtual-office and mail-scanning business will trigger enhanced bank scrutiny because the address is not a physical operating location.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, deposit, or mandatory conversion requirement. Exporters and service providers may retain proceeds in any permitted currency, convert freely at market rates, and report income under the Income Tax Act functional-currency rules. Records must support FINTRAC large-transaction and electronic-funds-transfer reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no foreign-exchange licensing or exchange-control regime; OSFI supervises federally regulated deposit-taking institutions and FINTRAC administers AML/ATF obligations under PCMLTFA. The Bank of Canada issues Canadian currency and conducts monetary policy but does not approve international payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary payment processing and low for primary account-opening. Canada does not provide an EU/UK-style EMI passport. A non-resident electronic money institution may only operate in Canada if it complies with the Bank Act for deposit-taking and with PCMLTFA registration as a foreign money services business (MSB). Digital-only EMIs are not acceptable substitutes for the segregated capital escrow account and operational account required for a Canadian LP. The CMRA business classification will subject any offshore EMI arrangement to enhanced due diligence and may cause rejection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 customer transfer from the investing partner's bank to the Canadian Schedule I bank. Tag 50 must show the full legal name and address of the ordering partner; tag 59 must show the exact LP legal name, the segregated capital-escrow account number, and the address on file with the bank; tag 70 must state "LP capital contribution per partnership agreement dated [DD-MM-YYYY]". Canada has no official central-bank purpose-code list, but the bank will record a Balance-of-Payments descriptor such as "inward non-resident direct investment / equity contribution" and may require a written purpose statement. Transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting, so originator and beneficiary data must be complete. Pre-verification must include executed capital commitment, subscription schedule, source-of-wealth dossier, and written clearance that the capital escrow account is enabled for credit.Trade disbursements must be made only from the operational account, never from capital escrow. High-value CAD payments clear through Lynx (Payments Canada real-time gross settlement system) or the Automated Clearing Settlement System for lower-value payments; USD payments clear through the bank's U.S. correspondent via Fedwire or CHIPS using SWIFT MT103. Commercial letters of credit are issued under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 with cover payments by MT202 or MT103. Documentary collections are available for commodity or service trade. For recurring card and Stripe settlement revenue, Stripe will settle CAD into the operational account through the standard ACSS payout rail after the card settlement date. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

38% (federal Part I rate) reduced to 28% after the 10% provincial abatement; 15% for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) on active business income up to the business limit. Non-resident-owned limited partnerships are subject to Part I tax on Canadian-source income and Part XIII withholding on certain payments.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25%, but limited partnerships are flow-through vehicles; distributions are generally not subject to withholding tax because they are treated as a return of capital or a share of income already taxed at the partner level. If the LP is treated as a corporation under Canadian domestic law, dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25%, deemed to be paid by the non-resident shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

T5013 must be filed within five months after the partnership's fiscal year-end. T2 must be filed within two months after the taxation year-end for CCPCs (three months for non-CCPCs). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (Canadian dollars) of gross revenue for the fiscal period for a taxpayer's Canadian reporting entity; a Local File and Master File are mandatory when the total amount of cross-border transactions with non-arm's length persons exceeds CAD 1,000,000 (or, for loan transactions, exceeds CAD 250,000), unless the taxpayer qualifies for the small business exception (revenue below CAD 10 million, assets below CAD 50 million, and annual cross-border transactions below CAD 1 million).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime; however, the CRA applies a substance-over-form doctrine, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 of the Income Tax Act, and the Canadian transfer pricing penalty provisions require meaningful economic substance. A limited partnership providing virtual office/mail scanning services must show real premises, employees, or agents in Canada sufficient to establish a permanent establishment and to support the deduction of expenses; otherwise, CRA may disregard the partnership's allocation and impose a 10% transfer-pricing penalty plus the associated tax and interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Section 239 of the Income Tax Act)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to two years; on indictment: a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. The court may also order a civil penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax for gross negligence under subsection 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable under sections 227.1 and 323 of the Income Tax Act for corporate tax withholdings and unremitted GST/HST; failure to remit invokes director's liability certificates and asset seizures. In cases of fraud or civil conspiracy, directors can be personally liable for the partnership's unpaid taxes and CTSBs (CRA civil restitution orders); travel bans under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act may accompany criminal charges. Imprisonment terms are available as stated.
Shell Company/Artificial Transactions (GAAR, Section 245)

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to deny the tax benefit; penalties under subsection 245(3) include the full reassessment of tax plus interest, and under subsection 245(4) a penalty of 50% of the denied benefit if the GAAR transaction is not disclosed on Schedule 6. Reassessment and penalty periods can be extended under a waiver or if misrepresentation is found.

Director personal liability: Directors or senior managers who knowingly cause the partnership to rely on sham arrangements may be found liable as promoters or counselors of tax avoidance under subsection 331.1(1); civil penalties of up to CAD 100,000 per individual and CAD 500,000 per corporation (for misrepresentation) may be imposed. Personal liability extends under provincial partnership statutes to general partners; a director of the corporate general partner may be pursued for conspiracy or fraud under the Criminal Code.
Anti-Money Laundering / Regulatory Failure (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) for non-compliance can reach CAD 1,000,000 per violation for a corporation and CAD 500,000 per individual; failure to comply with a compliance order can lead to criminal fines and imprisonment up to five years. FINTRAC can issue a notice of violation and freeze assets.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable if they knowingly acquiesce in AML non-compliance; they can be subject to travel bans under a criminal restraint order, asset freeze, and imprisonment for up to five years. Officers who provide false statements to FINTRAC face fines and up to five years' imprisonment. Civil restitution to victims of mail fraud or identity theft can pierce the corporate veil.
Foreign Account Reporting and Partnership Allocations (Part XIII and T1135)

Statutory sanction: Failure to file T1135 results in penalties starting at CAD 25 for each day late, to a maximum of CAD 2,500, and increased to CAD 100 per day for 24 months for gross negligence (maximum CAD 12,000). Part XIII failure to withhold invokes liability for 25% of the gross amount paid to non-resident recipients, plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholdings under section 227.1 if the corporation fails to deduct; the director must have been in office during the period of default and failed to exercise due diligence. Restitution claims may pierce the veil where the LP structure is used to conceal the foreign beneficial owner.
