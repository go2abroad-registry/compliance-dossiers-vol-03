Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; administering statutes: Excise Tax Act (ETA), Income Tax Act (ITA), and Partnership Filing Requirements (T5013).

E-government filing portal

1) Register the limited partnership under the applicable provincial Limited Partnerships Act to obtain a provincial registration number. 2) Apply to CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) for the nine-digit federal Business Number (BN). 3) Activate program accounts: GST/HST (RT) account (mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, or earlier by election), Payroll (RP) if employees are hired, Import/Export (RM) only if physical goods are moved across borders. 4) Because the LP is a distinct 'person' under ETA s. 123(1), the LP must hold its own BN and GST/HST account even though income tax flows through to partners; the T5013 partnership information return allocates income/losses to partners under ITA ss. 96-103.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — 1) Register the limited partnership under the applicable provincial Limited Partnerships Act to obtain a provincial registration number. 2) Apply to CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) for the nine-digit federal Business Number (BN). 3) Activate program accounts: GST/HST (RT) account (mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, or earlier by election), Payroll (RP) if employees are hired, Import/Export (RM) only if physical goods are moved across borders. 4) Because the LP is a distinct 'person' under ETA s. 123(1), the LP must hold its own BN and GST/HST account even though income tax flows through to partners; the T5013 partnership information return allocates income/losses to partners under ITA ss. 96-103.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; an LP is compulsorily flow-through (ITA ss. 96-103) without election. Relevant statutory elections and limitations: (i) ITA s. 249.1(4) partnership fiscal-period election (non-calendar year) available where all members are non-residents; (ii) ITA s. 39(2) foreign-currency election to crystallize FX gains/losses at settlement date; (iii) ETA s. 273 joint-venture election for GST/HST grouping of shared Canadian activities; (iv) anti-hybrid rules (ITA s. 18.4 and related provisions) may deny treaty or deduction benefits and require disclosure where a partner jurisdiction classifies the LP differently; (v) Income Tax Conventions Interpretation Act s. 4.2 governs treaty benefit flow-through for non-resident partners of the LP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) measured across the current calendar quarter or the last four consecutive calendar quarters - the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148; crossing the threshold makes GST/HST registration mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 where the Canadian LP supplies an advisory/consulting service to a non-resident recipient and: (i) the service is performed remotely and not rendered to an individual physically present in Canada at the time of supply; (ii) the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada (Canadian real-estate-linked golden-visa services are taxable); and (iii) the service does not relate to tangible personal property situated in Canada. Study-abroad/immigration visa consulting supplied to non-resident clients generally qualifies for 0% GST/HST and permits full input tax credit recovery for Canadian inputs used in the export activity; the zero-rated amount must be reported in the GST/HST return and the substantiation bundle retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) - not required for pure services; relevant only to ancillary physical shipments
  • Signed engagement letter / service contract identifying the non-resident client and the offshore consumption purpose
  • Zero-rated tax invoice bearing the BN and code 'Z' / 2319 with notation 'zero-rated export - ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7'
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank statement evidencing foreign-origin payment and settlement
  • Evidence of non-resident status: certified passport copy, foreign residential address, and tax-residence certificate where obtainable
  • Bill of Lading or courier waybill - retained only if incidental goods are shipped; otherwise marked N/A within the export file

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API stamping mandate exists in Canada. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework (Peppol/PINT-based) is voluntary. Invoices must still contain all prescribed information under ETA ss. 221-223, and books/records must be retained for six years; CRA accepts electronic invoices and records.

CRA may reassess a zero-rating claim within four years, with no limitation period in cases of misrepresentation or fraud (ETA s. 298). Civil penalty for false statements/defaults is the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the excess rebate or credit claimed (ETA s. 281). Criminal exposure under ETA s. 280: fines up to CAD 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to two years on summary conviction, with higher exposure on indictment. Scrutiny concentrates on late foreign settlement (receipts beyond 180 days), missing non-resident evidence, and any Canadian real-property connection; a failed claim is retroactively clawed back with prescribed-rate compound interest, and the partnership-level adjustment flows through the T5013 to each partner.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free floating exchange rate regime with full current account convertibility and substantially unrestricted capital account convertibility for resident and non-resident entities. There are no prior exchange control approvals for profit remittances, dividends, management fees, or capital repatriation. Payments to and from Canada may be made in any currency, and there is no requirement to convert CAD proceeds to other currencies. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to corporate law solvency tests and withholding tax compliance (e.g., Part XIII tax on dividends or interest paid to non-residents). A Limited Partnership with non-resident partners must monitor deemed Canadian-source income and file applicable information returns (NR4, T5013) to ensure remittance channels are tax-compliant.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no required reinvestment timeframes for export receipts in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in offshore or domestic accounts. However, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) requires virtual currency dealers, money service businesses, and certain financial entities to report international electronic transfers (IETs) of CAD 10,000 or more. Additionally, FINTRAC imposes record keeping and client identification obligations that indirectly require internal reconciliation of export receipts; Canadian customs regulations under the Customs Act and the Cross-Border Currency Reporting rules mandate reporting of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more when physically transported across the border.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Department of Finance Canada; the Currency and Foreign Exchange Administration under the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Canadian banking law does not recognize the European Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license. Off-shore digital EMIs operating without a Canadian regulatory footprint cannot provide a Canadian-denominated settlement account or a direct member account at Payments Canada. Such EMIs may partner with a Canadian Schedule I bank as a sponsored agent, but the limited partnership (CA_LP) would remain exposed to parent-institution risk and potential FINTRAC registration obligations as an MSSB (Money Services Business). Overall viability is rated Low-Medium for primary treasury operations — permissible only for low-value pre-funding, not for escrow or immigration visa fee settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Canadian Limited Partnership should originate from the general partner's or subscriber's own bank account using SWIFT MT103 with purpose code OTHR (Other) or GRP (General Transfer). To minimize FINTRAC suspicion, the wire instruction must include the LP's legal name, project reference (ISIC 7810 study-abroad and visa advisory mandates), and the escrow account number. Documentary back-up should include the executed partnership subscription agreement, capital call notice, source-of-funds letter, and CBCA/extract confirming the LP's active status. For legal-capital injections from non-resident partners exceeding CAD 50,000, banks generally demand a copy of the partnership resolution and a certified register of capital contributions. All inbound holdings must be recorded in the LP's capital accounts and used exclusively for evidenced business expenses, marketing fees, visa application processing costs, and disbursements to service providers.Outbound trade settlements for tuition, visa application fees, and overseas partner commissions should use SWIFT MT103 priority for low-value, immediate irrevocable payments. For large disbursements above CAD 250,000 and contractual threshold triggers, a Commercial Letter of Credit (export/import LC) routed through the Schedule I bank's trade finance desk provides documentary safeguards against missing deliverables. Payments must be made only to the stated payee under the underlying contract, and the full audit trail — invoice, contract, proof-of-service, and FX forward confirmation — must be retained for five years in line with PCMLTFA record keeping.Outright FX Forward (vanilla) — executed with a Schedule I Canadian bank or registered derivatives dealer for up to 12 months, with underlying exposure validation.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) — permitted only when the countercurrency lacks deliverability or the Canadian bank requires an offshore branch settlement, subject to OSFI counterparty credit limits and ISDA CSA documentation.Cross-Currency Swap — permissible for identified revenue streams or interest-bearing advances, requiring full ISDA Master Agreement, Credit Support Annex, and daily mark-to-margin posting for uncleared swaps.Plain Vanilla FX Options — allowed for hedging rigid tuition-receivable contracts, although documentation should note a non-speculative business-purpose statement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (aggregate fair market value of all non-arm's length cross-border transactions; CRA administrative threshold requires contemporaneous documentation beyond this)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations; however, GAAR under s.245 of the Income Tax Act and the substance-over-form doctrine will disregard transactions lacking economic reality. For immigration visa consulting, a physical office, active client management, and CICC licensing are mandatory; a shell-like LP with no real activity faces reassessment, penalties, and treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239 of the Income Tax Act: fine from 50% of tax evaded up to 200%, and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalty up to 50%

Director personal liability: Directors and partners jointly and severally liable for the full amount of evaded tax plus interest and penalties; corporate veil pierced permitting recovery against personal assets; imprisonment for principals involved in falsifying records
Shell Company / Sham Transactions

Statutory sanction: CRA may recharacterize the arrangement as a sham or apply GAAR; full reassessment disallowing all claimed losses, allocating income to the true beneficial owner; tax shelter penalties

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners exposed to personal liability for 100% of unreported income tax plus interest; individuals may be sued for unpaid corporate/partnership taxes after dissolution
AML / Counter-Financial-Terrorism Violations

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: fines up to CAD 1,000,000 for administrative violations; money laundering offences up to CAD 1,000,000 and 10 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Officers and directors personally liable for failing to ensure AML record-keeping and reporting compliance; individual fines and prohibition from regulated sectors; travel restrictions pending prosecution
Unlicensed Immigration Consulting

Statutory sanction: Offence under Immigration and Refugee Protection Act and College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants Act: fines up to CAD 100,000 (individual) or CAD 250,000 (corporation) per first offence, up to 2 years imprisonment, and prohibition on practice

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly participating in unlicensed practice liable personally and may be imprisoned; College can issue cease-and-desist orders and publish director names; funds received for unlicensed services may be forfeited under proceeds of crime legislation
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess income by net adjustment and impose a 10% penalty on net adjustment above CAD 5,000; in severe cases taxpayer may be barred from treaty relief and subject to compounded daily interest

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false TP documentation or approved mispriced transactions may be personally assessed under s.227.1 if entity fails to pay, and may be charged with conspiracy to evade tax
