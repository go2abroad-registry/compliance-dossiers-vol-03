Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online (BRO) portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian limited partnership must first complete any required provincial extraprovincial registration in the province where it carries on business. For federal tax purposes, the partnership registers with the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), providing the partnership declaration, general partner details, and non-resident partner information. The CRA will issue a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a Partnership program account (RP). If the partnership makes taxable supplies of design subscription services in Canada, it must obtain a GST/HST program account (RT) by completing the GST/HST registration portion of Form RC1 or using the BRO online portal. Non-resident suppliers of digital services without Canadian presence may use the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration process, supplying foreign business identity, country of residence, and estimated Canadian-source revenue. The invoice-visible tax identification is the GST/HST registration number composed of the 9-digit BN plus the two-digit 'RT' program account code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act. The partnership itself does not pay federal income tax, and all income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to partners, who are taxed at the partner level. Non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source amounts allocated to them through the partnership, subject to applicable income tax treaty relief.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime analogous to the United States. Entity classification is determined by Canadian domestic law and is not elective. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada has enacted mandatory anti-hybrid rules under Income Tax Act sections 18.2, 18.3, and 20.2 that disallow deductions for hybrid mismatch payments and are not subject to election. A Canadian limited partnership may nonetheless raise a treaty-based position with respect to non-resident partners, and in limited cross-border circumstances the CRA may consider mutual agreement procedure or advance income tax rulings to resolve double classification conflicts.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – Under the GST/HST small-supplier rules, registration is mandatory once the supplier's total worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. For non-resident suppliers of digital design services and intangible design subscriptions, registration is required when Canadian-source taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 within any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. Goods physically exported from Canada are zero-rated if documentary proof of export is retained. Services, including UI/UX design subscription services, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person and the service is not rendered in connection with real property situated in Canada nor physically performed in Canada on tangible personal property located in Canada at the time of performance. Supplies of intangible personal property, such as licensed design assets or digital subscription products, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person for use wholly outside Canada. The supplier must maintain contemporaneous commercial and banking records demonstrating the non-resident status of the client and the offshore consumption of the design deliverables.

  • Customs export declaration filed with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) where physical design deliverables or media cross the Canadian border
  • Bill of lading or airway bill covering the physical export movement from Canada to the non-resident client
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident client
  • Executed design subscription agreement, statement of work, and order form clearly identifying the non-resident recipient and confirming offshore delivery
  • Service delivery logs, digital delivery receipts, and server records demonstrating the supply occurred outside Canada where the deliverable is intangible
  • Client non-residency evidence, including tax residence certificate, business registry extract, or equivalent due-diligence documentation

Canada does not require real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval, or digital stamping of invoices for GST/HST purposes. A GST/HST registrant must issue a valid invoice containing the supplier's legal name, business number and GST/HST registration number, invoice date, description of the supply, tax payable or explicit zero-rated indication, and tax amount where applicable. Electronic invoices are legally permissible if they are legible and retainable for CRA audit. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically for most registrants using CRA digital services; however, there is no API-based invoice clearance or mandatory hardware device regime. The CRA is adopting the Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing standard based on Peppol, but participation is voluntary until otherwise mandated by law.

CRA may reassess zero-rated export treatment within the normal four-year reassessment period for ordinary errors, and without statutory time limitation where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful or careless behaviour is established. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include a penalty of 25% of the net tax amount for false statements or omissions (section 280), gross negligence penalties of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the amount (section 285), and separate penalties for failure to file returns. Retrospective denial of zero-rating results in reassessment of GST/HST, disallowance of input tax credits, interest, and possible clawback of refunds paid. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion may be pursued under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and section 239 of the Income Tax Act. Where export consideration is not repatriated within 180 days without a valid economic or legal justification, the CRA may treat the outstanding amount as a shareholder or partnership benefit or apply transfer pricing adjustments under section 247, with corresponding penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating and there are no statutory restrictions on profit remittances, dividend payments, or capital distributions to non-residents. For a Canadian Limited Partnership, goodwill and interest income may be exposed to Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, but no exchange-control approval is required. Corporate partners should structure distributions to minimize non-resident withholding exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirements on export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in any foreign currency, held freely in domestic or offshore bank accounts, and converted at market rates at any time. There is no statutory timeline or minimum conversion ratio; only standard record-keeping obligations apply for tax and money-laundering reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money laundering oversight; no separate foreign-exchange control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility for processing subscription revenues and cross-border disbursements via globally recognized digital payment institutions such as Wise and Stripe. Legal constraint: any entity providing payment services into Canada must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and obtain an MSB license under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. Unregulated offshore EMIs cannot hold CAD clearing accounts or settle through Canada’s large-value transfer system (LVTS).

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No mandatory statutory audit for a limited partnership under Canadian federal or provincial law. An audit is compulsory only if the partnership agreement or a 5%-or-more profit-interest partner requires it, the partnership is a reporting issuer, a lender imposes it, or CRA demands audited financial statements. The CBCA audit threshold for distributing corporations is not directly applicable; typical reporting-issuer thresholds are annual gross revenue above CAD 10 million or total assets above CAD 5 million.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Income Tax Act; fines up to $5 million and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Personal liability for unpaid taxes, director's liability for source deductions, potential criminal charges and imprisonment
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to $10,000 per day under Income Tax Act; possible license revocation

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors personally liable for tax liabilities, fines, and civil restitution
