Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), Customs and Excise Branch (GST/HST) and Income Tax Rulings Directorate; registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-number.html, supplemented by My Business Account (MyBA) and Represent a Client (RAC) portals.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian Limited Partnership must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT) before carrying on marine stevedoring activities in Canada. Procedure: (i) file Form RC1 or use BRO online; (ii) CRA issues the BN after identity verification of the non-resident general partner and the LP; (iii) where the LP has Canadian-source income through a permanent establishment, the partnership files the T5013 Partnership Information Return and the Canadian-resident or non-resident partners are taxed on their allocated share; (iv) if the partnership is deemed a 'person' for GST/HST purposes only, the BN-RT account is registered in the partnership name; and (v) foreign partners without branch operations may alternatively be registered directly as non-resident registrants for GST/HST, subject to the same BN/RC1 process. The tax identity must be established prior to first invoicing of port terminal demurrage, stevedoring fees, or handling charges.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Entity_Level_Taxation (restricted: the LP is fiscally transparent for income tax while it is an independent registrant for GST/HST) / Territorial_Scope (Canadian-resident partners taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners taxed on Canadian-source business and property income through the partnership; branch/permanent establishment filing rules apply to the LP's Canadian stevedoring business) / Tax_Exempt_Zone (none federally; no free-zone income tax holiday applies to marine dock operations within Canada's federal jurisdiction).

Available election forms

Canada does not employ a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; partnership pass-through classification is mandatory for a valid LP. Available statutory elections include: (i) subsection 150(2) of the Income Tax Act for the partnership to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period; (ii) GST/HST quick method election under section 221.1 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) for smaller registrants; (iii) closely-related corporation election under subsection 156(2) ETA for internal stevedoring-related service flows; (iv) section 216 election for non-resident partners where applicable to Canadian income reporting; (v) election to waive the general anti-avoidance limitation on foreign affiliate/partnership hybrid mismatch ordering under the excessive interest and financing expenses limitation (EIFEL) rules in sections 18.2 and 18.3 of the Income Tax Act; and (vi) provincial registration mechanics (e.g., Revenu Québec) remain outside federal mandate but do not alter the federal BN/RT scheme.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies under the small supplier threshold (per tax year and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters); non-residents carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies in Canada must register once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded. Stevedoring services rendered in Canada to non-resident customers are within the taxable supply computation, but suppliers making only zero-rated export supplies may nevertheless apply for voluntary registration to claim input tax credits; no threshold applies to mandatory registration for non-resident suppliers of imported taxable supplies except for specified digital services, which are irrelevant to marine terminal operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a supply of stevedoring services (loading, unloading, and handling of bulk cargo) at a marine dock in Canada where (i) the service is supplied to a non-resident person, and (ii) the goods are exported from Canada as part of a continuous export transaction, so that the service falls within section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The operator must establish contemporaneous evidence that the recipient is a non-resident and the goods physically left Canada; services performed in respect of goods consumed in Canada or supplied to a Canadian-resident customer (e.g., intra-Canadian waterside cargo transfer) are subject to 5% GST. Claiming zero-rating is per-invoice and supported by standard export documentation, with no advance approval required but full audit substantiation mandatory.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (or equivalent electronic declaration via the Canadian Export Reporting System - CERS) evidencing the bulk cargo export
  • Bill of Lading / sea waybill issued by the ocean carrier confirming movement of the bulk cargo from the Canadian marine terminal to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, stevedoring service contract, and vessel-port call manifest linking the service to the exported cargo
  • Proof of settlement from the non-resident customer through SWIFT MT103 / bank advice or port authority clearing records (Canadian practice does not use 'Inward Remittance Certificates'; equivalent evidence of foreign-currency receipt is required for audit purposes)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-approval, or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. Electronic invoicing is voluntary, with CRA-led pilots aligned to the PEPPOL/UBL 2.1 standard and phased B2G adoption for federal procurement; GST/HST registrants with annual revenues above CAD 1M must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; T5013 information returns and export declarations are filed electronically through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). Invoices must contain the mandatory information set in ETA section 3 of Schedule V, but no CRA fiscal code certification or clearance is required.

Canada does not prescribe a statutory 180-day export-proceeds realisation deadline comparable to FEMA-type regimes; the 180-day value is applied in this compliance framework as an evidentiary benchmark for documenting foreign-currency remittance within the ETA six-year record retention period under section 87. CRA audit protocols for marine terminal operators require contemporaneous proof of both export identity and non-resident status; failure to substantiate zero-rating results in reassessment at 5% GST, monetary penalties under subsections 285(1)/(2) ETA (CAD 250 plus up to 6% of the tax evaded), retrospective clawback of input tax credits, and potential seizure of goods while in transit. Fraudulent claims attract criminal liability under subsections 280(1) and 281(1) ETA, with fines up to CAD 10,000 and imprisonment up to two years; the CRA may also disgorge the full tax amount plus arrears interest and assess gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of the understated GST/HST under the comparable Income Tax Act penalty standards. The four-year normal reassessment window is the minimum lookback period, extending indefinitely where misrepresentation is alleged.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized exchange-rate system. There are no exchange-control laws restricting current account payments, capital transfers, or the repatriation of profits by limited partnerships. Non-resident partners are taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on their share of Canadian-source partnership income; no Part XIII non-resident withholding tax is incurred on allocations that are business income, but withholding may apply if the partnership derives passive Canadian-source income such as dividends, interest, or rents. CAD is freely deliverable and can be converted to any major currency at independent market rates. The Bank of Canada may intervene only to smooth excessive volatility and does not impose convertibility approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement for export proceeds in Canada. Stevedoring receipts denominated in foreign currency can be retained in foreign currency accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks or account balances held offshore at the partnership's discretion. For risk management, conversion to CAD is commonly executed via spot FX through the house bank. FINTRAC requires reporting of single or related electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more crossing the border, and reporting of large cash transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, but no conversion ratio or repatriation timeline is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as the supervisory authority for federally regulated deposit-taking institutions. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers cross-border transaction and money laundering reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Canada does not issue an 'EMI licence' distinct from bank or money-services licensing. A foreign digital EMI may provide cross-border payment services only if it registers with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business when it transacts with persons in Canada. Such providers generally cannot hold CAD deposit accounts at Canadian chartered banks and are not eligible for overdraft or treasury sweep products. They remain viable for low-value disbursements, virtual card spending, and currency exchange, but the primary operational account and escrow account must sit with a Schedule I bank for settlement finality and regulatory access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Onshore capital contributions from non-resident partners are transmitted via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the partnership's Capital Escrow Account using purpose code 'G2 – Investment in a Canadian partnership' or 'PROP – Payment of purchase price' as applicable. The bank will rely on the partnership agreement and a capital account statement to validate the funds. Transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are automatically reported by the remitting or receiving bank under FINTRAC's EFT reporting rules. Funds are held in the escrow account until construction milestones and bank-approved disbursement conditions are met.Trade settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 single-payment instructions for foreign subcontractors and suppliers. For purchase of heavy-duty port equipment, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit established at a Schedule I bank is the primary clearing rail, payable upon presentation of bills of lading and inspection certificates. Domestic vendor and payroll payments are cleared using the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day finality, with the Real-Time Rail (RTR) available for smaller instantaneous retail payments. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No federal statutory audit is mandatory for a Canadian limited partnership under the Income Tax Act or provincial Limited Partnerships Acts. An audit becomes compulsory where: (i) the partnership is a 'reporting issuer' under provincial securities law (National Instrument 81-106 requires annual audited financial statements); (ii) the partnership agreement or a lender/investor agreement requires an independent audit; (iii) gross revenue exceeds CAD 2,000,000 and a limited partner holding at least 10% of the capital invokes the statutory account-access right under the applicable provincial Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Ontario s. 13); or (iv) the CRA requires an accountant's audit pursuant to a discretionary reassessment or an industry-specific Schedule 50 compliance order.
