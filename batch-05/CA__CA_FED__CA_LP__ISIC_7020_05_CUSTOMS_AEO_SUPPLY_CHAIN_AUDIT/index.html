Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), portal: canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entities and partnerships must apply for a Business Number (BN) and, where required, a separate GST/HST registration account. The application is filed electronically through CRA My Business Account, by mail using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or for non-residents, via Form RC151 (GST/HST Registration for a Non-Resident). A partnership registers in the partnership's legal name and is issued a distinct BN and GST/HST account number. Voluntary registration is available to non-residents carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Non-resident entities and partnerships must apply for a Business Number (BN) and, where required, a separate GST/HST registration account. The application is filed electronically through CRA My Business Account, by mail using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or for non-residents, via Form RC151 (GST/HST Registration for a Non-Resident). A partnership registers in the partnership's legal name and is issued a distinct BN and GST/HST account number. Voluntary registration is available to non-residents carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election; tax classification follows legal form. For a limited partnership, the Income Tax Act treats the partnership as a flow-through vehicle, with income allocated to partners. Available statutory elections include the GST/HST joint venture election under Section 273 Prescribed Authority of the Excise Tax Act, partnership fiscal-period elections, and certain hybrid-mismatch elections under amended rules for structured financial arrangements. No entity-level classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies over four consecutive calendar quarters, based on the worldwide taxable supply threshold of the registrant and associated persons; voluntary registration is available at or below this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods exported from Canada in accordance with Schedule VI, Parts I and II of the Excise Tax Act, and to services supplied to non-residents if the services are performed wholly outside Canada or meet the use-based criteria (e.g., advisory and consulting services to a non-resident who does not consume the benefit in Canada). Management consulting and supply-chain audit services supplied to non-residents, including AEO security audit deliverables, are zero-rated under the appropriate Schedule VI provisions provided the supplier retains documentation showing the recipient is a non-resident, the service is for use outside Canada, and payment flows from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency G13 or equivalent export reporting where required)
  • Commercial Invoice or Service Agreement
  • Bill of Lading and/or Waybill for goods exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of funds from outside Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for GST/HST invoices. Taxable registrants must issue valid invoices containing prescribed information under Section 169/170 of the Excise Tax Act; electronic issuance is permitted. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants above prescribed thresholds, and platform operators must report non-resident seller information effective 2021. The Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoice network is voluntary and not a statutory clearance mechanism.

CRA auditors scrutinize zero-rating claims, particularly for services to non-residents, and may reassess and claw back zero-rated treatment where documentary evidence is insufficient. Penalties under the Excise Tax Act include: failure to register, failure to collect or remit tax, false statements (penalty equal to 5% of any understated tax plus 25% in case of gross negligence), and restitution of all input tax credits previously claimed. Criminal prosecution may result where fraudulent export claims are made. For AEO supply-chain audits, repeated infrastructure failures may also trigger loss of CBSA Trusted Trader status and increased customs bond requirements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible and freely floating. Canada maintains no restrictions on either current-account or capital-account convertibility: there is no exchange-control approval requirement for foreign direct investment in a Canadian limited partnership, no permit for opening foreign-currency accounts, and no restriction on profit remittance. Repatriation of distributable profits to non-resident limited partners is governed solely by the federal Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1, 5th Supp.): Part XIII non-resident withholding tax applies at the default rate of 25%, reduced by applicable income tax treaties (commonly 0% for arm's-length interest, 10%-15% for dividends, and treaty-exempt for partnership business income not attributable to a Canadian permanent establishment); treaty-based withholding relief is obtained through CRA forms NR301, NR302, or NR303 depending on the character of the payment. Documentary hurdles comprise (i) NR4 statements (Statement of Amounts Paid or Credited to Non-Residents of Canada) plus NR4 Summary filing, (ii) partner-level tax-residency and treaty-eligibility certification, (iii) FINTRAC reporting of any transfer at or above CAD 10,000, and (iv) transfer-pricing documentation for any management-fee or intercompany payment under Income Tax Act section 247. No Canadian governmental consent is required before a non-resident partner injects or withdraws capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory surrender rules: none. Canada imposes no mandatory timeline for repatriating export proceeds and no prescribed percentage of export receipts that must be converted into CAD. A Canadian limited partnership engaged in AEO certification and supply-chain security audit services may retain foreign-currency service revenues in a foreign-currency bank account (FCBA) at a Schedule I bank, may deploy those funds directly to settle foreign trade disbursements, or may convert them at a commercial exchange rate. The only compliance perimeter is FINTRAC: a Large Cash Transaction Report (LCTR) or Large Electronic Funds Transfer Report (LEFTR) must be filed within 15 days for any cash or electronic funds transaction at or above CAD 10,000 (or foreign-currency equivalent); the LP must maintain a five-year record of each cross-border transfer and the underlying commercial invoice or engagement contract evidencing the export of services. The Bank of Canada's balance-of-payments and current-account statistical reporting obligations apply only through the Canadian Business Patterns survey or the International Transactions in Services survey where the enterprise exceeds Statistics Canada thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2); prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision is performed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); no separate foreign-exchange control authority exists because Canada maintains no exchange-control statute.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High operational feasibility for disbursement-side clearing and invoice settlement, but not acceptable as the primary depository for partner capital contributions. Wise (Wise Payments Limited) and comparable cross-border electronic money institutions (Payoneer, Airwallex, etc.) may lawfully service the Canadian LP because the target payment rails expressly include 'Wise'; however, Canadian law requires any entity providing electronic-money transfer, remittance, or foreign-currency payment services to Canadian clients to be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC (unless operating exclusively through a registered Canadian agent), and the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) licensing regime imposes a retail-payment-activity licence requirement on payment service providers, with transitional registration effective 2024 and full compliance expected in 2025-2026. EMIs are not banks under the Bank Act, are not CDIC members, and cannot issue documentary credits such as Commercial Letters of Credit; they are therefore restricted to auxiliary clearing. The compliance-optimal treasury architecture is: Schedule I bank for escrow, core liquidity, and trade finance; fintech rails limited to low-value recurrent disbursements (each transaction capped below CAD 10,000 where commercially feasible to minimize FINTRAC LEFTR triggers); an active FINTRAC MSB registration certificate or confirmation of the EMI's Canadian registration must be retained in the LP's compliance file.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Non-resident limited-partner opacity: provincial registry does not disclose natural-person beneficial owners, and a foreign corporate general partner creates an entity-layering red flag.

Defense evidence: Provide (i) a completed beneficial-ownership register identifying all individuals owning or controlling 10% or more of the limited partnership, (ii) the full Limited Partnership Agreement with the capital-contribution schedule, (iii) notarized Certificate of Incumbency and charter documents of every corporate partner, (iv) source-of-wealth and source-of-funds questionnaires for each ultimate natural person, (v) tax-residency certificates (RC4288 equivalent or foreign counterparts), and (vi) a written economic-substance narrative demonstrating the general partner's place of effective management and the limited partnership's genuine AEO audit activity in Canada.

Root cause: Pass-through distribution concern: bank flags the limited-partnership structure because distributions to non-residents may be mischaracterized as Part XIII-dividend or unreported Canadian-source service fees.

Defense evidence: Deliver (i) the partnership information return (T5013, Statement of Partnership Income, and T5013 Summary) evidencing Canadian-source income allocations, (ii) a draft Part XIII withholding calculation with treaty positions (NR301/NR302/NR303 as applicable), (iii) executed engagement contracts proving arm's-length AEO audit service delivery, and (iv) an undertaking to the bank that all distributions will be released only after the partnership's accountant issues allocation statements and, where applicable, that Regulation 105 withholding on service fees payable to non-resident subcontractors has been satisfied.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: (i) execute a capital-contribution agreement and post a capital-account ledger entry before the wire is released; (ii) inject funds exclusively by SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent bank-to-bank credit transfer), never by cash, cheque, or third-party bearer instruments; (iii) use ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPC' (capital payments) for equity injections, or 'PREA'/'SERV' for advance payments on AEO audit fees; (iv) issue pre-injection notification to both the sending and receiving banks with the LP's exact legal name, account number, Canadian institution number, transit number, and account number (format [Institution][Transit][Account]), beneficiary address, and the compliant 35-character narrative 'LP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION CA_LP AEO AUDIT'; (v) verify the remitter of record against sanctions, PEP, and adverse-media screening before execution; (vi) when the injection exceeds CAD 50,000, deliver the source-of-funds dossier described in the onboarding blueprint so that funds are credited without clearance delay; (vii) retain the MT103 confirmation, any cover-payment details, and the SWIFT reference for FINTRAC's five-year record-keeping requirement. For United States-dollar injections, the receiving bank's USD correspondent (typically a New York clearing bank such as JPMorgan Chase, BNY Mellon, or CIBC's US correspondent) is used with the appropriate ABA routing number; for euro injections, settlement occurs through Target2 via the bank's European correspondent. A segregated Capital Escrow Account at TD or RBC is recommended so that partner capital remains separate from operating cash, accrues interest to the LP, and creates an unimpeachable tax-audit trail for the Canada Revenue Agency and the CRA's AEO-related supply-chain audit documentation.Three-tier clearing architecture: (1) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for all supplier, contractor, tax, and CRA remittances above CAD 10,000, with ISO 20022 structured remittance information; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits governed by ICC UCP 600) issued through BMO or Scotiabank trade-finance desks where the overseas counterparty requires payment against presentation of transport, customs, or inspection documents connected to AEO physical-audit logistics (container examination, bonded-warehouse access, security-audit site visits); standby Letters of Credit under ISP98 may support performance guarantees to customs-authority clients; (3) fintech rail (Wise) for low-value, recurring cross-border invoice payments below CAD 10,000, with the EMI's FINTRAC MSB registration confirmation retained in the compliance file. All outbound disbursements must be allocated a payment purpose code from the ISO 20022 catalogue (e.g., 'SERV' for audit subcontractors, 'INTC' for general-partner management-fee reimbursement, 'TAXS' for GST/HST and payroll remittances, 'SUPP' for trade supplies) and must be supported by an engagement contract or purchase order to satisfy CRA transfer-pricing review under Income Tax Act section 247. Settlement timing: Canadian-dollar domestic wires clear same-day via Payments Canada Lynx (high-value real-time gross settlement system, successor to LVTS); cross-border USD wires settle T+0/T+1 through the correspondent network; EUR wires settle T+1 via Target2. Foreign-exchange conversion for disbursements is executed by the clearing bank at the interbank rate plus a disclosed spread unless the LP establishes a pre-hedged FX line through the bank's treasury desk. Recurring low-value disbursements should be consolidated into one weekly MT103 with an attached remittance schedule to reduce FINTRAC LEFTR filing frequency and bank transaction costs.Deliverable vanilla forward contracts (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, CAD/GBP) with OSFI-regulated Schedule I banks, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or bank-standard FX forward confirmationsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for settlement currencies that are not freely deliverable in Canada (e.g., certain emerging-market currencies), cash-settled in CAD against a pre-agreed fixing sourceCross-currency swaps (FX swaps and principal-exchange swaps) for aligning USD-denominated partner capital contributions with CAD operating liquidity over multi-year AEO contract cyclesPlain-vanilla FX options (call and put on CAD/USD) where premium expenditure is commercially justified and approved by the general partner under the LP's investment-risk mandateNatural hedge instruments comprising foreign-currency bank accounts (FCBA) and dual-currency operational accounts, which are subject to FINTRAC reporting but are not treated as derivative contracts under OSFI conduct rules

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High operational feasibility for disbursement-side clearing and invoice settlement, but not acceptable as the primary depository for partner capital contributions. Wise (Wise Payments Limited) and comparable cross-border electronic money institutions (Payoneer, Airwallex, etc.) may lawfully service the Canadian LP because the target payment rails expressly include 'Wise'; however, Canadian law requires any entity providing electronic-money transfer, remittance, or foreign-currency payment services to Canadian clients to be registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC (unless operating exclusively through a registered Canadian agent), and the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) licensing regime imposes a retail-payment-activity licence requirement on payment service providers, with transitional registration effective 2024 and full compliance expected in 2025-2026. EMIs are not banks under the Bank Act, are not CDIC members, and cannot issue documentary credits such as Commercial Letters of Credit; they are therefore restricted to auxiliary clearing. The compliance-optimal treasury architecture is: Schedule I bank for escrow, core liquidity, and trade finance; fintech rails limited to low-value recurrent disbursements (each transaction capped below CAD 10,000 where commercially feasible to minimize FINTRAC LEFTR triggers); an active FINTRAC MSB registration certificate or confirmation of the EMI's Canadian registration must be retained in the LP's compliance file.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general corporate rate of 15% (statutory 38% plus surtax, less 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction); provincial general rates range from 10.0% to 16.0% depending on permanent establishment. The Canadian limited partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent, so the corporate rate applies at the corporate general partner/limited partner level rather than at the LP level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents; this rate is not directly applicable to LP profit allocations because the LP is fiscally transparent. Distributions from the LP to partners are generally treated as returns of capital or drawings and are tax-free at the partnership level; the LP must withhold 25% Part XIII on amounts of interest, rents, royalties, and management fees paid or credited to non-resident partners.
