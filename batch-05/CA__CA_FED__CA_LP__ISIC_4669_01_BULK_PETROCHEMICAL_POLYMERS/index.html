Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); federal tax services are administered through CRA online services, including My Business Account and Represent a Client. Provincial and territorial tax registrations may also be required.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership requiring Canadian tax accounts should apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) and the relevant program accounts, including an RT GST/HST account, before commencing taxable commercial activity where registration is required. Applications may be made through CRA’s online registration service where eligible or by submitting the applicable non-resident BN and program-account forms to the CRA. Provide legal formation documents, partnership agreement, foreign tax identification details, Canadian business and contact information, identification of partners and authorized representatives, and the effective date and nature of Canadian activities. Obtain provincial registrations separately where required. A BN is an administrative identifier and does not itself determine Canadian tax residence, treaty entitlement, or whether the partnership is carrying on business in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no general U.S.-style check-the-box election for a Canadian limited partnership. A partnership is generally fiscally transparent for Canadian income-tax purposes: it computes income at partnership level and allocates each partner’s share under the Income Tax Act; partners generally report their allocated shares. Classification of a foreign partnership depends on its legal characteristics and applicable Canadian rules and is not freely elected. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or require income inclusions in specified mismatch arrangements; they do not provide a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (excluding specified supplies) in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters for the general small-supplier test. The threshold and registration rules are subject to statutory exceptions; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada may be required to register without relying on the small-supplier threshold. HST rates vary by province.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are generally zero-rated where statutory conditions are met, including the recipient and export circumstances prescribed by the Excise Tax Act. A supplier should retain evidence that the goods were exported and satisfy applicable delivery, purchaser, and timing conditions. Certain services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated, subject to specific statutory exclusions and conditions; non-resident status alone does not establish eligibility. A registered exporter may generally claim input tax credits for eligible GST/HST incurred in making taxable, including zero-rated, supplies.

  • Canada Border Services Agency export declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), where required, or evidence of an applicable reporting exemption
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, sales contract, and packing list identifying the goods, purchaser, destination, and transaction
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, carrier manifest, or other transport and delivery records evidencing export from Canada
  • Customs release, destination-country import, or other third-party evidence of export where available
  • Payment records and foreign-exchange documentation supporting the transaction; an Inward Remittance Certificate is not a general Canadian statutory requirement

Canada has no general federal real-time e-invoice clearance, digital-stamping, or API pre-approval mandate for ordinary GST/HST invoices. Registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information, retain records, file GST/HST returns electronically where required, and comply with CRA record-retention and audit requirements. Provincial rules and customer-specific electronic invoicing requirements may apply separately.

Canada imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated to Canada. CRA may deny zero-rating or input tax credits where the supplier cannot establish statutory eligibility or substantiate the transaction, assess unpaid GST/HST, interest, and applicable penalties, and reassess within the applicable limitation period, subject to statutory exceptions such as misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. False statements or omissions may attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act; knowing tax evasion may result in prosecution and fines or imprisonment. Export documentation and supporting records should be retained for the statutory period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible under IMF Article VIII. Canada imposes no statutory current-account or capital-account convertibility restriction, and profit remittance or distribution remittance by a Canadian limited partnership to non-resident limited partners requires no exchange-control approval. The only relevant regulatory constraints are: (1) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) on distributions of Canadian-source income to non-resident partners; (2) FINTRAC electronic fund transfer reporting and AML-sanctions screening by the remitting bank; and (3) Canada's international sanctions regime, administered by Global Affairs Canada, blocking certain jurisdictions and prohibited counterparties from receiving CAD-denominated funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation requirement for export proceeds. A Canadian export-origin limited partnership may retain USD, EUR, or other foreign currency export proceeds indefinitely in a Canadian or offshore account. The compliance obligations are limited to: (a) CBSA export declarations when goods are controlled under the Export Control List or exceed prescribed thresholds under the Customs Act; (b) FINTRAC large-cash-transaction and electronic-funds-transfer reports for the CAD 10,000 threshold; and (c) record-keeping of the export sale, bill of lading, and commercial invoice sufficient to support the financial institution's source-of-wealth review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration in the Canadian federal jurisdiction is functionally coordinated by the Bank of Canada, the Department of Finance Canada, and, for compliance/reporting, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada does not operate a dedicated exchange-control authority equivalent to a classic foreign exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) can supply ancillary multi-currency payment rails and virtual account arrangements, but they are not chartered Canadian banks and cannot lawfully open primary Canadian deposit or escrow accounts without becoming subject to Canadian MSB registration and provincial money-services licensing if they carry on business in Canada. For a wholesale petrochemical-distribution limited partnership, an offshore EMI is not acceptable as the principal treasury or escrow provider; documentary letters of credit and confirmed settlement require a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank. An EMI may be used only for low-value collection or payable-cycle optimization after full FINTRAC/PB the PCMLTFA compliance verification and with contractual assurance that no client capital is held unlicensed in Canada.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's regulated bank to the Canadian LP's settlement account at a Schedule I chartered bank. The compliant routing protocol requires: (a) beneficiary name exactly matching the LP's registered name and account ownership; (b) beneficiary bank identifier with Canadian CNAPS/SWIFT BIC; (c) a clean and structured field 70 remittance narrative, e.g., 'INVESTMENT CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — [LP DATE] — [SUBSCRIBER NAME] — [PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT NO.]'; and (d) pre-transmission KYC completion in the receiving bank, including subscription agreement, partnership agreement, source-of-wealth materials, and tax-residency forms. Funds clear in CAD through Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS); USD contributions clear via US domestic correspondent clearing (usually through New York) and are converted to CAD only when required for the escrow account. The sending bank must confirm that the remittance is not sourced from a sanctioned jurisdiction, is not structured to avoid FINTRAC transaction reporting, and is supported by a board or general-partner resolution approving the capital call.Trade settlement for bulk polyethylene and polypropylene granules should be executed through irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued by the Canadian LP's bank via SWIFT MT700. The LC must be confirmed by the beneficiary's bank in the seller's jurisdiction, typically using sight-payment or usance tenors matched to shipping documents (bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of analysis, and weight certificate). Final settlement is completed through SWIFT MT103/MT202 instructions, or, for Canadian-dollar domestic settlement, through LVTS with final and irrevocable payment. Where the seller demands open-account settlement, the LP should use a standby letter of credit or trade credit insurance; however, the target rails selected for this transaction are Swift_LC and Irrevocable_LC, and these rails should govern all major supplier payments. Each shipment must be separately financed to preserve the LC's independence and documentary-compliance principle; no set-off, deduction, or netting should be introduced outside an express amendment.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards in USD/CAD and EUR/CAD matched to commercial trade settlement datesFX options (European-style) for asymmetric protection against adverse USD/CAD and EUR/CAD movesCross-currency swaps converting USD-denominated polymer payables/receivables into CAD floating or fixed exposureNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on currencies with limited convertibility, executed through a Canadian bank or a regulated non-resident bank for USD/restricted-currency exposureFX swaps (swap-by-sale) executed with the treasury desk of the Canadian clearing bank for short-term liquidity rebalancing between receipt legs and disbursement legs
