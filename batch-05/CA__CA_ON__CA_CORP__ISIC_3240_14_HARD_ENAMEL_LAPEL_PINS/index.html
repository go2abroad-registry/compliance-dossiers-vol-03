Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Corporate Tax and GST/HST administration for Ontario, under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement with the Ontario Ministry of Finance. Primary digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and GST/HST NETFILE.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a standard Canadian business corporation obtain either federal articles of incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or provincial articles under the Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA). Following incorporation, the corporation applies to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts: 'RC' for federal corporate income tax and 'RT' for GST/HST. The standard filing instrument is CRA Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts'; alternatively the TIN is auto-registered through the incorporation filing process when the articles are submitted to Corporations Canada or the Ontario Business Registry. A non-resident corporation without a Canadian charter may separately register under RC1 in its own name and must designate a Canadian agent or service address where required. The corporate BN with 'RT' program account constitutes the statutory tax identifier for GST/HST invoicing and customs export records.

The entity is not transparent; shareholders are taxed separately on dividends. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A CBCA or OBCA business corporation is a resident taxpayer under section 250 of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)). It computes federal corporate taxable income at the general or small-business rate and is subject to Ontario provincial corporate income tax. The entity is not transparent; shareholders are taxed separately on dividends. No flow-through or partnership status arises for this corporate vehicle.

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. The standard corporation is mandatorily classified as a separate taxable entity; hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act do not create a classification election. For GST/HST, a small supplier can elect under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act to voluntarily register before exceeding the CAD 30,000 threshold; such voluntary registration is irrevocable for at least one year. Affiliated or closely related corporations may elect a GST/HST group return under sections 239 to 240.1 of the Excise Tax Act, combining balances for filing purposes without eliminating the separate-entity tax identity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000. A supplier whose total annual worldwide gross revenues from taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies and supplies of financial services, but excluding capital sales of real property) exceeds CAD 30,000 over the current or any preceding four consecutive calendar quarters must register for GST/HST. For non-resident vendors, the same threshold applies where the non-resident is engaged in a 'commercial activity' in Canada; a non-resident not carrying on business in Canada may nevertheless voluntarily register and charge GST/HST on specified supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered exporter of hard enamel lapel pins (classified under ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339999) may apply the zero rate under section 1 of Part V, Schedule VI, of the Excise Tax Act when the tangible personal property supplied by the vendor is exported by the vendor, or is delivered to a common carrier, freight forwarder, or registered exporter for direct shipment to an address outside Canada, as part of the supply. The export supplies are taxable at 0%, enabling the vendor to claim input tax credits for related domestic inputs. Services performed for a non-resident client may also qualify under section 7, Part V, Schedule VI if the services are not rendered substantially in Canada and are not in connection with land or goods situated in Canada. All claims require continuous documentary proof of actual physical export in a commercial context, and the vendor must make reasonable efforts to retain evidence that the goods left Canada and were not re-supplied domestically.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA B13A form or electronic export report via the Canadian Export Reporting System (ERS), required for shipments equal to or exceeding CAD 2,000 in value or as otherwise prescribed
  • Commercial invoice signed by the vendor and purchaser, specifying the exporter, consignee, destination outside Canada, Incoterms rule, unit value, and full description of the hard enamel lapel pins
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill issued by the carrier or sea carrier, showing the consignee as a foreign party and the foreign port/airport destination
  • Proof of foreign remittance or inward payment, including SWIFT MT103, Etsy Direct Pay settlement statement, Stripe payout voucher, or other bank/acquiring documentation evidencing payment received from a non-resident in a foreign currency or into the exporter's Canadian account
  • Foreign customs import documentation, foreign delivery confirmation, or signed proof of delivery issued at the destination, demonstrating that the goods physically arrived in the export jurisdiction

No national real-time fiscal clearance system, continuous transaction control, API pre-clearance mandate, or digital stamping regime is in force in Canada. GST/HST invoices need not be approved by the CRA before issuance. A registrant must issue a 'tax invoice' or 'sales invoice' under section 221 of the Excise Tax Act and the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations, containing the vendor's business number, invoice date, name, description, consideration, and either the GST/HST charged or a clear statement that the supply is zero-rated/exempt. Electronic records are acceptable if legible and machine-readable, and must be retained for at least six years. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically by businesses whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1 million (GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST EDI); all other registrants may file by mail. Ordinary sales invoices are not separately stamped by the CRA and there is no pre-shipment fiscal authorization step.

The CRA will scrutinize zero-rated export claims for evidentiary completeness. If the required documentation is absent, the supply is reclassified as taxable at the Ontario HST rate of 13%, the exporter's input tax credits associated with the export are disallowed, and the resulting net tax is assessed against the registrant with arrears interest at the prescribed CRA rate. The CRA's normal four-year reassessment window is extended where a false statement or misrepresentation was made knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence, enabling retrospective clawback beyond the normal limitation period. Gross negligence penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act can reach 25% of any overstated credit or refund, with a minimum penalty of CAD 100. Criminal prosecution is possible under section 326 of the Excise Tax Act for intentionally evading GST/HST, with fines of up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to two years on summary conviction, or heavier penalties on indictment. Further, while the Excise Tax Act does not codify a fixed export-proceeds repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark for foreign currency settlement operates as a practical documentary control point; an unpaid receivable exceeding 180 days may be challenged as evidence that no true cross-border sale occurred, and the CRA may also invoke transfer-pricing or general anti-avoidance rules where the exporter is related to the foreign purchaser.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no advance approval, licensing, or repatriation-timing restrictions on capital injections, shareholder loans, trade payables, or dividend remittances. Non-resident profit repatriation is unrestricted, subject only to Part XIII federal withholding tax: 25% general statutory rate, treaty-reduced (e.g., 15% arm's-length US shareholders or 5% for a US corporate beneficial owner of 10%+ voting shares under the Canada-US Tax Treaty; similar 5-15% reductions under UK, EU and Asia-Pacific treaties). Arm's-length interest on trade debt is generally free of Part XIII withholding (2008 onward). Practical limits arise solely from OFSI and UN sanctions (Special Economic Measures Act, United Nations Act) and enhanced beneficial-ownership transparency rules under the CBCA/OBCA.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds. Hard enamel lapel pin revenues may be retained indefinitely in USD, CAD, or any freely convertible currency in domestic or foreign accounts. The exporter's only mandatory duties are (i) GST/HST zero-rating documentation for exports (CRA evidence retention), (ii) accurate T2 Canadian corporate income tax reporting with eligible mark-to-market or 2021 revised S.95 rules for foreign currency, and (iii) FINTRAC obligation to report large cash transactions above CAD 10,000 and 24-hour international electronic funds transfers where triggered. Canadian-controlled goods under the Export and Import Permits Act do not require permits for non-strategic decal/novelty merchandise.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — monetary authority; exchange-rate policy is administered jointly by the Department of Finance Canada and the Bank of Canada; prudential supervision of banks is delegated to OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions); AML/ATF supervision and reporting is enforced by FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada); physical currency movement is controlled by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Canada has operated under a free-floating foreign exchange regime since the Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Conditionally viable as complementary settlement rails, not as primary treasury accounts. Wise (TradeWise) and Payoneer, both registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, provide CAD/USD virtual account details and marketplace payout aggregation for Etsy and Stripe, reducing FX spread and allowing direct USD-to-CAD conversion at transparent rates. Balances held with such EMIs are not CDIC-insured and are not 'deposits' under the Bank Act; material corporate balances above CAD 100,000 should remain at a Schedule I bank. Stripe Treasury is not available in Canada as a standalone regulated bank (it relies on US partner banks); using it for payroll/HST would create OSFI licensing ambiguity. Recommended architecture: Schedule I operating account as treasury hub; Wise or Payoneer as a concentration/convert shield for Etsy USD receivables.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's home country bank into the corporate USD or CAD account at the Canadian Schedule I bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general corporate rate (Federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%); 12.2% combined rate for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income under the Federal Small Business Deduction (9%) and Ontario Small Business Deduction (3.2%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII dividend withholding tax on all dividends (including deemed dividends) paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders on the gross dividend amount; Canada's DTA with the United States commonly reduces the rate to 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% for beneficial-owner shareholders controlling at least 10% of the voting stock; non-treaty shareholders remain pinned at 25% (subject to income tax treaty or DTA override, never below the treaty's base rate).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of all reportable transactions between the Ontario corporation and each non-arm's length non-resident person during the taxation year triggers the mandatory documentation duty under ITA s.247(4) and the T106 information return filing obligation; formal OECD-aligned Master File / Local File documentation is demanded by CRA's Transfer Pricing Memorandum (TPM-08) and penalty legislation requires contemporaneous documentation proving arm's-length pricing on the date of filing.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no statutory 'Economic Substance Regulations' akin to offshore finance centres; however, the GAAR under s.245(2) allows the CRA to deny any tax benefit arising from an avoidance transaction lacking a genuine economic or business purpose. The 'central management and control' test determines corporate residence, so a mailed-in shell with no Canadian directors, no board meetings in Ontario, and no substantive pin-manufacturing or sales operations will be treated as a non-resident corporation, stripping the small business deduction, denying SR&ED credits, and potentially re-characterizing dividend repatriation as fully taxable branch income. Etsy/Stripe platform sales made through a bare nominee are similarly disregarded under agency and sham doctrine, and the CRA's GAAR purpose test applies to transactions whose sole object is avoiding Part XIII WHT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239(1), evasion is punishable by a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years on indictment (or 2 years on summary conviction); civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the understated tax; CRA may levy non-compliant penalties on the unreported balance with 7% daily-compounding interest; Ontario provincial penalties mirror the federal amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or caused false returns are personally liable for fines and are subject to prosecution as parties under s.239(1); fraudulent intent exposes individual directors to up to 5 years of incarceration, director-level restitution orders, and the Crown may freeze personal assets via proceeds-of-crime forfeiture; leaving Canada to evade collection can trigger a court order under the Income Tax Act's departure-restriction regime (passport surrender and travel ban).
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) permits the CRA to adjust the transaction to the arm's-length amount; s.247(3) imposes a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment where the upward adjustment exceeds CAD 1,000,000; a grossly negligent documentation gap attracts the 50% gross negligence penalty plus interest compounded daily; T106 non-filing triggers a separate fine of CAD 100 per day up to CAD 10,000 per return.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or directed the non-arm's-length pricing scheme, or who suppressed the T106, can be assessed jointly with the corporation for the 10% transfer-pricing penalty as 'representatives' of the corporation's wilful default; if the mispricing constituted fraud, directors face criminal tax-evasion charges (s.239(1)) and are barred from serving as officers of any CBCA- or OBCA-incorporated entity in Ontario during the prosecution.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under GAAR s.245(2), the CRA will re-determine the tax consequences so the corporate shell is taxed as a non-resident branch, clawing back the small business deduction, SR&ED ITCs, and any tax-deferred rollover benefits; GST/HST registrations may be cancelled retroactively for lack of a permanent establishment, and provincial penalties under Ontario's Tax Recovery and Enforcement Act may be imposed for failure to comply with Ontario assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who allowed the shell to continue reporting false status face personal penalties under CBCA s.118 / OBCA s.248 for insolvency-related filings and, more critically, can be held 100% liable for corporation tax debts under ITA s.227.1 where unremitted source deductions or Part XIII amounts were directed to the shell's treasury; departure bans are imposable where the director is non-resident and found to have stripped assets from Canada.
Profit Repatriation / Dividend WHT Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Part XIII s.214(3)(a) deems such payments to be dividends, subject to the full 25% WHT plus daily interest; the Canadian payor corporation is liable for the unwithheld amount (s.227(8)) and loses the deduction for the disguised payment; abuse penalties of up to 200% of the evaded WHT apply under s.163(2) if the mischaracterization was grossly negligent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed remittance forms without the 25% withholding are each personally liable under ITA s.227.1 for the unremitted Part XIII amount; a civil action by the CRA can pierce the corporate veil where the directors used the corporation as a mere conduit for diverting profits personally; criminal exposure includes 5 years' imprisonment for the underlying dividend-stripping fraud.
AML / Unregistered MSB and FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) of up to CAD 500,000 for an individual and CAD 2,000,000 for a corporate entity per violation; total AMPs can exceed CAD 10 million for repeat systemic non-compliance; parallel Criminal Code s.462.31 money-laundering charges carry imprisonment up to 10 years; FINTRAC registration today is mandatory for all businesses accepting e-commerce payments in Canada.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecutable for money laundering (Criminal Code s.462.31) and for consenting to FINTRAC-required reporting failures; conviction can lead to incarceration, travel bans disqualifying cross-border trade, asset freezes under the Criminal Code forfeiture regime, and a permanent record that prevents the director from acting as a corporate director under the OBCA for 5 years.
Non-Resident Director/Owner Governance Defect

Statutory sanction: Ontario Ministry of Government and Consumer Services may strike the corporation from the register; the entity loses its OBCA powers until reinstated; the CRA will treat the entity as being managed and controlled from abroad (deemed non-resident), rendering every T2 filing invalid from inception, and can reassess the full window of tax years with interest and penalties; an Ontario court may dissolve the company if three or more years of non-compliance affect third-party credit.

Director personal liability: Directors who allowed the invalid governance posture to persist are jointly liable for up to CAD 2,000 per employee of unpaid Ontario wages under the OBCA wage-liability rule, for employee source deductions under s.227.1, and for corporate debts if the corporation acted with no lawful de facto board; foreign directors who exercise control from within Canada risk criminal prosecution for aiding an unlicensed corporation.
