Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portals

E-government filing portal

Foreign limited partnerships must apply for a federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 or CRA’s Business Registration Online service. The BN serves as the GST/HST registration identifier and is required for any partnership carrying on business in Canada. Registration is mandatory for non-resident LPs whose taxable supplies in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold; voluntary registration is available. Applicants must submit the partnership agreement, foreign registration documents, and a Canadian contact/representative address where applicable. The CRA issues separate program accounts (e.g., RT for GST/HST) under the same BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian federal tax law has no elective check-the-box regime. A limited partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent under subsection 96(1) of the Income Tax Act. Available elections are limited and transaction-specific: section 97(2) of the Income Tax Act allows a tax-deferred transfer of partnership property to a corporation; certain GST/HST elections under the Excise Tax Act permit a partnership to be treated as a separate person for registration and filing simplification. No hybrid mismatch election mechanism exists; Canada’s anti-hybrid rules apply to prevent elective double-dip tax outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies? Effective threshold: CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold; non-residents carrying on business in Canada must register once exceeded)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Canada are zero-rated under Part V, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act provided the supplier exports them in accordance with required documentation. Services supplied to non-resident persons are generally zero-rated under section 7 of Part V, Schedule VI, unless a statutory exception applies (e.g., services relating to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada). B2B enterprise SaaS delivered electronically to non-resident customers qualifies for zero-rating when the recipient is a non-resident, the service is not performed in Canada for the benefit of a Canadian resident, and the place-of-supply rules in Schedule IX confirm the supply is made in Canada. The supplier must retain evidence establishing the non-resident status of the customer and the offshore supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / Canada Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Foreign remittance / SWIFT record evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident customer

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing stamping, or API clearance mandate exists for GST/HST. Taxpayers must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or through a registered third‑party service provider. Invoices must meet CRA content requirements under section 169/221 of the Excise Tax Act, including supplier name, BN, recipient name, description, and GST/HST amount. PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is voluntary and used mainly for public procurement, not tax clearance.

CRA audits export zero-rating claims, especially for digital services, by validating the non-resident status of the recipient and the existence of genuine foreign proceeds. False or unsupported zero-rating claims may be reassessed within four years (or longer for misrepresentation), with full GST/HST payable plus interest and arrears penalties. Civil penalties under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act expose claimants to a penalty equal to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus up to CAD 5,000 for knowingly making false statements. Criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act may result in fines of up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment for up to 18 months on summary conviction, or substantially greater penalties upon indictment. Claims are clawed back, input tax credits are denied, and the exporter bears the burden of proof.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is a full Article VIII member of the IMF and maintains a freely floating, fully convertible currency. Both current-account (subscription, license and service fees) and capital-account (equity contributions, LP distributions, M&A payments) transactions are unrestricted; the CAD has no surrender, licensing or conversion-margin regime. The only practical remittance hurdle is the Part XIII / Part XII.2 Income Tax Act withholding framework on non-resident partners of the Limited Partnership (standard 25% treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% to US LPs) and the applicable Investment Canada Act notification triggers for acquisition of control of a Canadian business; these are tax and informational constraints, not FX impediments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada repealed the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951 and there is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio and no central-bank repatriation requirement for export proceeds. B2B SaaS subscription receipts and service export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, CAD or any other currency, subject only to routine BOP/MSB transaction reporting by banks to Statistics Canada and the Bank of Canada on monthly international wire activity above notification thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - the sole authority for the issuance of Canadian dollars, monetary policy and financial system oversight. Foreign exchange administration is jointly performed with the Department of Finance, which controls the Exchange Fund Account; OSFI supervises chartered banks and FINTRAC administers AML/KYC reporting. Canada maintains no reserved FX control office or centralised permits bureau.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-High as a supplementary FX/payout rail; Low as sole banking counterparty. Canada has no 'EMI' licence class; the closest equivalent is the Money Services Business (MSB) or Foreign MSB registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act obligations administered by FINTRAC. Wise (Wise Payments Canada Inc.), Stripe Billing and similar entities may open agent or sub-account structures and facilitate cross-border transfers, but they are not Schedule I banks, cannot book deposits as a business under Section 573 of the Bank Act, and the operating account itself must remain at a chartered bank. Legal constraints: MSBs must register with FINTRAC, maintain a compliance officer and 5-year record-keeping, and are prohibited from accepting deposits repayable on demand. Recommended architecture: chartered bank holds operating USD/CAD balances; Wise B2B and Stripe Billing act as disbursement and FX execution rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital inflows are settled via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's bank to the LP's CAD or USD account under the three-part Canadian bank identifier (institution 3 digits, transit 5 digits, account). No advance FX licence or Bank of Canada permit is required. The remitter references the BOP income/equity type code corresponding to partnership direct investment (class 'equity investment' in a Canadian limited partnership) and the narrative field 'Capital Contribution - LP Interest - ISIC 6201 Enterprise SaaS'. For settlement risk reduction, worldwide payments are drawn through correspondent concentrations (e.g., New York USD clearing or Frankfurt EUR clearing). The bank will file the cross-border BOP return covering the transaction; the LP should preserve the GP contribution resolution, the LP agreement admission pages, and the source-of-funds statement to answer potential Investment Canada Act notification tests for non-resident control and Part XII.2 deemed-income computations.For a B2B SaaS exporter, disbursements separate into four rails: (1) domestic CAD and intra-province obligations via Payments Canada ACSS/EFT batch and, above CAD 50,000, via Lynx same-day finality; (2) USD and cross-border vendor settlements via SWIFT MT103 through the bank's correspondent hub, accompanied by the ISO 20022 payment code 'TRAE' for software licensing; (3) high-volume, lower-value FX vendor payments optimised to execute via Wise B2B or Stripe Connect under the bank's foreign-currency disbursement instruction, each settled back to the operating account; and (4) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) and standby LCs (MT760) are generally unnecessary for software-procurement contracts but remain permissible for capitalised hardware co-location agreements; enterprise SaaS vendors ordinarily settle against capital account transfers under MT103 rather than trade documents.Outright deliverable CAD/USD FX forwards - liquid 1-12 months, standard 2-year OTC quotes usable against the LP's banking line.CAD/USD cross-currency swaps - accommodating amortising principal, with 3-year, 5-year and 7-year tenors priced against fixed-rate hedges.Contingent vanilla FX options (call spreads and collars) - permissible for purchased options; banks typically require a written hedging policy.FX swaps on rollover maturities - operational pre-approved within the bank's FX master agreement (ISDA or Canadian Derivatives Agreement).NDFs for CAD are not conventionally available in North American liquidity pools because the CAD is fully delivered and convertible; the bank will quote deliverable forwards only.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal corporate income tax rate of 15% on general taxable income (after the 38% basic rate, 10% provincial abatement, and 13% general rate reduction); reduced to 9% on active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) up to CAD 500,000. A limited partnership (LP) is fiscally transparent and is not subject to entity-level corporate income tax; its income/losses are allocated to partners who are subject to tax at their applicable corporate or personal rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Canada's statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-resident shareholders is 25% (Income Tax Act subsection 212(2)). An LP is a partnership, not a corporation, so distributions of partnership profits are not subject to Part XIII dividend withholding; non-resident partners instead pay Part I tax on their allocated Canadian-source income, while certain passive payments such as interest and royalties distributed through the partnership may be subject to Part XIII withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the day that is five months after the end of the partnership's fiscal period. A partnership with no Canadian-resident partner and no Canadian business activity has no T5013 filing obligation, but withholding tax obligations on Canadian-source payments may still apply. Corporate partners must file their own T2 within six months after their fiscal year-end; balance-due dates are generally two months after year-end (three months for an eligible CCPC).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (the filing threshold for the T106 Information Return for non-arm's length transactions with non-residents; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation should be prepared for any transaction exceeding this threshold and retained for the CRA's review).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime, but economic substance is embedded in the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245 of the Income Tax Act) and in partnership jurisprudence. A limited partnership must demonstrate real business activity and commercial purpose to retain flow-through treatment; if the partnership lacks genuine substance, the CRA can recharacterize it as a deemed corporation, deny treaty benefits, or disregard transactions that lack a bona fide purpose. For cross-border situations, Canada applies the OECD's principal purpose test under the MLI to restrict treaty abuse, and transfer pricing adjustments may be made under section 247 if intercompany transactions do not reflect economic reality.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Misreporting of Revenue or Income

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 239(1) of the Income Tax Act, on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. The CRA may also reassess beyond the normal reassessment period and prohibit discharge from tax debt.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed, authorized, assented to, or acquiesced in the evasion may be personally liable for penalties and unremitted amounts under subsection 227.1(1). The general partner of an LP is jointly and severally liable for the partnership's liabilities, and the corporate veil may be pierced in cases of fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation.
Shell Company / General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply GAAR to deny the tax benefit, reassess the taxpayer, impose interest, and impose a GAAR penalty of 50% of the tax benefit if the misstatement is attributable to a misrepresentation made knowingly or under gross negligence (subsection 245(4)). Recharacterized income is subject to additional Part I tax and back interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax and penalties if they signed false documents or failed to remit required taxes. In serious avoidance cases, the CRA may issue third-party demands and seek director liability under section 227.1, and criminal fraud charges may ground travel bans and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Violations

Statutory sanction: The CRA may adjust the transaction price under section 247 and impose a penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment if the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to determine an arm's length transfer price and failed to provide contemporaneous documentation within 90 days of a request. The adjustment is added to income with compound interest.

Director personal liability: Officers who signed incomplete or false T106 returns may be liable under the Income Tax Act for penalties and, where withholding is involved, under subsection 227.1, which places personal liability on directors for unremitted amounts. Civil restitution may be claimed by the CRA, and egregious cases may result in prosecution of the directing mind.
Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime (AML)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 500,000 (up to CAD 1,000,000 for repeat violations) plus potential criminal fines and imprisonment under the Criminal Code for money laundering. FINTRAC may publicly identify non-compliant entities and revoke their registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally subject to fines and imprisonment for money laundering offenses. The criminal courts may also order forfeiture of the software company's profits, and the corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as an instrument of crime.
Failure to Register for GST/HST

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of unremitted GST/HST, plus interest; repeated failures may result in penalties of 6% per year on overdue net tax and criminal penalties for intentional evasion under the Excise Tax Act. The CRA may waive the small supplier threshold if the entity is part of a commercial enterprise.

Director personal liability: Directors of corporations are liable for unremitted GST/HST under section 323 of the Excise Tax Act if they were not duly diligent in preventing the failure; the same diligence standard applies to general partners of an LP. Personal liability includes civil restitution and, in fraud cases, travel bans and imprisonment.
