Available election forms

Canada does not offer a check-the-box election. Available mechanisms include: (i) Income Tax Act subsection 216(1) net-basis election (Form T1159) for non-residents earning rental/property income; (ii) Income Tax Act section 217 election for eligible income of non-residents; (iii) branch tax deferral/mitigation elections under subsection 219(2.1); (iv) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST74) for registrants with taxable supplies up to CAD $400,000; (v) annual GST/HST filing election (Form GST11); and (vi) hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed automatically under ss. 18.4–18.6 with no elective mechanism. Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 — the small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148; registration becomes mandatory the first day taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed CAD $30,000 over any four consecutive calendar quarters, measured on a Canadian taxable-supply basis for non-residents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of manufactured goods (UHT oat milk packaging) are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V when: (i) the supplier sells the goods and transfers possession to the purchaser; (ii) the goods are exported in commercial quantities by the supplier or delivered to a commercial/common carrier bound for a destination outside Canada; and (iii) conclusive documentary proof of export is obtained within 60 days of shipment and retained for six years. Additional zero-rating applies to services rendered on movable property destined for export (Schedule VI Part V s. 4). The domestic 'basic grocery' classification (Schedule VI Part I) does not apply to export supplies; export zero-rating follows the physical movement of goods out of Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A / e-manifest Canada Customs Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (ocean/air waybill issued as proof of export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 / forex confirmation evidencing repatriated export proceeds)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with Incoterms 2020
  • Carrier's signed export certification and CRA Form GST66 evidence package where requested

No real-time CTC fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary, though CRA supports a Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing framework (2024) for interoperable B2B exchange. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST returns via CRA GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account, EDI, or API once registration is in force; registrants generally must file electronically depending on reporting period and prescribed thresholds.

The CRA may undertake export-verification audits testing the existence, movement, and destination of goods and the receipt of foreign currency proceeds. Failure to substantiate zero-rating results in reassessment of 13% HST on the full consideration, with daily compound arrears interest. Penalties include: false statement/omission penalties (ETA s. 280, greater of CAD $100 or 25% of the net tax); gross negligence penalties of 25% of the net tax/refund claimed (ETA s. 285.1); and criminal prosecution for evasion. The normal reassessment period is four years after the return is filed, but extends to seven years or beyond in the presence of misrepresentation, fraud, or careless neglect; and exporters must retain export documentation for six years for verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with a free-floating currency and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. There are no administrative hurdles, pre-clearance permits, or waiting periods for profit repatriation, dividend remittance, interest/royalty payments, or liquidation proceeds; repatriation is governed exclusively by tax and corporate-law constraints. Dividends paid by an Ontario corporation to non-resident shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25%, reduced by treaty (typically 5% for 10%+ corporate shareholders and 15% otherwise under the Canada-US Tax Convention; analogous rates apply under Canada-EU and other income tax treaties). CBCA solvency requirements must be satisfied before any declaration of dividends. Intercompany interest, royalties, and management fees are deductible and remittable only to the extent arm's length under Section 247, and interest deductions are limited by the thin-capitalization rules (Section 18(4)) where shareholder debt from specified non-resident shareholders exceeds a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio. Inbound FDI is subject to Investment Canada Act notification for the establishment of a new Canadian business; review thresholds do not apply to a WTO-investor establishing a new non-cultural business below the indexed enterprise-value threshold (CAD 1,586M for 2024).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Mandatory foreign-exchange surrender and export-proceeds repatriation time limits were abolished in 1951; Canadian exporters are under no statutory obligation to convert, repatriate, or deposit export receipts within any fixed timeline. The corporate may freely hold USD, EUR, or CAD denominated accounts onshore or offshore, may maintain multi-currency operating and nostro accounts at Schedule I banks, and may net or sweep export proceeds across subsidiaries without Bank of Canada or FINTRAC pre-authorization. The only applicable compliance obligation is FINTRAC international EFT reporting by the bank (not the exporter) for wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation and supervision of deposit-taking institutions is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision of banks, money service businesses, and payment service providers is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Minister of Finance retains residual authority over monetary, foreign-exchange, and investment review policy; however, no Canadian authority operates a foreign-exchange licensing or capital-approval desk analogous to strict-exchange-control jurisdictions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM as an ancillary rail for supplier payouts, payroll, and low-value FX conversion; LOW as the primary treasury, deposit-concentration, or trade-finance rail. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs and non-bank payment service providers are not authorized to take deposits under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and cannot offer true commercial bank or capital-escrow accounts. In Ontario they must be registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC; their fund-pooling, custodial, and settlement structures are subject to significantly higher counter-party and continuity risk, balances are not CDIC-insured, and such firms cannot issue or confirm Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) or provide import LC facilities. Therefore, all capital escrow balances, LC cash coverage, and primary liquidity for the UHT oat-milk project must reside with a Schedule I Canadian bank; EMIs may be used only for small ticket, non-critical outbound disbursements where the bank account remains the settlement source of funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Preferred route: foreign sponsor bank SWIFT MT103 wire to the Ontario Schedule I operating account, instructed by the non-resident parent's own treasury account. Because Canada does not prescribe numeric FX purpose codes, the MT103 Field 70 narrative must state an explicit purpose such as 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - UHT PLANT OAT MILK MANUFACTURING EQUIPMENT' or 'NON-RESIDENT SHAREHOLDER LOAN - PROJECT CAPEX - OPEN ACCOUNT'; the bank's compliance officer will map this narrative to the internal BOP category for FINTRAC international EFT reporting at the CAD 10,000/24-hour threshold, and CBSA? no - and to FINTRAC records. For equity injections, corporate records must evidence share issuance under the CBCA and a directors' resolution authorizing the allotment; for shareholder loans, a promissory note or loan agreement fixing arm's-length terms, interest rate, and repayment schedule is required. The funds may be credited immediately; no Bank of Canada approval or foreign-investment permit is required. To ensure an audit-clean trail for the Canadian bank and to avoid Part XIII withholding leakage, disbursements from the parent should be MT103 direct, with UETR tracking, and should not pass through any pooled EMI wallet or third-party nominee account. Rail 2 - Raw materials (oat groats, hydrolyzed oat flour, enzymes, plant oils, packaging film): SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers to verified supplier bank accounts, each matched to a commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading; CAD legs settle via Lynx RTGS (same-day), USD legs via US correspondent nostro through Fedwire or CHIPS, and EUR legs via TARGET2/SEPA. Rail 3 - Cash concentration: daily multi-currency sweeps with SWIFT gpi UETR tracking and intraday position monitoring against treasury target bands; while the plant has no operating track record, the import LC facility should be cash-margin-covered or supported by a parent-company standby guarantee, with a documented path to unsecured facility conversion after 18 months of audited financial statements. All disbursements must be keyed to approved purchase orders and reconciled in the ERP procurement module.Outright deliverable forward FX contracts (CAD/USD and CAD/EUR) for firm procurement, capex, and sales commitments, tenor normally up to 24 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a credit support annex or cash-margin arrangement.FX swap contracts (combined spot and forward) for rolling trade-settlement conversions and short-term cash-flow matching without adding speculative market risk.Plain-vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars for budget-rate protection on EUR- and USD-denominated UHT equipment and ingredient import costs, subject to a documented treasury policy prohibiting naked optionality.Cross-currency swap (CAD/EUR) for medium-term EUR-denominated equipment-financing liabilities or an EUR capital lease, matched to the underlying debt service schedule and designated as a cash-flow hedge under IFRS 9.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible through Schedule I Canadian banks but disfavoured for CAD because the Canadian dollar is fully deliverable onshore; NDFs should be reserved only for exotic counterparty currencies outside the deliverable FX market.All hedging must be transactional and non-speculative, approved by the board treasury committee, and accompanied by contemporaneous IFRS 9 hedge-accounting designation documentation; no regulatory licence or central-bank approval is required for Canadian corporates entering such FX derivatives.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM as an ancillary rail for supplier payouts, payroll, and low-value FX conversion; LOW as the primary treasury, deposit-concentration, or trade-finance rail. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs and non-bank payment service providers are not authorized to take deposits under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and cannot offer true commercial bank or capital-escrow accounts. In Ontario they must be registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC; their fund-pooling, custodial, and settlement structures are subject to significantly higher counter-party and continuity risk, balances are not CDIC-insured, and such firms cannot issue or confirm Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) or provide import LC facilities. Therefore, all capital escrow balances, LC cash coverage, and primary liquidity for the UHT oat-milk project must reside with a Schedule I Canadian bank; EMIs may be used only for small ticket, non-critical outbound disbursements where the bank account remains the settlement source of funds.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general rate (Federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%); 12.2% combined small business rate (Federal 9% + Ontario 3.2%) on the first CA$500,000 of Canadian-controlled private corporation active business income; 25.0% combined rate on eligible Ontario manufacturing and processing income (Federal 15% + Ontario 10%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends paid by CA_CORP to a non-resident shareholder (ITA s. 212(2)); reduced to 5% or 15% under most comprehensive income tax treaties, including 5% for beneficial-owner corporate shareholders holding at least 10% voting interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory CA$ threshold for Local File preparation; CRA requires contemporaneous TPR-1 documentation for all cross-border non-arm's length transactions of a material size. Country-by-Country Report and OECD-aligned Master File are mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds CA$750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but the CRA and Canadian courts require real commercial substance. GAAR (ITA s. 245), transfer pricing recharacterization (ITA s. 247(2)), the Canadian-residence mind-and-management test (ITA s. 250), and Ontario corporate law registered-office requirements collectively invalidate artificial shell structures. The UHT oat milk plant must maintain premises, equipment, employees, operating expenses, and decision-making functions in Ontario to support its taxable nexus and treaty entitlement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. CRA may freeze bank accounts, garnish receivables, and seize corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize the fraudulent return are personally prosecuted; personal restitution orders, travel bans as bail conditions, and imprisonment apply, and the corporate veil does not shield willful tax fraud.
Gross Negligence / False Statements

Statutory sanction: 50% gross negligence penalty under ITA s. 163(2) on the entire understated tax; the normal reassessment period is extended by three years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorize the filing are jointly and severally liable for the penalty and interest; criminal referral is standard in high-value cases, and directors may face arrest and prosecution.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: 10% transfer pricing penalty on the net adjustment under ITA s. 247(3); the CRA may reallocate income, disallow deductions, and impose prescribed interest; documentary non-compliance can result in a CA$1,000-CA$2,500 penalty plus full reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the transfer pricing certificate without a reasonable basis cannot invoke the reasonable-efforts defense; they are exposed to gross negligence penalties and criminal referral where the documentation is fabricated.
