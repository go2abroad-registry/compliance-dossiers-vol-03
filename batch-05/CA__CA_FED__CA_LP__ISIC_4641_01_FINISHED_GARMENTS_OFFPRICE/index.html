Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities carrying on business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and, if required, a separate GST/HST account. Application is made via CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). The partnership itself registers as a non-resident partnership carrying on business in Canada; the partnership is the registrant, not the individual partners. A Canadian resident representative or agent may be required for non-resident partnerships. The BN is issued immediately online, while GST/HST account activation is typically processed within 5-10 business days. Supporting documentation includes partnership agreement, proof of foreign registration, and identification of all partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Canadian limited partnership is treated as a fiscally transparent pass-through entity for federal income tax purposes. No check-the-box election is available under Canadian federal law; the partnership is not an entity subject to tax. Partners report their share of income/loss directly. However, a partnership may elect under subsection 216(1) of the Income Tax Act to be taxed on Canadian-source rental income on a net basis, or make an election under section 142.6 for disposition of Canadian resource property. For GST/HST, the partnership may elect to have a fiscal year or to treat closely related corporations as a single registrant. Non-resident partners may be subject to Part XIII withholding on certain Canadian-source payments; a partnership may elect under subsection 116(2) to file a section 116 certificate to reduce withholding on dispositions of taxable Canadian property.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in global gross revenue derived from taxable supplies made in Canada during any rolling four-calendar-quarter period; once exceeded, mandatory registration is required. For non-resident businesses, the threshold applies only to Canadian-source revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Canada are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, provided the goods are exported 'by or on behalf of the supplier' in accordance with the prescribed documentary evidence requirements. Services that are 'supplied to a non-resident' and not received in Canada, and that are not related to real property or tangible personal property in Canada, may also be zero-rated under section 7 of Part V. For off-price wholesale liquidation of branded overstock apparel, the sale of goods to a non-resident buyer for delivery outside Canada qualifies for 0% GST/HST if the supplier maintains evidence that the goods were exported within the prescribed time frame.

  • Customs Export Declaration (usually CUSMA/Canada Customs Invoice or ACI eManifest export reporting; proof of export via CRA-approved evidence, e.g., Form B13A Export Declaration or carrier's bill of lading showing foreign destination)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill indicating consignee address outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of payment in foreign currency or evidence of consideration received from the non-resident purchaser; alternatively, a bank statement or wire confirmation)

Canada currently mandates electronic invoicing only for federal government procurement under the Business Number (e-invoicing via PEPPOL network for federal suppliers). No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is required for B2B or B2C invoices. GST/HST registrants must maintain paper or electronic invoices containing prescribed information (vendor BN, invoice date, tax amount, etc.). CRA may require electronic records to be accessible in Canada for audit purposes.

CRA applies strict verification of export evidence. If the supplier fails to produce sufficient documentary proof of export within a reasonable time (typically requested during a GST/HST audit), zero-rating is denied, and the net tax is reassessed with interest and penalties. Under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act, a penalty of 5% of the tax amount due (plus 1% per month up to 12 months) may apply for failure to file a return. Fraudulent claims can trigger gross negligence penalties under subsection 285(1) (up to 50% of the understated tax) and criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act. Additionally, if the exporter does not obtain the required export evidence within 2 years, the Minister may disallow the zero-rating retroactively. The CRA maintains specific Export and Commercial Goods verification programs; high-risk commodity classes, including textiles and apparel, may trigger enhanced document verification. Where a sale is zero-rated but proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, CRA may treat the transaction as a sham or conduit arrangement, reassess at the standard 5% rate, and apply transfer-pricing penalties under section 247 of the Income Tax Act if related parties are involved.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely and is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions; no exchange-control approval is required for inbound investment, loan repayment, repatriation of profits, or cross-border trade payments under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act. Residual hurdles for the CA_LP are fiscal and reporting only: (i) a non-resident limited partner carrying on business in Canada is taxable under s. 2(3) and s. 115 of the Income Tax Act on Canadian-source business income allocated through the T5013; (ii) distributions of income to non-resident partners may attract Part XIII withholding, or Part XIV branch tax in the case of corporate partners, in each case subject to applicable tax-treaty reduction; (iii) interest, rents, royalties, and management fees paid to non-resident partners carry 25% Part XIII withholding unless treaty-reduced; and (iv) all wires are subject to FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting at the CAD $10,000 threshold and to CRS/FATCA automatic exchange of information. Profit remittance is unrestricted at the exchange-control level provided T5013 allocation, the Canadian business number, GST/HST registration, and Part XIII remittance obligations are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada abolished its Foreign Exchange Control Board in 1951; there is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate or convert export receipts. For the CA_LP's off-price wholesale exports, the operative obligations are: (i) electronic export reporting to the CBSA for goods valued above CAD $2,000 per shipment under the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) and the CARM assessment and revenue management platform; (ii) FINTRAC record-keeping and reporting for every large cash or EFT transaction; (iii) transfer-pricing documentation under Income Tax Act s. 247 evidencing arm's-length payment terms; and (iv) the receiving bank's source-of-funds due diligence, matching each export wire to the underlying commercial invoice. Export proceeds may be retained in USD, CNY, EUR, or any other currency in a multi-currency operational account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary authority and retail payment-service supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Foreign-exchange and currency-disclosure administration rests with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act and with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) under the Cross-border Currency and Monetary Instruments Reporting Regulations. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers anti-money-laundering and large-transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially regulates the Schedule I clearing banks. Canada maintains no centralized foreign-exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional Medium. Canada does not recognize the European or UK Electronic Money Institution licence; a foreign digital payments firm (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, MultiPass, or similar) may operate only as (i) a money-services business registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and (ii) a payment service provider registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (mandatory since November 2024), including safeguarding and segregation-of-funds obligations. Any unregistered offshore EMI processing payments for Canadian persons is subject to administrative monetary penalties up to CAD $10 million, so the CA_LP must not place primary treasury rails with an unregistered EMI. Recommended architecture: the Schedule I bank account remains the settlement account; the fintech EMI is used only as a peripheral collection and disbursement adapter for marketplace remittances and small-ticket refunds, with daily zero-balance sweeps to the Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol: the remitting investor's bank issues a SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian Tier-1 receiving bank (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC), instructing credit to the CA_LP's account number and full legal name. The MT103 must carry ISO 20022 purpose code CAPM (capital injection) or an explicit narrative 'Capital contribution to limited partnership — no goods or services supplied', together with the originator's full legal name and reference. Verification: the receiving bank matches beneficiary name and account against its KYC registry, screens the originator against sanctions lists, and automatically files a FINTRAC EFT report for any amount at or above CAD $10,000; the CA_LP must retain the MT103 confirmation as the source document for its capital-account ledger. Where capital was raised under the NI 45-106 offering-memorandum exemption, the escrow agent releases funds only upon delivery of the general partner's closing certificate, after which the commercial bank sweeps the amount to the operational account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Conditional Medium. Canada does not recognize the European or UK Electronic Money Institution licence; a foreign digital payments firm (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, MultiPass, or similar) may operate only as (i) a money-services business registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and (ii) a payment service provider registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (mandatory since November 2024), including safeguarding and segregation-of-funds obligations. Any unregistered offshore EMI processing payments for Canadian persons is subject to administrative monetary penalties up to CAD $10 million, so the CA_LP must not place primary treasury rails with an unregistered EMI. Recommended architecture: the Schedule I bank account remains the settlement account; the fintech EMI is used only as a peripheral collection and disbursement adapter for marketplace remittances and small-ticket refunds, with daily zero-balance sweeps to the Tier-1 bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Canadian transfer-pricing documentation is mandatory when the aggregate value of covered transactions between the taxpayer and a non-arm's-length non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year. Local File and Master File content follows OECD Chapter V; CRA may request the documentation in writing, and the taxpayer must produce it within 3 months.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the UAE/offshore model. Substance is tested through CRA's general anti-avoidance rule (s.245 ITA), the permanent-establishment doctrine, and treaty anti-abuse rules. A non-resident partner carrying on the wholesale garment business through a Canadian LP will generally have a Canadian permanent establishment unless activities are limited to auxiliary or preparatory functions; real payroll, inventory-control, and management discretion must be present in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239 of the Income Tax Act, with fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to 5 years. CRA may re-assess on a gross-negligence basis and impose a penalty equal to 50% of the additional amount payable.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, or corporate directors of the general partner who authorize or know of the false filings are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the unpaid tax. Civil assessment, travel bans, asset seizures, and imprisonment are available where the veil is pierced.
Transfer-Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA will make a s.247(2) transfer-pricing adjustment and assess a penalty of 10% of the net upward adjustment. Interest is charged on reassessed tax, and deductions for the inflated expenses are denied.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation or knowingly orchestrate a s.247 adjustment may be assessed for the corporation's penalties and interest through a director's liability assessment where income was not remitted; travel bans are possible in contested criminal tax cases.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: The CRA may apply GAAR (s.245) to re-characterize the allocation, deny treaty benefits under OECD MLI principal-purpose tests, and impose the s.163.2 tax-shelter penalty of up to CAD 1,000,000 plus the promoter's/contributor's fine.

Director personal liability: Limited partners who take part in management lose limited-liability protection; the corporate general partner's directors are personally liable for restitution of the rejected allocations and any resulting unreleased taxes, and courts may impose personal asset freezes and travel bans.
AML / Proceeds of Crime (FINTRAC)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 500,000 for corporations; criminal conviction can result in fines up to CAD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 10 years, with seizure of assets as proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for fines up to CAD 500,000 for failing to establish an AML program; criminal sanctions include imprisonment, travel bans, and personal civil restitution for losses caused by the unlawful transactions.
