Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A limited partnership must maintain a registered office and service address in the province or territory of registration, subject to the applicable partnership statute and registry rules. A commercial virtual office may satisfy the registered-address function if it is a real address, accepts official service, maintains required records or provides access to them, and is accepted by the registry, bank, tax authorities, and licensing bodies. It does not by itself establish tax residence, permanent establishment, management-and-control location, transfer-pricing substance, or eligibility for treaty benefits. For a bubble-tea franchise and syrup-supply-chain business, a virtual registered office may be acceptable for a holding, licensing, or administrative LP, but it is insufficient for retail food preparation, storage, importation, warehousing, staff operations, local POS activity, or food-premises licensing. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian federal income tax does not offer US-style check-the-box elections. A limited partnership is presumed pass-through, but allocations may be adjusted under Income Tax Act s. 96(3) agreements. Elections are available under s. 97(2) (cost of property to partnership), s. 97(1) (rollover of property to partnership), and ETA s. 273 for 'qualifying partnerships' to treat the partnership as a separate person for GST/HST. Hybrid mismatch elections must be assessed against Part 6.1 of the ITA (hybrid mismatch rules) and ETA non-resident registration choices; no blanket federal election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (annual worldwide taxable supplies of the person and associated persons; non-residents carrying on business in Canada are required to register even below the threshold, and certain supplies of real property or goods into Canada may trigger mandatory registration regardless of turnover).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported by the LP where the goods leave Canada in accordance with ETA ss. 22 and 23, provided the LP obtains proof of export (POD) within 2 years. Services (including franchise royalties and licensing) supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under ss. 7–8 of Part V of Schedule VI if the recipient is outside Canada or, where the supply is made in Canada, the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is for use exclusive outside Canada. The LP must keep evidence of the beneficial recipient's non-resident status and the nature of the supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13A)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of export payment
  • Packing List

Canada has no real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping regime. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically where turnover exceeds CAD 1 million (mandatory e-filing), and large registrants must use GST/HST NETFILE; electronic invoices are accepted if original data integrity and audit trails are preserved. CRA may issue digital demand for records and require API-based bank or inventory data exchange for verification; however, there is no pre-clearance invoice stamping.

The CRA may reassess beyond the normal 4-year statute period on misrepresentation, assess a 10% unregistered penalty (ETA s. 280), and impose a 50% false-refund penalty (ETA s. 285) in addition to interest and criminal liability under s. 327 and Income Tax Act s. 239. Export zero-rating claims are subject to documentary review, and any overstated refunds are clawed back on reassessment, with gross negligence penalties up to $2,500 plus 50% of the misstatement penalty where applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely floating and fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada maintains no blanket exchange-control law, no requirement for central-bank approval of inward capital contributions, and no restriction on remittance of profits, dividends, or partnership distributions to non-residents. A non-resident limited partner in a Canadian LP may receive profit distributions in USD or CAD, but Canadian-source income and deemed-Canadian-source distributions remain subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25% absent treaty relief (e.g., Canada-US Tax Convention). Foreign-currency bank accounts can be held without prior authorization. Reasonable economic substance and arm’s length pricing must be evidenced to prevent CRA recharacterization of the LP’s capital infusion or royalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no prescribed conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Exporters, including franchise syrup and concentrate suppliers, may retain foreign currency receipts offshore or in a resident Canadian foreign-currency account. The sole statutory obligations are FINTRAC reporting thresholds: cross-border currency movements exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported, and financial institutions are required to submit large-value electronic funds transfer reports for international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more. These obligations do not impose any conversion or encashment duty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting. Canada has no centralized foreign-exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognize the EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) passport. Foreign EMIs such as Wise Business or Revolut Business may operate only as money-service businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC and may not hold deposits in the manner of a banking license. They can support operating disbursements and employee expense payables, but they cannot serve as the primary treasury clearing house for the Canadian LP's capital escrow, royalty escalation, or commercial letter-of-credit facility. OSFI-regulated banks will also show high risk aversion to initial funding routed through an unlicensed offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital is injected by inbound SWIFT MT103 wire to the Canadian LP’s resident account at a Schedule I bank. The wire instruction must include: (a) beneficiary: the exact registered legal name of the Canadian LP and its CRA business number; (b) purpose of payment coded as capital contribution or equity injection into a Canadian partnership; (c) remitter’s full name, address, and account number; and (d) a narrative comment such as 'Capital contribution — ISIC 5610 franchise operations'. The financial institution will automatically capture the FINTRAC EFT report for any international receipt at or above CAD 10,000. There is no exchange-control approval required before wiring. Turnover: No federal statutory audit is imposed solely because the entity is a Canadian limited partnership. A mandatory independent audit is triggered if: (i) the limited partnership agreement or franchise agreement requires it; (ii) the LP is a reporting issuer, investment fund, or otherwise subject to securities law audit requirements; (iii) a provincial regulator, lender, or franchisor requires audited financial statements; or (iv) a corporate general partner is a CBCA corporation and shareholders do not validly waive the auditor appointment.
