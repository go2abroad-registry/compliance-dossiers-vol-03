Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC) — national tax authority for GST/HST, corporate income tax and customs (via CBSA); registration portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and CRA My Business Account; Ontario Ministry of Finance exercises the provincial HST component and local taxes (e.g., Employer Health Tax) through CRA-administered collection.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate federally (Corporations Canada Online Filing Centre) or provincially in Ontario (Ontario Business Registry; extra-provincial registration under the Ontario Business Corporations Act if the foreign parent carries on business in Ontario). 2) Obtain a nine-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and GST/HST program account via BRO online or Form RC1 (domestic) / Form RC460 (GST/HST registration for a non-resident business). 3) Add required program accounts: GST/HST (RT), Corporation Income Tax (RC), Import/Export (RM/RE), and Payroll (RP). 4) Register with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for the importer/exporter identifier (linked to the BN) and, for defense-controlled hardware, obtain Controlled Goods Program (CGP) registration and Global Affairs Canada EIPA export permit coverage. 5) Complete one-time Ontario registration for provincial accounts (including EHT participation where payroll thresholds apply).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian-resident business corporation (federal/Ontario articles) is taxed as a separate taxpayer under ITA subsection 2(1); no pass-through regime applies to a limited business corporation. Territorial scope: worldwide income taxation with foreign tax credits; Ontario corporate income tax is integrated with the federal return. Non-resident parent shareholders are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on dividends (default 25%, treaty-reducible to 5%-10% under relevant treaties); the corporation is deemed resident for treaty purposes if incorporated in Canada.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide an entity-level 'check-the-box' election; classification follows incorporation and residence. Available elections relevant to cross-border defense manufacturing include: functional-currency reporting election (ITA s. 261); capital-dividend election (ITA s. 83(2)); GST/HST Quick Method election (ETA, Schedule VIII) and s. 167(1) election for taxable supplies of a business; hybrid-mismatch restriction rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6, effective for taxation years on/after 1 July 2020) override deduction/non-inclusion outcomes and cannot be bypassed by election; treaty 'tie-breaker' and foreign accrual property income rules further govern Canadian tax classification outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148): GST/HST registration is mandatory once a foreign or domestic supplier's worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; a manufacturing permanent establishment in Ontario generally exceeds this threshold immediately; registration is also recommended to claim input tax credits on production inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ETA s. 22 and Schedule VI, Part V, for military tactical vehicles, parts and related export services where: (a) the recipient is a non-resident or the goods are identified for export; (b) the vehicle is exported within the statutory window — generally 60 days from delivery or not later than the second reporting period following the supply, per the Export Goods Regulations; (c) the supplier obtains and retains prescribed export documents (Carrier's Certificate of Export, B13A/CERS export declaration, Bill of Lading, foreign customs entry) before claiming the 0% rate; and (d) for defense goods, an EIPA export permit and Controlled Goods Program clearance exist. Services performed directly on goods destined for export may be zero-rated under Schedule VI (ss. 7-9).

  • CBSA export declaration transmitted via Canada Export Reporting System (CERS) (B13A for controlled or regulated goods), including ISIC 3040 / HS 8710 customs classification
  • Bill of Lading (through bill via ocean/air waybill, or truck cargo manifest evidencing physical export from Canada)
  • Carrier's Certificate of Export (carrier attestation confirming export, destination and date of export)
  • Commercial invoice with declared value and defense-article identification (supporting EIPA permit application)
  • EIPA export permit or permit-exemption certificate issued by Global Affairs Canada for tactical/armored defense vehicles
  • Controlled Goods Program (CGP) registration certificate and controlled-article handling evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/letter-of-credit settlement evidence proving the foreign buyer's payment in foreign currency

No real-time fiscal-clearance or digital-stamping mandate exists in Canada; CRA requires electronic GST/HST return filing (GST/HST NETFILE / EDI/XML S-FTP) and invoice retention standards under ETA s. 286 requiring supporting export evidence. CBSA operates the Single Window and Canada Export Reporting System (CERS) with mandatory eManifest/ACI electronic reporting for export cargo; Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing through PEPPOL is voluntary. The system is evidence-based, not clearance-based.

Retrospective clawback exposure: CRA may reassess GST/HST returns within four years from the filing/assessment due date (ETA s. 298), with an unlimited reassessment period in cases of misrepresentation or fraud; exporters that cannot substantiate physical export are re-rated from 0% to 13% HST with interest and penalties. Penalties: gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the net tax understated under ETA s. 280.1; wilful evasion offence under ETA s. 285 (summary conviction: fine up to CAD 50,000 and/or up to two years' imprisonment; indictment: fine up to 200% of tax evaded and/or up to five years' imprisonment); CBSA administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) up to CAD 25,000 per violation under the Customs Act; false export declarations trigger Customs Act offences (ss. 109-110, 153.1), potential seizure of goods, suspension or revocation of CGP authorization and EIPA permits, and adverse federal defense procurement compliance findings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under both capital and current account transactions. Canada does not maintain exchange control regulations, and the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act establish a freely floating currency. Dividend and profit remittance is generally unrestricted, subject to Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, reduced by treaty) and transfer-pricing compliance for intercompany payments. No prior approval is required for inbound or outbound remittances, but reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) apply to large cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Export proceeds need not be surrendered to the central bank and may be retained in foreign currency accounts; however, all receipts must be reported for taxation and FINTRAC large transaction reporting where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada and Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); foreign exchange administration is decentralized, with no dedicated exchange control authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for foreign digital EMIs. Foreign fintechs are not chartered banks in Canada and must register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC, maintain a Canadian agent or presence, and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act if processing Canadian payments. Access to government defense contract disbursements is severely restricted because most federal procurement settlement requires an active Canadian bank account or a recognized Canadian clearing member.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 500,000 for transactions with non-arm's length parties requiring transfer pricing documentation (local file); Master File required for entities with global revenue exceeding CAD 50 million

Economic substance scope: Canada lacks a dedicated Economic Substance Regime; compliance enforced through transfer pricing rules, General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR), and Controlled Foreign Corporation (CFC) provisions to prevent artificial profit shifting

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Income Tax Act, fines up to $1 million for corporations and $5 million for individuals, imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for taxes not remitted due to gross negligence or willful default; potential disqualification, fines, and criminal charges
Shell Company Operations

Statutory sanction: Penalty of $10,000 per day for failure to file or maintain proper records; possible revocation of corporate status

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leading to personal liability for all corporate debts and taxes; possible imprisonment for fraudulent incorporation
Non-Compliance with Transfer Pricing Rules

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income, penalties up to 10% of transaction value, interest on additional taxes

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal liability if they directed or acquiesced in the non-compliance
AML and Regulatory Violations in Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to $500,000 for corporations, imprisonment for individuals; potential license revocation for defense-related activities

Director personal liability: Personal fines, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for willful AML breaches
