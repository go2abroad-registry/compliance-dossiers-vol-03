Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The corporate or business registry of the province or territory where the limited partnership maintains its registered office and carries on business. No federal Canadian registry has jurisdiction to constitute a limited partnership.

Governing statute

Canada has no federal statute establishing or registering limited partnerships. A Canadian limited partnership must be formed under the limited partnership statute of the selected province or territory, typically through a declaration or registration filing. The Canada Business Corporations Act does not apply to limited partnerships. Federal tax administration is governed principally by the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act.

Formation / constitutional filings

Provincial or territorial Declaration of Limited Partnership, Certificate of Limited Partnership, or equivalent registration instrument, depending on the selected jurisdiction.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 35 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No federal minimum paid-up capital requirement applies to a Canadian limited partnership. Capital contributions, partnership interests, withdrawal rights, allocation provisions, and liability protections must be specified in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account is generally required, although the general partner may require capital to be paid into the partnership bank account under the agreement.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial civil-defense clearance is ordinarily required for an office-based software business. The operator must comply with applicable provincial or territorial occupational health and safety legislation, workplace inspections, fire-code requirements, emergency procedures, occupancy approvals, and municipal building permits. Structural-load, electrical, accessibility, and fire inspections may be required for leased, renovated, or data-centre premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No federal minimum paid-up capital requirement applies to a Canadian limited partnership. Capital contributions, partnership interests, withdrawal rights, allocation provisions, and liability protections must be specified in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account is generally required, although the general partner may require capital to be paid into the partnership bank account under the agreement.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft

The general partner is authorized to complete all filings and execute ancillary documents.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No federal industrial licensing board is ordinarily required for a visual web-template or no-code web-application builder. Provincial or territorial business licensing offices and municipal licensing authorities may apply based on the premises and activities.

Category: No sector-specific federal manufacturing or operational permit is ordinarily required. A municipal business licence, zoning approval, occupancy permit, or home-based business approval may be required for the operating location.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada. Primary digital portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO), which is used to obtain the federal Business Number (BN), GST/HST program account, payroll account, and import/export account.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Complete extra-provincial registration of the Limited Partnership in the selected Canadian province, typically Ontario or British Columbia, under the applicable Limited Partnerships Act, and obtain the provincial Certificate of Registration or Certificate of Limited Partnership. Step 2: Apply to the CRA for the federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1, selecting NAICS 2022 code 511210 and ISIC Rev.4 code 6201 as the principal business activity, which covers software publishing, computer programming, and no-code website builder services. Step 3: Within the BN application, add the GST/HST program account (RT) using the CRA's simplified non-resident registration path if the LP has no physical presence in Canada and is operated from abroad; this path is available for suppliers of specified digital supplies to Canadian consumers. Step 4: Submit to the CRA the limited partnership agreement, evidence of the general partner's identity and authority, and, if the LP is relied upon as a Canadian partnership for income tax purposes, confirmation of the Canadian-resident partner requirements under section 102(2) of the Income Tax Act. Step 5: Enrol in My Business Account to file GST/HST returns, view reassessments, and manage the RT program account throughout the registration lifecycle.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Canadian Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for federal income tax purposes: it is not a taxpayer, and each partner is taxed on its allocated share of income and losses under section 96 of the Income Tax Act. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership is deemed to be a person in respect of its commercial activity and can hold its own BN-based GST/HST account. Canada has no elective check-the-box classification; the pass-through classification is fixed by Canadian law and is generally respected under fiscally transparent entity provisions of applicable tax treaties, including the Canada-US Treaty.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in the Canadian federal tax regime. The material statutory elections and mechanisms available to a Limited Partnership include: (i) the section 96(3) Income Tax Act framework under which the partnership computes business income or loss at the partnership level as if it were a person; (ii) the section 249.1(5) Income Tax Act election to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period for the partnership; (iii) a section 103 Income Tax Act agreement to allocate partnership income or loss in proportions different from the fixed sharing ratios, provided the allocation is reasonable; (iv) the joint election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act for closely related corporations and partnerships to treat certain supplies as made for no consideration for GST/HST purposes; and (v) hybrid mismatch self-assessment and CRA disclosure obligations under sections 12.7, 18.2, 18.3 and 94 of the Income Tax Act, which do not provide an elective deferral but require affected partners to report and adjust.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (thirty thousand Canadian dollars) of aggregate consideration for taxable supplies made in Canada in either the preceding four calendar quarters or the current calendar quarter under section 148 of the Excise Tax Act, this being the small supplier threshold. For non-resident suppliers of specified digital supplies, including software subscriptions, platform access, and no-code website builder services delivered electronically, the registration threshold is likewise CAD 30,000 of such specified supplies made in Canada over a 12-month period, with voluntary simplified registration available below that amount.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, supplies of services and intangible personal property, including software subscriptions, platform access, and low-code or no-code website builder services, are zero-rated at 0% GST/HST when made to a recipient who is not a resident of Canada, who is not registered for GST/HST, and who acquires the supply for consumption or use outside Canada. The supply must not relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the place-of-supply rules under sections 143 and 144 of the Excise Tax Act, including the 2021 specified digital supply rules, must result in the supply being treated as made outside Canada. Supplies made to Canadian residents, to GST/HST registrants, or to non-resident persons registered under the simplified GST/HST system do not qualify for export zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate (required where tangible goods form part of the supply chain; for pure digital no-code website builder supplies, no customs export declaration is mechanically issued and the CRA accepts an equivalent electronic export evidence trail)
  • Executed contract or master services agreement clearly identifying the non-resident recipient, the jurisdiction of the recipient, and the offshore consumption or use of the no-code website builder services
  • GST/HST-compliant tax invoices marked Zero-Rated Supply - Schedule VI Export, showing the supplier's BN, the recipient's non-resident address, and the applicable place-of-supply rule
  • Payment-rail evidence of offshore proceeds, including Stripe Billing and Paddle settlement reports, SWIFT deposit confirmations, and foreign exchange conversion records demonstrating that the consideration originated from non-resident sources
  • Non-resident status due diligence file, comprising registration documents, directors' residential addresses, foreign tax or VAT identification numbers, and signed certifications of non-resident status as referenced in CRA GST/HST Memorandum 17.4 on exports
  • Digital delivery and usage logs, including server metadata, geolocation records, and account access logs, demonstrating that the platform services were supplied to and accessed from locations outside Canada
  • Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act where the export transaction involves related parties, substantiating the arm's-length price and the agreed settlement schedule

Canada does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance, government-authorised digital stamping, or live API tax stamping for GST/HST invoices. Registrants must issue valid tax invoices containing the supplier's BN and GST/HST registration number, transaction date, supplier and recipient identification, description of the supply, and the tax status or amount, and must retain complete electronic records in accordance with CRA record-keeping rules. GST/HST registrants are required to file returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or the GST34 gateway for most programs. The Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing framework is voluntary and interoperable, and no central invoice clearance gateway is required for a supply to be taxable or zero-rated.

The CRA may audit zero-rating claims within the normal four-year reassessment period, which is unlimited in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or neglect. Where export zero-rating is denied, the CRA will claw back the GST/HST refund previously claimed, assess the 5% federal GST plus any applicable provincial HST or PST, charge prescribed daily compound interest on the outstanding amount, and impose statutory penalties: under section 281.1 of the Excise Tax Act a false statement or omission penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the tax sought to be evaded; under section 280 a failure-to-register penalty equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 1% of the collectible tax; and under section 281 a failure-to-file penalty equal to 6% of the arrears plus 1% per month up to a maximum of 12 months. Criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act or section 239 of the Income Tax Act carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to five years. Canada maintains no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for exported digital services; the 180-day figure in this schedule is the internal trade-compliance ceiling and audit-expectation benchmark applied by the taxpayer, and the CRA treats fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating of digital platform supplies as a high-priority compliance issue, using payment-processor data, including records from Stripe and Paddle, to match offshore revenue streams with export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible global reserve currency. Canada accepts the applicable obligations of Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, meaning there are no current or capital account exchange controls or discriminatory currency arrangements. Capital subscriptions into and out of Canadian limited partnerships are freely permitted, whether sourced from resident or non-resident partners. Profit remittance, distributions, dividends, interest, royalties, and service fees may be repatried to foreign beneficial owners without prior exchange approval. Stream of payments into and out of Canada is subject only to regular transaction reporting, AML/CTF obligations, and tax form completion, not to exchange conversion rules. For a no-code website builder entity, SaaS subscription revenues in USD from international merchants may be held in foreign currency or converted to CAD at market rates. No consent or declaration to the Bank of Canada is required for profit repatriation; however, non-resident partners should obtain a tax clearance certificate or waiver from the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) before capital distributions that could be treated as sale of Canadian property, and payments to non-residents for services may be subject to Part XIII withholding tax depending on treaty relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or forced conversion requirement in Canada for export receipts. A Canadian limited partnership may collect, hold, and remit export-derived foreign currency without any required time period for repatriation and without any mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained in USD, EUR, GBP, or other convertible currencies in foreign-denominated bank accounts at domestic authorized banks or in accounts held abroad through Canadian bank correspondent branches. The only obligations arising are documentary, not conversion-based: (i) maintaining books and records sufficient to demonstrate the source and disposition of export funds; (ii) FINTRAC large transaction and suspicious transaction report thresholds, generally CAD 10,000 for cash and possibly virtual currency; and (iii) for goods exports, reporting to the CBSA through the Canadian International Trade Single Window or prescribed customs declarations. Because the underlying business is the sale of low-code website templates and software subscriptions, the relevant export proceeds are intangible service and royalty flows, which are not subject to customs surrender or exchange-control return timelines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in concert with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Department of Finance Canada, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for trade and cross-border reporting. Canada does not maintain a centralized foreign exchange control ministry; monetary and macroprudential policy sits with the Bank of Canada, while licensing and prudential banking standards are administered by OSFI, and AML/CTF surveillance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) is administered by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-to-High for operational disbursement accounts, Low for capital escrow or high-value fiduciary balances. Canadian federal banking law restricts deposit-taking to entities authorized under the Bank Act as Schedule I or Schedule II banks; an offshore digital EMI cannot accept deposits in Canada absent a federal banking license or an authorized Canadian trust company sponsorship. Nonetheless, foreign fintech EMIs such as Wise Business, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, and sponsored Banking-as-a-Service providers can be used as collection/settlement rails for customer-facing card payments, international inventor remuneration, and small disbursements to contractors. They are not CDIC-insured and cannot provide lending, trade finance, or escrow-grade segregation for a Canadian limited partnership. To obtain high-touch treasury services, the LP must maintain its primary operating and escrow relationships with a Schedule I Canadian bank; an EMI may be used as a supplementary treasury technology layer but not as the regulated settlement substrate. Legal constraints include: no unauthorized lending, no advertising own Canadian bank accounts, no use of EMI payment acceptance where the LP itself requires a Money Services Business (MSB) registration under PCMLTFA if dealing in foreign exchange or transmitting funds; typical no-code SaaS merchants without physical money transmission activity will not become an MSB merely by using a licensed PSP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner contributions should be wired via the SWIFT MT103 system from the beneficial owner's or subscribing partner's bank to the LP's Canadian operating or capital escrow account. The MT103 field 50K must identify the remitter as the contributing partner, not an unrelated intermediary; field 59 must show the exact registered Canadian legal name of the Limited Partnership and its Canadian bank IBAN or account number, plus the bank's SWIFT/BIC. For CAD-denominated domestic wires, the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) or Payments Canada Real-Time Rail (RTR) can be used by Canadian banks; however, the compliance bailiwick remains the same. The remittance information field should contain an unambiguous purpose code or narrative such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION', 'PARTNERSHIP EQUITY INJECTION', or 'LP COMMITMENT PAYMENT'. A corresponding invoice is not required; a drawdown notice issued by the General Partner referencing the capital account schedule is sufficient. On the Canadian treasury side, the LP should produce a capital-account ledger entry immediately upon receipt, retaining the MT103 advice, the partner's source-of-funds correspondence, and the GSIN/sanctions screening for the remitter. For inbound contributions exceeding CAD 250,000, the bank will perform enhanced due diligence on the final beneficial owner; therefore the LP should pre-supply the partnership's bank with an executed subscription agreement, a register of significant control, and a commercially reasonable capital structure explanation before the wire enters.Outbound disbursements from the LP's Canadian treasury should use: (i) SWIFT MT103 single- or multi-currency transfers for cross-border royalty, licence-fee, contractor, and supplier payments; (ii) SWIFT MT700 commercial documentary credits for high-value software distribution or infrastructure procurements, where a freight forwarder or tech vendor requires LC-backed payment; (iii) Payments Canada ACSS/EFA electronic funds transfer for domestic CAD vendor payments; and (iv) commercial bank domestic wires over LVTS or the RTR using ISO 20022 messaging where the bank offers digital incorporation. No commercial LC is required for ordinary SaaS subscription purchases; it is available only where the LP contractually functions as importer of a physical appliance or hardware aggregate. For each outbound MT103, the LP should apply exact beneficiary name and address, a realistic payment narration matching the invoiced service, and evidence that no OFAC/UN sanctions screening exceptions apply. Vendor disbursements to non-residents may require Part XIII withholding tax if the payment is for services rendered in Canada or for a taxable Canadian-source royalty; before remitting, the LP must obtain a beneficial-ownership certificate and applicable treaty relief (e.g., IRS Form W-8BEN-E, or the Canadian NR301 authorizing reduced withholding) and remit the withholding through the CRA's payroll or tax accounts. Trade settlement for digital goods should never be made to a third-party nominee account absent a true assignment of receivable; all beneficiary accounts must physically match the vendor contract. For payments in USD and other cross-border currencies, the treasury may aggregate invoices and hedge the settlement amount so that the underlying LP does not inadvertently carry speculative overnight FX exposure.Outright deliverable forwards in CAD/USD, CAD/EUR, and CAD/GBP, entered into as principal with a Canadian Schedule I bank under an ISDA master agreement or a short-form FX confirmation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible in Canada for currencies where physical delivery is restricted or unavailable in the counterparty bank's offshore jurisdiction; for CAD/USD, the market custom is a deliverable forward, but an NDF overlay can be entered into with a global bank to hedge cash flows arising from overseas sales in emerging-market currencies or where the LP maintains foreign-currency accounts in restricted jurisdictions.Vanilla FX options, both European and American style, to protect against adverse CAD moves while retaining upside on USD revenue, subject to internal hedge-appropriateness committee review.Cross-currency swaps (CYS/CCS) to fix principal and economic-interest exchange rates on multi-year SaaS licence royalties or large intercompany capital contributions, provided the LP can evidence a genuine commercial underlying transaction under Part IV.1 of the Income Tax Act (Canada).FX swaps (spot/forward combinations) used solely for treasury-liquidity management, not for speculative trading, where the bank and LP enter into a simultaneous spot buy/sell and forward unwind in CAD/USD or CAD/any fully convertible G10 currency.Collar or zero-cost spread structures combining purchased and sold vanilla options, with the premium netted to zero, to hedge a known invoice pipeline subject to settlement date variance.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-to-High for operational disbursement accounts, Low for capital escrow or high-value fiduciary balances. Canadian federal banking law restricts deposit-taking to entities authorized under the Bank Act as Schedule I or Schedule II banks; an offshore digital EMI cannot accept deposits in Canada absent a federal banking license or an authorized Canadian trust company sponsorship. Nonetheless, foreign fintech EMIs such as Wise Business, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, and sponsored Banking-as-a-Service providers can be used as collection/settlement rails for customer-facing card payments, international inventor remuneration, and small disbursements to contractors. They are not CDIC-insured and cannot provide lending, trade finance, or escrow-grade segregation for a Canadian limited partnership. To obtain high-touch treasury services, the LP must maintain its primary operating and escrow relationships with a Schedule I Canadian bank; an EMI may be used as a supplementary treasury technology layer but not as the regulated settlement substrate. Legal constraints include: no unauthorized lending, no advertising own Canadian bank accounts, no use of EMI payment acceptance where the LP itself requires a Money Services Business (MSB) registration under PCMLTFA if dealing in foreign exchange or transmitting funds; typical no-code SaaS merchants without physical money transmission activity will not become an MSB merely by using a licensed PSP.
