Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Canadian limited partnership; capital commitments are fixed contractually in the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow account is legally mandated at formation, though the parties may direct closing funds through escrow; the corporate general partner has no minimum authorized capital under the CBCA and its stated capital is set by board resolution.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portals: CRA Register a Business (Business Registration Online), CRA My Business Account, and Represent a Client. GST/HST program accounts (RT) are administered federally by CRA. Quebec requires separate QST registration with Revenu Quebec when the LP has a permanent establishment in Quebec.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Register the limited partnership extra-provincially under the applicable provincial Limited Partnership Act in each province of operation, satisfying provincial registration requirements (a non-resident general partner is permitted in most modern provinces). Step 2 - Apply to CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts: GST/HST (RT), Payroll (RP), and Import/Export (RM), using Form RC1 or the CRA Business Registration Online service. Step 3 - File the annual T5013 partnership information return (T5013 Summary and T5013 slips) under the partnership BN; the LP itself pays no federal income tax. Step 4 - Obtain CRA tax ID numbers for non-resident limited partners (BN or NR-series) and apply Part XIII withholding on covered Canadian-source payments; consider the ITA s. 216 election to tax net rental income. Step 5 - Register for GST/HST as soon as taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold, or voluntarily before that to claim input tax credits (ITCs) on build-out and equipment. Step 6 - For import/export of servers, activate the RM account and report exports via CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS); if operating in Quebec, register separately for QST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope: fiscally transparent limited partnership under ITA s. 96; income and credits flow to limited partners pro rata; no entity-level income tax except information filing. Resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Canadian-source income allocated through the LP, including income from a Canadian business under ITA s. 253 and treaty business-PE rules. For GST/HST, the LP is a separate person and is a registrant with its own RT account. Income tax classification is not elective in Canada; an LP is always flow-through unless it is a publicly traded SIFT partnership or makes a hybrid-mismatch election for specific deduction disallowance.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada. Available statutory elections: ITA s. 216 election by a non-resident limited partner to file a Part I return on net Canadian rental/immovable-property income instead of gross-basis Part XIII withholding; ITA s. 249.1(9) election for a non-calendar partnership fiscal period (with CRA concurrence and member consent); ETA s. 240(2.1) voluntary GST/HST registration election; ETA s. 156(2) related-party joint election to eliminate GST/HST on intercompany supplies between closely related registrants; and the hybrid-mismatch election under ITA ss. 18.4-18.7 and 20.5, which is an irrevocable 5-year written election to recharacterize mismatched deductions of hybrid entities and financial instruments (relevant when a U.S. LLC or other fiscally transparent entity is a limited partner).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital and current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Canada maintains no exchange control licensing for remittance of profits, service income, or capital distributions. The data center colocation services invoiced to foreign tenants constitute current-account transactions freely payable in CAD or foreign currency. Non-resident limited partners are subject to Part XIII withholding tax on distributions classified as dividends or royalties, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty; capital returns are generally non-taxable and freely remittable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion ratios on export or service receipts. Foreign-currency lease and hosting invoices may be retained in non-resident or Canadian-dollar accounts without mandatory conversion. Bank reporting thresholds apply only for FINTRAC large-transaction reporting (CAD 10,000) and international electronic funds transfer reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange and payments supervision coordinated with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Payoneer) are not licensed as banks in Canada and lack full commercial lending, clearing, and FX hedging capabilities. They may operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses (MSBs) for cross-border payments, but for a limited partnership requiring direct SWIFT membership, daily CAD settlement, and commercial liquidity buffers, Schedule I domestic banks remain the primary rail. EMIs are suitable as secondary collection/payout lanes, with strict third-party settlement validation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5 million (total value of transactions with non-resident non-arm's length persons in the fiscal year) triggers Local File documentation. Master File required if total gross revenue exceeds CAD 100 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act. Transactions lacking economic substance may be recharacterized to deny tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the tax evaded, plus interest. Criminal prosecution may result in fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate partner may be personally liable for unpaid taxes withheld or remitted under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act. Civil restitution and travel bans may apply.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits under the Limitation of Benefits provision or apply GAAR. Penalties include reassessment of tax, interest, and a 50% gross negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for corporate penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in the sham arrangement. Travel bans may be imposed in serious cases.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities per violation. Criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for fines if they knowingly failed to implement AML compliance programs. Travel bans may be imposed for non-compliance.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million), and charge interest on the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the transfer pricing mispricing. Civil restitution and travel bans may apply.
