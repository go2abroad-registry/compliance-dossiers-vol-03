Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — official portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited partnership carrying on business in Canada must apply to the CRA for a statutory Business Number (BN) using Form RC1, 'Request for a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number'. The application must identify the limited partnership and the program account needed for GST/HST (RT suffix); financing or employee trust activities that are taxable in Canada are reported under the same BN. Non-resident applicants must provide the partnership's jurisdiction of formation, a Canadian representative or contact address, and sufficient partner identification for the CRA to validate the pass-through structure. Canada does not issue a separate VAT identification: the BN is the TIN for all federal taxes, and the GST/HST program account adds the RT suffix (e.g., BN-RT).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not have a US-style check-the-box election for domestic limited partnerships. Under section 96 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), a Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent and net income or loss is computed at the partnership level and flowed through to partners. A publicly traded limited partnership is subject to the Specified Investment Flow-Through (SIFT) rules on a mandatory basis, not by election. Available election mechanisms include a partner election under subsection 96(3) for the partnership fiscal year-end and allocation, and, for GST/HST purposes, the partnership may voluntarily register under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act (ETA), make an election under section 273 for closely related registrants to treat taxable supplies as having nil consideration, and request a refund of input tax credits in accordance with ETA section 261. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under sections 94.2 to 94.4 of the Income Tax Act apply to certain foreign limited liability entities, but are not an elective regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility for both current and capital-account transactions under IMF Article VIII obligations. Profit remittance is generally unrestricted, subject to withholding taxes under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for distributions to non-resident partners unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty, and to FATCA/CRS reporting obligations. No exchange control approval is required for capital injections, loan repayments, or dividend/distribution repatriation. Non-resident partners of a Canadian limited partnership may trigger Canadian-source income tax filing and reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists in Canada. There are no mandatory conversion ratios or prescribed timelines for repatriation of export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, converted freely, or used directly for cross-border trade settlement, subject only to financial-transaction reporting thresholds applicable under FINTRAC rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority; no dedicated foreign exchange control administration exists under Canadian law

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are subject to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), requiring registration as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC if they provide money-transfer, foreign-exchange, or payment services in Canada. Activities involving deposit-taking constitute banking business and require a banking license under the Bank Act. Under the Retail Payment Activities Act, payment-service providers must register with the Bank of Canada. Remote onboarding may be permitted for Canadian-registered MSBs but foreign-owned EMIs without a registered Canadian entity face significant legal and regulatory barriers to executing cross-border payments into or out of Canada. For ESOP trust purposes, a Canadian Schedule I bank is generally preferable to an unregulated offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Canadian limited partnership must be structured as documented capital contributions under the partnership agreement. The compliant routing is via SWIFT MT103 wire from the foreign parent or sponsor, with the field 70 narrative clearly stating 'Capital contribution to Canadian limited partnership' and referencing the trust or partnership name. Purpose codes used by Canadian banks are generally aligned with the bank's own payment coding system; a common narrative code is 'Cash Management, aggregate capital contribution' or 'Share capital/profit remittance' for bank reference. Under FINTRAC's Electronic Funds Transfer Reporting regime, wire transfers to a Canadian financial institution of CAD/CAD $10,000 or more (or the foreign-currency equivalent) must be reported by the reporting entity within 24 hours. To preempt compliance, the sending institution should provide a source-of-funds reference, and the Canadian bank's compliance team may require the partnership agreement, a board resolution, trust deed, and proof of the ultimate funding source for amounts exceeding corporate onboarding trigger thresholds.Outbound trade settlements from the ESOP trust or its operating partnership can be executed through SWIFT MT103 for foreign-currency payments and through Payments Canada's High Value Clearing System (HVCS) for large Canadian-dollar wire payments. For commercial letters of credit, the issuing Canadian bank will use SWIFT MT700 series messages to the beneficiary's bank. The preferred clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for USD, EUR, GBP, or cross-currency payments; (ii) HVCS for CAD high-value payments; (iii) Payments Canada's Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for standard low-value CAD ACH / direct payroll disbursements; (iv) Fedwire and CHIPS for USD central-currency settlements via correspondent banks; and (v) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600, with the related shipping and acceptance documents presented through the advising bank. Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties under the Income Tax Act

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (including withholding) under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for willful evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR may deny tax benefits; penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit; potential reassessment and interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in the arrangement; personal liability for tax amounts.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for entities; criminal penalties for deliberate non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly failed to comply; travel bans and imprisonment for money laundering offenses.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments, penalties up to 10% of the net adjustment (if gross negligence), and interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they participated in the transfer pricing mispricing; civil restitution and potential criminal charges for tax evasion.
