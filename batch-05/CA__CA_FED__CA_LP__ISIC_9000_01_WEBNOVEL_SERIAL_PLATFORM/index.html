Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration and digital services portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html; GST/HST account administration through CRA My Business Account (MyBA); Quebec-specific GST/HST and QST matters also sit with Revenu Québec where the LP has a Quebec establishment.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and applicable program accounts by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. The partnership must be registered in the name of the LP itself, with the general partner acting as the authorized representative. For GST/HST, the LP must file Form RC1 or apply through BRO selecting the GST/HST 'RT' program account; effective 1 July 2021, a simplified non-resident registration process applies to foreign suppliers of digital services (including webnovel subscription platforms and in-app digital content sold via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing) where no Canadian presence is required. Supporting documents include the executed limited partnership agreement, provincial certificate of limited partnership registration, foreign certificate of existence or good standing, and identity/authorization evidence for the general partner. Upon issuance, the BN format is a 9-digit business number plus a two-letter program identifier (e.g., 123456789 RT0001).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: The limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax; it is not a taxpayer and files an information return (T5013 Summary and T5013 Supplementary slips). Income, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners, including non-resident limited partners, who are subject to Canadian tax on their allocated share of Canadian-source income and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on distributions. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is treated as a distinct 'person' under the Excise Tax Act and may register, collect, and remit GST/HST in its own name; the GST/HST registration of the partnership does not extend to the partners individually. — A Canadian limited partnership must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) and applicable program accounts by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or via the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The limited partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax; it is not a taxpayer and files an information return (T5013 Summary and T5013 Supplementary slips). Income, losses, and tax credits flow through to partners, including non-resident limited partners, who are subject to Canadian tax on their allocated share of Canadian-source income and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on distributions. For GST/HST purposes, the LP is treated as a distinct 'person' under the Excise Tax Act and may register, collect, and remit GST/HST in its own name; the GST/HST registration of the partnership does not extend to the partners individually.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections include: (i) fiscal-period election under Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 249.1(3) to adopt a non-calendar partnership fiscal period; (ii) GST/HST Quick Method election under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 72 for eligible registrants; (iii) partnership income-allocation elections under ITA s. 96; (iv) election under ITA subsection 96(3) for a specified member's share; (v) hybrid mismatch and foreign affiliate elections under ITA ss. 91-94 and regulation 5907 to reduce double taxation or recharacterize payments where non-resident partners are involved; and (vi) agent-principal disclosures for platform sales through Apple App Store and Google Play to ensure the correct place-of-supply treatment and avoid duplicate GST/HST registration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per calendar quarter or cumulatively over four consecutive calendar quarters (the small supplier threshold). For non-resident suppliers of digital services and intangible personal property made in Canada (including webnovel serial subscriptions and in-app purchases through Apple IAP and Google Play), the registration requirement triggers when Canadian-source taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any 12-month rolling period, effective 1 July 2021. Once registered, the registrant remains liable to collect GST/HST on all taxable supplies made in Canada, with no re-application of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ETA Schedule VI applies to: (i) goods exported by the supplier (section 1); (ii) services supplied to a non-resident individual or a non-resident business where the service is not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada and not supplied to a consumer in Canada (section 7); and (iii) intangible personal property (IPP), including digital webnovel content licences and subscription access, supplied to a non-resident recipient where the place-of-supply rules under ETA s. 14.1 and SOR/2021-152 determine the supply is made outside Canada. For sales routed through Apple App Store and Google Play Billing, the LP must ensure that the platform arrangement is documented such that the LP is the supplier of the underlying digital subscription content to the non-resident end-user, or alternatively that the app store operator is the disclosed agent, to sustain a 0% GST/HST export treatment. Sales to Canadian end-users remain taxable at the applicable GST/HST rate.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (B13A) or Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) electronic filing confirmation, only for physical media or goods exported; not applicable to pure digital web-fiction downloads, in which case a written technical export memorandum should be retained
  • Commercial invoice or digital sales invoice evidencing the 0-rated supply of webnovel content and subscription access
  • Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console transaction and proceeds reports (weekly or monthly), including SKU-level sales by storefront, evidencing non-resident end-user billings
  • Digital evidence of non-resident place of supply, including subscriber IP-address country logs, billing addresses, and platform storefront country codes
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX advice (SWIFT/MT103 or local Canadian bank credit memo) evidencing foreign-source proceeds repatriated to the Canadian LP bank account within the applicable period

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or mandatory digital stamping regime exists at the federal level in Canada. However, registrants must issue GST/HST-compliant invoices containing the supplier's name, BN, date, and tax treatment where required under ETA s. 223 and GST/HST Memorandum 15-1. Returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST Registry XML/API integration) where taxable turnover exceeds the CRA electronic filing threshold, and all underlying electronic records, platform sales reports from Apple/Google, payment processor data, and general ledgers must be retained for six years. Non-resident digital platform operators are also subject to CRA record-keeping and information-reporting expectations, including seller-income reporting under international exchange-of-information frameworks as applicable.

The CRA may audit the LP's GST/HST returns and reassess denied zero-rated export claims within the normal four-year reassessment period, extended to six or ten years where misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud is established or where the taxpayer waives the limitation period. Where zero-rating is denied, the LP is liable for the uncollected GST/HST at the full rate (up to 15% in HST provinces), compounded daily interest (currently approximately 9% annually and variably adjusted), and a penalty of 25% of the tax evaded under ETA subsection 280(1)(a); gross-negligence penalties under ITA subsection 163.2(2) may also apply. Criminal prosecution is available under ETA ss. 327-330 and ITA s. 239 for wilful evasion or false documentation. Retrospective clawback of zero-rating may additionally trigger partner-level reassessments through T5013 allocations, Part XIII withholding on deemed or actual distributions to non-resident partners, and a denial of refunds under ETA ss. 261-262 for unsubstantiated export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency with full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Currency Act, the Bank of Canada Act, and Canada's alignment with IMF Article VIII obligations. Foreign exchange controls are not imposed; profit remittances, dividends, interest, and royalties may be transferred abroad without exchange-control consent, subject only to general tax withholding and AML reporting. No statutory approval is required for the conversion of Canadian dollars (CAD) into foreign currency or vice versa, and no surrender of export proceeds is mandated by the Bank of Canada or any other federal authority.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The federal legal framework (principally the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act) does not prescribe any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts, including digital service export proceeds derived from subscription and web-novel platform revenues. Exporters are free to retain, convert, or remit foreign currency at their discretion, subject to normal banking documentation and FINTRAC transaction reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), assisted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential supervision and FINTRAC for anti-money laundering enforcement

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with significant legal constraints. Non-resident offshore digital EMIs may serve the Canadian webnovel platform only after complying with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which requires registration with the Bank of Canada for any payment service provider that offers payment services to Canadian users. Alternatively, an offshore EMI may partner with a Canadian-registered payments processor or rely on global BIN sponsorship through a Canadian chartered bank. Direct virtual-account operation in CAD without local registration or a licensed partner is not permissible.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection routing for non-resident limited partner contributions is executed by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent FEDWIRE-transparent rail using the bank-allocated CAD account and unique client reference. The wire must indicate the partnership legal name and the purpose of the transfer as a capital contribution (e.g., 'LIMITED PARTNER CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION'). The receiving bank will require (i) an executed LP agreement, (ii) the partnership's CRA business number, (iii) ultimate beneficial owner identification, (iv) source-of-funds documentary evidence, and (v) a completed wire instruction authorization form. Canadian banks will verify compliance with FINTRAC provisions and may impose threshold reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.Outbound trade disbursements for subscription revenue, author royalties, and freelancer payments are typically cleared via SWIFT MT103 for single-cross-border wires and through bank-hosted batch APIs for repetitive vendor payments. Traditional Commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) are not ordinarily suited to digital-content service payments; their use is limited to large fixed-cost procurement contracts.
