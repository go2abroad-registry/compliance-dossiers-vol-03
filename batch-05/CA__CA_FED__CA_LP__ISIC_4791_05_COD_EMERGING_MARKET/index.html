Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Provincial Register of Limited Partnerships (e.g., Ontario Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery) for statutory creation; Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Business Registration Online for CA_FED federal tax registration.

Governing statute

Canada has no federal limited partnership statute; formation is effected under an applicable provincial Limited Partnerships Act (e.g., Ontario Limited Partnerships Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. L.7, ss. 2, 5-9) with federal operability under the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), s. 96 (partnership taxation) and the Excise Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. E-15, s. 240 (GST/HST registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Declaration / Certificate of Limited Partnership (Form 2 under the provincial Limited Partnerships Act) together with federal Business Number (BN) registration application (CRA RC1).

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online, My Business Account portal, GST/HST NETFILE, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for export customs reporting.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian limited partnership is a 'person' under the Excise Tax Act. The foreign LP must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and, if making taxable supplies in Canada, register for GST/HST by filing Form RC1 'Request for Business Number' with CRA or using Business Registration Online. The application must identify the partnership, the local/agent contact for service, and all partners. Registration becomes mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. No minimum capital is required, and non-resident ownership is permitted; however, at least one partner must be resident or the partnership must appoint a CRA-recognized representative with a Canadian address for service of notices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Territorial_Scope — the LP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act; income and losses flow through to partners, and non-resident partners are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source business income attributable to the partnership's permanent establishment. For GST/HST purposes, the partnership itself is the registrant rather than the individual partners.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election; the LP's pass-through classification is mandatory. For GST/HST, the partners may elect under subsection 273(1) of the Excise Tax Act that the partnership's commercial activities be treated as a designated joint venture and reported by one partner, and the partnership itself may be registered as a separate person. For income tax, the partnership may elect a non-standard fiscal period under subsection 249.1(5) of the Income Tax Act. Cross-border hybrid mismatch allocations are constrained by sections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act, which may deny deductions where the partner's jurisdiction treats the LP as a non-transparent entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada in a single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when supplied and exported from Canada as soon as is reasonably possible after the supply, under a contract of sale, with the exporter retaining documentary proof that the goods were shipped by commercial carrier to a foreign address (Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V). For COD mail-order sales to an emerging-market buyer, the Canadian LP must show that title and control of tangible goods passed to the non-resident buyer before use in Canada and that the shipment left Canada through customs. Ancillary freight or insurance services directly linked to the exported goods may also be zero-rated when included in the same supply.

  • Canadian Export Declaration filed through CBSA (Form B13A for legacy paper filing or the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) electronic declaration, including the Cargo Control Number)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods, terms of sale, and COD amount
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Carrier or freight-forwarder certificate of export / proof of delivery at the foreign destination
  • Destination-country customs import entry or clearance receipt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-exchange/wire transfer receipt evidencing COD settlement

No federal mandate exists for real-time fiscal clearance, API-linked e-invoicing, or digital stamping in Canada. The CRA requires that invoices and export records be issued and retained in an accessible format, and registrants meeting prescribed revenue thresholds must file GST/HST returns electronically through GST/HST NETFILE or compatible software. E-invoicing may be exchanged voluntarily through the Pan-Canadian Peppol network, but there is no pre-clearance tax-authority approval of individual invoices before export.

CRA treats export zero-rating as a high-risk area. If the LP fails to retain the prescribed export evidence or the goods are deemed not to have been exported, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 5% GST/HST. CRA may reassess outside the normal four-year period for negligence or fraud, assess unpaid tax plus interest, deny documentary refund claims, and impose civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act for false statements or omissions. Willful tax evasion or knowingly making false or deceptive records may result in criminal prosecution and further fines and imprisonment. Export records, including customs declarations, bills of lading, and remittance evidence, must be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange rate regime. The Foreign Exchange Control Act has been repealed, and the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) imposes no restrictions on current account or capital account transactions. Dividends, distributions, and partnership allocations to non-resident limited partners are freely remittable, subject solely to Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%, possibly treaty-reduced) and to transparent pass-through taxation of the LP. No prior regulatory approval is required for repatriation of profits or repayment of partner loans. The Investment Canada Act applies only to acquisitions of control of Canadian businesses above the monetary thresholds; portfolio flows and routine trade payments are unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation timeline, or queuing mechanism for export proceeds. A Canadian Limited Partnership may hold foreign currency receivables (including COD collections from emerging-market logistics partners) in domestic foreign-currency bank accounts, offshore accounts, or corporate treasury accounts without central bank reporting, except general FINTRAC reporting obligations (e.g., large virtual currency transactions, EFT reports over C$10,000, or terrorist property reporting).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) with shared authority over foreign exchange and monetary policy; prudential regulation of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); international payments infrastructure and the Lynx system operated under Payments Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High, with legal constraints. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident limited partners (foreign beneficial owners) holding disproportionate capital in the partnership

Root cause: Non-resident limited partners (foreign beneficial owners) holding disproportionate capital in the partnership

Defense evidence: Current limited partnership registration certificate; complete chain of ownership to ultimate natural persons; beneficial ownership attestation listing any partner holding 25% or more; notarized partnership agreement; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds documentation (bank statements, audited financials, tax returns); Canadian criminal record check and politically exposed person (PEP) searches for each partner; if the LP is used as an investment vehicle, a clear business plan evidencing the mail-order retail purpose.
Cash-on-delivery (COD) collections remitted by third-party logistics partners in emerging markets, triggering unexplained third-party payment flags

Root cause: Cash-on-delivery (COD) collections remitted by third-party logistics partners in emerging markets, triggering unexplained third-party payment flags

Defense evidence: Executed master logistics services agreement (with the courier) explicitly authorizing COD collection and remittance; detailed COD settlement statements with daily transaction reconciliation; expectation letter describing annual volume, average ticket size, and receiving markets; evidence of underlying customer orders (order IDs, delivery confirmations); AML/CFT policies and risk assessment of the LP; periodic independent audit confirmation of settlement flows.
High-risk geographic exposure to emerging-market jurisdictions with heightened sanctions or FATF monitoring

Root cause: High-risk geographic exposure to emerging-market jurisdictions with heightened sanctions or FATF monitoring

Defense evidence: Written country risk rating performed by the LP; sanctions screening (OFAC, UN, Canada sanctions list) of all counterparties; transaction monitoring program with geo-IP verification of customers; evidence that no COD deliveries are made to comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions; engagement of a reputable logistics partner with its own compliance program; capital injection trail proving legitimate 4791 e-commerce operations.
Unusually high velocity and cash-equivalent deposits through the operating account without invoice-backed trade documents

Root cause: Unusually high velocity and cash-equivalent deposits through the operating account without invoice-backed trade documents

Defense evidence: Monthly COD settlement summaries mapped to sales orders; commercial invoices, customs declarations, and postal receipt evidence; ERP-generated tax reports; audited financial statements of the LP (or its general partner); letter from a licensed Canadian accountant confirming the bookkeeping structure; detailed org chart demonstrating segregation of duties for collections and disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the General Partner or limited partners into the LP's Canadian operating account are executed via SWIFT MT103 (or domestic Lynx transfer for CAD) with purpose code 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'PARTNER LOAN'. Verification protocol includes a capital call notice, partner resolution, signed subscription agreement, and remittance slip referencing the unique capital call number. FINTRAC requires an approved EFT report for any single wire of C$10,000 or more; banks also apply standard bank secrecy act checks.
