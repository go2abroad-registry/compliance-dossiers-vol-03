Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/taxes

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Limited Partnership that commences business in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Registration is completed electronically through the Business Registration Online service or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The general partner, or a designated representative, must provide partnership details, principal place of business, and the nature of the Canadian business activity. If the partnership expects to make taxable supplies in Canada, a separate GST/HST account is established under the same BN. Non-resident entities may register for GST/HST without a Canadian permanent establishment. The BN is used for all federal and provincial tax remittances, payroll, import/export, and GST/HST purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box elective regime. A Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Canadian tax purposes; income is allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level. Non-resident partners may elect under section 216 of the Income Tax Act to be taxed on a net Canadian-source business income basis instead of being subject to Part XIII withholding on gross distributions. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under sections 18.4 to 18.6 of the Income Tax Act for payments giving rise to deduction/non-inclusion mismatches. Partnerships may also elect under subsection 34(1) to adopt a non-calendar fiscal year under specified conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$30,000 CAD in worldwide taxable supplies over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (trailing twelve months); registration is mandatory once exceeded, with voluntary registration available for persons making taxable supplies in Canada

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods and services that are exported are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act. Goods qualify where possession is transferred to a non-resident and the goods are exported as soon as practicable. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated when the service is performed wholly outside Canada, or when all consideration is reasonably attributable to an export. Electronically delivered software, SaaS, and financial data analytics services provided to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada generally qualify for zero-rating, subject to substantive documentary evidence and absence of use in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian Automated Export Declaration or equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading or other international shipping/transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign exchange record proving payment in foreign currency
  • Contract, invoice, and technical service description confirming foreign recipient and offshore use

Canada has no mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping regime. Taxpayers must retain all supporting records, issued invoices, and receipts in a legible business format. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) if required, and payments may be made via CRA online banking, My Payment, or financial institution. Registered suppliers must issue invoices showing supplier business number, date, description, and tax amount.

The CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess at the standard GST/HST rate where documentation is insufficient or the export supply cannot be substantiated. Penalties include interest on unremitted tax and penalties under the Excise Tax Act, including section 280 (failure to remit) and section 285 (false statements/omissions) of up to 25% of the tax amount, increased to 50% for gross negligence. Recaptured input tax credits may be imposed under subsection 296(1.1). Persons making false or deceptive export claims may face additional civil penalties and criminal prosecution under section 327, including fines and imprisonment. Records must be retained for six years to support the zero-rated treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account purposes. Canada operates under IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no exchange-control restrictions on inward or outward fund transfers, capital contributions, loan repayments, interest, dividends, or partnership distributions. Non-resident partners of a Canadian Limited Partnership may freely repatriate capital and profits without Bank of Canada approval. The only legal gates are (i) Part XIII withholding or Part I tax allocations on Canadian-source income of non-resident partners, (ii) FINTRAC reporting thresholds for international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, and (iii) standard AML/KYC source-of-funds verification. There are no administrative repatriation hurdles, licensed dealer mandates, or prior-approval requirements for converting CAD to foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for converting export receipts into CAD. SaaS/fintech exporters and LPs may invoice in CAD, USD, EUR, or any freely chosen currency and may retain foreign-currency balances in domestic foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts without restriction. Transparency obligations apply exclusively: international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC within 5 business days of the transaction, and large physical-cash transactions of CAD 10,000 or more (where they occur) must be reported within 15 days. Export documentation (e.g., G13 Canadian Export Declaration) is required only for physical goods crossing the border; software subscriptions and digital services are not subject to surrender or customs conversion rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary authority and retail payment system regulator; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — prudential regulator of federally chartered banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) — administrator of cross-border EFT and AML/CFT reporting. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration; exchange policy is conducted jointly by the Bank of Canada and the Department of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary liquidity and disbursement rail; LOW as a primary treasury account. Legal constraints: (i) foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury) that provide payment services to Canadian residents are treated as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which requires registration with the Bank of Canada; (ii) any entity transmitting funds in Canada must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and appoint a compliance officer, implement a compliance regime, and report EFTs over CAD 10,000; (iii) operating an unregistered offshore EMI for the LP's treasury would trigger OSFI, FINTRAC, and bank-on-boarding red flags when funds are swept to a Canadian Schedule I account; (iv) the fintech scope (ISIC 6201, NAICS 511210) itself does not require a PSP license, so the LP should treat an offshore EMI only as a segregated vendor-payout instrument with full audit trail, never as the custodial liquidity hub.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign partner capital contributions should be wired via SWIFT MT103 in CAD or USD to the LP's designated operating or escrow account at a Schedule I bank. Recommended reference lines: 'CAP CONTRIBUTION [LP legal name] / [partner name] / [capital call date]'. Canada imposes no exchange-control approval, but the receiving bank must identify the ultimate originator under FINTRAC EFT transparency rules. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax rate under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA). A Canadian limited partnership is fiscally transparent; the partnership itself is generally not liable for federal income tax. Each partner accounts for its allocated share of partnership income and pays tax at the applicable corporate rate (15% federal plus provincial general rate) or personal graduated rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII withholding tax at 25% applies to dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Under the Canada-US treaty the rate is 15% generally, 5% for a corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of voting shares, and 0% for certain qualifying pension funds. A limited partnership distribution is not a dividend unless it is recharacterized as a deemed dividend by the CRA; actual partnership distributions of accumulated capital are generally not subject to Part XIII withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 must be filed when the aggregate fair market value of all transactions with a non-resident related party or a specified non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. A Master File and Local File are not statutorily required forms, but Canada's 'Complete and Accurate' contemporaneous documentation requirement under s. 247(4) applies to all cross-border transactions with non-residents, with no lower threshold when the CRA requests documentation. A Country-by-Country Report is required for a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the Cayman Islands or BVI ESR. Instead, the ITA General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245) and the MLI Principal Purpose Test disallow tax benefits from transactions lacking genuine commercial substance. A limited partnership engaged in financial portfolio tracking must maintain real substance in Canada: a physical or virtual local office, Canadian employees or contractors performing development and support, locally maintained software and client data, and meaningful board or general-partner decisions in Canada. If the partnership is structured solely to access treaty reduced rates or to shift income to a zero-tax jurisdiction, the CRA will recharacterize the income and impose gross negligence penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s. 238(1) of the ITA: fine from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and restitution orders. Civil consequence: gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax; GST/HST fines up to CAD 100,000 plus daily penalties.

Director personal liability: CRA may pierce the LP veil under s. 160 if assets have been transferred to a non-arm's length party for less than fair market value. General partners and directors of a corporate general partner can be personally assessed for unpaid withholding taxes and penalties. Travel bans may be imposed when the tax debt exceeds CAD 50,000 and the taxpayer is attempting to leave Canada without arranging payment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization: income may be reallocated to the Canadian general partner, additional Part I tax assessed at the general corporate rate, Part XIII tax on deemed dividends, a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on adjustments exceeding CAD 100,000, and denial of treaty relief under the MLI PPT. Penalty under s. 163(2) for gross negligence: 50% of understated tax. If factual evidence shows a sham, criminal fraud charges may be filed.

Director personal liability: General partners are jointly and severally liable for all partnership debts under provincial partnership law. Directors of a corporate general partner may be assessed personally for the partnership's failure to withhold under Part XIII and for intentional suppression of revenue. Under s. 380(1) of the Criminal Code, defrauding the government could lead to imprisonment for up to 14 years. A travel ban can be issued under the Passport Act when the tax debt exceeds CAD 200,000.
Anti-Money-Laundering / Verification Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), FINTRAC may impose an administrative monetary penalty of up to CAD 2,000,000 for repeat violations; non-compliance with record-keeping and client identification requirements triggers a separate CRA audit risk. If the platform is classified as an MSB because it facilitates fiat conversion, failure to register with FINTRAC is a criminal offence with fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner who knowingly consent to AML violations are personally liable for FINTRAC penalties. A director who assists in laundering funds derived from instrumentalities of crime commits an offence under s. 462.31 of the Criminal Code, punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years. Travel bans may be imposed where the director has a CRA debt exceeding CAD 50,000 and has made irregular financial transfers.
