Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal: CRA My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE portal; British Columbia provincial sales tax administration: BC Ministry of Finance (PST – Consumer Taxation Branch).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without permanent establishment in Canada registers as a non-resident corporation for GST/HST by obtaining a 9-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) followed by one or two program identifier codes (e.g., 'RT' for GST/HST). Procedure: (1) Submit CRA Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' plus Form RC4058 'Application to register a non-resident’s GST/HST account' if required; (2) Provide the corporation’s legal name, governing jurisdiction, registered/head office address, names of directors or senior officers, foreign tax identification reference, and business activity codes; (3) Register through CRA's Business Registry online service under the 'Non-resident' category or by mailing/faxing to the International Tax Services Office; (4) Upon issuance, the BN serves as the federal tax identifier for income and payroll accounts; a separate PST number may be required from BC via BC eTaxService if taxable BC supplies occur; (5) Retain the BN, official letter, and registration date as evidence of Canadian tax identity for banking and TikTok/creator payout compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation_in_Canada_with_Foreign_Pass_Through_Eligibility: Under Canada's Income Tax Act, a BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is a legal corporation fully subject to federal Part I corporate tax, BC income tax, and GST/HST obligations; it is not treated as a pass-through by Canadian domestic law. For a single-member or parent-controlled ULC, foreign (especially U.S.) tax law may check-the-box and treat it as a disregarded entity or partnership for foreign tax purposes, but this does not alter Canadian domestic entity-level taxation; BC ULC remains an 'authorized foreign corporation' if extra-provincially registered and is subject to Part I tax on worldwide taxable income if resident, or on Canadian-source income if non-resident. Territorial scope: net income is allocated under Canada's source-residence rules; non-resident controlled ULC carries reduced Part I compliance but full Canadian-situs tax exposure.

Available election forms

No Canadian federal 'check-the-box' or pass-through election exists; elections relevant to this structure include: (1) Foreign tax classification elections (e.g., U.S. Form 8832 making a ULC a disregarded entity), which are constitutional status elections under foreign law, not Canadian tax elections; (2) Controlled Foreign Affiliate election under subsection 93(2) for surplus remittances; (3) Treaty-based substitution or hybrid mismatch elections related to Canadian hybrid arrangements under ITA subsection 18(4) and 18(5), and comparable BC provisions, applicable only to financing/mixed income; (4) GST/HST 'election' to have a reporting period of one year instead of quarterly for nil or sizable refund positions; (5) For a non-resident ULC, a Part XIII waiver election for reduced withholding on service fees through a tax treaty is possible; (6) Intra-group amalgamation or wind-up election under ITA section 88 to step up asset basis in a corporate reorganization of the ULC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST: registration is mandatory for any person, including a non-resident, that makes taxable supplies in Canada and whose 'worldwide annual gross revenue' of taxable supplies exceeds CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter and in the prior four consecutive calendar quarters, except for non-resident vendors that only supply zero-rated services (who may register voluntarily); for BC PST: no universal registration threshold for foreign suppliers of advertising or talent agency services unless the service is expressly listed under PSTA Schedule 1 or the supplier has BC presence; a PST vendor registration is triggered when the business makes taxable sales of tangible personal property or enumerated taxable services in BC, but pure export of agency services is generally outside PST scope.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An MCN agency that supplies creator management, digital marketing, TikTok campaign facilitation, and advertising agency services to a non-resident recipient can claim zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 of the Excise Tax Act (supply of a service to a non-resident) provided: (a) the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant; (b) the service is not performed wholly or partly in respect of real property situated in Canada; (c) the service is not performed in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed unless the property is exported by the recipient before the service is performed; (d) the supply does not appear to be supplied for consumption, use, or enjoyment in Canada wholly or partly; and (e) the service is not packaged in Canada for direct identification with exported goods. Creator-education and platform-commission services produced abroad for TikTok's global MCN division, delivered remotely to non-resident clients, and settled in foreign currency qualify as zero-rated export services. Royalties and platform fees from non-resident creators may be 'exported' and entitled to 0% HST if conditions concerning Canadian situs and use are met. VAT-refund mechanics: zero-rated supplies do not appear on the GST/HST net tax calculation; input tax credits for related provincial costs (e.g., BC office, software, and contractor services) remain claimable on the HST portion, subject to CRA's matching with audited export contracts.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration / Canada Export Reporting System declaration (where tangible promotional goods or equipment are moved outside Canada, including CERS unique shipment identifier)
  • Original commercial invoice describing creator-agency services and cross-border royalty flows, with explicit zero-rated supply indication
  • Bill of Lading, house air waybill, or through-bill issued for exported physical media/merchandise
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT MT103 confirmation evidencing receipt of proceeds in Canada within the prescribed FX repatriation window
  • Audited contract bundle with TikTok's non-resident MCN division or foreign creator, together with platform payout statements (TikTok Creator Payout / PSP statements) used to prove the non-resident purchase order
  • Certificate of non-residency (Canadian Form R or CD-ROM for treaty position) for the recipient, if applicable
  • Statement of services performed outside Canada and foreign consumption attestation, if the zero-rating is based on the non-resident service rule

Canada does not mandate a centralized real-time e-invoice fiscal clearance system; CRA accepts invoices in electronic form provided they are retrievable, legible, and include prescribed GST/HST invoice fields (vendor name/BN, date, description, HST rate/amount, and consideration). No national digital stamping requirement exists. Businesses may use CRA-approved XML, PDF, or ERP-based e-invoicing; British Columbia's PST regime likewise has no pre-clearance e-invoicing mandate. The chief compliance duty is complete underlying books and records within 7 years of the fiscal year, plus an obligation to provide an 'Information Return for Non-Resident GST/HST registrants' (RC460) when requested.

CRA audit scrutiny is intensive on 'exported digital services' for MCN agencies: the burden lies on the supplier to prove each zero-rated supply falls outside Canadian consumption and that proceeds were received within the commercial/fiscal period; false zero-rating leads to retrospective reassessment of GST/HST at 5/12 or 12/112 of revenue, denial/disallowance of all related input tax credits, and monetary penalties under Sections 280 and 285 of the Excise Tax Act. Knowingly making or participating in a false GST/HST statement triggers penalties up to the greater of CAD 100,000 or twice the tax evaded, plus civil interest, suspension of registration for non-residents, and director liability under Section 323. Criminal evasion, including fraudulent export declarations, is punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment and uncapped fines on indictment; the CRA may also issue a 'suspected fraudulent export scheme' recommendation, clawback VAT refunds, and disgorge claimed input tax credits for the past audit period (normally 4-year reassessment window; greater in cases of gross negligence).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with a free-floating Canadian dollar. No administrative FX license, exchange control approval, or profit-remittance permit is required for inward equity injections, creator payouts, or dividend distributions. The only compliance frictions are: (i) FINTRAC reporting of international or domestic electronic fund transfers (EFTs) at or above CAD 10,000; (ii) Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (25% statutory rate, treaty-reduced to 15% or 5% under Article X of the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention); and (iii) maintaining proper purpose codes and underlying agreements for all cross-border wire transfers. The Canadian dollar is fully fungible across CAD, USD, and major foreign currencies without conversion quotas.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rules or mandatory conversion ratios exist in Canada. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1952, and Canada has never re-imposed surrender obligations. The BC ULC may freely retain TikTok creator-payout revenues, MCN agency commissions, and other export service receipts in CAD, USD, or any foreign currency of its choosing, subject only to standard bank-level reporting of incoming international wires at or above CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating jointly with the Minister of Finance under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) and the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52). Foreign exchange policy is administered by the Department of Finance Canada, while the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises chartered banks and FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for AML/ATF compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH with enforceable legal constraints. Wise (Wise Payments Limited operating through its FINTRAC-registered Canadian money service business branch) and Airwallex (Canadian-registered MSB) are viable overlay rails for TikTok Creator Payout aggregation and Wise B2B mass disbursements. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not Schedule I banks under the Bank Act (Canada); deposits are not CDIC-insured and may be held in client trust accounts with commingling risk; (ii) EMIs must be registered under the PCMLTFA and are subject to FINTRAC supervision, requiring the ULC to maintain its own transactional monitoring and reporting interface; (iii) platform terms may exclude or restrict certain creator categories, crypto-adjacent traffic, or high-volume payout patterns; and (iv) treasury governance must cap EMI float at a prudent operating level (e.g., CAD 250,000) with automated daily sweeping to a Tier-1 chartered bank concentration account.
