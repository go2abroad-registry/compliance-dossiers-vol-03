Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; GST/HST and corporate income tax registrations are managed through CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account portals.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity not otherwise incorporated in Canada may register directly with the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) and, where applicable, a GST/HST program account or other program accounts (e.g., import/export, payroll). The standard procedure is: (1) submit Form RC1 Appendix B (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or use CRA's Business Registration Online for eligible entities; (2) provide the entity's legal name, country of incorporation, registered office or authorized representative address within Canada or abroad, and details of the commercial activity in Canada; (3) CRA will issue the BN and a separate 9-character program identifier (RT for GST/HST, RM for income tax, RC for payroll, etc.) through the CRA business account or by written confirmation; (4) the foreign entity must appoint an authorized representative or Canadian agent if no permanent establishment exists, and must maintain accurate books and records in either English or French for CRA audit access. The BN serves as the statutory tax identifier; there is no separate domestic TIN for foreign entities. Registration for GST/HST is mandatory where the entity makes taxable supplies in Canada and is not a small supplier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' pass-through election; every business corporation, including a federal or provincial entity, is automatically taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act. Available statutory elections relevant to tax classification include: (a) an election for a non-resident corporation to file using a functional currency under subsection 261(2) of the Income Tax Act; (b) associated-corporation elections to allocate the CCPC small-business deduction limit among related corporations; (c) group GST/HST election under sections 239-240 of the Excise Tax Act (qualifying persons can file combined GST/HST returns); and (d) elections available to non-resident corporations to deem a permanent establishment in a particular province for allocating federal/provincial income tax. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act and related regulations may recharacterize certain payments, instruments, or permanent establishments for Canadian withholding tax and deduction purposes, but they do not permit an elective flow-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — a person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant once worldwide taxable supplies from commercial activity in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters; once registered, the threshold no longer limits eligibility.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated for GST/HST purposes under section 23 of the Excise Tax Act where the goods are supplied for export and are actually exported from Canada in accordance with the Export and Import of Goods Regulations. The supplier must take reasonable measures to confirm export, including retaining transport documentation. A supply of services provided to a non-resident is generally zero-rated if the services are performed wholly outside Canada or are part of exported goods, subject to exceptions under the Off-shoring rules. In addition, certain qualifying medical devices may be zero-rated as domestic supplies under Schedule VI, Part II, but exported surgical suture products are eligible for zero-rating based on the export supply rules, irrespective of domestic classification. The exporter will claim the 0% rate on the GST/HST return and may claim input tax credits/refunds for attributable inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Border Services Agency Form B13/CAED or approved electronic export declaration via CBSA ACI/eManifest or Canadian Export Reporting System).
  • Bill of Lading or Equivalent International Transport Document.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation of foreign currency receipt) or equivalent SWIFT MT103 / TT wire documentation evidencing payment transfer into Canada.
  • Commercial Invoice with FOB/Incoterms and product description.
  • Packing List and purchase order or sales contract supporting the exported nature of the goods.

Canada does not maintain a mandatory real-time government fiscal clearance, clearance API, or digital stamping regime under the Excise Tax Act. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices with prescribed information under subsection 221(1) and retain all records under section 286, including electronic records. Electronic invoicing is permissible where the invoice format is accessible and reproducible for CRA examination; no CRA pre-approval or stamping is required. Registrants with large compliance profiles may be required by CRA to use electronic GST/HST NETFILE and to provide specified record and payment timeline responses, but there is no transactional government clearance of each invoice.

CRA may deny zero-rating retroactively if the exporter cannot, within a reasonable audit request period, substantiate that the goods were actually exported and that payment was received as described. False or fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to reassessment within the normal reassessment period of four years, extended to 10 years or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, negligence, or fraud. Penalties include gross-negligence penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act not exceeding the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the amount used in the false statement for larger claims, as adjusted; failure-to-collect penalties; failure-to-file penalties; and interest compounded daily. There is also an explicit criminal-fraud offence regime that may expose responsible officers to conviction fines of up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment for controlling minds, plus CRA may issue formal quantitative penalties under section 280 for wilful participation in false statements. CRA audits export zero-rating claims stringently for artificial export structures, transactions with related non-residents, and circular payment flows; recaptured tax, penalty interest, and loss of refunds are the primary clawback consequences.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Floating CAD exchange rate regime with unrestricted current account convertibility; capital account liberalized subject to Investment Canada Act foreign investment review thresholds and national security reviews. Profit remittance permitted without prior approval for most corporate distributions, subject only to tax withholding and beneficial ownership reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds required under Canadian law. Voluntary repatriation encouraged; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts. Banks must file FX transaction reports for amounts exceeding CAD 10,000; export receipts require CRA T1134 reporting for tax compliance.

Declare the treaty country of residence of each non-resident shareholder and attach valid Certificates of Residence (TRC); for management fees paid to non-residents, withhold 25% Part XIII tax under paragraph 212(1)(a) unless a treaty or arm's-length exemption applies. Late filing: 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per full month of default for up to 12 months; increased to 10% plus the monthly 1% where CRA issued a demand to file. Failure to report income: 10% penalty on the unreported amount (s.162(7)). Late payment interest accrues daily at the prescribed rate (approximately 9%-10% per annum in 2025). Criminal liability under s.238: fine of CAD 1,000 to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months for non-filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of all specified non-arm's-length cross-border transactions in a taxation year (Income Tax Act s.247(4)-(7)); above this threshold the taxpayer must prepare contemporaneous documentation (Local File and Master File harmonized with OECD Chapter V) by the T2 statutory filing date to evidence arm's-length pricing and to secure penalty relief.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations for ordinary manufacturing companies, but economic substance is enforced through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245) re-characterizing transactions lacking genuine business purpose; (ii) treaty limitation-on-benefits (LOB) and OECD Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test (PPT) denying DTA rates to conduit entities without substantive activity; (iii) the Global Minimum Tax Act (2024) imposing a 15% top-up tax on MNE groups with consolidated revenues exceeding EUR 750 million; and (iv) foreign accrual property income (FAPI) rules taxing passive income of controlled foreign affiliates. The Canadian assembly plant must maintain employees, premises, production equipment, R&D decision-making, and contractual risk in Canada to survive substance scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements (Income Tax Act s.239)

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (s.163(2)). Criminal conviction on indictment: fine of 100%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction: fine of 50%-100% and imprisonment up to 2 years. CRA may also impose the 10% repeated-failure penalty and daily compound interest.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who directed, authorized, acquiesced in, or participated in the offence are personally guilty under s.242 and may be prosecuted; directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding under s.227.1; conviction triggers asset freeze orders, arrest warrants, and border alerts preventing international travel.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing (Income Tax Act s.247)

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment at arm's-length prices; transfer-pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue and CAD 5,000,000; interest accrues from the original balance-due day. Foreign tax credit and Part XIII treatment are also applied to the corrected amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign deliberately false transfer-pricing documentation can be treated as accessories under s.239; on conviction they face fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil recourse for CRA may attach to director assets where the corporation is stripped or dissolved (ss.160 and 227.1).
Shell Structure / Treaty Abuse Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR application (s.245) denying the tax benefit entirely with a 25% penalty on the tax benefit; treaty denial under PPT/LOB resulting in reinstatement of the full 25% Part XIII withholding and a 25% s.163(2) penalty on any unreported distribution; repeated treaty-shopping structures can trigger the new 15% global minimum tax top-up.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Canadian operating company who structured or approved the abusive conduit are personally liable for unremitted Part XIII withholding and GST/HST under s.227.1 and s.323 of the Excise Tax Act; criminal charges under s.239 are available where the arrangement was knowingly misrepresented; CRA may obtain ex parte injunction freezing director assets and imposing travel restrictions.
AML / Customs Misdeclaration on Surgical Imports

Statutory sanction: Under the Customs Act (s.160): fine up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment for false statements; goods may be forfeited; administrative monetary penalties under the CBSA ACI program up to CAD 25,000 per violation; non-compliant wire structuring triggers FINTRAC STR filing and suspended correspondent banking access; repeat violations at criminal scale are treated as proceeds-of-crime with confiscation of the manufacturing enterprise.

Director personal liability: Corporate officers who direct the payment structuring or sign false customs documentation can be prosecuted individually under the Customs Act and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; individual sanctions include fines up to CAD 500,000, imprisonment up to 10 years, prohibition on holding directorships, and post-conviction travel bans.
