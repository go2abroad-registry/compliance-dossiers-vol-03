Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Dual character: Canadian domestic law treats the BC ULC as a separate 'corporation' subject to federal Part I tax (15% general rate, plus 12% BC provincial corporate rate = 27% combined; no small business deduction because the ULC is non-Canadian-controlled if owned by US residents). For US federal tax purposes the ULC is an eligible entity that may elect (Form 8832) to be disregarded (single member) or transparent (partnership), aligning with the Canada-US Income Tax Convention's fiscally transparent entity rules. Because Canada taxes the ULC at entity level, cross-border structuring must be screened against Part XIII non-resident withholding (25% statutory, reduced to 5%/0-15% under treaty) and the new EIFEL interest limitation and hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4/18.6).

Available election forms

1) US check-the-box election (IRS Form 8832/SS-4 filings) treating the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for US purposes only. 2) GST/HST closely related corporation election (ETA s. 156/273) to deem supplies between the ULC and US parent or BC affiliates to be for nil consideration. 3) GST/HST voluntary registration election for non-resident supplier status where the ULC invoices an offshore affiliate. 4) ETA s. 21? (recovery of any tax paid in error) and ETA s. 191? (rebate for non-registrant foreign persons) where available. 5) Canada-US Treaty limitation-on-benefits and hybrid mismatch screens: no 'round-trip' financing that triggers ITA s. 18.4/18.6 disallowance. 6) Provincial elections: BC PST direct-pay permit election for qualifying intermediaries.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — a ULC becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) exceed C$30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 240); non-resident digital-services suppliers use the same C$30,000 threshold for the simplified registration regime (effective July 1, 2021).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 7, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act: a supply of advisory, consulting and study-abroad/immigration-placement services is zero-rated (0% GST) when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST and who is outside Canada at the time the service is performed, provided the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time performed. Services of preparing and submitting visa/study permits, program placement and consular liaison for a non-resident client are therefore zero-rated exports; services that concern Canadian real property (e.g., relocation property purchase advice) remain taxable. The ULC must obtain documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status before invoicing at 0% and must not otherwise be carrying on business in Canada in a manner that re-characterizes the supply as domestic.

  • Customs Export Declaration (none required for pure services — for any tangible collateral, an EBS/CAED or PARS export declaration referencing the transaction)
  • Bill of Lading / courier airway bill (where brochures or supporting kits are shipped; otherwise a formal Service Delivery Confirmation signed by the offshore client)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident principal

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada; the CRA operates a periodic self-assessment filing model (GST/HST return via NETFILE, GST/HST Web Forms, or EDI/API with CRA). Canada is progressively adopting the Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Framework based on the PEPPOL network; federal departments accept PEPPOL e-invoices, and BC is an active participant in the national e-invoicing governance. Taxpayers must retain source documents (ETA s. 286) including contracts, invoices, bank wires and the non-resident status evidence; CRA may issue a requirement to provide information or a compliance audit, and failure to keep adequate books can trigger ETA s. 286 penalties.

Where the CRA denies zero-rating, the ULC is assessed unremitted 5% GST on each invoice together with interest (daily compounding at prescribed rates) and penalties. ETA s. 280 imposes a civil penalty of the greater of C$100 and 25% of the tax portion attributable to false statements or omissions made knowingly or under gross negligence; CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year window in cases of misrepresentation or fraud, and criminal prosecution is possible under ETA s. 327. For fraudulent export claims, CRA may also issue a 'penalty for failure to collect tax' under ETA s. 280.1 (up to 25% of the unreported net tax), cancel refunds and apply the general irrecoverable-tax and gross-negligence rules; principals (including US shareholders of a ULC, given unlimited liability) are jointly liable for corporate VAT debts on a winding-up under BC law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully open capital account and unrestricted currency convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There is no exchange-control approval requirement for equity injections, loans, dividends, or trade-related remittances. Profit repatriation from a British Columbia ULC is permitted without prior regulatory consent; however, a non-resident shareholder will be subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends and certain payments, generally at 25% absent treaty relief. The ULC’s hybrid tax classification may also give rise to deemed-dividend consequences or treaty-treaty complications for U.S. shareholders, requiring careful structuring. Any remittance exceeding $10,000 CAD is reportable by the bank to FINTRAC, and a ULC carrying on an immigration-consulting business must maintain source-of-funds documentation for every client trust contribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline for surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into Canadian dollars. Earnings from study-abroad advisory and immigration visa services may be retained in the invoiced foreign currency or converted at the entity’s commercial discretion. Foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks are permitted, and receipts from overseas clients may be held in USD, EUR, or other acceptable currencies. Nevertheless, all cross-border receipts routed through Canadian financial institutions are subject to the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, including large-cash and large-virtual-currency transaction reporting and record-keeping obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange administration is coordinated by the Canadian Department of Finance and OSFI, with anti-money-laundering supervision by FINTRAC. There is no separate foreign exchange control authority in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, limited to ancillary payment collection and disbursement only. Canadian-regulated FinTech payment service providers must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and/or be a money services business supervised by FINTRAC. Offshore EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not licensed as Canadian chartered banks and cannot provide insured escrow accounts, statutory trust accounts, or fully compliant retainer-account infrastructure for immigration and study-abroad client funds. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection from a foreign parent or shareholder into the ULC’s Canadian operational account should be routed through SWIFT MT103 using the foreign bank’s correspondent relationship with the Canadian tier-one institution. The beneficiary account must be held in the exact legal name of the ULC, and the remittance field should state the economic purpose, for example ‘Equity subscription for CA_ULC shares’ or ‘Shareholder loan pursuant to loan agreement dated [date]’. For a shareholder loan, the ULC must execute a written loan agreement with arm’s-length commercial interest and maintain a defensible debt-to-equity ratio to prevent recharacterization as equity by the Canada Revenue Agency. Inbound capital injections exceeding $10,000 CAD will trigger FINTRAC reporting; the bank may require a board resolution, source-of-funds statement, and corporate good-standing certificate. Canada does not impose exchange-control registration or purpose-code approval, but banks may map the payment to balance-of-payments reporting categories.Outbound trade and operating disbursements from the BC ULC should be cleared using the following rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border bank-to-bank payments to tuition providers, immigration lodges, partner agencies, and suppliers; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits) for high-value conditional admissions, bulk tuition settlement, or escrow-backed educational contracts; (iii) Lynx, Canada’s high-value CAD payment system, for urgent domestic disbursements; (iv) ACH/Payment Canada’s automated settlement for low-value recurring payments; and (v) USD correspondent banking through the ULC’s USD account for U.S. institutional partners. All outbound payments should be supported by invoices, student admissions contracts, visa-application records, and an approved disbursement policy that clearly separates operating funds from client trust funds.Outright spot contracts and plain-vanilla FX forwards for CAD/USD and CAD/EUR tuition and visa fee exposure, generally offered by all tier-one Canadian banks with tenors up to 12 monthsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currency pairs where CAD or the relevant emerging-market currency is restricted, as accepted by Canadian bank treasury desks on a case-by-case credit basisCross-currency interest rate swaps for notional exchanges under shareholder loan arrangements or long-term financing structuresVanilla FX call and put options to cap procurement costs for high-volume international student placement seasonsMulti-currency notional pooling capabilities offered by Canadian tier-one banks to reduce FX conversion spreads on recurring cross-border flows

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium, limited to ancillary payment collection and disbursement only. Canadian-regulated FinTech payment service providers must be registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and/or be a money services business supervised by FINTRAC. Offshore EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not licensed as Canadian chartered banks and cannot provide insured escrow accounts, statutory trust accounts, or fully compliant retainer-account infrastructure for immigration and study-abroad client funds. A BC ULC should therefore maintain a tier-one Canadian bank account as the primary clearing rail and use FinTech rails solely for low-value client fee collection or supplier payments, with all client trust monies flowing through a Canadian deposit-taking institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined Federal + British Columbia general corporate rate: 27% (Federal 15% + BC 12%). The 11% combined small business rate (Federal 9% + BC 2%) is available exclusively to Canadian-controlled private corporations and is unavailable to a BC ULC controlled by a non-resident shareholder.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian-resident ULC to a non-resident shareholder (Income Tax Act, s. 212).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Tax balance-due date: two months after fiscal year-end; the three-month extension applies only to Canadian-controlled private corporations and is therefore unavailable to a non-resident-controlled ULC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is exception-free and must be prepared where cross-border transactions with each related non-resident counterparty exceed CAD 1,000,000 per year for goods, services, and interest, or CAD 500,000 for royalties and intangibles. Master File documentation is required where the Canadian entity belongs to an MNE group whose consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000. Country-by-Country Report (Schedule 25) is required when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 750,000,000. All documentation must be in English or French and be submitted to the CRA within three months of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations framework as in offshore hubs; however, the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA) and the OECD Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test to disregard arrangements lacking genuine commercial substance. For a BC ULC, the CRA requires demonstrable substance in British Columbia: a physical office, resident employees, board decisions made in Canada, and books/records maintained in BC for intercompany deductions, treaty withholding reduction, and tax-deferred reorganisations to survive challenge. A ULC used exclusively as a conduit or pass-through shell will be recharacterised, and the related treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s. 239 ITA: fine of 50%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties: s. 163(1) false omissions penalty of 25% of the unreported amount; s. 163.2 gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax, plus interest at the prescribed CRA rate.

Director personal liability:
Unlawful Immigration / Visa Consultancy (Unauthorized Representation)

Statutory sanction: Immigration and Refugee Protection Act, s. 91 offence: summary conviction to fine up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years per occurrence; on indictment, fine up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years for systematic unauthorized representation. The ULC may be barred from the business, receive a forfeiture or restitution order, and all illegal fee revenue becomes assessable income with civil penalties.

Director personal liability:
Transfer Pricing / Withholding Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA transfer pricing adjustment under s. 247 plus a 10% adjustment penalty where the net adjustment exceeds the CAD 1,000,000 threshold. Unremitted Part XIII withholding creates a deemed trust in favour of the Crown over all corporate assets, interest at the prescribed rate (currently 9% per annum on demand), and penalties up to 50% of the unremitted amount under s. 162(11); criminal fines up to 200% of the evaded withholding may follow.

Director personal liability:
Shell Company / GAAR and Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: GAAR s. 245 recharacterisation and denial of the avoidance benefit, plus a 25% misrepresentation penalty on the denied deductions; denial of treaty relief results in a 25% Part XIII assessment on the entire outflow. Under the BC Business Corporations Act and the unlimited liability feature, the CRA may pierce the corporate shield and collect the full deficiency directly from shareholders; commercial bankruptcy orders and dissolution may follow.

Director personal liability:
Director and Officer Personal Liability

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 227.1: directors jointly and severally liable for all unremitted corporate withholdings and interest to the extent the amount is collectable from the corporation; bankruptcy/Insolvency Act s. 96 voidable preference provisions permit asset clawback from directors where transfers favour insiders. Criminal liability: imprisonment up to 2 years for fraud under the Criminal Code; travel bans and asset freezes are ordered where the court finds a risk of flight with unreported funds; directors may also face provincial securities/AML restitution orders and a ban from acting as directors for a period up to 15 years.
