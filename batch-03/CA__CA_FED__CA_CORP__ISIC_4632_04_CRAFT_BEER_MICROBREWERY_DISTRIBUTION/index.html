Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); digital portal: My Business Account (MBA) and Business Registration Online (BRO) via canada.ca; supplementary export reporting authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

1) A non-resident corporation carrying on commercial activity in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT suffix). 2) Submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) electronically through BRO or by mail/fax to the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre. 3) Attach certified corporate articles, certificate of status, and appointment of a resident agent for service where required. 4) Registration is mandatory once the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold is exceeded on taxable supplies made in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. 5) Once registered, the entity files GST/HST returns (Form GST34) via GST/HST NETFILE or electronic EDI and must maintain prescribed books and records for six years. 6) For direct export shipments of craft beer, the entity must also obtain a CBSA export account and report exports via ACI eManifest or B13A Export Declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections include: (i) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 156 election between closely related corporations to net GST/HST at nil consideration; (ii) ETA s. 167 going-concern election for zero-rated transfer of a business or part thereof; (iii) Income Tax Act (ITA) s. 216 election for a non-resident to compute Canadian-source rental or royalty income on a net basis; (iv) treaty withholding reduction elections via Form NR301; (v) mandatory hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.2-18.6, 94B and 95, which disallow deductions for hybrid instruments, imported mismatches and dual-resident arrangements with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - value of consideration for taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports, excluding exempt supplies and financial services) made in Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; charities and public institutions have a CAD 50,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of craft beer kegs qualify for zero-rating under s. 5, Schedule VI, Excise Tax Act, provided the tangible personal property is exported by the recipient 'as soon after the supply is made as is reasonable having regard to the circumstances', and the supplier retains written evidence of export in accordance with CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4.4.1/4.4.2. Drop-shipment scenarios involving non-resident purchasers are zero-rated under Schedule VI ss. 5-10. The beer must not be consumed or delivered to a domestic Canadian market prior to export; any domestic Canadian sale attracts 5% GST plus applicable provincial HST/PST/QST. Offshore freight, handling and insurance services directly tied to the exported goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI ss. 7-9, whereas marketing and advisory services rendered for the Canadian market remain taxable at 5%.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration or ACI eManifest (highway/rail/marine) evidencing physical export
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill / Air Waybill naming the foreign consignee and non-Canadian destination
  • Commercial Invoice with commodity description, HS Code 2203.00 (beer made from malt), keg count and declared value in CAD
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation demonstrating settlement by the foreign buyer
  • Packing List detailing keg identifiers, net quantity in liters and destination port

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or CRA pre-approval of invoices exists in Canada. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary, with the Pan-Canadian Peppol-based standard available through accredited access points; XML/EDI formats are acceptable. A GST/HST registrant must issue invoices containing the prescribed elements under ETA s. 169(4)-(s) - supplier name, BN/RT number, date, description, amount and tax payable - and a simplified invoice is permitted for taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 150. CRA audit verification relies on a traceable audit trail from invoice to export documentation.

CRA may assess or reassess within 4 years, and indefinitely where false statements, misrepresentation or fraud are established. Upon denial of zero-rating, CRA claws back the benefit by assessing 5% GST on the supply plus compound daily prescribed-rate interest. Specific penalties: ETA s. 280 (failure to register, remit or file - CAD 250 per occurrence); ETA s. 285 (false statements - 25% of the amount); ETA s. 285.1 (gross negligence - 50% of the amount); ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 (criminal evasion - imprisonment up to 5 years). Books and records, including all export evidence, must be retained for 6 years under ETA s. 286. Although Canada imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST/HST purposes, export receivables outstanding beyond 180 days are treated as an audit risk indicator and may trigger ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing adjustments on underpaid intercompany receivables.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account; the Canadian dollar is freely convertible with no standing exchange-control, surrender, or prior authorization requirements for either current-account trade settlements or capital-account inflows and profit remittances. Profit repatriation is constrained only by Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25% (treaty-reducible) and, for a controlled foreign affiliate, by the indirect filial attribution rules; no blockage or convertibility hurdle exists. Foreign direct investment remains subject to the Investment Canada Act national-security review, which is not a currency convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender ratio or mandatory conversion timeline applies to export receipts; a Canadian exporter may retain full export proceeds in any funded currency or CAD account. Proceeds may be settled in CAD or foreign currency at market rate. Reporting obligations: every SWIFT wire or currency transfer exceeding CAD$10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC; export commodities (beer) must be declared through the CBSA customs export declaration system; no central-bank repatriation filing is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank; exchange-control and currency administration is governed by the Currency Act and administered through the Department of Finance Canada, with prudential supervision of clearing banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability for offshore digital EMI and cross-border payment fintechs. An offshore EMI cannot operate as a principal clearing bank, hold client CAD settlement balances, or issue a Canadian IBAN-equivalent without OSFI authorization or a recognized foreign-bank branch license. For treasury concentration purposes an offshore EMI may be used only as an ancillary collection gateway if registered as a Money Services Business with FINTRAC and, where selling foreign exchange, licensed provincially as a foreign-exchange dealer; such accounts trigger enhanced transaction monitoring and cannot replace a tier-1 Canadian clearing relationship.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style ESR, but the Income Tax Act section 245 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule) and the common-law 'economic substance' jurisprudence under the FCA and Supreme Court (e.g., Shell Canada) mandate a substantive review of the transaction. The distribution entity must maintain real commercial substance in Canada, including core decision-making, physical cold-storage/deployment facilities, Canadian brewery licensing relationships, actual invoicing, and holding of corporate records. A pass-through entity that claims intercompany mark-ups but lacks board minutes, human-hours, and residual risk is subject to recharacterization under the anti-avoidance rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years for each count under section 327 of the Excise Act and section 239 of the Income Tax Act; fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded; restitution and interest; provisional assessment of unreported income based on net-worth audit.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally assessed under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act for unremitted withholdings and excise duties, and may face criminal travel bans, revocation of passport, and seizure of personal assets upon conviction.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Canada's section 247 adjustment to the arm's-length range; a transfer pricing penalty of 10% of the tax adjustment plus compound interest; if fraud is proven, a civil reconsideration to add a 50% gross negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Where the transfer pricing policy is merely a facade to strip profits from the Canadian liquor distribution, the CRA can seek a piercing of the corporate veil under section 239, assessing directors for civil remedies in the amount of the diverted funds, and obtaining judgments that attach to a director's offshore assets.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty (AMP) of up to CAD 2 million per violation for corporate entities under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; additional CRA late filing penalty of CAD 25 per day (up to CAD 2,500) for each T1135 omission; criminal seizure and freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or knowingly allow the unregulated wire transfers are personally liable for the penalty under the 'direction and authorization' test; they may be named in a Canadian civil asset freezing order and flagged by the RCMP's Financial Crime Section, resulting in travel restrictions at border crossings.
Shell Company and Sham Transaction Doctrine

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the Canadian entity as a sham for tax and excise purposes; disallowance of all deductions, and reattribution of the gross revenue to the foreign parent's Canadian permanent establishment; corporate loss of excise duty licenses and revocation of CSA registration; fines under the Food and Drugs Act for manufacturing/distribution without a sanitary establishment certification are also triggered.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil permits the CRA and provincial regulatory authorities to seize personal bank accounts and inventory inside a director's garage, hold the director for unpaid corporate excise remittances, and apply for a travel ban under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act when fraud is asserted.
