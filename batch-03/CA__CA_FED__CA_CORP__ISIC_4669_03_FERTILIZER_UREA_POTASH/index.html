Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian domestic law; entity classification is determined by incorporation status. Available statutory elections include section 216 election for non-residents earning Canadian rental income and section 216.1 deemed disposition election. Hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically and do not require an elective mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada; registration is mandatory for any non-resident carrying on a commercial activity in Canada when supplies exceed CAD 30,000 unless the non-resident is a small supplier exempt under section 240 of the Excise Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export supply applies to a sale of tangible personal property (e.g., prilled urea and potassium chloride) that is physically exported from Canada by a common carrier or by the non-resident recipient, or delivered to a point outside Canada for subsequent export. Documentary evidence confirming export must be retained. Offshore services may also be zero-rated if performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to a non-resident who is outside Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA) / Canadian Export Reporting System record
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill showing shipment from Canada to foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent proof of foreign payment
  • Transport and storage documents normally maintained in the ordinary course of business

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Canada. E-invoicing is voluntary and adopts Peppol interoperability standards. Invoices must satisfy the prescribed information requirements under the Input Tax Credit Information Regulations, and all books and records must be retained in Canada.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess uncollected GST/HST plus compound interest if export substantiation is insufficient or false. Penalties can include a 25% custodial penalty for knowingly making false statements, additional penalties under section 285.1 for issuing false or misleading invoices, and criminal prosecution for fraud. Refund claims are limited to a four-year reassessment window and can be retroactively clawed back.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada offers full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account transactions, consistent with IMF Article VIII status and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. No prior central-bank license or government authorization is required for cross-border wires, dividend/profit remittance, capital injection, or trade settlement. The only substantive hurdles are (i) Part XIII Canadian withholding tax on dividends/interest/royalties remitted to non-residents (domestic default 25%, treaty-reduced, e.g., 15% or 5% under an applicable income tax convention), (ii) corporate solvency and directors' resolution requirements under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) prior to dividend declaration, and (iii) FINTRAC reporting and sanctions-screening obligations (Canadian Special Economic Measures Act and OSFI consolidated sanctions list).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or obligation to repatriate or surrender export proceeds. A Canadian exporter of prilled urea or potassium chloride may lawfully retain foreign-currency receipts in USD-denominated accounts at Schedule I banks, convert at commercial rates, or use such funds for offshore working capital. The only binding requirements are corporate tax accrual/reporting under the Income Tax Act and FINTRAC record-keeping for electronic funds transfers of C$10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - monetary authority only; foreign-exchange controls were abolished in 1951 and no exchange-control department exists today. Prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision is exercised by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a standalone treasury rail; viable only as an ancillary payment facilitator. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorized to take deposits or issue Canadian banking accounts absent compliance with the Bank Act and OSFI licensing. Such entities may, however, register as money-service businesses with FINTRAC and operate a pass-through settlement account via a licensed Canadian PSP, but they cannot provide credit, commercial LC issuance, or Lynx access. A cross-border fintech EMI should therefore be limited to small-value supplier payments under C$10,000 per transaction and must never hold the primary operating liquidity of the Canadian corporation.

Defense evidence: Consolidated sanctions screening report (SEMA/OSFI consolidated list, OFAC-derived cross-reference), corporate residency proofs, supplier country-of-origin certificates, end-user/end-use attestations, logistics chain documentation (bill of lading, customs entry), and a licensed Canadian counsel legal opinion on sanctions compliance.

High-velocity cross-border activity and a newly incorporated federal entity anticipating a large upfront foreign direct investment (FDI) capital injection.

Defense evidence: CBCA certificate of incorporation, articles and corporate search, board resolution authorizing the capital contribution, share subscription agreement, a nine-month projected settlement and liquidity plan, external counsel's written confirmation of Part XIII withholding on any onward dividend remittance, and banker's references for the sending institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a non-resident shareholder capital injection: (i) execute a share subscription agreement and board resolution; (ii) wire via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the Schedule I operating account in CAD or USD using a balance-of-payments narrative 'Direct investment - equity capital' with the Canadian corporate identifier and invoice-equivalent reference 'Capital contribution - share subscription'; (iii) evidence of funds through audited financial statements, tax returns, or a professional asset-valuation report; (iv) record the transaction in the corporate securities register and file the CBCA notice of change of directors if applicable. FINTRAC EFT reporting thresholds and OSFI beneficial-ownership naming rules apply at the receiving bank. No central-bank approval is required.For high-value urea/potash cargo from non-sanctioned foreign suppliers, the primary clearing rail is a commercial documentary letter of credit issued under ICC UCP600 (SWIFT MT700/MT710) with settlement through the correspondent-bank network, drafts honored by sight or 90-day usance, and negotiation presented through SWIFT MT754/MT799. For open-account disbursements to repeat suppliers, SWIFT MT103 payments with ISO 20022 remittance data and a unique end-to-end transaction reference are settled in USD via CHIPS/Fedwire or in CAD via Lynx (Payments Canada high-value RTGS). Domestic CAD clearances under C$100,000 may use the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) until the Real-Time Rail fully replaces it, but cross-border treasury flows should remain exclusively on SWIFT/Lynx and correspondent clearing.OTC USD/CAD deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (tenors 1 week to 12 months) documented under IFXMA or ISDA 2002 with a Canadian Schedule I bankOTC non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for illiquid or restricted emerging-market currencies where legal convertibility is absent, subject to bank internal sanctions policyCross-currency swaps (USD/CAD) for tenor-matched hedging of long-term foreign-currency procurement facilitiesFX swaps for same-day or next-day liquidity rebalancing between CAD and USD treasury accounts
