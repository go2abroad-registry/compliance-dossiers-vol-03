Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility for current account transactions including trade remittances and profit distributions. Capital account transactions require prior Bank of Canada approval for certain investments exceeding CAD 100 million or are subject to reporting thresholds. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted post-corporate tax clearance and subject to 25% dividend withholding tax with FINTRAC reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Act, Canadian corporations must repatriate export proceeds within 90 days of receipt and surrender them to authorized foreign exchange dealers at the market rate on the surrender date. Non-compliance may attract administrative penalties and reporting obligations to FINTRAC.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

PayPal and similar fintech electronic money issuers are viable for inbound client payments with volume limits and mandatory FINTRAC registration. Offshore digital EMIs face legal constraints under Canadian AML regulations including enhanced due diligence for cross-border transfers and potential account restrictions for high-volume game asset transactions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 50,000,000 gross revenue (for mandatory Master File and Local File); also T106 filing required for transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 (goods) or CAD 500,000 (services)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have explicit ESR, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. For treaty benefits, the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause may require economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of tax evaded plus interest; criminal prosecution with fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they failed to withhold and remit, or if they participated in the evasion scheme.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CRA can reassess and impose penalties under GAAR; potential denial of treaty benefits and additional taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme or failed to ensure compliance.
AML

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to CAD 500,000 for first offense; criminal penalties for non-compliance, including fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fines if they failed to implement adequate AML policies or knowingly allowed non-compliance.
