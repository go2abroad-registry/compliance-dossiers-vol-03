Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) — Federal tax administration; business registration portal via Canada.ca and CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a federal Business Number (BN), which serves as the statutory tax identification number, before activating income tax or GST/HST accounts. Procedure: (1) Prepare Form RC1, 'Application for a Business Number (BN)', including legal entity name, registered office, Canadian agent or address for service, expected revenue, and nature of Canadian commercial activity; (2) Submit the form to the CRA by fax, mail, or through CRA's online non-resident registration portal; (3) Upon validation, CRA issues a 15-character BN, with separate program accounts, including a GST/HST account (BN-CRA program code 'RT') and a CBSA import/export account ('RM') if cross-border goods clearance is needed; (4) For deemed registration necessity, a non-resident supplier must register once annual world-wide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is available before that threshold; (5) Attach the BN to the corporate T2 income tax return or to annual non-resident Part I returns when a Canadian permanent establishment exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian business corporation (federal or provincial) is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act. A resident corporation is taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credit relief; a non-resident corporation is taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source business income carried on through a permanent establishment, certain Canadian-source property gains, and taxable Canadian property dispositions unless treaty relief applies.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s. 85 rollover election to transfer qualifying property to a corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (ii) Income Tax Act s. 216(1) election enabling non-residents to compute tax on Canadian rental income on net income rather than gross rent; (iii) Income Tax Act s. 115(1) election for non-residents carrying on business in Canada to file Part I tax returns on Canadian-source business income; (iv) Excise Tax Act s. 156 closely related corporation election treating certain supplies as being made for nil consideration for GST/HST purposes; (v) amalgamation elections under s. 256. Hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 18.4–18.6) operate automatically rather than as elective check-the-box regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — Annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding supplies of financial services, zero-rated exports, and certain capital supplies) unless the entity is a 'small supplier'; mandatory registration is triggered once taxable supplies exceed this amount, and voluntary registration is otherwise permitted for non-residents making supplies in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of goods is zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods directly from Canada as part of the supply, by ship, aircraft, rail, truck, courier, or other prescribed manner, and the goods are exported as soon as reasonably possible, in all cases within 12 months after the supply. Die-cast scale automotive model replicas sold to non-resident buyers and shipped from Canada to a foreign destination qualify for zero-rating if the supplier is a GST/HST registrant and retains sufficient export evidence. Services performed on or in relation to goods destined for export (e.g. tampo decoration or assembly services) are zero-rated if the services are performed for non-resident persons, the goods are exported, and the services are not likely to be consumed in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA Form B13A for exports over CAD 2,000 or prescribed goods, or electronic ACI/e-manifest export data for highway and rail shipments
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier shipping receipt evidencing that the goods left Canada from the named export terminal
  • Commercial invoice in CAD identifying the non-resident purchaser, description of goods, HS/Nomenclature code, quantity, value, and Incoterms
  • Packing list showing carton-level contents and destination while remaining consistent with the export declaration
  • Foreign remittance evidence — SWIFT MT103, PayPal transaction report, or bank inward remittance certificate demonstrating payment proceeds from the non-resident purchaser

Canada has no federal real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) system, no pre-clearance fiscal digital stamping, and no mandatory national e-invoicing platform. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies of CAD 1,500,000 or more; returns must be filed via CRA GST/HST NETFILE, XML/JSON EDI, or My Business Account. E-invoices are admissible as records if they are complete, legible, and retained in an auditable format. The Canada Border Services Agency requires electronic export data via the Canadian Export Reporting System or an approved portal, and for highway/rail exports through ACI e-manifest, but this is not equivalent to fiscal invoice clearance.

The CRA treats export zero-rating claims as high-risk audit inventory. A registrant must maintain complete export documentation at the time the export occurs; failure to furnish acceptable evidence within CRA's requested period results in reassessment of the supply at 5% GST/HST, with uncollected tax payable plus daily compound interest. The CRA may claw back input tax credits or refunds that were conditional on a valid zero-rated export. Under Excise Tax Act s. 280, knowingly making a false or misleading statement in a return, application, or record can trigger a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 20% of the net tax that was or would have been claimed. Where fraud or wilful evasions are established, criminal penalties and additional civil penalties may apply, and the statutory limitation period for reassessment extends from 4 years to 10 years in cases of misrepresentation or willful default. The absence of a statutory 180-day inward remittance deadline in Canada does not eliminate the evidentiary burden; the importer/registrant should document repatriation within the 180-day contract cycle to demonstrate genuine export and settlement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized convertible currency regime with no exchange-control permit system. Both current-account flows (trade payments, royalties, service fees) and capital-account flows (equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation, foreign direct investment) are unconditionally convertible into CAD or any foreign currency. Profit remittance hurdles are non-monetary and arise from: (i) the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) solvency test, which prohibits dividends or capital reductions if the corporation is insolvent; (ii) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties (statutory 25% reduced to 5-15% under tax treaties subject to a valid RC475 treaty residency certificate and NR4/NR601 filings); and (iii) Part XIV branch tax of 25% on unremitted branch profits of a non-resident corporation (treaty-reducible). Inward controlling investments by non-Canadians must file a notification or, for non-WTO investors above asset-based thresholds, an application for review under the Investment Canada Act (ICA); the 2025 WTO review threshold is CAD 1.313 billion enterprise value. There is no restriction on holding foreign currency, no profit-remittance ceiling, and no central-bank pre-approval for repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandated conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings offshore indefinitely. However, administrative obligations apply: (i) under the Customs Act and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) record-keeping requirements, exporters must retain sales contracts, invoices, bills of lading, and payment advices for six years; (ii) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer (EFT) reports (previously Form Type 9, now EFT transaction reporting under the 2025 AML regime) are mandatory for every incoming or outgoing international transfer of CAD 10,000 or more and for structured smaller transfers; and (iii) a Canadian-controlled foreign affiliate holding passive foreign earnings may trigger attribution and foreign accrual property income (FAPI) rules under sections 91-95 of the Income Tax Act, creating a tax-planning repatriation decision rather than an FX-control surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; prudential supervision and FX administration coordination delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for banking conduct and to the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Canada does not recognise the European-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence, and an offshore digital EMI without a Canadian licence cannot lawfully provide deposit-taking, payment-account, or clearing services to Canadian residents. A non-resident fintech must either (i) register with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB) and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), whose payment service provider authorisation regime is being phased to full enforcement by 2026, or (ii) access the Payments Canada clearing and settlement system indirectly through a sponsoring Schedule I bank. Feasibility for this die-cast exporter is rated Medium for ancillary disbursements (supplier payments under CAD 10,000 thresholds, cross-border payroll, virtual card issuance) and Low for primary treasury clearing, due to the absence of direct LVTS Membership and the higher anti-money-laundering compliance burden of unlicensed pass-through models. Recommendation: retain a Schedule I operating account as the liquidity backbone and use reputable RPAA-registered payment fintechs only for low-value PayPal and e-commerce collection rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loans from non-resident investors route through SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages to the corporation's CAD or USD account at a Schedule I clearing bank (e.g., RBC, BMO, or CIBC), which settles in Canadian dollars via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS). The remitting bank must use a transaction purpose code describing a foreign direct investment in a Canadian business or an intercompany capital contribution; the receiving bank will require the CBCA certificate of incorporation, the executed subscription agreement, a board resolution authorising the deposit, and a completed Investment Canada Act notification receipt where the transaction is notifiable. FINTRAC cross-border EFT reporting applies to every incoming transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, and the bank will perform source-of-funds verification on the remitter. For USD injections, the funds may be credited to a U.S. dollar correspondent account and subsequently converted to CAD at the prevailing market rate; there is no Canadian exchange-control approval requirement for either route.Supplier payments for zinc alloy die-casting, tampo printing inks, packaging, and tooling are executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 direct wires settled through the bank's correspondent network (RBC and Scotiabank maintain extensive Asian and European corridors). Larger and risk-managed transactions use documentary commercial letters of credit (LC) issued under ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600), with sight drafts presented against compliant shipping documents; supplier-side confirming banks may add their confirmation for country-risk mitigation. Domestic Canadian disbursements (payroll, warehouse, logistics) use the standard EFT bulk payment system, while PayPal is limited to low-value consumer collectible sales and is not used for bulk trade settlement. Feasibility for this die-cast exporter is rated Medium for ancillary disbursements (supplier payments under CAD 10,000 thresholds, cross-border payroll, virtual card issuance) and Low for primary treasury clearing, due to the absence of direct LVTS Membership and the higher anti-money-laundering compliance burden of unlicensed pass-through models. Recommendation: retain a Schedule I operating account as the liquidity backbone and use reputable RPAA-registered payment fintechs only for low-value PayPal and e-commerce collection rails.
