Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; federal portal: My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency; provincial counterpart in Quebec: Revenu Quebec (QST registration).

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Procedure: (1) establish whether the entity carries on business in Canada under ss. 253 and 253.1 of the Income Tax Act; (2) file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) online via Business Registration Online or by mail, attaching a certified copy of the foreign certificate of incorporation and director details; (3) open program accounts: RC (GST/HST), RP (corporate income tax) and RT (payroll) as applicable; (4) appoint a Canadian representative using Form RC59 when the corporation has no Canadian address; (5) complete extra-provincial registration in each province where a place of business exists; (6) if the commercial activity is in Quebec, register separately with Revenu Quebec for QST and provincial corporate tax. The BN is the statutory TIN for federal tax, GST/HST and customs purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a federal Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act is a distinct taxable person under s. 248(1) of the Income Tax Act; it is not a pass-through vehicle. A resident Canadian corporation is taxable on worldwide income under Part I with dividend withholding under Part XIII for non-resident shareholders (25% statutory, treaty-reduced). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a federal Business Corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act is a distinct taxable person under s. 248(1) of the Income Tax Act; it is not a pass-through vehicle. A resident Canadian corporation is taxable on worldwide income under Part I with dividend withholding under Part XIII for non-resident shareholders (25% statutory, treaty-reduced). Territorial scope: a non-resident corporation carrying on business through a permanent establishment in Canada is taxable on Canadian-source business income under Part I and may be subject to the branch tax under Part XIV; the applicable provincial corporate tax is administered by the CRA in all provinces except Alberta and Quebec, based on the permanent establishment attribution rules.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box regime; tax classification follows legal personality with no elective entity conversion. Available statutory elections: s. 85 rollover election (tax-deferred transfer of property to a taxable Canadian corporation); s. 216 election (net-basis tax for rental income); s. 219 branch tax election (Part XIV for non-resident corporations); s. 156.1(4) not applicable. Under the Excise Tax Act: s. 211 election (closely related corporations may treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration) and the section 156 election for qualifying groupings. Hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 18.2, 18.4 and Part VI.1, effective for payments after 30 June 2023) are mandatory and do not constitute elections; they neutralize deduction/non-deduction outcomes arising from hybrid instruments and hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - the small supplier threshold under s. 148 of the Excise Tax Act: a person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant once worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the four preceding calendar quarters. A non-resident corporation carrying on a commercial activity in Canada must register under s. 240 after crossing this threshold; voluntary registration is available when carrying on a commercial activity, even below the threshold. For imports of commercial aircraft parts, GST applies at the border under the customs accounting regime and is recoverable as input tax credits by registered importers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of surplus commercial aircraft parts are zero-rated as exports under s. 1(a), Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the supplier exports the goods as soon as is reasonably possible after the supply is completed, the goods are shipped to a non-resident buyer, and the exporter retains supporting documentation. Services performed for non-residents (e.g., brokerage, inspection certification, logistics co-ordination for offshore delivery) are zero-rated under s. 7, Part V, Schedule VI where the recipient is not resident in Canada and is not a GST/HST registrant, and the service is performed wholly outside Canada or is supplied in respect of goods situated outside Canada at the time of supply. For aircraft parts trading, the exporter must evidence: a valid contract with a non-resident principal; HS classification under 8807.90 (successor to 8803.30) or 8807.30; physical export from Canada; and receipt of the sales proceeds in the exporter's account.

  • Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) export declaration electronically filed with the CBSA prior to export for all goods valued above CAD 2,000 or regulated goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list stating part numbers, serial numbers for rotables, condition codes (NEW / SVC / OH), unit values, the Incoterm, and HS code 8807.90 (ex-8803.30)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the Canadian exporter of record and the foreign consignee, with proof of departure from Canada
  • Proof of export: outward manifest, carrier transport documents, or gateway exit report from the CBSA
  • Customs import entry records where the surplus parts were previously imported into Canada, together with transfer or warehouse release documents establishing the exporter's title
  • Airworthiness release documentation (TCCA Form One or FAA Form 8130-3) for certified civil aircraft rotables and avionics
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103/MT202, or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in the exporter's Canadian account

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. Tax invoices must be issued by a GST/HST registrant for every taxable supply exceeding CAD 30 (s. 223, Excise Tax Act) and must show the supplier's BN, invoice date, names and addresses, description, consideration, tax payable, and the customer's GST/HST registration number where zero-rating is claimed. Electronic invoicing is permitted but not mandatory; no CRA API clearance or digital stamp is required. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) by registrants with annual gross revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million and payments must be made electronically above the prescribed threshold; CERS export declarations, however, must be transmitted electronically to the CBSA before export.

The CRA will reassess an export zero-rating claim if the exporter cannot substantiate physical export and foreign currency repatriation, generally within the 4-year normal reassessment window extended to 10 years under s. 298 of the Excise Tax Act for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or willful default. Unsubstantiated zero-rated sales are re-characterized as taxable at 5% GST plus applicable HST, with interest compounded daily at the prescribed rate (currently approximately 9% for corporate balances). Penalties include s. 280 (15% of the unreported net tax) and s. 285 gross negligence (greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the tax amount). Fraudulent export schemes are prosecuted under s. 327 of the Excise Tax Act with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; the Customs Act separately authorizes seizure and forfeiture of goods and penalties of up to 50% of the value of false or omitted export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. No restrictions on dividend remittance or repatriation of capital for non-resident shareholders, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (typically 25% statutory, reduced by tax treaty) and compliance with sanctions screening. No exchange control approval is required for cross-border fund flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or specific timeline for repatriating export receipts. Foreign currency may be retained and managed through resident accounts, subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds for cross-border currency movements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act; external oversight of banking operations executed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); financial intelligence and anti-money laundering monitoring by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime and Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. Canada does not have a dedicated EMI licensing regime akin to the EU; non-bank fintech platforms must operate under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) for payment functions and may need to partner with a Canadian Schedule I bank or via a money-services business (MSB) registration with FINTRAC. Offshore digital EMIs face legal constraints; they cannot directly provide Canadian-domiciled corporate accounts unless licensed or registered in Canada or clearly operating as a correspondent/agent of a Canadian bank.
