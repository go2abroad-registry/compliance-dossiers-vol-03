Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - federal tax administration for Part I corporate income tax and GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act (ETA); online portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses.html). British Columbia provincial fiscal authority: BC Ministry of Finance, eTaxBC portal for PST and Insurance Premium Tax; BC Corporate Registry (OneStop) issues the initial federal Business Number (BN). Captive reinsurance licensing and notices: BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned BC ULC captive reinsurer: (1) Incorporate the ULC via BC Corporate Online; the system synchronizes with CRA to auto-issue a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with Corporate Income Tax (RC) and GST/HST (RT) program accounts. (2) Confirm financial institution status on Form RC1 or through Business Registration Online, as ETA sections 149 and 240 remove the CAD 30,000 small-supplier exemption for insurers; a captive reinsurer must register for GST/HST before making taxable supplies. (3) Register for BC PST via eTaxBC only if taxable retail sales will occur; reinsurance premiums are exempt financial services, so PST registration is typically not triggered. (4) Obtain a captive insurer licence or exemption order from BCFSA under the Insurance (Captive Company) Act. (5) Obtain a US Employer Identification Number via IRS Form SS-4 and appoint a CRA tax representative (Form RC59) to receive notices and remit income tax instalments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation under domestic Canadian law: a BC ULC is a resident Canadian corporation taxed under Part I on worldwide income (ITA s.2(2), s.89); unlimited shareholder liability does not confer fiscal transparency, and CRA does not recognize the ULC as a pass-through for Canadian tax. Pass_Through_Eligible only for foreign tax purposes: where all shareholders are US residents and file an IRS check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. s.301.7701-3, the ULC may be disregarded for US federal tax, with treaty coordination under Canada-US Tax Convention Article IV(7) and IRS Notice 2015-17. This election has no effect on Canadian entity-level taxation aside from hybrid mismatch recharacterization rules.

Available election forms

Available check-the-box and statutory elections: (i) IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election classifying the ULC as a disregarded entity (DRE) for US federal tax purposes; (ii) joint election under ETA s.156 to treat supplies between the ULC and closely related parent/subsidiary corporations as made at nil consideration for GST/HST, including zero-rated reinsurance flows; (iii) treaty-based disclosure election under Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Convention requiring each US member to file IRS Form 8833; (iv) select financial institution (SFI) attribution method election or rebate election under ETA s.209.1 where provincial HST components apply to a captive insurer with Canadian risk; (v) hybrid mismatch alignment: ITA ss.18.2 and 18.3 apply to payments made after 30 June 2022 that create deduction with non-inclusion in a hybrid entity, and taxpayers may elect to grandfather pre-existing arrangements under transitional rules; (vi) waiver of the normal reassessment period via T2029 to resolve risk-situs disputes with CRA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 general small-supplier threshold (ETA s.148) is expressly unavailable to a financial institution; a captive reinsurer is a financial institution under ETA s.149 and must register for GST/HST regardless of gross revenue. BC PST registration threshold: mandatory upon opening a BC place of business and making taxable sales, but reinsurance premiums are exempt, so no PST account is generally required. BC IPT applies to specified net premiums written for BC-resident risks; a captive whose insured risks are entirely foreign or parent-directed may apply for an exemption; no separate turnover threshold governs the IPT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A captive reinsurance supply is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part VII s.23 only where the recipient of the financial service is a non-resident and the financial service is not for use in Canada; for reinsurance, the underlying insured risk must be situated outside Canada as evidenced by a contemporaneous risk-location schedule. The general services export zero-rating under Schedule VI Part V s.7 cannot cover a financial service, making s.23 the exclusive route. Eligibility requires: (a) the ULC is a GST/HST registrant; (b) the ceding insurer is a non-resident and not registered for GST/HST; (c) no Canadian-resident risk is remitted under the treaty or facultative certificate; (d) premiums are paid from a non-resident account; (e) books and records are retained under ETA s.286. Canadian-resident risks remitted to the captive are exempt, not zero-rated, and the ULC may claim input tax credits only to the extent permitted for financial institutions under ETA s.169(2) and a limited financial institution rebate under ETA s.209.1.

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime operates in Canada. CRA does not mandate a specific B2B invoice format; PDF, EDI, and Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing are all accepted, and Peppol adoption is voluntary. Mandatory electronic filing applies only to prescribed filers: GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE by registrants with annual gross revenues exceeding CAD 1.5M, and T2 corporate returns must be filed electronically when gross revenue exceeds CAD 1M; no API tax clearance or blocking of premium settlement wires is imposed on insurance payments.

CRA GST/HST financial services audit teams and the international tax directorate scrutinize zero-rated s.23 claims; where risk-situs documentation is deficient, CRA recharacterizes the supply as exempt, reassesses net tax, and charges arrears interest. The normal reassessment period for a foreign-controlled corporation is 4 years (ITA s.152(4)); it extends to 6 years for neglect, and is unlimited in the case of fraud (ITA s.152(4)(a)(i)). Civil penalties: gross negligence penalty under ETA s.285 equal to 25% of the assessed amount (minimum CAD 100); false statements penalty under ITA s.163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax. Criminal evasion under ETA s.327 and ITA s.239 attracts fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. Retrospective clawback of zero-rating can also trigger Part XIII non-resident withholding on deemed dividends, GAAR invocation under ITA s.245, and hybrid mismatch deduction denial under ITA ss.18.2/18.3, each with separate interest and penalty regimes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating currency regime with no general foreign-exchange controls or restrictions on inward or outward remittance under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act. Current and capital account transactions are freely convertible into CAD through Canadian chartered banks. Profit remittances by a BC ULC captive reinsurer to a foreign parent are permitted but may attract Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act and, if applicable, treaty rate relief under a relevant income tax convention. Non-resident insurers providing reinsurance to Canadian risks are subject to provincial licensing or deeming rules; however, conversion and remittance are unhindered absent sanctions, unremitted tax, or FINTRAC reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory requirement for export proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation timeline, no minimum conversion percentage into CAD, and no central bank deposit obligation. Export receipts and other trade proceeds may be retained in foreign currency. However, for tax and financial reporting, all amounts must be converted to CAD using Bank of Canada daily exchange rates, and any wire transfer or EFT of C$10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC as a large EFT report under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/ATF supervision and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of federally regulated deposit-taking institutions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. A BC-incorporated captive reinsurer must maintain statutory reserves, premium funds, and claims provisions in trust with a Canadian chartered bank or a British Columbia authorized financial institution. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) that are not registered with FINTRAC as a money services business and do not hold a Bank of Canada retail payment activity licence under the Retail Payment Activities Act are not viable as principal clearing accounts. An EMI may serve only as a pass-through FX or payment facilitation provider if it is a Canadian-registered MSB and the operating and reserve accounts remain at a Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the parent or shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 wire to the BC ULC escrow account at a Canadian clearing member bank, using the beneficiary corporate name and stated purpose 'capital contribution to captive reinsurance company'. The field 70 remittance information must reference the reinsurance agreement and the ISIC 6512 commercial model, e.g. 'Policy/Reinsurance Agreement No. X, capital injection for captive reinsurance SPV.' The receiving bank verifies the BC registry certificate, board-resolution, and instructions, performs OFAC/UN sanctions screening, and files a FINTRAC large EFT report for any amount equal to or above C$10,000. For foreign-currency capital injections, a spot FX conversion into CAD is executed at the clearing bank's commercial rate; there is no requirement to seek prior Bank of Canada or OSFI approval for a capital-account transfer, although bank internal enhanced due diligence may apply for amounts above C$10 million.Clearing rails for outbound trade and reinsurance settlements: (i) international claim settlements, retrocession payments, and premium commissions are executed via SWIFT MT103 through a Canadian clearing bank; (ii) CAD domestic disbursements are cleared via Payments Canada Lynx or the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); (iii) commercial letters of credit are issued by the Canadian bank under UCP600 and settled via SWIFT MT400/MT700 series documents; and (iv) intercompany loss payments to foreign insurers use SWIFT MT202 or MT103COV as appropriate. All outgoing EFTs above C$10,000 are reported to FINTRAC. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined (15% federal general corporate rate plus 12% British Columbia provincial general corporate rate); the nominal 38% federal rate is reduced by a 10% provincial abatement and a 13% general rate reduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII tax on dividends paid to non-residents by a Canadian-resident corporation; the rate is reduced to 5% for US corporate beneficial owners holding at least 10% of voting stock under the Canada-US Treaty, and to 15% in other cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than six months after the taxation year-end under Income Tax Act s. 150(1); any balance of tax is due two months after year-end for a non-resident corporation without a permanent establishment in Canada, and otherwise three months after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 filing is mandatory when the aggregate value of non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents in a fiscal year exceeds CAD 1,000,000; international groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more must prepare a Master File and Local File and file a Country-by-Country Report.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply a standalone ESR; the CRA tests economic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s. 245, transfer pricing rules under s. 247, and the BC Insurance Act licensing requirements. A captive reinsurer must demonstrate a real office, licensed underwriting personnel, independent board decisions, paid-up capital, actuarial reserving, and actual risk bearing; otherwise the reinsurance arrangements may be disregarded and profits reattributed to the foreign shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239(1) - summary conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and up to 2 years imprisonment; indictment: fine of 50% to 200% and up to 5 years imprisonment; revocation of BC insurance licence and administrative monetary penalties by the BC Financial Services Authority.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or authorize the false filing may be charged criminally; upon conviction, travel bans, personal restitution orders, and director disqualification apply. The unlimited liability regime of the BC ULC additionally exposes shareholders to corporate liabilities on the failure of the company.
