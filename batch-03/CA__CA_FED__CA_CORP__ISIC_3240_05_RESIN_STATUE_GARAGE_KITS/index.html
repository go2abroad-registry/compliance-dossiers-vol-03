Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal: CRA My Business Account (GST/HST, payroll, corporate income tax) and the Canada Business Number (BN) registration portal.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada or earning Canadian-source income must register for a Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) via the CRA's 'Register a Business Number' online service or by completing Form RC1. For GST/HST purposes, the entity must open a separate GST/HST account under the same BN and file Form RC1A (Request for GST/HST Account Information). A foreign entity without a Canadian permanent establishment may register on a voluntary basis to claim input tax credits and to zero-rate exported goods and services; the BN/GST/HST account is obtained online using the entity's legal name, registered office address, and evidence of foreign incorporation. Non-resident registrants must designate a resident agent or representative for service of process and CRA correspondence where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a check-the-box regime under the Income Tax Act. A corporation incorporated in Canada is a taxpayer in its own right and is subject to federal Part I corporate income tax, with provincial income tax allocated by permanent establishment. Non-resident corporations are taxable under Part I on Canadian-source business income and on certain capital gains; no statutory election is available to treat a corporation as a partnership or disregarded entity for federal income tax purposes. For GST/HST purposes, a non-resident may elect to have a related 'qualifying registration' shared GST/HST group (section 150 of the Excise Tax Act) to streamline compliance. Canadian-resident partnerships and joint ventures holding the corporation's shares may be eligible for the 'look-through' or 'fiscally transparent' rules that are available in the applicable provinces, but these do not alter federal corporate classification. Hybrid mismatch rules under subsection 18.4(8) and section 247 of the Income Tax Act restrict deduction effectiveness for 'hybrid instrument' arrangements and for payments made to fiscally transparent foreign entities where the payee jurisdiction does not tax the income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PHP 0 (non-resident supplier making taxable supplies in Canada through a permanent establishment must register; elective registration permitted; no federal threshold applies to a non-resident with a physical presence, although the CRA generally applies the 30,000 CAD 'small supplier' threshold to residents; non-resident vendors of goods to Canadian customers may be required to register under the 2019 GST/HST framework for 'digital economy' supplies if their global revenues exceed CAD 30,000 and Canadian-source taxable supplies generate more than CAD 30,000).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero-rated supply of goods for GST/HST purposes includes a supply of tangible personal property (including resin statues and garage kits) that is exported from Canada by the supplier or by the recipient, as described in section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. The supplier must have documentary proof that the goods were exported within the required 60-day period (or longer for goods on consignment or in bond). For services provided to non-residents relating to the goods (e.g., design, prototyping, or custom molding of the resin statue), zero-rating applies under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI only if the recipient is outside Canada and the service is not for consumption in Canada. The entity must maintain an export trail showing ownership of the goods did not pass in Canada and that the goods left Canada in an unaltered state from the point of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B3 or CAED/CUSMA when applicable),
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice with Incoterms and stated export sale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank wire/payment proof from the foreign buyer)

Canada does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. The CRA requires that all GST/HST registrants issue, retain, and produce on request invoices that satisfy the requirements of section 169 of the Excise Tax Act: the supplier's name and BN, the date of issue, the recipient's name (or nominee), the total taxable amount, the rate, and the amount of tax charged (expressed separately). E-invoices in PDF or structured electronic format (XML, EDI, or API-transmitted) are accepted; a machine-readable summary must be archived for six years. There is no pre-clearance from the CRA before issuing an invoice; the CRA performs post-transaction verification through data matching. Registrants must file GST/HST returns electronically when their revenues exceed CAD 1.5 million, or by paper where lower. Real-time clearance, customs coding, or fiscal stamp obligations do not exist at the federal level.

The CRA may deny a zero-rating benefit where the exporter fails to produce the documentary evidence within the prescribed timeframe. The penalty for knowingly making a false statement or omission on an application for a rebate or refund is CAD 100 per false statement, plus an additional penalty equal to 50% of the amount of the rebate or refund claimed (Election, subparagraph 280(1)(c)(ii) and subsection 285(1)). Where a zero-rating is applied to a supply that did not in fact leave Canada, the CRA may assess the GST/HST due (5% or HST where applicable) on the full selling price, charge interest, and impose penalties under amended subsection 280(1.1). Fraudulent claims also expose the exporter to criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and severe monetary penalties of up to CAD 100,000 or imprisonment of up to 5 years for answerable offences. Additionally, where a rebate of input tax credits has been claimed in respect of a zero-rated export and the goods are subsequently returned into Canada, a GST/HST assessment will be raised on the value of the returned goods unless the exporter takes steps to re-export them, and export eligibility may be clawed back for a 12-month period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating regime. Current account and capital account transactions are generally unrestricted; there is no exchange control law requiring official authorization for dividend remittances, interest, royalties, or branch profits. Non-resident direct investment into a Canadian Business Corporation is lawful, and funds may be repatriated freely in CAD or foreign currency. The principal remittance hurdles are tax compliance rather than currency controls: Part XIII withholding tax on dividends/interest/royalties, transfer pricing documentation under sections 247 of the Income Tax Act, and adequate solvency and board authorization under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA). A resident Canadian director requirement under the CBCA and CBSA customs valuation reporting apply, but there is no convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in an authorized foreign-currency bank account. Exchange control reporting does not arise; however, Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys and FINTRAC large electronic funds transfer reporting may require the bank to report a cross-border transaction of CAD 10,000 or more, and export documentation must support the commercial purpose.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada has no separate foreign-exchange administration. Exchange and monetary policy functions are carried out by the Bank of Canada, the Department of Finance, and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), with anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction oversight by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: LOW-to-MEDIUM viability as a primary liquidity rail. Canada does not issue an electronic money institution licence; foreign digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may be used as secondary payment collection or vendor-payment gateways, but they are not CDIC-insured banks and cannot provide a full Canadian clearing account. A foreign EMI that services Canadian customers must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and comply with PCMLTFA. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocols: inbound equity capital from a foreign parent should be wired by SWIFT MT103 to the CA_CORP operating account at a Schedule I bank, with the beneficiary account name exactly matching the registered corporate name. The wire remittance information (Field 70) must state 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / SHARE SUBSCRIPTION / CA_CORP' and, if using ISO 20022 payment messages (pacs.009 or pacs.008), the purpose code should be mapped as 'EQUI' or the bank-approved 'capital transfer' purpose. Verification must include a board resolution approving the share subscription, a subscription agreement, updated share register, source-of-funds declaration, organizational chart, and a certificate of good standing for the parent. The bank will perform FINTRAC large EFT reporting if the transaction equals or exceeds CAD 10,000; funds settle through Lynx (formerly LVTS) for CAD or over the SWIFT gpi or CHIPS network for USD.
