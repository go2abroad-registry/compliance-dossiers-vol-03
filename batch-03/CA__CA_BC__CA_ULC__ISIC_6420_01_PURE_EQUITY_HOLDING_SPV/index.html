Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) is the national tax administrator for federal income tax and GST/HST; the British Columbia Ministry of Finance / BC Tax Services Branch administers BC provincial corporate income tax and the BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST). Corporate registration is effected through the BC Registrar of Companies via BC Corporate Online / BC OneStop. Principal portals are CRA My Business Account, CRA GST/HST NETFILE, BC eTaxBC, and the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate or continue the ULC under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act; British Columbia permits unlimited liability companies, imposes no minimum capital requirement, and allows non-resident ownership. Step 2: Obtain BC corporate registry documentation from the BC Registrar of Companies. Step 3: Register the ULC with the Canada Revenue Agency by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or through My Business Account; CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number with program account suffixes, including RC0001 for GST/HST, RM0001 for import/export reporting, and RP0001 for payroll. Step 4: Register through BC eTaxBC for PST if the ULC intends to make taxable sales of goods or services in British Columbia; PST registration has no small-supplier threshold. Step 5: Maintain the registered and records office addresses, directors information, articles of unlimited liability, and CRA account details in the corporate books. A foreign parent should also file Form RC1 where a non-resident director or officer is involved and provide evidence of authority, powers of attorney, and any treaty certification required by the CRA or the BC Registrar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For Canadian federal income tax, a BC ULC is a corporation resident in Canada and is taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act; Canada has no domestic check-the-box election allowing the ULC to be treated as a partnership. The Pass_Through_Eligible characterization is a cross-border elective overlay: under US Treasury Regulation section 301.7701-3, a wholly owned Canadian ULC is eligible to elect classification as a disregarded entity or partnership because its shareholders do not possess limited liability, subject to the applicable per-se rules and IRS guidance. The resulting hybrid entity status should be tested against Canada's hybrid mismatch and anti-arbitrage provisions in the Income Tax Act, the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework, and the relevant Canada-US tax treaty positions. Other statutory elections available in Canada include rollover and amalgamation elections under Income Tax Act sections 85, 87, and 88, and the annual corporate inform return and GIFI/T106 filing elections where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over a trailing four-quarter period for federal GST/HST registration under the small-supplier rules; BC PST requires registration before any taxable retail sale or taxable lease in British Columbia because there is no small-supplier exemption. Passive dividend income received by a pure equity holding SPV does not constitute taxable supply for GST/HST threshold purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For GST/HST purposes, a BC ULC may zero-rate a supply of goods if the goods are exported from Canada in commercial quantities and documented in accordance with Excise Tax Act sections 21 and 23 and CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4-4.2. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated under Excise Tax Act sections 7 to 9 where the service is supplied to a non-resident person, is not in connection with Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and is for consumption wholly outside Canada. Management, treasury, shareholder-loan, and dividend-receiving services may qualify only if actually supplied for consideration and all statutory tests are satisfied. Pure dividends, share transfers, and capital gains from passive equity holdings are not consideration for a supply and are outside the scope of GST/HST; they are not zero-rated because they are not taxable supplies. BC PST does not apply to goods sold for delivery outside British Columbia where the seller ships or delivers the goods directly outside the province and retains documentary proof of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Form B13A / CBSA CAED electronic export declaration for goods; for services, a signed contract evidencing an offshore supply to a non-resident recipient
  • Commercial Invoice with complete description of goods or services, destination, Incoterms, quantity, and value
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or other transport documentation showing the goods were physically delivered outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103/MT202 advice, or bank statement proving receipt of foreign currency payment from the non-resident purchaser
  • Export permit, licence, or CBSA movement record where applicable for controlled goods
  • CRA zero-rating evidence file containing purchase orders, correspondence, and, if claimed, Form GST11 or an applicable GST/HST ruling

Canada does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, CRA API-based invoice clearance, or digital stamping. A GST/HST registrant must issue prescribed invoices containing the required supplier and recipient details and retain electronic records for at least six years; EDI, XML, and PDF invoices are acceptable if complete and readable. For exported goods, the exporter must file electronic export declarations through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CAED) using Form B13A and retain the accompanying proof-of-export file. BC PST does not require a separate real-time fiscal clearance system.

Export zero-rating claims are audit-sensitive. The CRA may reassess within four years of the original assessment, and without time limits in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or waiver, and may disallow zero-rating where the proof-of-export documentation is not contemporaneous or is incomplete. Disallowed supplies become taxable at 5% GST and may attract BC PST and interest where applicable; CRA may claw back refunds and input tax credits. Part IX of the Excise Tax Act imposes failure-to-register, failure-to-report, failure-to-remit, false-statement, and gross negligence penalties, together with compound daily interest; intentional evasion may lead to criminal prosecution by the CRA or CBSA and substantial fines. The 180-day repatriation benchmark is commonly incorporated in Canadian bank and exporter compliance files for proof of foreign receipt, although it is not a standalone general federal statutory deadline for all exporters. A pure equity holding SPV should document the actual cross-border flow and the non-resident recipient status carefully before claiming any zero-rating and should confirm that dividend and shareholding activity remains outside the GST/HST supply framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility. Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no exchange-control licensing, repatriation consents, or surrender premiums on current-account or capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely in a fully deliverable onshore market. Profit remittance is fiscally taxed, not exchange-controlled: 25% statutory non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (RSC 1985, c 1, 5th Supp), reducible to 0-15% under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (1980, as amended by the Fifth Protocol), provided the US shareholder is the beneficial owner and the ULC is not a hybrid-mismatch structure under ITA sections 18.4-18.6. A pure-equity holding SPV must maintain paid-up-capital purity under ITA s. 89 and capital-dividend-account records; intercompany loans require transfer-pricing documentation under ITA s. 247. The Investment Canada Act mandates a post-closing Form A notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) whenever a non-Canadian acquires control of the ULC; a pre-closing net-benefit review applies to transactions exceeding the 2025 WTO-investor enterprise-value threshold of approximately CAD 1.325 billion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines under the Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, or Bank Act. For a pure-equity holding SPV, receipts are investment/dividend flows rather than trade proceeds. The sole compliance overlay is FINTRAC reporting: every electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more in one transaction must be reported by the bank via an Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR) or Large Cash Transaction Report (LCTR), and any suspicious pattern triggers a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (SC 2000, c 17).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential banking supervision delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT enforcement by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada has no separate exchange-control bureau; residual statutory authority sits with the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (RSC 1985, c C-52), Bank of Canada Act (RSC 1985, c B-2), Bank Act (SC 1991, c 46), and Investment Canada Act (RSC 1985, c 28, 1st Supp).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW-MEDIUM as primary treasury host; MEDIUM as auxiliary collection rail. FINTRAC-registered money services businesses and offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, Payoneer) cannot provide statutory deposit insurance, commercial letters of credit, CAD/USD nost account concentration, or overnight liquidity lines required by an equity-holding SPV. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI as the main clearing rail would violate Bank Act s. 539 (deposit-taking prohibition) and trigger enhanced FINTRAC scrutiny. Any payout from the ULC to an EMI account is a reportable cross-border EFT under PCMLTFA s. 7; accordingly, the EMI layer is legally viable only for low-value dividend pass-through and must be subordinated to the OSFI-regulated Tier-1 banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (i) the foreign parent sends a SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the ULC's CAD/USD concentration account at its Vancouver-based Tier-1 bank (e.g., RBC, Swift ROYCCAT2VCR; TD, Swift TDOMCATTTOR/TDCCUS33; BMO, Swift BOFMCAM2); (ii) the payment purpose code must identify an equity subscription or intercompany loan, with the reference field carrying the executed agreement number and the BC corporate registry number; (iii) the bank files the FINTRAC EFTR automatically for any inbound EFT of CAD 10,000 or more; (iv) USD-wire proceeds clear via CHIPS or Fedwire correspondent networks and should be paired with a separate spot or forward FX trade to manage CAD exposure; (v) a pre-closing ISED Form A notification is required if the injection brings the non-Canadian parent to majority control; (vi) all agreements must be pre-positioned with the bank's AML team to avoid hold on source-of-funds verification.For a pure-equity SPV, outbound flows comprise: (i) shareholder dividends — SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer with board resolution, Part XIII withholding evidence, and ISO 20022 purpose code DIVIDEND; USD legs clear via Fedwire/CHIPS, CAD legs via Payments Canada Lynx RTGS above CAD 250,000; (ii) intercompany loan advances — MT103 with purpose code LOAN, supported by the executed loan agreement and arms'-length interest accrual; (iii) subsidiary capital calls — wire or commercial letter of credit (MT700 issuance) where the ULC acts as guarantor for an operating subsidiary, plus standby LC (MT760) for credit enhancement; (iv) all USD-funded disbursements breach the bank's US correspondent edge (RBC Bank Atlanta, TD Bank NA Wilmington, or BMO Harris Chicago) and are cleared under the receiving beneficiary's Fedwire/CHIPS routing. Every outbound remittance must be tagged with ISO 20022 structured remittance information and conform to Bank Act s. 539 if any derivative overlay is used.Deliverable CAD/USD spot and outright forwards (tenors up to 24 months) under the ISDA 2002 Master Agreement; CAD is fully convertible onshore, so NDFs are non-standard and unnecessary for CAD/USD exposure.CAD/USD cross-currency swaps (CCS) on matched notional to hedge intercompany loan principal and interest, executed with OSFI-regulated counterparties under full ISDA/CSA credit support.Plain-vanilla CAD/USD vanilla options, zero-cost collars, and participations; permitted without Bank of Canada approval because Canada has no exchange-control licensing regime.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) strictly limited to third-currency portfolio exposures (e.g., a Latin-American or emerging-market subsidiary position) where the underlying currency lacks onshore deliverability; requires separate ISDA/CEMA documentation and board-approved counterparty limits.Interest-rate swaps linked to floating-rate intercompany debt to stabilize net treasury cost; permissible under Bank Act s. 539 with OSFI-regulated dealer counterparties and subject to CFTC Part 43 trade-reporting thresholds if the US dealer is counterparty.Prohibited instruments for the SPV treasury book: total return swaps and equity swaps on the ULC's own shares (dealer-board approval required and generally disfavored for passive holding entities).

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW-MEDIUM as primary treasury host; MEDIUM as auxiliary collection rail. FINTRAC-registered money services businesses and offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, Payoneer) cannot provide statutory deposit insurance, commercial letters of credit, CAD/USD nost account concentration, or overnight liquidity lines required by an equity-holding SPV. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI as the main clearing rail would violate Bank Act s. 539 (deposit-taking prohibition) and trigger enhanced FINTRAC scrutiny. Any payout from the ULC to an EMI account is a reportable cross-border EFT under PCMLTFA s. 7; accordingly, the EMI layer is legally viable only for low-value dividend pass-through and must be subordinated to the OSFI-regulated Tier-1 banking relationship.
