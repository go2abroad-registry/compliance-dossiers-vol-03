Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Business Registration Online (BRO); My Business Account (MyBA); GST/HST NETFILE; CBSA CARM for customs (Assess and Release / Canadian Export Reporting System).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine nexus — a non-resident corporation that carries on business in Canada or makes taxable supplies in Canada is generally required to register. Step 2: Obtain the 9-digit federal Business Number (BN), the universal Canadian tax identifier, through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts). Step 3: Open program accounts: RT (corporate income tax) where Canadian-source income exists, RC (GST/HST) for GST registration, RP (payroll) if employees are in Canada. A federally incorporated entity receives its BN automatically; provincial registrations may assign parallel provincial IDs. A foreign exporter of enamel pins that does not carry on business in Canada may voluntarily register for GST/HST solely to claim input tax credits on export inputs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian business corporation (federal or provincial) is a separate taxpayer; no US-style pass-through check-the-box designation exists under the Income Tax Act. Part I federal corporate income tax is levied at a 38% base rate, reduced by the 10% general rate reduction and any provincial allocation; Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) may claim the small business deduction (federal rate approximately 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income). Resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed on Canadian-source business income only, subject to treaty relief (e.g., Canada–United States Tax Convention).

Available election forms

Canada does not offer check-the-box entity classification elections. Available statutory elections and designations include: (i) section 216 election for a non-resident to reportCanadian rental income on a net basis; (ii) treaty-based income tax return election by a non-resident to report Canadian business income on a net basis; (iii) GST/HST section 156 election between closely related corporations to treat supplies as made for nil consideration / zero-rated intercompany supplies; (iv) quick method election under section 226 for simplified GST/HST accounting; (v) voluntary small-supplier GST/HST registration election below the CAD 30,000 threshold; (vi) GST/HST annual filing elections where revenue thresholds are met; and (vii) hybrid mismatch adjustment rules are mandatory, not elective, under section 247.1 of the Income Tax Act (OECD BEPS Action 2 framework).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: a person is a small supplier unless taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or CAD 30,000 in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Non-residents that carry on business in Canada apply the same threshold; non-residents that do not carry on business in Canada but make taxable supplies in Canada must register regardless of threshold. Exports of enamel lapel pins shipped from Canada are generally zero-rated and do not trigger registration, but voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Part V of Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act when a supplier transfers possession and ownership of goods and exports them, or delivers them to a common carrier, as part of the supply; alternatively, goods supplied to a non-resident recipient who exports them are zero-rated if the recipient uses or acquires them solely outside Canada. Die-struck brass cloisonné enamel lapel pins sold via Etsy Direct Pay or Stripe to buyers in the United States or other foreign markets qualify as zero-rated exports where shipment originates in Canada and written export evidence is retained. Services rendered in Canada for non-residents, including design and polishing services incorporated into export merchandise, may be zero-rated under Schedule VII when not consumed in Canada. The supplier must charge 0% GST/HST, maintain full export documentation, and retain records for six years; input tax credits remain claimable on qualifying inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Form B13A / Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) filing via CBSA CARM for shipments over CAD 2,000 or controlled goods
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List showing seller, buyer, goods description, value, and destination
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), or Highway Carrier's Waybill evidencing departure from Canada
  • Carrier proof of export (e.g., eManifest, PARS release documents, border crossing records)
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX advice (SWIFT MT103) evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Sales contract or purchase order and any customer declaration confirming final destination outside Canada
  • GST/HST zero-rating support log — tracking numbers, shipping dates, export evidence, and customer data retained for six years

Canada has no statutory national real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping mandate at the federal level. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable revenue above CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or CRA-certified software; remittances above CAD 10,000 must be made electronically. The Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing Service (Peppol network) is adopted for federal public procurement and is mandatory for suppliers to the federal government on a phased basis, but remains voluntary for B2B/B2C commerce (e.g., Etsy/Stripe sales). Regular GST/HST invoices must satisfy the prescribed information requirements of Part I of Schedule V of the Excise Tax Act; no CRA pre-clearance, digital stamping, or API authentication applies to export sales of enamel pins.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess within the normal reassessment period (3 years for corporations under the Income Tax Act; 4 years for GST/HST registrants under the Excise Tax Act), with an unlimited extension to 10 years where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. Upon denial, the exporter owes unremitted 5% GST, arrears interest, and penalties. False-statement penalties under section 280.1 of the Excise Tax Act equal the greater of CAD 250 and 5% of the net tax, plus 1% per month for up to 12 months; failure to retain export records triggers section 285.1 penalties. Gross-negligence penalties under section 163.2 of the Income Tax Act may reach 50% of the understated amount; directors may be personally liable under section 323 of the Excise Tax Act. Intentional export-zero-rating fraud is criminally prosecuted under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act and section 239 of the Income Tax Act, with fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment, and may result in GST/HST registration revocation and enhanced penalty ratios.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible. Canada maintains no exchange controls by the Bank of Canada or the Minister of Finance for either current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance through dividends, management fees, interest, and royalties is unrestricted; the sole fiscal impediment is the non-resident withholding tax imposed by Part XIII of the Income Tax Act. All conversions between CAD, USD, and other currencies may be executed at commercial rates. Only reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act apply to large cross-border electronic transfers and physical currency movements, not convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory export-proceeds surrender rules do not exist under Canadian federal or provincial law. An exporter of die-struck brass cloisonné enamel lapel pins may retain sale proceeds in USD, CAD, or any other currency and is not obligated to repatriate or convert any portion into Canadian dollars. The applicable obligations are limited to (i) record keeping under the Income Tax Act, Excise Tax Act, and Customs Act, (ii) FINTRAC reporting of incoming/outgoing electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and (iii) ordinary customs declaration and export documentation for goods leaving Canada. No mandatory conversion ratio or statutory repatriation timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada; banking regulation and supervisory examination authority is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary e-commerce payout rail, but Low as the exclusive operating bank account. Canadian law does not recognize the term 'Electronic Money Institution' as a licensed bank deposit-taking category. Digital payment providers operating in Canada are generally regulated as Money Services Businesses (MSBs), required to register with FINTRAC and, where applicable, under provincial money-services or consumer-protection statutes. Such platforms may collect Etsy/Stripe payouts and facilitate FX conversion but are not CDIC-insured and cannot legally accept deposits. The Canadian entity must maintain at least one Schedule I bank account for capital subscriptions, tax remittances, and treasury functions; fintech/EMI rails are permissible only for the payment-collection segment of the value chain, not for corporate liquidity reserve.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A non-resident shareholder subscribing for common shares routes a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for the cover leg) to the corporation's CAD account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The beneficiary field must exactly reflect the CBCA corporate legal name; the payment details/reference line should state 'Share subscription – contributed capital' and identify the subscriber. To preclude recharacterization as a loan or trade receivable, the corporate secretary must pass a board resolution accepting the share subscription, issue share certificates, and maintain a capital-call or subscription agreement indicating the currency and amount. If the foreign remitter uses a bank in a non-FATF-compliant jurisdiction, the receiving Canadian bank will require a source-of-funds certification; all incoming electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are automatically recorded and reported to FINTRAC. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% net federal corporate income tax rate under the General Rate applied after the federal-provincial tax abatement of 10 percentage points and the General Rate Reduction of 13 percentage points; a further reduced rate of 9% applies to the first CAD 500,000 of active business income earned by a Canadian-controlled private corporation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders from Canadian-source earnings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the end of each taxation year; balance of tax owing is generally due within two months after the fiscal year-end, with a three-month payment deadline for qualified Canadian-controlled private corporations in respect of active business income eligible for the Small Business Deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 reporting threshold: aggregate transaction value exceeding CAD 1,000,000 with a single non-arm's length non-resident in a taxation year triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. Country-by-Country Reporting (including Master File/Local File obligations) applies where consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply Gulf-Cooperation-Council-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the Income Tax Act's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245) and the common law sham doctrine invalidate transactions lacking genuine business purpose, economic substance, and real presence. The CRA may reallocate income or recharacterize transactions involving passive shell or non-resident intermediaries that lack employees, premises, and decision-making functions in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239(1), conviction may result in a fine equal to up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment for up to 5 years; CRA civil re-assessment authority is unlimited for fraud or misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participated in the evasion can be made personally liable for the corporate tax debt where the corporate veil is pierced on grounds of fraud; personal restitution orders, asset freezing, and travel bans to compel attendance are available.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangements

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the corporate entity as a sham, reassess income at the level of the beneficial owner, apply a 50% gross negligence penalty, and disallow cross-border payments.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally jointly and severally liable for the assessed tax where the court determines the corporation is an alter ego and the directors intentionally diverted corporate receivables into personal or related entities; equitable veil-piercing may lead to constructive trust and restitution orders.
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 for a corporation under FINTRAC's penalty framework; criminal money laundering offence under s.462.31 of the Criminal Code carries a maximum imprisonment term of 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, authorize, or participate in laundering transactions are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, forfeiture of proceeds, and restrictive bail conditions including travel bans; compliance failures can also trigger director and officer liability for fines where the corporation is unable to pay.
