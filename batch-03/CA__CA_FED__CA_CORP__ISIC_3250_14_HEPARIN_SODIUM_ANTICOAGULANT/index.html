Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Business portal: 'My Business Account' at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-business/business-account.html; registration hub: Business Registration Online (BRO); GST/HST NETFILE at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/gst-hst-netfile.html.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a Canadian business corporation (or a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada) must: (1) incorporate federally or provincially (CBCA requires at least 25% of directors to be resident Canadians; no minimum capital); (2) apply for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) via Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or the CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) service; (3) activate the applicable program accounts: 'RC' for corporate income tax (T2), 'RT' for GST/HST, 'RP' for payroll; (4) register for GST/HST under ETA s. 240 once taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold or as soon as 'carrying on business in Canada'; (5) file Form RC1WEB or use the XML API to batch-register multiple entities; (6) obtain cluster/representative authorizations via Form RC59 or the 'Represent a Client' portal; (7) for a CBCA-incorporated subsidiary, the entity is deemed resident under ITA s. 250(4) and must file a T2 return for each taxation year; the GST/HST registration number is the BN plus the suffix 'RT' (format 123456789RT0001); a non-resident corporation with a Canadian permanent establishment files T2 (Canadian-source branches) plus Part XIV branch tax. No minimum capital is required; 100% non-resident ownership is permitted for CBCA business corporations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification regime; every CBCA/provincial business corporation is taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act. Relevant elections include: (i) ITA s. 216(1) election for non-residents to file net-basis Canadian rental/royalty returns instead of gross Part XIII withholding; (ii) ITA s. 217 election for non-resident employees; (iii) ITA s. 95(2)(a) foreign accrual property income (FAPI) election for foreign affiliates; (iv) Part XIV branch-tax elections that allow a branch loss carry-back or treaty-reduced branch rates (Form T2068); (v) ETA s. 273(1) joint election for closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies as taxable supplies (real-time net-tax management) or as non-taxable; (vi) ETA s. 276 special election for non-resident suppliers of travel/hotels; (vii) EIFEL elective grouping under ITA s. 18.2(2) for financing expenses; (viii) eligible-dividend designation (Form T2054) and capital dividend election (Form T2054/Part III.1) applicable to CCPCs and other private corporations; (ix) post-2021 hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.5) are mandatory recharacterizations, no deferral election is permitted. Treaty-resident corporations may also elect to apply Canada's bilateral treaty (e.g., Canada-China Income Tax Agreement) to reduce Part XIII/branch rates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in any current calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 148(1) small-supplier threshold). Once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required. A non-resident supplier soliciting orders in Canada, maintaining a place of business, an agent, or an employee in Canada is deemed to be carrying on business in Canada and must register regardless of revenue. Non-resident sellers of cross-border digital services and digital products to Canadian consumers must register under the simplified 2021 GST/HST framework without any revenue threshold. For a heparin-sodium export sale to a non-resident, the supply is zero-rated and does not itself create a Canadian tax collection obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 of the ETA, a GST/HST registrant zero-rates a supply of heparin sodium API if the goods are (i) sold to a non-resident purchaser, (ii) exported from Canada as part of the supply or delivered to a common carrier for export, and (iii) exported 'as soon as reasonably practical' without substantial transformation while in Canada. Advisory, quality-control and offshore processing services supplied to the non-resident parent are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 17 where the service is not performed in Canada or is supplied to a non-registered non-resident and falls within the enumerated service categories. The supplier must retain export evidence (export declaration, bill of lading, commercial invoice, freight documents) under ETA s. 286(2). An 'Approved Exporter' authorization under the CRA program can streamline continuous export zero-rating and input tax credit substantiation.

  • Canadian Export Declaration (B13A) filed electronically through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) - retains the unique Export Declaration Number
  • Commercial Invoice identifying the non-resident purchaser, Incoterms (e.g., FOB Vancouver), and HS 3001.90 classification
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill - original negotiable ocean bill of lading for letter-of-credit transactions
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or third-party bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Carrier certificate or gate-out report confirming physical departure of the container from Canada
  • Health Canada export certificate or Drug Establishment Licence reference for the heparin drug substance, where required
  • GST/HST zero-rated supply reconciliation schedule supporting the GST34 return filing

There is no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-approval API requirement for B2B invoices in Canada. The CRA adopted the Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing standard (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0) effective 2022 on a voluntary basis; federal government suppliers are expected to submit e-invoices via the Supplier Invoice Management System (SIMS). GST/HST returns (Form GST34) are filed electronically via NETFILE, with API/XML integration for large filers. For export controls, the CBSA's Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) and eManifest portal mandate pre-departure electronic filing of export declarations through the Canadian International Trade Single Window (ITSW) using EDI/API channels; the system assigns the Export Declaration Number that must be retained for zero-rating evidence.

The CRA has a normal four-year reassessment window, extended without limitation where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default, and can deny zero-rating, treating the export as a taxable supply at 5%/HST 13-15% with compounded daily interest and arrears. Civil penalties include: ETA s. 280 gross-negligence penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 10% of understated net tax; ETA s. 296(1) record-keeping penalty of CAD 100 per day up to CAD 2,000; and CBSA penalties of up to CAD 25,000 per false export declaration under the Customs Act. Criminal conviction under ETA s. 327 for evasion carries fines up to CAD 25,000 and up to two years of imprisonment. For intercompany sales to the Chinese parent, ITA s. 247(3) imposes a 10% transfer-pricing penalty on the adjusted amount, and GAAR (s. 245) can recharacterize artificial export conduits. Canada-China tax treaty Article 26 (exchange of information) and the Multilateral Convention on Administrative Assistance enable cross-border audit cooperation; fabricated export documentation is referred to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada. Under Canadian federal law there is no statutory FX repatriation deadline, hence the 0-day figure; export proceeds are accrued on the Part I income account at the time of sale and must be received within the payment terms contractually agreed (usually within 180 days under trade letters of credit) to preserve contemporaneous export evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; no general exchange-control approval is required for profit remittance, dividends, interest, royalties, or repayment of shareholder loans. The sole friction is tax-based: Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25% on dividends/interest/royalties paid to non-residents, typically reduced to 10% on dividends and 10% on interest under the Canada-China DTA, subject to a Form NR302 treaty-residence certificate). Inbound foreign equity or shareholder-loan injections are governed by the Investment Canada Act (ICA): formation of a new Canadian business requires a Part III notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) within 30 days, irrespective of value; acquisition of control of an existing Canadian business is reviewable only above the WTO investor enterprise-value threshold (approx. CAD 1.366 billion in 2025). No ICA approval is required for this greenfield federal corporation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts offshore or in domestic USD accounts. Regulatory duties are limited to: (i) FINTRAC cross-border electronic funds transfer reporting (EFTR) for each wire of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) Large Cash Transaction Reports (LCTR) for cash equivalents above CAD 10,000; (iii) CBSA export declarations via the G7 system for heparin API (HS 3001.90), including the Importer/Exporter Profile number; (iv) Health Canada pre-export notification for a controlled-eligible API only if it falls under the Food and Drugs Act/Controlled Drugs and Substances Act schedules (heparin sodium is not a controlled substance, but a Drug Establishment Licence is mandatory for the exporter's facility); (v) Statistics Canada reporting for services and balance-of-payments if applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); FX policy is implemented jointly with the Department of Finance Canada. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration. Anti-money-laundering oversight of cross-border transfers is exercised by FINTRAC, export controls by Global Affairs Canada, and trade-document compliance by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Canada maintains IMF Article VIII status with fully liberalized payments.

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW as primary treasury rail; MEDIUM as a secondary disbursement sub-ledger. Canada does not licence 'EMI' as a statutory category; non-bank providers operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under PCMLTFA, and as Payment Service Providers (PSPs) subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), with licensing phased in from 2024-2025. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW as primary treasury rail; MEDIUM as a secondary disbursement sub-ledger. Canada does not licence 'EMI' as a statutory category; non-bank providers operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under PCMLTFA, and as Payment Service Providers (PSPs) subject to the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), with licensing phased in from 2024-2025. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, etc.) may route CAD settlement via Canadian sponsor banks, but cannot issue documentary letters of credit, confirm SWIFT MT700s, or operate true escrow accounts for capital injection; they also present a cross-border data-residency concern for Health Canada GMP records. Use them only for minor supplier payments where an LC is not required.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million (aggregate value of transactions with each non-resident for goods, services, royalties, and interest; separate thresholds for loans and other transactions).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. The CRA may challenge transactions lacking economic substance, and the 'business purpose' test is used. For pharmaceutical manufacturing, the CRA expects real operational substance, including physical presence, employees, and decision-making in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fine of 50% to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax (gross negligence penalty).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unremitted source deductions (s. 227.1) and for unpaid GST/HST (s. 323). For tax evasion, directors may face criminal charges if they directed or acquiesced in the conduct. Civil restitution may include seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and extradition.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment (s. 245) disallowing tax benefits, plus penalties of 25% of the tax benefit (s. 245(4)). CRA may also deny treaty benefits under the Limitation of Benefits clause if applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the corporation's tax debts if they knowingly participated in the scheme. They may face personal assessments under the 'director's liability' provisions for unremitted taxes, and in severe cases, criminal charges for fraud.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) up to CAD 1 million for corporations per violation; criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment for individuals. FINTRAC can also suspend or revoke registration.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AMPs if they knowingly or negligently failed to ensure compliance. They may also face criminal charges for money laundering (up to 10 years imprisonment) and be subject to asset forfeiture.
