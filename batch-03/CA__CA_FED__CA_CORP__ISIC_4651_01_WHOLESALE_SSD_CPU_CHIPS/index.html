Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the CBCA. Shareholders may subscribe for common shares for nominal consideration. No escrow account or government deposit is required for a federal CBCA corporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and building occupancy clearance under the National Fire Code of Canada and provincial building codes are required only if a physical office or warehouse is occupied. The corporation must obtain occupational health and safety registration with the applicable provincial workers' compensation body if employees are hired. For a purely virtual brokerage with no employees or physical premises, no civil defense clearance is mandated.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the CBCA. Shareholders may subscribe for common shares for nominal consideration. No escrow account or government deposit is required for a federal CBCA corporation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); Business Registration Online (BRO) portal; My Business Account (MyBA) digital service; customs and duty administration by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CARM client portal and Single Window Initiative.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) via CRA Business Registration Online (Form RC1) before commencing Canadian wholesale operations. Step 2: Activate required program accounts — RT (T2 corporate income tax), RM (GST/HST), and RN (import/export). Step 3: For non-resident foreign entities, complete the non-resident registration questionnaires and appoint a Canadian agent for service where applicable; register through the MyBA portal using the prescribed online process. Step 4: For GST/HST, register as a non-resident registrant using GST/HST NETFILE or the BRO; voluntary registration is permitted even below the statutory threshold to claim input tax credits and zero-rate exports. Step 5: Obtain a CBSA client identifier within the BN for import/export accounts and complete any applicable customs surety requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A federally incorporated or recognized Canadian business corporation is a separate resident taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act; taxed on worldwide income at the entity level; non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income unless deemed resident; no pass-through check-the-box classification exists; the corporation is itself the GST/HST registrant.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election for corporations. Available statutory elections include: (i) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident to file net-basis Part I tax on Canadian rental or timber income instead of Part XIII gross withholding; (ii) Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related Canadian-resident corporations to make supplies for nil consideration; (iii) hybrid mismatch rules enacted pursuant to Budget 2021, which recharacterize instruments or impose deduction restrictions where a cross-border hybrid mismatch arises; (iv) no election to dual-classify an entity for US and Canadian purposes simultaneously, so an entity-level Canadian corporation remains a corporation for Canadian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration is available below the threshold to zero-rate export sales and recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property (SSDs, server processors/CPUs) shipped by the supplier to a destination outside Canada and exported shortly after supply are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA; the supply must be made to a non-resident purchaser or the goods must be delivered to a common carrier for export; business-to-business services performed offshore for non-residents are zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident and the place of supply is outside Canada; strict documentary evidence of export and of receipt of export consideration must be retained in the registrant's books and records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for non-US export shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 in value
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or rail) or Air Waybill with the exporter named as shipper and the foreign buyer as consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice with HS classification for SSDs (HS 8523.51) and server processors (HS 8542.31) and statement of incoterms
  • Cargo Control Document (CCD) and CBSA/CARM electronic release record showing actual export movement
  • Proof of delivery to the non-resident recipient, including signed delivery receipt or customs broker export certificate

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime exists in Canada. GST/HST returns must generally be filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or the MyBA API, with monthly, quarterly, or annual frequency based on taxable-supply volume. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing Service based on the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard is interoperable with enterprise ERPs but is not a fiscal clearance mandate. CBSA operates the CARM Digital Commercial System through the Single Window Initiative, requiring electronic import and export declarations and release processing for customs and cross-border GST/HST.

CRA may audit export zero-rating claims for up to 4 years, extended to 7 years in cases of misrepresentation or gross negligence, with no limitation period in cases of fraud. Gross-negligence penalties under ETA s. 285.1 equal the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax or refund claimed; criminal evasion under ETA s. 327 attracts fines up to 200% of the evaded GST/HST and imprisonment on conviction. Non-repatriation of export proceeds within the 180-day standard creates a presumption of retained export profits and may trigger Part XIII reassessments, daily compounded interest at the CRA prescribed arrears rate, denial of input tax credits or refunds, and retrospective clawback of zero-rated treatment until documentary proof of receipt of export consideration is produced.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange rate regime. No statutory restrictions exist on current-account or capital-account transactions, and no regulatory approval is required for profit remittance. The only frictions are (i) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at the 25% statutory rate, generally reduced under tax treaties (e.g., 5-15% on arm's-length dividends under the Canada-US treaty network); (ii) the solvency tests of the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) applicable to dividend or capital-reduction distributions; and (iii) the Investment Canada Act notification/review regime, which does not apply to routine repatriation of earnings of an operating wholesale entity. Funds may be held, settled and cleared in CAD, USD or third currencies without Bank of Canada approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement exists. Canada repealed its exchange-control legislation in 1951, and exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in any currency and in any jurisdiction, including offshore accounts. Remaining statutory obligations are limited to (i) CBSA export declaration (B13A or authorized service-provider filing) for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 or for controlled goods/technology, including semiconductor products subject to export-control classification; (ii) FINTRAC EFT reporting by the receiving/remitting bank for any electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; (iii) zero-rated GST/HST accounting for export sales; and (iv) retention of trade documentation for six years for Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) audit purposes and five years after the last transaction for PCMLTFA records. No mandatory conversion of export receipts into CAD is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; prudential regulation of banks is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign exchange control administration (there is no SAFE-type FX regulator); cross-border payment transparency is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary collections acceleration, Low for core treasury backbone. Legal constraints: an offshore EMI (e.g., a UK FCA or EU e-money institution) cannot take deposits in Canada under section 545 of the Bank Act, and its home licence is not passportable into Canada. Any non-bank payments business must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian agent, file EFT reports for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and maintain a Canadian-dollar clearing relationship with a Schedule I bank. For a semiconductor wholesale operation requiring inspection-escrow wires, letters of credit, and trade-finance sub-limits, only a Schedule I chartered bank can provide the full clearing rail; EMIs may be used solely as a low-cost collection layer for marketplace receivables.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: High-risk industry classification (ISIC 4651 semiconductor wholesale) combined with rapid escalations in wire volume that appear inconsistent with the stated spot-trading revenue profile; FINTRAC suspicious-indicator 'large, frequent, and complex transactions'.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No foreign-exchange control certificate is required. The receiving bank verifies source of funds against the corporate resolution authorizing issuance of common shares or contributed surplus; funds must never be remitted by unrelated third parties. If the inflow is structured as a shareholder loan rather than equity, execute a formal loan agreement bearing the CRA prescribed interest rate to prevent a deemed benefit under Income Tax Act sections 15(2) and 80.4. Greenfield incorporation does not trigger Investment Canada Act review; for any subsequent acquisition of control by a foreign shareholder above the applicable thresholds, file the required notification and document the exemption through counsel. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (plus provincial/territorial corporate rate of approximately 8%-16%, e.g., Ontario 11.5%, Quebec 11.5%, British Columbia 12%, Alberta 8%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII I.T.A. (s. 212(2)) on any dividend paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, including deemed dividends from conduit loans or non-arm's length advances (s. 15/214); non-resident corporation branch profit is subject to a parallel 25% Part XIV branch tax (s. 219).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous TP documentation (Local File / Master File in the OECD format) is administratively expected by CRA for all controlled cross-border transactions exceeding C$1,000,000 in aggregate; statutory transfer-pricing penalty under s. 247(3) applies when the net adjustment exceeds the lesser of C$5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue unless the taxpayer can demonstrate 'reasonable efforts,' including the existence of acceptable documentation prepared before the filing deadline.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, but economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 I.T.A.), the common-law sham doctrine, the s. 247 arm's-length reallocation rules, and the specified corporate income (SCI) restrictions on the Small Business Deduction. A Canadian wholesale entity that merely books title to SSD/CPU inventory while all procurement, pricing, credit risk, warehousing and sales decisions are taken abroad will be recharacterized: CRA may reallocate the trading margin to the non-resident principal, deny the intercompany purchase price, and assess Part XIII deemed-dividend withholding or Part XIV branch tax on the reattributed profit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misrepresentation (Income Tax Act ss. 163(2), 238, 239)

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated federal tax (s. 163(2)); criminal tax evasion on indictment is punishable by a fine of between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment for up to 5 years (s. 239); failure to file a return is separately punishable (s. 238) by a fine of C$1,000-C$25,000 and/or up to 12 months' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers or agents who 'directed, authorized, assented to, acquiesced in, or participated in' the evasion are personally liable to the same fines and imprisonment even if the corporation is not prosecuted (s. 239(2)); CRA may additionally assess directors under s. 227.1 for unremitted source deductions or GST/HST; civil restitution (disgorgement) may be pursued through the Federal Court, and travel restrictions may be ordered as bail or sentence conditions.
Shell Company / Conduit Abuse and GAAR (ss. 245, 247; common-law sham)

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the sham, reallocate income under s. 247(2) on the OECD-approved arm's-length basis, impose the 10% net-adjustment penalty under s. 247(3), deny tax benefits under the GAAR (s. 245), and assess Part XIII deemed-dividend withholding or Part XIV branch tax on the shifted profit; interest runs daily on the reassessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements, issue false invoices, or approve circular wire flows can be prosecuted for evasion; the corporate veil is lifted under s. 239(2) and s. 227.1; CRA can also issue third-party demands (s. 224.1.2) against directors' bank accounts and enforce seizure of personal assets, and pursue travel bans under court orders in criminal proceedings.
