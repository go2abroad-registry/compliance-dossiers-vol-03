Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA); GST/HST NETFILE for electronic returns. Provincial authority for PST: British Columbia Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC / myPST portal).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine GST/HST registration obligation — a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada and making taxable supplies in Canada must register unless it qualifies as a 'small supplier' (taxable supplies of C$30,000 or less in the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters). Step 2: Apply online through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal or submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), providing the legal name, registered office, and non-resident particulars; a Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account (RT) is issued, typically within 15 business days. Step 3: Register separately for BC PST through eTaxBC (myPST) with the BC Ministry of Finance; the OneStop BC business registry can process both corporate and PST registrations. Step 4: Because the BC ULC is a Canadian resident corporation for Canadian tax purposes, the ULC itself (not the foreign shareholders) holds the BN and files T2 corporate returns and GST/HST returns; non-resident shareholders are subject to Canadian withholding tax on dividends and other distributions. Step 5: Where the Canada–US treaty transparency election will be relied upon, document the consistent flow-through computation by each US-resident shareholder and preserve the supporting election statement for CRA examination.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC unlimited liability corporation (ULC) is a Canadian 'corporation' taxed at the entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act, but it is electively fiscally transparent (disregarded entity or partnership) for US federal tax purposes under Article IV(7) of the Canada–US Tax Convention where all members are US residents and compute their US tax on a consistent flow-through basis; territorial scope: worldwide income for Canadian-resident status; no tax-exempt zone treatment applies.

Available election forms

Treaty-based pass-through election under Article IV(7) of the Canada–US Tax Convention (each US-resident member must compute US tax on the ULC's items on a fiscally transparent basis, consistent with the treatment claimed; a prescribed statement of election and consistent US Form 8832 classification may be required for US purposes). Hybrid mismatch overlay: Canadian rules under Income Tax Act ss. 18.4–18.6 (effective for transactions after 30 June 2022) deny deductions to the extent of non-inclusion and import structural mismatches; no domestic check-the-box election exists under CRA practice.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 (CAD) of taxable supplies in Canada over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); mandatory registration once the threshold is exceeded or where taxable supplies are made and the supplier is not a small supplier, with voluntary registration allowed. BC PST: no general small-supplier exemption — every seller making taxable sales or leases of tangible personal property or taxable services in BC must register for a PST number and collect and remit PST; sellers without a place of business in BC may qualify for limited exemptions but still must register if they make taxable sales in the province.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated supplies under Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act: (i) goods supplied by way of sale that are exported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf, or delivered to a non-resident recipient who exports the goods as part of the supply — 0% GST, provided export occurs as soon as is reasonable after the supply; (ii) services supplied to a non-resident that relate exclusively to real property or tangible personal property located outside Canada — 0%; (iii) intangible personal property (e.g., franchise trademarks, formulations, and know-how licences) supplied to a non-resident person who is not a GST/HST registrant, where the property is for use exclusively outside Canada — 0%. Syrups and boba ingredients sold to franchisees outside Canada qualify for zero-rating only when physically shipped out of Canada and supported by documentary evidence of export; domestic BC sales attract GST 5% plus PST 7% unless a specific zero-rating or exemption applies.

  • CBSA Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or the Canada Customs Invoice
  • Ocean or air Bill of Lading / air waybill evidencing the physical movement of goods from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods, quantities, and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange receipt evidencing payment in a convertible currency from the non-resident buyer
  • Supplier's shipping and delivery records (truck, rail, ocean, or air receipts) showing export within a reasonable time

No real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory B2B e-invoicing or Continuous Transaction Control (CTC) regime in Canada as of the 2025 compliance year. GST/HST tax invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under section 3 of Schedule V to the Excise Tax Act (supplier identity, BN, date, description, consideration, tax shown) and may be issued in paper, PDF, or EDI format. Registrants with annual taxable sales above C$1,000,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or compatible EDI; the CRA accepts recorded sales summary data on returns rather than line-item transaction clearance. PST invoices, receipts, and returns are filed through the eTaxBC / myPST portal.

The CRA may reassess within four years of the filing date (unlimited period for misrepresentation or fraud) and will reverse zero-rating with interest if export documentation is deficient, goods return to Canada, or the 'exported as soon as is reasonable' condition is breached. Substantiation failures cause clawback of input tax credits and reassessment of uncollected GST at 5%. Statutory penalties include: GST fraud penalty under ETA s. 285(1) of the greater of C$250 and 25% of the tax sought; false statement or omission penalties under ETA s. 280(1) between C$250 and C$10,000; penalties for failing to provide records under s. 280(2); and CBSA enforcement under the Customs Act (forfeiture or a fine up to C$25,000, or both) for false export declarations. Interest is compounded daily at the CRA prescribed rate on all clawed-back amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. No foreign-exchange license or prior central-bank approval is generally required for cross-border remittances. Profit repatriation by a BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is available via dividends, management fees, royalties, or interest, subject to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (normally 25% unless reduced by treaty, with Canada-US treaty considerations for a ULC's check-the-box pass-through treatment) and to transfer-pricing documentation for cross-border related-party charges. Inbound investment by non-residents is screened only under the Investment Canada Act for control or enterprise-value thresholds; no exchange-control restriction applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement that export receipts be deposited in a Canadian bank account. For the boba-tea franchise's syrup and equipment exports and cross-border licensing royalties, invoices may be settled in CAD, USD, or other freely agreed currencies; only customs/export reporting, tax attribution, and exchange-rate translation rules under the Income Tax Act and relevant provincial legislation apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for federally regulated bank licensing and prudential supervision; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT oversight and money-service-business registration. Canada does not maintain a centralized foreign-exchange controller or exchange-control authority under the former Foreign Exchange Control Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium as a primary clearing rail. Offshore digital EMI accounts are not recognized as Canadian deposit accounts and cannot route through the Canadian Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) or the Real-Time Rail. A non-resident fintech may operate only as a money-service business registered with FINTRAC, or as an authorized foreign bank branch if deposit-taking, and must comply with British Columbia consumer-protection and money-lending rules. Use as an ancillary virtual IBAN or settlement service is possible, but principal treasury accounts should be held with a Schedule I bank to ensure reliable CAD clearing and regulatory certainty.
