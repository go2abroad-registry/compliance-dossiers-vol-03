Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box regime. A BC ULC may, however, file US Form 8832 to elect to be treated as a disregarded entity or a corporation for US federal income tax. For Canadian tax purposes, the ULC remains a separate person subject to Entity_Level_Taxation. Relevant statutory elections include: (a) the s.114.2 election to treat certain ULC distributions as eligible dividends for treaty purposes; (b) the joint election under GST/HST ETA s.156 for closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration; (c) no protective election exists for hybrid mismatch rules, which apply automatically under ITA ss.18.4–18.8.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) for GST/HST registration under ETA s.148(1); BC PST requires registration upon the first taxable sale in BC with no revenue threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of cross-border tax structuring advisory services to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if the recipient is not resident in Canada at the time consideration becomes due and the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and no exception under s.7 of Part V applies. The supplier must retain proof of the recipient's non-resident status, the contract or written engagement, and evidence that the consideration was actually received from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form A8A/B3) for any goods element; not required for pure service exports
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical export (where incidental supply of goods is present)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT payment confirmation evidencing payment from outside Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-linked e-invoicing, or digital stamping for GST/HST. Electronic invoices are permissible and must be retained in an electronically readable format per CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4-3 and ITA s.286. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA My Business Account; pre-issuance clearance is not required.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess GST/HST at the full rate, with interest and penalties. Gross negligence penalties under ETA s.280 can be up to 25% of the amount reported incorrectly; deliberate evasion may lead to prosecution under ITA s.239 with fines of up to 200% of the amount evaded and imprisonment. The standard reassessment window is four years, extended to seven in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. Exports must be substantiated by contemporaneous documentation maintained for at least six years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating Canadian-dollar exchange rate with fully unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. Canada accepted Article VIII status of the IMF Articles of Agreement on March 25, 1952, and is a party to the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Consequently, there are no exchange-control permits, no currency-allocation licences, no repatriation approvals, and no transaction-level FX authorisation requirements. A British Columbia unlimited liability corporation (BC ULC) may lawfully receive equity injections, borrow abroad, pay management fees, reimburse foreign subcontractors, and remit dividends without prior central-bank consent. Practical hurdles are limited to: (i) FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting for physical currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) bank-level sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act, the United Nations Act, OFAC, and UK OFSI lists; (iii) Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) withholding taxes on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) (25% statutory, reduced to 0%, 5%, or 15% under the Canada–US Income Tax Convention of 1980 upon submission of Form NR301 or NR302 together with the applicable treaty certification); and (iv) Investment Canada Act net-benefit review thresholds for foreign acquisitions of control of a Canadian business. Profit remittance from a BC ULC is therefore a tax-compliance and banking-knowledge event rather than an exchange-control event.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable in Canada. The Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and the Foreign Exchange Control Board dissolved; there is no statutory timeline for surrendering export receipts, no mandatory conversion ratio into Canadian dollars, and no authorised-dealer system requiring export proceeds to be repatriated or converted. Exporters of services, including ISIC 7020 tax-structuring and transfer-pricing advisory firms, may invoice in foreign currency and retain USD, EUR, GBP, or any convertible currency proceeds indefinitely in a Canadian or offshore account. Residual obligations are limited to: (i) FINTRAC reporting for cross-border movements of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) translation of foreign-currency revenue into CAD for income-tax and GST/HST filing purposes using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate or a rate acceptable to CRA under s. 261 of the Income Tax Act; and (iii) accurate SWIFT / balance-of-payments purpose coding on incoming wires. Export Development Canada (EDC) provides receivables insurance, bonding, and trade-finance guarantees for Canadian exporters but imposes no conversion or surrender mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — Canada's central bank and monetary authority under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. B-2) — together with the Department of Finance Canada, which holds statutory responsibility for exchange-rate policy and international financial policy. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) regulates all domestic banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and supervises prudential solvency and liquidity. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17) for AML/CFT supervision and cross-border currency reporting. Payments Canada operates the clearing and settlement infrastructure: the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS, migrating to the Lynx Real-Time Rail platform), the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS), and the SWIFT gateway for cross-border payment messages. There is no standalone 'Foreign Exchange Administration'; Canada abolished its Foreign Exchange Control Board in 1951 and has not re-imposed exchange-control administration since.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary disbursement rail; Low as a substitute for the primary operating account. Since November 1, 2024, the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23) requires any payment service provider (PSP) performing clearing, settlement, or fund-transfer functions in CAD to be registered with the Bank of Canada; offshore digital EMIs (UK/EU-licensed platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex) are not automatically passportable into Canada and must either register under the transitional window ending October 31, 2025, or operate through a registered Canadian PSP agent. Material legal constraints for the BC ULC are: (i) funds held at an unregistered foreign EMI are unsecured receivables, not CDIC-insured deposits; (ii) the Schedule I bank may reject incoming wires from or outgoing wires to certain foreign PSPs under correspondent-bank due-diligence matrices; (iii) FINTRAC requires the ULC to perform enhanced due diligence on the PSP as a third-party service provider; and (iv) transfer-pricing risk under s. 247 of the Income Tax Act arises if the PSP charges non-arm's-length FX spreads to the foreign shareholder. Viable structure: maintain the ULC's operating and escrow accounts at RBC, TD, or BMO; use Wise Platform or Airwallex through the bank's embedded-finance API for low-value, high-frequency payments to international transfer-pricing subcontractors.

(3) for USD-denominated injections, maintain a USD daily-clearing account at the same bank instead of spot-converting at the teller, thereby avoiding FX spread and preserving a natural hedge against USD payables; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as a supplementary disbursement rail; Low as a substitute for the primary operating account. Since November 1, 2024, the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23) requires any payment service provider (PSP) performing clearing, settlement, or fund-transfer functions in CAD to be registered with the Bank of Canada; offshore digital EMIs (UK/EU-licensed platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex) are not automatically passportable into Canada and must either register under the transitional window ending October 31, 2025, or operate through a registered Canadian PSP agent. Material legal constraints for the BC ULC are: (i) funds held at an unregistered foreign EMI are unsecured receivables, not CDIC-insured deposits; (ii) the Schedule I bank may reject incoming wires from or outgoing wires to certain foreign PSPs under correspondent-bank due-diligence matrices; (iii) FINTRAC requires the ULC to perform enhanced due diligence on the PSP as a third-party service provider; and (iv) transfer-pricing risk under s. 247 of the Income Tax Act arises if the PSP charges non-arm's-length FX spreads to the foreign shareholder. Viable structure: maintain the ULC's operating and escrow accounts at RBC, TD, or BMO; use Wise Platform or Airwallex through the bank's embedded-finance API for low-value, high-frequency payments to international transfer-pricing subcontractors.
