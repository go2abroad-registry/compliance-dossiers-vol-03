Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency/Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC); portal: CRA My Business Account - https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html; BC provincial sales tax administered by BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC - https://etax.gov.bc.ca

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the BC ULC under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) via BC Registry Online and obtain the provincial incorporation number (no minimum capital). Step 2: Register the ULC with CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or Form RC1 to obtain the statutory 9-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts: corporate income tax (RC0001) and GST/HST (RT0001); a non-resident can register once it commences carrying on business in Canada. Step 3: Submit initial corporate intake/return and designating the registered office and Canadian-resident director required by the BCBCA. Step 4: If making taxable sales in BC, obtain a BC PST vendor account through eTaxBC (no monetary threshold). Step 5: If US-owned, file US Form 8832 (check-the-box) within 75 days of desired effective date so the ULC may be treated as a pass-through/disregarded entity for US federal tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Hybrid Entity Classification: Entity_Level_Taxation under the Income Tax Act (Canada) - a BC ULC is a corporation for Canadian tax purposes, subject to Part I federal and BC provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income; Pass_Through_Eligible under US check-the-box rules (Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3) for US federal purposes because shareholders bear unlimited liability; for GST/HST purposes, the ULC is a single registrant, and it may be eligible for territorial scope of taxation based on supplies made inside/outside Canada.

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists; the ULC cannot elect to be a partnership/disregarded entity under the ITA. Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (i) US Form 8832 check-the-box election for pass-through status of a foreign eligible entity; (ii) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident owners of Canadian rental real estate (not the core business here); (iii) treaty-based residency tie-breaker under Canada-US Convention Art. IV(3) to establish place of effective management; (iv) hybrid mismatch/EIFA elections and funding structures under ITA ss. 18.4-18.6 to manage excessive interest and financing expenses; and (v) ETA registrations and input tax credit allocation elections for non-resident registrants. Election outcomes must be documented in the ULC's books to avoid GAAR recharacterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 - a registrant is required if worldwide taxable (non-zero-rated) supplies exceed C$30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in the current and preceding three consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s. 240); non-resident digital suppliers of services/IPP to Canadian customers are subject to the same threshold and may use the simplified non-resident registration. BC PST: no monetary threshold - vendor registration is required from the first taxable sale/lease of goods or taxable services in BC, including online sales subject to PST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(a) Exported physical goods (prints or media): zero-rated under ETA s. 16 and Part V of Schedule VI when the supplier transfers possession/title to a non-resident and the goods are exported as part of a contract of carriage within the statutory time. (b) Licence of stock photos / generative AI visual assets: classified as a supply of intangible personal property (IPP) and zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V if the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST and the IPP is not for use/consumption in Canada; the contract must clearly convey a licence for use outside Canada. (c) Offshore services: a supply of services to a non-resident is zero-rated if rendered entirely outside Canada (ETA s. 9(b)); platform/hosting services must be verifiably performed outside Canada. The supplier must hold documentary evidence of non-resident status (foreign address, TIN), delivery/destination records, and repatriation of proceeds within the 180-day benchmark.

  • CBSA Export Declaration or equivalent export reporting (Form B13A / electronic single-window filing) where physical goods are exported
  • Commercial invoice and licence/subscription agreement expressly stating the supply is made to a non-resident and the applicable zero-rating basis
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill or courier waybill evidencing exportation from Canada within the required period
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank advice confirming receipt of foreign proceeds in Canadian dollars within the 180-day repatriation deadline

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control mandate. Suppliers must issue an invoice (paper or electronic) no later than 15 days after the supply, containing the supplier's BN, recipient name, description and tax amounts (ETA s. 223). Electronic invoices are accepted, and GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) by registrants with annual taxable supplies over C$1.5M or when using a tax professional. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing interoperability via Peppol is voluntary. BC PST: vendors must keep sales records/invoices and file PST returns via eTaxBC; no government digital stamping or fiscal API clearance is required.

CRA can reassess improperly zero-rated supplies within the normal 4-year period, extended to 8 years for misrepresentation attributable to negligence or fraud (ETA s. 298; ITA s. 152(4)). Overclaimed export refunds and input tax credits are clawed back with compound interest. Civil penalties: ETA s. 285 false-statement penalty of 25% of net tax understated or refund overstated (minimum C$250); ETA s. 280 failure-to-remit penalties. Criminal tax evasion (ETA s. 327 / ITA s. 239): fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment. CRA export audit practice requires contemporaneous export documentation (contracts, customs entries, carrier and bank remittance records) for digital supplies, and the application of general anti-avoidance rules (s. 245 ITA) may also unwind artificial export structures.
