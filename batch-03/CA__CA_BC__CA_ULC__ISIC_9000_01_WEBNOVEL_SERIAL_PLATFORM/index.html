Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; Business Registration Online via canada.ca and My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html)

E-government filing portal

A British Columbia Unlimited Liability Company is constituted under the BC Business Corporations Act and receives a provincial incorporation number. To obtain the federal Business Number (BN) and tax program accounts, the ULC must register with CRA using Business Registration Online or Form RC1, submitting the BC incorporation certificate, registered office address, expected revenue data, and director/shareholder particulars. An RT program account must be requested for GST/HST. In British Columbia, the BC Ministry of Finance Consumer Taxation Branch separately issues a PST registration number for retail sales taxable in BC. Non-resident shareholders are permitted, and a Canadian registered office or authorized representative must be designated for service of documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

CRA treats a BC ULC as a taxable corporation, and no Canadian check-the-box election is available. For U.S. federal income tax only, the ULC is an eligible entity that may file IRS Form 8832 to elect disregarded entity or partnership status; Canada's hybrid mismatch rules in Income Tax Act sections 93.1 and 94 may limit treaty or deduction benefits in such structures. Under the Excise Tax Act, registered closely-related corporations may also elect under section 156 to treat certain taxable supplies as made for nil consideration for GST/HST purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold under GST/HST, calculated on taxable supplies made over the preceding four calendar quarters; BC PST requires separate vendor registration for taxable retail sales in British Columbia and is not governed by a federal-equivalent small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital subscription services and web-novel content supplied by a Canadian-registered ULC are zero-rated under GST/HST when the recipient is a non-resident who is outside Canada, the supply is not in respect of real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada, and the intangible personal property is not for use in Canada. The supplier must obtain and retain documentary evidence of the recipient's residence and of offshore delivery/access, including payment-card origin, billing address, IP address, geolocation logs, subscription contract, and platform usage records. Tangible goods exports are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act when substantiated by customary customs documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for physical goods; for digital web-novel subscriptions, replaced by CRA-acceptable digital export records and subscriber geolocation logs)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (for goods; for online platforms, use CDN access logs, IP-resolution evidence, and API delivery logs)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice evidencing foreign-currency receipt and applicable FX repatriation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary through the Pan-Canadian Peppol network and becomes mandatory only for vendors supplying by electronic means to the federal government. CRA requires invoices and supporting records to contain prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act but does not operate an immediate fiscal approval server.

If zero-rating is claimed without sufficient substantiation, CRA may reassess the supply as taxable within the normal four-year period (or beyond where misrepresentation is shown), collect uncollected GST plus interest, deny input tax credits, and impose penalties. The Excise Tax Act authorizes a $100 plus 50% legal-confidence penalty where a false statement is made knowingly or in circumstances of gross negligence; the Customs Act separately permits monetary penalties and forfeiture for export misdeclarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no general exchange controls, approval mechanisms, or restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, or capital. A BC ULC may freely remit profits to non-resident shareholders after application of the relevant non-resident withholding tax (Part XIII), which may be reduced under treaty. Capital contributions and equity injections are unrestricted, provided they do not violate sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act or the United Nations Act. Beneficial ownership reporting to CRA and FINTRAC is required for transactions above prescribed thresholds (CAD 10,000).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds is required. Revenue from digital webnovel subscriptions and app-store sales may be retained in foreign currency. However, receipt of funds exceeding CAD 10,000 or equivalent must be reported to FINTRAC as a large cash transaction or electronic funds transfer if initiated cross-border. For Canadian income tax, all revenue must be converted to CAD at the CRA's published exchange rate for the relevant taxation year; no timeline-based conversion obligation is imposed by law.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) administers AML/ATF and cross-border transaction reporting; the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) administers currency reporting for tax and customs purposes. Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration body; exchange policy is legislated under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low–Medium. Canada does not issue digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licences comparable to the EEA; foreign EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise, N26) cannot hold Canadian deposit accounts or provide Canadian routing/accounts unless registered as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC. A cross-border EMI may be used as a supplementary disbursement tool for author payouts under an MSB arrangement, but the ULC must maintain its primary operational and capital accounts with a Canadian chartered Tier 1 bank for CAD clearing, FX, and regulatory compliance. The payment rails from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are typically acquired through Canadian PSPs that are also FINTRAC-registered MSBs.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) treated as a pass-through or hybrid entity for foreign tax purposes, causing ambiguity in beneficial ownership and economic substance.

Defense evidence: Provide a legal opinion from a Canadian corporate law firm confirming the ULC's valid incorporation under BC law and its unlimited liability attributes; produce the Certificate of Incorporation, Notice of Articles, Memorandum of Articles, corporate registers, and a detailed ownership chart showing ultimate beneficial owners. Submit CRA business number registration, GST/HST account confirmation, and, if applicable, a section 2602 election or tax treatment memorandum for the ULC's Canadian federal tax status.

Cross-border outbound remittances to non-resident authors or content licensors in high-risk jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Maintain a complete roster of authors and licensors with contractual agreements, copyright assignments, and tax forms (NR4, NR301, or W-8BEN-E). Apply enhanced due diligence for payees in sanctioned or high-risk countries; perform OFAC / Government of Canada sanctions screening; retain ITR (International Transfer Report) records for FINTRAC; use a payment aggregator with KYB/KYC vetting for each payee.

Defense evidence: Prepare a share subscription agreement, director resolutions approving the issuance, bank reference letters for the remitting institution, and a source-of-wealth statement from the subscriber. Use a designated Capital Escrow Account with a clear memo 'Share Subscription – CA_ULC', and complete the bank's beneficial ownership and politically exposed persons (PEP) questionnaires. Retain a legal opinion on the application of Canada's anti-money laundering rules to the inbound equity transfer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital to the BC ULC must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire/CHIPS for USD) to the Capital Escrow Account held with a Tier 1 Canadian bank. The payment instruction must reference the company legal name, the ULC registration number, and the memo 'ULC Share Subscription – [Investor Ref]'. Use the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' for equity injection, or 'BOND' if structured as intercompany debt. For amounts exceeding CAD 10,000, the bank will trigger FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting; it is prudent to submit a Notification of Payment Tracker (if applicable) and provide the bank with a ready evidence pack including: incorporation documents, share subscription agreement, board resolution, and source-of-funds declaration. No regulatory pre-approval is needed; however, the bank's compliance team must clear the injection before funds are made operational.Outbound author royalty and license payments should use SWIFT MT103 for non-Canadian beneficiaries, with standard 56A/D/57A beneficiary bank information and purpose code 'ROYL' (royalty) or 'GDSV' (digital service) as appropriate. For Canadian payees, use Payment Canada's EFT (Credit Clearing System) or Real-Time Rail (RTR, if available) with ISO 20022 messages. For high-volume serialized webnovel author payouts, an integrated mass-pay platform (e.g., through Wise, Airwallex, or a FINTRAC-registered PSP) can batch MT103/102 messages, but core operating cash positions must remain in the Canadian bank account to facilitate FX settlements. A Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) is generally not required because the business involves digital content licences; if an LC is demanded for platform exclusivity agreements, a stand-by LC may be issued by the Tier 1 bank against cash collateral.
