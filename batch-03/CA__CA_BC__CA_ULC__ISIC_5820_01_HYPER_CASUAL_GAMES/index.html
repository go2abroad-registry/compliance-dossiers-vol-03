Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. A share block may be issued for any consideration authorized by the directors, and consideration must be paid before or at issue. No escrow account is mandated; the registered office must be located in British Columbia.

Funding & unlock path: Premises must satisfy the BC Building Code and Fire Code occupancy requirements and, where applicable, obtain a fire safety certificate. WorksafeBC occupational health and safety compliance, including employer registration and workplace inspections, is required. No civil-defence clearance applies to a non-industrial, office-based software publisher.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. A share block may be issued for any consideration authorized by the directors, and consideration must be paid before or at issue. No escrow account is mandated; the registered office must be located in British Columbia.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) for federal GST/HST, T2 corporate income tax and Business Number issuance; online portal is CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO). The British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Provincial Tax Services, administers the BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST).

E-government filing portal

The BC ULC must first be incorporated under the BC Business Corporations Act via BC Corporate Online, obtaining a BC incorporation number. Next, register with CRA through BRO to obtain the 9-digit Business Number (BN) and activate program accounts RC0001 (GST/HST), RC0002 (T2 corporate income tax) and RP0001 (payroll). As a corporation incorporated in Canada, the ULC is a Canadian resident corporation and must file T2 returns. GST/HST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies other than zero-rated supplies exceed C$30,000; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits when supplies are exclusively zero-rated exports. For PST, submit BC Taxpayer Registration (FIN 419) to the BC Ministry of Finance before making taxable sales of software or digital products consumable in BC; no de minimis threshold applies for PST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under ITA Part I for domestic purposes, but under Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Tax Treaty its shareholders may file a US federal check-the-box election on Form 8832 to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity (single US shareholder) or partnership (multiple US shareholders), making it fiscally transparent solely for US federal tax purposes. Canada requires no parallel domestic election, so the entity remains an entity-level taxpayer in Canada; hybrid-mismatch outcomes are mitigated under the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework reflected in Canadian treaty policy statements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 under ETA s. 148; registration is required once worldwide taxable supplies other than financial services and sales of capital real property exceed C$30,000 in the current calendar quarter or over the preceding four calendar quarters. Under the simplified framework, a non-resident vendor without a Canadian establishment must register once its taxable supplies to Canadian recipients exceed C$30,000 in a 12-month period. Zero-rated export supplies to AdMob and AppLovin typically fall outside the counting of taxable supplies for threshold purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods are zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI, Part V. Services supplied to a non-resident — including advertising inventory services sold to non-resident ad networks (AdMob, AppLovin) and licensing/distribution services to non-resident app-store operators — are zero-rated under ETA Sch. VI, Part V, s. 7 provided the recipient is a non-resident at the time payment becomes due, the service is not connected to real or tangible property located in Canada, and the service is not performed wholly in Canada in circumstances where the recipient is deemed to be in Canada. The ULC must obtain and retain evidence of each payer's non-resident status and the foreign consumption of the service.

  • Canada Customs Coding Form B13A (Export Declaration) or CERS/NPDS electronic export report for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing export from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and ad-network/app-store agreements identifying the non-resident counterparty and foreign payee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statements showing foreign-currency consideration credited to the Canadian bank account
  • Tax residency certificates for the non-resident recipients; all books and records retained for at least six years under ETA s. 286 and ITA s. 230

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based continuous transaction control regime. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) by registrants with total annual taxable supplies above C$1.5M, and T2 returns must be e-filed for corporations with gross revenue exceeding C$1M. The voluntary pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing framework is built on the Peppol UBL/BIS 3.0 standard and operates through certified service providers without government clearance middleware.

CRA may reassess within four years of the original assessment, or indefinitely where misrepresentation, negligence, or wilful default is established; deficient export evidence causes zero-rating to be denied and full 5% GST plus retroactive interest to be charged. False statements or omissions attract the ETA s. 285(1) penalty (greater of C$250 and 5% of the amount assessed), and gross negligence on the income tax side triggers ITA s. 163(2) at 50% of the understated tax; deliberate export-fraud schemes are subject to criminal prosecution. BC PST clawbacks and penalties are enforced separately by the BC Ministry of Finance under the Provincial Sales Tax Act and the provincial Tax Administration Act.
