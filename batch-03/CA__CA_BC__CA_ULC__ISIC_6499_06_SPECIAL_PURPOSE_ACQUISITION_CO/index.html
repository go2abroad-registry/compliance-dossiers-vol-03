Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office and a records office in British Columbia are mandatory (BCA s. 53) and must be a physical street address, not a post office box. Registered-agent virtual office services are legally accepted for a private ULC. However, for a SPAC proceeding to reporting-issuer status, the BC Securities Commission and Canadian securities administrators expect a genuine principal office in Canada, substantive local directors and officers, and an independent Canadian auditor; a purely virtual presence is regulatorily and commercially disadvantaged for a public-market acquisition vehicle.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully convertible regime for both current and capital accounts pursuant to the Foreign Exchange and Other Transactions Regulations. Capital account transactions, including equity investments and profit remittances, are permitted without prior approval for legitimate corporate purposes. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal for SPAC entities when supported by audited financial statements, acquisition agreements, and FINTRAC-compliant documentation; repatriation of funds to Canadian shareholders or SPAC sponsors is unrestricted subject to standard beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is required under Canadian law. Export receipts may be converted freely into Canadian dollars or retained offshore with voluntary surrender permitted at any time. There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios; however, all FX transactions exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC, and SPAC-related export activities require alignment with the company's acquisition business plan for compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is constrained in Canada by OSFI licensing requirements and FINTRAC AML obligations. Offshore digital EMIs face significant legal constraints including mandatory registration as money services businesses and enhanced due diligence for SPAC structures. Remote onboarding via regulated platforms such as Stripe or Wise is feasible for operational accounts but prohibited for high-value capital injections without full KYC and source-of-funds verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from investors or SPAC sponsors is routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers using purpose codes such as 'ACQ' for acquisition funding or 'INV' for equity investment. All wires require matching source-of-funds documentation, beneficial ownership verification, and alignment with the SPAC's approved business plan. Verification protocols include FINTRAC reporting for transactions over CAD 10,000 and Bank of Canada FX reporting where applicable; purpose codes must be explicitly stated to facilitate regulatory scrutiny.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements utilize SWIFT MT103 for international wire transfers or Commercial Letters of Credit for structured trade finance. For SPAC-related acquisition activities, payments are directed to target company accounts with full documentation including purchase agreements, wire instructions, regulatory approvals, and escrow release conditions.
