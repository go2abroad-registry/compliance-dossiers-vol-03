Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/business-registration-online and CRA My Business Account portal (GST/HST account 'RT', corporate income tax, payroll, import/export accounts).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident foreign business corporation: (1) incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) via Corporations Canada, or register extra-provincially, to obtain a federal incorporation number; (2) request a 9-digit Business Number (BN) through BRO or by filing form RC1 with the CRA, which serves as the statutory TIN; (3) open the BN program accounts electronically: 'RT' (GST/HST), 'RC' (corporate income tax), 'RP' (payroll), and 'RM' (import/export); (4) register for GST/HST once the small-supplier threshold is exceeded (CAD 30,000 in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters) or voluntarily to claim input tax credits; (5) complete the non-resident account setup — no separate TIN is issued; the BN+RT and BN+RC identifiers are the operative tax registration numbers for federal indirect and direct tax filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through regime applies to a corporation. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada files a T2 return on its Canadian-source taxable income and is additionally exposed to Part XIV branch tax (25%, treaty-reducible) on the branch's Canadian earnings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under Part I of the Income Tax Act; no pass-through regime applies to a corporation. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada files a T2 return on its Canadian-source taxable income and is additionally exposed to Part XIV branch tax (25%, treaty-reducible) on the branch's Canadian earnings. Territorial scope: resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxed on business income attributable to a permanent establishment in Canada plus gains from taxable Canadian property.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian law. Available tax elections relevant at this stage: (i) Part XIV branch tax election under s.219(2) allowing a non-resident corporation to compute branch tax on a residual basis; (ii) s.216 election for net rental income of non-residents owning Canadian real property; (iii) s.39(4) and s.45(3) capital-gain deferral elections (as applicable); (iv) Excise Tax Act s.156 election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as not a supply and s.211 nil-consideration elections; (v) hybrid mismatch treatment under Income Tax Act ss.18.4–18.6 is mandatory and not electable; the OECD MLI and CFC attribution rules apply automatically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold — compulsory registration once taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (ETA s.240); voluntary registration is available and permits input tax credit recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods under s.1, Part V, Schedule VI ETA applies when: (i) goods are sold and physically delivered outside Canada by the supplier; (ii) goods are sold to a non-resident person and delivered by the supplier to a common carrier for export within 60 days; or (iii) goods are exported as commercial goods under the Customs Act by an operator registered under the CERS. For food manufacturing (instant rice noodles), the supply is a basic grocery zero-rated under s.1 Part III Schedule VI when sold domestically; exports additionally must satisfy Part V documentary proof requirements. Claims may be made on the GST/HST return claiming net-tax (refund) for exported supplies; the CRA applies a 4-year reassessment period (7 years for negligence/misrepresentation).

  • Customs Export Declaration (CERS/G7 electronic filing with the CBSA, incl. the Export Report or CAED)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods out of Canada
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (exporter, consignee, HS code 1904/1902, INCOTERM, and unit value)
  • Cargo Control Document or carrier manifest / terminal receipts
  • Bank-generated proof of foreign receipt — letter of credit, SWIFT MT103, or inward remittance certificate
  • Purchase order and customs clearance remarks from the destination jurisdiction (when obtainable)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping mandate applies in Canada. The CRA accepts electronic invoices and records under the Electronic Commerce Audit Technique (ECAT) guide; invoices must state the supplier's BN/RT registration number, date, description, quantity, amount, and GST/HST charged. Export supplies are substantiated through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) rather than through tax-invoice clearance.

Ineligible zero-rating or export-refund claims are reassessed with arrears interest (ETA s.280); misstatement penalties of 5%–10% of net tax apply (s.280(1)); gross negligence penalties up to CAD 25,000 (s.285); false or inflated rebate claims trigger the s.329 penalty and potential criminal prosecution (s.327/330 ETA; Criminal Code s.380). The CRA's record-retention requirement is six years (s.286 ETA), and the limitation window extends indefinitely in cases of fraud. Retrospective clawback includes denial of input tax credits attributable to the zero-rated exports and refund recovery from the exporter or intermediaries.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalized free currency regime with unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital accounts under the Bank of Canada Act and Exchange Control Regulations. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without prior approval, subject only to Canadian tax obligations and beneficial ownership disclosure requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts and repatriate them freely. All foreign exchange transactions must comply with Bank of Canada reporting thresholds (typically CAD 10,000 per transaction or annual aggregate reporting). For Canadian tax purposes, proceeds are generally required to be accounted for in CAD within statutory timelines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (38% base rate less 10% provincial abatement and 13% General Tax Reduction). Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) benefit from a 9% federal rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII on gross dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 must be filed no later than 6 months after the end of the taxation year (a December 31 year-end is due June 30). The unpaid tax balance is due within 2 months after year-end for most corporations and within 3 months for CCPCs eligible for the Small Business Deduction.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on the same date as the T2 return, being 6 months after the fiscal period end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 filing threshold: aggregate covered transactions with each non-arm's length non-resident exceeding CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) for any covered transaction above this threshold. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245), which permits CRA to deny the tax benefit of any avoidance transaction lacking bona fide economic substance, misusing or abusing the Act or a treaty. The GAAR penalty is 50% of the tax benefit for transactions after April 7, 2022. The common-law sham doctrine and the substance-over-form principle also apply. A federally incorporated Business Corporation must maintain a registered office, a board with at least 25% resident Canadian directors (CBCA requirement), statutory minute books, and complete financial records. A functioning rice noodle production facility with employees, equipment, and local sales constitutes substantive economic presence and must reflect arm's length profits in Canada.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Books

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s. 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment not exceeding 5 years. Gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2): 50% of the understated tax. CRA may reassess beyond the normal 3-year limitation period where fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false returns face criminal liability under s. 239(2) with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to CAD 200,000. Section 160 of the ITA makes shareholders who received transferred property at less than fair market value jointly and severally liable. A court may order asset restraint and travel bans under Criminal Code s. 462.33 where a director is a flight risk with recoverable proceeds.
