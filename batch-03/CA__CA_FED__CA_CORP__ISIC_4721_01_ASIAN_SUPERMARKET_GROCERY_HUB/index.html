Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), administered under the Minister of National Revenue; customs export/import filing authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Electronic portals: My Business Account (MBA), Business Registration Online (BRO), GST/HST NETFILE; corporate income tax (T2) and GST/HST returns are filed through CRA at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A federal/provincial business corporation is a separate legal taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act and cannot elect flow-through treatment. A corporation resident in Canada is taxable on worldwide income (ITA s. 2(1)); a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada is taxable on Canadian-source business income (ITA s. 2(2) and s. 115). Territorial scope: business income and capital tax (if any) are allocated to provinces via the permanent establishment formula; no federal tax-exempt zone applies to the ASIAN SUPERMARKET GROCERY HUB. If the corporation is a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), the federal small business deduction reduces the rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income; if controlled by one or more non-residents, the entity is a non-resident-controlled corporation (NRCC) and is subject to the federal general corporate rate.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box liability election; tax classification follows the incorporation statute. Available elections relevant to the grocery hub: (i) GST/HST group election under Excise Tax Act s. 156 — closely related corporations may treat intercompany supplies as made for nil consideration; (ii) ITA s. 85 rollover election to transfer an existing proprietorship/unincorporated business into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (iii) ITA s. 125 associated-corporation election (T2SCH125) to allocate the CAD 500,000 small business limit among associated CCPCs; (iv) ITA s. 216/115.1 elections to compute Canadian tax on a net basis for non-resident owners of Canadian real estate or in reliance on an income tax treaty. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.2 et seq. and Part XIII withholding on hybrid instruments) apply statutorily without an election; the corporation must document debt vs equity characterization and dual-inclusion income streams.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in gross worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; below this threshold the person is a small supplier and is not required to register; voluntary registration is available and permits input tax credit claims and zero-rating of exported groceries. Non-resident retailers making taxable supplies in Canada are subject to the same threshold unless a deemed-supply rule for electronic commerce applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1, a registered supplier's supply of tangible personal property (TPP) is zero-rated where the TPP is exported by the supplier or on its behalf from Canada in the ordinary course of the supplier's business and the goods are not further processed in Canada after export. Domestic sales of unprepared groceries and most condiments/vegetables are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part III provided the goods are food for human consumption and not excluded (beverages, confectionery, snack foods sold in single-serving packages, or prepared foods heated in-store). Exported groceries must independently satisfy the Part V export tests. Drop-shipment relief (ETA s. 144.1 and s. 152-153) permits a Canadian vendor to zero-rate goods sold to a registered non-resident recipient and delivered to a Canadian warehouse/agent for subsequent export. Export refund mechanics: net tax = GST/HST collected on taxable supplies (0%) minus input tax credits on rent, imported inventory, logistics and overhead; export-oriented registrants thereby file periodic GST/HST refund returns.

  • CBSA B13 Export Declaration (or Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) / G7 EDI equivalent) for shipments with a value above CAD 2,000, filed with the Canada Border Services Agency
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / carrier receipt showing physical movement of the consignment from Canada to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or corresponding bank/EFT statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds and foreign-currency repatriation
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and purchase order/sales contract, reconciled to the GST/HST return line item
  • Customs release or import entry documentation from the destination country, where available, and the customs broker audit trail
  • CAED confirmation number and exporter record (CBSA export transaction number) linked to the Canadian principal

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government API clearance exists at the federal level for GST/HST. Canada operates a self-assessed, document-based system. A valid invoice must show the supplier's name and BN/RM details, transaction date, total consideration and either the GST/HST amount charged or the zero-rating indicator. E-invoices generated in PDF, EDI or API formats are accepted without pre-clearance. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing specification (Peppol PINT/BIS 3.0) has been adopted for voluntary B2G and B2B interoperability; no CRA pre-authorization or stamping is required. Filing mandate: registrants with annual gross revenue above CAD 1.5 million must file GST/HST returns electronically; remittance by electronic funds transfer is mandatory where average monthly net tax exceeds CAD 10,000.

CRA and CBSA jointly audit export zero-rating claims and input tax credit refunds. If the exporter does not substantiate physical export and proceeds repatriation within the audit period, CRA recharacterizes the supply as domestic, applies 5% GST (or 13%-15% HST determined by the place of supply), compounds arrears interest at the CRA prescribed rate, disallows related ITCs and claws back any refunds paid. The normal reassessment window is four years, extended without limit where there is fraud, wilful default, neglect or a false statement. Civil penalties: Excise Tax Act s. 280(1) imposes the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the misstated net tax for gross negligence; Customs Act Administrative Monetary Penalty System assessments for false export declarations may reach CAD 25,000 per violation. Criminal prosecution for evasion (ETA s. 327 et seq. and ITA s. 238-239) may impose fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment; the CRA may also suspend or revoke the GST/HST registration and deny future zero-rating claims until security is posted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada subscribes to IMF Article VIII: the Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts with no exchange-control statute, no central-bank approval for imports, exports, dividends, interest, royalties, or loan servicing, and no licensing requirements for external payments. The historical Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951; market-driven floating exchange rates apply. Profit remittance is mechanically unrestricted, but a dividend paid by a Canadian corporation to a non-resident shareholder attracts Part XIII withholding tax of 25% under the Income Tax Act, reducible to the applicable treaty rate (typically 5% for a corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of voting shares, or 15% for portfolio holding) provided the shareholder supplies a valid NR301/NR302 treaty-residency declaration before payment. The Canadian entity importing Asian grocery products will settle supplier and logistics payables in CAD, USD, CNY, VND, THB, or other trade currencies at the prevailing interbank mid-market rate plus the bank's spread; there is no preferential or surrender-rate regime. Residual friction comes from FATCA/CRS reporting, NR4/NR6 withholding documentation, transfer-pricing contemporaneous records, and sanctions screening, not from exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Canada does not impose statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratios, repatriation timelines, or any deemed-conversion rule for export proceeds; an exporter may retain foreign currency offshore indefinitely. The only relevant obligations for a Canadian exporter are: (i) filing a CBSA B13A Export Declaration for controlled goods or exports exceeding the CAD 2,000 threshold; (ii) income-tax recognition of unrealized and realized FX gains/losses under subsection 39(1) of the Income Tax Act; and (iii) maintaining contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for related-party cross-border sales under section 247. For this import-centric Asian supermarket grocery hub, the functional equivalent documentary obligations are CBSA import entries (B3), commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, certificates of origin, and supplier due-diligence files; none of these documents creates an FX conversion or surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF administration by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); foreign-investment review by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) under the Investment Canada Act; and cross-border trade data and border controls by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). Foreign electronic-money institutions and cross-border payment fintechs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe) may lawfully operate ancillary accounts in Canada as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and as Retail Payment Activity Providers (RPAPs) registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act, whose registration regime became effective in November 2024 with phased transitional compliance. However, a foreign EMI cannot serve as the primary settlement account for a Canadian grocery retail corporation because Interac debit and Canadian card-network merchant settlement require membership in Payments Canada and a domestic chartered-bank relationship. EMIs are best used as supplementary cross-border payables rails for low-value supplier remittances in Asian currencies, offering cost-efficient SWIFT alternatives and multi-currency virtual IBANs. Key legal constraints: non-bank EMI balances are not CDIC-insured; the Canadian entity bears counterparty risk; EMI onboarding demands the full CBCA corporate pack plus enhanced UBO attestation; and the CRA may challenge undocumented intercompany flows if EMI wallet transactions are not properly supported by invoices and customs entries. Recommended sequencing: open the domestic operational account at a Tier-1 bank first, establish six months of clean account history and merchant settlement flow, then attach one FINTRAC-registered EMI solely for supplier payables, never for domestic POS settlement.

Recommended sequencing: open the domestic operational account at a Tier-1 bank first, establish six months of clean account history and merchant settlement flow, then attach one FINTRAC-registered EMI solely for supplier payables, never for domestic POS settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 annual aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions per non-resident counterparty triggers Form T106 mandatory filing (loans and advances separately tested at CAD 1,000,000). CRA contemporaneously expects Local File and Master File documentation consistent with OECD Chapter V for all significant cross-border transactions regardless of amount; Country-by-Country (CbC) filing threshold is EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue. Budget 2024 draft legislation proposes lowering the T106 threshold to CAD 100,000 and harmonizing documentation schedules for taxation years beginning after June 2025.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to offshore financial centers; substance is instead enforced through: (1) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA), which invalidates avoidance transactions lacking genuine economic reality and business purpose; (2) the MLI Principal Purpose Test (PPT) grafted onto most Canadian DTAs, which denies treaty benefits to arrangements structured principally to obtain reduced withholding rates; (3) the CRA's application of beneficial-ownership doctrine to Part XIII withholding. An Asian supermarket hub procuring branded goods through a Hong Kong or Singapore intermediary must demonstrate real employees, functions, risk-bearing capacity and decision-making authority in that intermediary to sustain intercompany pricing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 2 years under s. 239(1) ITA; CRA additionally imposes a civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax under s. 163(2), assesses full arrears interest, and publishes convictions on the CRA Tax Evasion Conviction list.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and managers who ordered or acquiesced in the evasion are chargeable as principals under s. 239(1) with identical fine and imprisonment exposure; a conviction constitutes an indictable offense, can generate a Canada Border Services Agency warrant preventing international travel, triggers civil restitution from personal assets, and results in corporate director disqualification.
