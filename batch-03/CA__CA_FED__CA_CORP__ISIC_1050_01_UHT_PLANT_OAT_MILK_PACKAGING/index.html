Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists under Canadian income tax law. A Canadian business corporation is inherently a separate taxpayer and cannot elect look-through treatment. Relevant statutory elections include the non-resident corporation election under Income Tax Act s. 216 for net rental income, branch tax reduction planning under s. 219(3), and the GST/HST quick method election under Excise Tax Act s. 230.1 for qualifying small suppliers. In hybrid mismatch contexts, Canada applies its own deemed-deduction and transfer-pricing rules rather than a check-the-box election; foreign hybrid entities are classified by Canadian domestic principles, subject to applicable tax treaty residency tie-breakers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made during the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once a person's taxable supplies exceed this amount, GST/HST registration is mandatory. Below the threshold the entity is a small supplier and may still register voluntarily to claim input tax credits. Non-residents supplying digital services or commercial activity in Canada are subject to the same general CAD 30,000 threshold under the simplified registration regime; provincial HST (e.g., Ontario 13%, New Brunswick 15%, Nova Scotia 15%, Newfoundland and Labrador 15%, Prince Edward Island 15%) is administered through the same CRA registration with no separate threshold, while Quebec imposes a separate QST regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a supply of tangible personal property (including UHT-packaged oat milk) that is exported from Canada under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI Part V. The supplier must prove the goods were delivered outside Canada to a non-resident recipient, either by shipment through a common carrier or by direct export, and must retain documentary evidence of exportation within the prescribed period. Exported services, such as offshore logistics management or custom formulation services performed and substantially used outside Canada, may also be zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V. An exporter that makes zero-rated supplies is entitled to deduct input tax credits on all related purchases, thereby generating real cash refunds from the CRA to the extent of uneroded credits.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export declaration / Canadian Export Report using the Automated Export Reporting System or the Canadian Export Reporting System with assigned export permit or B13A confirmation
  • Original Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing the Canadian shipper, foreign consignee, and port/place of export
  • Commercial invoice containing supplier GST/HST Business Number, exact product description, Incoterms, quantities, and export unit prices
  • Outward processing or phytosanitary/food export certificate issued by the Canadian Food Inspection Agency or provincial agency where applicable
  • Foreign exchange repatriation record (SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, foreign remittance certificate) evidencing inbound settlement
  • Freight forwarding instructions, insurance certificate, and/or customs broker export summary identifying the transported goods

Canada does not currently operate a national real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. The CRA requires prescribed GST/HST invoice information (supplier name, Business Number, date, description, tax rate, and tax amount) as set out in Excise Tax Act ss. 223 and following. Electronic invoices are accepted if they contain all mandatory particulars and are legible; there is no pre-approval API or government clearance of invoices. Large GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1 million are required to file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, and CRA supports digital integration through its My Business Account and Represent a Client APIs for authorized tax representatives.

CRA audit teams routinely withhold GST/HST refunds pending submission of the export documentation bundle. If zero-rating is claimed without adequate evidence, the CRA will reassess the supply as taxable, disallow input tax credits, and recover any refund paid with statutory compound interest. In cases of neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, the normal four-year reassessment period is extended to ten years. Penalties for false statements include the greater of CAD 4,000 and 25% of the tax sought to be avoided under Excise Tax Act s. 285, and criminal prosecution can result in fines, seizure of goods, and imprisonment where knowledge and intent to evade tax are proven.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction providing full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) floats freely, and a federally incorporated Canadian business corporation may purchase, hold, sell, and settle CAD, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency without central bank authorization. Capital injections by non-resident shareholders, intercompany loans, trade settlements, repatriation of dividends, and liquidation proceeds are not subject to exchange control approval. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than FX-related: dividends paid to non-residents are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax of 25% under the Income Tax Act, generally reduced to 5% or 15% under an applicable income tax treaty if the recipient satisfies the beneficial ownership and entitlement conditions; royalties and service fees face similar withholding or source tax exposure. The only statutory FX-related constraints are transparency measures: FINTRAC large-value electronic fund transfer (EFT) reporting for incoming or outgoing transfers of CAD 10,000 or more (or foreign currency equivalent), cross-border currency and monetary instrument reporting for physically carried amounts above CAD 10,000, OSFI anti-money-laundering expectations for unusual transaction monitoring, and the Income Tax Act functional currency election rules (section 261) for the corporation's reporting currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist in Canada. There are no statutory timelines for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratios into CAD, no designated collection accounts, and no sale proceeds surrender regime. The UHT oat milk exporter may invoice in USD or EUR and retain all proceeds in domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts. The compliance obligations that replace surrender rules are: (1) CBSA export reporting through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for goods exported from Canada, generally required when the value of the goods exceeds CAD 2,000 or for controlled, regulated, or permitted goods; (2) StatCan/International Merchandise Trade Program reporting and the CBSA customs declarations for statistical classification under HS codes; (3) FINTRAC EFT reporting by the Canadian financial institution for outbound transfers above CAD 10,000; (4) Income Tax Act foreign accrual property income (FAPI) analysis if export proceeds are parked in a controlled foreign affiliate earning passive interest or dividends; and (5) OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation to demonstrate that treasury, financing, and hedging arrangements with non-resident related parties are at arm's length.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority and final settlement agent; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of federally chartered banks and foreign bank branches; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the financial intelligence and AML/CTF authority under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and Global Affairs Canada as administrators of frontier trade controls, export permits, and sanctions enforcement under the Customs Act, the Export and Import Permits Act, the Special Economic Measures Act, and the United Nations Act. Canada maintains no single centralized foreign exchange control agency; the FX regime is fully liberalized and market-based.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as primary clearing infrastructure and MEDIUM as an ancillary micro-disbursement channel. Canada does not operate an EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime. Non-bank payment providers must register with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under PCMLTFA for money transmission, remittance, cybersecurity and cross-border flows; however, MSBs are not chartered banks, cannot accept Canadian deposit liabilities prohibited under the Bank Act, cannot hold settlement accounts at the Bank of Canada, and cannot access Payments Canada clearing systems (LyNX and the Automated Clearing Settlement System ACSS), which are restricted to banks, credit unions, and authorized financial institutions. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex operate under foreign licences and may lawfully be used by the Canadian corporation only for low-value ancillary supplier payments; they cannot be used for the capital escrow, commercial letter of credit settlements, equipment import disbursements, or dual-currency treasury pooling. Additional legal blockers include: unlicenced deposit-taking by foreign EMIs in Canada, ineligibility for CDIC deposit insurance, FINTRAC EFT reporting complications, and the reluctance of Canadian Schedule I banks to accept EMI-originated inbound funds without a complete beneficial chain and source-of-funds documentation. The recommended structure is a Schedule I chartered bank as the sole treasury hub with a fintech corridor retained only where necessary for sub-CAD 10,000 disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the foreign parent must be routed as a clear SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer to the Canadian Schedule I bank's account structure. Recommended sequence: (1) execute the board resolution and share subscription agreement before the wire; (2) ensure the CBCA corporate record, Business Number (BN) from the Canada Revenue Agency, and ISC register are current; (3) issue wire instructions containing the beneficiary's legal name under CBCA, the Canadian bank's SWIFT BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC, BOFMCAM2 for BMO), the beneficiary account number, and a payment reference in field 72 such as 'EQUITY SUBSCRIBE REF SUBSCRIPTION AGREEMENT DATED YYYY-MM-DD'; (4) recognize that Canada imposes no national statutory purpose code for capital inflows, but the bank's compliance and the Bank of Canada balance-of-payments data collection may map the transaction to an equity participation code under ISO 20022; (5) if the injection is structured as shareholder debt rather than equity, execute a written cross-border loan agreement, apply an OECD arm's-length interest rate, contemplate withholding tax and gross-up, and monitor the Income Tax Act thin capitalization limitation of 1.5:1 debt-to-equity for specified non-resident shareholders to preserve interest deductibility; (6) do not split the inbound wire into multiple sub-threshold amounts, as tranching triggers suspicion; instead report the full EFT to FINTRAC through the bank and provide the parent's source-of-funds documentation; (7) upon credit settlement, the treasury records paid-up share capital and, if CAD liquidity is required, executes a spot FX conversion under the approved FX facility.Outbound settlement rails comprise SWIFT MT103 for open-account trade payments, commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP 600) issued by the Canadian Schedule I bank for equipment purchases, SWIFT MT700 for LC confirmation and MT202/MT202COV for bank-to-bank reimbursement, and SWIFT MT760 for standby letters of credit or advance payment bonds. For capital machinery such as aseptic UHT towers and form-fill-seal packaging lines, the Canadian buyer should issue an irrevocable LC with the seller's bank as confirming or advising bank, specifying Incoterms 2020, inspection certificates, shipping documents, and the eUCP presentation format. For ongoing packaging material imports, the corporation may use open-account TT wires settled in USD against proforma invoices, while for first-time suppliers documentary collection (DP/DA) is the prudent fallback. Domestic CAD supplier and payroll disbursements clear through Payments Canada's ACSS or LyNX as electronic fund transfers, whereas USD disbursements clear through the correspondent banking network. The importer must file customs declarations with CBSA and pay applicable duties, GST (5%), or HST as applicable; non-resident service fees for technical licensing should be scrutinized for Part XIII withholding and GST/HST reverse-charge obligations; all outbound wires above CAD 10,000 are subject to FINTRAC EFT reporting by the bank, so the treasury must include reference fields that identify the underlying commercial invoice.Deliverable FX forwards (CAD vs USD or EUR) executed under an approved ISDA Master Agreement or the bank's master FX line, requiring physical settlement and documented commercial hedge rationale.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currency pairs where CAD or foreign deliverability is operationally constrained, used only where an underlying treasury or trade exposure justifies the cash-settlement structure.Plain-vanilla FX swaps (spot-forward combinations) to manage intra-month liquidity gaps between the CAD operating account and USD capital escrow facilities.Cross-currency swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) aligning long-term CAD-denominated capex debt service with USD-denominated oat milk export revenues.Vanilla FX options (puts and calls purchased from a Schedule I dealer) to cap adverse exchange rate movements on forecast export margins while preserving upside, subject to an approved derivatives policy and premium budgeting.Exchange-traded FX futures and options on USDCAD listed on CME or Bourse de Montreal (TMX Group) for transparent, margin-segregated micro-hedge adjustments during seasonal production and sales cycles.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as primary clearing infrastructure and MEDIUM as an ancillary micro-disbursement channel. Canada does not operate an EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime. Non-bank payment providers must register with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under PCMLTFA for money transmission, remittance, cybersecurity and cross-border flows; however, MSBs are not chartered banks, cannot accept Canadian deposit liabilities prohibited under the Bank Act, cannot hold settlement accounts at the Bank of Canada, and cannot access Payments Canada clearing systems (LyNX and the Automated Clearing Settlement System ACSS), which are restricted to banks, credit unions, and authorized financial institutions. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex operate under foreign licences and may lawfully be used by the Canadian corporation only for low-value ancillary supplier payments; they cannot be used for the capital escrow, commercial letter of credit settlements, equipment import disbursements, or dual-currency treasury pooling. Additional legal blockers include: unlicenced deposit-taking by foreign EMIs in Canada, ineligibility for CDIC deposit insurance, FINTRAC EFT reporting complications, and the reluctance of Canadian Schedule I banks to accept EMI-originated inbound funds without a complete beneficial chain and source-of-funds documentation. The recommended structure is a Schedule I chartered bank as the sole treasury hub with a fintech corridor retained only where necessary for sub-CAD 10,000 disbursements.
