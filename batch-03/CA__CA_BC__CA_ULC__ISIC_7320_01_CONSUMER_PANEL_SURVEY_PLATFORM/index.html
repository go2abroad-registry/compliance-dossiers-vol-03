Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital, no stated-capital escrow, and no authorized-capital ceiling under the BCBCA; a BC ULC can be incorporated with zero subscribed share capital. The Articles may authorize an unlimited number of shares without par value. Because shareholder liability is unlimited (BCBCA s. 2(2) and s. 36), the statute relies on mandatory liability disclosure and creditor-claims rules rather than an escrowed-capital deposit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Canada maintains full current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; capital movements are also unrestricted absent sanctions or Investment Canada Act intervention. Profit remittance is freely permitted, but dividends paid by a BC ULC to non-resident shareholders may incur Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reducible) and must be documented via NR4 or FD-24 filings.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None: Canada imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; export proceeds may be retained, converted, or remitted freely, subject only to FINTRAC reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and anti-money laundering oversight with FINTRAC.

Defense evidence: Contractual framework (terms of use and participant consent), granular survey completion logs with timestamps/IP addresses, payout policies and audit trail, anti-fraud screening outputs, and a data protection impact assessment (DPIA).

Root cause: BC ULC unlimited liability structure with non-resident shareholders and no audited financial statements.

        
Defense evidence: Current BC Business Corporations Act authorization, Articles and Notice of Articles, register of directors and shareholders, ULC tax classification election or shareholder indemnification side letter, and a funded capital commitment letter from the parent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000 aggregate annual fair market value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a single counterparty generally triggers the need for contemporaneous documentation, including a transfer-pricing policy, benchmark study, and functional analysis; Canada does not prescribe a separate mandatory Master File filing event but the Canada Revenue Agency may demand all supporting documentation on audit

      
Economic substance scope: Canada imposes no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; nonetheless, the Income Tax Act's multi-staged general anti-avoidance rule and transfer-pricing rules require genuine commercial substance, board governance in British Columbia, payroll for Canadian staff, an actual office platform, and arm's-length remuneration for every intercompany cross-border service rendered by or to the ULC

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all omitted income; gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the tax understated under ITA s.163(2); criminal tax evasion prosecution under ITA s.239(1) carrying a fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to two years

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be held jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII withholding under ITA s.227.1; criminal travel bans apply; civil restitution orders permit CRA seizure of personal assets; because the entity is a ULC, its shareholders are also personally exposed to corporate-tax liabilities

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Default
        
Statutory sanction: CRA may reprice transactions to an arm's-length amount, add statutory interest, and impose the 10% transfer-pricing penalty where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CA$5,000,000 and 10% of gross revenue; denial of the deduction and double-tax exposure may also result

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approved grossly under-priced intercompany contracts may be pursued for unpaid corporate tax as de facto controlling taxpayers; ULC shareholders face unlimited liability for the resulting deficiency, and CRA may issue director liability assessments and enforce personal assets

      

      
      

        Dividend Repatriation Avoidance
        
Statutory sanction: The Canada Revenue Agency will recharacterize the payments as deemed dividends; Part XIII tax is assessed on the grossed-up amount, plus interest and late-payment penalties; administrative monetary penalties apply for incomplete T5/NR4 filings and misreported treaty claims

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failure to withhold Part XIII tax; unlawful distributions made while the ULC is insolvent extend personal liability to shareholders and directors under Canadian corporate insolvency principles; travel restrictions and personal bankruptcy petitions are available to the CRA

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Substance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: The Canada Revenue Agency may apply GAAR to disregard the shell structure, reassess all income accruing outside Canada to the ULC, impose a 15% penalty on undue GST/HST credits, and assess withheld tax obligations for artificial fees; directors may face prosecution for conspiracy to commit tax fraud

        
Director personal liability: Directors are liable for unpaid corporate tax, GST/HST, and source deductions under ITA sections 227.1 and 323; the unlimited liability feature of the BC ULC gives tax-creditors direct recourse against every shareholder, and fraudulent-conveyance proceedings can trigger travel departure bans and personal criminal fines
