Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a BC Unlimited Liability Corporation is a Canadian tax-resident corporation subject to Part I corporate income tax on worldwide income (Entity_Level_Taxation under Canadian domestic law); for US federal tax purposes it qualifies as an eligible entity that can be classified as a partnership or disregarded entity (pass-through) under IRC Section 301.7701-3 check-the-box rules, creating hybrid entity status; no Tax_Exempt_Zone applies; territorial scope relief is limited to foreign tax credit and treaty mechanisms rather than a pure territorial exemption.

Available election forms

Form 8832 Entity Classification Election (check-the-box) filed with the IRS to elect US pass-through or corporate classification; CRA does not recognize the US check-the-box election and continues to treat the ULC as a corporation resident in Canada; Canada-US Income Tax Convention (Fifth Protocol) hybrid mismatch rules and deemed dividend treatment under Article 4 may apply; Election under section 216 of the Income Tax Act for certain non-resident rental income; GST/HST small-supplier registration election under subsection 234(2) of the Excise Tax Act remains available only if the entity stays below the CAD 30,000 threshold and does not issue tax invoices.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (other than zero-rated supplies) over any four consecutive calendar quarters for resident and non-resident suppliers; non-resident digital platform operators and suppliers of digital products (including app stores and in-app content) must register for the simplified non-resident GST/HST regime effective July 1, 2021 once the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded; BC PST has no small-supplier exemption for taxable digital property sold for use in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies where (i) goods are exported from Canada and the supplier retains proof of export as required by sections 24 and 26 of the Excise Tax Act and Schedule VI Part V section 1; and (ii) services, including digital webtoon platform access, subscription fees, and multilingual chapter unlock payments, are supplied to a non-resident person who is not in Canada at the time the service is performed under Schedule VI Part V section 7, provided the service is not in respect of real or tangible personal property situated in Canada; the supplier must be a GST/HST registrant, charge 0% GST, and remains eligible to claim input tax credits for expenses attributable to the zero-rated export supply; Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlement proceeds must be mapped to the underlying non-resident subscribers to support zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or the electronic equivalent under the Canadian Export Reporting System / GTRP)
  • Commercial Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank and foreign-exchange records evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration
  • Licensing and syndication contracts, subscriber-level payment records, and platform settlement reports (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing) linking each supply to a non-resident recipient

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate exists in Canada or British Columbia; e-invoicing is voluntary and may be exchanged via Peppol, EDI, or PDF with CRA accepting electronic records when complete and readable; GST/HST returns must be filed through GST/HST NETFILE or authorized web forms; registrants must retain all books and records, including export evidence and non-resident substantiation, for at least six years either in Canada or in a format electronically accessible from Canada.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess uncollected GST/HST plus interest where export substantiation or non-resident evidence is incomplete; penalties include a minimum 10% late-reporting penalty under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act, a 20% gross negligence penalty under subsection 285(1) for false statements or omissions, and criminal prosecution under section 327 for wilful evasion with fines up to CAD 20,000 or double the tax at risk and imprisonment up to five years; the normal four-year reassessment limitation period is waived where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default; CRA also cross-references customs export declarations, non-resident payment rails, and currency conversion records to detect and claw back improperly claimed zero-rated refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility for the Canadian dollar; there is no exchange-control law, no capital-export licensing regime, no repatriation approval gate, and no limit on holding or using foreign currency accounts in Canada. Profit remittance hurdles are purely tax-driven: dividends, royalties, and interest paid by the BC ULC to non-resident owners are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (statutory rate 25%, reduced by treaty: generally 15% on dividends and 10% on copyright royalties under the Canada-US Treaty, and 5% where the US parent owns at least 10% of the ULC's voting shares). The Investment Canada Act applies to acquisitions of control of Canadian businesses, but a digital webtoon syndication vehicle below the extended WTO-investor review threshold (post-2021 EBITDA-based threshold) will face only notification obligations, not a net-benefit review. Since the BC ULC is pass-through eligible, the non-resident owner(s) may jointly elect under subsection 45(3) of the Income Tax Act to treat the ULC as a partnership for Canadian income tax purposes, which eliminates the entity-level Part XIII withholding step on distributions and instead taxes the non-resident participants directly on their Canadian-source share of the digital-media income, subject to treaty relief; the banking counterparty nevertheless opens and operates the accounts in the corporate legal name of the ULC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation time limit; the BC ULC may retain webtoon syndication revenue and licensing receipts in USD, EUR, KRW, JPY, or any freely convertible currency indefinitely without penalty. The operative compliance obligations are accounting and tax based: translate foreign-currency receivables at the Bank of Canada daily noon spot rate for financial-statement presentation; recognize realized and unrealized foreign exchange gains or losses on income account under section 39 and related provisions of the Income Tax Act; and match Apple IAP and Google Play Billing net settlements to the underlying chapter-unlock or subscription revenue, including applicable GST/HST remittance, in the same reporting period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); official exchange-rate policy and national reserves are managed through the Minister of Finance's Exchange Fund Account under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act; prudential oversight of all Schedule I and Schedule II banking counterparties resides with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); transaction-level cross-border monitoring and AML enforcement rest with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration; Treasury Board and the Department of Finance Canada jointly administer sanctions under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Offshore fintech EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Corpay, and similar digital payment platforms) are lawful in Canada only where they are registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, where applicable, hold provincial money-services licensing; registered EMIs can provide efficient multi-currency treasury sweeps, fast international creator payouts, and low-value consolidation of Apple IAP and Google Play Billing proceeds. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a bank within the meaning of the Bank Act and therefore cannot hold CDIC-insured deposits, cannot settle directly through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and does not provide escrow-grade capital accounts; an unregistered offshore EMI soliciting business from a Canadian resident entity is non-compliant and exposes the ULC to FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties. The legally robust design is therefore a Schedule I chartered bank as the primary counterparty for capital escrow, reimbursement, LC, and LVTS rails, with the EMI permitted only as a secondary rail for corridor-specific creator disbursements and non-resident marketplace balances.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound wires into the BC ULC must carry the exact corporate legal name, the Canadian bank's Standard Settlement Instructions (SSI), and a purpose code aligned to the substance of the transaction: ISO 20022 ExternalPurpose code INTC for intra-group capital injections or equity contributions, LOAN where the inflow is booked as shareholder debt, DIVI for profit distributions returned to the parent, and ROYL for licensing royalties paid to the foreign licensor; where the ordering bank requires a legacy SWIFT code, the ULC must append a remittance narrative referencing the relevant subscription agreement, promissory note, or license agreement. The Canadian receiving bank will file a FINTRAC International Electronic Funds Transfer Report for every incoming electronic transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, will screen the ordering institution and all referenced parties against Canadian Special Economic Measures Act, United Nations Act, and aligned OFAC sanctions lists, and will apply enhanced source-of-funds verification to any unexplained jumbo inflow exceeding CAD 1 million. For Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements, the platforms remit net proceeds through their licensed payment entities (for example, Apple's iTunes S.à r.l. and Google Payment Corp.) using standard MT103 or US ACH transfers into the ULC's account, each referencing the Developer Program and Application identifiers; the treasury function must maintain a five-business-day settlement ledger reconciling (i) platform payout statements, (ii) bank receipts, and (iii) deferred-revenue recognition, with the full reconciliation available to the bank upon request to prevent adverse classification of legitimate platform income as unverifiable third-party volume.Primary clearing rails for outbound treasury operations: (i) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for creator advances, freelance content-development fees, and syndication royalties, executed through the Canadian bank's international payments gateway; (ii) SWIFT MT700 and MT760 documentary instruments (Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600) for procurement of third-party licensed content, print-on-demand inventory, or localization services; (iii) SWIFT MT202 COV cover payments where the beneficiary bank is not a direct LVTS or CHIPS participant; (iv) Payments Canada Automated Funds Transfer (ACFT) for recurring domestic creator royalty runs; and (v) real-time gross settlement of CAD-denominated instructions through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), with USD clearances settled through Fedwire or CHIPS. Every outbound wire of CAD 10,000 or more is reportable to FINTRAC as an International Electronic Funds Transfer, first-time payees are subject to a standard AML verification hold of one to five business days, and all payees and jurisdictions must pass sanctions screening; the ULC should pre-file its standing payee list, threshold matrix, and purpose-code catalogue with the relationship manager to eliminate rolling compliance queues and to preserve the Commercial LC facility for time-critical localization payments.Deliverable CAD/USD outright foreign-exchange forwards with tenors of 1 to 12 months standard (up to 24 months on credit approval), executed under an ISDA Master Agreement incorporating the ISDA 1998 FX Definitions and a Credit Support Annex; hedges must be tied to forecast export receipts and app-store net settlements, with no speculative purpose.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) in restricted or less-convertible currencies (for example KRW, CNY, IDR, PHP, and other emerging-market app-store corridors) settled in CAD or USD delta, permissible where onshore deliverable forwards are unavailable, regulated, or subject to local capital controls.Cross-currency swaps and FX swaps converting USD or EUR platform sweeps into CAD on fixed or floating bases, permissible with a Schedule I bank and subject to applicable Canadian Derivatives Clearing Service (CDCS) clearing where the instrument is standardized and thresholds are met.European-style foreign-exchange options, including purchased puts and calls and zero-cost collars on USD/CAD and EUR/CAD, with premiums settled in CAD and maturities aligned to the quarterly revenue repatriation cycle.Plain-vanilla FX forwards executed on the bank's electronic treasury dealing platform with same-day electronic confirmation via SWIFT MT300, daily mark-to-market collateralized under the Credit Support Annex.Centrally cleared Canadian-dollar futures and options listed on Bourse de Montréal where economically equivalent to the permitted OTC hedge; small end-users transacting below the substantial-end-user thresholds remain exempt from mandatory trade-repository reporting under provincial derivatives rules (including OSC Rule 91-507) provided the exemption is documented in the bank's counterparty file.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Offshore fintech EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Corpay, and similar digital payment platforms) are lawful in Canada only where they are registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, where applicable, hold provincial money-services licensing; registered EMIs can provide efficient multi-currency treasury sweeps, fast international creator payouts, and low-value consolidation of Apple IAP and Google Play Billing proceeds. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a bank within the meaning of the Bank Act and therefore cannot hold CDIC-insured deposits, cannot settle directly through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and does not provide escrow-grade capital accounts; an unregistered offshore EMI soliciting business from a Canadian resident entity is non-compliant and exposes the ULC to FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties. The legally robust design is therefore a Schedule I chartered bank as the primary counterparty for capital escrow, reimbursement, LC, and LVTS rails, with the EMI permitted only as a secondary rail for corridor-specific creator disbursements and non-resident marketplace balances.
