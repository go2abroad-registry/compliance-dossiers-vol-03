Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); My Business Account (MRA) portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO) system.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign ULC must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) through the BRO portal or by mailing the form to the nearest CRA tax centre. Required attachments: certified copy of the BC Certificate of Incorporation under the Business Corporations Act, SBC 2002, c. 57, articles of incorporation, registered office address, particulars of all directors/officers, and, where no resident director exists, a Form BC Corporate Registry appointment of an attorney for service of process. The BN is the single statutory TIN for income tax (corporate account), GST/HST, payroll, and import/export (RZ) accounts. Registration must occur within 30 days of becoming a registrant; a GST/HST account is opened simultaneously if worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian ULCs are taxable Canadian corporations under the Income Tax Act (Canada), but are eligible entities for US federal tax purposes: a check-the-box election (Form 8832, Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3) may treat the ULC as a partnership or disregarded entity for US tax. Under CRA administrative policy (Income Tax Folio S4-F6-C2), where a ULC is fiscally transparent under US rules, Canada respects the counterparty characterization for treaty exchange and re-sourcing purposes. A non-resident owner may also file a Canada s. 216(c) election on Form T1159 to report net rental income from Canadian real property instead of gross Part XIII withholding, and a s. 216(4) election on deemed disposition of Canadian real property by a foreign-owned ULC. The REIT may elect under ITA s. 130.1 to reduce taxable income by distributing net rental income to unitholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 240(1)); once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory within 30 days and becomes effective from the day the threshold was breached.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of real property services (rental, management, leasing, and brokerage) to a non-resident person not registered for GST/HST, who does not ordinarily reside in Canada and carries on no business in Canada, are zero-rated under ETA s. 23. Sales of used residential housing and long-term residential leases are exempt under Schedule V, Part I; commercial property sales and leases are taxable at 5% GST unless the non-resident tenant/supply conditions under ETA s. 23 are established. REIT distributions are exempt financial services under ETA Schedule V, Part VII when the REIT holds the property in the course of its business rather than as an adventure in the nature of trade.

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime exists at the federal level. CRA accepts electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE) and mandates e-filing for registrants with annual taxable supplies over CAD 1.5M. Pan-Canadian e-invoicing via the Peppol network is voluntary for B2B (via access points) and is being adopted for federal B2G procurement. CRA may reassess beyond the four-year normal period where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default (s. 298(4)-(5)) and may apply the general anti-avoidance rule penalty of 50% of tax avoided under ITA s. 239 where abusive cross-border or treaty-transparent planning is found.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible and free-floating currency; Canada meets IMF Article VIII obligations. Both current account and capital account transactions are liberalized, and no exchange control approval is required for inbound capital injection, profit remittance, REIT distributions, or repatriation of sale proceeds. Part XIII non-resident withholding tax applies to dividends and distribution-like amounts at a statutory rate of 25%, subject to bilateral treaty reduction (e.g., Canada-United States Convention provides 15% on dividends, with 5% available where the beneficial owner is a US resident corporation owning at least 10% of the voting shares). All remittance-related taxes must be withheld and settled with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); there is no exchange-control gate beyond normal tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender framework, no statutory conversion timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. A British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is free to retain export proceeds, rental receipts, or any foreign currency in domestic or foreign currency bank accounts without Bank of Canada or OFSI approval. Foreign exchange gains and losses from conversion of balance sheet amounts are brought into income under section 39(1) of the Income Tax Act (Canada).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange policy oversight is shared with the Department of Finance Canada, with OSFI supervising federally regulated deposit-taking institutions and FINTRAC administering AML/KYC obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Canada does not recognize the European Electronic Money Institution (EMI) authorization; a foreign or domestic fintech operating as a digital treasury facilitator must register with FINTRAC as a Money Service Business (MSB). An MSB is not a Schedule I authorized deposit-taking institution, does not have CDIC deposit insurance coverage, and cannot provide the same escrow integrity or Part XIII withholding infrastructure as a commercial bank. Cross-border payment platforms such as Wise or Payoneer are acceptable only for minor operational disbursements and as correspondent-level acceleration rails; they are insufficient as the primary treasury counterparty for a REIT-class ULC holding Canadian real estate, where segregated capital escrow, LYNX settlement, and non-resident distribution controls are mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol requires SWIFT MT103 (for payments up to typical remittance size) or SWIFT MT202 (for bank-to-bank backing of high-value capital) from the foreign investor to the ULC's CAD or USD operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The remittance information field must unambiguously reference the REIT unit subscription agreement, the BC corporate registry identifier, and the phrase 'REIT capital subscription - no goods or services invoiced'. Canadian banks will conduct a FINTRAC electronic funds transfer assessment for incoming wires equal to or above CAD 10,000 or any structured series; therefore the bank must be given the full investor breakdown and Canadian counsel-issued subscription validation. For foreign direct investment establishing a new Canadian business, an Investment Canada Act notification to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) may be required, and the use of a segregated capital escrow account is recommended until the initial real property acquisition closes.Outbound treasury operations use SWIFT MT103 for dividend and REIT distribution wires, operating expense payments, and capital repatriation; the payer of a distribution must withhold and remit Part XIII tax to the CRA before release of the residual wire. Turnover: All British Columbia unlimited liability companies are legally required to appoint a qualified auditor unless exempt under section 232 of the Business Corporations Act. A REIT operating through a BC ULC is a reporting issuer or investment fund if its securities are distributed to the public or held by more than 50 unitholders, which imposes a mandatory independent audit under National Instrument 81-106 (Investment Fund Continuous Disclosure) and NI 52-102. The audit must be performed by a licensed public accounting firm in accordance with Canadian Auditing Standards (CAS). Even if the private exemption applies, bank and institutional investors invariably require an audited financial statement to substantiate the REIT's tax-paid distributable income.
