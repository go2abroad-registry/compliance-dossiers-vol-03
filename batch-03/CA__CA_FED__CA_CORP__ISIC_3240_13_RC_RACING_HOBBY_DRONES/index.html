Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - Business Registration Online (BRO) and GST/HST Registry; official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a Business Number (BN) by registering through the CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) service or by submitting Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The registrant may simultaneously open a GST/HST program account. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada are required to register unless they qualify as small suppliers; voluntary registration is available for persons making taxable supplies. For export-only activities, a BN with an Import/Export Account may also be required by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Federal income tax classification for a Business Corporation is fixed as entity-level taxation; there is no check-the-box election for Canadian corporations. Under the Excise Tax Act, a person may elect to register for GST/HST if not required to register, may elect to use the Quick Method of accounting for GST/HST, and may make section 156 elections for closely related corporations. Provincial harmonization elections may apply for provincially harmonized taxes; hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act may require filing additional elections related to foreign entity classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies made in Canada over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter; however, non-resident persons who supply digital services to Canadian recipients may be subject to the simplified GST/HST registration regime without a threshold, and exporters of goods may be required to register if they regularly import goods into Canada.

Canada does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for e-invoices. GST/HST invoices must comply with the information requirements in section 3 of the GST/HST Regulations, including vendor name, BN, consideration, tax status and amount of tax. Electronic invoices are acceptable if they are legible and complete. Registrants with an annual gross revenue threshold exceeding CAD 1.5 million are generally required to file GST/HST returns electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account; machine-readable XML and API transmission is supported by CRA for integrated filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full Article VIII IMF convertibility for both current and capital account transactions; the Canadian dollar is a freely floating currency and there are no foreign exchange permits, approval licenses, or repatriation restrictions on dividends, loan repayments, or capital disposition. Profit remittance and capital transfer hurdles are limited to treaty-reduced withholding taxes (statutory 25% gross) and FINTRAC large-value EFT record-keeping; no exchange control authorization is required for inbound equity or outbound trade settlements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None - Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender regime on export proceeds. Exporters may retain, convert, or finance foreign currency receivables through any authorized FI; compliance is limited to FINTRAC international EFT records for transfers equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000, Statistics Canada balance-of-payments survey filings, and income tax recognition of foreign exchange gains and losses.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) in coordination with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision; cross-border fund transfer and currency reporting administration is jointly executed by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).
