Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Income Tax Act (Canada) s. 115.1, a Canadian ULC classified for US federal tax purposes as a partnership or disregarded entity (US check-the-box) triggers a deemed taxation year-end and deemed disposition/reacquisition of property at fair market value; the corporation remains a Canadian resident taxpayer, but participants are statutorily recognized for relevant policy elections; the election is made via US Form 8832 with corresponding notification to the CRA. There is no general Canadian check-the-box regime; hybrid mismatch (BEPS Action 2) rules under s. 18.4 ITA may defer or deny deductions on instrument or entity hybrid arbitrage. The limited special elections for ULCs do not alter Part I taxability of Canadian-source trucking revenue; entity-level federal (15%) and BC provincial (12%) corporate rates apply to taxable income absent a s. 115.1 deemed year-end alignment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST small-supplier threshold): registration mandatory where taxable worldwide revenues exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in any four consecutive calendar quarters under Excise Tax Act (Canada) s. 148; where the BC-resident ULC is engaged in commercial activity in Canada, the same $30,000 nexus applies to non-resident suppliers with an additional permanent-establishment nexus test for extraterritorial supplies; BC PST registration has no minimum threshold — any vendor making taxable sales of goods or taxable services in British Columbia must register and collect PST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% GST rate applies to (i) goods exported directly from Canada under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, and (ii) a supply of freight transportation services provided by a common carrier where the shipment originates in Canada and is destined for a point outside Canada under a through-continuous international contract of carriage (Schedule VI, Part VII); the GPS-sealed bonded road-feeder service qualifies only where the single waybill/Bill of Lading evidences continuous carrier liability from origin to foreign destination without a domestic breaking point; terminal handling, warehousing, storage, or any BC-only leg remains taxable at 5% GST. BC PST does not apply to freight transportation services as they are not enumerated taxable services, but PST applies separately to taxable vehicle repair, parking, and storage services acquired in BC.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) transmitted electronically via CBSA CARM/ECS or an authorized freight forwarder for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 in value
  • CBSA Export eManifest / ACI electronic cargo declaration with highway carrier code and active carrier bond
  • Commercial invoice with origin, destination, harmonized commodity code, and declared FOB value
  • Bill of Lading (sea/air) or Cargo Control Document (CCD) evidencing continuous international carriage
  • US PAPS (Pre-Arrival Processing System) barcode or in-bond documentation for trucks transiting through the United States
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing foreign-currency payment repatriated to Canada within 180 days of shipment

Canada has no real-time invoice clearance, digital stamping, or CRA pre-approval mechanism; GST/HST invoices must be issued in compliance with ETA s. 221(1) and the prescribed information regulations, may be electronic, and records must be legible and retrievable for six years. Mandatory electronic customs clearance applies at the border: CBSA requires the ACI/eManifest (Advance Commercial Information) electronic data interchange for all commercial highway cargo entering or leaving Canada, including the EXPORT eManifest transmission via a certified service provider or the CBSA CARM portal; US-bound bonded truck movements require a PAPS (Pre-Arrival Processing System) barcode. Voluntary Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing exists but is not statutorily mandated.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year GST/HST reassessment period or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default; failure to substantiate zero-rating with contemporaneous export documentation reverses the 0% rate, triggers net tax reassessment, a 5% accuracy penalty under ETA s. 285(1) (minimum CAD 250), a 20% gross-negligence penalty for knowingly making false statements, daily compounding interest under ETA s. 280.1, and criminal tax evasion liability under ETA s. 327 carrying fines of up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years; CRA also reserves the right to claw back input tax credits claimed on expenses allocable to unsubstantiated exports and, in the case of a ULC electing pass-through status, to apply the s. 115.1 deemed disposition rules to assets removed from Canadian taxing jurisdiction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Capital and current account transactions in CAD are fully liberalized; bonded trucking service receivables may be held, converted, or remitted without central-bank approval. The material friction is tax-driven: dividends and deemed distributions from the ULC to non-resident members attract Part XIII withholding at 25% statutory, generally reduced to 5% or 15% under the Canada-United States tax treaty where the member is the treaty-resident beneficial owner and the ULC is not fiscally transparent for treaty purposes. Hybrid characterization risk under US check-the-box elections must be managed because subsection 212.1(1) ITA surplus-stripping rules constrain distributions made within 24 months of an acquisition of shares for non-arm's length consideration; a treaty and hybrid-mismatch (BEPS Action 2) tax opinion should govern the dividend and capital-repatriation policy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil: Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriation. Treasury may retain USD/EUR service proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank or convert to CAD at the prevailing wholesale FX rate. Reporting obligations remain: FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reports are required for any cross-border wire of CAD 10,000 or more; CBSA bonded-carrier records must be maintained under the Customs Act for the Air Cargo (Bonded) Truck Carrier Program; and books, records, and exchange-rate support must be retained for six years under the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank and monetary/payments authority; the Minister of Finance holds sovereign authority over the Exchange Fund Account and foreign exchange reserves under the Currency Act; OSFI prudentially regulates Schedule I/II banks; FINTRAC administers PCMLTFA large-cross-border-transfer and EFT reporting; Statistics Canada collects inward/outward direct investment statistics. Canada has no dedicated FX-control administration analogous to SAFE: exchange controls were repealed with the Exchange Control Act in 1951, and the Canadian dollar operates under a free-floating, freely convertible regime compliant with IMF Article VIII.
