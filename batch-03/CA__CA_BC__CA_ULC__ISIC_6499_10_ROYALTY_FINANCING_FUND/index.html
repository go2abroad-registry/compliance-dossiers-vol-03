Default tax classification

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists under the Canadian Income Tax Act; the BC ULC is mandatorily taxed as a corporation. Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules apply to cross-border arrangements involving such entity classification differences and may recharacterise or deny deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 in taxable supplies over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters for GST/HST; BC PST requires registration upon first taxable sale, with a limited small-seller exemption for sellers whose BC taxable sales are below C$10,000 in the prior 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim 0% GST/HST on an export, the registrant must make a qualifying supply under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For goods, the goods must be exported from Canada in accordance with CRA/CBSA requirements and the registrant must maintain evidence of export. For services, intangibles or licence/royalty supplies, the recipient must be a non-resident not registered for GST/HST, the supply must not be for consumption/use in Canada, and it must not relate to real property situated in Canada. Financial services supplied by a royalty-financing fund are generally exempt, not zero-rated; where an exempt financial supply is made to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada, the fund cannot recover input tax credits on related exempt supplies.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or approved electronic export reporting through CBSA
  • Carrier-issued Bill of Lading or air waybill showing movement from Canada to foreign destination (for services/intangibles: contract, licence agreement, and delivery records)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/foreign-exchange record evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated GST/HST invoice identifying the non-resident recipient, description, consideration and supplier BN/RT account

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API e-invoicing system. GST/HST e-invoicing is permitted but voluntary; CRA requires records to be retained in readable electronic or paper form and that GST/HST invoices show prescribed particulars (supplier BN/RT number, date, description, consideration and tax). Pan-Canadian PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is carried out on a voluntary basis.

CRA may deny zero-rating and retroactively assess GST/HST, interest and penalties if export conditions or documentary requirements are not satisfied within prescribed time limits. False or misleading statements in GST/HST filings attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act (up to 25% of the amount involved for false statements), plus gross-negligence penalties of 50% of understated income tax under the Income Tax Act s. 163(2) for intentional cases. Fraudulent zero-rating schemes may be subject to criminal prosecution and director liability, and BC PST audits can claw back uncollected provincial sales tax with interest and administrative penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Canada has had a free-floating exchange rate since 1970 and abolished exchange controls in 1951; no Canadian approval is required to convert CAD, to hold USD or EUR, to receive foreign currency, or to remit dividends, interest, or royalties abroad. Profit-repatriation constraints are purely tax-driven: dividends paid by a BC ULC to non-resident shareholders attract Part XIII withholding tax (statutory 25%; treaty-reduced to 15% for portfolio US dividends and 5% for a US shareholder owning at least 10% of voting shares under Article X of the Canada-US Tax Convention). Interest paid at arm's length on non-participating debt is withholding-exempt (paragraph 212(1)(b)). Royalty payments to US residents are subject to a statutory 25% withholding with a 10% treaty rate under Article XII, unless the narrow copyright-royalty exception applies. As the ULC is eligible for pass-through tax treatment but is a Canadian resident under the treaty, the Canada-US protocol hybrid-entity rules must be verified to avoid double taxation or treaty-shopping denial. No exchange controls apply to repatriation of the royalty-financing fund's Canadian-source profits; only ordinary reporting of year-end foreign assets to Form T1135 is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not require export goods, services, or royalty proceeds to be surrendered, and there is no statutory timeline for their repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Revenue-based financing receivables denominated in USD may be kept in US-dollar operating accounts or held offshore. The only binding obligations are FINTRAC reporting requirements (any international EFT of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the financial entity; physical cross-border movement of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more must be declared to the Canada Border Services Agency) and the foreign-property reporting obligation of Form T1135 if the aggregate cost amount of the ULC's specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000 at any time in the taxation year.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); monetary authority and sole issuer of Canadian bank notes. There is no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration in Canada. FX and capital-movement policy sits with the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52). Prudential oversight of FX activities of banks and authorized foreign banks lies with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the Bank Act and OSFI Guideline B-13. Transaction-level anti-money-laundering reporting (international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more) is enforced by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Payment-clearing system oversight is performed by Payments Canada under the Canadian Payments Act, with the Bank of Canada operating wholesale settlement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary liquidity channel; not acceptable as the primary treasury bank. Legal constraints: Canada has no EMI passporting regime equivalent to the EU, so licences held by UK/EU EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) do not confer Canadian bank status, and funds held with such EMIs are not CDIC-insured. The ULC may lawfully maintain an offshore EMI account, but the balance triggers Form T1135 filing when aggregate foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000; the EMI is outside the OSFI supervisory perimeter and cannot issue a Canadian-domiciled account number usable for Canadian ACH debit origination. If the EMI provides third-party payment processing for the ULC's merchant disbursements, the ULC's primary bank will treat the EMI as a third-party payment processor (TPPP) and may require dual accounts, daily cash-flow reconciliation, and contractual indemnities. The ULC should not provide money-transfer services through EMIs unless it is registered as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA; a crypto or remittance component would trigger that registration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Design the inbound leg as SWIFT-based CAD wires to the ULC's operating account at a Schedule I bank, using ISO 20022 purpose code NGIN (non-resident investment in domestic entities). For USD-denominated injections, direct the wire to the ULC's USD correspondent account with RBC (SWIFT BIC ROYCCAT2) or TD (BIC TDOMCATTTOR), and execute a same-day FX conversion to CAD at the account bank. Every injection requires a share-subscription agreement, CRS/FATCA self-certification, updated registers, and pre-notification to FINTRAC via the bank's IEFT report; inbound investments exceeding CAD 10,000 are automatically reported, and the bank may request a Funds Transfer Declaration. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary liquidity channel; not acceptable as the primary treasury bank. Legal constraints: Canada has no EMI passporting regime equivalent to the EU, so licences held by UK/EU EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) do not confer Canadian bank status, and funds held with such EMIs are not CDIC-insured. The ULC may lawfully maintain an offshore EMI account, but the balance triggers Form T1135 filing when aggregate foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000; the EMI is outside the OSFI supervisory perimeter and cannot issue a Canadian-domiciled account number usable for Canadian ACH debit origination. If the EMI provides third-party payment processing for the ULC's merchant disbursements, the ULC's primary bank will treat the EMI as a third-party payment processor (TPPP) and may require dual accounts, daily cash-flow reconciliation, and contractual indemnities. The ULC should not provide money-transfer services through EMIs unless it is registered as a Money Service Business with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA; a crypto or remittance component would trigger that registration.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Federal general corporate rate 15% plus British Columbia general corporate rate 12%, yielding an aggregate headline rate of 27% before any provincial abatement. A BC Unlimited Liability Corporation is a Canadian resident taxpayer subject to tax on worldwide income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident person from Canadian sources, absent treaty relief.

The T2 return is due within 6 months after the end of the fiscal period; for a December 31 year-end, the filing deadline is June 30 of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate non-arm's-length transactions with any one non-resident party per taxation year, triggering Form T106 and contemporaneous Local File; Master File and Country-by-Country Report may be required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations of the UAE/OECD type; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s.245), the beneficial ownership doctrine, and OECD BEPS recommendations permit the CRA to disregard or recharacterize a royalty financing ULC that lacks real management, employees, material risks, or economic activities in BC. CRA may defer to economic substance when applying intercompany financing and treaty anti-abuse provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Conviction under ITA s.239 carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil consequences include a 50% gross-negligence penalty, compound arrears interest, and re-opening of the statute-barred years.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or knowingly participate in evasion can be prosecuted as parties, subjected to restitution, personal monetary penalties, asset seizure, and a travel ban pending proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.247(3), a penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment is imposed; additional late T106 penalties of CAD 100 per day (max CAD 2,500) apply; CRA reassesses and charges prescribed interest on adjusted amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly signing false T106 or approving fictitious intercompany prices may be jointly liable for the assessed penalties and subject to criminal charges under s.239 if wilful.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA may invoke the GAAR to deny treaty benefits, treat the ULC as a sham for tax purposes, assess Part XIII at 25% on undeclared distributions, and apply transfer-pricing penalties; the BC Business Corporations Act may strike the company for non-compliance with registered-records requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate a conduit can be personally assessed for withholding liabilities under s.227.1, named in civil recovery proceedings, and barred from re-entering Canada if criminal tax evasion is alleged.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative penalties up to CAD 500,000 per corporation and CAD 100,000 per individual; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31 is an indictable offense punishable by up to 10 years imprisonment and full asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers with knowledge of, or wilful blindness to, laundering face personal prosecution, forfeiture orders, imprisonment, and can be subjected to travel bans and asset freezes during the criminal investigation.
