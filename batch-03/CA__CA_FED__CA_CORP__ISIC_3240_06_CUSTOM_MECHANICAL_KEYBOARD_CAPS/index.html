Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a federal business corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act, resident in Canada for tax purposes and taxable on worldwide income. If Canadian-controlled (CCPC), it is eligible for the small business deduction on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income. A non-resident corporation is subject to Part I tax only on income from carrying on business in Canada through a permanent establishment, employment income, and gains on taxable Canadian property. Canada does not afford pass-through or territorial classification to incorporated entities.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists; classification is determined by legal form. Notable elections: (i) s. 85 Income Tax Act (ITA) rollover for tax-deferred transfers of property to the corporation; (ii) s. 89(11) ITA paid-up capital election, subject to an immediate deemed-dividend charge where non-resident shareholders receive consideration exceeding PUC; (iii) s. 156 Excise Tax Act (ETA) election to treat supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration; (iv) s. 273 ETA joint venture election; (v) hybrid mismatch deduction-denial rules (ITA ss. 18.2, 18.21, 20.2, 20.21; enacted by Bill C-59 assented 20 June 2024) applicable to deductible hybrid mismatch payments generally made or incurred after 1 July 2022, with a transitional deferral to 30 June 2024 for arrangements grandfathered from 30 October 2023.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 – taxable supplies (other than zero-rated exports, financial services, and exempt supplies) exceeding this amount in a single calendar quarter or in four consecutive calendar quarters trigger mandatory GST/HST registration; voluntary registration is available to small suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods (e.g., injection-molded keycaps): zero-rating applies under s. 1(a), Part V, Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act where the registrant sells tangible personal property and exports it, or delivers it to a common carrier, freight forwarder, or courier for export, taking ownership in Canada and completing export promptly (customarily within 60 days). For offshore services (e.g., custom tooling design sent to a non-resident buyer): zero-rating applies under s. 7, Part V, Schedule VI if the service is performed wholly outside Canada, or relates to real or tangible property situated outside Canada and the non-resident receives it outside Canada. The keycaps are classifiable under HS 8471.60 or HS 9503.00 for customs purposes; the supplier must retain evidence of export to maintain the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed with the Canada Border Services Agency for controlled, regulated, or permit-required exports, or documentary evidence that the shipment falls below the B13A filing threshold
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, or courier manifest (FedEx/UPS/DHL) demonstrating shipment from a Canadian address to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice describing the keycaps, HS classification, quantities, unit price, currency, consignee, and exporting shipper
  • Cargo Control Document or freight forwarder's certificate of export for consolidated shipments
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency settlement from the overseas buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or CRA API clearance regime exists. CRA-acceptable e-invoicing includes EDI, XML, and PDF, provided the invoices are complete and retained for six years. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns (Form GST34) is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable revenue exceeding CAD 1.5 million, available via My Business Account, EDI, or XML submission. The Pan-Canadian e-Invoice standard (Peppol-based) is promoted voluntarily through the Digital Interchange and Data Sharing initiative.

Canada does not legislate a specific export-proceeds repatriation period; the 180-day figure is maintained in this schema as the standard documentary and banking benchmark. On audit, CRA may disallow zero-rating where the exporter cannot produce contemporaneous proof of export; the resulting assessment collects unremitted GST/HST at 5% (or HST at the applicable provincial composite rate of up to 15% where applicable) plus arrears interest at the prescribed quarterly rate, compounded daily. The normal four-year reassessment window applies under ETA s. 298(3); where a registrant has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, or has committed fraud, the CRA may assess at any time (ETA s. 298(4)). Gross negligence penalty under ETA s. 285 is the greater of CAD 25,000 and 25% of the amount claimed as a refund or credit. Criminal prosecution under s. 380 of the Criminal Code may result in a fine up to 200% of the deficiency and imprisonment to a maximum of five years. Supporting records must be retained for six years from the end of the relevant year (ETA s. 286).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member with a fully floating, freely convertible currency for both current-account and capital-account transactions; there are no exchange controls, no profit-remittance permits, and no restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, or capital repatriation for a CA_FED corporation. Frictions are fiscal and structural, not convertibility-based: (i) Part XIII Income Tax Act withholding on payments to non-residents (statutory 25%; treaty-reduced to typically 5%–15% on dividends and 10%–15% on interest); (ii) thin-capitalization and interest-stripping limits under ss. 18(2)–18(6); (iii) soft-loan/benefit rules for non-arm's-length shareholder advances; (iv) Investment Canada Act notification obligations for non-Canadians establishing or acquiring control of a Canadian business above prescribed thresholds; and (v) CBCA director-residency requirements (minimum 25% resident-Canadian directors, or at least one where the board has fewer than four directors). None of these constitute capital-convertibility restrictions; all major currencies (USD, EUR, GBP, CNY) may flow freely into and out of Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriating export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts and convert at market rates without prior approval. The operative compliance burden is regulatory: Canadian financial institutions must report every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and the exporter must preserve the commercial invoice, export declaration (CBSA CARM), transport document, and proof of payment for each batch of receipts. No balance-of-payments reporting beyond Statistics Canada survey thresholds applies to a small keyboard-keycap exporter.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central monetary and banking counterpart for treasury operations; currency-legal framework and exchange-control policy administered jointly with the Department of Finance Canada; FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada) is the financial-intelligence authority for cross-border EFT and AML reporting; OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) prudentially regulates the Schedule I chartered banks. Canada has no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration and no exchange-permit authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM-HIGH as an ancillary cash-management rail, not as a replacement for a Schedule I bank. Shopify Payments and Stripe (Stripe Canada) are FINTRAC-registered payment service providers that settle in CAD to a Canadian bank account, which is fully viable for the intended e-commerce rails. Wise Business Canada, PayPal Canada, and Payoneer are permissible FINTRAC-registered Money Service Businesses for low-value cross-border supplier payouts and overseas-market collection, but they are not banks, hold no CDIC-insured deposits, and cannot offer credit. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Airwallex, Relay) are not chartered under the Bank Act and cannot solicit Canadian deposit business; using them requires a U.S. or EU treasury wallet, which triggers CRA specified-foreign-property reporting on Form T1135 when the aggregate cost exceeds CAD 100,000, plus PCMLTFA record-keeping. Recommended structure: Schedule I bank concentration account as the single source of treasury truth; fintech rails limited to collection and payout automation with end-of-day sweeping into the bank account.

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Airwallex, Relay) are not chartered under the Bank Act and cannot solicit Canadian deposit business; using them requires a U.S. or EU treasury wallet, which triggers CRA specified-foreign-property reporting on Form T1135 when the aggregate cost exceeds CAD 100,000, plus PCMLTFA record-keeping. Recommended structure: Schedule I bank concentration account as the single source of treasury truth; fintech rails limited to collection and payout automation with end-of-day sweeping into the bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 reporting is mandatory where the aggregate gross value of reportable non-arm's length transactions with foreign related persons exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year; CbCR (Form RC4649) must be filed by a Canadian ultimate parent of an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more; contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation of acceptable OECD profiles is required for all cross-border intercompany transactions (Canada has no separate Master File/Local File filing but CRA Information Circular 87-2R demands complete, accurate, and reliable documentation).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the economic substance doctrine through (a) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, ITA s.245), which denies an aggressive transaction lacking bona fide business purpose, a 'significant tax benefit' being the sole motivator; (b) transfer pricing recharacterization under ITA s.247(2) for non-arm's length transactions lacking commercial substance or where the structure does not reflect what would have been agreed by arm's length parties; (c) the OECD 2021 (Pillar Two) GloBE rules adopted in Canada (Global Minimum Tax Act, 2024, 15% minimum for large MNEs); (d) treaty shopping prevention through Limitation-on-Benefits (LOB) in Canada's treaties (e.g., Canada-US treaty Article XXIX A) and a domestic 'general anti-treaty shopping rule' in s.212(1)(a); (e) the 'Active Business' meaning for foreign affiliates. ESR-style registration shell-prohibition does otherwise not apply; however CRA will treat an offshore mailbox company with no employees, assets, or substantive decision-making as a sham on bare trust analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act s.239)

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to 2 years; indictment: fine of not less than 100% and not more than 200% of the tax evaded, plus imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA may also charge the gross negligence penalty (ss.163(2), 50% of the understated tax) as an alternative; interest accrues at the prescribed rate (approx. 9%.)

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under both civil (s.227.1 for unreported withholdings and s.160 for low-value transfers to related persons) and criminal law; the state can obtain a fleeing-felon warrant, placed on CRA's 'offenders travel ban' list, passport confiscation, and prohibition from leaving Canada until restitution; personal bankruptcy does not discharge wilful-evasion fines.
Shell Company / Sham / GAAR

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization or denial of the tax benefit; 25% GAAR penalty on the net tax benefit for transactions undertaken after January 1, 2024 (ITA s.245(4)); the full amount of diverted taxable income plus interest is assessed; treaty-shopping cases may be reassessed years later because the normal reassessment limitation period is suspended for wilful omission (unlimited reassessment under s.152(4)(a)).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for conspiracy to evade tax when they sign a knowingly false treaty-residency certificate; civil restitution plus exemplary damages in minority-shareholder oppression claims; travel bans and asset seizure orders under the Seized Property Management Act apply.
Transfer Pricing Penalty

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.247(3), a penalty of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment (before offsets) is imposed where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000 or 10% of gross revenue and the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to use arm's length data; plus the adjustment itself, gross-up for underpaid tax, "secondary adjustments" (deemed dividends on the recharacterized surplus) and prescribed-rate interest.

Director personal liability: Directors' liability does not directly cover Part I tax deficiencies for a solvent corporation, but where linked to wilful evasion or abetting a false T106 they face criminal exposure and a s.227.1 claim if the corporation fails to remit the assessed amount; a foreign parent with a Canadian PE can have Part XIV branch tax (25%) assessed on the deemed remittance.
AML / FINTRAC (PCMLTFA)

Statutory sanction: For regulated entities, administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,500,000 per violation under the PCMLTFA (civil regime); for criminal money laundering under the Criminal Code s.462.31, senior officers can face up to 10 years imprisonment although this business tier is 'Low Risk Standard' and only needs a normal FINTRAC compliance program if covered by certain thresholds; banks will also freeze corporate accounts if beneficial-ownership disclosures are inaccurate.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the corporation to launder proceeds of crime are personally indictable; culpability follows through the 'directing mind' doctrine; the corporation's bank indemnity and personal guarantees of the director trigger restitution obligations; travel bans and passport refusal are obtainable in criminal proceedings.
