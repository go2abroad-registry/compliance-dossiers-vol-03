Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Generally permissible for registered office purposes if the virtual office provider is a BC registered corporate services provider with a physical street address in British Columbia, and if service of process can be made at that address during business hours. BCBCA s. 35 requires a registered office to be situated in BC, but it does not require a physical staffed reception if the registered office provider is authorized to accept service. However, FINTRAC and financial institutions expect some commercial substance; for a virtual post-production studio without a physical premises, the registered office may be a virtual address, but the company's operational activities must be tracebale and records accessible. If the company professes to be an audio studio but has no physical premises, that is permissible at law but raises the AML risk assessment of banks and processors; the company must maintain a business address, business phone, email, and actual human management in BC if it wants to use a virtual office. Many Canadian banks require a physical business location for merchant card processing, but Stripe and Wise generally accept remote companies with a registered office. The BC registrar does not perform a site visit solely based on a virtual office, but a non-profit or tax may cause a behind-the-scenes check. Industry-standard ULCs often use virtual offices for corporate registry filings while conducting business remotely.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — national tax administration; GST/HST and T2 corporate income tax registration via Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca. BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, via the eTaxBC portal (etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the BC ULC via the BC Corporate Registry (online, no minimum capital); obtain the incorporation number. (2) CRA automatically issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) upon corporate registry integration; a foreign non-resident entity without a BN must apply via BRO or paper application. (3) Open a GST/HST account (RT program account) once annual worldwide taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 or earlier if the ULC issues taxable invoices; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits (ITCs) on production costs (GPU compute, software, subcontractor audio engineers). (4) Register for BC PST via eTaxBC only if selling enumerated taxable goods or services to BC consumers; digital subtitle/voice services exported to non-residents are outside PST. (5) Foreign shareholders holding significant interests should file CRA foreign reporting forms and, for US tax purposes, Form 8832 to elect pass-through classification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (for US federal tax only under check-the-box). For Canadian tax purposes, the BC ULC is mandatorily a resident corporation taxed at the entity level under ITA s. 250(4); Canada has no domestic check-the-box election. The pass-through label derives from the unlimited-liability feature, which permits US shareholders to elect partnership or disregarded-entity status — creating a hybrid entity subject to BEPS Action 2 mismatch scrutiny.

Available election forms

(i) US check-the-box: Form 8832 election (partnership or disregarded entity) for US shareholders under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3. (ii) ITA s. 88(1.2) election unique to ULCs: tax-deferred wind-up of a ULC subsidiary into its parent without the usual 90% ownership requirement. (iii) GST/HST elections: quarterly/annual filing periods and the quick method election (ETA s. 190). (iv) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident realty rental income of the ULC. (v) ITA s. 128.1 continuation election on cross-border corporate migration. (vi) Deemed-dividend elections under ITA s. 84 for surplus repatriation. No election is available to alter the ULC's corporate status for Canadian domestic tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 — the Small Supplier Threshold under ETA s. 148 (worldwide taxable revenues of the ULC and associated persons); mandatory GST registration upon the first taxable supply once the threshold is exceeded. Non-resident digital suppliers providing services to Canadian customers must register at C$30,000 of Canadian-source revenue under the July 2021 Digital Economy rules. BC PST has no small-supplier exemption: a PST permit is required before making enumerated taxable retail sales or providing taxable services in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act. Post-production subtitle transcription and AI voice-cloning services exported to a non-resident (not GST-registered) client qualify under s. 7 (services to non-residents) and as offshore services under s. 10, provided the services are not performed in connection with real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The ULC must hold contemporaneous evidence: written contract with the non-resident counterparty, foreign billing address, delivery of mastered files abroad, proof of foreign payment, and verification of the client's non-registered GST status. Cloud-based digital delivery is zero-rated where the recipient receives the benefit outside Canada. Domestic supplies to Canadian broadcasters or OTT platforms are taxable at 5% GST; AI-generated dubbing may be recharacterized as a taxable supply of software if not genuinely exported — legal structuring is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A via the Canadian Export Reporting System) — mandatory for physical media; not required for pure digital file delivery
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (physical goods) or signed Service Contract / Statement of Work (digital services)
  • Commercial invoice with explicit 'Zero-rated — Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act' notation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT bank credit confirmation evidencing foreign currency payment into the ULC's Canadian bank account
  • GST/HST non-registration verification of the foreign client (CRA registration status search or statutory declaration)
  • Technical delivery log (download links, cloud transfer receipts, IP geolocation) proving offshore receipt of deliverables

None mandatory. Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, digital seal, or QR-code validation for GST purposes. The CRA accepts paper, PDF, EDI, and XML records; since 2024 the CRA participates in the Peppol network on a voluntary basis for e-invoice exchange. European or Asian clients may contractually require Peppol/UBL 2.1 XML files, but non-compliance does not invalidate GST zero-rating. Records must be retained for six years under ETA s. 286.

CRA subjects export zero-rating claims to intensive audit. Reassessment window: four years from the assessment date (ETA s. 298), with no time limit in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud. Clawback: denied ITCs and unreported net tax may be reassessed retroactively with daily compound interest at the prescribed rate plus four percentage points of arrears. ULC-specific exposure: under the BC Business Corporations Act, ULC shareholders are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's liabilities, including GST debts, upon an enforcement shortfall; directors additionally remain liable for unremitted GST under ETA s. 323.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full IMF Article VIII convertibility; current-account flows (royalties, post-production service fees, dividend remittances) and capital-account flows (intercompany equity, shareholder loans) are unrestricted and require no Bank of Canada or government approval. CAD floats freely with no parallel market or surrender premium. The only material remittance hurdle is tax-withholding architecture: Part XIII tax on dividends paid to non-resident ULC shareholders at 25% statutory, reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-US treaty, including the BC ULC look-through eligibility for a 90%+ US corporate shareholder; banks apply OFAC-equivalent sanctions screening and FINTRAC automatic reporting on CA$10,000+ transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio into CAD, and no minimum repatriation threshold. The ULC may retain USD/EUR receipts indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at Canadian banks; only routine FINTRAC reporting triggers apply (single transactions of CA$10,000 or more and cross-border periodic reports of CA$250,000 or more).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; prudential banking regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CTF surveillance and reporting by FINTRAC; trade documentation monitored by Statistics Canada and Canada Border Services Agency. Canada has no dedicated exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Feasibility rating: High as payment and collection rails; Low as treasury depository. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal Part I + 12% BC provincial = 27% combined general corporate rate; 9% federal + 2% BC (11% combined) small-business rate unavailable because a BC ULC with non-resident shareholders is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on every dividend paid by the BC ULC to a non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: T106 Local File documentation threshold: CAD $1,000,000 aggregate annual fair market value of cross-border non-arm's length transactions. Master File mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding CAD $50,000,000 and cross-border related-party transactions exceeding CAD $5,000,000. Contemporaneous documentation must satisfy the 'reasonable efforts' standard in section 247(4) and be produced within 90 days of CRA demand.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, the modernized General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, section 245, effective 20 June 2024) denies a tax benefit from a transaction lacking genuine economic substance, purpose, and arm's-length character; the revised GAAR shifts the burden to the taxpayer on reasonable, economic realignment. The CRA characterizes a shell ULC holding AI IP without employees, premises, or business functions as a 'sham' or 'artificial transaction', enabling reattribution of income to the US shareholder and application of section 163(2) gross-negligence penalties.
