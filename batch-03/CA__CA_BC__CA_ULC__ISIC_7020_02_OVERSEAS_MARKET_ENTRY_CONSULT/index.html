Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: www.canada.ca (CRA My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE, Business Registration Online).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the BC ULC under the Business Corporations Act (SBC 2002, c. 57) via BC Corporate Online, obtaining the BC company number and incorporation certificate. Step 2: Register the ULC for a federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), by phone at 1-800-959-5525, or by filing Form RC1. Step 3: Open the required CRA program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll, if employees are hired). Step 4: A foreign ULC shareholder does not require a separate TIN for Canadian registration; the corporation's BN is the statutory tax identifier. Step 5: Where taxable activities occur, also register for BC PST (eTaxBC) only if making taxable retail sales; consulting services generally fall outside PST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a BC Unlimited Liability Corporation is treated as a corporation for Canadian Income Tax Act purposes and must file a T2 corporate income tax return at entity level (federal Part I 9%-15% plus BC provincial corporate tax 2%-12%, depending on active/small-business income); no domestic pass-through is permitted. The ULC is, however, Pass_Through_Eligible for U.S. federal tax purposes only, via a check-the-box election on U.S. Form 8832, which treats it as a disregarded entity or partnership under the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty fiscally transparent rules.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election; the ULC is always entity-level taxable domestically. For U.S. tax purposes, the ULC may file U.S. Form 8832 (Entity Classification Election) to elect disregarded entity or partnership status with the support of Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty Article IV(7) (fiscally transparent entities). Hybrid mismatch restrictions under ITA ss. 18.4 and 20.2 deny double-deduction outcomes for hybrid entities; no statutory hybrid-mismatch election is available. The ULC may also use standard corporate elections such as an ITA s. 85 rollover for contributed property, but none alter the domestic entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (GST/HST small supplier threshold measured on the value of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, in the preceding four calendar quarters; a resident corporation that is a 'large corporation' with associated group revenue exceeding CAD 10,000,000 must register for GST/HST even if below CAD 30,000, and a person making taxable supplies in Canada who is not a small supplier is required to register).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A service supply is zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 when supplied to a non-resident person who is not registered under Subdivision d of Division V of the ETA, unless the service is performed in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. For ISIC 7020 overseas market entry consulting, zero-rating applies only where the contractual recipient is a non-resident (e.g., a foreign or U.S. client); the benefit must be realized outside Canada and the non-resident must not be a GST/HST registrant. If the ULC advises a Canadian-resident client about entering foreign markets, the supply is a domestic taxable supply at 5% GST. CRA will scrutinize contracts, invoice routing, and payment provenance to distinguish true exports from domestic services; the ULC should document foreign nationality of the recipient, absence of Canadian place of supply, and commercial substance.

  • Signed service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, scope of work, and overseas market-entry deliverables
  • GST/HST invoice expressly marked "Zero-rated export of service - ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7" and stating 0% GST
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the client (foreign registration certificate, foreign business address, passport/ID for individuals, or non-Canadian jurisdiction tax number)
  • Proof of payment or inward remittance from abroad (SWIFT, Wise B2B, bank wire, foreign currency deposit record) demonstrating consideration was received from the non-resident counterparty
  • Work product and performance evidence establishing that the service was performed and consumed outside Canada
  • Where tangible goods are supplied incidentally with the consulting engagement: Canadian customs export declaration (B13A or CAED electronic report), ocean/air waybill or bill of lading, and inward remittance certificate from the paying bank

No real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory government e-invoicing API exist in Canada. GST/HST is self-assessed; net tax is remitted with the GST/HST return (GST34 or GST/HST NETFILE). CRA accepts paper, PDF, EDI, XML, and Peppol BIS invoices as valid books and records, provided they are kept in a readable format for six years. Peppol-based e-invoicing is voluntary and limited to pilot use.

Canada does not impose a general statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline on service exporters; the 180-day figure is applied as an internal CRA commercial-reasonableness benchmark for payment cycles and non-resident supply audits. Where zero-rating is challenged, CRA may reassess the supply at 5% GST, claw back input tax credits, and assess arrears interest (ETA s. 280) plus penalties: 10% of the net tax for false statements (ETA s. 285), 50% gross negligence penalty (ETA s. 285.1), and criminal tax evasion penalties up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment on summary conviction or indictment (ETA s. 239). The standard reassessment period is four years; it is unlimited where misrepresentation, negligence, or wilful default occurred. Red flags include repeated zero-rating without contracts, invoices routed through tax haven intermediaries, lack of non-resident status evidence, and failure to repatriate or trace export receipts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full Article VIII current- and capital-account convertibility applies. No central-bank approval is required for foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, dividends, royalties, or management fees. Profit remittance is unrestricted at exchange-control level but subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory; 15% or 5% portfolio/direct-dividend rates under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (1980), as applicable to an unlimited liability corporation with US shareholders). Additional friction points are AML/FINTRAC transaction thresholds (CAD 10,000), cross-border information returns (Form T1135), and economic-substance expectations of OSFI-supervised banks. No exchange-rate tiering or dual-rate system exists; the CAD floats freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or export-proceeds repatriation quota exists under Canadian law. Consulting and licensing revenues (ISIC 7020 scope) may be retained in foreign currency, held in CAD/USD multi-currency accounts, converted at commercial rates, or hedged indefinitely. Reporting obligations are AML/CTF-driven only: FINTRAC EFT reporting for international wires of CAD 10,000 or more; Cross-Border Currency and Monetary Instruments Report for physical movements of CAD 10,000 or more; and Statistics Canada direct-investment reporting where applicable. Bank of Canada imposes no surrender requirement on resident corporations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; foreign exchange administration resides with the Department of Finance Canada under the Currency Act and Financial Administration Act. Prudential regulation of banks and authorized foreign bank branches is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). AML/CTF oversight of cross-border funds and money services businesses is exercised by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control directorate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a secondary payment rail only. Wise B2B (Wise Payments Ltd. Canada branch) operates as a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business, not a Schedule I or Schedule II bank; it holds no OSFI banking license, no Bank of Canada Lynx settlement account, and no CDIC deposit insurance. Funds are pooled in omnibus partner-bank accounts, creating commingling/counterparty risk. Wise cannot issue cheques, grant credit, or serve as the ULC's primary regulatory-banked treasury. Legal constraints include FINTRAC beneficial-ownership record requirements, bank-side payee-mismatch screening on Wise-originated credits, and prudent reliance rules that require a Canadian chartered bank for high-value flows. Viable uses: sub-CAD 10,000 international vendor payouts, contractor payroll in 40+ currencies, and last-mile disbursements where SWIFT correspondent coverage is thin. Not viable for initial parent capital injection or as the sole settlement hub.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Preferred compliant route: direct SWIFT MT103 (USD) or Fedwire through the Canadian bank's own US clearing branch, converted to CAD at the bank's treasury desk, then credited to the ULC operating account via Lynx. Requirement of exact match: beneficiary name must equal the registered ULC name and address; field 50K must show the actual investing entity only; remittance information (field 70/72) must read 'Capital contribution / equity subscription per BC Board Resolution dated [date]'. The receiving bank must hold the funding board resolution and, for non-resident direct investment, confirm the StatCan direct-investment reporting obligation. All wires at or above CAD 10,000 trigger FINTRAC EFT reporting. Use the bank's Standard Settlement Instructions and the ULC's own BIC-tier routing; do not interpose Wise or any MSB aggregator in the injection chain. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.00% combined effective statutory headline (15.00% federal Part I general corporate rate + 12.00% British Columbia provincial general corporate rate); a BC ULC with non-resident shareholders is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation and therefore does not qualify for the 9% federal or 2% BC small business deduction rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.00% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by the BC ULC to non-resident shareholders under ITA s.212(2).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 is due no later than six months after the fiscal year-end (a December 31 year-end is due June 30 of the following calendar year); quarterly corporate tax installments are payable on the last day of each fiscal quarter, for a calendar year-end: March 31, June 30, September 30 and December 31.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of controlled transactions with a single non-resident counterparty in a taxation year triggers the mandatory Form T106 and contemporaneous Local File documentation; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the MNE group's consolidated revenue reaches CAD 850,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate standalone Economic Substance Regulations of the UAE/offshore type; however, treaty entitlement under the Canada-US Limitation on Benefits Article, the GAAR (s.245), and the OECD commercial-profit attribution framework require genuine business activity. A BC ULC must maintain a registered office in BC, corporate records and minute books, substantive local decision-making, and real fee-earning functions for its overseas market-entry consultancy revenue; a paper conduit with no employees, premises, or local management will be recharacterized and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: ITA s.239(1): fines of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; civil penalty under s.163(2): 50% of the understated tax plus prescribed interest.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director knowingly participates in or authorizes the evasion; directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions under s.227.1, face civil restitution for taxes, and non-resident directors may be exposed to extradition proceedings and cross-border travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Penalty

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(3): penalty of 10% of the net adjustment with a CAD 5,000 minimum, together with gross-up of the adjustment and prescribed interest on the resulting deficiency.

Director personal liability: No automatic statutory personal liability for the corporate TP penalty; however, a director who signs a knowingly false T106 return is exposed to the s.163.2 general civil penalty and loss of voluntary-disclosure protection for the corporation.
Shell Company / General Anti-Avoidance Rule

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s.245) re-determines the tax benefit and imposes the tax that would have been payable absent the avoidance transaction; treaty withholding relief is denied under Limitation on Benefits; the 50% gross-negligence penalty may apply; the BC registrar may strike the ULC for failure to file annual reports under the Business Corporations Act.

Director personal liability: CRA may disregard the corporate shell as a sham, exposing shareholders directly by reason of the ULC's unlimited-liability structure; directors who architect the conduit face accessorial fines, the 50% gross-negligence penalty, and in serious cases criminal liability for counselling tax evasion.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for a first violation, higher on subsequent violations, plus potential criminal fines and confiscation of the funds.

Director personal liability: For a low-risk consultancy, standard AML reporting is limited, but a director who launders proceeds through the ULC bank account is liable under s.355 of the Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 10 years; directors may also be made personally liable for unpaid FINTRAC penalties where the corporation is a mere sham.
