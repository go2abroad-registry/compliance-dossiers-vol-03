Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office address in British Columbia may satisfy the statutory registered office requirement if it is a physical street address in BC designated for service of process and statutory records access. However, for an airline in-flight catering kitchen, a virtual office is not acceptable as the operational commercial substance location; airport security, Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA), Transport Canada and airport landlord requirements mandate a physical, bonded, food-safe production and ramp-delivery facility at or adjacent to the airport.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required for a British Columbia unlimited liability company. Consideration received for unlimited-liability shares must be authorized by the Articles and must be fair to the company and its shareholders.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the BC Fire Code with UL 300-compliant automatic kitchen suppression system; building department structural load approval; WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety clearance covering commercial kitchen equipment and material handling; equipment gas-line and ventilation inspection; airport security pass and ramp vehicle permit for direct aircraft delivery.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required for a British Columbia unlimited liability company. Consideration received for unlimited-liability shares must be authorized by the Articles and must be fair to the company and its shareholders.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — the central bank created under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2) — operates the Lynx high-value payment system and the Real-Time Rail settlement infrastructure, but is not an exchange-control enforcement agency. Foreign-exchange regulation and administration in Canada are distributed across: (i) the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), which prudentially supervises Schedule I banks and their market-risk/FX capital standards; (ii) the Department of Finance Canada, which administers investment-review policy under the Investment Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 28 (1st Supp.)); (iii) the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), which enforces cross-border EFT reporting and AML/ATF surveillance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17); and (iv) the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52), which establishes the Canadian dollar as lawful money. The Minister of Finance, through the Financial Administration Act, may issue emergency foreign-currency directions only in declared national emergencies; no peacetime exchange-control regime currently exists.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 100 Taxable income (net of deductions and adjustments) Report Canadian-source taxable income; apply treaty provisions if applicable. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 10% per month (max 12 months), plus daily interest on arrears.
Box 200 Federal tax payable before abatement Compute federal tax at 15% of taxable income; then apply 10% provincial abatement. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 10% per month (max 12 months), plus daily interest on arrears.
Box 400 Provincial tax payable (BC) Compute BC provincial tax at 12% of taxable income allocated to BC. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 10% per month (max 12 months), plus daily interest on arrears.
Box 550 Total tax payable (federal + provincial) Sum of federal and provincial tax; consider foreign tax credits if applicable. Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax plus 10% per month (max 12 months), plus daily interest on arrears.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if total value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million; Master File required if exceeds CAD 10 million.

Economic substance scope: Not applicable; Canada relies on General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and transfer pricing rules under Section 247 of the Income Tax Act.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; fines up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate tax under Section 227.1 if they failed to withhold or remit; civil restitution and potential travel bans.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined or imprisoned if they knowingly fail to comply; travel bans may be imposed during investigation.
