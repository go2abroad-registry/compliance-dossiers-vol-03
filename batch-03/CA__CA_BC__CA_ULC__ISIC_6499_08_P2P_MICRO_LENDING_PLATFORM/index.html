Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal: CRA My Business Account via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; federal Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://bro-ssr.cra-arc.gc.ca; provincial counterpart: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Victoria, BC.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the BC Unlimited Liability Company through BC Corporate Online (CORES) to obtain the BC incorporation number and company key. 2) Register immediately for a federal Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 ('Request for a Business Number'); provide the BC registry number, registered office address, director names, date of incorporation, and estimated annual revenue; the BN is typically issued in real time. 3) Open a GST/HST account under the BN (via BRO or Form RC1) classifying the entity as a 'financial institution' for Excise Tax Act (ETA) purposes; note that non-resident shareholders must designate a Canadian resident agent and provide an address for service in Canada. 4) If the platform hires Canadian employees, open a CRA payroll account (RC account) for source deductions. 5) Register for BC PST only if the company sells taxable goods or software in British Columbia; pure lending and interest revenue are outside BC PST. 6) File annual federal T2 corporate income tax returns and BC Provincial Corporate Tax schedules using the BN; maintain permanent books of account in Canada under ITA s. 230. The unlimited liability feature of the ULC subjects shareholders to ultimate liability for unpaid corporate taxes, so the CRA may require additional security or updated NR4/Part XIII filings for non-resident shareholders.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' election; a BC ULC is mandatorily taxed as a resident corporation under Part I of the Income Tax Act, subject to entity-level federal and provincial corporate tax on worldwide income. For Canada-US treaty purposes, Article IV(7) of the Convention permits a ULC to be treated as a resident of the US (and fiscally transparent for US federal income tax) if so classified under US domestic law; this does not alter Canadian entity-level taxation. No hybrid mismatch election exists under the ETA or ITA; however, the ULC structure is commonly used for 'loss packaging' with a US parent through consolidating losses at the Canadian entity level while preserving US flow-through treatment. Elections available include the associated-company elections and the section 216 election for rental income, neither of which is core to fintech lending.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Capital injections from non-residents, profit or dividend remittances, loan repayments, and service fees face no exchange-control approval requirements. The sole impediment to outbound remittance is tax, not FX: Part XIII withholding (ITA ss. 212/215) at 25% statutory, reduced to 5% dividend/interest under the Canada-US Treaty for a US-resident shareholder owning at least 10% of the ULC. FINTRAC threshold EFT reporting (C$10,000 within 24 hours) applies to inbound and outbound wires, with the bank as report filer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or repatriation obligation exists in Canada. A BC ULC earning cross-border micro-lending platform fees or service revenue may hold such proceeds indefinitely in CAD or in foreign-currency bank accounts without any mandatory conversion ratio or timeline. For income tax purposes, CRA requires conversion of each transaction into CAD at the Bank of Canada daily noon rate; no regulatory filing is triggered solely by the retention of foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority; no separate Foreign Exchange Administration exists in Canada. FX-related supervision is conducted by OSFI for prudential matters and by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) for transaction reporting. Cross-border currency movements are subject to reporting, not authorization.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW (1.5/5) for direct deployment of an offshore digital EMI into Canada; MEDIUM (3/5) when combined with a Canadian FINTRAC-registered MSB sponsor or a Schedule I bank white-label. Legal constraints: an offshore EMI has no Canadian banking charter, cannot clear through Payments Canada ACSS or Real-Time Rail, and must register as a Payment Service Provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) with the Bank of Canada if handling CAD-denominated payments above C$10M annually. Non-resident EMI staff cannot be named as designated AML compliance officers without a Canadian nexus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act, fines up to $1 million or double the evaded tax, imprisonment up to 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for criminal sanctions, fines, and imprisonment if they directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the evasion
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization of transactions, denial of deductions and losses, penalties up to 25% of transaction value plus interest

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil, personal liability for unpaid taxes, civil restitution claims, and potential regulatory sanctions
AML / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to $10,000 per violation, potential FINTRAC registration suspension or revocation, and license sanctions

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for corporate penalties and potential criminal charges under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act
