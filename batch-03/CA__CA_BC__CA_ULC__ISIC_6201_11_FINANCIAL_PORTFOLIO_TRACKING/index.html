Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible for U.S. federal tax purposes because a Canadian Unlimited Liability Company is not a per se corporation under Treas. Reg. §301.7701-2(b)(8); a single U.S. shareholder may treat the ULC as a disregarded entity and multiple U.S. shareholders as a partnership under check-the-box default rules. For Canadian federal and British Columbia income tax purposes the ULC is an entity-level taxable corporation resident in Canada (deemed resident under ITA s.250(4)(a)), with no statutory flow-through for Canadian tax and no territorial exemption — worldwide income is taxable and distributions may attract deemed-dividend treatment.

Available election forms

U.S. owners may make a check-the-box election (IRS Form 8832, effective date plus 75-day filing window) choosing disregarded entity/partnership/corporation status solely for U.S. federal tax; CRA is not bound by the U.S. election. Canadian law provides no check-the-box; a ULC remains a Canadian corporation. GST/HST planning election: ETA s.156 allows closely related corporations to elect that intercorporate supplies are made for nil consideration. Hybrid mismatch: Canada's draft hybrid arrangement rules (proposed ITA ss.18.2/18.3) disallow deductions for hybrid mismatches and contain no taxpayer election; OECD Action 2 planning should be documented in the master file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility exists for the Canadian dollar, a freely floating G10 currency with no exchange-control approval, quota, surrender, or two-tier rate mechanism. Profit remittance is unrestricted by statute, subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada): 25% statutory rate on dividends, typically reduced to 5%–15% under Article X of the Canada–U.S. Tax Treaty. For a British Columbia ULC, particular attention is required for the deemed-dividend rule in subsection 84(2) of the Income Tax Act when share capital is reduced, shares are redeemed, or the corporation is wound up, because such distributions may be recharacterized as dividends. Cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC EFT reporting obligations for the Canadian bank, not prior authorization by the state.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export or service proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to surrender foreign currency earned by a resident exporter. The ULC may freely maintain foreign-currency accounts in Canada or offshore, subject to ordinary AML due diligence, FATCA/CRS account disclosure, and, where aggregate specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000, Form T1135 information reporting under section 233.3 of the Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; however, Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration. The regulatory perimeter for cross-border treasury activity is jointly governed by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing surveillance under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and the Department of Finance Canada for sanctions and trade policy. No approval from the Bank of Canada is required for capital account transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity injection is executed via SWIFT MT103 wire from the foreign shareholder's bank to the ULC's Schedule I Canadian bank account. The wire must identify the beneficiary as the exact legal name of the BC ULC, include the account number, and reference the share-subscription agreement in the remittance field. The Canadian bank will require a board resolution authorizing the share issuance, the executed subscription agreement, paid-up-capital schedule, and source-of-funds attestation before the capital-escrow funds are released to the operational account. EFT reports are filed by the bank with FINTRAC for each wire at or above CAD 10,000. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal-provincial headline general corporate rate in British Columbia: 27% (federal 15% net of general tax reduction + BC 12%). For a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) earning active business income up to C$500,000: 11% combined (federal 9% + BC 2%). An unlimited liability corporation (BC ULC) is taxable as a corporation under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and BC Income Tax Act; it is not a domestic pass-through entity.
