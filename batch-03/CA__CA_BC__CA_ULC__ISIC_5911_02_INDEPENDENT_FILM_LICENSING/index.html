Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); national online portal: My Business Account (canada.ca/revenue-agency). British Columbia PST is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

Apply to CRA using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) via My Business Account or by mail/fax. Attach the BC incorporation certificate and, where applicable, the unlimited liability clause. CRA issues a 15-character Business Number (BN) with program accounts for corporate income tax (RC) and GST/HST (RT). Non-resident applicants must provide a Canadian business address or appoint a recognized tax representative, and evidence of Canadian business activities may be required. BC PST registration must then be obtained separately through the BC Taxpayer Registration service before taxable sales are made in BC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A BC ULC is taxed as a resident corporation under the Canadian Income Tax Act and cannot elect partnership treatment domestically. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the ULC is eligible to make a check-the-box election under Treasury Regulation Section 301.7701-3 (Form 8832) to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership because shareholders have unlimited liability. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules, including Income Tax Act Section 18.2, apply automatically and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (aggregate of taxable supplies in the current or preceding four calendar quarters for mandatory GST/HST registration; voluntary registration is available for non-residents making zero-rated exports)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of film distribution and licensing rights to a non-resident is zero-rated under GST/HST when the copyright or licensed rights are not for use in Canada. The licensing agreement must restrict the grant exclusively to non-Canadian territories. Ancillary amounts such as minimum guarantees, royalties, and participation payments follow the principal zero-rated supply. Where physical deliverables (DCP, M&E tracks, artwork) are shipped, zero-rating applies only if such goods are exported under customs supervision.

  • BC ULC GST/HST zero-rated supply invoice covering royalties and minimum guarantee
  • Executed film licensing or distribution agreement restricting territory to foreign markets
  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A or AES filing) for physical deliverables such as film prints, DCP, and M&E tracks
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for any physical release materials shipped to the licensee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/FX confirmation evidencing receipt of license fees from abroad
  • Tax residence certificate or W-8IME form confirming the non-resident status of the licensee

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or CRA API mandate for B2B/B2C invoices. Canada uses a PEPPOL network for voluntary B2G e-invoicing. GST/HST registrants must maintain invoices in Canada in paper or electronic format and file returns electronically above prescribed thresholds.

CRA specifically audits zero-rated exports of intangible property for territorial misuse. If documentation fails to prove the recipient is a non-resident or that the licensed rights are not for use in Canada, the zero-rating is revoked, GST/HST is assessed at 5% on the full license fee, and gross negligence penalties may apply under ETA Section 285 (the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net understated tax). Interest accrues from the original remittance due date. Fraudulent claims may lead to criminal prosecution under ETA Section 327, and the four-year normal reassessment period is extended indefinitely where misrepresentation or fraud is established. PST audit consequences may apply separately where physical goods or taxable services are supplied in BC.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada observes IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under a free-floating CAD regime. Film-licensing royalty inflows, distribution advances, loan repayments, and equity injections are unrestricted and require no regulatory approval. Profit remittance from the BC ULC is constrained only by (i) the solvency and net-asset tests of the BC Business Corporations Act (SBC 2002, c. 57) applicable to dividend declarations and (ii) Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, which imposes 25% withholding on non-resident distributions, treaty-reduced to 5% under the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (Article X) for corporate shareholders owning 10% or more of the voting shares. The ULC remains a taxable Canadian corporation for Canadian tax purposes irrespective of any foreign check-the-box election.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no foreign-currency earmarking for film-licensing receivables owed to a BC entity. Export/royalty receipts may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies in Canadian banks. Statutory compliance is limited to FINTRAC reporting of electronic fund transfers at or above CAD 10,000, cross-border currency declarations to CBSA at CAD 10,000, and standard CRA foreign-income reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), exercising monetary authority under the Bank of Canada Act and the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52). Canada has no foreign-exchange administration or capital-control regulator; the wartime Exchange Control Act (1939/1946) was formally repealed in December 1958 and no successor regime exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for operational collections and micro-royalty aggregation; Low (not viable) for capital-escrow custody, letter-of-credit issuance, or first-line treasury mandates. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may clear payments into Canada only as FINTRAC-registered foreign Money Services Businesses and are now also within the Bank of Canada registration regime under the Retail Payment Activities Act (in force 1 November 2024). Such providers cannot accept deposits as authorized banks, cannot issue UCP600 LCs, and Canadian Schedule I banks treat EMI-originated credits as higher-risk third-party processor flows. Constrain EMI usage to low-value digital-platform rental residuals and pre-funding of small distribution expenses.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (15% federal + 12% British Columbia provincial; general corporation rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Canadian Income Tax Act (subject to tax treaty reduction).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within six months after fiscal year-end. Balance of tax due must be paid within two months after fiscal year-end; three-month rule applies only to CCPCs, which is generally unavailable to a non-resident-owned ULC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate related-party cross-border transactions requires mandatory T106 disclosure and robust transfer-pricing documentation in accordance with CRA Form RC-5008 guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies economic substance through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, section 245) and through treaty-limitation jurisprudence. A BC ULC engaged in independent film licensing must demonstrate active management, physical presence in BC (directors, staff or contracted managers), independent administration of licenses, authentic bank accounts, and substantive distribution decisions. A pure conduit or letter-box presence will cause CRA to disregard the ULC as a treaty-resident entity, deny treaty benefits, and retroactively re-characterize royalties as deemed dividends subject to 25% Part XIII tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fines up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted individually for authorizing or acquiescing in false tax returns; civil restitution, travel bar, and up to five years’ imprisonment upon conviction. CRA may pierce the ULC veil and assess directors jointly and severally under common-law fraud and the Tax Court’s umbrella doctrine.
Failure to File / Non-Disclosure of Cross-Border Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty of 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% per month up to 12 months; per-form fees for missing T106 up to CAD 7,500 per failure; criminal failure-to-file charge under subsection 238(4) with fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 12 months.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly fails to file may be fined up to CAD 10,000 personally; the court may impose a travel prohibition as a condition of bail or criminal release. If unpaid withholding tax remains owing, subsection 227.1(1) may activate director’s joint liability for unremitted Part XIII taxes.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation (IP Royalty Stripping)

Statutory sanction: CRA renegotiation under subsection 247(2) adjusts taxable income back to arm’s length; ten percent penalty under subsection 247(3) on the eliminated transfer-pricing amount, plus interest and arrears; potential GAAR application to disregard the repayment scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve artificial royalty arrangements can be civilly liable for the corporation’s tax shortfall in cases of fraudulent conveyance; CRA may assert under the BC Law and Equity Act and the Income Tax Act section 160 to show unjust enrichment, leading to personal restitution and potential asset freeze orders.
AML / Shell Company Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 per violation; criminal fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years for senior officers knowingly involved.

Director personal liability: Directors can be subjected to individual AML penalties up to CAD 250,000 for negligent oversight; in egregious cases, personal criminal liability, travel bans, and orders requiring reconstruction of all license files may be imposed by FINTRAC/CBCA coordination.
