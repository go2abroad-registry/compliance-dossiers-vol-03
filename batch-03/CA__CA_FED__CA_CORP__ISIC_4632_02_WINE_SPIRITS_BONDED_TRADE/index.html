Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal income tax, GST/HST, and excise duty administration; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) - customs accounting, export reporting, and bonded warehouse controls under the Customs Act. Taxpayer portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); Customs portal: CBSA CARM Client Portal (https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/services/carm-mrca/ccp-pcce-eng.html).

E-government filing portal

A Canadian business corporation or a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number). The following program accounts are appended to the BN: RT (GST/HST), RM (import/export), RP (payroll), and RD (corporate income tax). Non-resident entities making supplies through a permanent establishment (or registering voluntarily) must apply to the CRA Non-Resident Registration Desk and may be required to post security under section 307 of the Excise Tax Act. For bonded trade in wine and spirits, two additional authorizations are required: (i) an Excise Warehouse Licence under the Excise Act, 2001 (CRA Form L500), which permits duty-suspended storage of excisable spirits and wine; and (ii) where applicable, a Customs Bonded Warehouse licence under the Customs Act administered by CBSA. Both licences require a premises description, a responsible-person declaration, and evidence of financial security; no minimum stated share capital is required for federal incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election for corporate entity classification; a corporation is always a separate taxable entity. Relevant elections include: (i) GST/HST elections - subsection 156(1) election for capital property, section 122 election for joint venture operators, and section 128 election for closely related corporations; (ii) income tax elections - subsection 256(7) joint loss-consolidation election for amalgamating corporations, subsection 89(11) paid-up capital election, and section 111.0 amalgamation election; (iii) hybrid mismatch rules under subsection 18.4(1) of the Income Tax Act and Part XXVI of the Income Tax Regulations (BEPS Action 2) which deny or defer deductions in Canadian-resident payor entities; and (iv) excise duty deferral elections under the Excise Act, 2001 allowing periodic payment of duty on goods withdrawn from an excise warehouse, subject to CRA-approved financial security.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - small supplier threshold under subsection 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act, measured on taxable supplies made in Canada during the current and preceding three calendar quarters. Registrants exporting bonded wine or spirits should register for GST/HST (RT program account) even if below the threshold, in order to zero-rate exports and recover input tax credits; non-resident suppliers making supplies in Canada may be required to register under section 240(2.1).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Wine and spirits supplied by way of sale are zero-rated under section 1 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier is a GST/HST registrant and the goods are exported by the supplier or by a non-resident recipient in accordance with the proof-of-export requirements of section 23(2.1) and the Proof of Export Regulations. Goods withdrawn from a licensed excise warehouse for direct export are also relieved from federal excise duty under subsection 29(1) of the Excise Act, 2001. Ancillary services supplied to a non-resident (e.g., bonded storage, handling, and logistics in respect of exported goods) may be zero-rated under section 7 of Part V of Schedule VI. The exporter must obtain and retain documentary evidence of export and, in an export refund context, must file a GST/HST rebate or apply the zero-rating at source; fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating is denied with retroactive effect.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A transmitted via CERS / CAED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (with carrier and vessel/aircraft identifiers)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • CBSA Customs Accounting Document (Form B3-3 or CARM electronic entry) for the underlying importation
  • Excise Warehouse Withdrawal / Duty-Suspension Transfer Document (CRA excise records)
  • Non-Resident Purchase Order or International Sale Contract
  • Proof of Foreign Payment (SWIFT wire confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoice authorization exists in Canada as of 2025. Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million (via GST/HST NETFILE or TELEFILE). Licensed excise warehouses must file excise duty returns and remit payments electronically through the CRA Excise Duty System. Commercial import and export declarations must be transmitted electronically through the CBSA CARM (CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management) platform and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). Voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is being phased in under the national e-invoicing roadmap but is not yet compulsory.

CRA and CBSA jointly audit export zero-rating claims for bonded goods. Where proof of export is absent or the goods are not in fact exported, the supply is re-characterized as taxable at 5% federal GST (plus applicable provincial HST), with interest and penalties assessed. Penalties include: 25% of the net tax misstated under section 280 of the Excise Tax Act; a further late-filing penalty of 1% of unremitted amounts plus 25% for repeat non-compliance; and fines up to CAD 25,000 for knowingly making false or deceptive statements under section 285.1. Criminal evasion under section 327 is punishable by fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Under the Excise Act, 2001, false declarations in respect of bonded goods attract penalties up to CAD 25,000 and forfeiture of the goods; the excise warehouse licence may be suspended or revoked and any posted security called. CRA also applies a commercial-reasonableness benchmark for export proceeds: where consideration is not repatriated within approximately 180 days of export without documented credit terms, the claim may be reassessed on the basis that the supply was not genuinely made to a non-resident for export, with consequential GST/HST clawback, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a freely floating exchange rate regime; Canada maintains current and capital account liberalisation consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no exchange control approvals, no surrender requirements, and no central bank authorization gates for profit, dividend, interest, or capital remittances; dividend distributions are nevertheless subject to Part XIII federal withholding tax (generally 25 percent, reduced under applicable tax treaties, e.g., 5 percent for a US corporate shareholder holding at least 10 percent of voting stock under the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention). Bonded wine and spirits exporters may keep USD, EUR, and GBP balances onshore or offshore without regulatory restriction, provided that FINTRAC large-value ETR reporting and Statistics Canada balance-of-payments reporting obligations are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts; export earnings may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency or cleared through multi-currency accounts. Compliance triggers are limited to FINTRAC reporting for international electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more (or foreign equivalent) and CBSA documentation requirements for export and re-export of alcohol from bonded warehouses.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), acting with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential regulation of commercial banks, FINTRAC for anti-money laundering and cross-border electronic funds transfer surveillance, and the Department of Finance for exchange rate authority under the Currency Act and the Bank Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. Canada does not recognise the EU electronic money institution (EMI) licence; an offshore digital EMI cannot take deposits, extend credit, or directly clear through Payments Canada. A non-bank payment services provider must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC and re-mediate through a Canadian Schedule I bank or an authorised foreign bank branch. Offshore EMIs may be used only as a secondary payment rail, not as the principal treasury depository, because uninsured offshore balances create concentration risk and may be viewed unfavourably in the enhanced due diligence required for the spirits and alcohol wholesale sector.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-subordinated debt is routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the non-resident parent to the Canadian operational account; no Bank of Canada or exchange control approval is required. Wire references should clearly state paid-up capital or shareholder loan for bonded trade working capital, supported by board resolutions and CBCA share register updates; international transfers of CAD 10,000 or more trigger FINTRAC ETR reporting by the receiving bank. Non-resident investments must also be verified against Investment Canada Act notification thresholds (establishment of a non-cultural Canadian business is generally notifiable only for national security risk, while acquisitions require filing), and balance-of-payments forms such as Statistics Canada B-115/ITR must be completed if requested.Settlements for Bordeaux and aged Scotch purchases are cleared through (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wire from the CAD/EUR/GBP operational account for routine low-risk transactions; (ii) irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by the Canadian bank under UCP 600 and URDG 758 for high-value or new supplier relationships, with payment against warehouse receipts and shipping documents; (iii) documentary collection (DP/DA) as an intermediate instrument; and (iv) Payments Canada Lynx high-value CAD wires for domestic distributor payments and CBSA excise settlement. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (Income Tax Act, ss. 123(1.1) and 123.4; effective for taxation years commencing after 2018). Separate provincial/territorial rates apply to taxable income allocated to the province of establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder (s. 212(2), Part XIII, Income Tax Act).
