Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account at canada.ca. Revenu Québec administers GST/HST and QST in Quebec on behalf of CRA.

E-government filing portal

Incorporate the Business Corporation federally or provincially, then obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts from CRA. Use BRO to open the RT (corporate income tax), RC (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RN (import/export) accounts; the RT account is the federal corporate TIN. Non-resident-owned Canadian corporations must provide foreign documentation and resident director details; CRA issues the BN upon successful registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a check-the-box or elective pass-through classification; a Business Corporation is always an entity-level taxpayer for federal and provincial income tax. Relevant statutory elections include the GST/HST closely related group election, joint venture election, fiscal-period elections, and Income Tax Act rollover elections. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory and do not grant elective recharacterisation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) in either the current calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding this small supplier threshold mandates GST/HST registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Schedule VI to dental zirconia blocks sold to non-resident customers when the goods are exported as part of the supply, originate from a Canadian supplier, leave Canada before use in Canada, and are delivered by the supplier or a common carrier engaged by the non-resident buyer. The supply is a taxable supply at 0%, allowing full input tax credit recovery. The goods must not be consumed, processed or forfeited in Canada, and the supplier must retain proof satisfying CRA of export and non-resident beneficial purchase.

  • CBSA Canada Export Report / CERS electronic export declaration with unique export reference number
  • Commercial invoice with zero-rated export notation, non-resident buyer identity and export HS classification
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading or air waybill showing foreign destination and export from Canada
  • Destination-country customs import documentation or other outward-clearance evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 payment confirmation from the non-resident buyer
  • Carrier tracking, terminal movement and insurance records evidencing continuous export movement

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API invoice approval regime exists in Canada. CRA accepts electronic invoices as long as prescribed records are kept and returns are filed electronically by registrants above CAD 1,500,000 in annual taxable supplies. Export declarations must be transmitted electronically to CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), which is a customs reporting requirement, not an invoice clearance mechanism.

CRA audits export zero-rating strictly and may reassess unsupported invoices at 5% GST (or applicable HST) with interest. False zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to gross-negligence or fraud penalties, arrears interest, denial of input tax credits, and unlimited reassessment periods where misrepresentation or wilful default is established. Continued non-compliance may lead to criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act and formal penalty and demand measures under the CRA compliance framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account. No exchange-control approval, allocation license, or profit-remittance permit is required for dividend distributions, capital repatriation, or trade payments. The only fiscal friction is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced as applicable) on dividend remittances; currency conversion is executed at freely negotiated market rates through authorized financial institutions. CRA tax reporting (e.g., T106 for foreign parent-entity allocations) and FINTRAC $10,000+ transaction reporting apply but do not constrain convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export receipts. The corporation may retain foreign-currency balances in domestic or offshore bank accounts, subject only to Income Tax Act s.261 foreign-currency income conversion rules, standard CRA reporting of foreign-source revenue, and AML declarations under FINTRAC rules. No Bank of Canada or federal authority requires conversion of USD or other export currency into CAD.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with ancillary regulatory authority exercised by the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Exchange-control authority is not centralized in a dedicated foreign-exchange administration; governance derives from the Bank of Canada Act, the Currency Act, the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, and the Special Economic Measures Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Non-bank payment service providers (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) are legally viable as payment-only rails and must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC. However, no fintech EMI holds a Schedule I or II bank charter in Canada, and deposit-taking remains a regulated banking monopoly. Client funds must be safeguarded in segregated trust accounts at a chartered bank, and the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires a Bank of Canada retail-payment service provider license. Fintech rails are appropriate for low-value trade disbursements, supplier payments, and receivable collection, but cannot substitute for a chartered bank in capital deposit, credit facilities, or export-financing structures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol follows four steps. First, the foreign parent or shareholder originates a SWIFT MT103 wire through a correspondent bank to the Canadian chartered bank; the remittance narrative must state 'equity contribution / share subscription' and identify the Canadian corporate beneficiary. Second, the Canadian entity must record the receipt as paid-up capital or contributed surplus in its corporate ledger, with the board of directors adopting a resolution approving the share allotment. Third, the CAD or USD credit is cleared through Payments Canada (LVTS for large-value CAD settlements) or the bank's USD clearing infrastructure; the receiving bank files the FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report where the amount exceeds CAD 10,000, extinguishing any separate corporate reporting obligation. Fourth, the corporation must preserve the MT103 confirmation, bank statement, share certificate, and CRA share-issuance schedules (including Form T106 if a foreign parent exists) to evidence the capital injection in any subsequent tax or immigration-compliance review.Primary clearing rails are (i) SWIFT MT103 wires for cross-border supplier settlements and distributor refunds; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit, governed by UCP600 and issued by the Canadian bank, for large capital-equipment purchases of dental milling systems and sintering furnaces; (iii) CAD-denominated domestic payments to dental laboratories and service providers cleared via the Real-Time Rail (RTR) or LVTS; and (iv) USD-denominated trade disbursements cleared through the bank's U.S. correspondent network. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory dollar threshold under s.247 ITA; contemporaneous TP documentation is mandatory for all non-arm's-length cross-border transactions; CRA audit trigger and penalty threshold are focused on transactions exceeding CAD 1,000,000 per class, and the 10% gross-valuation penalty applies where adequate documentation is not produced by the filing due date

Economic substance scope: Canada does not codify OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, corporate residence is based on directors' management control and the 'central mind and management' test, and s.245 GAAR denies tax advantages from transactions lacking genuine business purpose, economic substance, or that misuse a DTA residence certificate; a shell CA corporation with no employees, premises, or local sales activity will be reassessed as non-resident with branch tax exposure

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to Report Revenue

Statutory sanction: s.239(1) ITA: summary conviction fine of 50-100% of the evaded tax plus up to 2 years imprisonment; indictment fine of 100-200% of evaded tax plus up to 5 years imprisonment; gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) equal to the greater of 10% of understatement or 50% of the benefit

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for any unremitted Part XIII and payroll withholding under s.227.1; on indictment, officers face travel bans, extradition risk, criminal imprisonment, and civil restitution orders for diverted receivables
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: s.247(3) ITA penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where insufficient contemporaneous documentation exists; reassessed Part I tax plus prescribed-rate interest is not insurable

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign incomplete T106 returns can be found guilty of an offence under s.244(1) with fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 12 months; no indemnity from the corporation applies for the penalty
Shell Company / Sham Residence

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment under s.245 nullifies the tax advantage, plus s.163(2) penalty and interest; CRA may apply the substance-over-form doctrine to reallocate income back to the non-resident principal

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face CRS/false-statement prosecution in relation to a sham residence certificate; personal tax liability may be imposed for the whole Canadian-source income under s.160(1)
AML / FINTRAC Reporting Shortfall

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 500,000 for first non-compliance and CAD 1,000,000 for repeat, plus Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) Act criminal fines and up to 5 years imprisonment for laundering of proceeds

Director personal liability: Officers who direct transaction splitting are personally liable for AML fines and imprisonment under s.83 PCMLA; CRA will also deny tax deductions for fines imposed on a related party
Surplus Stripping / Dividend Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: Part XIII WHT of 25% recharacterization on deemed dividends under s.84.1/ s.212.1, denial of interest deductions under thin-capitalization / earnings-stripping provisions, and a 50% penalty under s.163(2) where intent to avoid WHT is established

Director personal liability: Directors remain jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding on deemed dividends; the CRA can issue a director liability assessment under s.227.1 without first exhausting the corporation's assets
Unlicensed Medical Device Manufacturing

Statutory sanction: Food and Drugs Act s.31/ s.31.2: fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment for repeated offence; health product compliance orders halt sale, and gross income traceable to unlicensed units is taxable under the Misrepresentation doctrine without offsetting deductions

Director personal liability: Senior officers who cause, authorize, or acquiesce in the violation are personally liable on summary conviction to fines up to CAD 50,000 or imprisonment up to 6 months, and may be subject to director fitness-to-act orders
