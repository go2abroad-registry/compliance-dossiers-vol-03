Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal corporate income tax and GST/HST administration; CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca; British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch — British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administration and BC eTax portal at etax.gov.bc.ca.

E-government filing portal

A BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is deemed resident in Canada under ITA s.250(4); obtain BC incorporation certificate and articles, then apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) with a GST/HST program account using Form RC1 or Business Registration Online. The BN is the federal TIN; GST/HST registration is mandatory when the small-supplier threshold is exceeded. For BC PST, register separately with the BC Ministry of Finance via BC eTax using the ULC's BN and corporate details; there is no separate BC TIN. A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada without a Canadian incorporation also files Form RC1 and may be issued a BN with applicable program accounts; for this gaming operation, Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlement records must be tied to the BN for tax compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the BC ULC is eligible for pass-through classification under applicable foreign (e.g., U.S.) tax rules; for Canadian income tax purposes, the ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation taxed at entity level, and for foreign-owner hybrid situations the CRA may apply hybrid mismatch restrictions.

Available election forms

No domestic CRA 'check-the-box' election exists; the ULC is a corporation under the Income Tax Act. Available elections include ITA s.85 rollover elections for asset transfers, ITA s.93(1) foreign affiliate dividend elections, and ETA s.156 GST/HST group registration. For U.S. federal tax purposes, an IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election may elect the wholly owned BC ULC to be disregarded or treated as a partnership; Canada may then apply hybrid mismatch rules under ITA s.18.6 to deny double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion benefits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold measured over the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; non-resident suppliers of digital services to Canadian consumers must register for simplified GST if Canadian-source digital revenue exceeds CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period. BC PST: no small-supplier threshold; registration is triggered before the first taxable sale, lease, or licence of software/gaming services in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated treatment applies under Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act for exported goods and for services or intangible personal property supplied to non-resident recipients where the supply is not for use or consumption in Canada. For simulated social casino and virtual slots games, the digital gaming service may be zero-rated when the user and delivery point are outside Canada and the recipient is not registered under the GST/HST. Zero-rating is denied where the Canadian market is targeted or where app-store transaction data identifies Canadian consumers. The supplier must retain credible evidence of non-resident status and foreign delivery, including App Store / Google Play storefront country, IP geolocation, payment origin, and customer contractual location. BC PST is not chargeable on software delivered outside British Columbia, but PST does not constitute an input tax credit and is not refundable through the GST/HST system.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration (or equivalent electronic export reporting for physical goods)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / transport documentation for exported products
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement records evidencing foreign-currency consideration received
  • Executed master agreement, end-user licence agreement, and invoices referencing the non-resident recipient
  • App-store user-country, IP geolocation, and payment-origin reports proving delivery outside Canada for digital gaming services

Canada does not currently impose mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is optional and operates through the voluntary PEPPOL network. The CRA requires records sufficient to determine tax on taxable supplies, including invoices, app-store settlement reports, and transaction-level consumer country data; BC PST returns are filed via BC eTax and require no fiscal clearance prior to issuance of invoices.

CRA may scrutinize zero-rated export claims and require substantiation before refunding input tax credits or issuing export refunds. The normal reassessment period is four years, but the period is unlimited where misrepresentation or fraud is established. Penalties include civil penalties under ETA s.285.1 for false statements or omissions — $100 plus 25% of the federal tax purportedly evaded — plus statutory interest on reassessed GST/HST and clawed-back refunds. Criminal liability under ETA s.327 applies to wilful evasion. For income tax, ITA s.163(2) imposes a 50% penalty for gross-negligence false statements. BC Consumer Taxation Branch may reassess uncollected PST and impose provincial penalties where purported export delivery is not substantiated.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; no exchange control legislation restricts capital inflows or profit remittance for non-resident owners; CAD is freely deliverable, and USD/CAD parity risk is managed through the market rather than administrative conversion quotas; ULC dividends and redemption proceeds may be repatriated without withholding deferral if subject to Canada-BC tax treaties, subject only to standard Part XIII non-resident withholding tax where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export proceeds, including Apple/Google Play settlement remittances; foreign currency receipts may be held in multi-currency accounts indefinitely.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (monetary authority); Department of Finance Canada (foreign exchange policy lead); Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) (banking supervision); Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) (AML/EFT surveillance)

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal and British Columbia general corporate rate: 15.0% + 12.0% = 27.0% (2024 rates, non-CCPC active income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax under ITA section 212(2) on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder. Under the Canada-US Tax Treaty, reduced to 5% where the U.S. payee is a company that owns at least 10% of the voting stock of the Canadian payer, otherwise 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada imposes a contemporaneous documentation expectation where the aggregate value of covered cross-border non-arm's length transactions in a taxation year exceeds CAD 1 million; CRA administrative practice also considers gross revenue of CAD 10 million as part of a materiality test. For country-by-country reporting, an ultimate parent or surrogate parent must file CbC Report when the MNE group's consolidated revenue is at least CAD 750 million. Documentation must include a Master File and a Local File when requested; the Local File must identify the tested parties and apply a comparability analysis.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to those in the UAE or other offshore finance centres. However, Canadian jurisprudence applying the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under ITA section 245 and the doctrine of sham requires every taxpayer to show economic reality, business purpose, and genuine operational substance. For a BC ULC, tax authorities will examine whether the corporation has Canadian offices, employees, board activity, decision-making, books, records, and actual risk-taking; a ULC lacking such substance may be disregarded or the transactions recharacterized for Canadian tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty under ITA section 163(2) in the amount of 50% of the federal tax understated; provincial penalty on the BC income tax understated. Criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, up to five years imprisonment on indictment, restitution orders, and court-ordered asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize false filings may be jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and GST/HST under section 227.1; CRA can seize personal bank accounts, garnish wages, and pursue travel bans or criminal convictions that bar cross-border movement. Directors may also be personally liable to the Crown for civil fraud under common law piercing of the corporate veil.
Shell Corporation / GAAR Abuse / Unrealistic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR permits CRA to deny the tax benefit and reassess; transfer-pricing penalty under section 247(3) may impose a 10% penalty on understated taxable income; treaty benefits may be denied under Canada-US Treaty Limitation on Benefits provisions; BC registrar may dissolve the corporation for failure to meet annual filing requirements under the BC Business Corporations Act.

Director personal liability: In a ULC, shareholders bear unlimited liability for corporate obligations, and directors can be personally liable for knowing participation in fraudulent preferences, illegal dividends, or misrepresentations in corporate filings. Court-ordered restitution may extend to personal assets, and directors may face travel bans in connection with civil fraud proceedings.
AML / Money Laundering / Unlicensed Real-Money Gaming

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act of up to CAD 1 million per violation for entities and up to CAD 500,000 for individuals; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code section 462.31 punishable by up to 10 years imprisonment; unlicensed casino-related gaming in British Columbia is also an offence under the Gaming Control Act, exposing the corporation to fines and forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be criminally liable where they direct or participate in money laundering or knowingly benefit from the proceeds. Under Bill C-45 provisions on criminal liability of organizations, senior officers may be personally convicted if they knew or were criminally negligent. Civil restitution to victims of unlicensed gaming and asset freezes may also be ordered, with director travel restrictions imposed pending extradition or fraud investigations.
Part XIII / NR4 Withholding Violations on Non-Resident Distributions

Statutory sanction: Under ITA section 227(8), interest accrues on unremitted withheld amounts; section 227(9) imposes a penalty of 10% of the amount that should have been withheld. CRA may assess the payer for the entire unreported Part XIII tax amount under section 227(4), with no corresponding deduction in the payer's income; failure to file NR4 returns attracts penalties of CAD 25 per day per return, up to CAD 2,500 per return, plus late-filing penalties.

Director personal liability: Section 227.1 imposes joint and several personal liability on directors for unremitted withholding tax if they failed to exercise reasonable diligence. In cross-border structures, the Canadian agent or resident representative of a non-resident ULC can also be personally liable for unreported Part XIII tax. Directors may face civil suits for restitution, seizure of personal assets, and in egregious cases travel bans while CRA and the Public Prosecution Service pursue criminal sanctions.
