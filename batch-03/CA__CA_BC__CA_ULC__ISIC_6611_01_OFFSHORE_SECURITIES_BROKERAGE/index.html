Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Limited. At the registry level, BC permits a registered office and records office in the form of a professional registered-office service address, provided it is a real physical address within British Columbia where process can be served and where statutory records (including the UBO significant-control register) are physically accessible during business hours; a pure PO box or co-working virtual maildrop is not compliant. However, for the regulated retail securities brokerage scope (ISIC 6611), BCSC and CIRO dealer registration requires substantive commercial presence in Canada: a physical business office with client record retention in Canada, a registered Chief Compliance Officer, a designated Key Individual, and segregated client property accounts. A virtual office is therefore acceptable only for the pre-licensing holding phase and for the as-yet-unregistered ULC shell; it is not sufficient to obtain dealer registration for retail equities, CFDs, and derivatives. Once trading activity commences, an audited physical-premises commitment is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported pursuant to Schedule VI, Part V, ETA (and Part VII for export delivery services) are zero-rated; services supplied to a non-resident who is not a GST/HST registrant are zero-rated where the service is performed wholly or partly outside Canada (Part V, s. 7); certain financial services supplied to non-resident non-registrants, not relating to Canadian financial instruments or Canadian-resident debt, are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part IX; the offshore brokerage must document that the client is a non-resident and the service does not relate to Canadian-resident issuers or Canadian real property.

  • Customs export declaration (CBSA B13A or processed e-manifests/electronic export report; permits for controlled goods)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill (ocean/air transport evidence)

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is a fully convertible, freely floating currency. Canada has no exchange-control law and does not restrict capital-account or current-account convertibility. There is no central-bank approval, no non-resident deposit restriction, and no forced FX trading channel for ordinary cross-border securities brokerage. Profit remittance is constrained only by tax rules: a Canadian ULC distributing dividends to non-resident shareholders is subject to Part XIII withholding tax, generally 25% unless reduced by an income tax treaty. The withholding is administered by the Canada Revenue Agency and does not create a currency-access or convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada does not impose export-proceeds surrender rules: no statutory repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export and foreign-brokerage receipts may be retained in any currency, and the prescribed conversion ratio is 0%.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority. Canada does not operate a separate foreign-exchange control administration; banking prudential supervision rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/ATF reporting supervision with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Ramp Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as the principal settlement bank; Medium as an ancillary FX or payment-receive utility. Canada does not issue a standalone 'electronic money institution' licence to non-bank fintech firms. Foreign digital EMIs cannot passport into Canada; a Canadian fintech payment company is generally structured as a money services business (MSB/FMSB) registered with FINTRAC, not as a bank. For an IIROC/CIRO regulated securities broker, retail client funds must be held in trust in a Canadian financial institution; an offshore EMI cannot be the depository of segregated client funds and cannot rely on CDIC insurance. It may, at best, provide collection/on-ramp services before funds are swept to the Canadian bank trust account.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be filed within six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end; balance-due date is normally three months after year-end (extended to six months for eligible CCPCs claiming the small business deduction).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

A non-resident ULC must compute Canadian-source income; all business income attributable to Canadian operations is subject to Canadian tax unless a tax treaty tie-breaker applies, in which case a T2 with treaty factual disclosures is still mandatory. Late filing penalty is 5% of the unpaid balance, plus 1% per month up to 12 months (maximum additional 12%); interest is compounded daily at prescribed rates; failure to file electronically may also incur an additional CAD 250 penalty.
Line 130 - Taxable income Deduct losses carried forward, non-taxable amounts, and any special deductions purportedly available to the ULC's tax classification. Taxable income for non-resident investors is reduced only by expenses genuinely incurred to earn Canadian business income; no arbitrary allocation to a foreign situs is allowed. Late filing penalty is 5% of the unpaid balance, plus 1% per month up to 12 months (maximum additional 12%); interest is compounded daily at prescribed rates; failure to file electronically may also incur an additional CAD 250 penalty.
Line 160 / Schedule 1 - Federal tax calculation Apply the general rate or small business rate; compute tax abatement and general reduction; include refundable dividend tax on investment income if applicable. Federal Part I tax applies at the statutory rate; treaty rates and foreign tax credits are claimed on Schedule 21 and T2209 for non-Canadian source income. Late filing penalty is 5% of the unpaid balance, plus 1% per month up to 12 months (maximum additional 12%); interest is compounded daily at prescribed rates; failure to file electronically may also incur an additional CAD 250 penalty.
BC provincial T2-BC supplement Compute British Columbia corporate income tax at the general or small business rate and apply any provincial schedule deductions. The BC allocation factors on T2-BC require income apportionment among jurisdictions; a permanent establishment in BC triggers BC filing obligations. Late filing penalty is 5% of the unpaid balance, plus 1% per month up to 12 months (maximum additional 12%); interest is compounded daily at prescribed rates; failure to file electronically may also incur an additional CAD 250 penalty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 5,000,000 aggregate value of non-arm's length cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties in a taxation year; Country-by-Country Report threshold is consolidated group revenue of CAD 750,000,000 (or equivalent).

Economic substance scope: Canada imposes substance over form through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and transfer-pricing rules. A ULC has no special economic-substance exemption; an arrangement lacking genuine business purpose may be ignored or recharacterized under s.245 and s.247 of the Income Tax Act.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil gross-negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax under s.163(2); criminal conviction under s.239(1) can impose a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and, under BC unlimited liability provisions, shareholders may be personally jointly and severally liable for corporate tax obligations on insolvency; directors may be assessed personally when tax evasion is attributable to their direction or acquiescence.
Unjustified Transfer Pricing / Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may deem an arm's-length transfer or recharacterize the transaction; a 10% penalty under s.247(8) applies to net transfer-pricing adjustments where reasonable efforts were not made; GAAR can additionally deny all tax benefits and impose penalties.

Director personal liability: If the ULC is operated as a sham, the corporate veil is overridden; directors may be assessed personally under s.227.1 for unremitted GST/HST and employee source deductions, and CRA may pursue injunctions, personal asset liens and travel bans in severe cases.
Money Laundering / Unregistered Securities Dealing

Statutory sanction: Under the BC Securities Act, unregistered trading can result in administrative penalties up to CAD 5,000,000 or twice the profit from the misconduct; criminal money-laundering penalties under the Criminal Code and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act apply, with imprisonment of up to 10 years; FINTRAC administrative penalties can reach CAD 500,000 for a corporation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized a ULC's unregistered brokerage or failed to maintain identifiable beneficial ownership face personal civil liability in securities class actions, director bans, personal AML fines and disgorgement of profits under s.155 of the BC Securities Act.
