Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; primary tax portals are CRA My Business Account (MNBA) for account administration, GST/HST NETFILE for indirect tax return filing, and Business Registration Online (BRO) for new entity and program-account registration. Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada) administers federal incorporation separately, but all tax identity obligations are discharged exclusively with the CRA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent or newly formed Canadian business corporation obtains its CRA Business Number (BN) by: (1) completing federal or provincial incorporation, or lodging the foreign entity's charter evidence where the entity operates via a branch; (2) filing the BN application through Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or Form RC1A (non-resident without a Canadian presence), declaring that the entity carries on business in Canada under Income Tax Act s. 253 or maintains a Canadian establishment under Excise Tax Act s. 121; (3) providing certified articles of incorporation or foreign corporate registry extract, registered-office address, full director/officer identities, and a completed beneficial-ownership declaration; (4) receiving the 9-digit BN and activating the applicable CRA program accounts: RC (corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RM (customs-import-export); and (5) registering at provincial level for employer health/payroll taxes if the IVD manufacturing facility has Canadian employees. BRO registrations generally process in 5-15 business days; a non-resident with no Canadian establishment and only passive export activities may apply for voluntary GST/HST registration to recover input tax credits on Canadian feedstock, but the CRA may require Form RC59 (authorizing representative), an auditor confirmation, and proof of foreign tax residence before activating the RT account.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A federal or provincial Business Corporation is a separate person under the Income Tax Act, is deemed resident in Canada by virtue of incorporation under ITA s. 250(2), and is subject to tax on worldwide income. It is not a pass-through entity; no entity-level check-the-box is available to convert it into a partnership or disregarded branch for Canadian tax purposes. The federal general corporate rate after the 10% provincial abatement and general rate reduction is 15%, plus provincial corporate income tax of broadly 8%-16% depending on province; a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) can use the 9% federal small-business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, subject to the 12-month business limit aggregation rules. No federal tax-exempt-zone classification applies to onshore manufacturing, and the scientific research and experimental development (SR&ED) investment tax credit remains the principal federal incentive for IVD equipment R&D. — A foreign parent or newly formed Canadian business corporation obtains its CRA Business Number (BN) by: (1) completing federal or provincial incorporation, or lodging the foreign entity's charter evidence where the entity operates via a branch; (2) filing the BN application through Business Registration Online (BRO), Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), or Form RC1A (non-resident without a Canadian presence), declaring that the entity carries on business in Canada under Income Tax Act s. 253 or maintains a Canadian establishment under Excise Tax Act s. 121; (3) providing certified articles of incorporation or foreign corporate registry extract, registered-office address, full director/officer identities, and a completed beneficial-ownership declaration; (4) receiving the 9-digit BN and activating the applicable CRA program accounts: RC (corporation income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RM (customs-import-export); and (5) registering at provincial level for employer health/payroll taxes if the IVD manufacturing facility has Canadian employees. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A federal or provincial Business Corporation is a separate person under the Income Tax Act, is deemed resident in Canada by virtue of incorporation under ITA s. 250(2), and is subject to tax on worldwide income. It is not a pass-through entity; no entity-level check-the-box is available to convert it into a partnership or disregarded branch for Canadian tax purposes. The federal general corporate rate after the 10% provincial abatement and general rate reduction is 15%, plus provincial corporate income tax of broadly 8%-16% depending on province; a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) can use the 9% federal small-business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, subject to the 12-month business limit aggregation rules. No federal tax-exempt-zone classification applies to onshore manufacturing, and the scientific research and experimental development (SR&ED) investment tax credit remains the principal federal incentive for IVD equipment R&D.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections relevant to the IVD corporate structure include: (i) ETA s. 156 joint election between closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration, simplifying GST/HST input-tax-credit recovery within the corporate group; (ii) ETA s. 211 election to zero-rate a sale of capital personal property to a non-resident where title and export conditions are met; (iii) ITA s. 216 election enabling a non-resident to file a net-basis Part I return for certain Canadian-source rental or real-property income instead of the Part XIII gross withholding tax; and (iv) automatic application of the hybrid mismatch rules in ITA Part 6.1 (ss. 18.2 et seq.), with no opt-in or opt-out: deduction denial is mandated for payments in a deduct/deduct or deduct/exempt outcome involving a US or other foreign hybrid entity, and imported-mismatch rules extend the denial to Canadian taxpayers indirectly funding hybrid results. A Canadian corporation may also elect under ITA s. 93 in respect of foreign affiliate surplus, which affects the tax character of dividends repatriated from non-Canadian IVD distribution subsidiaries.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total consideration for taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated supplies) made during the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; once exceeded, the entity ceases to be a small supplier and must register for GST/HST under ETA s. 240. A non-resident supplier that solicits orders in Canada in the regular course of its business, or that sells goods in Canada through a consignee or Canadian intermediary, is required to register regardless of the CAD 30,000 threshold; a non-resident without a Canadian establishment may register voluntarily (ETA s. 240(2)) to claim input tax credits on Canadian purchases used in export manufacture.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of IVD goods, including automated immunoassay analyzers, microfluidic cartridges, reagents and consumables, are zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V Item 1 where the registrant transfers possession to a common carrier for export, or delivers the goods to a non-resident purchaser in Canada who in turn exports them, provided the goods are exported as soon as is reasonable after the supply and are not further processed or transformed in Canada. US-bound shipments qualify under the USMCA with standard proof-of-export documentation. Related services supplied to non-residents, including installation, calibration, field service and remote software maintenance of IVD equipment located abroad, are zero-rated where the place of supply is deemed outside Canada under the place-of-supply rules or the supply is made to a non-resident in the commercial service category. Input tax credits for Canadian manufacturing inputs, overhead, packaging, and freight supporting export sales are fully recoverable by the registrant, and the resulting net refund position is substantiated by the export record bundle below.

  • Customs Export Declaration: electronic declaration filed through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or, for legacy commercial shipments, CBSA Form B13A, pursuant to Customs Act s. 95 and associated Export Reporting Regulations; USMCA low-value and US-destined non-permit shipments may qualify for limited reporting exemptions while still requiring the exporter to retain full commercial documentation
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or highway e-manifest (PARS/ACI) evidencing the physical dispatch and international carriage of the automated point-of-care IVD analyzers and microfluidic cartridges from Canada to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (evidence of foreign payment): Canadian law does not issue a statutory instrument named 'Inward Remittance Certificate'; however, CRA audit practice requires equivalent banking proof of export consideration, namely a SWIFT MT103, export letter-of-credit settlement, bank foreign-exchange contract, or freight-collect payment record referencing the invoice, together with the FX conversion statement and the CRA-prescribed invoice cross-reference

No real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance digital stamping, or government-controlled e-invoicing platform exists in Canada. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and is currently aligned with the Pan-Canadian PINT/Peppol-based standard for federal procurement interoperability. GST/HST returns are required to be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE) by registrants with total annual revenues exceeding CAD 1.5 million; CRA can designate other registrants as specified registrants required to e-file. All registrants issuing taxable supplies above CAD 30 must provide a supporting tax invoice with the supplier's Business Number, invoice date, description of goods or services, consideration, and tax amount, pursuant to ETA s. 169(1) and CRA Information Circular IC 4029.

Where the exporter fails to retain seven years of export documentation, or the CRA determines that goods were diverted, consumed, used, or further processed in Canada prior to leaving the country, the zero-rating is revoked and the supply is reassessed as taxable at the applicable GST/HST rate over the normal four-year reassessment period (ETA s. 298), with statutory interest and late-filing penalties. A knowingly false export claim triggers the ETA s. 296 penalty equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the refund or credit improperly obtained; under the Customs Act administrative monetary penalty (AMPS) schedule, false export declarations and non-reporting offenses can draw penalties up to CAD 25,000 per violation. Criminal prosecution under ITA s. 239 may result in fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to five years. The 180-day repatriation figure is not a fixed statutory deadline in Canadian tax law; it is retained in this dossier as the contractual FX and trade-credit benchmark used by export credit insurers and applied by the CRA in monitoring ITA s. 39 foreign-exchange gains or losses and in transfer-pricing examinations of related-party export sales of point-of-care IVD systems.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Canadian dollar floats freely, and no administrative approval or central-bank authorization is required to convert or remit profits, dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, or capital proceeds. Repatriation frictions are limited to tax compliance: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends (generally 25%, reduced to 0–15% under an applicable tax treaty), on interest (25%, with treaty/statutory carve-outs to 0–15% for arm's-length and qualifying exempt recipients), and on royalties (25%, treaty-reducible); the Excessive Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) regime restricts interest deductibility for large corporations; and CBCA solvency tests govern lawful dividend declarations. Foreign-currency deposit accounts are freely permitted. The Bank of Canada may intervene in FX markets only to counter disorderly market conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no retention deadline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may lawfully hold, retain, and redeploy export proceeds indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts. Residual compliance obligations: (i) FINTRAC large-cross-border-transaction reporting by the reporting financial institution where the CAD equivalent of a transfer equals or exceeds CAD 10,000 within a 24-hour aggregation period; (ii) Statistics Canada Canadian International Merchandise Trade declarations for exported goods captured by merchandise-trade statistics; (iii) Canada Border Services Agency export reporting where the goods or destinations are regulated; and (iv) Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence record-keeping for IVD products.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — sole monetary authority and issuer of Canadian dollars; prudential supervision of banks and foreign bank branches is delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control agency comparable to SAFE or ACDI; the residual exchange-control regulatory regime was repealed with the abolition of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1952.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal limits. Canada does not recognise 'electronic money institution' or 'EMI' as a licensed bank category; foreign EMIs and payment fintechs are not authorised to take deposits or offer Canadian bank accounts under the Bank Act. They may register as money services businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC and operate as cross-border payment-corridor providers, but they cannot support capital escrow, documentary letters of credit, or statutory treasury functions. Recommendation: use one or two FINTRAC-registered MSB/EMI rails for low-value, ancillary supplier payables while placing all primary liquidity, escrow, and trade-finance clearing with a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 COV from the parent's correspondent bank to the Canadian beneficiary account. The remittance narrative must identify: (i) beneficiary legal name and CBCA corporate number; (ii) purpose reference of 'share subscription – CBCA' or 'intercompany loan – EIFEL aware'; (iii) the business rationale referencing the IVD manufacturing facility expansion; and (iv) the ultimate remitter name and account to permit FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting where the CAD equivalent of the transfer meets or exceeds CAD 10,000 within a 24-hour aggregation window. Canada applies no transaction-purpose-code regime analogous to SAFE codes; the remitting bank maps the payment to SWIFT tags 50K/52A and the receiving bank assigns an internal purpose category for balance-of-payments statistics. Pre-funding verification requires: certificate of good standing, executed board resolution, source-of-funds documentation, and PEP/sanctions screening of the ultimate beneficial owners. Equity contributions are non-taxable upon receipt; intercompany loans must comply with the EIFEL limitations, with interest paid to non-residents subject to Part XIII withholding where applicable.Outbound supplier, distributor, and logistics settlements are cleared via three rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 ordinary telegraphic transfers for open-account payables, the default for trade with North American and European counterparties; (ii) documentary commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700) with conforming draft documents for equipment and high-value supply contracts; and (iii) standby letters of credit (SWIFT MT760) issued as performance and bid security for distributors and tender contracts. CAD-denominated domestic disbursements settle through Payment Canada's Lynx high-value system; cross-border USD disbursements clear via US correspondent bank arrangements on Fedwire or CHIPS; EUR and other currency disbursements clear through SWIFT correspondent networks. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory T106 reporting and contemporaneous Local File documentation generally apply when the aggregate fair market value of covered non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a fiscal period; the CRA may request a Transfer Pricing Master File where consolidated group revenue is below CAD 750,000,000 at its discretion, while Country-by-Country Reporting and Master File are mandatory for MNEs with consolidated group revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more; all documentation must be contemporaneous and available to the CRA within three months of a written request

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to certain offshore financial centres; however, Canadian-resident corporations are subject to worldwide taxation and the CRA applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245 to disregard artificial transactions lacking genuine economic substance. DTA benefits are denied to a resident of a treaty country if obtaining those benefits was one of the principal purposes of the transaction, and transfer pricing rules under section 247 require that transactions reflect real business function, assets, and risk allocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code and Income Tax Act section 239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to two years; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated amount under section 163(2); late-filing penalty of 5% plus 1% per month, increasing to 10% plus 2% per month for repeated non-filing

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or assent to the false filing are personally exposed to criminal conviction, imprisonment, fines, and civil assessment; CRA may pursue the director for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII amounts, and a criminal prosecution permits court-imposed travel restrictions, asset freezes, and personal restitution
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Section 247(2) arm's-length adjustment for any non-arm's-length transaction; section 247(3) penalty of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where documentation and reasonable-effort defences are absent; GAAR recharacterization under section 245; additional per-day or fixed penalties for failure to file T106 or provide Master File/CbC documentation

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the tax of an artificial shell under shareholder-attribution and director-liability rules, particularly for unremitted Part XIII tax; flagrant facilitation of sham arrangements can lead to criminal tax evasion charges, personal fines, travel bans under legal process, and imprisonment
Part XIII Withholding Tax Non-Remittance

Statutory sanction: Section 227(8): penalty of 5% of the unremitted amount plus 1% per full month of failure, up to 12 months, and prescribed interest accruing daily; the amount remains collectible even if not actually deducted from the payment

Director personal liability: Section 227.1 imposes joint and several personal liability on directors who failed to exercise care, diligence, and skill to ensure withholding remittances; CRA can register a Federal Court judgment against a director personally and enforce through seizure, garnishment, and, where criminal non-compliance is present, travel restrictions or prosecution
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering and Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code section 462.31: money laundering offence punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years, with enhanced exposure to 14 years for offences committed in connection with a criminal organization; Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties and FINTRAC compliance orders; CRA will deny deductions and reassess the underlying sham transactions

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in laundering transactions are criminally liable as parties to the offence; personal assets derived from the unlawful conduct may be forfeited; upon criminal charge, courts may impose travel bans, reporting conditions, and imprisonment, and a conviction bars future directorship or managerial service in a Health Canada-regulated IVD establishment
