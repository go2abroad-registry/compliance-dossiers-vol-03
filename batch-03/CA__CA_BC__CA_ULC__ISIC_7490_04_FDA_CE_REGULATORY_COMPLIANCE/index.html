Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: for Canadian federal income tax, a BC ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax; for U.S. federal tax purposes, a wholly owned ULC may be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership through an IRS check-the-box election. CRA does not recognize the U.S. election and continues to treat the ULC as a corporation.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a check-the-box regime for federal income tax. Available elections include the GST/HST closely related corporation election under Excise Tax Act s. 156, Income Tax Act s. 85 corporate rollover elections, and, for a U.S. parent, an IRS Form 8832 entity classification election. Hybrid mismatch consequences are determined by statutory rules rather than elective status; the ULC structure should be documented to manage foreign tax credit and earnings-characterization issues.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 for GST/HST small-supplier threshold based on worldwide annual taxable supplies; BC PST has no general registration threshold, so registration is required upon the first taxable sale or taxable service in BC unless an exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating applies to the export of tangible goods when the registrant exports or delivers the goods to a carrier for export outside Canada and retains satisfactory evidence. For FDA/CE regulatory consulting services, zero-rating applies when the recipient is a non-resident not registered for GST/HST, the service is supplied in the course of the registrant's business, and the service is not performed in relation to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply; such consultancy to a non-resident medical device client will generally qualify as an exported service. BC PST generally does not apply to consulting services or exported goods, unless the service falls within a specifically enumerated PST-taxable service.

  • Canada Border Services Agency B13 Customs Export Declaration or approved alternative confirmation of export
  • Commercial invoice and consulting services agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and service description
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill showing physical export of goods, if applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • FDA 510(k) / CE filing status records and proof of delivery of regulatory deliverables to the foreign client
  • Carrier certificate or freight forwarder export certification where issued

No federal or BC real-time e-invoicing clearance mandate is currently in force. GST/HST registrants may issue electronic invoices provided the invoice contains all statutory information under the Excise Tax Act; CRA does not operate a digital stamping or continuous transaction clearance system. BC PST is administered through eTaxBC for return filing and payment, not for per-invoice fiscal authorization; records must be retained in an accessible, auditable format.

CRA may deny zero-rated treatment and claw back input tax credits or refunds where export evidence is incomplete, false, or obtained by misrepresentation. Normal reassessments are generally limited to four years, but the limitation period is unlimited for misrepresentation, fraud, or negligence. Penalties include interest, excise tax penalties for false statements, gross-negligence penalties, and for egregious cases criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment. BC PST audits may assess uncollected tax plus interest and penalties, including 10% penalties for VDP declines or 75% penalties for failure to collect PST with intent to evade.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and therefore maintains full convertibility across both current and capital account transactions. There are no general licensing requirements, no prior-approval gates for equity injections, and no administrative hurdles to remitting consulting fees, dividends, or repatriated capital from a British Columbia ULC. Distributions by a ULC to non-resident shareholders are nonetheless subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25%, reducible under applicable income tax treaties (e.g., the Canada-US Convention), and management/technical service fees paid offshore may be recharacterized if not at arm's length; such withholding exposure is a treasury-planning, not an FX-control, issue. The ULC's unlimited liability form does not impair convertibility, though it amplifies personal legal exposure for UBOs absent liability-mitigation holdco structures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender rules exist in Canada: there is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no ceiling on foreign-currency balances for export or service income. A BC ULC may retain USD and EUR receipts indefinitely in a Canadian bank foreign-currency account. The only obligatory controls are FINTRAC reporting and record-keeping under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act — e.g., reporting of all incoming/outgoing international wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within 24 hours, retaining underlying invoices and client identification for at least five years, and maintaining an MSB-style compliance program if the ULC itself provides money-transfer services (which the FDA/CE consulting model does not).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating under the Bank of Canada Act and the Financial Administration Act, is the sole monetary authority; however, Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration analogous to a SAFE-type agency. Prudential supervision of federally chartered banks rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/ATF obligations, and Payments Canada operates national clearing and settlement rails. Exchange-control policy is delegated to the market, with the Minister of Finance retaining emergency powers under the Currency Act and the Special Economic Measures Act for sanctions-based restrictions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary home for treasury liquidity. Canada's Bank Act does not recognize an 'Electronic Money Institution' licence; non-bank payment firms must either hold a FINTRAC Money Services Business registration (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) or rely on sponsoring-bank client-fund segregation. For a BC ULC whose payments are predominantly USD/EUR-regulated FDA and CE fees, a foreign EMI can serve as an auxiliary collection and vendor-payout rail, but it cannot substitute for a Schedule I bank in escrow functions, LC issuance, or Part XIII withholding administration, and settlement finality risk plus sanctions-screening shortfalls make it unsuitable as the sole clearing venue.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity injection for a BC ULC is executed as a SWIFT wire from the non-resident shareholder to the ULC's Schedule I bank account, with payment narrative 'Share subscription — [ULC legal name] — BC registry no. [BC#######]'. Canada assigns no mandatory purpose code; however, the bank's eDDA system will tag the credit as 'Corporate Equity Injection' and FINTRAC wire reporting applies at the CAD 10,000 threshold. The documentary defense pack consists of a shareholders' resolution authorizing share issuance, a subscription agreement, the updated share register and register of directors, a closing statement allocating proceeds to stated capital versus contributed surplus (important for any future ULC wind-up deeming rules), plus confirmation that no financing condition or escrow-release protocol is required because the BC Business Corporations Act sets no minimum capital. Injected capital should be isolated from operating revenue in a 'Capital Retention' sub-account to simplify Part XIII equitable analysis on deemed dividends under subsection 84(2).Settlement rails are selected by payment type: (i) SWIFT MT103 via RBC/BMO/TD/Scotiabank for USD/EUR consulting invoices, notified-body milestones, and vendor wires, using structured 72-character remittance fields (contract ID, milestone ID, ISIC 7490 descriptor); (ii) commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 — advised or confirmed by a Canadian Schedule I bank — for physical prototype or clinical-sample shipments to contract research organizations; (iii) Payments Canada Lynx (large-value, real-time) and ACSS (retail batch) for domestic CAD disbursements to Canadian subcontractors; and (iv) a USD sweep line (BMO, CIBC, RBC) consolidating US-dollar FDA receivables into a US clearing account, net-settled into CAD via spot FX at deal rates. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate rate: federal 15% general corporate rate plus British Columbia provincial 12% general corporate rate. A foreign-controlled BC ULC is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation, therefore the small business deduction and the 9% federal SBD rate are unavailable.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% nondeductible Part XIII withholding tax on all dividends paid by the Canadian-resident ULC to non-resident shareholders. Under the Canada-US Tax Convention the rate is reduced to 15% for portfolio dividends and to 5% for dividends beneficially owned by a US corporation that owns at least 10% of the voting stock of the ULC; certain US pension trusts qualify for 0%.
