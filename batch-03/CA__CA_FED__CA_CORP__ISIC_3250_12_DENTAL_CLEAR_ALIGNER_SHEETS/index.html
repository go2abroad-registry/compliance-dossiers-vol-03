Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — sole federal tax administrator for corporate income tax, GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts; official portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html, My Business Account (MBA) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html, and the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal for customs/export declarations.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Incorporate a Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) federal corporation (or provincial continuation) with Corporations Canada; STEP 2: Apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) together with program accounts — RT (corporate income tax), RT0001 (GST/HST), and RM (import/export) — through Business Registration Online (BRO), which issues the BN instantly for most applicants; STEP 3: Where BRO is unavailable, submit Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' via mail or fax with certified corporate documents (Articles of Incorporation, registered office address, directors, and business address); STEP 4: For foreign-owned Canadian corporations, appoint at least one resident director (CBCA requires a majority of resident Canadians for board quorum; a single non-resident owner may designate a resident director), and register for GST/HST even where the small-supplier threshold is not yet exceeded if the corporation will export goods or import inputs (voluntary registration enables ITC recovery); STEP 5: Activate CRA My Business Account, enrol an authorized representative using RepAcess/Form RC59, and select the GST/HST filing frequency (monthly, quarterly, or annually) based on projected annual taxable supplies; STEP 6: Register with CBSA for a carrier/customs client account (CARM) and obtain a separate 9-digit import/export account number to transmit electronic export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A CBCA business corporation is a separate taxpayer resident in Canada, subject to federal Part I corporate income tax on worldwide income under a residence-based territorial model; no check-the-box or pass-through election is available for Canadian corporations; the general federal corporate rate is 15% (after the 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction), reduced to 9% for the first CAD 500,000 of active business income if the corporation qualifies as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC); branch-level provincial/territorial income taxes also apply to a permanent establishment in each province; Canada maintains no federal tax-exempt free-zone for manufacturing corporations (the 'Territorial_Scope' and 'Tax_Exempt_Zone' classifications do not apply to a federal CBCA corporation).

Available election forms

Available elections and designations affecting the Canadian entity include: (i) section 85 corporate rollover election to defer capital gains on transfers of property to the corporation in exchange for shares; (ii) section 89(4) Capital Dividend Account (CDA) election to pay tax-free dividends to shareholders; (iii) section 256(2.1) associated-corporation election or designation to allocate the small business limit among associated CCPCs; (iv) sections 18(2), 18(9) and 21(1) elections to capitalize interest, foreign exchange losses and soft costs; (v) GST/HST elections under Excise Tax Act: section 156 joint election for closely related corporations to treat cross-charges as outside the scope of GST/HST, section 167 election on taxable sale of a business, section 150 election to file annually, section 211 Quick Method election for small suppliers, and section 177 election for nominated persons; (vi) hybrid mismatch and BEPS Action 2 statutory statements/elections under Income Tax Act sections 18.2(6) and 18.4(5) to identify the payee's residence or permanent establishment for purposes of denying or allowing interest deductions; (vii) foreign affiliate surplus designation under Regulation 5907(2.1) for repatriation planning; and (viii) Part XIV branch tax treaty waiver elections for non-resident corporations operating as a Canadian branch (applicable where the composite entity is a US LLC treated as a corporation under the Canada-US Treaty).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — mandatory GST/HST registration is triggered once taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act s. 240(1)); the small-supplier threshold applies to non-residents only where they are not carrying on business in Canada; a manufacturer of dental clear aligner sheets that exports goods or imports raw materials may voluntarily register below the threshold to claim input tax credits (ITCs); registration is mandatory for all non-residents carrying on business in Canada, and electronic GST/HST filing (GST/HST NETFILE) is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible dental clear aligner sheets (multilayer PETG thermoforming sheets, HS 3920.90; NAICS 339114) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, sections 1 and 5 of the Excise Tax Act where: (i) the goods are supplied by way of sale and are exported by the supplier or by the recipient in accordance with s. 1(a) and s. 5(a); (ii) the goods are delivered to a common carrier, freight forwarder, or the recipient's own carrier in Canada for immediate shipment outside Canada; (iii) possession and risk of loss pass to the purchaser in Canada (FOB/EXW Incoterms 2020); (iv) the goods are actually exported as soon after the supply as is reasonable in the circumstances; and (v) the supplier retains contemporaneous documentary proof of export; processed services directly performed on the goods (e.g., thermoforming, automated trimming, finishing of aligner sheets) are also zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 10 where the goods are exported concurrently; supplies of services rendered exclusively to non-residents who are not in Canada during the performance of the service may additionally qualify under Part V s. 7; zero-rating operates alongside the ordinary ITC refund mechanism, allowing the exporter to recover the 5% GST embedded in domestic inputs through the normal GST/HST return.

Canada does not impose any real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, pre-approval API validation, or mandatory e-invoicing clearance for tax purposes; CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for large registrants and electronic record-keeping with a reliable audit trail, but business-to-business e-invoices are not pre-cleared by CRA; the Pan-Canadian PEPPOL e-invoicing framework is increasingly adopted for public-sector procurement, but it is not a tax clearance mechanism; the GST/HST invoice must show the supplier's BN, the recipient's name, the date, a description of the dental aligner sheets, the consideration, and applicable taxes, with no government-issued validation number required; supporting customs and shipping documentation must be retained to substantiate the zero-rated export treatment.

CRA audits of export zero-rating require contemporaneous proof that the goods left Canada and that the sale was a true commercial export; where documentation is missing or inconsistent with CBSA export declarations, CRA will reassess the supply at 5% GST (or 13%/15% HST as applicable) on the full consideration, deny ITCs, and impose daily compound interest at the prescribed rate plus a 4% penalty increment for large corporations; the normal reassessment window is 4 years for GST/HST and income tax, extended to 7 years where misrepresentation, neglect, or wilful default exists, with no limitation period for fraud; civil penalties include ETA s. 285 at 25% of the amount sought to be evaded (50% for a second contravention within 5 years), ETA s. 285.1 and ITA s. 163(2) gross negligence penalties at 50% of understated tax, and ETA s. 280 interest; criminal prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 can result in fines of up to 200% of the evaded amount and imprisonment of up to 5 years; CRA cross-verifies CARM customs data, bank inward remittances, and platform payout records (Stripe/credit card) and will claw back zero-rating and ITCs retrospectively if export proceeds are not repatriated within a reasonable commercial period — the 180-day internal benchmark is adopted as the statutory EPC compliance ceiling, and diversion of goods to the domestic Canadian market is treated as a fraudulent export claim subject to full clawback, penalty, interest, and prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts with no residual exchange-control approval gates for inbound equity, intercompany debt, dividends, royalties, or trade settlement. Profit remittance is taxed under Part XIII domestic withholding (generally 25%, treaty-reducible to 0–15% depending on instrument and treaty counterparty) and is subject to CRS/FATCA classification duties, but no central bank or exchange administration authorization is required. Non-resident-controlled corporations may freely hold CAD and foreign currency operating balances onshore without statutory position limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender, compelled conversion, or repatriation ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain receivables in USD, CNY, EUR, or any invoiced currency indefinitely. Mandatory compliance is limited to (i) export declaration obligations under the Customs Act, (ii) FINTRAC reporting of any incoming or outgoing cross-border EFT of CAD $10,000 or more, and (iii) record-keeping requirements under the Income Tax Act for related-party export transactions. No end-of-day conversion or minimum CAD holding ratio applies to the operating treasury.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central monetary authority, with prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and AML/CTF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada does not operate an administrative exchange-control approval body; FX policy is executed through the Bank of Canada's floating-rate framework and FINTRAC cross-border reporting thresholds.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Canada does not maintain a statutory Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing tier; non-bank payment firms either register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC or operate as unregulated payment service providers when not carrying on FX dealing or money remittance. Digital platforms such as Wise, Nuvei, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, and Vialet are feasible as supplementary collection and payout rails for e-commerce aligner sales and low-value supplier disbursements, but are rated Low for primary capital escrow, statutory paid-up capital verification, and treasury concentration. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Canada does not maintain a statutory Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing tier; non-bank payment firms either register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC or operate as unregulated payment service providers when not carrying on FX dealing or money remittance. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate rate, reduced to 13% for qualifying manufacturing and processing profits, and 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income for a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) claiming the small business deduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid or credited to non-residents, subject to treaty reduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A T106 Information Return must be filed for any non-arm's-length transaction with a non-resident where the aggregate fair market value of the transaction or series exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. Canada has not introduced domestic Master File legislation, but multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750 million are subject to Country-by-Country Reporting (Form RC4649) and the CRA may request contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for all controlled transactions regardless of size.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not administer separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in offshore centers; however, economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245), transfer-pricing substance requirements (s. 247), and OECD BEPS Action 6 treaty-shopping limitations. A Canadian distribution or aligner-sheet production entity that merely holds contracts but lacks employees, premises, risk management, and decision-making functions will likely be recharacterized and reassessed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Criminal Fraud

Statutory sanction: On indictment, a fine of between 50% and 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years under s. 239 of the Income Tax Act; civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under s. 163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, authorize, or participate in the evasion can be jointly charged; conviction can result in imprisonment, court-ordered travel bans, passport seizure, and restitution orders against personal assets.
Gross Negligence and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Penalty under s. 163(2) equal to 50% of the tax understated; late-filing penalties and daily compounding interest; public disclosure under the CRA's Voluntary Disclosures Program is unavailable after the filing of a false return.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly make or participate in false statements may be assessed as principals under s. 163(2) and can be personally pursued for the civil penalty.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and GAAR

Statutory sanction: CRA applies s. 245 GAAR to deny the tax benefit, adjusts transfer prices under s. 247, and imposes a transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment in excess of the lesser of CAD 100,000 and 10% of gross revenue; interest runs from the reassessment date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false T106 or arm's-length documentation may be liable for the corporate penalty if they were grossly negligent; in egregious cases, the CRA can pursue directors for conspiracy to defraud the Crown.
Failure to Remit Withholding Taxes

Statutory sanction: Section 227.1 makes directors jointly and severally liable for the unremitted amounts; CRA assessments include prescribed interest compounded daily and administrative penalties; a 10% penalty applies on amounts not remitted by the prescribed deadline.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the full unremitted balance, plus interest and penalties, for any period in which they were directors; the CRA may issue a director's assessment within two years of the director ceasing to hold office; personal bank accounts, real estate, and travel are subject to civil collection.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations (higher for repeat violations); criminal prosecution for officers who knowingly facilitate laundering proceeds under Criminal Code s. 462.31, punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who direct or allow AML breaches face criminal culpability, imprisonment, and travel restrictions as a bail condition; they may also be personally liable for FINTRAC administrative penalties if they authorized or acquiesced in the contravention.
