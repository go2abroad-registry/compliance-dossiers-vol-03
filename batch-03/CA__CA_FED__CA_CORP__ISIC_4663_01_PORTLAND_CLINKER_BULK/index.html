Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); primary online services include Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, and the non-resident tax services channels. Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), including the CARM Client Portal, administers commercial import and export customs processes.

E-government filing portal

A corporation carrying on business in Canada, or required to file a Canadian tax return or administer Canadian tax accounts, must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN), generally a nine-digit identifier. The entity should incorporate federally or provincially, obtain its corporate legal documents, identify directors and authorized representatives, establish a Canadian business address or authorized non-resident contact arrangement, and register for the applicable CRA program accounts. A resident corporation generally obtains the BN through CRA Business Registration Online or the applicable incorporation and registration process. A non-resident corporation may request a BN and applicable program accounts through CRA non-resident registration procedures, including Form RC1 where required, or by contacting the CRA Business Enquiries and Non-Resident Tax Services channels. The GST/HST program account uses the BN followed by the RT program identifier. The corporation must register for GST/HST when required, may register voluntarily when eligible, and must separately register for payroll, corporate income tax, excise, customs, or other accounts where applicable. Importers and exporters must establish the required CBSA commercial account and CARM access. Provincial corporate, sales-tax, and employer registrations are separate from the federal BN and may be required based on the province of operations, inventory, employees, or taxable supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no general United States-style check-the-box election for corporations. A corporation is generally taxed as a separate legal person under the Income Tax Act. Specific statutory elections may apply to particular transactions or structures, including amalgamations, wind-ups, section 85 rollovers, loss-utilization transactions, partnerships, and certain foreign-affiliate or non-resident transactions. Canadian-resident corporations are generally taxed on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are generally taxed on income from carrying on business in Canada, taxable capital gains from taxable Canadian property, and other amounts subject to Canadian withholding rules. Hybrid-entity and mismatch outcomes are addressed through specific statutory rules, including the hybrid mismatch rules, anti-avoidance provisions, transfer-pricing rules, thin-capitalization rules, foreign-affiliate rules, and treaty limitation provisions. There is no general election that converts a Canadian corporation into a pass-through entity for Canadian income-tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies, excluding zero-rated supplies only where the applicable small-supplier test permits their exclusion, measured under the GST/HST small-supplier rules. The threshold generally applies over a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, subject to detailed statutory calculations. A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada may be required to register even without a conventional Canadian establishment, depending on the place-of-supply rules, carrying-on-business analysis, and applicable non-resident registration rules. Voluntary registration may be available before the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods that are exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated for GST/HST if the statutory export conditions are satisfied, including delivery or making the goods available outside Canada in accordance with the applicable rules and retention of evidence establishing export. For bulk Portland clinker, the supplier should establish the purchaser, contractual delivery terms, point of transfer, physical movement from Canada, export reporting where required, carrier details, destination, and customs evidence. Exported services may be zero-rated only where the specific place-of-supply and statutory zero-rating conditions are met; a non-resident customer alone is not sufficient. Input tax credits or GST/HST rebates may be claimed for eligible tax paid or payable on inputs used in zero-rated activities, subject to registration, documentary support, allocation, filing, and limitation rules. Imported goods subsequently exported may also involve customs duty relief or drawback programs administered by CBSA, which are separate from GST/HST zero-rating.

Canada has no general federal real-time e-invoice clearance or mandatory digital tax-stamping system for ordinary GST/HST invoices. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices or other documentary evidence containing prescribed information sufficient to support tax collection, input tax credits, and audit verification. Electronic invoices and electronic records are generally permitted if they preserve integrity, accessibility, and readability. CRA electronic filing and payment obligations apply to specified registrants and return types, but they do not constitute transaction-level invoice clearance. Commercial import and export declarations are administered through CBSA systems, including CARM and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), where applicable. Provincial e-invoicing or sector-specific requirements should be reviewed separately.

Canada generally imposes no mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a condition of GST/HST zero-rating. CRA may deny or reassess zero-rating where export conditions, documentary evidence, recipient status, place-of-supply requirements, or transaction records are insufficient. Denied zero-rating can result in assessment of the applicable GST/HST, arrears interest, penalties for failure to collect or remit, gross-negligence penalties where warranted, and reassessment of related input tax credits or rebates. False statements, omissions, fabricated export records, or knowingly participating in fraudulent claims may attract civil penalties, gross-negligence penalties, third-party or promoter penalties, and criminal prosecution, including fines or imprisonment under applicable federal tax legislation. Customs misdeclarations may result in seizure, administrative monetary penalties, duty and tax reassessments, accounting corrections, and other CBSA enforcement measures. Records must generally be retained for the statutory retention period, commonly six years from the end of the relevant year, subject to extensions and specific record-keeping rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible and floats freely; Canada maintains no exchange-control legislation restricting current-account or capital-account transactions, and no central-bank approval is required for any remittance or repatriation. Profit distribution to non-resident shareholders is subject to Part XIII withholding tax at 25%, reduced under Canada's tax treaties (typically 5% on intercorporate dividends and 15% on portfolio dividends), and a non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada through a branch is additionally subject to the Part I.2 branch tax of 25% on after-tax branch profits not reinvested in Canadian property. Enterprise-level foreign investment is governed by the Investment Canada Act: acquisitions of control of a Canadian business by a WTO investor require a net-benefit review only above the annually indexed enterprise-value threshold (exceeding CAD 1.3 billion), while the establishment of a new Canadian business by a non-Canadian investor requires a simple post-closing notification to ISED. Wholesale cement and clinker trading activities below those thresholds are freely open to non-resident ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement on export receipts. Exporters may retain, convert, or reinvest foreign-currency proceeds freely without Bank of Canada approval. The only applicable compliance obligations are: (i) FINTRAC reporting by the Canadian financial institution of every international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more and every currency transaction involving cash over CAD 10,000; (ii) full taxation of foreign-source income on a worldwide basis for Canadian-resident corporations; and (iii) Form T1135 (Foreign Income Verification Statement) disclosure where the cost amount of specified foreign property held by the Canadian exporter exceeds CAD 100,000. USD-denominated clinker sale proceeds may be held in USD clearing accounts at Schedule I banks or converted on the interbank market without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) as monetary authority, with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as AML/CFT supervisor, and the Investment Review Division of Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) administering foreign-investment screening under the Investment Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as primary treasury rail; Medium for ancillary disbursement execution only. Foreign digital EMIs and payment-service providers (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) may lawfully serve a Canadian wholesale corporation only by partnering with OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institutions or by registering as money service businesses with FINTRAC. They cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, cannot provide access to Lynx (Canada's high-value RTGS system), cannot hold CAD deposits insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation, and are not recognized as banks under the Bank Act. For clinker and cement trade, such EMIs are unsuitable for escrow or capital-contribution accounts and should be limited to small-value supplier disbursements or expatriate payroll payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan capital is wired by SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian operating account. The remitter must complete the remittance-information field with a bona fide purpose narrative (e.g., 'Shareholder loan drawdown / paid-in capital for wholesale cement distribution, ISIC 4663'), and the Canadian receiving bank files an International Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC for every transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. No exchange-control approval or import certificate is required; the bank may, however, require the board resolution authorizing the capital injection, the shareholder register, and the certified articles. Where a non-Canadian parent establishes a new Canadian business, an ISED notification must be filed within 30 days of the transaction unless the activity falls within designated reviewable sectors; Statistics Canada may also collect direct-investment information under its international investment reporting framework.Trade disbursements for clinker and cement purchases are executed through four mutually complementary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 and MT202 COV wires for open-account settlements, with invoice-matching narrative codes and FINTRAC reporting on all transfers above CAD 10,000; (ii) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit governed by UCP 600, issued by the Canadian Schedule I bank and transmitted by SWIFT MT700 series, with MT740 reimbursement authorizations and discrepant-document handling at the presenting bank; (iii) domestic high-value CAD payments via Lynx, Canada's real-time gross settlement system operated by Payments Canada, providing finality without intermediary risk; and (iv) USD-denominated payments cleared through the CLEAR Payment and Settlement System, Canada's multilateral US-dollar clearing house, thereby avoiding US ACH delays and preserving delivery-versus-payment finality in USD. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low as primary treasury rail; Medium for ancillary disbursement execution only. Foreign digital EMIs and payment-service providers (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) may lawfully serve a Canadian wholesale corporation only by partnering with OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institutions or by registering as money service businesses with FINTRAC. They cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, cannot provide access to Lynx (Canada's high-value RTGS system), cannot hold CAD deposits insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation, and are not recognized as banks under the Bank Act. For clinker and cement trade, such EMIs are unsuitable for escrow or capital-contribution accounts and should be limited to small-value supplier disbursements or expatriate payroll payments.
