Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal administrator of the Income Tax Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. 1) and Part IX of the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST). Official portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); registration also facilitated through Business Registration Online (BRO).

E-government filing portal

A federal Business Corporation (or a non-resident entity with a Canadian commercial nexus) obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN). A federally incorporated company is automatically assigned a BN and an 'RC' corporate income tax program account upon articles of incorporation. A non-resident or person without automatic assignment files Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) via BRO or by mail. The GST/HST program account 'RT' is added in the same registration transaction, or subsequently by Form RC1/BRO amendment. Non-residents seeking to claim input tax credits must register voluntarily under ETA s. 240(2.1) and identify a resident representative or maintain books and records in Canada (ETA s. 286). The BN must be quoted on all returns, customs export declarations, invoices and zero-rating substantiation files. No minimum capital or local shareholding is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box regime; classification is statutory. Material elections for this MedTech manufacturer: (i) ITA s. 85 rollover election permitting transfer of eligible property to a taxable Canadian corporation at agreed elected amounts; (ii) ITA s. 89(4)/s. 89(5) deemed-dividend and paid-up-capital elections; (iii) ITA s. 216 election for a non-resident corporation receiving Canadian rental income to compute net taxable income and obtain Part XIII withholding refunds; (iv) ITA s. 125(6) associated-corporations agreement for allocation of the Small Business Deduction; (v) ETA s. 241.1 joint election where a non-resident and a Canadian corporate group opt to have a branch treat supplies as taxable group transactions. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA s. 18.4–18.7 and s. 12(2.2)–(2.36)) operate mandatorily with no election, disallowing deductions in relation to payments subject to hybrid transfer mismatch; the BEAT-like Excess Interest and Financing Expenses Limitation (EIFEL) under ITA s. 18.2 applies to large corporations for taxation years commencing on or after 1 October 2024 (30% fixed-ratio EBITDA cap on net interest expense).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1) (taxable supplies made worldwide in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters). A non-resident making taxable supplies in Canada in the course of a commercial activity that exceeds the threshold must register; below the threshold registration is voluntary, and an entity engaged solely in zero-rated export supplies may register in order to recover input tax credits on Canadian inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies to tangible personal property (including Class III coronary drug-eluting stents) supplied by the Canadian manufacturer where (i) the supplier ships or delivers the property to a non-resident purchaser who takes delivery outside Canada, or (ii) delivers the property to a common carrier for export. For the offshore laser micro-machining service component performed on goods to be exported, the supply is zero-rated under paragraph 4(b), Part V, Schedule VI provided the goods are actually exported within 60 days of the service being rendered (ETA s. 1.4; ministerial extension available up to 12 months). Domestic zero-rated supply of prescription medical devices (Part II, Schedule VI) is separately available in Canada, but export zero-rating does not depend on domestic classification. Documentary evidence must satisfy the prescribed-export records rules under ETA ss. 101.1, 108 and 273(2).

  • Customs Export Declaration — CBSA Canada Export Report (Form B13A or ACI/electronic export manifest report) evidencing the physical exit of the coronary stent inventory, with HS/ECC classification of the medical device;
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing the non-resident consignee and foreign destination port with vessel/flight number and date of export;
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank MT-103 SWIFT wire credit advice or foreign-currency receivables reconciliation demonstrating remittance by the non-resident purchaser for the zero-rated supply.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory third-party e-invoice approval exists at the federal level. The CRA joined the PEPPOL network and, as of April 2024, makes voluntary PEPPOL B2B e-invoicing available through certified service providers. GST/HST registrants must file returns electronically (GST/HST E-file / NETFILE). Books and records must be kept in Canada or be made available electronically for six years (ETA s. 286; ITA s. 230); no API-based pre-clearance gateway applies to invoice issuance at the federal level. Quebec imposes separate QST obligations not part of this federal nexus.

The CRA may reassess GST/HST within 4 years of the date the return was required to be filed; the limitation does not apply where misrepresentation or fraud is established (ETA s. 298(1)–(2)). Zero-rating claims are high-risk audit flags for manufactured medical-device exporters with incomplete export trails: upon disallowance, the full 5% tax is reassessed on the supply with daily compound interest, net tax and input tax credits are clawed back, and penalties attach: 5% + 1% per month for failure to file (ETA s. 280(2)); 25% of the understated tax for false statements or omissions (ETA s. 285); prosecution for evasion (ETA s. 327) with fines up to 50%–200% of tax evaded depending on conviction mode and imprisonment up to 5 years. Falsified bills of lading or dummy consignees trigger referral for criminal prosecution and unlimited reassessment periods. Canada does not impose an explicit statutory FX-repatriation deadline for GST/HST zero-rating; the 180-day timeline in this dossier is the transactional/AML evidentiary standard applied to export receivable substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully liberalised exchange-control regime; the Foreign Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1951 and Canada is an IMF Article VIII member, so both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible with no central-bank approval or conversion licensing. Profit remittance faces no FX hurdle; the only obstacle is Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (25% statutory on dividends, interest, and royalties, treaty-reduced to 5% on qualifying direct-investment dividends to a 10%+ corporate shareholder under the Canada-US Treaty). Paid-up-capital redemption and repatriation of equity injection are free of withholding under the CBCA 'paid-up capital' rules. The CAD floats freely; Bank of Canada intervention is exceptional and sterilised.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canadian law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or statutory deadline for export receipts; proceeds may be retained indefinitely in CAD or any foreign currency. The only binding obligations are (i) filing CBSA B13 export declarations through the Canadian Export Reporting System for commercial shipments above CAD 2,000, (ii) compliance with EIPA Export Control List and Area Control List screening, and (iii) statistical balance-of-payments reporting to Statistics Canada. FINTRAC electronic-fund-transfer reporting is a monitoring duty, not a currency-surrender mechanism.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential bank regulation delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), AML/ATF transaction surveillance under the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and export/import permits and trade sanctions administered by Global Affairs Canada pursuant to the Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA) and the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for core treasury functions. The Bank Act reserves deposit-taking to Schedule I/II banks; non-bank fintech EMIs may register as Payment Service Providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) or as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC, but cannot accept CDIC-insured deposits, hold settlement accounts at the Bank of Canada, or issue trade letters of credit and documentary collections. Offshore digital multi-currency EMIs are viable only as minor expense-disbursement and payroll-adjunct wallets; all capital injections, trade settlements, and LC operations for a regulated Class III medical-device manufacturer must traverse Schedule I bank clearing infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate under ITA s.123 (after the 10% provincial abatement); reduced to 13% for qualifying Manufacturing and Processing profits under ITA s.125.1; combined federal-provincial effective rate of 23.0% to 31.0% depending on the province of permanent establishment; large corporations are additionally subject to the refundable part I tax on investment income of 10.67% and a federal large corporation tax of 0.225% on taxable capital employed in Canada exceeding CAD 10,000,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax under ITA s.212(2) on dividends paid or credited to non-residents from taxable Canadian corporations in the coronary stent business; reduced to 15% portfolio rate and 5% qualifying 10%-or-more voting shareholder rate under the Canada-US treaty and similar DTAs; in addition, a 25% branch tax under ITA s.219 applies to after-tax income remitted by a Canadian permanent establishment to a foreign head office, reduced to 5% under the Canada-US treaty; interest payments recharacterized as deemed dividends by the thin capitalization rules attract the dividend withholding rate

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada's contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation duty under ITA s.247(4) applies whenever a Canadian corporation transacts with non-arm's length non-residents and the aggregate fair market value of the controlled transactions exceeds CAD 5,000,000 for goods or services, or CAD 1,000,000 for interest, rent, royalties and transactions involving intangible property in the fiscal year; a Master File and Local File are mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000; the Country-by-Country Report (Form RC5126) must be filed with the CRA within 12 months of the group's fiscal year-end where the Canadian entity is the ultimate parent or the surrogate parent of a group meeting the CAD 750,000,000 consolidated threshold; documentation must be prepared and available within 30 days of the CRA's written request

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to the UAE or Bermuda, but the doctrine is enforced through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under ITA s.245 which recharacterizes transactions lacking an authentic commercial purpose and genuine economic substance; (ii) the beneficial ownership requirement for treaty relief confirmed in Prévost Car Inc. v. The Queen (2009 FCA 57) - a holding company must have real substance including bank accounts, local directors' meetings, accounting records and decision-making in the residence country; (iii) the transfer pricing penalty under ITA s.247(3) of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment (with a CAD 5,000 gross minimum) when the taxpayer fails to make reasonable efforts to establish arm's length terms; and (iv) the thin capitalization rules under ITA s.18(4)-(6) which disallow interest on shareholder debt exceeding a 1.5:1 debt-to-equity ratio, treating the excess as a deemed dividend subject to Part XIII withholding; a Canadian stent subsidiary with no employees, no production floor and no independent decision-making capacity is recharacterized as a sham agent and the entire profit allocation is reassessed

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Wilful Misrepresentation of Income

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded under ITA s.239(1) plus interest at the prescribed daily compound rate; false statements and omissions under s.163(2) carry a 50% gross negligence penalty; on summary conviction the corporation is liable to a fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and up to 5 years imprisonment for the controlling officers on indictment; Criminal Code s.380 tax fraud carries a maximum sentence of 14 years for amounts exceeding CAD 5,000,000; the CRA may freeze the corporation's bank accounts and assets for the full amount assessed

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or acquiesced in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid source deductions, GST/HST, and Part XIII withholding under ITA s.227.1(1) and ETA s.323; the corporate veil is pierced where the corporation is a mere agent of the director (Smith v. The Queen); a director faces civil restitution of the full evaded amount, a court-issued travel ban under the Criminal Code, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment of up to 5 years on indictment; non-resident directors are subject to extradition for Canadian tax fraud
