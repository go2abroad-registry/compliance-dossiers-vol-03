Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); official national portal is canada.ca/en/revenue-agency, with operational access through CRA 'My Business Account' (Mon dossier d'entreprise); GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act and federal corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act are administered by CRA, while the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers import/export declaration and customs controls through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) Business Corporation: (1) incorporates federally via Corporations Canada Online Filing Centre by filing Articles of Incorporation; (2) automatically receives the 9-digit Business Number (BN), which Corporations Canada issues in coordination with CRA, or alternatively files Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; (3) opens CRA program accounts on My Business Account, namely RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll), selecting the RT account for taxable supplies; (4) for a non-incorporated branch of a foreign entity, files Form RC1 with proof of foreign incorporation, Canadian registered address and authorized representative information; the statutory tax identifier for federal corporate income tax is the BN followed by the RC program code, and the GST/HST registration number is the BN followed by the RT program code; CRA issues the BN immediately for online incorporation and generally within 4-6 weeks for paper applications.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a CBCA Business Corporation is a separate taxperson subject to Part I of the Income Tax Act; a corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident under ITA s. 250(3) and taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credit relief; no pass-through of Canadian losses or credits to shareholders is permitted; an unincorporated foreign branch operating in Canada is territorial in scope (taxable only on Canadian-source business income plus Part IA branch tax); tax-exempt status is confined to narrow categories such as certain Crown corporations and registered charities and is not available to a commercial implant manufacturer. — A foreign parent establishing a Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) Business Corporation: (1) incorporates federally via Corporations Canada Online Filing Centre by filing Articles of Incorporation; (2) automatically receives the 9-digit Business Number (BN), which Corporations Canada issues in coordination with CRA, or alternatively files Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number'; (3) opens CRA program accounts on My Business Account, namely RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), and RP (payroll), selecting the RT account for taxable supplies; (4) for a non-incorporated branch of a foreign entity, files Form RC1 with proof of foreign incorporation, Canadian registered address and authorized representative information; the statutory tax identifier for federal corporate income tax is the BN followed by the RC program code, and the GST/HST registration number is the BN followed by the RT program code; CRA issues the BN immediately for online incorporation and generally within 4-6 weeks for paper applications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a CBCA Business Corporation is a separate taxperson subject to Part I of the Income Tax Act; a corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident under ITA s. 250(3) and taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credit relief; no pass-through of Canadian losses or credits to shareholders is permitted; an unincorporated foreign branch operating in Canada is territorial in scope (taxable only on Canadian-source business income plus Part IA branch tax); tax-exempt status is confined to narrow categories such as certain Crown corporations and registered charities and is not available to a commercial implant manufacturer.

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a domestic 'check-the-box' regime and does not allow reclassification of a CBCA corporation as a flow-through or transparent entity; classification follows corporate legal form. Available statutory elections and mechanisms include: (i) ITA s. 85 rollover election on taxable transfers of eligible property to a Canadian corporation at elected amounts; (ii) ITA s. 216 election permitting a non-resident to compute net-basis tax on Canadian rental income; (iii) Part IA branch tax computation under ITA s. 219 for non-resident corporations; (iv) Excise Tax Act s. 273 election among closely related corporations for a single GST/HST registrant; (v) ETA s. 150(2) joint election to zero-rate certain supplies between registrants; (vi) ETA s. 179 drop-shipment zero-rating conditions for supplies to non-resident recipients; and (vii) for cross-border hybrid structures (e.g., a US 'check-the-box' LLC), the CRA applies Canadian domestic and treaty characterization together with the hybrid mismatch ordering rules in ITA ss. 18.2 and 18.21 (effective for transactions after June 30, 2023), which operate without taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 50,000 (small supplier threshold effective January 1, 2025, increased from CAD 30,000 by Budget 2024 amendments to the Excise Tax Act s. 123(1) definition of 'small supplier'); the threshold is applied to total worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters, excluding capital property sales and financial services; once exceeded, registration is mandatory under ETA s. 240; a person making solely zero-rated exports may still elect voluntary registration to recover input tax credits; non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada compute the threshold on the same worldwide basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST/HST) is available as follows. (A) Export of goods - Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1 of the ETA: a supply of tangible personal property (e.g., titanium porous hip joint prosthetics) made by a registrant is zero-rated where the goods are delivered or made available outside Canada and are not further processed, manufactured or incorporated in Canada before export, supported by credible shipping documentation; drop-shipment supplies to a non-resident recipient for subsequent export fall under Schedule VI, Part V, s. 2 and ETA s. 179, requiring confirmation of the recipient's non-resident status and export purpose. (B) Export of services - Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7: manufacturing, engineering, quality-assurance, assembly or technical services supplied to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated where the recipient is not a GST/HST registrant and the service is not in respect of Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada at the time of performance. (C) Domestic medical device zero-rating - Schedule VI, Part II, s. 20 of the ETA: a supply of a qualifying medical device, including an orthopedic implant holding a valid Health Canada Medical Device Licence under the Medical Devices Regulations (Class III for hip joint prosthetics), is zero-rated even without export; the manufacturer must also hold a Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL). Export zero-rating is claimed on the GST/HST return through the net tax calculation, producing refunds where input tax credits exceed output tax.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) filed through the CARM portal for shipments exceeding CAD 2,000 or for controlled or prohibited goods, citing the correct HS classification for artificial joints (HS heading 9021) for titanium orthopedic implants
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill evidencing the exporter, foreign consignee, port or airport of exit, and date of physical export outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List stating HS code, NAICS 339113 classification, quantity, unit price, currency, and Incoterms governing the export sale
  • Inward Remittance / Foreign Exchange Settlement Evidence - SWIFT MT103 wire credit advice or confirmed Letter of Credit negotiation record serving as the functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate held by the Canadian exporter

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API clearance and digital stamping are not in force under the federal GST/HST regime; CRA permits paper or electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, XML) provided content satisfies ETA s. 286 and CRA Information Circular IC 04-04, with records retained at least six years; the federal government supports a voluntary Pan-Canadian Trust Framework built on the Peppol/EN 16931 standard, with early adoption launched in 2025; mandatory digital filing applies through CRA My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE and GST/HST e-remittance APIs using CRA XML schemas; for export goods, CBSA CARM is the mandatory digital platform for export declarations, cargo reporting and accounting, requiring API or portal submission of export documentation.

CRA and CBSA jointly target export zero-rating abuse through GST/HST international desk audits, CARM export-data matching, and T2 risk scoring; claims lacking documentary substantiation are reassessed as taxable at the applicable 5%/13%/15% GST/HST rate with arrears interest, and input tax credits may be denied if the export chain is incomplete. Specific exposures: (i) ETA s. 281.1 false statement or omission penalty, the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax understated; (ii) failure to register and late-filing penalties under ETA ss. 280 and 280.1 together with interest under ETA s. 280; (iii) ITA s. 163(2) gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated income tax; (iv) evasion prosecution under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239 carrying fines up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years; (v) Customs Act false-declaration penalties and seizure or forfeiture of exported goods under Customs Act ss. 109-124; (vi) retrospective reassessment without time limit upon misrepresentation or fraud, extending beyond the normal four-year reassessment window under ETA s. 298 and ITA s. 152(4), with cumulative interest; (vii) CRA may offset claimed GST/HST refunds against any existing tax debt, demand security or financial guarantees from continuing registrants, and publish named-entity tax alerts in egregious cases; while no statutory FX repatriation deadline is codified under the ETA, the 180-day benchmark is applied under export-evidence and transfer-pricing reviews, and payment deferrals beyond 180 days attract scrutiny of economic substance, financial capacity and cross-border pricing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction. There are no exchange-control approvals for inbound capital, trade payments, or dividend and interest remittances. The principal hurdles are documentary: Part XIII withholding taxes on qualifying remittances to non-residents, transfer-pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act (Canada), FINTRAC reporting for international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and the bank's own internal export-control and sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, allocation, or conversion ratio on export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency in foreign-currency bank accounts or convert to CAD at the prevailing interbank market rate. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation; however, an international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC, and shipments require CBSA advance commercial reporting under the Customs Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential regulation and anti-money laundering supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-as-primary-rail, Medium-as-supplementary. Canada does not have a standalone electronic money institution (EMI) licence. Fintech firms holding client funds or facilitating payments must be a Schedule I/II bank, a trust company, or a money services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Offshore digital EMIs cannot access Payments Canada settlement infrastructure or offer insured deposit accounts, and therefore cannot serve as the principal treasury account. They may be used only as pre-funding and disbursement facilitators through a licensed Canadian MSB, while the core escrow and operational accounts must remain at a regulated Canadian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder capital must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the CA_CORP CAD-denominated operational or escrow account. The wire must include the exact registered corporate name and account number, a purpose code equivalent to 'B010' or 'CAPITAL-INJ' for direct equity investment, and a narrative referencing the executed share-subscription document and board authorization. For sums of CAD 10,000 or more, the receiving bank will file an international electronic funds-transfer report with FINTRAC. The treasury relationship manager must be pre-notified with the full corporate due-diligence package to avoid a pending-compliance hold. If the inflow is structured as a shareholder loan rather than equity, the corporate legal department must provide an exemption certificate or complete Part XIII withholding analysis before the wire is accepted; pure equity contributions are generally not taxable upon receipt. Banks will reject wires with vague references or mismatched beneficiary names; all remittance details must match the previously established account signature slate.Trade disbursements for titanium raw material, EBM 3D-printing equipment, and technology licence fees will use: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account settlements; (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600 for high-value capital-equipment purchases, with presentations comprising commercial invoice, transport document, packing list, and EBM equipment compliance certificates; (iii) Payments Canada Real-Time Rail or large-value transfer system for domestic Canadian disbursements. Every cross-border transaction requires the beneficiary bank BIC, a relevant SWIFT payment purpose code, and FINTRAC reporting for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-as-primary-rail, Medium-as-supplementary. Canada does not have a standalone electronic money institution (EMI) licence. Offshore digital EMIs cannot access Payments Canada settlement infrastructure or offer insured deposit accounts, and therefore cannot serve as the principal treasury account. They may be used only as pre-funding and disbursement facilitators through a licensed Canadian MSB, while the core escrow and operational accounts must remain at a regulated Canadian bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory dollar threshold triggers a separate Canadian Local File/Master File; however, contemporaneous TP documentation is mandatory to avoid penalty, and a T106 Information Return is statutorily required when the aggregate fair market value of non-arm's-length reportable transactions exceeds C$1,000,000 in a fiscal year. CbCR reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue above C$750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no named Economic Substances Regulations comparable to offshore DTA hubs; instead, substance is evaluated through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), arm's-length transfer-pricing requirements (ITA s.247), residency/PE treaty attribution, and anti-branch abuse jurisprudence. A titanium orthopedic implant manufacturer must maintain real personnel, physical production premises, inventory-risk functions, and substantive decision-making in Canada; a paper deferral or shell entity with no Canadian operational substance will not survive recharacterization and will be denied treaty-based withholding relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ITA s.239(1): fine up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to five years (indictment) or two years (summary conviction); civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under ITA s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in the fraudulent scheme are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's Part XIII unremitted withholding under ITA s.227.1; are personally exposed to civil restitution and Quebec/other province director-disqualification orders; and may face criminal prosecution, passport seizure/travel bans during extradition or forfeiture proceedings, and imprisonment.
Transfer-Pricing Adjustment and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to arm's-length prices under ITA s.247(2)-(3); denial of deduction for the excessive royalty/fees; transfer-pricing penalty of 10% of the net adjustment under ITA s.247(12); T106 filing penalties of C$100/day up to C$25,000; plus interest and late-payment penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the corporation to knowingly file without TP documentation may be liable under s.227.1 for Part XIII amounts not withheld on the disallowed royalty; may have limitation defences severed where negligent or fraudulent conduct is shown; and can be joined in civil restitution actions by CRA under ITA s.160, culminating in seizure orders and travel restrictions.
Failure to Withhold Part XIII Tax on Repatriated Profits

Statutory sanction: Full 25% withholding tax liability becomes chargeable against the payor (ITA s.215(1)); failure to remit triggers 5% late remittance penalty plus interest; payer remains liable for the gross amount; criminal false-statement penalties if deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly and severally liable for the unremitted Part XIII withholding under ITA s.227.1; no due-diligence defence applies where the director knew of or intentionally ignored the withholding obligation; personal assets may be garnished, and directors may be placed on the CRA directors' liability registry and subjected to cross-border travel restrictions pending payment.
GAAR / Treaty Abuse / Shell Holding Structure

Statutory sanction: CRA invokes GAAR under ITA s.245 to disregard the structure and reassess the tax benefit; treaty-shopping arguments are rejected, so Part XIII tax (25%) plus interest is imposed on the full repatriated stream; a 25% GAAR penalty may apply under ITA s.245(5) (where the transaction scenarios are not reasonable), and professional fees/asset freezes follow.

Director personal liability: Directors who arranged the letterbox holding company with intent to defeat Canadian tax are treated as promoters of an avoidance transaction; they face civil restitution claims for the unpaid Part XIII/Part I tax, CRA third-party penalty proceedings, post-judgment asset forfeiture, potential criminal charges for counselling evasion, and judicial travel-bans during cross-border investigation.
