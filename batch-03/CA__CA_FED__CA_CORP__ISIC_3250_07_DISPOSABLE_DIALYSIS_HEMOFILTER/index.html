Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A federal corporation must maintain a registered office in the province or territory specified in its articles and must maintain corporate records and records-office access as required by the Canada Business Corporations Act. A virtual office may be acceptable for registered-office and service-of-process purposes if it is a real Canadian location, receives legal notices, permits timely access to corporate records, and satisfies the service provider's KYC requirements. It does not by itself establish manufacturing substance, tax residence, permanent establishment, transfer-pricing support, employment presence, or regulatory compliance. For extrusion and potting of hollow-fiber polyethersulfone dialyzers, a virtual office alone is inadequate for Health Canada licensing, quality-system evidence, manufacturing controls, device complaint handling, importation, storage, and inspection readiness. The business should maintain a documented Canadian operational address, qualified personnel, quality records, supplier controls, device-history records, and a validated manufacturing or contract-manufacturing arrangement.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — principal tax administration; digital portals: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); federal incorporation by Corporation Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada); customs and export reporting by Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned CA_CORP must: (1) incorporate federally or extra-provincially through Corporations Canada; (2) file Form RC1 or register online via Business Registration Online to obtain the nine-digit Business Number (BN); (3) activate tax program accounts under the BN (RC income tax, RT GST/HST, RP payroll, RM importer/exporter); (4) obtain a Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence and the applicable medical device licence for the disposable dialysis hemofilter prior to any commercial sale; (5) where no Canadian place of business exists, appoint an agent for service and maintain a Canadian banking channel for tax remittances; non-resident ownership is unrestricted and no minimum capital is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a CA_CORP federal/provincial business corporation is taxed at the entity level and is not a pass-through vehicle; a corporation incorporated or managed and controlled in Canada is resident under section 250 ITA and taxable on worldwide income with foreign tax credits; non-resident shareholders of a Canadian subsidiary are not directly taxable on operating earnings except on dividends/branch obligations; Canada has no general territorial exemption and no tax-exempt manufacturing zone for dialysis consumables.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; relevant elections for this structure include: (a) GST/HST section 156 election to treat supplies between closely related Canadian corporations as made for no consideration; (b) voluntary GST/HST registration for small suppliers; (c) non-resident section 216 election to compute Part XIII rental/royalty-type income on a net basis; (d) foreign affiliate regulatory elections under section 5907 for FAPI computations; (e) section 256 associated-corporation allocation elections; hybrid mismatch rules (sections 18.2/18.3 ITA) are mandatory when in force and provide no elective carve-out for a Canadian manufacturing subsidiary.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — GST/HST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies made in Canada, by a person carrying on commercial activity in Canada, exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters; below this small-supplier threshold registration is voluntary; separate QST/PST registrations apply in Quebec and British Columbia; corporate T2 income tax filing is mandatory from the company's first taxation year regardless of revenue level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible personal property exported from Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, Excise Tax Act where the registrant ships the goods to a destination outside Canada or delivers them to a carrier for export; single-use dialysis hemofilters are typically zero-rated on export regardless of hospital end-use; services performed for non-residents that are not for Canadian consumption qualify under section 9, Part V, Schedule VI. The exporter must hold a valid Health Canada Medical Device Establishment Licence and maintain contemporaneous transport evidence; input tax credits for GST/HST paid on manufacturing inputs are recovered through the exporter's periodic GST/HST return, with no separate VAT refund application required.

  • CBSA Canadian Export Declaration transmitted via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES/GCA) within 15 days of shipment for goods valued above CAD 2,000
  • Commercial invoice stating HS classification (e.g., 9018.90), full description, quantity, unit value and zero-rated export statement
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/house) or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Canada and foreign destination
  • Packing list and container manifest identifying dialysis hemofilter SKUs, lots and quantities
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 or TT wire confirmation evidencing settlement under the Swift_LC/TT_Wire payment rails
  • Retained section 223 ETA invoice copies and six-year CRA export records retention log

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or digital invoice certification; CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants above the small-supplier threshold and electronic T2 filing for corporations above prescribed revenue thresholds, with electronic remittance of large payments; invoices supporting export zero-rating must contain the particulars required by section 223 of the Excise Tax Act; PEPPOL-compatible e-invoices are acceptable but not compulsory.

Canada imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied as prudent treasury policy aligned to documentary LC maturity. If an export zero-rating claim fails substantiation, CRA recharacterizes the supply as taxable in Canada: (i) reassessment for GST/HST at the applicable standard rate plus interest under section 280 ETA; (ii) gross-negligence penalty of up to 25% of the tax understated for false statements under section 281.1 ETA; (iii) prosecution for tax evasion under section 239 ETA and the Criminal Code with fines and imprisonment; (iv) CBSA Administrative Monetary Penalty System penalties up to CAD 25,000 per export declaration failure plus possible seizure of goods; (v) income tax reassessment on unrepatriated export revenue with section 163(2) ITA 50% gross-negligence penalty and section 247(3) ITA 10% transfer-pricing penalty where arm's-length documentation is deficient; the normal reassessment window is four years, extended indefinitely in cases of fraud or wilful misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Agreement (Article VIII). No exchange controls restrict profit remittance, dividend distribution, royalty payments, or capital repatriation. The Canadian dollar floats freely; banks are permitted to transact in any currency. Only reporting obligations apply (e.g., FINTRAC EFT reports, Statistics Canada Balance of Payments surveys).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirement exists. Canadian exporters may retain export proceeds in foreign currency indefinitely, hold them in foreign-denominated accounts, and use them for trade settlement. No mandatory deadline or minimum conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); banking regulatory authority: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervisory authority: Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Canada does not have a distinct EMI passporting regime; offshore digital EMIs must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under FINTRAC and obtain provincial licensing where applicable. They may provide payment/clearing services but cannot accept deposit-taking or offer banking products without a banking license. Cross-border referral to Canadian Schedule I banks is typically preferred.
