Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portal: CRA 'My Business Account' (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/e-services-businesses/business-account.html); Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/business-registration-online.html

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) through Corporations Canada (or provincially under e.g., Ontario Business Corporations Act) and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and Articles. Step 2: On incorporation, Corporations Canada transmits the registry data to the CRA, which automatically issues the nine-digit Business Number (BN) to the Canadian-resident corporation; if no automatic BN is received, file Form RC1 or call CRA Business Registration. Step 3: Activate the required CRA accounts through BRO or My Business Account: GST/HST (RT) account (mandatory for taxable supplies unless small supplier), payroll (RP) account (if employees), import/export (RM) account (required to clear customs and for export reporting in the CBSA CESR system). Step 4: For a non-resident foreign corporation without incorporation in Canada, apply directly to CRA for a BN using Form RC1 (or the online non-resident GST/HST registration), declaring a Canadian representative and potentially posting security under subsection 240(3) of the Excise Tax Act if required; the non-resident will be issued a BN with a RT 'non-resident' designation for GST/HST collections on Canadian supplies. Step 5: Register with the CBSA for an Import/Export (RM) account using the BN; obtain a Customs Client Identifier (CCID) and, if the entity will export, register in the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CESR). Step 6: All registrations should be completed before the first commercial shipment, and the GST/HST registration number must appear on all invoices and export documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not employ the U.S. check-the-box classification election; a CBCA corporation is per se a corporation taxable at entity level under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA) and, if non-resident-owned, is also subject to Part XIV branch tax on Canadian-source earnings. Available elections relevant to the target: (1) ITA s.85(1) rollover election to transfer property to the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (2) ITA s.249.1(1) election to adopt a non-calendar fiscal period; (3) election under ITA s.216(4) for a non-resident to file a net-basis Canadian return for property income; (4) Excise Tax Act (ETA) s.273 election for closely related corporations to treat themselves as a single person for GST/HST, eliminating invoicing between members; (5) GST/HST Quick Method election (Form GST187) available to eligible small businesses (gross annual taxable supplies of all associates not exceeding CAD 400,000) to simplify tax calculations; (6) GST/HST simplified input tax credit method for eligible businesses under CAD 500,000; (7) annual GST/HST filing election where net tax remittances are under CAD 3,000 per year. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.2, 18.3, 20.2) automatically deny deductions for payments that are deductible in the payor country without corresponding income in Canada; no elective check-the-box mechanism exists, and CRA may apply the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA s.245) to synthetic hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold: a person becomes a mandatory registrant for GST/HST once total worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter and the immediately preceding four calendar quarters; persons making taxable supplies in Canada through a fulfilment warehouse or digital marketplace must apply the same CAD 30,000 threshold; non-residents carrying on business in Canada or making remote or online taxable supplies to Canadians must register at CAD 30,000; voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted to recover input tax credits

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Canada or supplied to a non-resident and removed directly out of Canada are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.1 of the Excise Tax Act, provided title passes and the goods are delivered to a destination outside Canada either by the supplier or by the recipient acting as principal. The target's off-price apparel liquidation qualifies when: (i) the garments are sold to a foreign wholesale buyer; (ii) the buyer's purchase order identifies a foreign destination; (iii) the goods are shipped directly from the supplier's Canadian warehouse or the buyer's freight forwarder's premises out of Canada; (iv) export documentation is obtained by the supplier within a reasonable period after delivery; and (v) the supplier has no reason to believe the goods will be returned to Canada. Offshore services provided to a non-resident (e.g., liquidation consulting) are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 where the service is performed in Canada but is for the benefit of and consumed outside Canada. Merely selling goods to a non-resident who then takes delivery in Canada and later exports them does not qualify; an outright zero-rating claim must be supported by full chain-of-custody evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canadian B13A / CBSA Export Report filed via CESR, including ACI e-manifest data)
  • Bill of Lading (clean, negotiable ocean, air, or rail waybill showing foreign consignee and destination port)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 proof of payment from the foreign buyer; for Canada this is customarily a certified bank advice proving export proceeds received)
  • CBSA Export Manifest and freight documentation
  • Packing list and commercial invoice in triplicate showing the GST/HST registration number
  • Purchase order, sales contract, and any intercompany or third-party agreement stating risk and loss transfer
  • Insurance certificate and freight contract demonstrating that the goods left Canadian jurisdiction
  • CRA Form GST370 'Export Sales' or equivalent export summary schedule attached to the GST/HST return

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime comparable to Italy's SDI or Brazil's NF-e. GST/HST returns are filed periodically (monthly, quarterly, or annually) through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE electronic return, and remittances are due one month after the end of the reporting period. The CRA requires that any invoice issued by a registrant contain the BN/RT account number, the amount of GST/HST charged, and the sale terms; e-invoices are accepted without pre-clearance if they remain electronically retrievable. Exporters must submit electronic export declarations via the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CESR) or, for low-value shipments, through approved service providers; the CBSA ACI/e-manifest rules require advance electronic data for exported goods. A voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing standard (PEPPOL-based, 2023-2024 rollout) exists but is not yet mandatory; invoice and export records must be retained for at least six years under ETA s.286 and ITA s.230.

CRA auditors require that all export zero-rating be substantiated within a reasonable time; a 180-day benchmark is used for payment collection and repatriation evidence. Where export documentation is incomplete, the CRA will recharacterize the sale as a domestic taxable supply and reassess GST/HST at 5% (or applicable HST up to 15%) on the full gross selling price, with refunds and input tax credits clawed back and prescribed interest accruing from the original filing date. Penalties include: (i) ETA s.280 — failure to collect or remit tax attracts a penalty of 6% of the tax amount plus interest, and repeated failures can increase the penalty; (ii) ETA s.285 — knowingly making a false or misleading statement in a return attracts a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax involved; (iii) ITA s.163(2) — gross negligence penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of the understated tax; (iv) ITA s.239 — criminal tax evasion fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years; (v) the CRA may also revoke registration, seize books and records, and impose director liability under ETA and provincial equivalents. A fraudulent zero-rating claim involving false bills of lading or forged remittance advices also triggers suspicious-transaction reporting to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, potentially leading to money-laundering charges.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Canada imposes no exchange-control restrictions on trade settlements, dividend remittances, interest payments, management fees, or repatriation of contributed equity. Non-resident shareholders of a Canadian Business Corporation may freely remit profits, subject only to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5-15% under the Canada-US treaty) and standard FINTRAC reporting for international electronic funds transfers exceeding CAD 10,000. Capital outflows require no prior Bank of Canada or Minister of Finance approval except where goods or counterparties are sanctioned under the Special Economic Measures Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada maintains no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriation of foreign currency receivables. Exporters of finished garments may retain proceeds indefinitely in USD or any other convertible currency; however, international EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more routed through Canadian financial institutions must be reported to FINTRAC, and payments to sanctioned persons or jurisdictions are blocked under federal sanctions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) as monetary authority and manager of the national foreign exchange reserves; there is no standalone foreign exchange administration in Canada because exchange controls were eliminated in 1951. Prudential supervision of Schedule I banks is mandated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and AML/CFT supervision of financial entities and Money Service Businesses rests with the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM - supplementary rails only. Canada does not possess an EU/UK-style EMI licensing regime; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may only serve a registered Canadian entity if the provider is registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and holds end-client funds through a safeguarding account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Such platforms are legally capable of supporting low-value cross-border ACH/wire collections for overstock apparel trade, but they cannot issue Canadian deposit accounts, cannot be used to satisfy capital-escrow or statutory tax-filing banking expectations, and must not supersede a chartered bank as the primary treasury clearing node.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for non-resident shareholder capital: the foreign investor initiates a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank) to the beneficiary Schedule I bank's CAD correspondent route, quoting the exact legal name of the Canadian corporation, the account number, and SWIFT BIC; Remittance Information field 70 must clearly state 'Equity Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan - [Agreement Reference]'. Canada requires no exchange-control pre-approval and applies no SAFE-style purpose code regime, but the receiving bank will require a board resolution authorizing the injection, a share subscription agreement or loan agreement, and UBO/source-of-funds declarations for every wire at or above CAD 10,000 (FINTRAC EFT reporting threshold). Inbound USD wires are best routed via Fedwire or CHIPS to the bank's US branch before internal conversion to CAD, avoiding double FX spreads; funds are first credited to the Capital Escrow Account and released to the Operational Account only after compliance clearance.Four compliant clearing rails are recommended: (1) SWIFT MT103 via the bank's commercial payment portal with gpi tracking for supplier settlements above USD 5,000; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit issued and confirmed under UCP600 for high-value purchases of brand overstock from overseas suppliers, with presentation of commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and certificate of origin through the bank's trade services desk; (3) cross-border ACH using International ACH Transaction (IAT) format for high-volume, low-value payments below USD 10,000 to US suppliers, settled through Payments Canada and the US ACH Network; and (4) LVTS real-time CAD wires for domestic clearing prior to FX conversion. Every outbound international EFT over CAD 10,000 is reportable to FINTRAC, and the bank will require underlying invoices and purchase order numbers; apparel shipments require correct HS codes 6201-6217 and the company's valid Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) importer/exporter number.Deliverable CAD/USD FX forwards (vanilla contracts with standard tenors up to 24 months quoted by Schedule I bank dealing desks)FX swaps (simultaneous spot and forward legs for treasury cash-flow smoothing on trade receivables)Cross-currency swaps (fixed-to-floating CAD/USD for committed multi-year trade or escrow-backed financing)Vanilla FX options (CAD call/USD put and CAD put/USD call for contingent settlement exposure on liquidation auctions)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not available or economically rational for CAD, because CAD is fully convertible with a deep onshore deliverable market; NDFs are restricted to regulated or inconvertible currencies and will not be offered by Canadian banks as a standard hedging rail.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM - supplementary rails only. Canada does not possess an EU/UK-style EMI licensing regime; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may only serve a registered Canadian entity if the provider is registered as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and holds end-client funds through a safeguarding account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. Such platforms are legally capable of supporting low-value cross-border ACH/wire collections for overstock apparel trade, but they cannot issue Canadian deposit accounts, cannot be used to satisfy capital-escrow or statutory tax-filing banking expectations, and must not supersede a chartered bank as the primary treasury clearing node.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% federal general corporate Part I rate after the 13% general rate reduction; provincial and territorial corporate income tax rates of 8.0% to 16.0% are superimposed, producing combined federal-provincial headline rates of approximately 23.0% to 31.0% depending on the province of permanent establishment (e.g., Alberta 23.0%, Ontario 26.5%, Quebec 26.5%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., a December 31 year-end is due June 30). The balance-due day for payment of tax is 2 months after the fiscal year-end for non-CCPCs, and 3 months after the fiscal year-end for CCPCs entitled to the Small Business Deduction. Instalments are required where prior-year federal or provincial tax payable exceeded CAD 3,000; instalments are due on the last day of each month, or quarterly for smaller corporations.
