Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html); registration online via Business Registration Online (BRO). Goods and services tax (GST/HST) and corporation income tax accounts are both administered by the CRA; exports are administered jointly with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

The entity is a federally incorporated Business Corporation (CBCA) and must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or using the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) single-window service, usually integrated with the provincial/territorial corporate registry. Required program accounts: RP (Corporate Income Tax), RT (GST/HST), and, if applicable, RM (Import/Export) with CBSA. Non-resident corporations without Canadian incorporation apply via the same RC1 pathway and must provide a Canadian registered office address, directors' information, a completed Form RC1-S (non-resident account request) where needed, and the certificate of corporate existence. The T2 Corporation Income Tax Return is due within 6 months after fiscal year-end; installments are due monthly/quarterly. The CRA also assigns a CRA program account tax ID for each levy; no separate VAT ID exists beyond the RT program account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists for Canadian business corporations under the Income Tax Act (ITA); a corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to Part I tax (federal rate 15% on the general rate, 9% on Canadian-controlled private corporation active business income up to the business limit, plus provincial corporate tax within provinces). Available statutory elections: (i) s.216 ITA election for a non-resident with rental or timber royalties to file on a net basis; (ii) s.219 ITA election for non-resident insurers; (iii) GST/HST Quick Method election (annual revenue thresholds apply); (iv) GST/HST streamlining election for small suppliers; (v) partnership-interest elections under s.96(3) ITA where applicable; (vi) the CRA respects the US 'check-the-box' classification of a US hybrid for Canada-US Treaty purposes only where the entity is fiscally transparent in the US under Article XXIX A(8) of the Treaty; hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss.18.2-18.3) limit interest deductions where hybrid mismatches are present.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (small supplier threshold under Excise Tax Act s.148). A non-resident that solicits orders in Canada or maintains a place of business in Canada while making taxable supplies in Canada is deemed to carry on a commercial activity in Canada (ETA s.240) and should confirm registration status with the CRA. For a manufacturing corporation with exports, registration is mandatory once taxable Canadian supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold; once registered, GST/HST at the federal rate of 5% applies (13% HST in Ontario, 15% in Nova Scotia/PEI/New Brunswick/Newfoundland, 12% in British Columbia, if the place of supply is relevant for domestic sales).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods is available under Part I of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (section 1) for intraocular acrylic lenses (medical device; HS 9004.90 or HS 9021.39, subject to a CBSA National Customs Ruling). Criteria: (i) the supplier must have exported the goods from Canada; (ii) the goods must be delivered to a consignee outside Canada or shipped directly outside Canada; (iii) the supplier must retain evidence satisfactory to the Minister (documentation of export); (iv) the goods must be exported as soon as reasonably possible after delivery. The manufacturer may claim input tax credits (ITCs) for GST/HST paid on inputs attributable to zero-rated export sales. Supplies to Canadian hospitals of qualifying medical devices may be zero-rated under Part II of Schedule VI only for listed devices; exports remain the clearly zero-rated channel. Pure services performed wholly outside Canada, or supplied to a non-resident, may also qualify under section 7 of Schedule VI.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration submitted via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) or legacy Form B13A (mandatory for exports valued at or above CAD 2,000 or controlled goods)
  • Commercial invoice describing the intraocular lenses, quantity, and HS classification (9004.90 / 9021.39)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the consignee outside Canada and outside-CANADA destination
  • Proof of payment/inward remittance (SWIFT MT103 trace, letter of credit settlement documentation, or bank receipt evidencing receipt of sales proceeds)
  • Carrier's certificate of export or forwarder's attestation confirming physical departure from Canada
  • Customs broker export summary and internal GST/HST schedule documenting zero-rated supplies under Schedule VI, Part I

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API-based clearance, or digital stamping regime in Canada. The CRA supports voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing through the Pan-Canadian Peppol framework (operational since 2023-2024); adoption is optional. GST/HST registrants with annual revenue exceeding CAD 1,500,000 must file returns electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST electronic filing); all registrants must retain source documents in machine-readable format for audit. No e-发票 stamping or pre-clearance approval is required for export invoices.

The CRA requires documentary proof of export within 30 days of a written audit request; failure to substantiate zero-rating results in reassessment at 5% GST (or 13% HST for Ontario-based suppliers) on the gross consideration, plus arrears interest at the prescribed rate (corporate arrears rate = base rate plus 4 percentage points, set quarterly; currently in the mid-single-digit to 9% range). Civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act: s.280(1) late filing penalty of 1% of net tax plus 0.25% per full month (max 4%); s.280(2) false statement or gross negligence penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the net tax shortfall; s.285 failure to register penalty of CAD 2,000; s.285.1 failure to file electronic return penalty. Criminal sanctions under ETA s.327 for knowing or intentional evasion: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment up to 5 years. Record-retention obligations: 6 years under ETA s.286 (GST/HST records) and 5 years under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act for inbound wire/L/C records. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for export proceeds in goods; the 180-day figure is applied as a conservative documentary benchmark aligned with trade finance tenors. However, under ITA s.247 transfer pricing rules, intercompany export receivables must bear arm's length terms; overdue balances beyond 180 days without interest or note terms may trigger deemed interest under ITA s.17 or shareholder benefit treatment under ITA s.15(2), and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared to avoid the s.247(3) penalty (10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment in excess of CAD 5,000).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is a fully convertible floating currency under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Current-account flows (dividends, royalties, interest, management fees) face zero exchange-control approval, subject only to Income Tax Act Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (25% statutory; 5-15% under DTA treaties). Capital-account flows (equity injection, intercompany loans, FDI) are unrestricted; the Investment Canada Act requires an ISED notification only for acquisitions of control above prescribed thresholds, never an exchange permit. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are unrestricted but require bank source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or retention rules exist. Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A Canadian exporter may retain 100% of FX proceeds in CAD, USD or EUR accounts. Only residual obligations are: (i) Statistics Canada trade declaration (B13), and (ii) automatic FINTRAC reporting by the bank for any cross-border EFT of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary and note-issuing authority. Foreign-exchange administration is split: Department of Finance Canada holds policy authority; OSFI supervises authorized FX trading banks; FINTRAC executes transaction monitoring under PCMLTFA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (goods), CAD 500,000 (services), CAD 250,000 (other transactions, e.g., royalties, interest) – thresholds for filing Form T106 and preparing contemporaneous documentation.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Section 245 of the Income Tax Act disallows transactions that lack economic substance and are primarily tax-motivated. Additionally, the CRA may apply the 'substance over form' doctrine and assess penalties for aggressive tax planning. For manufacturing entities, the CRA expects real operational presence, including physical facilities, employees, and risk management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Section 239 of the Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax (gross negligence penalty).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties under Section 227.1 if they fail to withhold and remit employee source deductions or GST/HST. Civil restitution, travel bans (via court orders), and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits under the Limitation of Benefits clause (if applicable) or apply GAAR to recharacterize transactions. Penalties: 10% of the tax benefit (minimum $5,000) under Section 163.2 for false statements, plus interest. For non-resident corporations, may be deemed to have a permanent establishment and subject to Part I tax on full Canadian-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell corporation may be personally liable for taxes owed if they knowingly participated in a sham arrangement. They may face civil penalties, asset freezes, and travel bans under the Income Tax Act's collection provisions.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 for corporations, and criminal penalties up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years for individuals. Directors may be personally liable for fines if they knowingly fail to comply.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be held personally liable for AML violations if they directed, authorized, or acquiesced in the non-compliance. They may face imprisonment, fines, and travel bans as part of enforcement actions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices to arm's length, impose a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment (minimum CAD 5,000) under Section 247(3), and charge interest on the additional tax. For repeated or gross negligence, penalties can be up to 25% of the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for TP penalties if they signed the tax return knowing the TP documentation was inadequate or false. They may face civil penalties and, in extreme cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
