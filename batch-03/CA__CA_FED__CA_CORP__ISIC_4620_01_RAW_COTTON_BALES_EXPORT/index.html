Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The CBCA requires a registered office address in the province or territory specified in the articles. A commercial registered-office or virtual-office provider may generally be used if it is a genuine physical address, can receive legal documents during required business hours and is properly recorded in corporate filings. Name reservation / lock period

Canada does not generally provide a separate statutory federal name-reservation period equivalent to a 30-day reservation. A Nuans report is normally required to be no more than 90 days old at filing, subject to the then-current Corporations Canada requirements. If a proposed name is rejected, obtain the written or electronic deficiency reason, compare the name against CBCA section 12 and the applicable regulations, conduct a new Nuans search and trademark search, and either amend the name or submit a written reconsideration request to Corporations Canada with supporting evidence of distinctiveness, consent, prior use or non-confusion. A refusal may be challenged through applicable federal administrative-law remedies, including judicial review in the Federal Court, but filing a revised or numbered name is usually faster. Provincial registration may also be required to carry on business under a federal corporate name in a province or territory.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement under the CBCA; nil authorized and paid capital is permissible. Issued shares must be fully paid in money, property or past services as determined by the directors (CBCA ss. 24-25). No escrow account requirement applies at federal incorporation; subscriber funds are held in the corporate treasury at the board's direction. The statutory preconditions are: a registered office in Canada, first directors satisfying the CBCA residency ratio (s. 105(3)-(4)) or an s. 105(5) exemption, and the board's acceptance of the share subscription.

Funding & unlock path: For office-only operations, register under the applicable provincial occupational health and safety statute (e.g., Ontario OHSA) or Part II of the Canada Labour Code if the corporation becomes federally regulated. For any future warehouse: fire safety certification and inspection under the applicable fire code (e.g., Ontario Fire Code), building structural load certification for cotton bale stacking, electrical safety inspection under the local electrical code, and workplace emergency-evacuation and accommodation clearance from the municipal authority.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); registration portal: Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca; ongoing filing and compliance portal: My Business Account (RMRA).

E-government filing portal

A federal business corporation must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with a program-account suffix ('RC' for GST/HST; 'RT' for Part I corporate income tax). New incorporations may register simultaneously via BRO; otherwise file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with certified articles, registered office address, and directors' particulars. A non-resident corporation must also file Form RC1, maintain a Canadian address for service, appoint a resident representative if required, and provide evidence that it carries on business in Canada or makes a supply that is taxable in Canada; CRA generally issues the BN and accounts within four to six weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian federal corporation is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income with no pass-through availability; it may qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) under Income Tax Act s. 125(7), preserving access to the small business deduction. — A federal business corporation must obtain a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with a program-account suffix ('RC' for GST/HST; 'RT' for Part I corporate income tax). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Canadian federal corporation is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income with no pass-through availability; it may qualify as a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) under Income Tax Act s. 125(7), preserving access to the small business deduction.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Canada. Relevant elections: (i) voluntary GST/HST registration by a 'small supplier' under Excise Tax Act s. 240(2); (ii) the s. 156 election for nil consideration on supplies between closely related corporations; (iii) the taxation-year election under Income Tax Act s. 249.1; and (iv) ordering and allocation elections contemplated by the hybrid mismatch rules in Income Tax Act ss. 18.2-18.4 and 20.2 (generally applicable to expenses incurred on or after July 1, 2023).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (including the single-quarter exceedance test under Excise Tax Act s. 148); threshold increased to CAD 50,000 effective January 1, 2025. Zero-rated export supplies count as taxable supplies for the registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of ginned raw cotton bales qualify for zero-rating under section 1, Part V, Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act where the supplier exports the goods, or delivers them to a common carrier for export, as part of a continuous journey to a foreign destination; the exporter must retain contemporaneous documentary proof of physical export and must file the applicable GST/HST return claiming the zero-rated treatment before the statutory filing deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13A or Export Reporting System electronic transmission)
  • Bill of Lading (through bill of lading or waybill naming a foreign consignee and destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or foreign exchange receipt evidencing remittance of the invoice proceeds)

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority pre-approval, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force. GST/HST registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,500,000 must file returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE; the CRA has announced a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol network with targeted availability from 2026.

CRA and CBSA may reassess an export zero-rating claim and claw back input tax credits when the documentation bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or payment is not received within the contractual foreign-exchange repatriation period; while no codified federal statutory repatriation deadline exists, the 180-day benchmark is adopted by Canadian trade-finance practice and used by CRA in substantiation reviews. Excise Tax Act s. 285(1) imposes a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of net tax understated for false or misleading statements; s. 280 compounds daily interest; s. 327 creates strict criminal liability for evasion; and Income Tax Act s. 163(2) applies a gross-negligence penalty of 50% of any understated tax where records are falsified, extending the CRA reassessment period to six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency and imposes no general exchange controls under the Currency Act or related legislation. Current account and capital account transactions are freely permitted. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders through dividends is legally permissible but is subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate, subject to reduction under an applicable tax treaty, and must be reported using an NR4 statement. Repatriation of invested capital, repayment of shareholder loans, and distribution of liquidation proceeds are not subject to exchange control authorization and may be remitted without delay.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory obligation requiring exporters to surrender export proceeds to the central bank, to convert foreign currency receipts into Canadian dollars, or to observe a prescribed retention period. Exporters may maintain foreign currency accounts domestically or abroad at their discretion. Financial institutions are required to report international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period to FINTRAC; no economic or trade-based surrender/retention obligation otherwise applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Non-bank electronic money institutions and payments fintechs (e.g., international virtual account providers, e-money issuers) are permitted to operate as money services businesses (MSBs) in Canada only if registered with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. They may not accept deposits as part of a banking business and cannot issue commercial letters of credit or provide full trade finance facilities. For a cotton-bale export treasury structure requiring documentary credits, SWIFT-based settlement, and committed liquidity lines, a Schedule I domestic chartered bank clearing account remains the legally viable and operationally necessary rail; foreign EMIs generally provide supplementary collections and FX conversion support only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Since Canada maintains an open capital account, inbound equity capital injections or shareholder loan advances may be wired using standard SWIFT MT103 messages or ISO 20022 pain.001 credit transfers. The wire should cite the beneficiary corporate name exactly, include the account number at the Canadian clearing institution, and use payment reference 'Capital Injection - CA_CORP - Raw Cotton Export'. For amounts of CAD 10,000 or more in a single 24-hour period, the receiving bank must submit an international electronic funds transfer (IEFT) report to FINTRAC. All outbound international transfers of CAD 10,000 or more require FINTRAC IEFT reporting by the sending institution.
