Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); primary online services are CRA My Business Account and the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs import and export account functions through the CARM Client Portal.

E-government filing portal

A Canadian corporation obtains a nine-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online, by telephone, or through incorporation-related registration where available. The corporation then registers the applicable CRA program accounts, including the corporate income tax account (RC), GST/HST account (RT), payroll account (RP), and import-export account (RM) where relevant. A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada must apply for a BN and the applicable CRA program accounts using BRO or the CRA non-resident registration process. GST/HST registration requires identification of the legal entity, directors or authorized representatives, business activities, Canadian business address or non-resident contact details, expected taxable revenues, effective registration date, fiscal year, and authorized person. The BN and program account identifiers must be used on tax filings, invoices where required, customs records, and CRA correspondence. Provincial corporate registrations, provincial sales-tax registrations, and provincial tax account numbers are separate from the federal BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally separate taxable entities and there is no Canadian equivalent of the United States federal check-the-box election or S corporation election. A corporation may elect or make statutory designations under specific Canadian rules, including eligible capital dividend account designations, subsection 85(1) or 85(2) rollover elections for qualifying transfers, section 86 share-reorganization elections, functional-currency reporting elections under section 261, and certain GST/HST elections such as the closely related-party election, joint venture election, nil consideration election, and simplified GST/HST accounting methods where eligibility conditions are satisfied. Canadian anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act, including rules addressing foreign dividend deductions and hybrid arrangements, operate by statute and are not elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, determined under the small-supplier rules over the applicable preceding or immediately succeeding four-quarter period. The threshold generally applies to taxable supplies made in Canada and excludes certain supplies, including supplies of financial services and goodwill. Registration is voluntary below the threshold and generally mandatory when the small-supplier threshold is exceeded. HST applies at 13% in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia, and 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island. The federal GST rate remains 5%. QST, Manitoba Retail Sales Tax, Saskatchewan Provincial Sales Tax, and British Columbia Provincial Sales Tax may impose separate registration obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exports of goods made in Canada and exported to a destination outside Canada are generally zero-rated for GST/HST when the statutory export conditions are met, including delivery or transfer of possession outside Canada or shipment by the supplier to a destination outside Canada, depending on the applicable rule. The supplier must retain evidence that the goods left Canada, such as customs records, carrier documentation, bills of lading, delivery records, purchase orders, invoices, and proof of foreign delivery. Certain services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident and the service falls within the relevant exported-service rule. Services relating to real property, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or services physically performed in Canada may be excluded or subject to specific exceptions. Digital products and services require transaction-specific analysis under the place-of-supply and exported-service provisions. Exporters may recover eligible GST/HST incurred on inputs through input tax credits, subject to registration, documentation, timing, and use-in-commercial-activity requirements.

Canada has no general federal real-time electronic invoice clearance or digital tax-stamping mandate for ordinary domestic GST/HST transactions. GST/HST registrants must issue compliant invoices, receipts, or other supporting documentation and maintain books and records sufficient to substantiate tax collected and input tax credits. Electronic records are generally acceptable if they remain reliable, accessible, readable, and capable of reproducing the original information. Electronic invoicing may be required contractually or under specific government procurement arrangements, but ordinary commercial invoices are not generally subject to pre-clearance by the CRA. Customs declarations are filed electronically through CBSA systems, including the CARM Client Portal for applicable commercial import processes. Certain electronic filing, payment, information-return, or platform-reporting obligations may apply based on taxpayer status and transaction type.

Canada has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days or any other fixed period. The exporter must substantiate that the supply qualified for zero-rating and must correct tax returns where the conditions were not met. The CRA may deny zero-rating, assess GST/HST retroactively, deny related input tax credits, assess interest, and impose gross-negligence or other applicable penalties. False statements or omissions may attract civil penalties and, in serious cases, prosecution under the Excise Tax Act or Criminal Code. A person who knowingly obtains or claims an improper GST/HST refund may face repayment, interest, penalties, and criminal sanctions. Records must generally be retained for six years from the end of the relevant year, subject to statutory extensions and specific record-retention rules. Customs misdeclarations may result in seizure, reassessment of duties and taxes, administrative monetary penalties under the CBSA's compliance regime, and prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating exchange rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Obligation. There are no general exchange controls, surrender requirements, or central-bank approval gates for profit remittance, foreign direct investment, or trade settlement. Dividends and branch profits remitted to non-resident shareholders are subject to applicable Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (typically 25% statutory, reduced under tax treaties to 5-15% depending on ownership) and must be reported on NR4, but no FX approval is required. Sanctions and anti-money-laundering screening remain the only practical constraints on cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory obligation to surrender, convert, or escrow export proceeds with the central bank or any governmental agency. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts, settle in any freely convertible currency, and determine conversion timing and counterparty at their discretion. The only compliance requirement is accurate bookkeeping and the mandatory reporting of cross-border wire transfers and electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority; foreign exchange policy oversight resides with the Department of Finance; currency and anti-money-laundering transaction regulations are administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for fully offshore financial technology / electronic money institutions. Canada does not recognize the EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence; non-bank fintech payment service providers must register as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and may also require provincial licensing under a patchwork of payment acts. A purely offshore EMI without Canadian registration cannot offer CAD-denominated bank accounts or access Payments Canada clearing rails. Such entities are viable only for ancillary cross-border collection or expedited remittance corridors, not as primary accounts for capital escrow or treasury concentration.

Inbound capital injection shall be executed through SWIFT wire transfers from the foreign parent or shareholder to the Canadian corporate operating or capital escrow account. For CAD-denominated funds, use the Canadian Clearing Code (institution/transit) and the corporate account number. For USD-denominated funds, use the Canadian bank's US Correspondent (ABA SWIFT) network or a dedicated USD clearing account. Documentary verification includes corporate board resolutions authorizing the injection, shareholder subscription / loan agreements specifying whether the payment is equity (paid-in capital, share premium) or shareholder loan (promissory note), and clear remittance instructions citing 'Equity Contribution / Paid-In Capital' or 'Shareholder Loan' as the purpose. The receiving bank and the corporate taxpayer are also expected to maintain source-of-funds records, an updated schedule of beneficial ownership, and a consent to share information with the Canada Revenue Agency upon request.
