Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and Business Number registration; BC Ministry of Finance, Taxation Branch — BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration via eTaxBC

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA. Registration is available online through CRA Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1. The entity must register for GST/HST once its worldwide taxable supplies of Canadian goods/services (including deemed supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in a consecutive four-quarter period. A separate BC PST account must be opened with eTaxBC before the first taxable sale/lease of software or other PST-taxable goods is made in British Columbia; there is no minimum PST registration threshold. The ULC must provide its registered office address in BC, director/officer information, and Canadian withholding/agent details where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a federal check-the-box election. A BC Unlimited Liability Corporation is a separate taxable corporation under the Canadian Income Tax Act. For U.S. federal income tax purposes only, eligible U.S. owners may make an entity classification election on IRS Form 8832 to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity/pass-through; however, Canadian hybrid mismatch legislation (Income Tax Act subsection 18(f) and paragraph 12(2.2)) may apply to deny deductions or require income inclusions on certain hybrid/non-recognition transactions. No other entity-level pass-through election is available for Canadian GST/HST purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 (worldwide taxable supplies threshold). BC PST: no minimum threshold; registration required before making the first taxable sale/lease in British Columbia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of digital property or service is zero-rated for GST/HST where the recipient is a non-resident and the supply is not received or enjoyed in Canada, and the supplier has the required evidence of the non-resident status and delivery to an address/geographic location outside Canada. For physical goods exported by the supplier, the goods must be exported as part of the sale, and the supplier must retain proof of physical export. Mobile utility app sales made through Canadian app-store channels to consumers physically located in Canada remain subject to GST/HST and, where applicable, BC PST; sales clearly made to non-residents and not enjoyed in Canada may be zero-rated if full documentary support is maintained.

  • Canadian Customs Export Declaration (B13A Export Declaration or equivalent)
  • Commercial invoice or sales contract showing non-resident purchaser and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / carrier proof of export for physical goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or FX receipt showing foreign currency settlement
  • App store transaction report, IP/geo-location evidence, and non-resident attestation for digital supplies
  • BC PST exemption and export collection documentation where applicable

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing, or digital stamping by the CRA. GST/HST tax invoices and simplified invoices must contain the prescribed information under the Excise Tax Act and Regulations. For app-store sales through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the app-store operator may issue transaction records directly to consumers; the Canadian seller must retain electronic sales reports, payment settlement records, and detailed invoices sufficient to support filed GST/HST and BC PST returns. No government API clearance is currently required.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict CRA audit scrutiny. If evidence of export or non-resident receipt is lacking, the CRA may reassess the supply at the full 5% GST/HST rate plus arrears interest and may impose a wilful-participation penalty under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act. Knowingly issuing false or misleading export invoices or refund claims can trigger penalties of up to 25% of unreported tax plus criminal prosecution under section 239 of the Income Tax Act and comparable provincial offences. BC PST audits may also claw back uncollected/undocumented PST of 7% plus interest and penalties under the BC Provincial Sales Tax Act. Any retroactive clawback generally begins from the date of the original zero-rated filing.

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a current account convertibility regime with unrestricted repatriation of software export proceeds; capital account transactions require Bank of Canada approval for investments exceeding CAD 100 million per year and are subject to foreign investment review.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if gross revenue ≥ CAD 50 million and cross-border related party transactions ≥ CAD 1 million (services) or ≥ CAD 5 million (other); Master File if gross revenue ≥ CAD 100 million and cross-border related party transactions ≥ CAD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking commercial substance. ULCs must maintain real economic activity and substance to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties up to 50% of tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under s.227.1; travel bans and asset freezes possible; personal bankruptcy may not discharge tax debts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment disallowing tax benefits; penalties up to 50% of tax avoided; potential treaty shopping denial under LOB provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for corporate debts if the company is deemed a sham; courts may pierce the veil for fraudulent or improper conduct.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal charges for directors.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for willful non-compliance; civil liability for damages.
