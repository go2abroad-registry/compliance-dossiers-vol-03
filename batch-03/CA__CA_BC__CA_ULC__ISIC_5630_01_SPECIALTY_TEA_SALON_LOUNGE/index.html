Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) via Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MyBA) at canada.ca; BC Provincial Sales Tax administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch via eTaxBC at gov.bc.ca/pst

E-government filing portal

A non-resident ULC carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online (Form RC1 or RC171 for non-resident simplified registration), then activate a GST/HST account, an import/export (RD) customs account, and, if applicable, a payroll (RP) account. GST/HST registration is mandatory once total taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive quarters, or immediately upon making taxable supplies of admitted goods or services performed in Canada. BC PST registration is required without any monetary threshold through eTaxBC for taxable on-premises retail sales of prepared tea beverages and food. The ULC certificate of incorporation and BC registered office particulars must accompany the application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists under the Canadian Income Tax Act: a BC ULC is per se a taxable Canadian corporation subject to federal Part I tax and provincial income tax. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the ULC may elect pass-through classification via IRS Form 8832 (check-the-box), creating a hybrid entity; such arrangements are constrained by ITA sections 18.4 to 18.7 (excessive interest and financing expenses) and section 212.3 (hybrid mismatch rules). The Canada-U.S. Tax Convention former Article IV(6) may apply to grandfathered ULCs. Other statutory elections relevant to a ULC include the ETA section 216 election for non-resident rental property income and the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration election under Form RC171.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter or in the last four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); non-residents making supplies of admission or services performed in Canada must register regardless of the threshold. BC PST: no registration threshold; any person making taxable sales of prepared beverages or food for immediate consumption must register and collect 7% PST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST/HST registrant may zero-rate exported goods under Schedule VI, Part V, section 1 where title transfers in Canada, the goods are exported without delay, and the registrant retains contemporaneous documentary evidence; packaged specialty loose-leaf tea sold for export qualifies. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered under the ETA are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7, unless the service is performed in Canada with the individual present, relates to real property situated in Canada, or relates to tangible personal property situated in Canada. In-store on-premises tea service remains taxable at 5% GST plus 7% BC PST.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CES), including the G7/G8 export declaration and export control number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or equivalent carrier Cargo Control Document evidencing physical removal from Canada
  • Commercial invoice describing the packaged tea goods sold for export
  • Packing list and warehouse dispatch records corroborating shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank remittance statement proving receipt of foreign consideration

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping, or transactional digital clearance mandates. GST/HST registrants above the electronic filing threshold must file through CRA electronic channels (GST/HST NETFILE), and electronic books and records are permitted subject to CRA record retention rules. The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PCTF) supports voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing, but there is no centralized government clearance database. BC PST returns are filed through eTaxBC on a monthly or quarterly basis without real-time machine-level stamping requirements.

CRA export zero-rating verification requires contemporaneous proof of export and receipt of foreign consideration; missing substantiation triggers reassessment of uncollected GST, denial of related input tax credits, and arrears interest. Knowingly false statements attract ETA section 285 penalties of CAD 250 plus 25% of the understated tax, and ETA section 327 evasion penalties of up to 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; ITA section 163(2) imposes 50% of the federal tax amount for repeated false statements. BC PST audits may impose penalties up to 25% of unreported tax plus interest under the Provincial Sales Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts pursuant to Canada's IMF Article VIII obligations; the Currency Act and the Financial Administration Act impose no exchange-control approvals, quotas, or central bank pre-screening. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject only to Part I corporate income tax and Part XIII withholding tax on dividends (statutory 25%, reduced to 5%-15% under the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention and other applicable treaties). Capital repatriation involving a disposition of taxable Canadian property by a non-resident may require a clearance certificate under Section 116 of the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or statutory timeline for export receipts; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts at their discretion, subject only to FINTRAC reporting obligations for cross-border EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) supervises federally chartered banks; the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers AML/CTF and cross-border EFT reporting. Canada maintains no standalone foreign-exchange control authority, and no treasury approval is required for capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada does not recognize the EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence; cross-border fintechs (e.g., Wise Solutions Inc., Payoneer) operate as money services businesses (MSBs) registered under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and licensed provincially under British Columbia's Money Services Businesses Act. Such platforms cannot extend insured deposit accounts or credit and must settle through a Schedule I bank; they are suitable only as supplementary disbursement rails, not as the primary treasury account for a BC ULC.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined Canadian federal and British Columbia general corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 12% BC); 9% combined rate for eligible CCPC small business profits up to CAD 500,000 (9% federal + 0% BC small business rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal period end. Corporate tax balance is due within 2 months after fiscal year-end for most corporations, and within 3 months for an eligible CCPC claiming the Small Business Deduction. Instalments are due quarterly for corporations whose prior-year federal and provincial tax payable exceeds CAD 3,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation equivalent to a Local File and Master File must be prepared for penalty protection where the corporation's gross revenue exceeds CAD 1,000,000 and the aggregate fair market value of cross-border non-arm's-length transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada and British Columbia do not impose Economic Substance Regulations comparable to offshore jurisdictions. However, economic substance is tested through the arm's-length principle in ITA section 247, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in ITA section 245, and DTA limitation-on-benefits and beneficial ownership provisions. A BC ULC with real staff, premises, inventory, governance and operational activity will satisfy Canadian substance expectations; a shell ULC arranged solely to access treaty benefits or shift profits will be recharacterized by CRA and may be denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Books and Records

Statutory sanction: ITA section 163(2) imposes a civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax. Criminal prosecution under ITA section 239 can result, on indictment, in a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years, or on summary conviction, a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in or authorize tax evasion can be charged as parties to the offence, personally fined or imprisoned, and ordered to pay restitution. The corporate veil may be pierced where the corporation is used as a facade for fraud.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust taxable income upward and assess a transfer pricing penalty under ITA section 247(3) of 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, subject to statutory de minimis and reasonable-efforts protection. Interest and provincial BC tax adjustments accrue on the deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for corporate TP penalties, but directors who sign false returns, knowingly misprice cross-border transactions, or direct fraudulent documentation face personal gross-negligence penalties, criminal liability, and potential director assessments if withholding obligations were also involved.
