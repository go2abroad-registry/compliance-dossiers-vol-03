Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Primary portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account (MBD) - https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; Customs export reporting through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned federal Business Corporation: (1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) via Corporations Canada online filing to obtain the federal corporation number; minimum 25% of directors must be resident Canadians, but 100% foreign shareholding is otherwise permitted. (2) Apply for the 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or BRO; activate program accounts: RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RM (import/export program account required for customs export reporting), and RP (payroll) where applicable. (3) GST/HST registration under the RT account is mandatory from the day the corporation's worldwide taxable supplies (including exports) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive quarters; voluntary registration is available. (4) Since the corporation may lack resident officers, appoint an authorized representative through CRA's 'Represent a Client' service for all electronic filings. The BN is the single identifier for income tax, GST/HST, customs and excise programs; a corporate bank account receives export remittances via SWIFT/TT, with an Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Canadian financial institution for audit evidence.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A resident Canadian corporation is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income at the federal general rate of 15% (after the provincial abatement and general rate reduction), with a federal small-business rate of 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, plus provincial corporate income tax allocated to each permanent establishment (e.g., Ontario M&P rate approximately 10%). Canada has a credit-method worldwide system: foreign branch income is taxable with foreign tax credits, and active foreign subsidiaries are generally exempt from Canadian tax on repatriated dividends (exempt surplus regime). No pass-through, C-Corp/S-Corp, or US-style check-the-box classification exists; dividends paid to shareholders attract a separate shareholder-level tax under the dividend gross-up and dividend tax credit mechanism. — For a foreign-owned federal Business Corporation: (1) Incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) via Corporations Canada online filing to obtain the federal corporation number; minimum 25% of directors must be resident Canadians, but 100% foreign shareholding is otherwise permitted. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A resident Canadian corporation is taxed under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income at the federal general rate of 15% (after the provincial abatement and general rate reduction), with a federal small-business rate of 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income, plus provincial corporate income tax allocated to each permanent establishment (e.g., Ontario M&P rate approximately 10%). Canada has a credit-method worldwide system: foreign branch income is taxable with foreign tax credits, and active foreign subsidiaries are generally exempt from Canadian tax on repatriated dividends (exempt surplus regime). No pass-through, C-Corp/S-Corp, or US-style check-the-box classification exists; dividends paid to shareholders attract a separate shareholder-level tax under the dividend gross-up and dividend tax credit mechanism.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under the Income Tax Act. Relevant statutory elections for a foreign-owned manufacturer: (i) ETA s. 156 joint election for closely related corporations, treating inter-company GST/HST taxable supplies as nil consideration; (ii) ITA s. 85 tax-deferred rollover election for transfers of qualifying property to the Canadian corporation; (iii) ETA s. 167(1) election for the transfer of a business as a going concern (zero-rated asset sale); (iv) ITA s. 216 election (net-basis filing) for non-resident owners of Canadian real property, and s. 217 election for non-residents earning Canadian employment/business income, to file Part I returns on net income; (v) hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 18.2-18.21 remain deferred by Budget 2024, with only the placeholder rule in ITA s. 18(4) currently operative for excessive interest and royalty deductions; (vi) Advance Pricing Arrangement (APA) with CRA for transfer pricing certainty on intercompany export pricing; (vii) for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, elective registration for the 15% global minimum top-up tax under ITA Part 12.4 (OECD Pillar Two), effective for fiscal years beginning after 31 December 2024.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1), measured on total worldwide taxable supplies of the corporation and associated persons in a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident carrying on business in Canada is subject to the same threshold, and once exceeded, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory for the RT program account).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST/HST) is available under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, Section 1 for a supply of tangible personal property made in Canada where: (a) the registrant supplier ships the goods to a destination outside Canada in the course of the supply, or (b) the goods are subsequently exported by or on behalf of the purchaser (typically within 60 days) and the supplier retains documentary proof of export. Surgical laparoscopy towers, ultra-HD 4K rigid endoscopes and components qualify as tangible personal property; shipment terms such as FOB origin or Ex Works with transfer of title to a non-resident purchaser before export preserve zero-rating. Services performed wholly outside Canada or provided in respect of the exported hardware to a non-resident non-registered person may be zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, Section 20 (subject to the exclusions for services relating to real property or goods in Canada at the time of performance). The manufacturer must be a GST/HST registrant, claim the zero rate on the GST/HST return, and retain complete export evidence in English or French for six years; CRA pre-approval is not required, but refunds are subject to post-assessment audit.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A filed electronically via CERS/G13 EDI, including the Export Legal Declaration and unique carrier code)
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list showing the foreign purchaser and Canadian exporter
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, airway, truck or rail waybill) evidencing delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Signed proof of delivery or customs-cleared arrival certificate from the foreign destination
  • SWIFT/TT Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Canadian bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Foreign customer purchase order or export contract demonstrating the non-resident counterparty
  • Customs broker instructions and carrier receipts documenting the physical movement of goods
  • Insurance certificate (optional) and certificate of origin (as required by the destination jurisdiction)

Canada does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or API-based invoice pre-clearance. The CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian E-Invoicing initiative based on the Peppol network, accessible through accredited access points, with a proposed phased mandate for large suppliers expected after 2026. GST/HST registrants must issue tax invoices (ETA ss. 223-224) containing the supplier's name, BN, invoice date, and tax amount, file GST/HST returns electronically (NETFILE or EDI), and retain books and records for six years (ETA s. 286). For exports, every shipment must be reported electronically through CBSA CERS (G13 EDI) using an Export Declaration (Form B13A) linked to the BN's RM program account, with an export reporting exclusion for low-value shipments of CAD 2,000 or less.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST return within 4 years of the filing due date (ETA s. 298), and the limitation period is unlimited where the registrant has made a misrepresentation, committed fraud or wilfully defaulted. If export evidence is deficient, proceeds are not repatriated within a reasonable commercial window (CRA audit benchmark typically 180 days for hard goods), or goods are diverted into the domestic market, the zero-rating is reversed and CRA will assess the full 5% federal GST (plus the applicable provincial HST component) retroactively on the export sale value, with compound daily prescribed interest from the original return due date. Penalties include: 25% of the net tax understatement for false statements or omissions (ETA s. 282), evasion penalties under ETA s. 283, failure-to-register penalties under ETA s. 281, and a 50% gross-negligence penalty on unreported income under ITA s. 163(2). Criminal prosecution is possible for wilful evasion. Export pricing is also reviewable under the ITA s. 247 transfer-pricing rules, with penalties of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment if the exporter cannot demonstrate arm's-length pricing documentation. The taxpayer bears the burden of proving that the goods both left Canada and were not redirected into the Canadian market; CRA memorandum GST/HST 7-2-2 (Exports) governs the documentation standard.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully convertible currency regime. Current account transactions including trade settlements and profit remittances are freely convertible without restriction. Capital account transactions are subject to liberalized rules with mandatory reporting to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) and the Foreign Account Reporting regime. Profit remittances by resident Canadian corporations face no convertibility or remittance hurdles beyond standard tax compliance, beneficial ownership disclosure, and anti-money laundering verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement for surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds in Canada. Exporters must report foreign exchange transactions to the Bank of Canada if the value exceeds CAD 10,000 per transaction under the Exchange Control Regulations, but no conversion ratios or surrender obligations apply to legitimate commercial trade transactions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if the aggregate value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million (for goods) or CAD 500,000 (for services, royalties, interest, etc.) in a fiscal year. Master File required if the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like some jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles apply. Transactions must have genuine economic substance; artificial arrangements designed to avoid tax are subject to reassessment and penalties. The CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act (s. 239): fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and full restitution of taxes owed plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (including withholding taxes) under section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act if they failed to exercise due diligence. Civil restitution, travel bans (via court orders), and potential imprisonment for criminal evasion.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment: denial of tax benefits, penalties of 25% of the tax benefit (section 163.2), and interest. CRA may also impose transfer pricing penalties (10% of the net adjustment) if documentation is inadequate.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for penalties if they participated in or acquiesced to the arrangement. Civil penalties and potential criminal charges for conspiracy to defraud the Crown.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) up to CAD 1 million for corporations per violation; criminal penalties for wilful non-compliance include fines up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for AMPs if they knowingly or negligently failed to comply. In severe cases, travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under court orders.
