Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html). For British Columbia corporate registration and provincial sales tax: BC Registrar of Companies via BC Corporate Online (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business) and BC Ministry of Finance eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must (1) incorporate a BC Unlimited Liability Corporation through the BC Registrar of Companies or register the entity as an extraprovincial/foreign corporation; (2) obtain a Canadian Business Number (BN) from CRA using form RC1 or online applications through My Business Account; (3) register for GST/HST through My Business Account once mandatory threshold is met or voluntarily to claim input tax credits; (4) obtain a CBSA importer/exporter number or carrier code and arrange customs suretyship if the entity is an exporter/importer of dangerous goods; (5) register for BC PST through the BC eTaxBC portal if making taxable sales in British Columbia. The BN serves as the federal TIN and GST/HST registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: For Canadian income tax purposes, a BC ULC is an entity-level taxpayer (a corporation under the Income Tax Act). For foreign tax purposes, particularly under U.S. check-the-box rules, the ULC may be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership, yielding hybrid-mismatch flexibility. Canadian taxation is based on residence and carrying on business in Canada, and the ULC is not a territorially exempt entity.

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic 'check-the-box' election. The relevant elections are: (i) a U.S. federal entity-classification election on IRS Form 8832 (check-the-box) designating the ULC as disregarded, partnership, or corporation; (ii) treaty tie-breaker and competent authority elections under the Canada-U.S. tax treaty for dual-residence conflicts; and (iii) Canadian hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act that impose adjustments for structured arrangements, imported mismatches, and payments whose tax treatment is affected by entity transparency. These rules prevent double non-taxation without creating a Canadian entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies for mandatory GST/HST registration; registration is required immediately after the small-supplier threshold is exceeded. BC PST has no general small-supplier threshold; PST registration is required before the seller makes taxable sales in BC. A BC ULC engaged in dangerous-goods freight forwarding typically registers for GST/HST and maintains a CBSA export/import account.

Canada imposes no national real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate, no continuous-transaction-control requirement, and no digital stamping. GST/HST invoices must contain prescribed particulars, including supplier name, BN, GST/HST registration number, invoice date, taxable amount, and tax charged. Electronic records and export support documents must be retained for at least six years. Cross-border cargo clearance is effected through electronic customs systems: CBSA eManifest, ACE (U.S.), and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). No government API clearance or XML stamping is mandatory for commercial invoices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII obligations; there are no convertibility impediments for equity contribution, profit repatriation, dividend declarations, or liquidation distributions from a British Columbia unlimited liability corporation (CA_ULC). Remittance friction is exclusively fiscal: non-resident shareholders may face Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced only when the hybrid-protective rules of the Canada-United States Fifth Protocol are satisfied) and, for a check-the-box transparent ULC, treaty benefits may be denied. The Investment Canada Act (notification/application for significant direct investment) and Statistics Canada FDI questionnaires are regulatory filings, not currency controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no surrender requirement, no repatriation time limit, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds of dangerous-goods forwarding; a CA_ULC may hold 100% of export receipts in CAD, USD, EUR, or any free currency offshore indefinitely. The only obligations are statistical: exporters must file export declarations with the Canada Border Services Agency for shipments valued at CAD 2,000 or more, and FINTRAC cross-border currency reports apply to monetary instruments in excess of CAD 10,000; none of these obligations requires conversion to Canadian dollars.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the monetary authority and supervisor of retail payment service providers, operating jointly with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT oversight, and the Department of Finance Canada for exchange-rate and foreign-investment policy. Canada has no statutory foreign-exchange control authority; no prior license or allocation certificate is required for cross-border payments, and the Canadian dollar is free-floating.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (complementary use only). A domestic Canadian payment fintech or EMI can serve as a non-bank settlement node for operational disbursements if it is a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business and, where applicable, registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Offshore digital EMIs without Canadian registration are legally prohibited from offering payment services to a Canadian-resident business; Canadian banks will not fund or accept funds from such unlicensed EMIs, and EMIs carry no Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation coverage. Capital escrow and equity injections must never pass through an EMI; those flows require a Schedule I bank account. The CA_ULC itself should not register as an MSB because its receipts are own-account payments for freight services, not the holding or transmitting of third-party funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For CAD settlement, the receiving bank clears through Lynx (Payments Canada's real-time gross settlement system); for USD settlement, the wire clears via Fedwire or CHIPS at the bank's US node and is converted internally. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is administratively required when the aggregate fair market value of all non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 1,000,000 for goods and services or CAD 5,000,000 for intangibles. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for consolidated multinational groups with annual revenue of at least CAD 1.14 billion (approx. EUR 750 million). Canada does not impose a separate statutory local-file or master-file filing to the CRA, but a taxpayer invoking the s.247 reasonable-efforts defense must produce complete and contemporaneous documentation within 6 months of fiscal year-end.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no formal Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to Gulf Cooperation Council jurisdictions. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245 Income Tax Act recharacterizes arrangements lacking genuine commercial substance, and common-law substance-over-form principles apply. For a BC ULC, the CRA may challenge a shell structure used solely to shift DG-forwarding profits to non-residents, reclassify intercompany payments as deemed dividends, and impose Part XIII liability. Canada's residency deeming rule in s.250(2) treats a corporation incorporated in Canada as resident in Canada, preventing a ULC from 'commuting' its Canadian tax residence despite non-resident management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): a fine of not less than 100% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years. Criminal Code s.380 adds fraud penalties where other deceit is involved.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who cause, authorize, or acquiesce in tax evasion can be prosecuted personally, assessed jointly with the ULC for unreported taxes, subjected to asset seizure, denial of bail on overseas means, and passport cancellation/travel bans pending criminal trial.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR under s.245(2), deny the avoided deduction or payment, deem the outflow as a dividend subject to 25% Part XIII withholding, and impose a 50% gross negligence penalty under s.163(2). The BC Registrar may also dissolve a ULC for filing false declarations of registered office or resident director.

Director personal liability: The CRA can pierce the veil where the ULC is a bare agent, making directors personally liable for unremitted Part XIII tax under s.227.1; directors who sign knowingly false returns face criminal liability and restitution orders, plus travel bans where forensic accounting flags asset dissipation.
AML / Money Laundering / Trade-Based Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Under PCMLTFA s.75.2, administrative monetary penalties can reach CAD 500,000 for a corporate entity and CAD 100,000 for an individual for non-compliance; criminal liability under Criminal Code s.462.31 carries imprisonment up to 10 years, forfeiture of proceeds, and disgorgement.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate trade-based money laundering may be charged as parties to the criminal offence, face extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties, be subject to interim travel bans, and incur civil restitution to the Crown; compliance officers who fail to file suspicious transaction reports expose themselves to FINTRAC prosecution and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.247(2) adjusts taxable income upward and denies the deduction; s.247(3) imposes a penalty equal to 10% of the aggregate adjustment, with no relief if fewer than the required contemporaneous documents exist; gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) may also be stacked.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise the overstated deduction are jointly and severally liable under s.227.1 for any Part XIII tax not withheld on the mispriced payment; they also risk criminal exposure for knowingly participating in a transfer-pricing scheme designed to evade Part I tax.
Failure to File / Information Return Defaults

Statutory sanction: Failure to file T106 attracts a penalty of CAD 100 per day up to CAD 2,500, plus reduced reasonable-efforts relief; late filing of T2 attracts 5%-10% escalating penalties plus monthly surcharges; CbC non-filing risks penalties up to CAD 12,500 and daily penalties of CAD 1,200 per calendar day. The Crown may add gross negligence penalties equal to 50% of any understated tax flowing from missing disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot avoid liability by claiming delegation: directors are personally liable for unpaid withholding and source deductions under s.227.1, and for knowingly failing to file required returns may be subject to prosecution, director liability orders, restitution to CRA, and, in aggravated money-laundering contexts, travel bans to prevent departure.
