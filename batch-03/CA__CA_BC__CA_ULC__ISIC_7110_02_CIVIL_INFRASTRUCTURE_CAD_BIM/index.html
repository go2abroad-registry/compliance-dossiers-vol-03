Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — GST/HST and Business Number (BN) programme. Portals: Business Registration Online (BRO) at www.canada.ca/business-registration, GST/HST NETFILE for electronic filing, and My Business Account (MyBA) for account administration.

E-government filing portal

The BC ULC must obtain a statutory 9-digit CRA Business Number (TIN) and a GST/HST program account (BN suffix 'RT') by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' via Business Registration Online (BRO), or by fax/mail to the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre. A non-resident ULC (deemed non-resident under ITA 250(5.1)) must identify its Canadian permanent establishment or appointed agent for service on Form RC1. Registration for GST/HST is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold (measured over the preceding four calendar quarters); voluntary registration is permitted from the first taxable supply and is strategically advantageous for zero-rated exporters because it enables full recovery of input tax credits. The CRA may require security from a non-resident registrant lacking a Canadian establishment (ETA 240).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold, ETA 148(1)); the threshold is measured over the preceding four calendar quarters and applies equally to non-resident suppliers without a Canadian establishment. Registration is mandatory once exceeded; voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 9 to a supply of a service made to a non-resident, provided the non-resident is not registered for GST/HST, does not carry on business in Canada at the relevant time, and the service is not performed in respect of (i) real property situated in Canada, (ii) tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or (iii) intangible personal property usable only in Canada. Offshore BIM and structural drafting deliverables transmitted electronically to a non-resident EPC client qualify for zero-rating under section 9. Input tax credits on Canadian inputs (software subscriptions, CAD workstations, office overhead) are fully recoverable by a registrant whose supplies are zero-rated, generating net refunds. The BC ULC, whether resident or deemed non-resident under ITA 250(5.1), must obtain and retain evidence of the client's non-resident status.

  • Signed export services contract / Statement of Work specifying BIM and structural drafting deliverables for offshore civil infrastructure projects
  • Commercial invoice annotated 'Zero-rated export pursuant to ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.9'
  • Non-resident status evidence of the client (foreign tax identification number, tax residence certificate, or statutory declaration of non-residence)
  • Payment evidence: SWIFT MT103 wire transfer receipt or Wise business payment confirmation
  • Deliverable transmission records (digital handoff logs, IP transmission receipts, download records for CAD/BIM files)
  • GST/HST returns and input tax credit working papers substantiating zero-rating and ITC allocation

None. Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government API invoicing, or digital stamping. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed information (ETA 223), but invoices are neither pre-cleared nor validated by the CRA. Electronic filing of GST/HST returns is mandatory for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million, with monthly or quarterly reporting periods. Peppol-based e-invoicing is voluntary and not integrated with CRA fiscal clearance.

Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for export proceeds; zero-rating eligibility is not contingent on repatriation timing. The CRA reassessment window is 4 years (ETA 298), extended to 7 years for neglect, misrepresentation, or fraud. If zero-rating is denied (for example, the client is found to be GST-registered, carrying on business in Canada, or services relate to Canadian realty), the CRA assesses 5% GST on the full supply, disallows related ITCs, and charges arrears interest (ETA 280). Penalties include 50% of the tax sought to be evaded for knowingly or grossly negligently making a false statement (ETA 285); fines up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment, or CAD 25,000 and up to 2 years on summary conviction, for evading GST (ETA 327-328); and director liability for unremitted amounts (ETA 323). Books and records substantiating export zero-rating must be retained for 6 years (ETA 286).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a freely convertible currency under the IMF Article VIII framework; Canada maintains no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. For the British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation, the principal converting-and-remittance hurdle is not FX regulation but the income tax characterization of the entity and potential Part XIII withholding tax on certain non-resident payments, specifically dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees. The ULC's pass-through eligibility must be managed so that repatriation of contributed surplus and return of capital is clearly documented as non-taxable, while any deemed or actual dividend is reconciled against transactional creditors and CRA filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist under Canadian law. Export revenue generated by the CAD-BIM services may be held in USD or other convertible foreign currency, converted through commercial banking channels, or applied directly against foreign-currency supplier and contractor liabilities. The ULC must nevertheless preserve source-based invoicing, SWIFT narrative references, and bank statements that demonstrate such proceeds are genuine earned professional fees rather than unexplained inbound payments.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC); foreign-exchange monitoring and anti-money-laundering surveillance administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); prudential oversight of federally regulated banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. A fully regulated cross-border fintech or electronic money institution is a supplement only and cannot replace a Canadian bank for the CA_ULC because corporate account opening, Lynx membership, and credible treasury clearing require a Canadian bank or credit union. Services such as Wise are viable for low-value vendor disbursements, employee reimbursements, and micro FX conversions, provided the fintech holds a FINTRAC money-services-business registration, maintains customer funds segregated, and can produce transaction reconciliation exports. Offshore digital EMIs that lack FINTRAC registration must not be used, as such usage materially increases AML-KYC blocking risk and may justify a bank account termination by a Schedule I lender.
