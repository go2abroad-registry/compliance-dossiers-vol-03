Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada treats a BC/AB ULC as a corporation and taxes it at the entity level under the Income Tax Act, but the ULC is 'pass-through eligible' for U.S. federal tax purposes under Treasury Reg. §301.7701-3: a qualifying foreign unlimited liability company may file Form 8832 (Entity Classification Election) to be classified as a disregarded entity (single member) or partnership (multi-member). Canadian anti-hybrid rules (ITA s. 18.4, s. 18.5, and s. 247(2)) will deny deductions or adjust amounts where hybrid mismatch arrangements exploit differing entity classifications between Canada and the ULC's jurisdiction. GST/HST elections include the Quick Method election (ETA s. 16.1, limited to small suppliers with taxable supplies not exceeding CAD 400,000 annually) and the election by a registrant to rebate/recovery under ETA s. 232. Non-resident owners may elect under ITA s. 216 to file a net-basis return for rental income derived from Canadian real estate. Partnerships can elect a non-calendar fiscal period under ITA s. 249.1.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD $30,000 in global taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter and across the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (small supplier threshold); once exceeded, mandatory GST/HST registration is required regardless of residency. BC PST: no small-supplier threshold exists; registration is mandatory once a vendor makes taxable retail sales into British Columbia, including remote/electronic supply where the vendor is deemed to be carrying on business in the province. Non-resident digital-service suppliers and platform operators may also face nexus-triggered registration under the ETA place-of-supply rules for intangible property and services performed in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA s. 6 and Schedule VI Part V, a supply of goods is zero-rated where the GST/HST registrant exports the goods 'as soon as may be' after the supply is made, generally within a reasonable post-sale period (commonly benchmarked at 60 days) and retains qualifying export evidence. For services, zero-rating applies under Schedule VI, Part V (s. 7) to supplies made to a non-resident recipient provided the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, is not performed wholly or substantially in Canada for an individual present in Canada, and is not supplied to a non-resident's permanent establishment in Canada. Fractional CFO advisory, bookkeeping, and multicurrency ledger services rendered to a non-resident client on a cross-border basis ordinarily qualify for the 0% GST/HST rate; the supplier must nevertheless substantiate the non-resident status of the client and the foreign situs of the underlying services. A registered exporter may claim input tax credits for inputs attributable to zero-rated export supplies and file an ETA s. 262 refund/rebate claim where the taxable amount exceeds allowable credits.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA CAED electronic export declaration or Canada Customs Coding Form B3)
  • Bill of Lading (or straight bill, air waybill, or courier docket evidencing physical export movement)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank credit advice / SWIFT confirmation evidencing actual receipt of proceeds from the non-resident client)

Canada does not impose a government fiscal pre-clearance, digital stamping, or real-time invoice approval regime akin to Latin American RTM systems. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD $1.5 million (ETA s. 273.1 and related regulations) and electronic T2 corporate income tax filing for corporations whose gross revenue exceeds CAD $10 million (ITA s. 150.1). E-invoicing operates on a voluntary national PEPPOL framework adopted by the Government of Canada; no API-based tax authority clearance is mandated. BC PST returns must be electronically filed through eTaxBC. Export documentation supporting zero-rated lines must be retained for CRA inspection, but no pre-approval of invoice batches by the tax authority is required.

CRA's GST/HST and International & Large Business Audit divisions routinely scrutinize export zero-rating claims, particularly for service-based exporters lacking physical customs declarations. The normal reassessment window is four years from the filing date; the window extends to ten years where a filer has made misrepresentations, committed fraud, or supplied false statements. Civil clawback: CRA may reassess to disallow the zero-rating, collect uncollected GST/HST, and assess arrears interest under ITA s. 161/ETA s. 280. Civil penalties include: (i) ETA s. 285.1 false-statement penalty equal to the greater of CAD $100 and 25% of the tax loss; (ii) ITA s. 163.2 gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the federal tax understated where export transactions are part of an aggressive arrangement; and (iii) repeated failure to register/remit penalties under ETA s. 280 and ITA s. 227/237.1, including director/owner liability under ETA s. 323 and ITA s. 227.1 for net tax remittance failures. Criminal exposure arises under ETA s. 327 and ITA s. 239(1) for wilful evasion: fines up to 200% of the tax evaded plus imprisonment of up to two years, and CRA may additionally impose gross-negligence penalties on advisers who counsel fraudulent export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no exchange controls, no Central Bank conversion registry, no repatriation licensing, and no onshore/offshore segmentation. Profit remittance from the CA_ULC is unrestricted at the wire level, but Part XIII of the Income Tax Act imposes a statutory 25% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents. The Canada-US tax treaty reduces this to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for a corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of voting stock, but under the 2018 protocol hybrid rules the ULC must carefully verify treaty eligibility where the US owner has elected check-the-box flow-through treatment, because an Article IV(6) denial can restore the full 25% rate. No exchange commission, no conversion quota, and no volume limitation applies to CAD/USD/EUR/GBP settlement for a services exporter; capital and current account transactions are equally free of administrative pre-approval except where OECD/EU sanctions screening or the Investment Canada Act national-security review applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts. The BC ULC may retain 100% of its foreign-currency export proceeds in any OSFI-regulated bank account and may convert them at market rates at its sole discretion. The only binding obligations are (i) FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting by the receiving and sending financial institution for any cross-border wire equal to or exceeding CAD $10,000, (ii) the annual T1135 Foreign Income Verification Statement where specified foreign property exceeds CAD $100,000, and (iii) GST/HST accounting on taxable cross-border supplies of advisory services. No Foreign Exchange Administration return, no export certification, and no central bank consent is required before converting or deploying export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the sole independent central monetary and currency authority; it issues legal-tender Canadian bank notes under the Currency Act, manages Canada's official international reserves, and operates reserve-management FX intervention policy. There is no standalone Foreign Exchange Administration; functional supervision is distributed among OSFI (prudential licensing and capital adequacy of authorized banks), Department of Finance Canada (sanctions, investment review, and policy), Global Affairs Canada (export controls), and FINTRAC (cross-border electronic funds transfer surveillance). A BC ULC must therefore satisfy Bank of Canada policy expectations indirectly through its OSFI-regulated banking counterparty and file no direct FX control application.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High as a supplementary receivable and disbursement rail; Low as the primary treasury rail. Canada does not issue an Electronic Money Institution licence; Wise Canada Ltd., Payoneer, and similar platforms operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, not as chartered banks. The ULC may lawfully open a Wise Business account to collect client invoices in CAD/USD/GBP/EUR and to pay remote sub-contractors, but treasury-grade standards require: (i) float balances capped below CAD $100,000 equivalent per platform; (ii) immediate daily sweep-back to a Schedule I bank account; (iii) no client-fund custody or fiduciary wording in the service contract; (iv) explicit disclosure that MSB balances are not CDIC-insured; and (v) a written policy banning crypto-to-fiat bridge rails except for pre-approved payments subject to enhanced due diligence. Stripe Billing is the merchant/card presentment rail, Wise is the low-value settlement rail, and the Schedule I bank remains the only legal counterparty for hedging, credit, and irrevocable payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Because the BC ULC has zero minimum paid-up capital, the preferred inbound rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident shareholder to the ULC's Schedule I CAD operating account. The wire must carry a narrative identifying the transfer as 'Share Subscription - CA_ULC_BV' and reference the share-purchase resolution; the bank's internal purpose code is typically 'TVC - Transfer of shares of an enterprise' or the aggregate commercial code 'B107 - Other business/capital contribution' depending on the institution's SWIFT code book. For amounts above CAD $100,000, the receiving bank will require a signed subscription agreement, a copy of the board resolution authorizing the share class, and the BC summary corporate register before releasing funds. All inbound international EFTs of CAD $10,000 or more are captured by the bank's FINTRAC reporting stream, but no pre-clearance approval is needed. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High as a supplementary receivable and disbursement rail; Low as the primary treasury rail. Canada does not issue an Electronic Money Institution licence; Wise Canada Ltd., Payoneer, and similar platforms operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, not as chartered banks. The ULC may lawfully open a Wise Business account to collect client invoices in CAD/USD/GBP/EUR and to pay remote sub-contractors, but treasury-grade standards require: (i) float balances capped below CAD $100,000 equivalent per platform; (ii) immediate daily sweep-back to a Schedule I bank account; (iii) no client-fund custody or fiduciary wording in the service contract; (iv) explicit disclosure that MSB balances are not CDIC-insured; and (v) a written policy banning crypto-to-fiat bridge rails except for pre-approved payments subject to enhanced due diligence. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined federal and British Columbia general corporate rate for a non-CCPC BC ULC with non-resident shareholders (15% federal Part I general rate reduction + 12% BC provincial corporate tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax under s.212(2) ITA on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian-resident BC ULC to non-resident shareholders. If the ULC validly elects partnership treatment and all shareholders are Canadian residents, distributions are partnership allocations and do not attract Part XIII withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate annual fair-market-value of non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents triggers mandatory Form T106 and contemporaneous documentation under s.247 ITA. Master File and Local File documentation are expected for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above C$50 million, aligned with the OECD transfer-pricing guidelines adopted by CRA.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not enforce a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute; instead, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA) and common-law substance-over-form principles invalidate transactions that lack genuine economic purpose. The BC ULC must demonstrate real operational substance: contracted staff, actual performance of virtual CFO services, bookkeeping deliverables, business premises or co-working lease, own bank accounts, and physical records. Absent substance, the CRA can recharacterise a ULC as a shell, deny tax losses, disallow expense deductions, and impose GAAR penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Income Tax Act

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239 ITA: imprisonment up to 5 years, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, or both; civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) equal to 50% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus interest and full reassessment within 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for corporate tax debts, penalties, and interest under s.160 and s.227.1 when the ULC fails to remit. Criminal liability extends to a 'directing mind' who authorises evasion; courts can impose a travel ban, asset freeze, and personal bankruptcy via restitution orders.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Part XIII Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: The ULC is jointly and severally liable for the full withheld amount under s.227(8); penalty of 10% of the unreported amount under s.227(10) plus prescribed interest compounding daily; a tax lien may be registered against all ULC property.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the entire unremitted withholding amount, interest, and penalties under s.227.1. Personal property of directors can be seized, and directors face travel restrictions where the CRA pursues a departure prohibition order under s.226.1.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty under s.247(3) equal to 10% of the gross transfer-pricing adjustment, applied on top of the tax reassessment; reassessment window extends indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, or seven years for ordinary reassessment. There is no cap in dollars.

Director personal liability: Directors risk personal liability for filing a false or misleading T2 return that underpins a transfer-pricing adjustment; they can be assessed under s.163(2) gross negligence penalties and held personally responsible for the ULC's unpaid tax where they signed or certified false information. Travel bans may be imposed under serious tax-fraud enforcement.
Shell Company and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, unregistered money-service business penalties reach up to C$1 million per violation and C$500,000 for each subsequent offence; the BC Registry can dissolve a non-complying ULC; a court may liquidate the asset base and restrict the entity from obtaining contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally amenable for AML fines where they directed or participated in the breach, up to C$1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years for knowingly executing money-laundering transactions; a travel ban and seizure of passports is standard in serious federal money-laundering enforcement proceedings; the corporate veil is pierced entirely in such matters.
