Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and income tax registration and administration; My Business Account portal. British Columbia Ministry of Finance — Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration, filing, and remittance administration via eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the BC Unlimited Liability Company through BC Corporate Online and obtain the BC incorporation or registry file number. 2) Apply to CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) via Form RC1, the New Business Registration Online service, or My Business Account. 3) Register for the GST/HST program account under the same BN; GST/HST registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single quarter or in the trailing four calendar quarters, and voluntary registration is available. 4) Register for BC PST with the BC Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC before making PST-taxable sales or providing PST-taxable services in BC; there is no small-supplier threshold for PST registration other than engaging in taxable activity. 5) The CRA Business Number plus program account code (RT for GST/HST) serves as the statutory tax identifier; Canada does not issue a separate VAT ID.

Available election forms

For Canadian federal income tax purposes, a BC ULC is a resident corporation subject to entity-level tax under the Income Tax Act; CRA does not recognize domestic check-the-box or general pass-through elections. However, for U.S. federal tax classification purposes, a wholly-owned BC ULC may be eligible for U.S. check-the-box treatment as a disregarded entity or partnership if an effective election under Treasury Regulations Section 301.7701-3 is made by the U.S. owner. The ULC may use ordinary Canadian elections such as section 85 asset rollovers, qualifying amalgamations, continuance elections, and related-party loss utilization elections to the extent available. Hybrid mismatch and foreign branch measures under Income Tax Act sections 18.4 and 91.6 must be assessed where dual classification creates a hybrid instrument or payment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian Dollar (CAD) is freely floating and fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act (1951) and Canada's acceptance of IMF Article VIII status, there are no exchange controls, pre-approvals, or repatriation restrictions. Non-resident shareholders of a BC ULC may remit dividends, management fees, interest, and sale proceeds freely in CAD or foreign currency. Part XIII withholding tax applies to certain outbound payments (typically 25% statutory; 5%–15% treaty-reduced under the Canada–US Tax Convention), and for a ULC owned by a US disregarded-entity parent, distributions can be structured as return of paid-up capital to reduce withholding leakage. The only practical hurdles are FINTRAC large-transaction reporting (CAD 10,000 or more) and bank-level source-of-funds verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation deadline for export or service-fee receipts. A BC ULC earning UN38.3 drop-testing and CE-RED laboratory fees may retain foreign currency in CAD or USD bank accounts indefinitely. However, under the PCMLTFA: (i) every foreign currency exchange transaction over CAD 3,000 must be recorded; (ii) every incoming/outgoing wire transfer of CAD 10,000 or more triggers a FINTRAC EFT report; and (iii) banks may internally require the underlying testing contract and invoice to substantiate trade-related flows. Practical treasury policy: invoice in CAD or USD with clear PO numbers, and reconcile each inbound wire to a matched test-report deliverable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) under the Bank of Canada Act and Currency Act, with the Department of Finance Canada holding the Exchange Fund Account; banking prudential supervision by OSFI; anti-money-laundering supervision by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: HIGH as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail, but LOW as a primary operating bank. Wise (a FINTRAC-registered MSB in Canada) provides a multi-currency business account in the ULC's name with CAD, USD, and EUR balances, and offers CAD direct-deposit routing details via a partner Canadian-chartered arrangement. Legal constraints: Wise is not a Canadian bank under the Bank Act, holds no CDIC deposit insurance, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, cannot provide overdrafts or Lynx/ACSS cheque clearing, and may impose enhanced due diligence on transactions above CAD 10,000. Recommended use: inbound client collections from foreign battery manufacturers under CAD 10,000 per transaction, and low-value supplier payments, with all treasury-level flows (capital injection, payroll, LC settlements) executed through a Tier-1 Canadian bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 100,000 aggregate non-arm's length cross-border transactions

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); substance is assessed through GAAR (ITA s.245), OECD treaty limitation-of-benefits and principal-purpose provisions, and transfer-pricing entity recognition tests. A BC ULC that functions as a pass-through entity for US/Canada tax purposes remains a Canadian resident corporation for T2 filing and must maintain bona fide premises, employees, and core risk-bearing operations to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Wilful Defalcation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239: offence punishable by summary conviction – fine up to 200% of tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years; indictment – fine up to 200% of tax sought and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil reassessments and 50% gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly assisting or acquiescing in evasion are subject to piercing the corporate veil – joint and several liability for assessed tax, penalties, and interest; CRA can issue a director's liability letter under s.227.1 for withheld taxes; criminal conviction may trigger travel bans and apprehension on red notices.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(3): penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment if the taxpayer did not make 'reasonable efforts' to determine arm's-length terms; related adjustments to taxable income, interest, and indirect benefits; T106 filing failure subject to informational penalties up to CAD 8,000 per unreported transaction under s.162(7).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of knowing false transfer-pricing documents may be assessed personally as authorizers; CRA may sue under civil restitution for unpaid corporate tax using alter ego/agent theories; no automatic veil pierce, but threatened criminal investigation and asset restraints are possible.
Shell Company / No Real Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA can disregard corporate existence under ITA s.20(1)(bb), s.67, s.248(1) or apply GAAR (s.245) to recharacterize income to the US principal; transfer-pricing rules may reallocate all Canadian-source revenue to the actual economic parent; no valid business purpose invokes judicial sham doctrine invalidating the whole structuring.

Director personal liability: If a no-arms and no-wallet shell, courts lift the veil and hold directors liable for all unrealized trade debts, bank overdrafts, and unpaid GST/T2 balances; cross-border collection may include BC Supreme Court charging orders and asset-freezing injunctions; non-resident directors face potential travel restrictions upon entering Canada (civil arrest warrants for judgment enforcement).
AML / Unregistered Money-Service Business

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): administrative monetary penalty up to CAD 500,000 for a serious non-compliance by a reporting entity (up to CAD 2 million for systemic non-compliance); criminal sanctions for knowingly laundering proceeds up to 10 years imprisonment; authorities may require external audit.

Director personal liability: Directors of a non-registered entity can be personally penalized for failure to report large cash transactions or suspicious transactions if they directed or influenced the activity; travel restrictions and denial of beneficiary ownership hidden behind ULC unlimited liability.
