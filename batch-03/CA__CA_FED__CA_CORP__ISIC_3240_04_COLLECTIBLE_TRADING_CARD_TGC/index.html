Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; administration of corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act and of GST/HST under the Excise Tax Act; front-end registration portals are CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and 'My Business Account' (MyBA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business or making taxable supplies in Canada must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) as its statutory tax identifier and must open a GST/HST program account with the CRA; streamlined registration is available through the BRO web service, by Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts), or for non-resident corporations with no Canadian physical presence by Form RC460 (GST/HST Registration Application for Non-Resident Businesses), which additionally requires designation of a Canadian agent/resident representative acceptable to the CRA and provision of evidence of Canadian legal address, director information, and bank account details.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a federally or provincially incorporated Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer; if resident in Canada for tax purposes, it is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income and is not eligible for pass-through treatment; Canada applies a quasi-territorial outcome through the foreign accrual property income regime, foreign tax credits, and the foreign affiliate exempt surplus rules, but the statutory base remains worldwide.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; tax identity is fixed by corporate law and residence. Available material elections include: the Part I.3 and subsection 150(2) elections for corporate return bases, the section 216 election for non-resident net rental income, the subsection 115(2) election for treaty-protected business income of non-residents, the GST/HST section 273 joint election for closely related corporations, and the subsection 148(?) exempt-loss/reorganization elections; hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized under Canada's BEPS Action 2 rules and anti-hybrid amendments (sections 18.2, 18.3, 12.5, and 12.6 of the Income Tax Act).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Goods and Services Tax (GST) / Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) — federal GST is 5%; in participating provinces HST (13% in Ontario, 15% in Nova Scotia/PEI/New Brunswick/Newfoundland and Labrador) is administered by the CRA as a single federal collection regime; Quebec applies QST separately.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a fully free-floating Canadian dollar and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible without exchange-control approval, separate capital licences, or currency allocation quotas. Profit remittance, dividends, interest, royalties, and capital repatriation are not blocked by FX rules; the material legal constraint is Income Tax Act Part XIII withholding tax on cross-border payments to non-residents, and payments must satisfy FINTRAC AML, sanctions, and suspicious-transaction duties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory exchange-settlement requirement, and no forced conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency, hold it in Canadian chartered bank foreign-currency accounts, or convert it by ordinary commercial negotiation with their bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); Canada does not maintain a dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration. The Department of Finance Canada manages official international reserves, while the Bank of Canada supplies monetary policy and payment-system settlement infrastructure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, but only as a supplemental payment rail. Canada does not recognize the European EMBI/e-money institution licensing model; offshore digital EMIs cannot take retail or corporate deposits or issue e-money as deposit liabilities without an authorization under the Bank Act. A foreign fintech may operate in Canada as a FINTRAC-registered money services business providing FX dealing or remittance services, and may be subject to the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act registration and operational standards. The primary cross-border settlement route for this entity should remain a federally regulated Schedule I commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital can be wired in CAD or USD via SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian corporate operating account. Canada has no central-bank approval requirement and no statutory wire-purpose-code taxonomy; the remittance information must state the corporate legal name, transaction reference, and explicit purpose such as 'SUBSCRIBE FOR COMMON SHARES / CA_CORP CAP-001' or 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO CA_CORP.' High-value CAD payments settle through Payments Canada's Lynx RTGS system; USD payments clear through correspondent banks on CHIPS or Fedwire. The Canadian receiving bank will verify the corporate registration package, UBO declaration, source of funds, and the bank's own sanctions screening. If the inbound equity injection also creates foreign direct investment control or an acquisition of a Canadian business, a separate Investment Canada Act notification/review may be required before closing; otherwise no foreign-exchange authority consent is required.For ordinary operating and trade payables, use SWIFT MT103 for international USD/EUR wires and Lynx/ACH rails for domestic CAD payments. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal general corporate tax rate after the General Rate Reduction; a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) pays an effective 9% on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income via the Small Business Deduction)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII (ITA s. 212(2)) on dividends paid or credited to a non-resident of Canada; treaty relief typically reduces the rate to 15% for portfolio dividends and to 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of the voting shares (e.g., under the Canada-United States Tax Convention).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing: no later than 6 months after the end of the taxation year. Final balance due: 2 months after the taxation year-end for most corporations; 3 months for a CCPC claiming the Small Business Deduction (ITA s. 157(2)). Instalments: monthly or quarterly according to the CRA instalment schedule, with no statutory extension.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate specified cross-border non-arm's-length transactions in a taxation year triggers the requirement to prepare contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R). The ITA s. 247(3) penalty of 10% of the understated taxable income applies where the corporation's gross revenue exceeds CAD 5,000,000 and documentation is not prepared before the filing due date; a failure-to-provide-documentation penalty of CAD 1,000 per month (maximum CAD 24,000) applies under s. 247(6).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s. 245) to disallow avoidance transactions lacking genuine economic substance, business purpose, or topological integrity; treaty shopping, circular financings, and hybrid mismatch arrangements are prime targets. Canadian corporate residency is determined by central management and control, requiring the Canadian board to exercise substantive strategic decision-making rather than formal ratification of foreign-parent directions. Transfer-pricing substance requires that the Canadian entity bears the actual entrepreneurial risk, performs the claimed functions, and is capitalized consistently with the arm's-length principle; CRA auditors will collapse arrangements lacking commercial reality.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years. Indictable offence: fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years (ITA s. 239).

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and employees who knowingly sign or assent to a false return are personally liable to the same fines and imprisonment; courts may impose restitution orders in favor of the CRA, confiscation of proceeds, and travel bans prohibiting departure from Canada pending trial. The corporate veil provides no shield in criminal prosecution.
Gross Negligence in Filing (Civil Penalty)

Statutory sanction: 50% penalty of the total additional tax assessable for the year under ITA s. 163(2), in addition to reassessment of tax and daily compound interest; no reasonable-efforts defence applies unless the taxpayer prepared contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors are generally not personally liable for a mere civil gross-negligence penalty, but directors who authorized or acquiesced in the false filing may be assessed under ITA s. 323 for the corporation's unpaid tax if the corporate entity is insolvent and the amounts remain unremitted.
Intercompany Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment under ITA s. 247(2); 10% penalty of the understated taxable income where gross revenue exceeds CAD 5,000,000 and documentation is absent (s. 247(3)); CAD 1,000-per-month documentation penalty (maximum CAD 24,000) under s. 247(6); daily interest on the reassessed balance.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for transfer-pricing reassessments, but deliberate documentation failures or sham pricing arrangements can escalate to tax-evasion charges under s. 239; ITA s. 160 permits recovery from related parties, including the foreign parent, for assets transferred without fair consideration.
General Anti-Avoidance Rule / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of the tax benefit and re-characterization of the transaction under ITA s. 245; the CRA reassesses according to the economic substance; a 50% gross-negligence penalty may be added under s. 163(2); interest runs from the original statutory due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute a sham or conduit structure may be personally assessed under s. 323 for unremitted tax and may face Criminal Code s. 380 fraud charges, restitution orders, and judicial travel bans; the CRA also applies cross-border information exchange (BEPS MLI) to pierce the veil of conduit entities.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Reporting (Proceeds of Crime)

Statutory sanction: Hybrid offence: on summary conviction, a fine up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months; on indictment, a fine up to CAD 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; corporate officers can be separately prosecuted under PCMLTFA s. 75.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be charged personally for directing, authorizing, or acquiescing in AML reporting failures; the corporate veil is not a defence; courts may order forfeiture of tainted property and impose travel restrictions. Payment processors such as Stripe and Authorize.Net will typically suspend access upon FINTRAC enforcement action.
Unremitted Withholding Taxes and Payroll Remittances

Statutory sanction: Immediate assessment of arrears against the corporation; 10% penalty for failure to withhold or remit under ITA s. 227(8); daily compound interest on all arrears; criminal prosecution for wilful non-remittance with fines of 50% to 200% of the amount and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted source deductions and Part XIII tax under ITA s. 227.1 where the corporation fails to pay and the due-diligence defence cannot be established; the CRA may enforce against personal assets, place liens on property, cancel the Business Number, seek court-ordered asset freezes, and impose travel bans. Imprisonment is a direct personal consequence of wilful remittance fraud.
