Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — federal tax and GST/HST administration, transacted through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and the My Business Account (MyBA) portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency).

E-government filing portal

A Business Corporation (federal or provincial) is registered for the nine-digit Business Number (BN) upon incorporation, either via Corporations Canada online filing or CRA Form RC1. The BN is the sole statutory tax identifier; the entity must add an RC program account for corporate income tax, an RT program account for GST/HST (Form RC1 or GST Form 500 for non-residents), and an RM program account for import/export, the latter being mandatory to transmit export control data through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). The registration certificate must name the legal entity exactly as incorporated and include a Canadian address or agent for service, which the CRA also accepts for non-resident exporters.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxable person under ss. 2 and 45 of the Income Tax Act, without pass-through eligibility; income accruing to the corporation for the year is taxed at the entity level under Part I, then again at the shareholder level under the dividend regime (Part XIII withholding for non-residents), and no check-the-box mechanism exists to convert the corporation into a fiscally transparent vehicle for Canadian tax purposes.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or CFE election is recognized by the CRA. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST joint election for closely related corporations (Excise Tax Act s. 156) to reduce GST/HST between related entities; (ii) Income Tax Act s. 216 net-basis election for non-residents deriving Canadian rents or royalties; (iii) treaty-based branch tax mitigation under Part XIV and applicable income tax conventions; (iv) proposed anti-hybrid rules plus the general anti-avoidance rule under s. 245 to recharacterize export financing structures; and (v) an administrative election to adopt a non-calendar taxation year under Income Tax Regulations s. 1502 for the first fiscal period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — worldwide taxable supplies made by the entity and associated entities over the current or preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; below this threshold the supplier is a small supplier but may voluntarily register to zero-rate export supplies and recover input tax credits. Mandatory GST/HST registration applies to non-resident suppliers as of July 1, 2021 where taxable supplies in Canada exceed the threshold or where the non-resident is deemed a supplier through an e-commerce marketplace or fulfilment arrangement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim the 0% GST/HST rate on export sales of manufactured polyhedral dice, the producer must hold a valid GST/HST registration (BN with RT account), deliver the goods to a destination outside Canada in a continuous unbroken chain, ordinarily within 60 days after the supply and before any Canadian use, and maintain contemporaneous evidence of physical export. Related services (e.g., game design and prototyping services) are zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Canada or reasonably ancillary to the exported goods. No statutory FX repatriation deadline exists for zero-rating purposes, but CRA audit practice treats remittance received within 180 days as supporting the commercial reality of the export sale.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration transmitted through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), or an approved equivalent for below-threshold and low-value shipments
  • Original or certified Bill of Lading / Master Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing departure of the goods from Canada and a foreign destination
  • Inward remittance or payment settlement records (bank wire, PayPal/Stripe payout reports) together with a fully executed export sales contract or commercial invoice

Canada does not operate real-time tax authority clearance, digital fiscal stamping, or API-based e-invoicing validation for GST/HST. However, Excise Tax Act ss. 223 and 286 require suppliers to issue invoices containing the supplier's BN, date, purchaser name, description, GST/HST rate and tax amount, and to retain electronic invoices and supporting books and records in an audit-ready digital format for six years. Since July 2021, online payment facilitators and marketplace platforms (including Kickstarter, Stripe and PayPal rails) may be treated as deemed suppliers required to collect and remit GST/HST on facilitated supplies unless the underlying vendor holds a valid BN and GST/HST registration.

The CRA may deny zero-rating, disallow input tax credits, and claw back the corresponding refund with compound interest where the exporter fails to produce the documentation bundle described above. The normal four-year reassessment period is extendable indefinitely for misrepresentation, neglect or fraud. Civil penalties include up to 10% of the net tax avoided, false-declaration penalties of 5% to 10% of tax sought to be evaded, and per-day record-retention penalties of CAD 250. Criminal conviction under Excise Tax Act s. 239(1) for knowingly making false or misleading export declarations carries fines up to 400% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years; directors may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid GST/HST and Part XIII amounts where the corporate structure is used to conceal export activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under a floating exchange-rate regime. Canada imposes no exchange-control approvals for profit remittances, dividend declarations, interest payments, royalties, or repatriation of equity capital. Non-resident withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act apply at the point of remittance, but there is no FX-related administrative blocking. Inward direct investment and outward payments are subject to standard AML recordkeeping and threshold reporting, not to currency convertibility rationing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not maintain a statutory export-proceeds surrender regime. Exporters and commercial corporations may retain export receipts in any permitted currency, and conversion into Canadian dollars is a voluntary market transaction. FINTRAC requires reporting of international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and cross-border currency movement of CAD 10,000 or more must be disclosed per the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. No percentage-based mandatory conversion or repatriation timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (in coordination with the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada for AML/financial-intelligence matters)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low direct viability. Canada does not recognize EU/UK EMI passports or the EMI licensing regime; a foreign electronic-money institution cannot act as a primary clearing bank unless it becomes a registered Money Services Business under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and, where applicable, obtains a retail payment services registration under the Retail Payments Activities Act. Settlement funds must ultimately sit with a Schedule I Canadian bank or a Payments Canada participant. Fintech gateway rails can be used for initiation and reconciliation, but the legally protected clearing spine must be a domestic Schedule I bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal general corporate income tax rate (after 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction; combined federal/provincial rate typically 25% in high-tax provinces such as Ontario and Quebec)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, unless a higher or lower rate is mandated by an applicable tax treaty. No withholding tax applies to dividends paid to Canadian resident shareholders

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed within 6 months after the corporation's fiscal year-end (e.g., December 31 year-end is due by June 30 of the following year). Instalment payments are due on the 15th day of every third month for instalment-based filers. There is no automatic extension for non-resident corporations; offshore reporting deadlines remain fixed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory T106 filing and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) are required when a Canadian corporation has reportable non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceeding CAD 1 million in aggregate during the fiscal year. Lower thresholds apply to specific categories: CAD 50,000 or more for management or administrative fees, CAD 50,000 or more for payments of royalties, interest, or research and development services involving a designated foreign affiliate. The CAD 1 million threshold is per non-resident, per fiscal period, and requires exclusion of transaction categories covered by separate treaty reporting. A MNEs Canadian entity must prepare a documented economic analysis and Form T106 information schedules by the filing due date.

Economic substance scope: Canada applies substance-based anti-avoidance doctrines, notably s.245 GAAR, s.247 transfer pricing adjustment, and the general anti-avoidance principle requiring economic substance where a transaction has been arranged for a tax benefit. Canadian tax law does not implement the Caribbean/UAE Economic Substance Regulations; instead, CRA assesses whether a corporation is resident and whether a permanent establishment exists. The Canadian ESR equivalent is the 'beneficial ownership' and 'place of effective management' doctrine. A shell company that does not carry on real economic activities with staff and resources in Canada will be taxed based on mind and management from its foreign jurisdiction, or face treaty denial under principal purpose tests.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Under s.238 and s.239 of the Income Tax Act, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment upon summary conviction for up to 2 years plus a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded; on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to 200% of the tax evaded. Reassessment period is 3 years; if gross negligence or fraud is established, the Minister can reassess any taxation year without time bar and impose a 50% gross negligence penalty under s.163.2.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unremitted employee source deductions and GST under s.227.1 of the Income Tax Act, plus penalties and interest. Where funds are diverted or corporate veil pierced, directors and shareholders may face joint and several civil restitution through CRA discovery orders and a permanent CRA civil fraud action. Directors' liability is not available for routine corporate taxes payable but arises for source withholdings.
Shell Company / Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: CRA will apply GAAR under s.245 to deny the tax benefit and reassess; the corporation may face the denial of the small business deduction, loss of treaty benefits under the Canada-U.S. Limitation on Benefits clause, and 25% Part XIII withholding on undeclared deemed dividends. The Tax Court can uphold gross negligence penalties under s.163.2 and interest of 5% plus 1% monthly compounded.

Director personal liability: A finding that the corporation is a sham or conduit gives CRA standing to assess the sole shareholder personally for the corporate income under judicial anti-avoidance doctrines. Directors who withdraw funds while debts to CRA are outstanding may face s.227.1 source withholding liability; the CRA can issue director's liability assessments up to CAD 25,000 per director, and in extreme cases, a court can order repatriation of foreign funds or a travel advisory for non-cooperation.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINTRAC Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Failing to file large virtual currency transaction reports (CAD 10,000+) or failing to maintain a compliance program can attract administrative monetary penalties of up to CAD 100,000 for corporate violations per occurrence; criminal sanctions for laundering proceeds of crime carry up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be charged if they knowingly direct, authorize, or permit money laundering activity. Courts routinely pierce the corporate veil under s.462.33 of the Criminal Code for forfeiture of assets; travel bans and restraint orders can be issued under the Mutual Legal Assistance in Criminal Matters Act.
Non-Filing / Misrepresentation of Cross-Border Related-Party Dealings

Statutory sanction: Under s.247(8), a CRA transfer pricing adjustment can be made with a reduction of the taxpayer's deduction or increase of Canadian income, and penalties for a materially inaccurate transfer pricing return are 10% of the net adjustment, up to CAD 500,000, where the taxpayer was not reasonably prudent. Subsequent filing failures trigger late-filing penalties and a zero tolerance for interest relief.

Director personal liability: If the transfer pricing plan is arranged to evade Canadian tax and no documentation exists, the director may be assessed as a co-trustee of the tax benefit; CRA can make the director jointly liable for penalties, and a criminal charge of fraud under s.380 of the Criminal Code carries maximum imprisonment of 14 years.
