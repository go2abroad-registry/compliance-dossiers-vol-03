Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — national tax authority for federal corporate income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act and for GST/HST under Part IX of the Excise Tax Act. Portal: CRA My Business Account (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE, and Corporations Canada for CBCA incorporation.

E-government filing portal

For a federally incorporated Business Corporation (CA_CORP): (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act via Corporations Canada; (2) CRA automatically issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) upon incorporation; (3) activate the corporate income tax program account (RC) and GST/HST program account (RT) through CRA MyBA or Form RC1; (4) GST/HST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or the trailing four quarters, and is recommended immediately for an exporter to enable input tax credit recovery. For a non-resident entity acting as exporter without a domestic subsidiary, the same Form RC1 procedure obtains a BN and RT account; carrying on business in Canada also requires an RC account and a T2 corporate return.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under ITA ss. 2 and 89(1), with no pass-through eligibility. Territorial scope: resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credit relief under ITA s. 126; non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income. The entity is not located in a tax-exempt zone; for GST/HST it is a taxable registrant whose exported goods qualify for zero-rating. — For a federally incorporated Business Corporation (CA_CORP): (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act via Corporations Canada; (2) CRA automatically issues the 9-digit Business Number (BN) upon incorporation; (3) activate the corporate income tax program account (RC) and GST/HST program account (RT) through CRA MyBA or Form RC1; (4) GST/HST registration is mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in the current calendar quarter or the trailing four quarters, and is recommended immediately for an exporter to enable input tax credit recovery. For a non-resident entity acting as exporter without a domestic subsidiary, the same Form RC1 procedure obtains a BN and RT account; carrying on business in Canada also requires an RC account and a T2 corporate return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Canadian Business Corporation is a separate taxpayer under ITA ss. 2 and 89(1), with no pass-through eligibility. Territorial scope: resident corporations are taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credit relief under ITA s. 126; non-resident corporations are taxable only on Canadian-source income. The entity is not located in a tax-exempt zone; for GST/HST it is a taxable registrant whose exported goods qualify for zero-rating.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a US-style check-the-box classification election; CBCA corporate status is conclusive. Relevant elections and rules: (i) ETA s. 156 election for non-taxable supplies between closely related corporations; (ii) ETA s. 167 joint election on sale of a business; (iii) ETA s. 173 Quick Method election for small GST/HST registrants; (iv) ITA s. 249.1(2) election for a non-calendar fiscal period; (v) hybrid mismatch treatment is governed by ITA ss. 18.3 and 18.4 effective July 1, 2022, with no elective carve-out, and cross-border characterization follows foreign law as interpreted by CRA Folio S4-F8-C1.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in Canada in the current calendar quarter or the last four consecutive calendar quarters; exceeding this small-supplier threshold triggers mandatory registration. Non-resident vendors conducting commercial activity in Canada are also subject to this threshold, while an exporter generally registers voluntarily to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, a supply of bitumen/asphalt goods is zero-rated where tangible personal property is sold in Canada and: (i) delivered by the vendor to a common carrier or consignee for shipment to a destination outside Canada; (ii) shipped, mailed, or exported as part of the vendor's commercial operations; and (iii) not re-supplied, further processed, or returned to Canada. The vendor must retain documents showing that title passed to a non-resident purchaser and that export occurred within a commercially reasonable window. Containerized shipments under FOB/CIF/CIP Incoterms qualify, and the corresponding input tax credits on procurement, freight, and terminal handling are fully recoverable through the GST/HST return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A Export Declaration / CAED electronic filing)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing containerized movement of bitumen to a destination outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank settlement evidence proving receipt of the non-resident purchase price
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List aligned with Incoterms
  • Terminal Receipt, Cargo Release, or Container Status Event History as independent export proof
  • Signed Sales Contract evidencing sale in Canada and the foreign destination

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal invoice clearance, API stamping, or digital tax seal mandate at the federal level. Electronic invoices are accepted where they contain the prescribed data supporting an input tax credit under ITA s. 169 and the Input Tax Credit Information (GST/HST) Regulations. Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1,000,000 must file GST/HST returns electronically. CRA has consulted on a future Pan-Canadian Peppol e-invoicing framework, but no mandatory clearance regime is in force.

CRA export-verification and GST/HST audit programs test the documentary bundle and the economic substance of each export. Where zero-rating is not substantiated, CRA reassesses the supply at 5% GST, disallows input tax credits, and imposes compound daily prescribed interest. Penalties include the ETA s. 280(1) civil penalty equal to the greater of C$250 and 5% of the net tax understatement, doubled to 10% for a repeat failure within four years, and the ITA s. 163(2) gross-negligence penalty of 50% of any corresponding corporate income tax misstatement. Criminal tax evasion under ETA s. 327(1) carries fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to five years. Refund clawbacks may reach back four years on ordinary reassessment and indefinitely where fraudulent misrepresentation is proven. Although the ETA does not codify a formal foreign-currency repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is treated in this dossier as the treasury/letter-of-credit settlement milestone, and absence of repatriation evidence within it materially increases the risk that the transaction is recast as a domestic taxable supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility of the Canadian dollar, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. No exchange-control permit is required for bitumen/asphalt wholesale earnings, capital injections, trade settlements, or profit remittances. Profit repatriation is unrestricted but may attract Part XIII non-resident withholding taxes (default 25%, generally reduced by treaty) and must satisfy corporate solvency and board authorization requirements. Sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act remains mandatory for cross-border counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts offshore or with Canadian authorized banks, and may invoice in CAD or any permissible currency. Export documentation must be maintained, including the CBSA B13A/Canadian Export Reporting System declaration where the value exceeds C$2,000 or the goods are controlled. No repatriation timeline or mandatory exchange ratio is imposed; however, all large wire transfers and monetary instruments above C$10,000 are subject to FINTRAC reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada / Banque du Canada (Bank of Canada Act); foreign exchange administration functions are not centralized in a dedicated 'SAFE' institution. The functional ecosystem includes OSFI for bank prudential regulation, FINTRAC for AML/CFT intelligence, and CBSA for trade/export reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary FX/payment gateway, Low as the primary settlement rail. Canada's Bank Act restricts deposit-taking to authorized bank entities, so an offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully operate a Canadian checking account or accept callable deposits for this corporation. An offshore EMI may offer e-money and cross-border payout services only if properly registered as a money services business with FINTRAC and, once the Retail Payment Activities Act licensing framework is fully implemented, registered with the Bank of Canada. Even then, final CAD settlement must flow through a Canadian authorized payment service provider or Schedule I bank account. For containerized bitumen/asphalt wholesale settlement, an offshore EMI is not recommended as the main operating account because it lacks direct LVTS access, documentary letter of credit capabilities, and full trade compliance origination.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from a foreign investor to the Canadian corporation should be transmitted as SWIFT MT103 wire instructions to the designated Schedule I bank account, with the remitter name matching the subscribed shareholder. Canada does not operate a centralized foreign-exchange 'purpose code' system; the remittance narrative should unambiguously state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / SUBSCRIPTION FOR SHARES' and include the corporation's exact legal name. The bank will verify the wire against the share subscription agreement, board resolution, UBO declarations, and sanctions screening. For USD funds, the sender should provide the Canadian receiving bank's U.S. correspondent ABA and complete beneficiary account details. For CAD funds, settlement can occur through Payments Canada LVTS using the bank's transit and institution numbers. The receiving bank may place a brief hold if the originator is a non contracted third party or if source-of-funds documentation is incomplete.Outbound supplier and freight payments should be executed using SWIFT MT103 for standard telegraphic transfers or MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover payments. USD disbursements are cleared through the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent via Fedwire or CHIPS; CAD payments settle through Payments Canada LVTS with the beneficiary bank's institution and transit number; EUR payments clear over TARGET2. For containerized bitumen/asphalt supply contracts, the preferred documentary rail is an irrevocable, sight or usance commercial letter of credit issued under UCP600 and ISBP 821, with confirmation from the supplier's bank where needed. The LC must require presentation of the commercial invoice, original clean-on-board bill of lading, independent certificate of quality/quantity and penetration grade, certificate of origin, packing/weight list, insurance certificate, and container-level verification. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to non-residents of Canada, subject to treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be filed within six months after the end of the taxation year (e.g., December 31, 2024 year-end due June 30, 2025).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Identification Legal name, Canadian business number, CRA account, registered address, jurisdiction of incorporation, and tax status. Non-resident corporations must enter the Canadian business number and indicate non-resident status; if no permanent establishment is maintained, the foreign address and parent ownership should be disclosed. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form T106 reporting of non-arm's length transactions is mandatory when the aggregate fair market value of reportable transactions between the Canadian corporation and one or more non-resident related persons exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year. Canada does not prescribe a separate statutory Master File or Local File threshold; however, the CRA requires OECD-aligned contemporaneous documentation for material cross-border intercompany transactions. Country-by-Country reporting is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue in the prior fiscal year exceeds CAD 830,000,000 (approximately EUR 750,000,000).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the Gulf or OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations as a separate statutory code, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 245), the sham doctrine, and the business purpose rule require genuine economic substance. A non-resident holding company or financing vehicle with no real employees, office, or management in Canada may be deemed not to be the beneficial owner of dividends or royalties; treaty relief can be denied and the entity re-characterized as a conduit. CRA also applies the management and control test to determine corporate residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act section 239(1) and 239(1.1): upon conviction, fine not less than 50% and up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus a statutory fine of CAD 1,000 to CAD 25,000; imprisonment up to 12 months on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment. Civil gross-negligence penalty under section 163(2) equal to 50% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct or participate in the evasion are personally liable as â€œaiders and abettorsâ€. Criminal courts may impose restitution, forfeiture, and bail or probation conditions including surrender of passports and travel restrictions. The corporate veil is pierced for fraud, allowing execution against personal assets.
Transfer Pricing / Non-Arm's Length Transactions

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under section 247(2). Penalty under section 247(3) is 10% of the net adjustment to taxable income, applied when the upward or downward adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue and CAD 5,000,000. Late filing of Form T106 attracts additional information-return penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for corporate transfer-pricing penalties, but if they knowingly prepared or consented to false related-party documentation, they may face criminal liability for fraud or tax evasion. In cases where transfer-pricing adjustments create unpaid corporate liabilities, directors may also be liable for unpaid payroll and excise remittances under section 227.1.
Shell Company / Abusive Treaty Shopping (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section 245 disallows the tax benefit and recharacterizes the transaction. The penalty under section 245(3) is 25% of the tax benefit sought to be avoided. Where false statements are involved, section 163(2) gross-negligence penalties and criminal tax evasion charges under section 239 may also apply.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the shell company is used to conceal the true beneficial owner or to commit fraud. Directors who knowingly caused the corporation to enter a sham arrangement are personally liable for resulting tax liabilities, may be subject to criminal fines and imprisonment, and can be barred from acting as directors under the Canada Business Corporations Act upon conviction for an indictable offence.
AML / Proceeds of Crime (Trade-Based Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act provides for FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,100,000 for corporations and criminal fines for substantive offences. Under the Criminal Code, money laundering (section 462.31) carries imprisonment up to 10 years and asset forfeiture orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who control or direct the laundering operations are liable as principals even if the corporate entity is used as a shield. Criminal trials may impose travel bans as a condition of bail, freeze or seize personal and corporate assets, and result in imprisonment. The Federal Court may also issue compliance orders and prohibit convicted persons from holding corporate office.
