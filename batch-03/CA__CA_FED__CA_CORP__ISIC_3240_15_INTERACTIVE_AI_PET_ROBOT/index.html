Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); self-service portal 'My Business Account' and Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; customs and export administration vested in the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident corporation obtains a statutory Business Number (BN) and, where required or elected, a GST/HST program account by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or by using CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO). The BN is a 9-digit federal identifier augmented by a two-letter program account code (e.g., RT for GST/HST) and a 4-digit reference number (format 123456789 RT0001). A non-resident supplier without a Canadian permanent establishment is not obliged to register unless worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000, but may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits and to zero-rate exports. Supporting documentation includes the certificate of incorporation or equivalent corporate existence evidence, a board resolution appointing an authorized representative with authority to file, a completed Form AUT-01 (Power of Attorney) designating a representative resident in Canada where applicable, and, if the corporation carries on business in Canada through a branch, federal payroll (RP), import/export (RM) accounts, and provincial accounts administered by CRA or, uniquely in Quebec, by Revenu Quebec.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Canada; a CBCA business corporation is a per se corporation and not eligible for pass-through classification. Canadian statutory elections include: Income Tax Act s. 216 election (net-basis taxation of non-residents' rental income), s. 217 election (taxation of non-residents' employment income in Canada), ss. 85/86 rollover elections for corporate reorganizations, and the hybrid mismatch neutralization elections under ITA ss. 18.4 to 18.6 (BEPS Action 2). Under the Excise Tax Act, the small supplier election and the agent/principal election (ETA s. 177) are relevant for indirect tax structuring.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (annual worldwide threshold of taxable supplies other than financial services; the 'small supplier' ceiling). Non-resident registrants whose worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 must register; voluntary registration is permitted under ETA s. 240. Special reduced thresholds apply to taxi and limousine services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible personal property qualifies for zero-rating under Item 1, Part V, Schedule VI ETA where the vendor exports the goods or transfers them to a common carrier, freight forwarder, or courier for export from Canada, or ships them to a destination outside Canada. The interactive AI pet robot, being tangible personal property sold to foreign end-users, is eligible for 0% GST/HST provided that evidence of physical export is retained within the applicable documentary timeframes; CRA administrative guidance is set out in GST/HST Memorandum 8-3 and Technical Bulletin B-094. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Item 7, Part V, Schedule VI ETA if the service is not performed in Canada on real property owned in Canada and is not supplied to an individual present in Canada at the time the service is performed.

  • CBSA Export Declaration (Form B13A) or CBSA electronic export reporting (CESR) / Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED) filing confirmation
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier consignment note evidencing shipment to a destination outside Canada within the prescribed export period
  • Commercial Invoice and sales contract identifying the foreign consignee and ultimate destination outside Canada
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt / inward remittance certificate or equivalent FX settlement records evidencing cross-border proceeds repatriation

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-mandated invoice authorization regime. Mandatory electronic GST/HST return filing applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies above CAD 1.5 million, using GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or XML through the CRA gateway. Invoices must contain the supplier's BN and GST/HST account number, the tax rate, and the tax amount, but no API-based clearance or unique fiscal code is required. CRA conducts post-filing data-matching between CBSA export declarations and GST/HST zero-rating claims.

CRA's Export Verification Program cross-matches CBSA customs transaction data with GST/HST returns; high-risk zero-rating claims are selected for review. Penalties and clawback exposure include: ETA s. 285 gross negligence penalty equal to the greater of CAD 5,000 and 5% of the excess refund or credit claimed; extension of the reassessment limitation period from 4 years to 7 years where misrepresentation or neglect is established (ETA s. 298); daily compounding interest on unremitted or over-claimed GST/HST; joint and several director liability for unremitted amounts (ETA s. 323); and criminal prosecution for evasion or attempted evasion (ETA s. 327) with fines up to CAD 10,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. In addition, fraudulent export zero-rating triggers full clawback of input tax refunds, foreign-currency translation adjustments, and penalties for false statements, with no statute-barred protection for wilful misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has no exchange-control law. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a free-floating, fully convertible currency under the Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act; both current-account flows (royalties, interest, dividends, import/export payments) and capital-account flows (equity injections, shareholder loans, M&A proceeds, profit repatriation) are unrestricted and require no central-bank approval. Residual non-FX hurdles: (i) Investment Canada Act net-benefit review applies only to non-Canadian acquisitions of control of Canadian businesses; a de novo incorporation plus capital injection for a robotics toy manufacturer is notifiable only above the WTO-investor threshold and is otherwise non-reviewable; (ii) Part XIII Income Tax Act imposes 25% statutory withholding tax on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties, reduced to 5-15% for dividends and 0-10% for interest under the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention; (iii) CRS/FATCA reporting and transfer-pricing documentation under section 247 of the Income Tax Act apply. No trapped-capital or forced-conversion mechanism exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirement exists in Canada; exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds indefinitely in resident or non-resident foreign-currency accounts. The mandatory financial-compliance duties are: (a) all international electronic funds transfers (EFTs) of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported to FINTRAC by the bank within 24 hours with a truthful purpose-of-payment statement; a false narrative supporting a suspicious transfer exposes the account to a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) and freeze; (b) Statistics Canada requires monthly merchandise export documentation (B13A via customs broker EDI) feeding balance-of-payments data; (c) under the Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA), robotic goods with artificial-intelligence capabilities must be screened against the Export Control List; consumer companion-pet robots generally qualify under the General Export Permit as non-controlled goods, but a written classification opinion is recommended.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank and sole issuer of Canadian dollars; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as prudential regulator of federally regulated Schedule I banks; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the national financial intelligence unit under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); Global Affairs Canada and Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administer export/import controls and customs.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (ancillary-only). Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may be used legally for low-value outbound disbursements (contractors, SaaS subscriptions, small supplier invoices), but they cannot serve as the primary treasury concentration point because: (i) they are not authorized to accept Canadian deposits under the Bank Act; (ii) the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), in force since November 2024 with registration and risk-management compliance obligations phased through September 2025, requires any payment service provider operating in Canada to register with the Bank of Canada and implement operational safeguards; (iii) inbound equity injections arriving via EMI card/wallet rails rather than a Tier-1 bank account generate enhanced due diligence, delayed CAD conversion, and an unclear source-of-funds trail for CRS and Investment Canada files. EMIs are permissible as auxiliary disbursement rails, funded by one or two consolidated monthly SWIFT wires from the Tier-1 operating account; all capital, hedging, and large trade settlements must remain on licensed bank accounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual aggregate non-arm's length cross-border transactions with a foreign related/associated enterprise exceeding CAD 1,000,000 triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation and Form T106 filing for the Canadian taxpayer.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in offshore jurisdictions, but substance matters: s.245 GAAR nullifies avoidance transactions lacking bona fide commercial purpose; treaty benefits can be denied under s.94.1 and under the BEPS Principal Purpose Test in applicable DTAs. A shell corporation without employees, assets, or operational substance cannot claim treaty protection on dividends or royalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under ITA s.239(1), on indictment: up to 5 years imprisonment and a fine of up to 200% of evaded tax; gross negligence penalty under s.163(2): 50% of understated tax; CRA may publicly identify conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors/officers who participate in fraudulent misstatements may be criminally prosecuted; civil joint liability under s.160 for property transferred with intent to defeat tax; asset freezing orders; travel ban through criminal courts.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR penalty under s.163(1.1): 25% of additional tax resulting from avoidance transaction; Part XIII withholding tax assessed (25%) on undeclared deemed dividend distributions; denial of treaty benefits under s.94.1.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally for withholding tax liabilities under s.159 (if wages/source deductions were not remitted) and for unlawful stripping under s.160; shareholder disputes or CRA civil claims can pierce the veil where corporations are being used as agents for the principal.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under PCMLFTA, non-compliance with record-keeping and reporting can lead to administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000; knowingly dealing with proceeds of crime is indictment under s.462.31 Criminal Code, imprisonment up to 10 years; forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted as principals or conspirators; personal assets subject to restraint and forfeiture orders; international Interpol Red Notices and travel bans can be issued.
Transfer Pricing Understatement / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under s.247(2) to arm's length price; transfer pricing penalty under s.247(3) of 10% of the net adjustment; daily interest on unpaid amounts; potential adjusted taxable income and Part XIII deemed withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors signing the T2 or T106 without ensuring documentation may be assessed as co-persons for penalty under s.247(3); where penalties remain unpaid, CRA may pursue directors' property under s.160 for dividend or asset distributions.
