Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Canadian default is entity-level taxation: a BC ULC is a resident 'corporation' for Income Tax Act Part I federal tax and BC provincial corporate income tax, and is therefore not automatically fiscally transparent in Canada; the pass-through label attaches only in cross-border/treaty planning, principally when a US owner classifies the ULC as a partnership or disregarded entity for US federal income tax purposes, subject to the Canadian statutory deeming and hybrid mismatch rules).

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a domestic 'check-the-box' election. Income Tax Act section 137.1 deems an unlimited liability company to be a corporation and its shareholders to be shareholders of a corporation for Canadian tax purposes. The operative pass-through/hybrid election is made outside Canada: under US Treasury Regulation section 301.7701-3, a BC ULC is an eligible entity and its US owner may file US Form 8832 to elect partnership or disregarded-entity classification, while the CRA continues to tax the ULC as a corporation, producing a hybrid entity that must be tested against the hybrid mismatch rules and the Canada-US tax treaty. GST/HST elections: the Excise Tax Act section 273 election permits a group of closely related registrants to file as a single GST/HST group, and the section 156 election permits supplies between closely related persons to be treated as made for nil consideration, both subject to statutory eligibility conditions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account bases under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; Canada has not imposed exchange controls and fulfils IMF Article VIII obligations. Dividend and royalty remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted at the banking and central-bank level, and no CAD conversion queue, repatriation licence, waiting period, or prior-authorisation requirement exists. The only material hurdle is federal withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (RSC, 1985, c 1 (5th Supp.)): the domestic rate is 25% on dividends and 25% on royalties. Under the Canada-US Tax Convention (1980, as amended), the dividend rate is reduced to 5% for a qualifying US corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of the voting shares, and the royalty rate to 10%; under the Canada-China Income Tax Agreement (1986), the dividend and royalty rates are each 10%. For the BC ULC, the US IRS may treat the entity as a disregarded or pass-through entity for US tax purposes, but for Canadian banking and regulatory purposes the ULC is a resident corporation; this distinction affects tax certification (W-9 versus W-8BEN-E) but does not impede CAD convertibility or profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules exist under Canadian law. There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; proceeds may be retained in any freely convertible currency. For the Malatang master-licensing model, franchise fees and royalty receipts constitute intangible-service export proceeds and remain fully freely disposable. The only compliance duties are FINTRAC reporting obligations: cross-border currency reports and electronic funds transfer reports (EFTRs) are triggered by single or linked transactions of CAD 10,000 or more within a 24-hour period, and physical cross-border movements of CAD 10,000 or equivalent must be declared under the PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority and fiscal agent for the Government of Canada's Exchange Fund Account, which is managed by the Minister of Finance (Department of Finance Canada). Canada maintains no foreign-exchange control administration and no central bank approval regime for inward or outward remittances; the statutory framework is the Currency Act (RSC, 1985, c C-52), the Bank of Canada Act (RSC, 1985, c B-2), and the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (SC 2000, c 17) for reporting obligations only.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary treasury counterparty; MEDIUM as a supplementary disbursement and FX conversion rail. Canada does not issue a distinct e-money institution (EMI) licence. A non-bank holding customer funds must be either an OSFI-regulated deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act or a money services business (MSB) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA, and in British Columbia the provider is also subject to the Money Services Act (SBC 2022) once fully in force. Providers such as Wise Payments Canada Inc. and Airwallex operate as FINTRAC-registered MSBs and may lawfully execute cross-border wires and FX conversion for the ULC, but they cannot accept the inbound equity injection as a principal account, cannot offer CDIC deposit insurance, and should hold only a de-minimis operating float. The ULC's principal operating, reserve, and escrow accounts must remain with CDIC-insured Schedule I banks to preserve depositor protection and to evidence treasury substance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million in aggregate cross-border transactions with non-residents (per CRA guidance).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and treaty abuse provisions require that the entity have real economic substance (e.g., actual business operations, employees, and decision-making in Canada) to obtain treaty benefits and avoid reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution may result in fines up to CAD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for willful evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties up to 10% of the transaction value; potential loss of corporate status.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held liable for taxes and penalties if they authorized or acquiesced to the sham arrangement; travel bans may be imposed under tax enforcement.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 500,000 for corporations; criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for officers.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to comply with the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income, penalties of 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation incomplete), and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they signed false or misleading returns; in severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion.
