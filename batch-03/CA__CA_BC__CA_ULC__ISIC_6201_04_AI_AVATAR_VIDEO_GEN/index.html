Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - primary portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html); provincial counterpart: British Columbia Ministry of Finance (PST registration) - BC Tax Account.

E-government filing portal

A BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) first files incorporation with BC Corporate Online and receives a BC company number. It then registers with the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and the required program accounts: Corporate Income Tax (RC0001), Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (RC0002), and payroll (RP0001) if applicable. Non-resident entities without a place of business in Canada may register for a non-resident GST/HST program account by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or using the CRA's Non-Resident GST/HST Registration service. The BN serves as the exclusive tax identification number for all federal filings and indirect tax returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canadian domestic tax law does not offer a 'check-the-box' election for a ULC; it is treated as a corporation under the Income Tax Act and is a separate taxable entity. However, a BC ULC may be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership for foreign income tax purposes through IRS Form 8832 (Check-the-Box) where the ULC qualifies as a hybrid entity. In Canada, no statutory election exists to treat a ULC as a pass-through for Canadian income tax. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed under sections 94 to 94.4 of the Income Tax Act, and cross-border filings may include a Canadian corporate return plus foreign transparent disclosure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold) - a GST/HST registrant is mandatory once total taxable supplies made in a single calendar quarter exceed CAD 30,000; the threshold applies equally to resident and non-resident suppliers carrying on business in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST-registered BC ULC may zero-rate exported goods if the goods are delivered outside Canada and exporting evidence is maintained (e.g., shipping documents, destination control statements, or customs outbound documentation). For services (including AI avatar video generation and SaaS digital products), zero-rating under s. 7, Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act applies when the supply is made to a non-resident person, provided the service is not supplied to a non-resident individual while present in Canada and does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. Digital services delivered to foreign customers via cloud servers are zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not consumed in Canada.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Export Declaration / Cargo Declaration - Customs Coding Form B13A) or NTE (Non-Trade Export) Declaration as applicable
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / carrier tracking evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank statement evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Commercial invoice with explicit 'zero-rated export' notation
  • Packing list and freight/cargo manifest or other acceptance documentation

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for GST/HST. CRA permits electronic invoices and records provided they meet the requirements of Information Circular IC 70-6R6 (Electronic Record Keeping). There is no mandatory API-based fiscal invoice authorization. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE, EFILE, or EDI) for registrants with annual revenues exceeding CAD 15,000; all registrants using the recovery credit system or claiming large refunds are also required to e-file.

Under the Excise Tax Act, improper zero-rating or false export claims may result in CRA reassessment within the standard three-year period (extended to seven years for misrepresentation or gross negligence), recovery of unremitted GST/HST, monthly compounding arrears interest, and penalties. Specifically, s. 285.1 imposes penalties for false statements of at least CAD 500 plus 50% of the net tax assessable; s. 327 provides criminal penalties for evasion, including fines up to CAD 10,000 and/or imprisonment. Director and officer liability may attach under s. 323. Export zero-rating refunds are subject to enhanced audit scrutiny where the CRA reviews bank statements, export declarations, and beneficiary country data.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a fully convertible currency under IMF Article VIII. Both current-account items (trade in services, dividends, interest, royalties) and capital-account items (foreign direct investment, share subscriptions, loans, repatriation) are free from exchange control licencing or mandatory conversion approval. Remittance of profit from a BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) is effected via normal banking channels; the only taxation concern is Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders at 25% statutory rate (generally reduced to 5%-15% under an income tax treaty), and no foreign exchange approval is needed. Financial institutions must report incoming and outgoing international electronic funds transfers (EFTs) of C$10,000 or more to FINTRAC, determined on a 24-hour aggregation basis. There are no surrender, deposit, or currency conversion restrictions; however, all transactions remain subject to sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act and the United Nations Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or retiming requirement for export receipts. There is no regulated 'export proceeds account' and no minimum/maximum conversion ratio. The only formal data requirement is the filing of an Export Declaration (via the Canadian Automated Export Declaration, CADEX) with Statistics Canada under the Customs Act for goods exceeding the prescribed threshold (usually within 15 days after the month of export), which is used for trade statistics, not foreign-exchange controls. Accordingly, the mandatory conversion ratio is 0% and the statutory retention timeline is 'unlimited'; however, any international EFT exceeding C$10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC in accordance with regulatory deadlines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), supported by the Department of Finance Canada and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). Canada does not maintain a separate exchange control administration; exchange policy is exercised through the Bank of Canada's foreign exchange reserves and the Currency Act. AML-based currency monitoring is enforced by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium-High for transactional SaaS settlement, Low for capital escrow and core treasury custody. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs), including Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex, are not chartered banks in Canada and may not accept deposits nor originate CAD wires as a bank. They are permitted to operate as money services businesses (MSBs) or payment service providers under BC's Money Services Business Act (MSBA) and as FINTRAC MSBs registered under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act if they carry on business in Canada. Additionally, the federal Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) will require payment service providers to be registered with the Bank of Canada. Such EMIs are suitable for receiving AI SaaS subscription payouts via payment gateways, but a ULC's subscription agreement and capital preservation require the ULC to maintain its primary operating and reserve accounts with a domestic Schedule I bank. The capital injection from the parent cannot be lawfully routed through an unregulated foreign EMI as this could trigger bank-account or AML characterisation and undermine the legal certainty of the equity subscription.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions to the BC ULC should be routed exclusively through a Canadian Schedule I bank operating account or a solicitor's trust account, using SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) with unambiguous purpose codes. Recommended purpose field: 'INVS' (Investment and Securities) or equivalent bank code for a capital contribution, with free-text 'BC ULC shareholder capital contribution'. The remitting bank must include the beneficiary ULC's full legal name, Canadian banking number (transit + institution + account), address, and SWIFT BIC. For USD inbound wires, use correspondent banking networks (e.g., Chase, BMO/BNY) and the ULC's USD account; for CAD, clearing will occur via Payments Canada's Large-Value Transfer System (LVTS) or wire. Verification requirements: (i) a signed subscription agreement and call notice; (ii) bank reference for the remitter; (iii) source of funds statements; (iv) FINTRAC EFT report will be generated by the receiving bank if the amount is C$10,000 or more. No approval from the Bank of Canada, OSFI, or the BC Securities Commission is required for the inward remittance itself. The ULC must ensure the wire's beneficiary field does not name an EMI or payment intermediary unless the account is closed out via a trust; otherwise, the bank may query or suspend the credit.For trade settlements of AI avatar video licensor fees, cloud compute invoices, and infrastructure commissions, the following clearing rails are compliant: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor payments and dividend remittances (with purpose codes such as 'LICY' for intellectual property licensing, 'ROYA' for royalty, and 'DIVI' for dividend distribution); (ii) Payment Canada EFT/ACSS for domestic CAD payments and Interac e-Transfer for small disbursements; (iii) Fedwire/CHIPS for high-value USD payments, or correspondent USD drafts; and (iv) Commercial or Standby Letters of Credit (MT700/MT720) only if the counterparty contractually requires bank-issued payment assurance, which is rare for AIGC SaaS but permissible if collateralised by a Canadian bank trade finance line. The ULC should employ a cash-flow waterfall ensuring outbound USD wires are matched with FX deals executed on the same day using forward contract rates to avoid negative carry. Statutory deliverableDeadline window Government feeDelinquency sanctions
BC Annual Report under section 58 of the British Columbia Business Corporations Act (BCBCA) Within 2 months after the anniversary date of incorporation (the annual report date); equivalent to anniversary date + 60 days $42 USD Late fee and delinquent corporate status; failure to file annual reports for 2 consecutive years permits administrative dissolution or strike-off under BCBCA; restoration requires registrar or court order and reinstatement fees.
T2 Corporation Income Tax Return On or before the last day of the sixth month after the ULC's taxation year-end $0 USD Late-filing penalty of 5% of the unpaid balance plus 1% per full month up to 12 months; repeat failures may attract a 10% penalty; daily compounding interest applies; directors may be liable for unremitted source deductions.
GST/HST Return (annual, quarterly, or monthly reporting period as registered) For annual filers, within 3 months after the end of the fiscal year; for quarterly filers, within 1 month after the end of each quarter $0 USD Late-filing and late-remittance penalty of 3% (or 6% for repeat failures) plus prescribed daily interest; false statements and omissions may lead to additional civil penalties.
T106 Information Return (reportable transactions with non-residents, if applicable) Within 6 months after the ULC's taxation year-end, filed with or before the T2 return $0 USD Failure to file a complete T106 information return may trigger penalties under the Income Tax Act, including monetary penalties per form and per month overdue; gross-negligence penalties may apply.
T4 Statement of Remuneration and T4 Summary (if payroll is maintained) No later than the last day of February following the calendar year in which remuneration was paid $0 USD Penalties are calculated as a percentage of wages withheld, escalating for repeat failures; interest accrues on unpaid source-deduction remittances.
BC Employer Health Tax Return (if BC remuneration exceeds the annual exemption threshold) For calendar-year employers, by March 31 following the calendar year; otherwise within 3 months after fiscal year-end $0 USD Late-filing and late-payment penalties, interest on outstanding amounts, and potential director liability for unremitted employer health tax.
