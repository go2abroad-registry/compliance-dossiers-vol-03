Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); portal: CRA My Business Account and Form RC1 registration; BC sales tax administered separately by British Columbia Ministry of Finance (Consumer Taxation Branch) via BC PST registration portal.

E-government filing portal

The BC ULC is incorporated under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act. Upon incorporation, the CRA automatically creates a Business Number (BN, 9-digit) and corporate income tax account. For GST/HST, the ULC must open a separate BN-C (GST/HST) account by filing Form RC1 or using CRA online registration; a BC PST number must be separately obtained from the BC Consumer Taxation Branch if the ULC makes taxable sales of software or digital services in BC. Non-resident shareholders and directors of a BC ULC should verify director-residency requirements and appoint a Canadian representative/agent where required; all foreign-source ownership documentation and banking details must be kept on file to support registration and zero-rating claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists. A BC ULC is a taxable corporation under the Income Tax Act and BC tax law, subject to entity-level federal and provincial taxation. For U.S. tax purposes only, the ULC may be treated as an eligible entity under U.S. Treasury Regulation §301.7701-3 and may elect to be a pass-through/disregarded entity via Form 8832, because it has unlimited liability. Hybrid entity characterization may trigger Canadian s.94.1/GAAR or OECD hybrid mismatch analysis where payments are treated inconsistently in Canada and the United States; elections must be documented contemporaneously.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable sales for GST/HST small-supplier registration; CAD 10,000 in BC taxable sales for BC PST small-seller registration. Digital supply via app stores is generally subject to these thresholds once taxable sales exceed the relevant threshold in a calendar quarter/12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST/HST zero-rating is available on supplies of digital/intangible software exported to non-resident recipients who will use the software outside Canada, provided the supply is made to a non-resident and is not in respect of real or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply. Physical exports require the goods to be delivered outside Canada as soon as is reasonably possible. Zero-rating documentation must demonstrate the non-resident status of the recipient, the offshore location/use, and the arm's-length nature of the transaction. BC PST does not apply to sales of software delivered outside BC to non-resident customers.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) export documentation (for physical goods; not required for pure digital downloads)
  • Commercial invoice or sales contract identifying the non-resident recipient and payment terms
  • Bill of Lading / proof of delivery outside Canada for tangible exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence demonstrating payment from abroad
  • GST/HST zero-rating supporting records: Schedule VI Part V analysis and software usage/location documentation

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or government e-invoicing APIs. CRA accepts electronic invoices/records (PDF, XML, EDI) if they are accurate, complete, and retained for six years. BC PST requires invoices or sales receipts issued to BC purchasers to state the seller's PST number and the PST amount separately where PST applies; no pre-clearance or digital stamping requirement exists.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess within the normal four-year reassessment period, or without time limit in cases of misrepresentation or fraud. False or inflated export claims attract penalties under the Excise Tax Act (including up to 25% gross-negligence penalty on the amount claimed), compound arrears interest, loss of input tax credits, and potential criminal prosecution for wilful evasion. BC PST audits may also claw back PST-exempt treatment where export evidence is insufficient, with late-payment penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely convertible currency on both current and capital accounts; Canada imposes no general exchange control legislation, and the former Foreign Exchange Control Board regime was abolished in 1951; pursuant to the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) the legal tender is CAD, and non-resident investors may freely repatriate capital, dividends, royalties, and service fees without prior regulatory approval; the Minister of International Trade may impose limited export/import permit restrictions under the Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA) but none apply to digital health application software or biometric telemetry data streams; profit remittance hurdles are practically limited to compliance with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) withholding tax rules on dividends (Part XIII of the Income Tax Act, 25% standard, reduced to 5% for arm's-length U.S. shareholders under the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty of 1980 as amended by the 2007 Protocol), plus provincial payroll and corporate registrations (BC Corporate Registry, WorksafeBC where employees are engaged).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement; the Bank of Canada, the Treasury Board, and the Department of Finance do not mandate repatriation timelines or conversion ratios for export or services receipts; for a British Columbia ULC exporting digital health and fitness telemetry application services, all foreign currency (primarily USD) remittance inflows from Apple App Store and Google Play Billing settlement cycles may be held abroad or converted freely; prudent treasury practice dictates converting USD to CAD within 5-7 business days to mitigate FX volatility, and netting of costs is permissible; only licensing fees for exported intellectual property under the EIPA (Goods and Services Export Permit) are exempt, and there is no obligation to file a reporting declaration with Statistics Canada unless the value of exported software exceeds the annual thresholds of the Canadian International Merchandise Trade or the International Trade in Services program (threshold at C$100,000-category-specific exemption for royalty-based software fees).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); currency issuance and monetary policy are governed by the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), while prudential bank regulation is administered by OSFI under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); FINTRAC enforces anti-money laundering (AML) obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) as the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (conditional). Legal constraints: Fintech EMIs operating in Canada (e.g., Wise Canada, Airwallex Canada, Payoneer, and Nuvei) are chartered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA; they are NOT 'banks' under the Bank Act and cannot hold Canadian deposit liabilities; thus for the BC ULC they are viable as supplementary payment-pre-processing and settlement-clearing agents, but the primary operating account must remain with a Schedule I OSFI-regulated bank; cross-border EMI routing imposes mandatory 24-hour hold requirements on EFTs (Retail Payment Activities Act, effective 2024, RPSA licensing by the Bank of Canada), and each transaction must individually satisfy identity verification thresholds for the ULC's ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) if the ULC is a pass-through entity for U.S. tax purposes; foreign EMIs domiciled in the EEA (e.g., no longer accessible post-Brexit from a UK EMI) have no reciprocity with Canadian deposit insurance (CDIC) or provincial securities deposit protection, making them non-compliant as the sole treasury rail if the entity has a physical BC Nexus; practical architecture: use a tier-1 bank as anchor, and if required for acquiring sub-accounts (Google/Apple payout aggregation), use a FINTRAC-registered MSB with a valid Money Laundering branch in Vancouver or Toronto.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the initial equity injection (U.S. parent to BC ULC): use the U.S. parent's Schedule I bank (e.g., JPMorgan or Bank of America) to initiate a USD SWIFT MT103 wire to the ULC's RBC Canadian Dollar account via the RBC New York branch (SWIFT: ROYCCAT2), with the payment purpose code 'GIFTCAPINJ' - equivalent to a direct investment; for Canadian regulatory purposes the instruction must include: (i) the ULC's corporate CRA Business Number (BN) and BC registration number; (ii) the remitter's full name and account number; (iii) in the 70-line: 'Equity injection under BC Business Corporations Act; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are permissible but not needed for a digital-health services model; however, for app-development subcontracting to an offshore engineering vendor (e.g., India or Eastern Europe), the standard routing is a documentary collection or irrevocable LC (ICC UCP600) issued by the ULC's domestic bank in favor of the offshore vendor, with the ULC's operating account providing clean reimbursement; the bank will require an underlying software development agreement, a pro-forma invoice, and a work-statement to fulfill the 'invoiceless' disbursement rule; (3) for royalty payments to a U.S. parent (IP license), the outbound rail must include a NOTICE TO THE CRA under Part XIII: the bank is not the withholding agent, but the ULC must effect the withholding via the CRA's electronic remittance (or pay-at-source) using Form NR4 or T2-S13; international clearing rails: SWIFT gpi (Global Payments Innovation) is strongly recommended to reduce transit time to 30 minutes for SDGL (Same-Day Global Low value) transactions; SWIFT MT103 is the only accepted message type for Trade Finance clearing, whereas MT202 COV must be used for cover-through correspondent bank clearing.FX Forward (deliverable) - CAD/USD: OTC with a Canadian Schedule I bank (RBC, TD, BMO), available for a ULC with audited financial statements or a bank credit line; standard tenor 1M-18M; a 10% collateral margin required for unrated ULC; ISDA Master Agreement (2010) or local Canadian Master Agreement with a supporting Credit Support Annex (CSA) if not clearing through the Canadian Derivatives Clearing Corporation (CDCC).Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF): permitted in Canada for CAD crosses only where the counterparty offshore jurisdiction has capital controls (e.g., exotic currency conversion); for CAD/USD, NDFs are disallowed if the underlying flow is FX deliverable; NDF may be layered for hedging the ULC's CNY-denominated Chinese App Store settlement flows (Apple/Google aggregate China payouts) where only an offshore CNY NDF is executable; executed via RBC Capital Markets or TD Securities under a non-cleared margin rules exemption (certainty exemption for commercial hedgers).Cross-Currency Swap (CAD/USD principal exchange): a plain-vanilla cross-currency interest rate swap between the BC ULC operating cash flows and a U.S. parent debt service; applicable only when the ULC holds a bilateral loan from the U.S. parent and wants to swap floating-rate USD liabilities into fixed-rate CAD; typical minimum notional C$20 million; empowered by CDCC rules or QFC stay rules; not relevant for a pre-revenue or early-stage ULC (excess cost), but incorporated into the treasury roof for future scale.Flexible-Forward (window forward): contract allowing the ULC to execute in partial amounts within a forward window (e.g., monthly app-store settlement sweeps), maximizing hedging precision of variable-volume USD/CAD passive income; requires corporate treasury policy approval signed by secretary; available at BMO and RBC without central clearing if the counterparty risk is capped at ULC net asset value.Zero-Cost Collar (long put/short call coupon-neutral): moderately permissible instrument for a ULC where the Canadian entity has a stable USD revenue history from Apple/Google payout; must be executed under the pre-clearance of the ULC Board and a senior secured inter-creditor agreement with the bank; physically or cash-settled at expiry, ensuring that any equity-marking gain is taxable as eligible capital gain; the structure prevents a forward mark-to-market liability exceeding the ULC's paid-up capital.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium (conditional). Legal constraints: Fintech EMIs operating in Canada (e.g., Wise Canada, Airwallex Canada, Payoneer, and Nuvei) are chartered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA; they are NOT 'banks' under the Bank Act and cannot hold Canadian deposit liabilities; thus for the BC ULC they are viable as supplementary payment-pre-processing and settlement-clearing agents, but the primary operating account must remain with a Schedule I OSFI-regulated bank; cross-border EMI routing imposes mandatory 24-hour hold requirements on EFTs (Retail Payment Activities Act, effective 2024, RPSA licensing by the Bank of Canada), and each transaction must individually satisfy identity verification thresholds for the ULC's ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) if the ULC is a pass-through entity for U.S. tax purposes; foreign EMIs domiciled in the EEA (e.g., no longer accessible post-Brexit from a UK EMI) have no reciprocity with Canadian deposit insurance (CDIC) or provincial securities deposit protection, making them non-compliant as the sole treasury rail if the entity has a physical BC Nexus; practical architecture: use a tier-1 bank as anchor, and if required for acquiring sub-accounts (Google/Apple payout aggregation), use a FINTRAC-registered MSB with a valid Money Laundering branch in Vancouver or Toronto.
