Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); corporate tax and GST/HST administration through CRA My Business Account portal; federal incorporation via Corporations Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada); customs border and export declaration administration via Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a federally incorporated resident business corporation is a separate taxpayer under Part I of the Income Tax Act (ITA), taxed on worldwide income at federal corporate rates; provincial corporate allocation applies through the federal abatement mechanism; dividends received from connected Canadian subsidiaries are deductible, portfolio dividends attract Part IV tax, and cross-border dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents are subject to Part XIII withholding tax; no pass-through or transparent treatment is available for federal corporate tax purposes.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box or entity classification election. Available statutory elections: (a) functional currency election for Canadian tax reporting under ITA s. 261; (b) non-calendar fiscal year-end election under ITA s. 249.1; (c) asset and share rollover elections on incorporation and reorganization under ITA ss. 85 and 85.1; (d) treaty-based relief and s. 216 election for non-resident taxpayers on rental and treaty-protected business income; (e) late-filed election relief under ITA s. 220(2.1); (f) GST/HST closely related corporation election under ETA s. 156, available where a 90% ownership connection exists, to disregard inter-corporate supplies; (g) proposed hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5 (BEPS Action 2) remain in legislative process and are not yet fully proclaimed in force.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — small supplier threshold; a person becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant when taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; zero-rated export supplies do not count toward the threshold but create input tax credit refund entitlement; voluntary registration is available to the Canadian subsidiary immediately, enabling recovery of GST/HST embedded in manufacturing equipment, R&D expenditures, construction, and professional services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods, including digital X-ray flat-panel DR detectors, exported from Canada by the supplier to a non-resident are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act where title and risk of loss pass to the non-resident purchaser abroad and the goods are exported in the course of the supply, generally within 60 days of delivery; related offshore installation, commissioning, and logistics services are zero-rated when incidental to the exported goods. Eligible medical devices for human use, including diagnostic imaging apparatus and associated digital detectors, supplied to qualifying purchasers such as hospitals, licensed health care institutions, or individual patients, are separately zero-rated under Schedule VI Part II, irrespective of export status, if they fall within the prescribed medical device class and are supplied for human medical use. No federal statute imposes an FX repatriation deadline on zero-rated export proceeds; the 180-day benchmark reflects customary SWIFT/TT settlement practice and CRA audit expectations on commercial substance.

  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing shipment from a Canadian port to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/TT credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the non-resident buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory third-party API clearance, or digital stamping regime is in force at the federal Canadian level. CRA requires registrants to retain complete books and records in electronic or paper readable format under ETA s. 286(1). Mandatory electronic filing of GST/HST returns applies to registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 10,000,000 for reporting periods beginning after December 31, 2023, and will extend to registrants exceeding CAD 1,500,000 for reporting periods beginning after December 31, 2025; filings are made through GST/HST NETFILE or certified electronic data interchange. E-invoicing via Peppol interoperability was announced in the 2023 federal budget as a voluntary national standard; it is not a clearance mandate. Electronic remittance of GST/HST amounts owing is required for most monthly and quarterly filers once registration is active.

CRA may deny zero-rating and reassess net tax within the normal four-year assessment window under ETA s. 298(1); the limitation is extended to seven years where misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, willful default, or fraud is established under s. 298(2); no limitation period applies where a false statement is made with intent to evade payment. Penalties include failure-to-file and failure-to-remit penalties of 6% of the balance plus additional monthly components under ETA ss. 280-282, gross negligence penalties equal to 50% of the tax sought to be hidden under s. 285, civil false statement penalties under s. 285.1, and criminal liability under s. 286 with fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 12 months for fraudulent export zero-rating schemes. CRA may recover refunded GST/HST with prescribed compound interest, cancel the registration under s. 242.1, and assert personal liability against directors and corporate officers under s. 323; contemporaneous CERS filings, freight forwarder documentation, customs stamps, and bank remittance records constitute the sole mitigating evidence in audit and objection proceedings.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a fully convertible currency on both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange control laws, no central bank licensing requirements for cross-border remittances, and no restrictions on the repatriation of capital or profits for the residence-based taxation model. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is subject to Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (generally 25%, reduced by applicable treaty), but no prior exchange-control approval is required. Dividends paid out of a business corporation engaged in ISIC 3250 medical hardware manufacturing are treated as ordinary remittances; the payor must withhold and remit the required amount to the Canada Revenue Agency and file NR4/TPR declaration returns.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no specific deadline for export proceeds. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency in overseas accounts, invoice in USD or other currencies, and hedge freely. Reporting obligations are limited to the CRA's T1134/T1135 information returns for foreign affiliates and specified foreign property, U.S. customs filings for cross-border shipments, and the Canada Border Services Agency's export reporting under the Customs Act. A corporate exporter of amorphous silicon cesium iodide DR detectors must maintain complete commercial invoice, bill of lading, and proof-of-export documentation for GST/HST zero-rating purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential supervision of banks and foreign exchange dealing oversight is shared with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administrative body; FINTRAC monitors cross-border fund movements under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for supplementary disbursement channels but Low as a sole treasury rail. Canada does not recognise a separate 'EMI' licence; fintech and cross-border payment platforms operate as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) required to register with FINTRAC, and those engaged in retail payment activities may need authorisation under the Retail Payment Activities Act administered by the Bank of Canada. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud white-label partners) may obtain Canadian MSB registration and pass through reputational due diligence provided they implement FINTRAC KYC beneficial-ownership controls. However, for high-value ISIC 3250 medical device imports, an EMI cannot issue or discount documentary letters of credit and does not offer true deposit insurance. Use regulated Schedule I bank accounts as the primary rail, with EMI accounts as a secondary conduit for small vendor payments only after legal review of accession agreements and OSFI outsourcing guidance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity routing requires SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the Canada-based operating account, with message field 70 clearly designating remittance information as 'share capital contribution for issuance of common shares' or 'paid-in surplus'. The corporation must issue a board resolution specifying share consideration, and the bank may require evidence that the source of funds is held with a financial institution in the shareholder's country and subject to FATF-equivalent AML controls. Canada has no exchange-control registration or central bank approval for capital injection; however, the recipient must file cross-border electronic fund transfer declarations only for FINTRAC-required amounts, and the shareholder must report the investment under the OECD CRS / FATCA framework if applicable. For prudent intra-group liquidity, structure the injection as a first tranche of non-amortising subordinated debt or share subscription agreement, with a legal opinion confirming the transfer does not breach Canadian economic sanctions or the Investment Canada Act threshold for controlling a Canadian business. The account should be labelled 'Capital Escrow / Subscription Clearing Account' until the share issuance certificates are certified and the CRA T2 share capital schedule is prepared.Primary settlement rails are SWIFT MT103 for vendor invoices and SWIFT MT202/MT202COV for treasury interbank transfers to correspondent banks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium for supplementary disbursement channels but Low as a sole treasury rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000 in aggregate controlled cross-border transactions in a taxation year (Form T106 reporting threshold); contemporaneous Local File is required for controlled transactions, and a Master File is expected for multinational arrangements with consolidated group revenue above C$2 billion per OECD-aligned CRA guidance.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no separate standalone ESR, but CRA may recharacterize an arrangement lacking economic substance under the general anti-avoidance rule (s.245) and common law substance-over-form doctrines. Beneficial ownership and actual business activity are necessary to claim treaty benefits and to avoid GAAR reassessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false statements

Statutory sanction: Fine under s.239 of the Income Tax Act from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded, plus imprisonment for up to two years on summary conviction (up to five years on indictment); CRA will also reassess and add gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2) and compound interest.

Director personal liability: A director who directs or assents to fraudulent filings may be jointly and severally liable for civil fraud; CRA may obtain a court order imposing travel restrictions/denial of a Canadian passport where payment is evaded; criminal prosecution of directors can result in incarceration.
Shell company or sham corporate arrangement

Statutory sanction: CRA may disregard the corporate entity, reassess income at the Canadian beneficiary/operator level, apply a full Part XIII withholding tax of 25% on deemed payments, invoke GAAR anti-avoidance adjustments and transfer pricing penalties, and issue an assessment disallowing treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud, inequitable conduct, or wrongful preference, a director may be held personally liable for the corporation's assessed tax, including unremitted Part XIII and employee payroll taxes under s.227.1; CRA can obtain a court order against personal assets.
AML compliance failure

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to C$1 million per violation for a large business, plus potential criminal liability under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; the company may be struck from the register after persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be subject to personal fines if they participate or knowingly fail to prevent AML violations; executive officers can be prosecuted for money-laundering offences with imprisonment up to 10 years.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing penalty under s.247(3): 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds CA$5 million or 10% of gross revenue; CRA will otherwise make full intercompany adjustment and may impose gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false or incomplete T106/T2 returns risk joint personal liability for the corporate tax assessment and criminal sanction if the documentation failure is part of a deliberate evasion scheme.
