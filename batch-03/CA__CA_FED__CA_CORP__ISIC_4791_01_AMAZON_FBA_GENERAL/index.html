Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the CBCA. The corporation may issue one or more shares with or without par value, and no escrow account, capital deposit, or paid-up capital minimum is imposed. Consideration for issued shares may be satisfied in money, property, or past service under CBCA s. 25(1). Non-resident ownership is permitted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the National Fire Code and provincial fire codes; occupancy clearance under the National Building Code; and occupational health and safety compliance under Canada Labour Code Part II for all physical workplaces. No specialized structural load inspection is required unless racking, mezzanines, or other modifications exceed the applicable building permit threshold; WHMIS training is required for staff handling cleaning or hazardous materials.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the CBCA. The corporation may issue one or more shares with or without par value, and no escrow account, capital deposit, or paid-up capital minimum is imposed. Consideration for issued shares may be satisfied in money, property, or past service under CBCA s. 25(1). Non-resident ownership is permitted.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: (1) The Corporation subscribes for and accepts all issued shares described in the Articles of Incorporation and records paid-up capital in accordance with the Canada Business Corporations Act. (2) The registered office shall be and remain at the address filed with Corporations Canada until changed by board resolution. (3) The following officers are appointed: Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer, and Corporate Secretary, each with standard executive functions, signing powers, and duties imposed by the by-laws; (4) Officers are directed to register the Corporation with taxation authorities, open the Corporation’s bank accounts, execute operational contracts, and take all necessary industrial and municipal licensing steps.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no federal or provincial industrial licensing board administers an operating permit for ISIC 4791 (retail sale via internet). For physical premises, the competent authority is the municipal business licensing office in the operating city (e.g., Municipal Licensing and Standards Division).

Category: Not applicable — no statutory operating or manufacturing license is mandated for a non-manufacturing e-commerce retail marketplace activity. A general municipal business license and occupancy certificate may be required where an office or fulfillment space is leased.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – official portal: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers import/export fiscal controls via the CARM portal.

E-government filing portal

A federally or provincially incorporated Business Corporation obtains its 9-digit Canadian Business Number (BN) through CRA after incorporation (Corporations Canada or provincial registry). Program accounts: RC for GST/HST and RM for corporate income tax. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST within one month of exceeding CAD 30,000 in taxable Canadian supplies in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; the non-resident simplified registration is available via Form RC4592 or online through CRA's Non-Resident Registration desk. For Amazon FBA marketplace-facilitated sales, Amazon is the deemed GST/HST supplier effective 1 July 2021 and collects/remits tax; the seller remains liable for corporate income tax registration and must file Form RC1 to obtain the BN if no other program account exists. Voluntary registration is permitted below threshold only if a taxable supply is being made in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box flow-through election exists for a Canadian Business Corporation. Domestic elections include: section 85 rollover election (tax-deferred property transfer to a corporation); section 83(2) capital dividend election; section 249.1 non-calendar-year fiscal period election; Capital Cost Allowance class elections; and section 150(2) treaty election for foreign affiliates. Hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act ss. 12.8, 18.7, 20.2, 94A) apply to payments made after 1 December 2023 and contain ordering and adjustment rules rather than elective opt-out mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies made in Canada in the immediately preceding four calendar quarters and the current quarter (federal small-supplier threshold); non-resident suppliers using the simplified digital/e-commerce GST/HST regime apply the same CAD 30,000 specified threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated status applies to tangible personal property physically exported by the supplier (Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V, section 20) and to services supplied to a non-resident meeting the exported-service criteria (Schedule VI, Part V, section 7) when the service is not enjoyed in Canada. The supplier must export the goods as soon as reasonable after delivery and before tax becomes payable, and must retain contemporaneous evidence of physical export, foreign payment, and commercial terms to support the 0% rate and associated input tax credit/refund claims.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA ACI eManifest via Canadian Export Declaration or CARM portal)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Control Document evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order specifying exporter, buyer, HS classification, value, and destination country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange proof of payment from the non-resident purchaser for offshore services or intangible supplies)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Canada E-Invoicing Interoperability Framework (Peppol BIS 3.0). Registrants must retain electronic books and records in a coherent machine-readable format (Excise Tax Act, section 286) for six years; export zero-rating evidence must be retained as required to substantiate claims upon CRA audit.

CRA may reassess zero-rated supplies within the four-year normal reassessment period, or without time limit in cases of misrepresentation or fraud, clawing back uncollected GST/HST plus compounding daily arrears interest. Civil penalties include Excise Tax Act section 285 false-statement penalties (25% of the misstated net tax), section 280.1 failure-to-file penalties, and Income Tax Act section 163(2) gross negligence penalties (50% of understated corporate income tax). Fraudulent export zero-rating also exposes the seller to CBSA seizure, forfeiture, loss of export privileges, and criminal prosecution under the Customs Act and Criminal Code.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility for current account transactions. Capital account transactions are subject to Bank of Canada review and approval for certain thresholds and investor types. Profit remittances from Canadian corporations are permitted without restriction when tied to operational business activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Bank of Canada. Foreign exchange transactions must be reported to FINTRAC for AML compliance. For cross-border e-commerce sales, proceeds are received in foreign currency and converted at market rates through licensed banks.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$5,000,000

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) framework (as in offshore financial centres) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245 to disregard arrangements lacking genuine economic substance. Additionally, CRA may re-characterize transactions under transfer pricing rules and rely on treaty abuse denials in cross-border situations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239(1): on indictment, fine of up to 200% of tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil reassessment and gross negligence penalties of 50% of understated tax are also applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or authorize evasion may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax and penalties; criminal exposure can lead to travel bans, extradition procedures, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment under GAAR (s.245) denying the tax benefit and imposing interest and penalizing adjustments; possible transfer pricing re-characterization under s.247.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable as promoters or planners of the avoidance arrangement; penalties include civil restitution, future compliance obligations, and in file-closure cases, criminal sanctions for culpable conduct.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.247(2)(3) transfer pricing adjustments, secondary adjustments, and administrative penalties equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the aggregate value of transactions exceeds CA$5,000,000 in the year.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly sign false transfer pricing documentation can be liable as participants in a false statement for tax purposes; penalties may include fines and imprisonment under s.239(1).
Failure to Remit Part XIII Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Unpaid withholdings being assessed as a debt owed to the Crown, plus interest and penalties; CRA may reassess the payer as the agent of the non-resident and collect the amounts due.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under Income Tax Act s.227.1 if they were in control at the time the obligation arose, without benefit of the due diligence defence.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, failure to comply can result in administrative monetary penalties up to CA$500,000 for a corporation and imprisonment for individuals; Criminal Code laundering offences carry sentences up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or fail to prevent laundering activities may be criminally liable and personally subject to asset seizure, civil forfeiture, travel prohibition, and conviction-based imprisonment.
