Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the BCBCA; a ULC may issue shares without par value and no escrow or capital deposit is required. The board must ensure any issued shares are fully paid for by lawful consideration; for an IP HoldCo, in-kind contribution of licensed intellectual property is permissible if approved by the board at fair market value.

Funding & unlock path: Exempt for a passive IP HoldCo. The only occupational clearance required is the landlord's compliance with the British Columbia Building Code (structural safety) and the Fire Safety Act / BC Fire Code for office use. The Company itself has no employees performing physical manufacturing; any administrative staff working in the office must comply with worker safety provisions of the British Columbia Workers Compensation Act and WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety regulations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; business registration portal: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-registration-online.html, and My Business Account (MyBA) at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic entity carrying on business in Canada must apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using Form RC1, 'Request for a Business Number', or through the CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) service. The BN is the core national tax identifier and can have program accounts (e.g., GST/HST, payroll, import/export) attached. For a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (BC ULC), the first step is incorporation or extra-provincial registration under the BC Business Corporations Act via BC Corporate Online, after which the ULC must complete CRA registration for both corporate income tax and GST/HST program accounts. Non-resident corporations without Canadian residency or a permanent establishment must nevertheless register for GST/HST where they make taxable supplies in Canada, and may be required to provide treaty residency documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For Canadian federal and provincial income tax purposes, a BC ULC is treated as a regular taxable corporation; there is no domestic equivalent of a US check-the-box election. However, for US federal tax purposes, a BC ULC may make a check-the-box election under IRC § 7701 using IRS Form 8832 to be treated as a disregarded entity (pass-through) if all members consent. Additional hybrid-mismatch-centered election mechanisms include elections under Income Tax Act subsection 216(1) for non-resident rental income, subsection 219(5) regarding branch tax allocation, and treaty-based 'competent authority' determinations regarding residency and dual application of Canada-US tax treaty rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (the small-supplier threshold); non-residents must register if their taxable supplies exceed this threshold unless the supplier is a small supplier. BC PST: a business must register before making taxable sales or leases in British Columbia on a regular or continuing basis; BC PST does not provide a general economic turnover threshold exemption for non-resident vendors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act, supplies of patents, trademarks, copyrights and similar intangible property are zero-rated for GST/HST purposes when made to a recipient who is a non-resident of Canada, is not registered for GST/HST, and is not using the property in the course of a GST/HST business carried on in Canada. Services intended for offshore use may also be zero-rated if the supply is made to a non-resident person and the service is not performed in, or consumed in, Canada. For goods, export supplies qualify for zero-rating when the vendor exports them or delivers them to an exporter within 60 days, supported by objective export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA Form B13 / Canadian Automated Export Declaration, or CARM export reporting) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill, freight manifest, or courier documentation showing departure from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT beneficiary message evidencing the FX conversion and repatriation of the royalty payment

Canada has no mandatory national electronic-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate. CRA accepts electronic records in a variety of formats, including EDI and Peppol BIS-based documents; however, large businesses must file GST/HST returns and certain information returns electronically through CRA's web services. No mandatory CRA API clearance or fiscal authorization is imposed for each invoice; record-keeping requirements under the Excise Tax Act must still be satisfied.

The CRA may deny zero-rating on audit and reassess within the normal three-year limitation period, or indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, fraud, or failure to file. A knowingly false statement or omission in an export zero-rating claim can trigger penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act, including up to 25% of the tax avoided, or up to 50% for false or fraudulent representations. Additionally, interest is compounded daily on all unpaid amounts, and exports made without substantiation may be clawed back with GST, PST, interest, and penalities; criminal prosecution is possible where export documentation is forged or the transaction is a sham.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility, having accepted IMF Article VIII status since 1952. There are no exchange controls, no prior-authorization requirements for cross-border payments, and no approval gate for royalty or dividend remittance. Practical constraints are tax-driven rather than FX-driven: royalty payments to non-residents are generally subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Income Tax Act Part XIII (25% statutory rate), with a 10% rate for patent royalties where the recipient qualifies as a beneficial owner under the Canada-United States tax treaty and satisfies the limitation-on-benefits tests. Profit repatriation itself is structurally unrestricted, but banks will apply KYC, sanctions, and FINTRAC electronic-funds-transfer reporting to all high-value flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export or licensing proceeds to Canada. A BC Unlimited Liability Corporation may lawfully retain patent royalty receipts in foreign-currency bank accounts (e.g., USD clearing accounts at a Schedule I Canadian bank) indefinitely. The only practical requirements are (i) bank reporting to FINTRAC of all incoming/outgoing electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within 24 hours, (ii) sanctions/FATF screening, and (iii) transfer-pricing documentation where related-party royalties are involved.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BOC) as the central monetary authority; exchange administration and financial-sector supervision are coordinated with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and the Department of Finance Canada. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control authority; sanctions compliance is administered by Global Affairs Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary treasury venue and MINORITY as an auxiliary settlement channel. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) are not chartered banks in Canada and cannot legally accept deposits as banks; they may operate only as money services businesses and, where applicable, registered PayTech providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act with FINTRAC MSB registration. In practice, most offshore EMIs reject or restrict passive IP-holding structures, impose velocity limits, and cannot provide the formal account relationship required for a commercial loan, an escrow-capital account, or long-dated FX hedging. The ULC should maintain its core liquidity, capital escrow, and credit relationships exclusively with a Schedule I Canadian bank; a specialized EMI may be used only for discrete low-value cross-border receivables and must not hold corporate funds over CAD 10,000 for extended settlement windows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound route uses SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank funding) from the parent or shareholder, or a domestic participant-to-participant wire through the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS) for same-day CAD finality. The wire narrative must clearly state 'SHAREHOLDER CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN ADVANCE' together with the bank's assigned purpose code for direct investment/equity injection. No Canadian exchange-control authorization is required; the receiving bank must perform sanctions screening and file a FINTRAC Large Value Transaction/EFT report for CAD 10,000 or above. Funds exceeding CAD 50,000 typically require evidence of source, board resolution authorizing injection, and shareholder identification documentation. If the injection is USD-denominated, the bank will route through its US correspondent (CHIPS/Fedwire) and may open a USD clearing sub-account under the ULC's corporate banking agreement.Patent-royalty disbursements to non-resident licensees or upstream IP owners should settle by SWIFT MT103 wires through the Canadian bank's correspondent network; CAD payments to Canadian-based counterparties move through LVTS with same-day availability. USD cross-border payments are cleared by one of the bank's US correspondents (CHIPS or Fedwire) with a stable corporate identifier and instructing party name. Commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) are permitted and can be structured for bundled intellectual property licensing transactions with documentary milestones, but they are not required for pure royalty route: for LC usage, the issuing Schedule I bank will require a facility agreement, an underlying licensing contract, and statused delivery certificates. Turnover: BCBCA imposes a governance-based audit obligation rather than a revenue/asset threshold: an auditor must be appointed at the annual general meeting unless shareholders pass a special resolution (or written unanimous consent for a private company) waiving the appointment. Mandatory audit applies if the ULC is a public/reporting issuer, if the waiver resolution is not renewed annually, or if a regulator, lender, or the Canada Revenue Agency requires audited financial statements.
