Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the BCBCA; a ULC may be capitalized with one share issued for no par value and nominal consideration. No escrow account or capital deposit is required at incorporation. Shareholders are statutorily jointly and severally liable without limit for the company's debts and liabilities under BCBCA s. 51.6, which liability persists notwithstanding any capital contribution made.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the local authority having jurisdiction under the BC Fire Code, including emergency egress and suppression systems; structural load inspection certifying that office floor systems support IT server racks, battery backup systems and associated equipment per BC Building Code live load limits; occupational health and safety clearance registered with WorkSafeBC under the Workers Compensation Act, covering ergonomics, electrical safety and emergency evacuation planning; accessibility compliance per BC Building Code Division B.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – My Business Account portal (GST/HST, Business Number); British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (BC PST registration)

E-government filing portal

The BC ULC must obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from CRA by filing Form RC1 via My Business Account or by mail. It must register for GST/HST if its taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in the previous four consecutive quarters or if it is making taxable supplies in Canada other than as a small supplier. Non-resident entities should use CRA's simplified non-resident registration process. For BC, a separate PST registration is required with the BC Ministry of Finance where the corporation has a physical presence or makes PST-taxable sales (including specified software/digital services) into British Columbia. The ULC, being locally incorporated, registers using its BC registered office address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under subsection 115.2(2) of the Income Tax Act, a qualifying ULC whose shareholders are all residents of a treaty partner may elect to be deemed a non-resident corporation for Canadian tax purposes. For U.S. shareholders, a check-the-box election (Form 8832) can treat the ULC as a disregarded entity for U.S. federal income tax purposes, but this does not alter its Canadian entity-level taxation unless the section 115.2 election is available. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules under section 18.4 of the Income Tax Act may adjust tax character mismatches resulting from such elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small supplier threshold over the trailing four calendar quarters); for non-resident digital suppliers, mandatory GST/HST registration also arises when Canadian consumer sales exceed CAD 30,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The cybersecurity SaaS subscription and associated pen-testing/vulnerability-scanning services may be zero-rated for GST/HST if the supply is made to a non-resident recipient, the recipient is not in Canada, and the service is performed entirely outside Canada or delivered for consumption outside Canada. Intangible personal property (SaaS access) is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 and applicable rules for intangible personal property supplied to non-residents. The supplier must demonstrate that no domestic use occurs in Canada and the supply qualifies as an export for Excise Tax Act purposes.

  • Executed SaaS agreement and commercial invoice specifying the non-resident buyer, Canadian or foreign place of performance, and remote delivery of cloud-based security services
  • Customs export declaration (B13A/Canadian export report) only if tangible equipment or software media is physically exported; not required for pure cloud SaaS deliveries
  • Bill of lading or airway bill, when physical deliverables exist; for SaaS, infrastructure hosting logs and IP geolocation evidence showing non-Canadian access points
  • Bank statement and Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) evidencing wire transfer or foreign-currency payment from the non-resident purchaser's bank account
  • Third-party technical records (e.g., SaaS tenant audit logs, cloud provider reports, penetration-testing reports addressed to the buyer) demonstrating that the service was supplied and consumed outside Canada

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. GST/HST registrants must issue invoices containing prescribed particulars and file returns electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account. For zero-rated exports, suppliers must retain sufficient documentary evidence. Non-resident simplified-registration suppliers use CRA's electronic portal. No government pre-approval of invoices is required.

CRA may reassess within the four-year normal period for corporations, and longer in cases of negligence, wilful misrepresentation, or fraud. If zero-rating substantiation fails, the supply is recharacterized as taxable and subject to 5% GST plus applicable BC PST, with interest compounded daily. Gross negligence penalties under the Excise Tax Act (section 285.1) can reach 50% of the amount of tax evaded, plus a minimum of CAD 500. Criminal prosecution is possible for fraudulent export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under the Currency Act and the Bank of Canada Act. No exchange controls, no approvals for inward/outward FDI, and no restrictions on profit remittance; non-resident shareholders may freely repatriate dividends, interest, royalties, and capital proceeds, subject only to Income Tax Act (Canada) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax and treaty-based rate reductions. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is a freely floating, widely traded currency; settlement occurs through CLS and Canadian Large Value Transfer System (LVTS)/Lynx.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or export-proceeds surrender obligation. Export receipts (e.g., SaaS subscription revenue from US customers) may be retained in foreign currency bank accounts or converted to CAD at the commercial bank's discretion; income recognition and functional currency alignment are governed by IFRS/ASPE and, for Canadian tax purposes, the Income Tax Act s. 261 functional currency election.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/ATF supervision by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC). No standalone Foreign Exchange Administration authority exists, as Canada maintains no exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Feasibility: Medium-High with legal constraints. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent through its correspondent bank to the Canadian bank's SWIFT BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC); the credit to the ULC operating account must be tagged with purpose of 'capital contribution / equity injection' and supporting instruction (remittance detail) referencing the executed subscription agreement. Cross-border wire transfers of C$10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC. For high-value settlement (CAD > C$100,000), clearing is via Lynx (formerly LVTS) or the new Real-Time Rail (RTR) ISO 20022; the bank requires an Advance OSFI-compliant instruction and may request an FX conversion ticket (USD to CAD) at the agreed commercial exchange rate. Copies of the SWIFT MT103 and proof of origin are retained as part of the CDD/EDD record.Disbursements for trade settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 (single payment) for vendor/contractor payments; for larger SaaS infrastructure procurement or IP licensing, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) is issued by the Canadian bank's trade finance desk (commonly BMO, RBC or CIBC) with clean/document presentation against invoices and AWB/electronic delivery certificates. Domestic Canadian disbursements clear through Lynx/RTR and Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS); cross-border USD payments can be processed via the Canadian bank's US correspondent (clearing through CHIPS or Fedwire).
