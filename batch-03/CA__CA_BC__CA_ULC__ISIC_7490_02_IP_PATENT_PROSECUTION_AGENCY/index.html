Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC). Online portal: My Business Account (MyBA) and Business Registration Online (BRO) at cra-arc.gc.ca; provincial BC integration via OneStop BC / Corporate Online.

E-government filing portal

On incorporation of the ULC under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act (SBC 2002, c.57), the CRA automatically issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with a Corporate Income Tax program account (RC). If the ULC carries on commercial activity and is not a small supplier, it must open a GST/HST program account (RT) by filing Form RC1 or through the CRA 'Register' function in MyBA. A BC resident attorney or agent for service of process is required for non-resident owners. The ULC must file an annual T2 Corporate Income Tax Return and a BC Corporate Tax Return, even if no federal or provincial tax is payable, and must maintain a BC Registrar-filed annual report.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: For Canadian domestic tax, a BC ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation subject to federal Part I corporate income tax and BC provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income if resident in Canada; Pass_Through_Eligible: For US federal tax purposes only, the ULC is an eligible entity that may be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity via IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election; Territorial_Scope: Canada taxes the ULC on worldwide income, with foreign tax credits for eligible foreign taxes.

Available election forms

No Canadian domestic check-the-box election exists; the operative pass-through election is the US IRS Form 8832 by US shareholders. For GST/HST, an election under s.156 of the Excise Tax Act may be filed to treat supplies between closely-related registrants as non-taxable. Under the Canada-US Tax Convention, Article IV residence tie-breaker and Articles X and XII rate reductions for cross-border dividends and royalties apply; a 5% withholding rate on dividends is available to a US corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of the ULC's voting stock. Hybrid mismatch legislation consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2 is not yet effective in Canada.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: the GST/HST small-supplier threshold applies when worldwide taxable supplies are below CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter and in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Mandatory registration applies once either test exceeds CAD 30,000; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Patent prosecution and international trademark services supplied to a non-resident client may be zero-rated under s.9 of Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act if: (1) the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST at the time the service is performed; (2) the service does not relate to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, or an individual present in Canada; and (3) the supplier retains contemporaneous evidence of the foreign recipient's status, the written contract, and receipt of payment from outside Canada. The legal destination of the underlying patent or trademark registration does not, by itself, defeat zero-rating for the exported professional service.

  • Executed engagement letter or patent prosecution agency agreement with the non-resident client, including material terms and jurisdiction of the underlying IP filings
  • Zero-rated commercial invoice or WIPO/CIPO fee transmittal referencing 'Exported service - zero-rated under ETA Schedule VI Part V s.9'
  • SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or Wise transaction record evidencing inward remittance from the non-resident client's foreign bank account
  • Non-resident status evidence: foreign certificate of incorporation, tax residency certificate, or notarized declaration from the client confirming non-residency
  • PCT, CIPO, USPTO, or EPO correspondence confirming the client's ownership of the patent or trademark application and the ULC's prosecution role
  • Where physical supporting exhibits are shipped, a CBSA export declaration via CERS (B13A) may be required above the CAD 2,000 threshold; otherwise, no customs export declaration is mandatory for pure services

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing clearance regime. CRA accepts paper and electronic invoices under ETA Part IX; registrants with annual revenues above CAD 1 million must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI. CRA is promoting voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing, but issuance remains optional.

CRA may reassess within 4 years from the original assessment, extended to 6 years for misrepresentation and unlimited for fraud. If zero-rating is not substantiated at audit, the full service fee is assessed with GST at 5%, plus daily compound interest at prescribed CRA rates, and any refund or input tax credit previously claimed is clawed back. Knowingly or grossly negligent false statements attract a penalty under s.285(1) ETA equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of the tax sought to be evaded. Canada imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is an internal control standard. All export substantiation records must be retained for at least 6 years from the end of the taxation year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account. The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely floating, with no surrender, licensing, stamp, or prior-approval requirements for capital inflows, dividend repatriation, loan servicing, or conversion into USD, EUR, or CHF. A British Columbia Unlimited Liability Company (BC ULC) formed under the Business Corporations Act, S.B.C. 2002, c.57, s.51.1 may, when all shareholders are US residents, be treated as fiscally transparent / disregarded for US federal tax purposes under Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Tax Convention (Fifth Protocol), while remaining a resident Canadian corporation for domestic Part I income tax. Profit remittance is unrestricted; the only structural hurdle is Part XIII non-resident withholding tax on dividends (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced to 5%/15% under the Canada-US treaty) plus NR4 filing obligations. Practical banking constraints are limited to FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Reports for each inbound/outbound transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, CRS/FATCA self-certifications, and source-of-funds evidence for non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts or service proceeds. A BC ULC earning patent prosecution fees, PCT filing revenues, and international trademark registration income may invoice in CAD, USD, EUR, or CHF, retain foreign currency indefinitely in resident multi-currency accounts at Canadian Schedule I banks or in offshore nostro accounts, and settle liabilities in any convertible currency. For tax purposes, foreign-exchange gains and losses on revenue account are taxable and deductible under subsection 39(2) of the Income Tax Act, R.S.C. 1985, c.1 (5th Supp.), and transfer pricing rules under section 247 require arm's-length documentation for any intercompany fee or royalty flows.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the central monetary authority and fiscal agent for the Exchange Fund Account of the Government of Canada; foreign exchange administration is minimal because Canada abolished statutory exchange controls with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act in 1951. The prudential and conduct perimeter is shared by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for Schedule I and Schedule II banks, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision and large/electronic transfer reporting at the CAD 10,000 threshold, and Payments Canada for the operation of the Lynx real-time gross settlement (RTGS) system and the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). No Canadian ministry or agency requires pre-approval of individual spot or forward foreign-exchange transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary vendor disbursements (Wise Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer operate as FINTRAC-registered Money Services Businesses in Canada and can perform low-value CAD/USD/EUR conversion for monthly operating expenses below CAD 50,000). Legal constraints: (i) client retainer funds and official-fee escrow monies of a patent agency must be held at a Canadian deposit-taking financial institution because the College of Patent Agents and Trademark Agents (CPATA) Code of Professional Conduct and trust-account bylaws require client funds to be safeguarded with a regulated Canadian chartered bank, trust company, or credit union that is CDIC-insured; fintech EMIs are not CDIC members and therefore cannot serve as a trust depository; (ii) EMIs lack direct access to Lynx RTGS and ACSS high-value clearing, routing instead through partner banks and adding intermediary delay risk for time-critical patent office filings; (iii) WIPO, USPTO, and CIPO may decline second-tier EMI correspondent routings. Conclusion: Wise is viable only as a supplementary rail for small vendor and expense payments, never as the primary clearing, escrow, or capital account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Simplified/comprehensive contemporary TP documentation generally expected where aggregate non-arm's-length cross-border transactions exceed CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year; mandatory Country-by-Country and Master File reporting triggers at CAD 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute equivalent to UAE ESR operating as a defined carve-out regime; instead the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, treaty limitation-on-benefits doctrines, beneficial ownership analysis, and functional/risk substance review to deny treaty and domestic deductions when a BC ULC is a nominee shell or lacks real Canadian management, employees, or transaction substance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Blindness

Statutory sanction: Gross negligence penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax under ITA subsection 163(2); additional 100% penalty for repeated evasion; criminal prosecution under ITA section 327 carries imprisonment up to 5 years plus a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded; interest assessed daily on unpaid balances

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate or authorize non-reporting are jointly and severally liable for the unreported tax; the CRA may issue director liability assessments under sections 227.1 and 160; criminal convictions can trigger conditional jail sentences, travel bans, and permanent disqualification from board service in Canada
Transfer Pricing Disqualification

Statutory sanction: CRA reassessment of the transaction at an arm's-length amount; transfer pricing penalty under ITA subsection 247(3) equal to 10% of the net adjustment where reasonable efforts are absent; minimum penalty provisions apply; daily compound interest added to all reassessed amounts

Director personal liability: Officers who sign false transfer pricing documentation may be pursued for civil fraud and negligent misrepresentation; statutory director liability may extend to unpaid Part XIII withholdings and unremitted tax if the corporation defaults; directors can be barred from cross-border travel pending tax collection investigations
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Loss of treaty protection reclassification of the BC ULC as a taxable Canadian agent or permanent establishment; GAAR denial of treaty and domestic tax benefits; reinstatement of full 25% Part XIII withholding on dividends; penalties up to 25% of the tax benefit and reassessments without the normal limitation period where misrepresentation is shown

Director personal liability: Unlimited liability corporation shareholders and directors may be held directly liable for the corporation's unpaid taxes, statutory interest, and penalties if the entity is treated as a sham; civil piercing of the corporate veil allows tax authorities to collect liabilities from personal assets; director/officer disqualification and travel bans may follow asset tracing orders
AML/CFT and FINTRAC Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalty of up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for a business entity under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act; criminal money-laundering convictions carry imprisonment up to 10 years and fines in the discretion of the court

Director personal liability: Senior officers bear personal liability for failure to maintain a FINTRAC compliance program, appoint a compliance officer, and train staff; individual penalties up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment for reckless non-compliant conduct; directors can be personally disgorged for secretly funneling client funds through unlicensed payment rails
