Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). British Columbia income tax is collected under the federal-provincial tax collection agreement; BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign parent establishing a BC ULC must (1) incorporate the ULC under the BC Business Corporations Act and obtain a BC Corporate Registry number, incorporation documents, and a registered office address in BC; (2) apply to CRA for a statutory 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or the online business registration portal, simultaneously opening GST/HST (RT), corporate income tax (RC/RM) and, if applicable, payroll (RP) program accounts; (3) appoint a Canadian resident representative acceptable to CRA for service of process and maintain corporate records (minute books, directors register, share register) at the BC registered office; (4) register with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch for a PST account if any taxable supplies will be made in BC; (5) file the initial T2 Corporate Income Tax Return and annual BC provincial filings; non-resident owners must provide their home-jurisdiction TINs for withholding tax administration and treaty rate reduction claims.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A BC ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under the federal Income Tax Act and the BC Income Tax Act; however, a ULC wholly owned by US shareholders is eligible for US check-the-box pass-through classification (disregarded entity or partnership), and, pursuant to Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Income Tax Convention as applied in Canadian administrative practice, the entity may be treated as fiscally transparent for treaty purposes so that Canadian-source income is reported directly by US owners. Domestic Canadian law continues to apply entity-level corporate taxation to the ULC unless the treaty look-through treatment is elected and recognized. — A foreign parent establishing a BC ULC must (1) incorporate the ULC under the BC Business Corporations Act and obtain a BC Corporate Registry number, incorporation documents, and a registered office address in BC; (2) apply to CRA for a statutory 9-digit Business Number (BN) using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or the online business registration portal, simultaneously opening GST/HST (RT), corporate income tax (RC/RM) and, if applicable, payroll (RP) program accounts; (3) appoint a Canadian resident representative acceptable to CRA for service of process and maintain corporate records (minute books, directors register, share register) at the BC registered office; (4) register with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch for a PST account if any taxable supplies will be made in BC; (5) file the initial T2 Corporate Income Tax Return and annual BC provincial filings; non-resident owners must provide their home-jurisdiction TINs for withholding tax administration and treaty rate reduction claims.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible — A BC ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under the federal Income Tax Act and the BC Income Tax Act; however, a ULC wholly owned by US shareholders is eligible for US check-the-box pass-through classification (disregarded entity or partnership), and, pursuant to Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Income Tax Convention as applied in Canadian administrative practice, the entity may be treated as fiscally transparent for treaty purposes so that Canadian-source income is reported directly by US owners. Domestic Canadian law continues to apply entity-level corporate taxation to the ULC unless the treaty look-through treatment is elected and recognized.

        
Available election forms
(1) US federal check-the-box election on Form 8832 under Treas. Reg. Section 301.7701-3 as amplified by Rev. Proc. 2009-15, permitting a wholly US-owned ULC to be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax purposes; (2) joint election and application to CRA for look-through or fiscally transparent treatment under Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Income Tax Convention to align Canadian treaty treatment with the US classification; (3) hybrid mismatch elections, ordering rules, and surrogate provisions under the Income Tax Act hybrid mismatch regime (effective for payments arising on or after 1 July 2022) to neutralize deduction or non-inclusion mismatches caused by entity classification differences; (4) GST/HST joint election under Excise Tax Act section 273 permitting related corporations or persons to file consolidated GST returns; (5) Part XIII withholding tax elections on dividends and distributions to US residents to obtain treaty-reduced rates where the applicable conditions are satisfied.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (GST/HST small supplier threshold per Excise Tax Act section 148(1)); once exceeded, GST/HST registration is mandatory. Non-resident suppliers making supplies in Canada must register upon exceeding the threshold or immediately upon commencing a taxable business in Canada where required. BC PST registration is required before the first taxable sale is made in BC, with no monetary threshold exemption applicable.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
A B2B enterprise SaaS supply by a BC ULC is zero-rated under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 (supply of a service to a non-resident recipient) where the recipient is a non-resident not registered under the Excise Tax Act and none of the statutory exceptions apply: the service must not be supplied to an individual present in Canada, must not relate to real property situated in Canada, must not relate to tangible personal property situated in Canada, and must not be an advisory or consultancy supply to a Canadian resident. The SaaS must be delivered electronically to the foreign enterprise, with the licensed software and consuming systems located outside Canada. Certain exports of software licences as intangible personal property may also qualify for zero-rating under Schedule VI, Part V where the recipient is a non-resident. BC PST does not apply to electronic SaaS consumed by non-residents outside British Columbia.

Canada does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing regime for GST/HST. CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian B2B e-invoicing pilot aligned with the PEPPOL network and accepts electronically generated invoices and records. GST/HST returns, including zero-rated export schedules, must be filed electronically through CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE. Invoices must satisfy Excise Tax Act section 223 information requirements including the supplier GST registration number, tax status, and amounts; supporting records must be retained for six years. BC PST returns are filed online with the Consumer Taxation Branch.

      
CRA export zero-rating audits focus on proof of non-resident status, actual offshore delivery, and commercial substance. Knowingly making a false or fraudulent statement in a return attracts penalties under Excise Tax Act section 280 of the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax understated, escalating to 50% for repeat violations; gross negligence penalties are similarly applied to unsubstantiated zero-rating claims. CRA may reassess beyond the normal four-year limitation period under Excise Tax Act section 298 where there is neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, reverse the zero-rating, and assess compound daily arrears interest under section 286. Directors and senior officers of the ULC face joint and several personal liability for unremitted GST under section 323. Criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion under Excise Tax Act section 289 and the Income Tax Act may result in fines and imprisonment, and clawbacks include repayment of input tax credits claimed on inputs attributable to the zero-rated exports, together with interest and penalties computed from the original filing date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime; Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no exchange controls, FX licensing, quotas, prior approval, or repatriation restriction on current or capital account transactions. A BC ULC may lawfully receive capital subscriptions, hold CAD and foreign-currency accounts, invoice foreign customers, and freely remit dividends, interest, royalties, or intercompany payments. The only material friction is fiscal: Part XIII withholding tax applies to dividends, interest, royalties, rent, and management fees paid to non-residents (statutory rate 25%, generally reduced by treaty; e.g., the Canada-US Treaty provides a 5% qualified-rate dividend for a company that owns at least 10% of the voting shares, and 15% in the general case), with NR4 information returns and timely remittance required. No profit-repatriation licence, blocked-funds mechanism, or convertibility hurdle exists at the federal or British Columbia provincial level.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio in Canada. A BC ULC may retain 100% of foreign-currency export receipts in Canadian or offshore foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. The relevant compliance duties are record-keeping and reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: every financial institution must report cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period to FINTRAC (ECFTR), maintain certain large-value transaction records, and individuals must declare physical currency or monetary instruments valued at CAD 10,000 or more when entering or leaving Canada via CBSA.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the central monetary authority; prudential regulation of federally incorporated banks is exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT supervision and cross-border wire reporting obligations are administered by FINTRAC. Canada does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange control administration or exchange-control permit office.

            Fit: High

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Non-resident sole director or beneficial owner with no Canadian physical presence or address nexus
        
Root cause: Non-resident sole director or beneficial owner with no Canadian physical presence or address nexus

        
Defense evidence: Certified passport and national identity documents, residential proof of address, source-of-wealth declaration and supporting bank statements, professional resume or business background dossier, notarized corporate resolutions from BC Registry extract, and appointment of a BC-resident director with a verifiable physical office address. If enhanced due diligence remains, provide a Canadian legal opinion confirming no sanctioned-party exposure and audited financial statements evidencing legitimate B2B SaaS operations.

      

      
      

        ULC unlimited liability structure not recognized by the onboarding officer, causing misclassification as a partnership, trust, or foreign entity
        
Root cause: ULC unlimited liability structure not recognized by the onboarding officer, causing misclassification as a partnership, trust, or foreign entity

        
Defense evidence: BC Registry extract showing ULC status, annotated provisions of the British Columbia Business Corporations Act establishing the unlimited liability company, the articles of incorporation and share register, a Canadian tax counsel legal opinion confirming that the ULC is a corporation for Income Tax Act purposes and that unlimited liability does not create nominee, trust, or unlicensed fiduciary risk, and, where applicable, the shareholders' agreement and foreign flow-through election documentation.

      

      
      

        Complex or opaque ownership chain involving offshore intermediate holding companies in non-transparent jurisdictions
        
Root cause: Complex or opaque ownership chain involving offshore intermediate holding companies in non-transparent jurisdictions

        
Defense evidence: Complete organizational chart to ultimate beneficial owners, certified registry extracts and certificates of good standing for each intermediate entity, consolidated source-of-funds documentation for the entire chain, and an independent AML legal opinion confirming that no nominee shareholders are used. Remove or replace any bearer-share or bare-nominee structures before opening the account.

      

      
      

        Revenue profile inconsistent with the stated B2B enterprise SaaS business, such as unexplained large or frequent inbound wire transfers from high-risk jurisdictions
        
Root cause: Revenue profile inconsistent with the stated B2B enterprise SaaS business, such as unexplained large or frequent inbound wire transfers from high-risk jurisdictions

        
Defense evidence: Executed enterprise subscription agreements, public pricing and licensing schedules, invoicing pipeline and payment history, platform analytics or usage logs demonstrating active SaaS delivery consistent with ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210, customer KYC summaries for material counterparties, and a written business-model description addressing the recurring-revenue nature of the entity and the absence of money-transmission activity.

      

      
      

        Registered office is a mail-forwarding agent and no physical operational presence in British Columbia can be verified
        
Root cause: Registered office is a mail-forwarding agent and no physical operational presence in British Columbia can be verified

        
Defense evidence: Lease or sublease agreement for BC premises, payroll records or T4 filing evidence demonstrating local employees, utility or telecom invoices in the ULC's name, current BC corporate registry excerpt showing the actual business establishment, and director declarations supported by site photographs and a detailed operations plan establishing that active business is conducted in Canada rather than in a tax haven or shell arrangement.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant protocol for a foreign parent or subscriber funding the BC ULC: (1) execute a board resolution together with a subscription agreement identifying the class, number, and price of shares issued or the terms of a shareholder loan; (2) remit by SWIFT MT103, preferably with gpi tracking, from the remitting bank to the ULC's Canadian account, using the reference field 'Capital Contribution / Share Subscription' and, where the receiving institution requires a purpose code, the SWIFT purpose code T0300 (Equity Capital / Investment) or the bank-specific equivalent indicating direct investment; (3) deliver to the receiving bank the post-funding BC corporate registry update or an internal share register entry as evidence of completion; (4) ensure intra-group ledgers record the transaction accurately and that the receiving bank's compliance team receives the source-of-funds dossier; (5) report the transfer where applicable under FINTRAC's EFT threshold of CAD 10,000 in a 24-hour period. No Bank of Canada approval, FX permit, or exchange-control filing is required because Canada maintains full capital-account convertibility. For escrow-funded transactions, the funds should be credited to a segregated capital escrow account and released only upon satisfaction of closing conditions under a written escrow agreement.Primary clearing rails for outbound disbursements from the BC ULC: (a) SWIFT MT103 / MT103 gpi wires for international settlements, intercompany payments, licensing fees, and dividend or shareholder-distribution remittances; (b) Lynx, Canada's high-value RTGS system, for individual large-value CAD settlements, providing final and irrevocable payment; (c) the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS) for batched same-day and next-day low-value CAD credits; (d) the Real-Time Rail (RTR) for instant retail payments where enabled by the ULC's bank; and (e) foreign-currency bank drafts or cheques for low-volume vendor settlements through correspondent arrangements. Trade-related documentary instruments, including commercial letters of credit, standby letters of credit, and documentary collections in CAD or foreign currency, are available from all Tier-1 banks and remain permissible, although B2B SaaS purchases under ISIC 6201 are typically settled by wire or card rather than by documentary letter of credit. Dividend remittances to non-residents must be reported on NR4 information returns, with Part XIII withholding tax remitted at the applicable treaty-reduced rate, and all outbound EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period are subject to FINTRAC reporting and institutional transaction-monitoring.Deliverable over-the-counter FX forwards (outright forwards) in CAD/USD and other major trade currenciesVanilla European-style FX options, including calls and puts with cash settlement or physical delivery in CADCross-currency interest rate swaps (cross-currency swaps) for longer-tenor USD or multi-currency treasury liabilitiesFX swaps (foreign-exchange swaps) combining a spot and a forward leg for liquidity managementExchange-traded currency futures and options on the Montreal Exchange (TMX Group), particularly the USD/CAD futures contractNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible but commercially atypical and not recommended for CAD, given the currency's full convertibility and the existence of a liquid deliverable forward market

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
27% combined general federal-provincial corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 12% British Columbia provincial).

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% statutory rate under subsection 212(2) of the Income Tax Act (Canada). The Canada-US tax treaty reduces the rate to 5% for direct dividends paid by a US resident corporation which beneficially owns at least 10% of the voting shares of the Canadian payer; portfolio dividends are generally subject to 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 (Canadian dollars) of aggregated non-arm's length cross-border transactions with a related non-resident in a taxation year triggers the mandatory contemporaneous documentation requirements under subsection 247(4) of the Income Tax Act of Canada; additional country-by-country reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of C$750,000,000 or more.

      
Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain dedicated Economic Substance Regulations of the kind enacted in zero-tax or 'onshore shell' jurisdictions. However, economic substance is enforced indirectly through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 245, which disallows tax benefits from avoidance transactions lacking a genuine business purpose and commercial substance. The CRA also applies the sham doctrine, transfer pricing rules, permanent establishment analysis, and treaty abuse anti-conduit principles. A BC ULC that is disregarded for US federal income tax purposes must maintain Canadian board minutes, employment substance, IP ownership and administrative facts sufficient to demonstrate that it is not a hollow conduit.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act, s. 239)
        
Statutory sanction: On criminal conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalties up to 50% of the understated tax; fraud-based reassessment limitation period extended to 10 years.

        
Director personal liability: Directors, officers and senior employees who directed, assented to, or acquiesced in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, interest and penalties; criminal prosecution can result in imprisonment, travel bans, and asset freezes under the Criminal Code.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Documentation Failure (s. 247)
        
Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to the arm's length price and apply a penalty of 10% of the gross underpricing or excessive deduction if documentation is not made or furnished in a timely manner; additional penalties arise for a failure to file or comply within 90 days of CRA demand; late interest accrues from the statutory payment date.

        
Director personal liability: Transfer pricing penalties are generally corporate-level; however, a director who knowingly executes a false or misleading pricing scheme can be exposed to criminal tax evasion charges, personal director assessments for unremitted withholdings, and travel bans where the arrangement is part of a fraud or breach of fiduciary duty.

      

      
      

        Non-Resident Withholding Tax Failures (ss. 215, 227)
        
Statutory sanction: The payer is assessed the full amount that should have been withheld, plus compound daily interest; late remittance penalties start at 10% and rise to 20% for repeated failures; the offending payment may also be disallowed as a deduction from the payer's Canadian income.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s. 227.1 of the Income Tax Act for unremitted withholding tax where the corporation is insolvent and the director acted without due diligence; CRA director's certificates permit immediate civil enforcement, and deliberate failure may bring criminal liability, travel bans and imprisonment.

      

      
      

        GAAR / Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency
        
Statutory sanction: The CRA may deny the tax benefit and recharacterise the transaction under GAAR (s. 245); the expanded GAAR framework (Budget 2023) additionally imposes a 25% penalty on the avoided tax benefit in respect of avoidance transactions entered into after June 12, 2023; prolonged reassessments with interest are applied and other anti-avoidance penalties may stack.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who authorise a hollow ULC arrangement may be held personally liable for related unremitted withholding amounts if corporate assets are diverted; courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraudulent conveyance or unjust enrichment, and the CRA/DOJ may seek personal recovery, asset freezes, travel bans and criminal charges in egregious cases.

      

      
      

        AML / CFT Red Flags (PCMLTFA and Criminal Code)
        
Statutory sanction: FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1,000,000 per contravention for regulated businesses; criminal money-laundering convictions can result in imprisonment up to 5 years; failure to keep required records and reports, or failure to institute a compliance programme, yields independent penalties and OCR audits.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or approve illicit cross-border financial transactions, or who manifest criminal negligence in failing to maintain a compliance programme, risk personal criminal liability, asset seizures, travel bans, and disqualification from directorship under the Canada Business Corporations Act.
