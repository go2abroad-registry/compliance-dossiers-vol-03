Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC) — GST/HST and corporate income tax registration; portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html

E-government filing portal

For a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (BC ULC) with non-resident ownership, the statutory TIN is the federal Business Number (BN) plus a Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) program account. The foreign entity and its BC branch must submit Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) or register online via CRA Business Registration Online (BRO); article of incorporation, registered office address, and director/resident officer information must be provided. For non-resident corporations, Form RC452 (non-resident account application) or an equivalent may be required. The BN is issued immediately upon validation and program accounts (RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll) are activated. Provincial registration with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST and Alberta/BC corporate registry synchronization may be required if the ULC has BC taxable activities. A BC ULC without Canadian residents may need to appoint an agent for service and file non-resident income tax returns on a Canadian-resident basis for deemed residency under the Canada–U.S. treaty if applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a federal check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election equivalent to U.S. Reg. §301.7701-3. A BC ULC remains a taxable Canadian corporation for Canadian income tax purposes unless it is deemed a trust/partnership under a specific treaty article; however, it is eligible for U.S. tax check-the-box elections via IRS Form 8832 to be treated as a disregarded entity (DRE) for U.S. federal tax purposes. Domestically, elections under Income Tax Act (Canada) s.216 (net-basis taxation of non-resident rental/timber income) and s.219 (additional tax on Branches) may apply. Treaty-based hybrid entity classifications under the Canada–U.S. Tax Treaty are available to prevent double taxation and must be documented with the CRA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) triggers mandatory GST/HST registration for non-residents under ETA s.240. For BC PST, registration is generally required when a person makes taxable sales in British Columbia exceeding CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter; both thresholds are evaluated on historical and prospective revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A remote BPO call-centre service (inbound helpdesk and outbound tele-sales) is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 of the Excise Tax Act if the recipient is a non-resident person (not a consumer), the service is supplied to the non-resident for use wholly outside Canada, and the service does not relate to real property in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada, or a service rendered to a resident at the time of supply. Where the call-centre agent physically performs the service in Canada but delivers via telecommunications to an offshore recipient, the place-of-supply rules may deem the supply to be made where the recipient is located for B2B supplies, preserving zero-rating for exported services. The supplier must obtain and maintain contemporaneous documentary evidence of the non-resident status and offshore use, including the recipient's tax registration, service agreement, and call-delivery infrastructure wherever possible.

  • CBSA export declaration (Form B13A / CAED 150) only when the services are bundled with or include physical deliverables (e.g., hardware, documentation) crossing the Canadian border
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the movement of any physical media accompanying the BPO service
  • Inward remittance certificate, foreign-exchange purchase contract, or SWIFT/receivable ledger confirming receipt of export proceeds from the offshore recipient within 180 days
  • Signed B2B service contract and purchase order from the non-resident counter-party
  • Call detail records, time-stamped outbound customer invoices, and service delivery logs demonstrating the offshore destination and use
  • CRA Form GST66 (or equivalent GST/HST rebate claim for exported services) and the corresponding general ledger tax-code reconciliation

No federal real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory clearance API is currently implemented in Canada. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns for registrants with revenue above CAD 1,500,000 and supports API-based XML submission through GST/HST NETFILE and EDI protocols. Peppol-based B2B e-invoicing is voluntary in Canada; BC PST does not impose an independent e-invoicing clearance mandate.

CRA may reassess zero-rated supplies and clawback any refund claimed if documentary evidence is missing or inconsistent. Fraudulent zero-rating, including knowingly false evidence of offshore supply, invokes civil penalties under ETA s.280 (false statements or omissions) of up to 25% of the tax that would have been payable, and under s.285 for wilful evasion fines up to CAD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Where the taxpayer has claimed a GST/HST net refund on zero-rated exports and the classification is reversed, CRA applies 5% statutory interest and may impose an additional 25% penalty on the unpaid tax. BC PST misclassification of exported services likewise attracts reassessment and a 25% civil penalty. Criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code or the Excise Tax Act can result in fines of up to CAD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are free of quantitative restrictions; no prior Bank of Canada permission, no balance-of-payments registration, and no approval requirement applies to service receipts, capital injections, or profit repatriation. The only frictions are tax-based: Part XIII withholding tax at a statutory 25% on dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees paid to non-resident shareholders, reduced by treaty, and for a BC ULC that files the Income Tax Act section 150.2 election, the entity is taxed as a corporation for Canadian tax purposes while remaining eligible for 'check-the-box' pass-through treatment in the United States. Dividend repatriation to a US ultimate shareholder holding at least 10% of the ULC is commonly eligible for the 5% treaty rate, and interest to an arm's-length non-resident lender may be exempt from Canadian withholding if the treaty exception applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada does not impose any export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. The BC ULC may retain invoiced BPO service receipts indefinitely in CAD, USD, or other foreign currencies. Nevertheless, every incoming or outgoing electronic funds transfer (SWIFT, wire, or fintech rail) of CAD 10,000 or more requires a FINTRAC Electronic Funds Transfer Report, filed by the financial institution within 24 hours of the transaction. Currency conversion itself is entirely unrestricted and available at market rates from Schedule I banks and MSBs.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) - the sole monetary authority and issuer of the Canadian dollar; there is no dedicated foreign-exchange administration and no capital-control approval bureau. Financial institutions are chartered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while AML/CFT supervision is conducted by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a complementary settlement rail but not as a primary treasury account. Canada does not have an 'electronic money institution' licence category; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) must either rely on a Canadian MSB registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) or partner with a Schedule I chartered bank for local CAD clearing. Wise operates as a FINTRAC-registered MSB with dedicated USD ABA routing and mid-market FX conversion; it is fully viable for low-value recurring vendor payouts under the Wise B2B rail, but it is not a substitute for a Tier-1 depository account because it does not carry CDIC deposit insurance, does not extend credit, and exposes the ULC to payment-pool commingling risk. The prudent structure is a primary operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank paired with Wise B2B for small-ticket disbursements in PHP, INR, COP, and other local currencies, with every Wise top-up traceable via SWIFT MT103 references.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a BC ULC receiving non-resident shareholder capital, the compliant routing is an incoming SWIFT MT103 from the UBO's bank with field 70 reading 'S/I SHARE CAPITAL' or 'S/I BRIDGE LOAN'; the remitting bank must provide the beneficial owner's KYC attestation, and the receiving Canadian bank will classify the transaction under FINTRAC EFTR rules if the amount is CAD 10,000 or more. For offshore BPO service fees, the client remits via SWIFT MT103 with field 70 containing the invoice number and the notation 'BPO SERVICES / INVC'; Canada imposes no mandatory balance-of-payments purpose code, but the bank will solicit a declared business purpose, so the ULC must retain the invoiced contract, a service-delivery log, and any correspondence evidencing the underlying arrangement. Inbound USD wires may be converted to CAD at the receiving bank's market rate or, for larger recurring receipts, netted through a Tier-1 FX forward contract. Where the client pays through Wise B2B, the funds arrive as a domestic CAD or USD transfer from Wise's Canadian settlement account, and the ULC should obtain, for audit and AML records, Wise's statement plus the originating client invoice.Primary clearing rails for the BC ULC's outbound flows are: (1) domestic CAD disbursements for payroll, CRA tax remittances, rent, and vendor payments through the Lynx high-value payment system or the ACSS automated clearing settlement system; (2) cross-border CAD/USD and foreign-currency disbursements via SWIFT MT103 through the Tier-1 bank's correspondent network, using field 70 for invoice and FX reference; (3) Wise B2B for low-value subcontractor settlements in local currencies, executed through local instant rails (for example, InstaPay in the Philippines, PIX in Brazil, IMPS in India) with a SWIFT-equivalent transaction reference; (4) standby letters of credit, which are the only commercial LC variant relevant to a services BPO; a Tier-1 bank may issue an SBLC to guarantee client-default escrow obligations where the BPO master agreement requires it. For every outbound transfer of CAD 10,000 or more, the sending bank must file a FINTRAC EFTR; the ULC must retain invoicing, sub-vendor agreements, and time-log evidence to satisfy audit and suspicious-transaction-review requirements.Deliverable CAD/USD forward contract with a Tier-1 Schedule I bank under an ISDA 2002 and CSA master agreement, hedging the ULC's recurring USD-denominated BPO fee stream.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) for non-deliverable currency pairs (for example, USD/PHP, USD/INR, USD/IDR), permissible where the underlying exposure is a genuine BPO invoicing stream in a restricted offshore currency.Cross-currency swap (CCS) for intercompany capital and interest remittances between the BC ULC and a US/UK parent, requiring ISDA/CSA documentation and OSFI-compliant derivative risk governance.Vanilla FX options (both calls and puts) for hedging multi-year client pricing exposure; retail binary or exotic OTC options remain prohibited under Canadian securities law for this corporate user.Zero-cost FX collar (dual-currency sweep facility) combining a purchased cap and a sold floor to minimize conversion volatility on large client receipts without upfront premium.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a complementary settlement rail but not as a primary treasury account. Canada does not have an 'electronic money institution' licence category; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) must either rely on a Canadian MSB registration under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) or partner with a Schedule I chartered bank for local CAD clearing. Wise operates as a FINTRAC-registered MSB with dedicated USD ABA routing and mid-market FX conversion; it is fully viable for low-value recurring vendor payouts under the Wise B2B rail, but it is not a substitute for a Tier-1 depository account because it does not carry CDIC deposit insurance, does not extend credit, and exposes the ULC to payment-pool commingling risk. The prudent structure is a primary operating account at a Canadian Schedule I bank paired with Wise B2B for small-ticket disbursements in PHP, INR, COP, and other local currencies, with every Wise top-up traceable via SWIFT MT103 references.
