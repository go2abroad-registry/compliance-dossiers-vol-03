Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for a BC unlimited liability company (ULC). Shares may be issued without par value; no escrow account, capital deposit, or paid-up capital verification is mandated at formation. The ULC is an unlimited liability entity in respect of which the issued share capital provisions are contractually defined through the Articles.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Federal tax administration and principal GST/HST authority; CRA My Business Account portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html. British Columbia Ministry of Finance – Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and Employer Health Tax administration for provincial tax accounts.

E-government filing portal

A British Columbia unlimited liability corporation (ULC) is created under the Business Corporations Act (BC) through the BC Corporate Online registry. The federal Business Number (BN) and associated income tax account are generally established when CRA records are created after incorporation. If not auto-assigned, the corporation must apply by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' with CRA. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must similarly obtain a BN. For GST/HST, the ULC registers for a GST/HST program account through CRA Business Registration Online, Form RC1, or My Business Account. A valid Business Number plus GST/HST account number constitutes the statutory TIN for federal indirect tax purposes. BC PST account registration is made separately through the BC Tax Registration system (eTaxBC).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian tax resident corporation under Income Tax Act; no Canadian check-the-box. For U.S. federal tax purposes, a wholly-owned ULC may be eligible for check-the-box election as a disregarded entity / pass-through, but this does not alter Canadian entity-level taxation.)

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Canadian domestic tax law. Available statutory elections relevant to a ULC include: voluntary GST/HST registration under the Excise Tax Act for markets below the mandatory threshold; section 216 Income Tax Act election for non-resident rental income; joint elections for closely-related corporations under GST/HST rules; and in the cross-border context, U.S. Form 8832 Entity Classification Election for U.S. check-the-box treatment (affecting only U.S. tax characterization). Hybrid mismatch rules under CFC and anti-deferral provisions may apply to the ULC's income in the owner's foreign jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies made in Canada in any single calendar quarter or in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; non-resident persons carrying on commercial activity in Canada exceeding this threshold must register for GST/HST. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A ULC may claim zero-rated GST/HST treatment on exports of goods and services. Tangible personal property is zero-rated if the supplier exports it as part of a supply of goods to a recipient outside Canada, with proof of export in the required form and timeframe. Services, including digital advertising and programmatic ad services, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and the service is for use wholly outside Canada; advertising services supplied to a non-resident for use in Canada are taxable. The supplier must retain documentary evidence demonstrating exportation and the non-resident destination/use. Input tax credits associated with zero-rated exports remain recoverable.

  • Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) Export Declaration (B13A form or Electronic Export Reporting via CERS)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire transfer record proving payment in foreign currency from outside Canada
  • Copy of sales contract or service agreement identifying non-resident recipient and place of use
  • Proof of delivery outside Canada (including carrier tracking, terminal receipts, and customs destination stamps)

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping regime. CRA permits electronic invoicing and record-keeping, but does not require a government pre-clearance or digital tax stamp. GST/HST registrants with monthly or quarterly reporting periods must file returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE, CRA's XML/API filing interface, or through a tax préparer. Records and third-party data supporting intra-community or export type supplies must be retained and made available on CRA audit request.

Under the Excise Tax Act, CRA may reassess and claw back zero-rated treatment and input tax credits if export substantiation is deficient. False or dishonest statements in a GST/HST return attract a civil penalty equal to the greater of CAD $100 and 50% of the net tax or input tax credit claimed, plus arrears interest and compliance penalties. Knowingly evading payment or claiming fraudulent zero-rating may result in penalties under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act, including fines of up to CAD $25,000 or up to 200% of the amount sought to be evaded and imprisonment. CRA also applies a normal reassessment period of four years (longer in cases of misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud) and may assert the general anti-avoidance rule in respect of artificial export arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible under both the current and capital accounts. Canada maintains a liberalized cross-border capital movement regime under the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and the IMF Articles of Agreement Article VIII. There are no exchange controls, no approval thresholds, and no repatriation licensing requirements for current account transactions. Capital account inflows and outflows are generally unrestricted, subject only to statutory reporting, sanctions screening, and FATCA/CRS information exchange protocols. Dividends, management fees, royalties, interest, and proceeds from the disposition of shares in a BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (BC ULC) may be repatriated freely to foreign parent shareholders; however, remittances are subject to Canadian corporate income tax on the ULC's taxable Canadian income, Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (typically 25% absent treaty relief), and GST/HST obligations where applicable. A BC ULC that elects to be treated as a flow-through entity for Canadian tax purposes may nonetheless be subject to the branch tax under section 219 of the Income Tax Act upon remittances of branch profits to the offshore parent; advance rulings and professional tax advice are required to structure the optimum distribution and withholding framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Canada. Exporters are not required to surrender foreign currency earnings to the Bank of Canada or any central authority. All export proceeds may be retained in freely convertible foreign currency accounts, such as USD or EUR settlement accounts maintained with Canadian Schedule I banks, or may be converted to Canadian dollars at any time through a licensed authorized foreign exchange dealer or bank. Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), FINTRAC reporting obligations apply to cross-border electronic funds transfers in excess of C$10,000, and to currency transaction reports of C$10,000 or more, irrespective of whether the proceeds are retained in foreign currency for service remittance through a programmatic advertising platform.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) with prudential regulation and anti-money laundering oversight concurrently exercised by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), and provincial securities/consumer protection authorities in British Columbia (BC Financial Services Authority) for non-prudential conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. The concept of an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) does not exist as a standalone regulated entity within the Canadian federal or British Columbia provincial banking framework. Digital banks, payment service providers, and fintech money-service businesses (MSBs) are subject to registration with FINTRAC and, if engaging in payment services, may need to be incorporated as bank or credit union subsidiaries under OSFI regulation. Offshore EMIs, such as UK FCA authorized EMIs or European e-money institutions, cannot hold Canadian dollar client funds nor provide local clearing account facilities in Canada without becoming a regulated Canadian MSB or obtaining a foreign bank branch licensing order from the Minister of Finance under the Bank Act. For a BC ULC programmatic AdTech entity, reliance on an offshore EMI is feasible only for non-Canadian denominated settlement flows where the offshore EMI has adequate Sanctions and FATCA/CRS compliance, but local CAD payroll, GST/HST remittances, and corporate payments should remain with a Schedule I Canadian bank. The ULC should therefore maintain a primary operational account with a tier-1 Canadian bank and use a regulated global FX/fintech platform strictly for optimized cross-currency conversions or as a secondary non-reserve settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and working capital injections from a foreign parent into the BC ULC should be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 through the parent bank's correspondent relationship with a Canadian Schedule I bank. The Beneficiary Account should be the ULC's operational account at a tier-1 Canadian bank, with the Beneficiary line should clearly state the exact legal name and account number of the ULC. The purpose code should be populated with a cross-border payment narrative explicitly referring to 'Share Capital Contribution / Subscription for Common Shares of BC Unlimited Liability Company' in the remittance information field, and if the Canadian bank asks for FINTRAC EFT reporting information, thepayer and beneficiary must be identified by legal entity identifier or full physical address. For foreign direct investment (FDI) statistical reporting under the Balance of Payments framework, Statistics Canada relies on enterprise surveys rather than transactional exchange controls; the ULC should notify the CRA of its foreign ownership status through the-designated Canada Revenue Agency forms and provide supporting board resolutions, share subscription agreements, and proof of landed funds. The Canadian bank will also screen the remittance against Canadian sanctions lists and may require a completed source-of-funds questionnaire for inbound amounts exceeding C$250,000; the defence package should include the parent company's audited financials, the ULC's capital contribution minutes, and a written declaration that the funds are free of proceeds of crime. A separate legal entity escrow account may be opened concurrently for the exact deposit of subscription funds pending issuance of shares; the account should be styled as 'Capital Escrow – BC ULC – Share Subscription', with disbursement only against a share issuance resolution and statutory records update under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia).Outbound payments for trade obligations should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for high-value, one-time supplier payments, and through Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (L/C) for contracted media inventory or technology-hosted services when supplier credit terms and documentary conformity are required. For programmatic advertising inventory settled through DSP/SSP partners, the primary commercial clearing rail will be an automated ACH/Direct (ACH Business-to-Business) for low-value/polling-frequency CAD and USD disbursements, supplemented by SWIFT MT103 for cross-border partner payouts where the payee is not within the ACH network. All outbound wire instructions should attach the relevant CIS (Corporate Identification Structure) and include a purpose description such as 'Payments for Digital Advertising Media Brokerage Services', 'Infrastructure Software Subscription', 'Royalties for DSP SSP Technology Licensing', or 'Intercompany Management Fee' as supported by contracts. The bank should be supplied with a standing disbursement mandate authorizing same-day approvals up to an aggregate limit, with any disbursement above the limit requiring a second bank officer approval and the ULC's telex/SWIFT authentication by a two-person rule. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium. The concept of an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) does not exist as a standalone regulated entity within the Canadian federal or British Columbia provincial banking framework. Digital banks, payment service providers, and fintech money-service businesses (MSBs) are subject to registration with FINTRAC and, if engaging in payment services, may need to be incorporated as bank or credit union subsidiaries under OSFI regulation. Offshore EMIs, such as UK FCA authorized EMIs or European e-money institutions, cannot hold Canadian dollar client funds nor provide local clearing account facilities in Canada without becoming a regulated Canadian MSB or obtaining a foreign bank branch licensing order from the Minister of Finance under the Bank Act. For a BC ULC programmatic AdTech entity, reliance on an offshore EMI is feasible only for non-Canadian denominated settlement flows where the offshore EMI has adequate Sanctions and FATCA/CRS compliance, but local CAD payroll, GST/HST remittances, and corporate payments should remain with a Schedule I Canadian bank. The ULC should therefore maintain a primary operational account with a tier-1 Canadian bank and use a regulated global FX/fintech platform strictly for optimized cross-currency conversions or as a secondary non-reserve settlement rail.
