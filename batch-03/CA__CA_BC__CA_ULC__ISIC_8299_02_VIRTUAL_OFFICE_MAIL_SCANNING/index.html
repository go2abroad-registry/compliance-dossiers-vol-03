Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) - GST/HST Program via My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-individuals/account-register-cra.html); BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for PST registration, plus BC Registry for corporate licensing of Commercial Mail Receiving Agencies (CMRA) under the Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act.

E-government filing portal

A BC-registered ULC must apply to the CRA for a 9-digit Business Number (BN) with an RT (GST/HST) program account using Form RC1A or RC1, either online through My Business Account, by mail, or by telephone; the BN is linked to the BC corporate registry number. GST/HST registration is mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters; non-resident persons carrying on business in Canada must register regardless of threshold. The ULC must separately apply for BC PST registration via Form FIN 418 once annual taxable PST sales exceed C$10,000, and must hold the statutory CMRA licence before operating the mail-scanning agency. Foreign shareholders of the ULC must obtain a CRA non-resident account number and may be exposed to unlimited liability for unpaid creditor claims under BC law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: A BC ULC is a taxable Canadian corporation under the Income Tax Act (Canada) subject to federal net corporate income tax of 15% and BC provincial corporate income tax of 12% (combined approximately 27%); however, under Article IV(1) of the Canada-US Tax Convention the ULC meets the 'fiscally transparent' test, permitting a US member to elect check-the-box treatment (Form 8832) to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax purposes. Canada does not recognize the ULC as a partnership and it files T2 corporate returns in Canada.

Available election forms

Domestic: ULC may elect a non-calendar fiscal period under ITA s.142.2(3); elect simplified GST/HST filing using Form GST114; or waive small supplier status voluntarily via Form GST25. Cross-border: US shareholders may file Form 8832 check-the-box election converting the ULC into a disregarded entity; the hybrid mismatch rules introduced in Canada (ITA s.12.6 and s.18.3, effective for payments after 30 June 2022) restrict deduction-without-inclusion outcomes for such hybrid entities; no domestic check-the-box election exists under Canadian corporate tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

C$30,000 worldwide gross taxable supplies in a single quarter or over four consecutive quarters for GST/HST registration; C$10,000 annual taxable PST sales threshold for BC PST registration; non-resident digital service providers supplying Canadian recipients may be subject to GST/HST under the simplified 'specified electronic services' regime without any monetary threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital administrative, virtual-office, and mail-image scanning services qualify as zero-rated exports under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 where the recipient is a non-resident (not registered for GST/HST), the service is not rendered to an individual present in Canada, the service is not in respect of real property situated in Canada, and the service is not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada (physical mail handling in BC can breach this condition; the service contract must emphasize remote digital delivery of scanned images, not custody of mail). If the recipient is a non-resident GST/HST registrant, CRA Form GST494 with the recipient's BN must be obtained. BC PST does not apply to services exported to non-residents.

  • Executed service agreement declaring the non-resident recipient, place of supply, and absence of in-Canada service delivery
  • CRA zero-rating documentary evidence: Form GST494 non-resident declaration or foreign supplier registration certificate
  • Commercial invoice endorsed 'ZERO-RATED EXPORT - Schedule VI, Part V, s.7 Excise Tax Act' showing the ULC's GST/HST registration number
  • SWIFT MT103 or inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration within 180 days
  • Digital delivery evidence: server access logs or cloud delivery receipts showing downloading outside Canada
  • Where physical scanned originals are exported: Canada Border Services Agency export declaration (Form B13A) with bill of lading or international waybill

Canada does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns (GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or certified software API) for registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding C$1.5 million, with a C$100 penalty per un-filed e-return; registrants must retain original invoices, transaction logs, and machine-readable audit trails for six years under ETA s.286. The CRA B2B clearance-of-e-invoices pilot is voluntary; Stripe and credit-card settlement records must reconcile with declared net GST/HST.

CRA may retroactively deny zero-rating treatment where documentary requirements are not met, reassessing 5% GST on all consideration plus interest and penalties; ETA s.280(1) imposes penalties for false statements or omissions equal to the greater of C$100 and 50% of the tax evaded, with s.281 gross negligence penalties not exceeding the tax sought to be evaded; BC PST non-collection penalties run at 10% of the uncollected PST plus daily interest (currently 9% per annum); virtual-office and mail-scanning entities attract heightened CRA audit scrutiny as perceived invoice-mill or tax-shelter arrangements, and the four-year general reassessment period is extended indefinitely where fraudulent or negligent misrepresentation is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: Canada is an IMF Article VIII member; both current-account and capital-account payments are free of exchange-control approval. A BC ULC may receive USD/CAD from non-resident clients, convert CAD/USD at any Schedule I bank or FINTRAC-registered MSB, and remit dividends, interest, royalties, intercompany loans, and repatriated capital without central-bank authorization. The only substantive profit-remittance hurdle is Canadian Part XIII non-resident withholding tax: statutory 25% on dividends/interest/royalties, reduced under the Canada-US Treaty for a US parent company holding 10%+ of the ULC's voting equity (dividends 5%), arm's-length non-participating interest (0%), and royalties (10%). Treaty benefits may be denied where the ULC is fiscally transparent under US check-the-box treatment unless ultimate US members satisfy Article XXIX A limitation-on-benefits and derivative-benefits tests; therefore a treaty-position memorandum and complete W-8BEN-E/NR-301 documentation chain must be maintained before any outbound distribution. No CFIUS or other FDI consent applies to the virtual-office/CMRA business scope.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil — Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation window, and no forced conversion ratio for export or cross-border service receipts. A BC ULC may invoice clients in USD, retain export/service proceeds in USD accounts at a Schedule I bank or licensed MSB wallet, and convert to CAD at commercial rates at its discretion. The only compliance layer is FINTRAC: every incoming or outgoing international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more (or foreign equivalent) must be reported by the financial institution as an Electronic Funds Transfer Report (EFTR); the ULC must preserve underlying commercial contracts, invoices, and source-of-funds evidence to demonstrate legitimate origin. Canadian income tax rules may impute unrealized foreign-exchange gains/losses annually on the ULC's monetary items, but this does not create any surrender or conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central bank. Foreign-exchange regulatory authority formally vested in the Minister of Finance under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act; no operative exchange-control restrictions are administered. Prudential regulator of deposit-taking institutions: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Anti-money-laundering and cross-border transaction-reporting supervisor: Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Viability rating: Medium for operational/payout activity (Stripe Recurring, Wise/Payoneer wallets, card settlement sweeps); Low for treasury-grade escrow, intercompany loans, trade finance, and FX hedging. Legal constraints: (1) offshore/foreign EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise EU/UK, Payoneer) are not deposit-taking institutions chartered in Canada, are not CDIC-insured, and settle CAD through pooled trust accounts at Schedule I banks, exposing the ULC to counterparty and commingling risk; (2) the EMI must be a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business (MSB) and cannot access Payments Canada Lynx or ACSS settlement directly; (3) the ULC itself must not inadvertently become an unregistered MSB under the PCMLTFA — if Stripe is the regulated payment-service provider holding merchant funds, the ULC may rely on an MSB-registration-exemption analysis, but if the ULC collects, holds, or disburses third-party client money, it must register with FINTRAC and adopt a full AML/ATF program; (4) no EMI can provide capital escrow accounts, documentary letters of credit, or deliverable FX forwards. Recommended architecture: maintain the primary treasury relationship (escrow, operating account, wires, FX) at a Schedule I bank; use Stripe solely as the front-end card/recurring-billing rail with daily sweeps to the operating account; use an EMI only for small cross-border mass payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: Medium for operational/payout activity (Stripe Recurring, Wise/Payoneer wallets, card settlement sweeps); Low for treasury-grade escrow, intercompany loans, trade finance, and FX hedging. Legal constraints: (1) offshore/foreign EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise EU/UK, Payoneer) are not deposit-taking institutions chartered in Canada, are not CDIC-insured, and settle CAD through pooled trust accounts at Schedule I banks, exposing the ULC to counterparty and commingling risk; (2) the EMI must be a FINTRAC-registered Money Services Business (MSB) and cannot access Payments Canada Lynx or ACSS settlement directly; (3) the ULC itself must not inadvertently become an unregistered MSB under the PCMLTFA — if Stripe is the regulated payment-service provider holding merchant funds, the ULC may rely on an MSB-registration-exemption analysis, but if the ULC collects, holds, or disburses third-party client money, it must register with FINTRAC and adopt a full AML/ATF program; (4) no EMI can provide capital escrow accounts, documentary letters of credit, or deliverable FX forwards. Recommended architecture: maintain the primary treasury relationship (escrow, operating account, wires, FX) at a Schedule I bank; use Stripe solely as the front-end card/recurring-billing rail with daily sweeps to the operating account; use an EMI only for small cross-border mass payouts.

Turnover: No compulsory audit is triggered by revenue, asset, or shareholder-count thresholds for a private BC ULC.
