Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the CBCA for non-reporting trading corporations. Incorporation may proceed with nil authorized capital and one or more shares. No government-mandated escrow account is required; however, if share consideration is subscribed, it must be paid to the corporation before share certificates are issued, and directors must ensure consideration is at least equal to the fair value of the shares.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a trading-only office. If a marine terminal or storage facility is operated, compliance with local fire code, building structural load permits, and provincial occupational health and safety clearance is mandatory before physical handling commences.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST Registry and Business Number (BN) administration; online portal: Canada.ca/revenue-agency / My Business Account (mba.cra-arc.gc.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian business corporation obtains a Business Number (BN) by incorporating under the Canada Business Corporations Act or an applicable provincial Act and applying for a BN/GST/HST account via Form RC1 or authorized electronic filing. CRA verifies corporate records, directors, and the residence/representative for service; the GST/HST program account is then issued as a nine-digit BN followed by the ‘RT’ account identifier. Non-resident corporations may designate a tax representative or agent in Canada and must provide certified documentary evidence of corporate existence and board authorization.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident business corporation; no federal pass-through or S-corporation equivalent; non-resident shareholders are taxed at the corporate level in Canada and on dividends/interest by withholding provisions in the Income Tax Act). — A foreign-owned Canadian business corporation obtains a Business Number (BN) by incorporating under the Canada Business Corporations Act or an applicable provincial Act and applying for a BN/GST/HST account via Form RC1 or authorized electronic filing. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian-resident business corporation; no federal pass-through or S-corporation equivalent; non-resident shareholders are taxed at the corporate level in Canada and on dividends/interest by withholding provisions in the Income Tax Act).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada. The Income Tax Act does not permit a corporation to elect flow-through status. Available statutory elections relevant to GST/HST include Excise Tax Act (ETA) s.156 joint election for supplies between closely related corporations and the normal GST/HST registration election for closely related entities. Hybrid mismatch elections are not generally available; Canada applies entity-level matching and deduction rules by statute, not by taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000: a person whose total taxable supplies (other than zero-rated? No — including zero-rated supplies) exceed CAD 30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive quarters must register for GST/HST; non-residents with no Canadian taxable supplies may nevertheless register voluntarily to claim input tax credits and support export transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, to a supply of goods made by the supplier when the goods are exported from Canada by the supplier or delivered to the recipient for immediate export, including bulk dry-marine export of granular elemental sulfur. Goods must be actually removed from Canada and must not be acquired for the supplier’s, recipient’s, or related party’s Canadian use; the vendor must retain contemporaneous export evidence. Freight, carriage, and brokerage services supplied to a non-resident in connection with the export of such goods from Canada to a foreign port may also be zero-rated under the ETA where the services are performed in relation to export-bound tangible personal property and are supplied to a person that is not ordinarily resident in Canada, provided the property is exported as part of the same transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / CERS electronic export declaration G29 or CERS message set, including HS tariff classification 2503 for sulfur)
  • Bill of Lading (marine to a destination outside Canada, identifying exporter, consignee, cargo description, and foreign port discharge)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or corresponding bank instrument (e.g., SWIFT MT103) showing settlement of export proceeds from the foreign purchaser
  • Commercial invoice, purchase/sales contract, packing list, and, where applicable, marine insurance certificate and customs cargo control verification

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API mandate for GST/HST invoices. Electronic invoices are accepted if they preserve full audit trail integrity and display the supplier’s Business Number and GST/HST registration status. GST/HST returns are filed through CRA’s GST/HST NETFILE, electronic mandate, or EFILE; no national PEPPOL or continuous transaction control system is mandatory at the federal level, though commercial e-invoicing may be required in large corporate supply chains.

CRA scrutiny focuses on whether the goods were actually exported and whether input tax credits claimed on the export supply are substantiated. If supporting documentation is incomplete, unauditable, or inconsistent with the customs export declaration and bank settlement records, CRA will deny the zero-rating, reassess GST/HST at the applicable rate, disallow related input tax credits, and charge interest from the due date. Where a participant makes, participates in, assents to, or acquiesces in a false statement or misrepresentation relating to export zero-rating, the ETA imposes civil penalties for gross negligence, and willful evasion or fraudulent claims may lead to criminal prosecution. Normal reassessment periods are four years from the date of the return; where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established, CRA may reassess beyond that period and recover the resulting tax deficiency, penalties, and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) under a free-floating exchange-rate regime. Canada is an OECD and WTO member with no restrictions on current-account or capital-account convertibility, no exchange-control permits, no investment-licensing requirements for ordinary cross-border payments, and no repatriation licensing regime. The only statutory hurdles to profit remittance are: (i) Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act on dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees paid to non-residents (statutory 25%, reduced to 15% or lower under applicable tax treaties); (ii) the Investment Canada Act notification requirement for a non-Canadian establishing a new Canadian business; and (iii) ordinary FINTRAC cross-border transaction reporting. No approval is required from the Bank of Canada or OSFI for capital injections, dividend remittances, or inland FX conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. Sulphur and mineral-ore exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts indefinitely in domestic or foreign accounts. The only compliance obligations are: (i) FINTRAC's cross-border reporting duty for imports or exports of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more; (ii) client-monitoring duties of the bank under the PCMLTFA; and (iii) Statistical Survey reporting to Statistics Canada (international transactions reporting, IMF BPM6 direct-investment classification) where thresholds are exceeded.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); prudential and banking-market conduct oversight is vested in the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); cross-border financial transaction enforcement is vested in the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). Canada has no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration equivalent to a reserve-currency authority; exchange-rate policy is a matter of the Minister of Finance and the Bank of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium, strictly as a supplementary settlement rail. Canada does not issue standalone e-money institution (EMI) licences; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, Airwallex) must register as Money Service Businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC and are not chartered banks. They are legally prohibited from deposit-taking, are ineligible for CDIC deposit insurance, cannot issue or confirm commercial documentary letters of credit, and are unsuitable as primary treasury depositories. Legal constraints include PCMLTFA registration, sanctions-screening obligations, and FINTRAC reporting duties. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal Part I general rate after 10% provincial abatement and 13% general rate reduction; basic statutory rate 38%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act on dividends paid by a Canadian corporation to non-residents, unless a tax treaty provides a reduced rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 aggregate transaction value with non-resident non-arm's-length parties triggers mandatory T106 filing and the CRA's expectation of contemporaneous Transfer Pricing documentation; Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under s.245 ITA, the sham doctrine, and the business purpose test empower CRA to deny tax benefits of an entity lacking real economic substance. A Canadian subsidiary with no office, no employees, no local decision-making, or no real trade execution will be treated as a conduit and its income may be reattributed to the foreign principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s.239(1) ITA: fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil gross negligence penalty under s.163(2) ITA of 10% of the understated tax; restitution and interest; publication of taxpayer name in CRA penalty lists.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable under s.227.1 ITA for unremitted employee source deductions and GST/HST. Where directors authorized or acquiesced in evasion, they can be criminally charged, ordered to pay restitution, prohibited from leaving Canada, and subject to director/executive penalties under federal tax law.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Application of GAAR under s.245 ITA to disregard the tax benefit; reassessment of income to the entity with real substance; third-party civil penalty under s.163.2 ITA; availability of sham doctrine and anti-avoidance rulings; potential disqualification of legal representative.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil when the entity is a mere agent or alter ego; directors can be personally assessed for the corporation's taxes if they deliberately used the corporate form to evade, and may face an injunction, repayment order, or criminal sanction for fraudulent conduct.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length consideration under s.247(2) ITA; penalty under s.247(3) ITA equal to 10% of the net adjustment (if documentation absent or unreasonable); T106 late-filing penalty equal to CAD 100 per day up to CAD 24,000 and interest; in aggravated cases criminal prosecution for false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or prepared false transfer pricing documents may be prosecuted under s.239(1) ITA; personal liability may arise under Canadian fraudulent conveyance legislation where assets are moved out of Canada to avoid tax; directors may also be assessed for unremitted amounts if they diverted corporate funds.
AML-CTF and Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties (AMP) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) up to CAD 1,000,000 per violation for corporations; criminal money laundering conviction under the Criminal Code: fine and imprisonment up to 10 years; asset freezing; federal commissioner enforcement order and suspension of business approvals.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable if they were knowingly involved in a money laundering arrangement or wilfully blind. Under PCMLTFA s.75, directors may be subject to AMPs and compliance orders; criminal assets can be seized from them personally if traceable to unlawful conduct.
