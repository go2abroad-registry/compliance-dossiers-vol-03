Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — federal GST/HST and income tax administration via My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html); Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for customs bonded warehouse licensing and export declaration processing via the CARM portal; British Columbia Ministry of Finance for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration via eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity procedure: (1) Register extra-provincially or incorporate in British Columbia through the BC Corporate Registry (BC Online); (2) obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online (BRO); (3) open a GST/HST account immediately when carrying on commercial activity in Canada or once taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148(1)); (4) register for BC PST via eTaxBC when gross annual taxable sales in BC exceed CAD 10,000 (PST Act s. 35); (5) apply to CBSA for a customs bonded warehouse licence under the Customs Bonded Warehouses Regulations, s. 24, if operating the bonded depot in a free trade zone.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A BC ULC is a resident corporation under ITA s. 250(1)(a) and is subject to federal Part I corporate tax (general rate 15%; small business rate 9%) plus BC provincial corporate income tax (general rate 12%; small business rate 2%); the Pass_Through_Eligible attribute under ELF_CUSTOM_ULC applies strictly to foreign check-the-box jurisdictions and produces no Canadian domestic pass-through effect. — Foreign entity procedure: (1) Register extra-provincially or incorporate in British Columbia through the BC Corporate Registry (BC Online); (2) obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) using Business Registration Online (BRO); (3) open a GST/HST account immediately when carrying on commercial activity in Canada or once taxable worldwide supplies exceed CAD 30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148(1)); (4) register for BC PST via eTaxBC when gross annual taxable sales in BC exceed CAD 10,000 (PST Act s. 35); (5) apply to CBSA for a customs bonded warehouse licence under the Customs Bonded Warehouses Regulations, s. 24, if operating the bonded depot in a free trade zone.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A BC ULC is a resident corporation under ITA s. 250(1)(a) and is subject to federal Part I corporate tax (general rate 15%; small business rate 9%) plus BC provincial corporate income tax (general rate 12%; small business rate 2%); the Pass_Through_Eligible attribute under ELF_CUSTOM_ULC applies strictly to foreign check-the-box jurisdictions and produces no Canadian domestic pass-through effect.

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box regime exists under Canadian law. Available elections: (i) US Treasury Reg. § 301.7701-3 check-the-box election for the ULC (eligible for disregarded entity/partnership treatment solely for US federal tax purposes); (ii) ITA s. 216 election for non-resident rental income; (iii) ITA s. 219 branch tax election by non-resident corporations; (iv) Excise Tax Act s. 156 GST/HST election for closely related corporations; (v) ETA s. 273 joint election for non-arm's length supplies; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 govern cross-border arrangements exploiting ULC pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total taxable worldwide supplies (including zero-rated supplies, excluding financial services and goodwill) measured over four consecutive calendar quarters, per ETA s. 148 small-supplier threshold; separate BC PST registration threshold is CAD 10,000 in gross annual revenue from taxable sales in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under ETA Schedule VI Part V: (i) supply of goods by way of sale shipped by the supplier to a non-resident person who is not registered for GST/HST, with the goods exported as part of the supply; (ii) goods exported in compliance with regulations where the supplier retains prescribed export evidence; (iii) goods placed into a CBSA customs bonded warehouse or free trade zone and subsequently removed for export; (iv) certain offshore services supplied to non-residents and services performed in respect of export goods (Schedule VI items 7 and 8); the supplier must hold a valid GST/HST registration and maintain the full CRA custody-to-export documentary chain.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A filed through CARM/Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill issued by the carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign exchange transaction record evidencing settlement of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • CBSA Customs Bonded Warehouse Withdrawal / Transfer Record
  • Non-resident customer identification records proving non-registration for GST/HST

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory e-invoicing, and no digital stamping for GST/HST; CRA accepts B2B electronic invoices in EDI/XML/PDF subject to ETA s. 286 record-keeping (six-year retention); customs export and bonded warehouse transactions are cleared electronically through the CBSA CARM system with API-based Integrated Import Declarations; BC PST filings are submitted through eTaxBC without invoice approval mandates.

CRA may reassess GST/HST beyond the normal four-year limitation period, and indefinitely in cases of misrepresentation, to claw back zero-rated claims lacking acceptable export evidence; negligence penalties under ITA s. 163(2) reach 50% of the understated tax; ETA s. 280 imposes a 25% penalty for false statements or omissions; ETA s. 285 evasion convictions carry fines up to 250% of the evaded amount plus imprisonment; Customs Act offences for false or misleading export declarations attract summary conviction fines up to CAD 25,000 as well as seizure and forfeiture; interest accrues retroactively on all reassessed shortfalls and penalties.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current account convertibility with unrestricted profit remittances for residents. Capital account transactions involving non-residents require prior Bank of Canada approval for certain investments and securities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be repatriated to Canada promptly. Mandatory surrender to authorized foreign exchange dealers at prevailing market rates with no fixed statutory timeline but prompt repatriation required under Bank of Canada foreign exchange regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited viability for offshore digital EMIs due to FINTRAC and Bank of Canada regulatory oversight. Cross-border services require licensed Canadian bank partnerships.
