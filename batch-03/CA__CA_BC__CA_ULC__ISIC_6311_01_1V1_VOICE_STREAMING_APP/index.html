Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST and corporate income tax; Business Registration Online (BRO) portal at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/business-registration-online.html. Provincial counterpart: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch for Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration; BC Registrar of Companies (Corporate Online) for the ULC incorporation certificate and company number.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a BC incorporation number by filing Articles of Incorporation/Continuance under the Business Corporations Act (BC) via Corporate Online. Next, obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) and the GST/HST program account (RT0001) by applying through BRO or Form RC1, indicating the entity is a non-resident ULC. Non-resident digital suppliers without a Canadian establishment may use the CRA Simplified GST/HST Registration procedure (Form RC4616). Required supporting documents include certified Articles of Incorporation, certificate of existence/good standing from the BC Registrar, director/officer information, jurisdiction of residence of beneficial owners, and expected activity schedules. The BN is normally issued within 1 to 5 business days; the GST/HST account is backdated to the date of first taxable Canadian supply. A separate BC PST account is required only if the ULC makes enumerated PST-taxable supplies (voice-streaming subscriptions are generally outside the PST tax base and are therefore not PST-taxable).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. The BC ULC is a distinct legal person with unlimited shareholder liability and is taxed at the entity level as a corporation for Canadian federal and BC provincial income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act (ITA). For US federal tax purposes, the ULC is an eligible entity under Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3 and may be treated as a disregarded entity (pass-through) by its sole member, thereby permitting single-level US taxation while remaining a corporation in Canada. The fiscally transparent entity rule in Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Tax Convention (1980), as amended by the Fifth Protocol, resolves dual-residence conflicts by attributing residency to the Contracting State that taxes the ULC's income in the hands of a resident. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The BC ULC is a distinct legal person with unlimited shareholder liability and is taxed at the entity level as a corporation for Canadian federal and BC provincial income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act (ITA). For US federal tax purposes, the ULC is an eligible entity under Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3 and may be treated as a disregarded entity (pass-through) by its sole member, thereby permitting single-level US taxation while remaining a corporation in Canada. The fiscally transparent entity rule in Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Tax Convention (1980), as amended by the Fifth Protocol, resolves dual-residence conflicts by attributing residency to the Contracting State that taxes the ULC's income in the hands of a resident. For GST/HST purposes, the ULC itself, not the US shareholder, is the person liable to register and collect tax.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (1) US check-the-box election on Form 8832 (Entity Classification Election) designating the ULC as a disregarded entity or as an association taxable as a corporation, effective from the selected date; (2) treaty-based residency characterization under Article IV(6) of the Canada-US Tax Convention, including applications for a competent authority ruling on the extent to which the ULC's income is subject to Canadian tax; (3) GST/HST elections: voluntary registration as a small supplier before the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded, election to simplify non-resident GST/HST reporting, and the affiliated-persons consolidated registration election under subsection 156(1) of the Excise Tax Act where a Canadian affiliate exists; (4) hybrid mismatch elections relevant to the ULC structure where the same payment is classified as equity in Canada and debt in the US, requiring careful application of the hybrid mismatch rules in the ITA and the Income Tax Conventions Interpretation Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual taxable supplies determined on a rolling four-consecutive-calendar-quarter test under subsection 148(1) of the Excise Tax Act. Once exceeded, registration becomes mandatory and the supplier must collect GST effective on the 16th day of the month following the quarter in which the threshold is breached. For platform-mediated Canadian sales through Apple/Google, the ULC's Canadian-source revenue attributable to those sales is excluded from the ULC's own threshold only because the platform operator is the deemed supplier; direct sales to Canadian customers count toward the threshold. A non-resident ULC may voluntarily register at any time.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of voice-streaming services and intangible personal property are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act (section 7 for services supplied to a non-resident person, section 10 for intangible personal property supplied to a non-resident person), provided the non-resident is outside Canada at the time of supply and the supply's performance or use does not relate to goods, real property, or taxable services situated in Canada. Place-of-supply rules in sections 142 and 143.1 apply. Alternatively, digital supplies to a non-resident individual who is outside Canada are considered made outside Canada and are therefore not subject to GST. In all cases the ULC must evidence the recipient's non-residence and the supply's territorial location. Because the ULC's zero-rated export supplies are greater than its taxable supplies, the ULC is entitled to claim full input tax credits on Canadian inputs (cloud infrastructure, legal, accounting, payment-processing hardware) and will normally file GST/HST returns reflecting a net refund position.

  • Customs Export Declaration / CBSA electronic export report - inapplicable to pure digital voice-streaming; substitute a documented zero-rating schedule reconciling GST/HST return line items to underlying platform export sales and direct export sales consistent with CRA export policy (Form RC4080 and GST/HST Memorandum 21.2)
  • Subscriber non-residence evidence: Apple App Store Connect jurisdiction-of-sale reports, Google Play Console Financial Reports, IP-address geolocation logs, user-profile attestations of habitual residence, and server session logs demonstrating that the chatroom service was delivered outside Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank FX advices (SWIFT MT103 / payment gateway settlement dockets) showing periodic settlement in CAD or USD from Apple Inc., Google LLC, or Google Ireland Ltd. representing sales proceeds for non-Canadian end users
  • Legal agreements with Apple and Google evidencing platform distribution, commission structure, territorial scope, and the parties' GST/HST collection obligations under the digital platform deemed-supplier rules

Canada does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, transactional API validation, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices for GST/HST purposes. Registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1M must file GST/HST returns electronically via NETFILE, EDI, or TELEFILE; all registrants must maintain complete electronic books and records in a CRA-accessible format. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing initiative (implementing the Peppol/CIUS-EN-16931 standard) is progressing voluntarily for procurement and inter-vendor transactions and is not a tax clearance mandate. For Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the ULC must retain App Store Connect and Google Play Console jurisdiction-of-sale reports, payout dockets, and invoice remittance records as supporting electronic documentation.

The CRA may reassess zero-rated export claims within the normal four-year reassessment period (section 298 of the Excise Tax Act); where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established, the limitation period is unlimited. Penalties include: denial of the zero-rating with reassessment of 5% GST on the full consideration; arrears interest under section 280; gross-negligence penalty of 25% of the tax amount under section 285; penalties for inadequate books and records under subsection 280.1(1); and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under section 327 and section 239 of the Income Tax Act, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment. For digital exporters, CRA auditors increasingly rely on app-store analytics, IP geolocation sampling, payment-processor data, and platform payout reports to verify the non-residence of Canadian consumers. With respect to the 180-day FX repatriation benchmark, no identical statutory deadline exists in Canada; however, deferred or unrepatriated proceeds may raise transfer-pricing adjustments under section 247, shareholder-benefit or appropriation rules under subsection 15(2) and section 246, and, if funds are retained in offshore affiliates, foreign accrual property income attribution under subsection 91(1), while contemporaneous 180-day repatriation practice provides a strong evidentiary basis of arm's-length conduct.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 (goods) / CAD 500,000 (services) for Local File; Master File required if gross revenue > CAD 50 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for transactions to be respected. A ULC must have real business operations and substance to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid corporate tax if they fail to withhold/remit; also for negligence or willful participation.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, penalties up to 50% of tax avoided

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participated in the scheme.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 10% of net adjustment, plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable if they knowingly approved or ignored transfer pricing violations.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: fines up to CAD 2 million for corporations; imprisonment for individuals

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for fines if they knowingly fail to comply with AML obligations.
