Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca and the CRA My Business Account (MyBA) portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN). Non-resident corporations apply to the CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by filing Form RC4601 'Application for Non-Resident Business Number and/or Program Accounts'. The application requires the entity's full legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered address, Canadian agent (if any), directors, estimated annual taxable supplies made in Canada, and anticipated GST/HST obligation. After the BN is issued, the corporation opens the GST/HST 'RT' program account, the corporate income tax 'RC' account (T2), the payroll 'RP' account (if it has Canadian employees), and the import/export 'RM' account for customs and CBSA reporting. The CRA assigns a 2-digit program account suffix (e.g., RT0001) which must appear on returns, remittance vouchers, and invoices. Electronic enrolment is the standard route; paper filing is available only to small suppliers or in exceptional circumstances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Canadian federal or provincial Business Corporation (Inc./Corp.) is a legally separate domestic taxpayer resident in Canada and computes federal Part I income tax on worldwide income at the entity level. The general federal corporate rate is 15%; a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) may claim the reduced 9% small-business rate on active business income up to the business limit. Canada has no pass-through regime; corporate income is taxed at the entity level, and dividends paid to shareholders are grossed-up and subject to the dividend tax credit.

Available election forms

Canada does not offer a U.S.-style check-the-box election; classification follows legal form, place of incorporation, and central management and control. Available statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act s. 85 rollover election to transfer property into the corporation on a tax-deferred basis; (ii) Excise Tax Act s. 150 election to treat taxable supplies between closely related corporations as made for nil consideration; (iii) GST/HST reporting-frequency elections under ETA ss. 156 and 157 (annual, quarterly, or monthly); (iv) capital cost allowance (CCA) and immediate-expensing elections for depreciable manufacturing and processing property under Income Tax Act s. 13 and Income Tax Regulations s. 1100; (v) the BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules in Income Tax Act ss. 18.1 and 18.2 (generally phased in from 1 July 2023 and 1 October 2023) which contain mechanical ordering and transparency rules rather than elective classification, but carry transitional conformity elections; and (vi) for non-resident corporations, a s. 216(1) election to pay net-basis tax on certain Canadian rental/resource income in lieu of Part XIII withholding. Foreign-owned Canadian corporations may also rely on income tax treaty residence and non-discrimination articles.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility for both capital and current accounts. Canadian corporations may freely convert CAD to foreign currencies for trade settlements, operational funding, and profit remittances without prior approval, surrender requirements, or quantitative restrictions. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted without regulatory hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender to the Bank of Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore accounts or repatriate freely to Canadian bank accounts. All material export transactions exceeding reporting thresholds must be reported to the Bank of Canada via the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS). No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada

Late filing penalty: 5% of the unpaid tax owing at the filing due date plus 1% per full month of default (maximum 12%); repeat offenders within any 3-year period: 10% plus 2% per full month (maximum 20%); daily compound interest at the CRA prescribed rate (approximately 8%-9% in 2024) on all unpaid balances and instalment shortfalls; a late-filed T2 may invalidate loss carry-backs, trigger loss of federal tax credits, and expose directors to assessment for unremitted amounts.
