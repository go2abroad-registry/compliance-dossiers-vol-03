Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a BC ULC; no escrow account or capital deposit is required. The Company may be incorporated with nil or nominal paid-up share capital, with paid-in amounts determined by the share subscription and accounts.

Funding & unlock path: No fire or structural clearance is required solely for incorporation. Before occupying an office, warehouse, or yard, the company must obtain a municipal fire and occupancy permit, a WorkSafeBC occupational health and safety clearance or registration, and, where relevant, structural load certification for crane pads, ramps, and heavy-haul staging surfaces. Over-dimensional route movements require BC Commercial Vehicle Safety and Enforcement approval and load security documentation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) - federal tax administration for GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll and excise taxes; registration portal is CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/business-registration. Import/export program accounts are administered jointly with the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal. Provincial complement: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, administering BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) registration through eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned BC ULC carrying on commercial activity in Canada must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) with program accounts relevant to its operations. SOP: (1) ensure valid incorporation under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) and obtain certified constitutional documents; (2) register through CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) or by submitting Form RC1, using the corporation legal name, registered office address, and a Canadian or authorized foreign service address; (3) select the RT (GST/HST) account, RC (corporate income tax) account if the ULC has a permanent establishment or derives Canadian-source income, RP (payroll) account if it employs individuals in Canada, and RM (CBSA import/export) account for customs transactions; (4) register for BC PST with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch through eTaxBC using a BCeID before making taxable sales in British Columbia; (5) retain the CRA confirmation letter and all program account numbers. A non-resident ULC making only zero-rated export supplies is not required to register for GST/HST but may register voluntarily to claim input tax credits; a non-resident with Canadian-source business income may have a T2 corporate filing obligation even absent a permanent establishment, and is subject to Part XIII withholding on dividends, interest, rents, and management fees paid to non-resident shareholders.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

For Canadian federal income tax purposes, a BC ULC is an opaque resident corporation, but under US check-the-box principles it is generally treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax purposes, creating hybrid character. The Fifth Protocol to the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (Article IV(7)) permits a ULC to be elected, in writing to the CRA and the IRS competent authorities, to be treated as a corporation for treaty purposes, thereby neutralizing hybrid outcomes on dividends, interest, and royalties flowing to US shareholders. Domestically, the hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 12.5 and 18.4 may deny a deduction or include an amount in income where the ULC is fiscally transparent in another jurisdiction, and structured arrangements attract a 25% penalty under ITA s. 18.4(4). For indirect tax purposes there is no check-the-box regime: a ULC may elect under ETA s. 211 to have all or substantially all of its taxable supplies deemed made in Canada, and closely related corporations may elect under ETA s. 156 to form a GST/HST registration group so that intercompany supplies are disregarded. Income tax elections include optional returns under ITA s. 216 for Canadian rental income (not applicable to active logistics income) and treaty-based residency tie-breaker assertions where central management and control is outside Canada.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital accounts: Canadian dollar (CAD) is a free-floating currency; profit remittance, dividend declarations, and capital repatriation require no exchange-control approvals; statutory gateways are limited to the Investment Canada Act net-benefit review for large inbound equity injections and to FINTRAC/OSFI record-keeping for transactions exceeding CAD 10,000; USD/CAD settlement is freely available through CLS Bank and correspondent networks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None — Canada abolished exchange controls in 1951; there is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; OOG heavy-haul receivables in foreign currency may be retained offshore, partially hedged, or held in USD treasury accounts; the only obligations are functional-currency accounting under IAS 21 as adopted by IFRS and tax translation under the Income Tax Act, plus standard FATCA/CRS disclosure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC) — central monetary authority; prudential regulation by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT oversight by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); exchange and currency administration pursuant to the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the Bank of Canada Act; no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration bureau exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — workable as an auxiliary settlement corridor, not as a primary treasury bank. Canadian EMIs (e.g., Koho, Wealthsimple) and foreign EMIs licensed in Canada as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the PCMLTFA (e.g., Wise Canada, Payoneer, Airwallex) are restricted: they cannot issue letters of credit, cannot provide credit or escrow, and must hold pooled client funds at a Schedule I bank trust account. Suitable for low-value vendor payments, small FX conversion layers, and emergency payouts; legal constraint: MSBs must be registered with FINTRAC and, if applicable, the BC Financial Services Authority; banks do not recognize EMI statements as acceptable treasury evidence for LC-backed milestones.
