Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration via My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE (cra-arc.gc.ca); British Columbia Ministry of Finance – Provincial Sales Tax (PST) administration via eTaxBC (etaxbc.gov.bc.ca)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC): (1) incorporate the ULC through the BC Corporate Registry (Corporate Online); (2) obtain a nine-digit Canada Revenue Agency Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 or applying online through the CRA BizPortal; (3) register within the BN for a federal GST/HST account (required if taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold or if zero-rating refunds are sought); (4) register for BC PST through the Ministry of Finance eTaxBC portal using the same BN, where the taxable supplies comprise PST-taxable goods or enumerated services in British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No Canadian domestic check-the-box election exists; a BC ULC is a corporation per se under the Income Tax Act (Canada) and is subject to Canadian federal and provincial entity-level income taxation. For US federal tax purposes only, eligible US shareholders may file IRS Form 8832 to classify the ULC as a disregarded entity, but such election is not recognized for Canadian tax purposes. Canada has adopted hybrid mismatch rules under the OECD Multilateral Instrument and domestic provisions that may defer or deny deductions where the same payment is treated inconsistently across jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CRA GST/HST small-supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 in total taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) summed over the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; registration becomes mandatory once exceeded. For BC PST, a vendor making PST-taxable sales in British Columbia must register without a de minimis threshold; however, digital API services supplied to non-residents are not generally subject to PST unless they constitute taxable software/media services used in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Excise Tax Act (Canada), a supply of digital AI/API services to a non-resident recipient is generally zero-rated for GST/HST if the service is not supplied to, or used by, a person in Canada. A BC ULC providing generative AI and LLM inference APIs to foreign customers can claim the 0% rate when the recipient is non-resident and the service is not consumed in Canada. Supporting the zero-rating claim relies on the place-of-supply rules (including use-in-Canada indicators), contracts, payment records, and the supplier maintaining reasonable books and records evidencing non-resident receipt.

  • Executed written contract or master service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and describing the AI/LLM API service supplied
  • Commercial invoice incorporating the supplier's GST/HST registration number, service description, quantity/tokens consumed, and amount in Canadian dollars
  • Third-party payment settlement evidence (e.g., SWIFT/MT103, Stripe payout report, or bank confirmation) demonstrating foreign-source consideration received within the required period
  • Certificate of non-resident status or foreign tax identification from the customer (e.g., foreign VAT registration certificate, board resolution, or signed declaration of non-residency and non-use in Canada)
  • System-generated API usage logs or platform transaction metadata evidencing that the digital service was delivered to and consumed outside Canada

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing/API clearance exists at the Canadian federal or British Columbia provincial level. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically via GST/HST NETFILE once registered; PST returns must be filed through eTaxBC. Digital service suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed information (name, BN, date, description, tax calculation) and retain all records in Canada or accessible electronically in Canadian currency where required.

CRA may reassess within the normal four-year reassessment period and retroactively apply GST/HST to supplies previously treated as zero-rated where evidence of foreign receipt and use is deficient, together with prescribed interest. Administrative penalties under the Excise Tax Act may include up to C$50,000 for knowingly false statements, a 50% gross-negligence penalty on unreported tax, and director liability in certain cases. BC Ministry of Finance may similarly reassess PST obligations within statutory limitation periods, levy penalties and interest, and pursue collection by distraint or legal action where PST-taxable software/digital sales are established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada abolished exchange controls on December 14, 1951 with the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Act, and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, making the Canadian dollar fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no restrictions, licensing requirements, or advance approvals on inbound equity injections, dividend remittances, repatriation of invested capital, or settlement of trade debts. Profit remittance is unrestricted at the exchange-control layer; the only fiscal friction is Part XIII withholding on payments to non-residents (dividends 25%, reduced to 15% or 5% under the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention for treaty-eligible shareholders; interest at 25%, reduced to 10% or 0% for US arm's-length lenders; royalties 25%, reduced to 10%, with Canada-US protocol nuances), plus the deemed-residency rule in subsection 250(4)(a) of the Income Tax Act that renders a BC-incorporated ULC resident in Canada and taxable on worldwide income. As a statutory backstop, the Currency Act and the Emergencies Act permit temporary exchange controls only during a declared national emergency, which is not in effect.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory surrender, conversion, repatriation, or holding-period requirement on export proceeds or foreign currency receipts. API token sale revenues, license fees, and usage-based fees earned by the ULC from foreign customers may be retained offshore in USD, USDC, or any other currency, or converted to CAD at market rates without central bank approval. The applicable constraints are indirect: (i) functional-currency reporting under IFRS/ASPE with CRA approval for a USD functional currency; (ii) GST/HST zero-rating documentation for exported digital supply; and (iii) FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., CAD 10,000 incoming/outgoing wire reporting and electronic funds transfer records) under the PCMLTFA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as central bank and payment-system supervisor under the Retail Payment Activities Act (in force November 1, 2024); foreign exchange administrative functions lodged with the Minister of Finance acting through the Exchange Fund Account under the Currency Act (RSC 1985, c C-52); prudential regulation and deposit-taking institution supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); anti-money laundering supervision by FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. No separate foreign exchange administration authority exists because Canada maintains no standing exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Canadian law does not recognize the EU/UK 'Electronic Money Institution' licence and a foreign EMI cannot passport into Canada. A BC ULC treasury may lawfully use: (i) a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) in Canada — either a separate licensed MSB subsidiary of the ULC or a commercial MSB payment facilitator — required also under the BC Money Services Business Act (regulated by Consumer Protection BC) for money remittance activity in British Columbia; (ii) Canadian challenger-bank or banking-as-a-service fronting arrangements (e.g., embedded platforms backed by an OSFI-regulated Schedule I chartered bank account); and (iii) cross-border payment aggregators such as Wise operating through its Canadian MSB/PSP registration. Any payment service provider serving the ULC must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act unless an exemption applies, and the ULC itself must not become an unlicensed money transmitter or securities dealer. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound CAD wires must transit Payments Canada's Lynx system (ISO 20022, same-day final settlement). SWIFT instructions require: (i) beneficiary bank BIC (e.g., ROYCCAT2 for RBC, TDOMCATTTOR for TD, BOFMCAM2 for BMO, NOSCCATT for Scotiabank, CIBCCATT for CIBC); (ii) beneficiary account in the 8-digit transit-institution format (e.g., 00010-003 for a RBC Vancouver transit with institution 003), since Canada does not use IBANs; (iii) field 70 remittance information expressly stating 'capital contribution to ULC' with reference to the share subscription resolution; and (iv) where the transfer exceeds CAD 10,000, the ULC must retain the SWIFT record for FINTRAC electronic funds transfer reporting. For USDC: convert to USD/CAD through a regulated Canadian MSB or licensed US state trust on-ramp, record the trade tickets and wallet addresses, and credit the resulting fiat to the operating account; (2) Lynx ISO 20022 pacs.008 or EFT batch files for domestic CAD disbursements; (3) Interac e-Transfer for low-value payments under CAD 10,000 per transaction with receipt discipline and vendor-matching; (4) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600/eUCP (SWIFT MT700/MT707) for prepaid AI infrastructure procurement where the counterparty requires bank payment undertakings; and (5) crypto-native disbursements only for registered blockchain vendors and capped at USD 50,000 per transaction absent an enhanced FX/AML review; USD disbursements above USD 100,000 should be batched and routed through the bank's US correspondent or a USD clearing account to minimize fees and unlock interest-sweep.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards (CAD/USD, CAD/EUR) up to 12 months tenor under the ISDA 1998 FX and Currency Options Definitions, executed under a bank FX Master Agreement with the ULC.FX swaps (spot-offset-forward) and plain-vanilla FX options (CAD calls/puts) for asymmetric hedging of volatile USDC/USD revenue peaks, with premiums expensed on revenue account under ordinary Income Tax Act principles (section 9).Cross-currency swaps (CCS) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex to convert USD-denominated future API license receivables into CAD.Zero-cost collars / participating forwards for USDC-to-USD floor protection, offered by OSFI-regulated Canadian banks as non-speculative treasury hedges.Permissible only as documented hedging in a board-approved FX Risk Management Policy; prohibited instruments: NDFs on CAD (market convention favors deliverable forwards because CAD is fully convertible), physically settled crypto derivatives, and speculative ETH/BTC futures positions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Canadian law does not recognize the EU/UK 'Electronic Money Institution' licence and a foreign EMI cannot passport into Canada. A BC ULC treasury may lawfully use: (i) a FINTRAC-registered money services business (MSB) in Canada — either a separate licensed MSB subsidiary of the ULC or a commercial MSB payment facilitator — required also under the BC Money Services Business Act (regulated by Consumer Protection BC) for money remittance activity in British Columbia; (ii) Canadian challenger-bank or banking-as-a-service fronting arrangements (e.g., embedded platforms backed by an OSFI-regulated Schedule I chartered bank account); and (iii) cross-border payment aggregators such as Wise operating through its Canadian MSB/PSP registration. Any payment service provider serving the ULC must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act unless an exemption applies, and the ULC itself must not become an unlicensed money transmitter or securities dealer. For USDC flows, the ULC must use a regulated Canadian MSB or US state/OCC-chartered on-ramp and off-ramp; holding USDC with an unregulated custodian may constitute dealing in or advising on 'crypto assets' under BC securities law (Securities Act RSBC 1996 c 418) if the token confers investment rights. Feasibility rating: Medium for Stripe-native and Wise-native CAD/USD rails; Medium-Low for Unlicensed direct USDC banking without an MSB/custody wrapper.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1 million for services, CAD 5 million for tangible goods, CAD 5 million for intangible property (per transaction category); Master File required if total revenue > CAD 50 million

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal ESR, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) applies; a ULC must demonstrate real economic substance (e.g., employees, assets, risks) to avoid recharacterization of transactions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Income Tax Act: fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under Section 227.1 of the Income Tax Act, including civil restitution and potential travel bans
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR may deny tax benefits, reassess income, and impose penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for negligence or fraud, leading to personal asset seizure and criminal charges
AML

Statutory sanction: Fines up to CAD 2 million per violation for corporations; imprisonment for individuals up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent AML violations, including civil penalties and travel bans
