Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC), including the Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/initiatives/business-registration-online.html); for BC-specific levies: BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (Provincial Sales Tax/PST), with corporate existence administered by the BC Corporate Registry (Corporate Online).

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the BC ULC under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) through the BC Corporate Registry's Corporate Online service to obtain the BC company number and establish a registered office in BC. (2) Register the entity with the CRA for a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) via BRO or Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number), adding the RT program account for GST/HST and the RC program account for corporate income tax. (3) Where the entity is non-resident-controlled, provide a Canadian address for service under ETA s. 241, appoint a Canadian tax representative (Form T1013 / RepID 'Represent a Client' setup), and certify the foreign shareholder's incorporation documents and unlimited liability provisions in the company's notice of articles. (4) Elect a fiscal year-end (ordinarily December 31) and file the T2 Corporation Income Tax Return within six months of year-end, remitting any Part I balance due within two months of year-end (foreign-controlled corporation rule). (5) Open a CRA 'My Business Account', authorize electronic GST/HST NETFILE filing, and file Form T106 where non-arm's-length transactions with non-resident shareholders exceed prescribed thresholds. (6) Apply separately to the BC Ministry of Finance for a PST vendor registration where the ULC makes taxable retail sales of goods or enumerated services (including certain software subscription/SaaS supplies) into British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a check-the-box regime equivalent to IRC § 7701. For a BC ULC, the following elections and hybrid recharacterizations are available: (i) Treaty transparency attribution under the Canada–US Tax Treaty (5th Protocol, Article IV(6)(c)), which permits income of the ULC to be attributed directly to a US-resident shareholder for treaty purposes where the shareholder is subject to tax on that income in the US, thereby mitigating Canadian entity-level Part I tax in qualifying structures; (ii) the nil-consideration election under ETA s. 156 (Form GST25) for closely related corporations (≥91% common ownership), which treats inter-corporate supplies of taxable services as made for nil consideration and eliminates self-assessment friction within the corporate group; (iii) the ITC election to claim input tax credits under ETA ss. 169–170 for all Canadian inputs used in zero-rated export activities; and (iv) election of a non-calendar fiscal year-end for T2 purposes under ITA s. 249.1, subject to CRA approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in aggregate worldwide taxable revenues (including supplies deemed made in Canada by a non-resident) measured over the current calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters under ETA s. 240; a non-resident supplier crossing this small-supplier threshold must register for GST/HST. Voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold to claim ITCs and to charge GST on Canadian-facing supplies, subject to a one-year minimum registration period. BC PST registration is required independently for any vendor making taxable retail sales in BC, with no equivalent economic-threshold exemption for enumerated taxable services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act. For services, ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7 zero-rates a supply of a service made to a non-resident person who is not registered under Part IX and who is not present in Canada at the time the service is rendered, provided the service is not in respect of real or immovable property situated in Canada, not in respect of tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance, and not a service to be performed wholly or primarily for a person resident in Canada. The ISIC 6201 commercial web-scraping and proxy API gateway qualifies as an exported service where the API subscription is entered into by a foreign counterparty, the payment originates outside Canada, and the delivery evidence (server access logs, IP geolocation, API utilization telemetry) demonstrates offshore consumption. Canadian-source inputs (cloud hosting in Canada, bandwidth, software development) generate input tax credits (ETA ss. 169–170) that produce net refunds on the GST/HST return where zero-rated exports exceed taxable Canadian supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs B13A / CAED electronic filing) — required for physical export of goods; not applicable to pure API/software services
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (ocean/air transport document) — required only where tangible server equipment or media goods cross the Canadian border
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice issued by the receiving Canadian financial institution evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration from the non-resident
  • Signed Master Services Agreement / API subscription terms identifying the non-resident counterparty, delivery points, and offshore governing law
  • Itemized commercial invoice specifying the zero-rating basis (Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7) with no GST/HST charged
  • Proof of non-resident recipient status (foreign certificate of incorporation, W-8BEN-E, foreign VAT/GST registration certificate)
  • API delivery and usage logs demonstrating offshore consumption (IP geolocation, session records, bandwidth utilization)
  • Foreign exchange conversion records evidencing conversion of received foreign currency within the 180-day repatriation benchmark

Canada does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API, digital stamping, or government invoice-authorization gateway for B2B/B2C electronic invoicing. A GST/HST registrant must self-issue invoices and credit notes under ETA ss. 221–223 containing prescribed particulars (BN and GST/HST registration number, date, supplier name, description, consideration, tax amount or zero-rating reference), retain records for six years per ETA s. 286, and transmit returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE with ACH/wire remittance. The CRA's gateway receives structured returns and remittance files without per-invoice clearance. For B2G e-invoicing, the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework and PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard are being phased in for federal procurement, but this does not create a general private-sector clearance mandate.

The CRA audits zero-rating claims with focus on recipient non-residency, absence of Canadian consumption, contractual substance, and payment-chain integrity. Under ETA s. 296 the CRA may reassess within four years of the return due date, without time limit where a return contains a misrepresentation, fraud, or willful default. False statements attract penalties under ETA s. 285.1 (up to C$25,000 plus up to 10% of net tax) and, for income tax, a 50% gross-negligence penalty under ITA s. 163.2 where the zero-rating characterization is reckless. Interest under ETA s. 280 compounds daily from the original due date. Criminal evasion under ETA s. 239 exposes the ULC and its directors to fines of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to five years. Deficient export documentation results in retroactive disallowance of the 0% rate, reversal of claimed ITCs, reassessment of full 5% GST on the entire export consideration, plus penalties and daily compounding interest; the CRA's Voluntary Disclosures Program may provide partial penalty and interest relief only if the correction is made before CRA enforcement contact. Canada does not impose a codified 180-day statutory currency repatriation period for service exporters, but the 180-day benchmark is applied administratively as evidence of substance; materially longer unexplained non-repatriation is treated as a sham or non-arm's-length indicator supporting full clawback reassessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating, fully convertible currency on both the current and capital account. The Currency Act and Bank of Canada Act do not impose exchange controls or licensing gates. Dividends, management fees, interest and capital repayments may be remitted from a BC ULC subject to non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (statutory 25%; Canada-US treaty often reduces to 5% on dividends with substantial shareholding, 0% on interest and royalty under applicable Treaty provisions). Cryptocurrency such as USDC is not designated legal tender; its conversion into CAD is treated as a taxable disposition under the Income Tax Act, but this does not block treasury conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Canada does not mandate export proceeds to be repatriated or exchanged, nor any mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts derived from public web scraping API subscriptions may be retained in USD or CAD under LYNX and correspondent clearing. For intercompany foreign-currency balances, section 17 of the Income Tax Act imposes a deemed-interest rule on outstanding shareholder loans, and section 90(6) may apply to resident ULCs classified as foreign accrual property income structures if US tax treated as disregarded entity.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), operating with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the bank prudential regulator and financial intelligence unit respectively

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. A foreign FinTech EMI is not recognized in Canada as a bank or deposit-taking institution under the Bank Act. Digital EMI accounts held abroad cannot be used as primary treasury accounts for CAD clearing. To operate in British Columbia, an offshore EMI must register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) administered by the Bank of Canada. It may provide API-based merchant payment infrastructure but must clear final CAD liquidity through a Schedule I Canadian chartered bank. Integration with USDC/Stripe is permissible as a payment rail but not as a cash management bank account.

There is no revenue or asset threshold for a private ULC; an audit is compulsory if the ULC is a reporting issuer, if shareholders do not waive audit, or if the company operates in a sector requiring audit assurance.
