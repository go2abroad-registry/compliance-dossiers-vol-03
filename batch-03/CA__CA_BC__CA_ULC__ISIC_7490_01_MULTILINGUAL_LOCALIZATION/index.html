Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Domestic Canadian default is entity-level taxation of the BC ULC under the Income Tax Act; no check-the-box election exists under Canadian domestic law. However, the BC ULC is eligible for a U.S. federal check-the-box election (Form 8832) to flow through as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. income tax purposes, and may rely on the Canada-U.S. Tax Treaty fiscally-transparent-entity provisions. No Canadian hybrid mismatch election; ULC business profits remain net taxable income computed under Part I on a CBCA/BCA basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST small supplier threshold: CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated but excluding exempt supplies and grants) per any four consecutive calendar quarters; BC PST: generally CAD 10,000 annual gross revenue from taxable BC sales for small-seller exemption, subject to specific service exceptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: exported from Canada by the supplier or on behalf of the non-resident recipient, satisfying the documentary evidence requirements under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, sections 20-23. For professional multilingual translation/localization services: zero-rating is generally available when the supply is made to a non-resident who is not registered/not carrying on business in Canada, the service is not performed substantially in Canada (or is ancillary to exported tangible property), and the service does not relate to Canadian real property or in-Canada tangible personal property; contracts, performance location evidence, and payment invoices are required. BC PST does not apply to goods exported by the seller or services delivered/consumed outside BC if title and possession pass in the export transaction.

  • Bill of Lading, airway bill or freight/cargo control manifest evidencing physical departure from Canada to the non-resident destination

No national real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or e-invoice digital authorization in Canada; CRA does not mandate a single e-invoice protocol. Businesses are required to issue invoices containing the GST/HST registration number, charge GST/PST, and retain records in readable electronic form. BC PST returns and eTaxBC filings are electronic; Canada Border Services Agency selects and verifies export entries through the Export Reporting Program (GEM/B13A/CERS).

CRA may reassess within the normal 4-year reassessment period or 8 years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect or fraud; false or inflated zero-rating claims are subject to zero-rating reversal, GST/PST reassessment, interest (daily compound prescribed rate), and penalties. A false statement or omission knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence triggers penalties under ETA section 285.1 (25% of net GST payable sought) and section 280.1, plus criminal sanctions for export fraud. BC PST authorities may issue 12% penalty (25% on first assessment) plus interest for failure to remit, and may pursue directors of a ULC because of unlimited liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is a fully convertible jurisdiction under IMF Article VIII: both the current account and the capital account are liberalized, with no prior approval, license, quota, or repatriation constraint on trade settlements, intercompany funding, equity injections, or profit dividends. The Canadian dollar floats freely and there is no central-bank conversion window, no surrender obligation, and no administrative rate. Profit remittance was a BC ULC to a non-resident parent is permitted without Bank Act authorization, subject only to Part XIII Income Tax Act non-resident withholding (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced) and the documentary expectations of the paying bank. For a US-resident shareholder of a BC/AB unlimited liability company, the Canada-US Income Tax Convention (Article VII(8) recharacterization rule) may reclassify dividends as business profits attributable to a US permanent establishment, eliminating Part XIII withholding on that portion. Practical frictions are confined to FINTRAC and cross-border currency reporting thresholds (CAD 10,000 or more), CRS/FATCA self-certification, Statistics Canada balance-of-payments surveys, and OSFI-conducted bank-level source-of-funds diligence; none of these constitutes a legal convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed settlement timeline, and no designated conversion rate for export receipts. A BC ULC may retain export invoices in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency in resident or non-resident bank accounts and may convert at any Canadian chartered bank or licensed FX dealer. The only mandatory easements are: (1) accurate booking of foreign receivables in the entity's functional/Canadian-dollar financial statements under IFRS or ASPE; (2) inclusion in Income Tax Act reporting where foreign income exceeds CAD 10,000 or foreign assets exceed CAD 100,000 (T1135 foreign income verification statement); (3) reporting to Statistics Canada under the International Transactions Reporting System when annual cross-border payments or receivables exceed threshold amounts (currently CAD 30,000 for certain financial claims); and (4) FINTRAC/CBSA reporting of cash or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more entering or leaving Canada.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — central bank and sole issuer of Canadian banknotes; Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) — prudential supervisor of federally regulated banks and trust companies; Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) — AML/ATF regulator for financial institutions and money services businesses; Department of Finance Canada — custodian of the Currency Act, exchange-rate policy and sanctions administration; Statistics Canada — administrator of the International Transactions Reporting System for balance-of-payments. Canada has no dedicated 'Foreign Exchange Administration' or exchange-control tribunal, as exchange controls were abolished in 1951 and the Canadian dollar has floated freely since 1970.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viability rating: HIGH as supplementary settlement and payout rails; LOW as sole repository of corporate liquidity. Wise B2B and Payoneer are registered with FINTRAC as money services businesses (MSBs); they are not 'banks' or 'authorized foreign banks' under the Bank Act, cannot take deposit liabilities in Canada, and funds held with them are not CDIC-insured nor prudentially supervised by OSFI. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general corporate income tax rate (15% federal general rate after provincial tax abatement plus 12% British Columbia general corporate rate); 11% combined rate on active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation within the federal CAD 500,000 business limit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada; British Columbia imposes no separate dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation should be prepared and retained when the annual aggregate value of non-arm's-length cross-border transactions with a non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000, which is also the T106 information-return filing threshold. For intercompany loans, a principal balance exceeding CAD 1,000,000 triggers documentation. Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more; CRA administrative policy expects Master File and Local File standards for substantial intercompany transactions.

Economic substance scope: Canada is a full-tax jurisdiction and is not subject to offshore Economic Substance Regulations. However, the Income Tax Act s.245 General Anti-Avoidance Rule denies the tax benefit of arrangements lacking genuine commercial substance, and the OECD Multilateral Instrument applies a principal purpose test that can strip treaty benefits from a BC ULC inserted purely for treaty shopping. CRA auditors will review payroll, premises, revenue-generating assets, and management decision-making to verify that the ULC has real economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misleading

Statutory sanction: I.T.A. s.239: fine of not less than 50% and not more than 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 5 years on indictment; civil gross negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax under I.T.A. s.163(2); prescribed-rate interest and potential asset freeze by the CRA.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly concur in the offence are co-perpetrators; the corporate veil is pierced, and directors become liable for civil restitution, fines, travel bans pursuant to court warrants, asset freezes, and imprisonment under I.T.A. s.239.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / TP Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing reassessment and permanent denial of deductions; I.T.A. s.247(3) penalty equal to 10% of the adjustment; prescribed-rate interest; statutory penalties for failure to comply with a CRA documentation demand under s.247(4)-(5); and possible criminal sanction where the transfer misstatement is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors are not generally liable for simple transfer-pricing adjustments, but knowing participation in fraudulent transfer pricing exposes them to personal liability for misleading returns, civil restitution, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment if the conduct constitutes tax evasion.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse, and AML Trigger

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of tax benefits; denial of treaty benefits under the OECD Multilateral Instrument limitation-on-benefits / principal purpose test; recharacterized amounts subject to full 25% Part XIII withholding; violations of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act may attract civil penalties up to CAD 500,000 per violation for entities; Criminal Code money laundering is an indictable offence carrying imprisonment up to 10 years and possible fines.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell ULC may be held personally liable upon piercing the corporate veil for fraudulent transfers, unlawful distributions, or equitable fraud; courts may impose civil restitution, travel bans, asset freezes, and criminal liability on directors who sign false statutory declarations or knowingly facilitate de facto treaty shopping.
