Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; official portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes.html and the CRA My Business Account portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html

E-government filing portal

A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada must first obtain a federal Business Number (BN) from the CRA, either through Business Registration Online (BRO) or by submitting Form RC1. For federal incorporation, the entity applies via Corporations Canada under the Canada Business Corporations Act; the resulting incorporation number is integrated into the BN. After obtaining the BN, the corporation opens a separate GST/HST program account (RC0001) using Form RC1G or RC1A, or through BRO. Registration becomes mandatory once taxable supplies in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; below that threshold, registration is voluntary.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box pass-through election. A Canadian business corporation is a separate taxpayer subject to net federal corporate income tax on worldwide income if resident, and on Canadian-source income if non-resident. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) section 85 Income Tax Act rollover election for tax-deferred transfers of property to a corporation; (ii) associated corporation designation under section 256 to allocate the small business deduction; (iii) partnership elections under section 96 or 103 for agreed allocation of taxable incomes; (iv) hybrid mismatch elections affecting regulatory capital instruments under section 18.4; and (v) section 216 election available to non-residents to file a Canadian net rental income return instead of being subject to Part XIII gross-basis withholding tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a supply of tangible personal property sold to a non-resident if the property is exported from Canada as part of the supply (Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V), and for services supplied to non-residents where the supply falls within Schedule VII (e.g., services performed wholly outside Canada or supplied to persons not resident in Canada and not used in Canada). For export of Grade A refined copper cathodes, the supplier must obtain and retain documentary evidence of exportation, including proof of transfer of ownership to the non-resident, confirmation of international shipment, and evidence of receipt of foreign proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) transmission with export permit where applicable
  • Bill of Lading: original through bill of lading (or equivalent multimodal/ocean/air waybill) indicating Canadian port of lading and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/remittance documentation evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds in Canada

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based government approval is required for invoices in Canada. Registered GST/HST filers must file returns electronically using GST/HST NETFILE or GST/HST TELEFILE; mandatory electronic filing applies to registrants with annual revenues exceeding CAD 1 million. Invoices must state the supplier's Business Number, but there is no pre-clearance or continuous transaction control system (CTCS) analogous to jurisdictions such as Mexico, Brazil, or Spain.

The CRA conducts targeted audits of export zero-rating claims, requiring full documentation of physical export and proof of payment. If the zero-rating is disallowed, the GST/HST originally uncollected becomes payable, plus interest and penalties. Under the Excise Tax Act, a registrant that knowingly makes a false or omission-inclusive statement may be liable to a penalty of 50% of the net tax attributable; fraudulent evasion carries criminal penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded, fines, and imprisonment. The normal reassessment period of four years is extended where misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime and imposes no quantitative convertibility limits, no foreign-exchange licensing, and no approval requirement for profit remittance by a federally incorporated Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders. Remittance feasibility is restricted only by the Income Tax Act (Canada) Part XIII withholding tax at the statutory 25% (treaty-reduced), by thin-capitalisation rules under subsections 18(4) through 18(7), and by Investment Canada Act review thresholds applicable to a non-Canadian controlling shareholder acquiring control of a Canadian business. FINTRAC cross-border currency reporting of CAD 10,000 or its foreign equivalent applies to physical cash transport and to electronic funds transfers under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose any statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, any mandatory conversion ratio, or any prescribed timeline for repatriating export receipts. A Canadian exporting entity may invoice, hold, and borrow against foreign-currency receivables without restriction. Compliance obligations are limited to FINTRAC reporting of single or linked electronic funds transfers in excess of CAD 10,000 conducted through banks or money service businesses, and to record-keeping and client-identification duties under the PCMLTFA and OSFI Guideline E-13. Profit retained offshore requires attention to Canadian controlled foreign corporation rules (sections 91 to 95 of the Income Tax Act) but is not an exchange-control matter.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). No dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration exists in Canada; exchange-rate policy is implemented jointly by the Department of Finance Canada and the Bank of Canada under the Bank of Canada Act, and prudential supervision of banks is conducted by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). AML/ATF transaction reporting is administered by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium. Canada has no dedicated 'Electronic Money Institution' licensing category. Non-bank payment providers are regulated as Money Service Businesses registered with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA and, where they engage in retail payment activities on an ongoing basis, must be registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer may technically support ancillary operating disbursements, vendor payments, and payroll; however, they are generally not accepted as the principal clearing or settlement bank for LME-Grade A copper cathode transactions, documentary letter-of-credit operations, or LME warrant transfer settlements, because those require a Canadian Schedule I chartered bank's correspondent banking network, DVP capabilities, and SWIFT-based commodity trade finance infrastructure. Feasibility rating for the primary treasury rail is Low; for ancillary non-trade disbursements it is Medium.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (1) Equity injection — a corporate resolution authorises the share issue; the non-resident shareholder sends SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the Canadian bank's correspondent account using purpose code 'equity contribution' or 'share subscription', with the beneficiary reference clearly noting the Federal Corporate registry number and CA_CORP entity name. The bank credits the Capital Escrow Account before release to the operating account, with source-of-wealth and source-of-funds documentation on file. (2) Shareholder loan — intercompany loan agreement, arm's-length interest, and Part XIII withholding assessment are required; thin-capitalisation must be monitored under subsections 18(4)-(7). (3) Verification and reporting — FINTRAC EFT reporting applies at CAD 10,000; OSFI E-13 client identification and ongoing transaction monitoring govern the bank's release of funds; no Bank of Canada or Department of Finance approval is required for CAD convertibility of the inward remittance.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for supplier invoices, logistics providers, inspection services, and commissions; MT203/MT202 for correspondent-bank gross settlements. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the end of the taxation year irrespective of the balance-due date. The final tax payment (Part I balance due) must be remitted no later than three months after the year-end for Canadian-controlled private corporations and two months after the year-end for all other corporations (including non-resident-owned federal corporations). Quarterly instalments are mandatory where the estimated tax payable for the current year or the actual tax payable for the preceding year exceeds CAD 3,000; large corporations (taxable capital above CAD 10 million) must pay monthly instalments. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must also file a T2 Part III election and a schedule allocating taxable income among provinces and to the federal base.
