Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC). Primary portals: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) and Business Registration Online (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/business-number.html).

E-government filing portal

A CBCA corporation receives its nine-digit Business Number (BN) automatically from the CRA upon federal incorporation through Corporations Canada. A foreign-incorporated parent or branch must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) electronically via Business Registration Online, by telephone (1-800-959-5525), or by mail; online filings receive the BN immediately. The applicant must then activate program accounts under the same BN: corporate income tax, GST/HST ('RT'), payroll ('RP'), and import/export ('RM'). GST/HST registration is made through Part 4 of Form RC1 or the online GST/HST application; voluntary registration is permitted for small suppliers to claim input tax credits. Provincial coexistence: the BN remains the federal identifier, while each province of permanent establishment (e.g., Revenu Québec NEQ, Ontario Master Business Licence) requires separate extra-provincial registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box election. A CBCA business corporation is mandatorily a Canadian resident corporation subject to Part I federal income tax at entity level on worldwide income. Available statutory elections affecting cross-border exporters: (i) ITA subsection 216(1) election for non-residents to pay net Canadian rental tax instead of 25% gross Part XIII withholding; (ii) ITA subsection 217(1) election for specified Canadian-source pension income; (iii) GST/HST Quick Method accounting election for eligible small suppliers; (iv) associated-corporation elections allocating the small business limit; and (v) hybrid-mismatch results are non-elective: ITA sections 18.4 to 18.6 automatically deny deductions for hybrid arrangements. Export zero-rating itself is statutory under Schedule VI of the Excise Tax Act and requires no election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold: a person carrying on commercial activity in Canada must register for GST/HST once worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or in the four preceding consecutive calendar quarters. Non-residents making taxable supplies in Canada are subject to the same threshold and may voluntarily register to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act, a supply of tangible personal property (the Baijiu goods) is zero-rated when the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada in direct or continuous passage, or transfers them to a common carrier for export. Supporting services (distribution, logistics, marketing, brokerage) supplied to a non-resident buyer who is not a GST/HST registrant are also zero-rated unless the service relates to real or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The exporter must demonstrate that title and risk passed to the foreign buyer, that the goods physically left Canada, and that the consideration was paid. GST/HST paid at importation on Baijiu subsequently exported may be claimed as an input tax credit.

  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign-buyer bank wire / FX settlement evidence)
  • Commercial Sales Invoice (HS 2208.90, description, quantity, export destination)
  • Packing List and Container Manifest
  • CBSA Commercial Accounting Declaration (Form B3 or equivalent border release document)

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital-invoice validation regime for private-sector transactions as of the 2025 tax year. Mandatory electronic requirements are: (1) GST/HST returns must be filed electronically (GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account) by registrants with annual gross revenues exceeding CAD 1 million, and all remittances of CAD 50,000 or more must be made by electronic funds transfer; (2) large suppliers (annual worldwide taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million) must remit electronically and file monthly; and (3) exporters must transmit export declarations to the CBSA through the Export Reporting System (ERS) prior to shipment, obtaining a transaction number. The CRA participates in the voluntary PEPPOL network for federal government procurement e-invoicing (PEPPOL-BIS 3.0); no digital stamp is required for export invoices.

Failure to substantiate physical export and receipt of consideration exposes the exporter to CRA reassessment of the full sale consideration at 5% GST (plus applicable HST by place of supply), together with compound daily arrears interest. Statutory consequences include: a false-statement penalty under subsection 285(1) of the Excise Tax Act equal to 25% of the tax sought to be evaded (50% for gross negligence or repeat conduct); denial of all related input tax credits (full ITC clawback on Baijiu inventory, warehousing, and freight); registration revocation; and criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code, with fines and imprisonment. The normal GST/HST reassessment window is four years, but it is unlimited for misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default, permitting retrospective clawback. Records must be retained six years; although Canadian law imposes no fixed 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation statutory deadline, the absence of an inward remittance record within the 180-day trade-finance benchmark defeats zero-rating evidentiary requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the Canadian dollar is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control approvals for trade payments, dividends, royalties, or intercompany loan repayments. The only substantive constraints are: (i) Investment Canada Act notification or national-security review for foreign investments; (ii) Global Affairs Canada sanctions screening; and (iii) Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, reduced to 10% for dividends and royalties under the Canada-China Income Tax Convention). Profit remittance is executed as a standard SWIFT wire supported by a board resolution and Part XIII/NR4 compliance documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation deadline. Legal obligations are confined to trade reporting: commercial exports valued above CAD 2,000 must be declared to CBSA through the Canadian Export Reporting System (B13A), and monthly totals must reconcile to Statistics Canada's Canadian International Merchandise Trade dataset. Proceeds may be retained in CAD, USD, CNY/CNH, or any freely convertible currency without Bank of Canada or OSFI intervention.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary, clearing, and wholesale-settlement authority but does not operate exchange controls. Banking prudential supervision is exercised by OSFI; AML/ATF reporting is supervised by FINTRAC; economic sanctions and import/export permits are administered by Global Affairs Canada under SEMA; customs and border enforcement is handled by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). Together these institutions define the FX-adjacent compliance perimeter for a Canada-based spirits exporter.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - supplementary use only. Fintech EMIs such as Wise Business, Payoneer, and Corpay are not licensed banks in Canada; they must either hold a provincial money-service-business license or register under the Bank of Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and cannot accept CDIC-insurable deposits. They are legally viable for low-value CAD/USD receipts and small supplier top-ups, but must not be the primary clearing rail because they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, cannot access Lynx correspondent settlement, and introduce IFRS 9 hedge-accounting complexity for CNY-denominated trade flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CA$1,000,000: total fair market value of all non-arm's length transactions with non-residents in a taxation year triggering the T106 reporting requirement. Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue over CA$1.1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply standalone Economic Substance Regulations like the UAE. Instead, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, s.245 of the Income Tax Act) recharacterizes transactions lacking genuine commercial purpose. Treaty relief also requires the recipient to be the beneficial owner with substantive business activity. A Canadian baijiu exporter must maintain actual physical presence, payroll, bank accounts, management decisions, and wholesale distribution functions in Canada; a brittle shell or letter-box entity will be denied treaty benefits and profit attribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s.239(1): fine of 50% to 200% of tax sought to be evaded; disgorgement of taxes, interest, and penalty; criminal investigation by the Canada Revenue Agency Criminal Investigations Program.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or authorize the false filings may be personally assessed for withheld tax debts under s.227.1, ordered to pay civil restitution, subjected to indiscriminate asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to five years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Aggressive Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Section 247(3) penalty: 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment; reassessment of taxable income to the arm's length level; daily T106 filing penalties up to CA$25,000; interest on additional assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and attest to arm's length outcome can be held personally liable for gross-negligence penalties; CRA may seek contribution through the courts for negligent supervision.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s.245) denial of the deduction or treaty benefit; issuance of an unfair tax benefit recharacterization; 50% GAAR penalty (subject to taxpayer relief provisions); treaty benefits denied under the beneficial ownership requirement and OECD BEPS Action 6; possible loss of DTA reduced rates.

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly liable under s.160 of the Income Tax Act for property transferred to shareholders in the course of a sham transaction; travel ban and personal tax liability if CRA proves the director knew of the treaty misuse.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act administrative monetary penalties: up to CA$1 million for serious violations; criminal money-laundering offense under the Criminal Code carries imprisonment of up to 10 years; corporate officers may be prosecuted.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow untraceable wire transfers or fail to implement an AML compliance program may be personally fined, criminally prosecuted, placed under travel bans, and imprisoned for up to five years.
