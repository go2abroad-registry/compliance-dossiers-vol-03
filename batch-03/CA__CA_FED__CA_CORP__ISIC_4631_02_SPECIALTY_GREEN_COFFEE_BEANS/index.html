Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA); online portal: My Business Account (MyBA) and Business Registration Online (BRO)

E-government filing portal

A Canadian federal business corporation is incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA). The CRA assigns the 9-digit Business Number (BN), which is the statutory tax identification number for federal tax purposes. A GST/HST program account is obtained by filing Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number' or by registering through CRA's Business Registration Online. Foreign-owned or non-resident-managed corporations may use an authorized Canadian resident representative or registered agent for service and registration. The BN/RT account is required before issuing GST/HST invoices and before claiming input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a US-style check-the-box entity classification election; every CBCA corporation is taxed as a corporation under the Income Tax Act. Available statutory elections relevant to the group include the Excise Tax Act s. 156 election for closely related corporations to treat certain taxable supplies as made for nil consideration and the GST/HST quick method election for small suppliers. Hybrid mismatch rules in ITA ss. 18.1-18.7 are mandatory and cannot be elected away.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 (small-supplier threshold); mandatory GST/HST registration applies where taxable supplies made in Canada exceed CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the preceding four calendar quarters, including zero-rated supplies. A non-resident carrying on business in Canada has the same threshold; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Excise Tax Act Schedule VI, Part V, a GST/HST registrant's supply of green coffee beans exported from Canada is zero-rated where: (i) the beans are sold by the registrant; (ii) they are delivered to a common carrier, freight forwarder, or consignee for direct shipment outside Canada; (iii) the shipment leaves Canada without being consumed or further processed in Canada; and (iv) the registrant retains evidence of export satisfactory to the Minister of National Revenue under ETA s. 169(4). The registrant may claim input tax credits for GST/HST paid on commercial importation and domestic inputs to the extent attributable to the zero-rated export sales. Domestic sales to Canadian roasters for use in Canada remain taxable at 5% GST, or the applicable HST rate in participating provinces.

  • CBSA B13A Export Declaration or approved electronic export report
  • Commercial invoice (vendor, purchaser, description, quantity, unit price, HS code)
  • Packing list
  • Original bill of lading or airway bill consigned to a foreign destination
  • Cargo control document or carrier manifest evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Inward remittance evidence (bank SWIFT/MT103 credit advice or documentary letter of credit payment record)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime exists in Canada. Electronic invoicing is permitted if the invoice contains all prescribed particulars under Excise Tax Act s. 223, such as supplier name, BN, date, description, consideration, and GST/HST rate or amount. Electronic records must be retained in auditable form for at least six years and be accessible to CRA upon request.

CRA verifies export zero-rating by comparing export declarations, shipping documents, contracts, and payment records. Where the prescribed export evidence is missing, incomplete, or inconsistent, the zero-rating is reversed and GST/HST is assessed on the supply at 5% (or HST where applicable), together with statutory interest. Intentional or gross-negligent misstatements can give rise to civil penalties under the Excise Tax Act and criminal liability for tax evasion; the normal four-year reassessment period is extended indefinitely where there is misrepresentation, fraud, or wilful default. Canada does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline; export records must nevertheless be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, which Canada has accepted since 1952. No exchange control legislation restricts current-account or capital-account transactions. Foreign direct investment in a specialty green coffee import distributor is not subject to exchange approval; however, acquisitions above statutory thresholds trigger a net-benefit review under the Investment Canada Act. Profit remittance is unrestricted at the FX level but is taxed via Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada): a 25% non-resident withholding tax on dividends, typically reduced to 5–15% under Canada's tax treaties (e.g., 5% for a 10%+ corporate shareholder under the Canada–US Treaty). No permit, quota, or license is required to convert CAD to USD or other currencies for the commercial settlement of green coffee container transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriation of export receipts. A Canadian-incorporated entity may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in offshore or onshore non-resident-denominated accounts. For tax filing completeness, foreign-source income and foreign currency gains must be reported on the corporation's T2 return in CAD using the Bank of Canada daily exchange rate series; however, no central bank approval is required to hold or dispose of such funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act, in conjunction with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential bank supervision and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for low-value, open-account vendor payments; Low as the sole treasury rail because no Canadian fintech or offshore EMI can issue documentary letters of credit or provide a committed USD/CAD banking facility. Legal constraints: an EMI (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business) operating into Canada must be registered with FINTRAC as a Money Services Business (MSB), and if domiciled in Québec, licensed under the Money-Services Businesses Act. An MSB is not a 'bank' under the Bank Act: it cannot accept deposits lendable to first parties, is not CDIC-insured, and cannot originate an MT700 LC. Settlement of LC-backed green coffee container purchases must therefore route through a Schedule I bank. The EMI is acceptable only for incidental repatriation of sample revenue or minor marketing reimbursements, and must be disclosed to the primary banker to avoid unexplained third-party credit flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Instruct the foreign shareholder to send a SWIFT MT103 wire in USD or CAD to the Canadian operating account using SWIFT field 70 (Remittance Information) with the exact wording: 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION – [CORPORATE LEGAL NAME] – [ARTICLES OF INCORPORATION NUMBER]'. No statutory FX control filing is required. The receiving bank will release the funds into the CAD-demand account or a USD sweep account within 1 business day, subject to standard source-of-funds verification. Immediately upon value date, the corporation shall issue the corresponding common shares, record the allotment in the ISC register, and ensure the shareholder's subscription agreement and board resolution are pre-stamped and uploaded to the bank's digital onboarding portal. If the injection exceeds CAD 10 million, OSFI's borrowing/funding rules do not apply to equity, but the bank may require a CFI (beneficial ownership) closure of the wire through a specialized foreign-exchange dealer to avoid an undesignated credit.For specialty green coffee container purchases (FOB or CIF terms), the primary clearing rails are: (1) Irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700, governed by UCP 600, with the issuing bank — e.g., RBC, TD, or Scotiabank — settling the presentation in USD against compliant shipping documents (clean bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin); (2) SWIFT MT103 for open-account or trust-receipt settlements after quality inspection, typically cleared via Fedwire/CHIPS for USD or via Payments Canada's Large Value Transfer System (LVTS, now Canadian-dollar RTGS) for CAD; (3) CAD-to-USD conversion executed at the bank's prevailing spot desk, with a standing instruction to allocate US dollars into a dedicated trade settlement sub-account to avoid FX leakage on each LC drawdown. For shipments from Central or South America, the LC should incorporate a tolerance of ±5% on quantity and a provision for the negotiation bank to present a certificate of origin under CIFTA or the Canada–Colombia FTA to minimize customs duty surprise. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (federal general corporate income tax rate; 38% less 10% provincial abatement less 13% general rate reduction; provincial corporate rates separately apply at 11.5%–16%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII tax on taxable dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident of Canada, subject to reduction under an income tax treaty; non-treaty shareholders are withheld at 25%; under the Canada–U.S. treaty the rate is 5% for a U.S. corporate shareholder owning at least 10% of voting shares, otherwise 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (the Canadian equivalent of a Local File/Master File) is expected for reportable non-arm's length transactions; the mandatory T106 information-return threshold is CAD 1,000,000 in aggregate per non-resident non-arm's length counterparty. Country-by-Country filing is triggered at CAD 1,000,000,000 consolidated group revenue. CRA may request documentation in audit for any transaction regardless of amount.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation. Instead, commercial substance is enforced through: (i) the permanent-establishment threshold for non-resident profit taxation; (ii) Part XIII beneficial-owner and treaty shopping limitations; (iii) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s. 245; and (iv) CRA's right to recharacterize transactions. A Canadian green-coffee importer-distributor must maintain actual offices, employees, procurement and logistics decisions, inventory title and risk, and financial records in Canada; an invoice-only shell used to move Canadian sales margins offshore will be attacked under GAAR and sham doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act s. 239(1) criminal offence: fine of 50%–200% of tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years. Civil gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2): 50% of understated tax; plus statutory interest, arrears, and foreign tax credit forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or consent to evasion can be prosecuted as parties to the offence; they are personally liable for penalties and unremitted assessments if they knowingly signed false returns. CRA may obtain a departure prohibition order under s. 226 where a corporation's controlling director flouts the order with unpaid debt over CAD 50,000; criminal restitution and seizure of personal assets can follow fraud prosecutions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Section 247(2) adjustment to an arm's-length profit; penalty under s. 247(3) equal to 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the taxpayer has not made reasonable efforts to determine an arm's-length amount; contemporaneous documentation must be delivered within statutory timelines or penalties will be applied. Gross-negligence and false-statements penalties can stack, and the normal reassessment limitation period is extended to 10 years.

Director personal liability: The 10% penalty is a corporate liability, but a chief financial officer or director who knowingly signed a manipulated transfer-pricing return can be found personally liable under fraud and gross-negligence provisions, may be joined to the CRA assessment for the tax debt, and can be subject to seizure orders, departure bans, and criminal sanctions if the transfer mispricing is found to be a sham.
