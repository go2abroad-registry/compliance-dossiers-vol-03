Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), including the Business Registration Online service, My Business Account portal, and GST/HST administration platform. British Columbia provincial taxes are administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A British Columbia ULC must obtain a nine-digit CRA Business Number upon incorporation or commencement of Canadian business activities. The entity must register for a corporate income tax program account (RC), file annual Canadian corporate income tax returns using Form T2, and obtain a fifteen-character GST/HST program account (RT) when required. Registration is generally completed through CRA Business Registration Online, My Business Account, telephone registration, or authorized representative channels. A non-resident entity carrying on business in Canada must first establish its CRA Business Number and then register for applicable GST/HST, payroll, import-export, or non-resident withholding accounts. A non-resident digital supplier may use the CRA simplified GST/HST registration regime where eligible, but simplified registrants generally cannot claim input tax credits. Provincial British Columbia PST registration is separate and is made with the British Columbia Ministry of Finance where taxable software, telecommunications, digital products, or other taxable supplies are made in British Columbia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Canadian ULC is generally treated as a corporation and is subject to Canadian corporate income tax, regardless of its unlimited-liability status. Canada does not provide a general domestic check-the-box election allowing a corporation to become fiscally transparent for Canadian income tax purposes. A Canadian ULC may be eligible under United States Treasury Regulations to make a U.S. Form 8832 entity-classification election to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for U.S. federal tax purposes, subject to ownership, timing, documentation, treaty, foreign tax credit, and hybrid-mismatch limitations. The U.S. election does not change the ULC's Canadian corporate status. No Canadian GST/HST election converts the entity into a pass-through vehicle.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, measured under the small-supplier rules over the applicable single calendar quarter or preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. The threshold generally applies to the supplier and associated persons. Non-resident digital suppliers selling directly to Canadian consumers may be required to register under the simplified GST/HST regime when the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded during the relevant twelve-month period. The GST/HST rate is generally 5% in British Columbia; the provincial PST registration threshold and taxability rules are separate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated for GST/HST where the statutory export conditions are satisfied and the supplier retains adequate evidence that the goods were exported. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated where the place-of-supply and exported-service rules are met, including that the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not excluded by specific statutory exceptions. Services relating to Canadian real property, certain tangible personal property, personal services physically performed in Canada, and certain services concerning litigation or regulatory proceedings in Canada may not qualify. Electronically supplied entertainment, streaming, application access, and digital content consumed by Canadian customers are generally taxable based on the applicable place-of-supply rules; offshore servers or foreign payment settlement do not alone establish zero-rating. Export zero-rating requires contemporaneous commercial, customer-location, delivery, contractual, and payment evidence and does not automatically extend to platform commissions or other separately supplied services.

  • Canadian export declaration submitted through the applicable Canada Border Services Agency export reporting system, including the Canadian Export Reporting System where required
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, purchaser or consignee, goods, value, currency, and terms of sale
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other carrier document evidencing shipment outside Canada
  • Proof of export, delivery confirmation, customs import record, or other reliable evidence that the goods left Canada
  • Sales contract, purchase order, customer correspondence, and records identifying the non-resident purchaser or recipient
  • Payment records and accounting records supporting the supply and consideration received
  • Inward remittance certificate is not generally a statutory Canadian GST/HST document; bank statements, payment processor reports, or other reliable settlement evidence should be retained instead
  • For exported services, service agreements, customer residency records, IP or access-location data where relevant, work-delivery records, and evidence supporting the statutory exported-service conditions

Canada has no general nationwide real-time GST/HST invoice-clearance or mandatory continuous transaction-control system. There is no general requirement for CRA pre-clearance, digital tax stamping, or government-issued invoice numbering for ordinary supplies. Registered suppliers must issue invoices or other supporting documentation containing sufficient GST/HST information, maintain electronic books and records in an accessible and readable form, and retain records generally for six years from the end of the relevant year. Digital records must support tax determination, collection, filing, audit, and input-tax-credit substantiation. Apple App Store, Google Play, Stripe, and other intermediaries may have contractual marketplace tax-collection obligations; responsibility depends on the legal supplier, billing flow, customer location, and applicable platform rules.

Canada has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. CRA may deny or reverse zero-rating and assess uncollected GST/HST, applicable provincial tax, interest, and penalties where export conditions or documentary support are inadequate. The supplier may be assessed for tax that should have been collected, plus arrears interest and applicable filing, remittance, gross-negligence, or failure-to-file penalties. Gross-negligence penalties can generally equal 50% of the understated tax or improperly claimed amount. Knowingly making false statements or participating in tax evasion can result in prosecution, substantial fines, and imprisonment. Input tax credits or GST/HST refunds supported by false, incomplete, or fabricated export evidence may be reassessed and clawed back, with extended reassessment periods where misrepresentation, fraud, or negligence is alleged. Separate customs penalties, seizure, administrative monetary penalties, and criminal consequences may apply to inaccurate export declarations or prohibited conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and broadly open capital-account convertibility consistent with IMF Article VIII. No central-bank authorisation is required to convert CAD, hold foreign currency, or repatriate profits. Service-export revenue from the vertical micro-drama streaming app may be retained in foreign currency or converted through a Schedule I bank. Profit remittances from the BC ULC are effected as dividends or interest and are subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (25% statutory, treaty-reduced, commonly 5% on dividends for corporate shareholders under applicable treaties), plus CRS/FATCA reporting and the applicable arm's-length and thin-capitalisation rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime exists in Canada: there is no mandatory conversion ratio, no conversion deadline, and no obligation to repatriate export receipts into CAD. A BC ULC may retain USD/EUR treasury balances in Canadian bank operating or capital accounts. Export-related electronic transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC; Canadian-resident shareholders and the ULC must also maintain records under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and comply with specified-foreign-property reporting (T1135) where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (central monetary authority); Canada does not maintain a dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration or exchange-control licensing agency. OSFI supervises federally chartered banks; FINTRAC supervises cross-border transaction reporting and AML/ATF requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Offshore EMI passporting is not recognised in Canada. A foreign digital EMI may serve only as an ancillary payment-collection or disbursement utility; if it services Canadian persons or carries on business in Canada it must register as a money-services business with FINTRAC and cannot obtain direct LVTS or CAD clearing access. Therefore a foreign EMI is not a viable primary treasury counterparty for the BC ULC, but may be used as a targeted app-store settlement channel if funds are promptly swept into a Schedule I bank-owned treasury account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (federal general rate 15% + British Columbia general corporate rate 12%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% federal Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid or credited by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner, subject to tax treaty reductions. Under the Canada-US tax treaty, the rate is 5% if the beneficial owner is a US resident company owning directly or indirectly at least 10% of the voting shares of the Canadian ULC, and 15% in other cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return due date is six months after the corporation's taxation year-end; balance-due day for tax payable is generally two months after year-end for most corporations and three months after year-end for eligible CCPCs with a limited small business deduction entitlement

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the T2 corporation income tax return due date, generally six months after the taxation year-end; the return accompanies or references the T2 in the prescribed filing stream

Deadline: · Frequency:

NR4 information must be filed with CRA and supplied to the non-resident payee by the end of February in the year following the calendar year in which the payment was made

Part I - Non-resident related party identification Identifies each non-resident related corporation, partnership or trust, its jurisdiction, relationship with the reporting ULC, and direct/indirect ownership percentages. Complete a separate T106 for each non-resident related person and provide the treaty residence country, tax identification number if available, and the OECD affiliate category. Failure to file T106 or to provide materially incorrect information can lead to CRA discretionary penalties, daily penalties for continued non-compliance, and a loss of the reasonable-efforts defence against transfer-pricing penalty assessments; interest and gross valuation misstatement penalties may also apply.
Part II - Transaction-by-transaction description Lists every non-arm's length transaction or arrangement with a non-resident, including the goods/services, quantity, dollar value in Canadian dollars, and the transfer-pricing method applied. Use the exact C$ amounts for royalties, licence fees, distribution fees, loans, advances, guarantees and cost-sharing arrangements involving the vertical micro-drama IP and streaming revenues; identify a prescribed method for each transaction. Failure to file T106 or to provide materially incorrect information can lead to CRA discretionary penalties, daily penalties for continued non-compliance, and a loss of the reasonable-efforts defence against transfer-pricing penalty assessments; interest and gross valuation misstatement penalties may also apply.
Part III - Transparency and certification Declares whether contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation has been prepared and certifies that all information is complete and accurate. Attach the transfer-pricing method selection and comparability analysis; if treaty relief is claimed, note the protection from penalties for failing to make reasonable efforts to determine an arm's length transfer price. Failure to file T106 or to provide materially incorrect information can lead to CRA discretionary penalties, daily penalties for continued non-compliance, and a loss of the reasonable-efforts defence against transfer-pricing penalty assessments; interest and gross valuation misstatement penalties may also apply.
Box 14 - Type of income Codes identify the payment type: dividends, interest, royalties, management or administration fees, pension benefits, or other amounts paid to non-residents. For profit repatriation from a BC ULC, designate the payment as a dividend under Canadian tax law even if the ULC is fiscally transparent in the shareholder's jurisdiction; Part XIII withholding applies unless a treaty exemption applies. Failure to file NR4 forms, failure to remit Part XIII withholding, and incorrectly reduced withholding based on invalid treaty claims are subject to reassessment, interest, penalties, and personal liability for directors under Income Tax Act section 227.1.
Box 16 - Gross amount paid/credited Sets out the total Canadian-source gross payment in Canadian dollars before withholding tax. Gross amount must include deemed dividends, in-kind distributions and any amounts paid to a hybrid entity; the payor must consider the entity classification recognized by Canada for the relevant treaty. Failure to file NR4 forms, failure to remit Part XIII withholding, and incorrectly reduced withholding based on invalid treaty claims are subject to reassessment, interest, penalties, and personal liability for directors under Income Tax Act section 227.1.
Box 18 - Tax withheld/remitted Reports the Part XIII non-resident withholding tax deducted at source by the payor. Enter the reduced withholding amount only where a valid NR301 treaty-benefit certificate or other CRA-recognized documentation is held; maintain copies of the treaty residency and ownership declarations for six years. Failure to file NR4 forms, failure to remit Part XIII withholding, and incorrectly reduced withholding based on invalid treaty claims are subject to reassessment, interest, penalties, and personal liability for directors under Income Tax Act section 227.1.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 aggregate fair market value of reportable non-arm's length cross-border transactions in a taxation year triggers the mandatory T106 Information Return filing and corresponding expectation of contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation; CRA may also request or rely on OECD Master File / Local File benchmarks for any material intercompany transaction regardless of a fixed local-file dollar threshold.
