Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; e-portal: 'My Business Account' at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html; export customs counterpart: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) acting through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

A CBCA federal business corporation receives its 9-digit Business Number (BN) automatically upon incorporation through Corporations Canada. A non-resident foreign entity not incorporated in Canada must file Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) with the CRA Sudbury Tax Centre (International and Large Business Directorate), attaching certified constitutional documents, director identification, evidence of a Canadian agent for service of process, and a Canadian business address. CRA then activates program accounts RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RM (CBSA import/export). For exports, the entity must separately enrol in CERS to transmit B13A electronic export declarations. Non-resident RC1 processing generally takes 5 to 15 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a CBCA business corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation (ITA s. 89(1)) subject to federal Part I tax on worldwide income; no pass-through or check-the-box classification is available for corporations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a CBCA business corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation (ITA s. 89(1)) subject to federal Part I tax on worldwide income; no pass-through or check-the-box classification is available for corporations. For GST/HST purposes, the entity becomes a registrant once taxable supplies made in Canada exceed the CAD 30,000 small supplier threshold; it then charges 5% federal GST (plus provincial HST where the supply occurs in a participating province) on domestic taxable supplies and zero-rates qualifying exports.

Available election forms

Canada has no check-the-box classification regime. Relevant statutory elections include: (1) voluntary GST/HST registration before the CAD 30,000 threshold is exceeded (Excise Tax Act s. 240(1)); (2) nil-consideration supply election for closely related corporations (ETA s. 273(1)); (3) GST/HST Quick Method election (GST/HST Regulations s. 166); (4) deemed-dividend election under ITA s. 93(1) for dividends from foreign affiliates; (5) protective positions under the hybrid-mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4, 20.2, 92.2, 93.3, 113.3), which impose automatic recharacterization rather than elective 'check-the-box' outcomes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange permits, no quantitative restrictions, and no prior approval requirements for inbound equity injections, loan servicing, or dividend repatriation at the federal level. Repatriation of profits is constrained only by CBCA solvency conditions (sections 42–43, Canada Business Corporations Act) and, if applicable, non-resident shareholder tax withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export surrender requirement, no compulsory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation deadline for export receipts. Resident and non-resident entities may hold export earnings in any freely convertible currency (CAD, USD, EUR, CNH, etc.) in domestic or offshore accounts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) – the country's central bank; foreign exchange policy is administered jointly with the Minister of Finance and the Bank of Canada as the government's fiscal agent. Canada maintains no separate foreign-exchange control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) can provide trade settlement and multi-currency rails, but they are subject to FINTRAC registration as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). If the EMI does not maintain a registered Canadian MSB entity, it cannot lawfully provide cross-border transfer services to Canadian residents. Additionally, EMIs do not enjoy CDIC deposit insurance, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and may impose withdrawal limits that inhibit high-volume OEM disbursements. A licensed Tier-1 Canadian bank is therefore preferred for core treasury custody.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: C$1,000,000 in aggregate reportable transactions with non-arm's length non-residents triggers Form T106 filing; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared for all specified cross-border transactions at any value. Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding C$750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) framework. Substance and commercial purpose are enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), transfer pricing substance requirements, and BEPS anti-hybrid rules. A plush toy OEM with minimal Canadian operational footprint and profits booked in offshore shell entities will face CRA GAAR challenge and reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under Income Tax Act s.239(1): imprisonment up to 2 years, fine of 50% of evaded taxes, plus civil reassessment and gross negligence penalties up to 50% of net tax and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under ITA s.227.1 for unremitted source deductions and can be jointly and severally liable for fines under s.239(2); civil restitution, travel bans, and freeze of personal assets are available enforcement measures.
Transfer Pricing / Shell Company Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing reassessment restoring arm's length income; penalty under ITA s.247(3) equals 10% of the net adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of 10% of gross revenue and C$5,000,000; denial of the deduction for sham payments and associated interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed or authorized mispricing can be assessed for penalties; fraudulent or grossly negligent participation may cause a court to pierce the corporate veil, exposing directors' personal assets.
Anti-Money Laundering / Shell Company Offence

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: on indictment, fines up to C$2,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction fines up to C$500,000; FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties up to C$100,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly direct or authorize the offence are personally liable; penalties can include imprisonment, travel restrictions, asset freezing, restitution orders and disqualification from directorship.
