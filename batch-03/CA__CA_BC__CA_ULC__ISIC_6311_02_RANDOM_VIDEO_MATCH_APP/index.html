Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) — federal authority for the Business Number (BN), GST/HST (Part IX of the Excise Tax Act), and Part I corporate income tax; online portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), CRA My Business Account, and GST/HST NETFILE (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Provincial component: British Columbia Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch (BC PST administration) and BC Registry Services (corporate registry for the ULC).

E-government filing portal

For a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (BC ULC): (1) incorporate under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act via BC Corporate Online/OneStop Business Registry to obtain the BC incorporation number; (2) register with the CRA for the nine-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts — RT (GST/HST), RC (federal corporate income tax), and RM (import/export, if applicable) — through BRO or Form RC1; GST/HST registration becomes mandatory when taxable supplies made in Canada exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters under ETA ss. 148-149, and may be elected earlier to recover input tax credits; (3) where the ULC is owned by a non-resident parent, the ULC itself is the Canadian resident registrant and no separate non-resident registration is required; if the platform were instead operated by a non-resident entity without Canadian incorporation, registration under the CRA non-resident digital-economy GST/HST regime would apply; (4) register separately with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch (Form FIN 418) for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) if the ULC makes taxable PST supplies such as telecommunication services; (5) retain the BN confirmation and GST/HST registration certificate in CRA My Business Account as proof of identity for cross-border counterparties and zero-rating substantiation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian statutory default) with Pass_Through_Eligible attributes for foreign members. Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), the BC ULC is a corporation (ITA s. 248(1)), a resident taxable Canadian corporation subject to Part I entity-level tax: federal 15% general rate (9% small-business rate) combined with British Columbia 12% general rate (9% small-business rate on active business income up to C$500,000, subject to associated-corporation rules), filing Form T2. Canada does not permit a domestic check-the-box or partnership election for the ULC; however, for U.S. federal income tax purposes the ULC is an eligible entity under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3, so a single-member ULC may be disregarded and a multi-member ULC may be taxed as a partnership (Form 8832), with Canadian income flowing through to U.S. members subject to the fiscally-transparent tie-breaker in Article IV(6) of the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention and to the Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules enacted under the 2021 federal budget. For GST/HST, the ULC is a distinct person from its shareholders and all supplies are reported by the ULC. — For a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (BC ULC): (1) incorporate under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act via BC Corporate Online/OneStop Business Registry to obtain the BC incorporation number; (2) register with the CRA for the nine-digit Business Number (BN) and program accounts — RT (GST/HST), RC (federal corporate income tax), and RM (import/export, if applicable) — through BRO or Form RC1; GST/HST registration becomes mandatory when taxable supplies made in Canada exceed C$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters under ETA ss. 148-149, and may be elected earlier to recover input tax credits; (3) where the ULC is owned by a non-resident parent, the ULC itself is the Canadian resident registrant and no separate non-resident registration is required; if the platform were instead operated by a non-resident entity without Canadian incorporation, registration under the CRA non-resident digital-economy GST/HST regime would apply; (4) register separately with the BC Consumer Taxation Branch (Form FIN 418) for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) if the ULC makes taxable PST supplies such as telecommunication services; (5) retain the BN confirmation and GST/HST registration certificate in CRA My Business Account as proof of identity for cross-border counterparties and zero-rating substantiation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian statutory default) with Pass_Through_Eligible attributes for foreign members. Under the Income Tax Act (Canada), the BC ULC is a corporation (ITA s. 248(1)), a resident taxable Canadian corporation subject to Part I entity-level tax: federal 15% general rate (9% small-business rate) combined with British Columbia 12% general rate (9% small-business rate on active business income up to C$500,000, subject to associated-corporation rules), filing Form T2. Canada does not permit a domestic check-the-box or partnership election for the ULC; however, for U.S. federal income tax purposes the ULC is an eligible entity under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3, so a single-member ULC may be disregarded and a multi-member ULC may be taxed as a partnership (Form 8832), with Canadian income flowing through to U.S. members subject to the fiscally-transparent tie-breaker in Article IV(6) of the Canada-U.S. Income Tax Convention and to the Canadian hybrid-mismatch rules enacted under the 2021 federal budget. For GST/HST, the ULC is a distinct person from its shareholders and all supplies are reported by the ULC.

Available election forms

(1) U.S. check-the-box election: filing IRS Form 8832 under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3 to classify the BC ULC as a partnership (multi-member) or disregarded entity (single-member) for U.S. federal income tax purposes, with no Canadian domestic equivalent — the ULC remains a corporation under the Income Tax Act; (2) treaty-based flow-through relief: CRA administrative recognition of U.S. members' treaty claims where the ULC is fiscally transparent under Article IV(6) of the Canada-U.S. Convention, subject to the hybrid-mismatch limitation rules; (3) GST/HST elections: early small-supplier registration, quarterly or annual filing frequency under ETA s. 238(1), the Quick Method of accounting (CRA Form GST74) for qualifying registrants with annual taxable revenues not exceeding C$400,000, and appointment of a Canadian representative under ETA s. 267.1 for non-resident registrants or refund claimants; (4) the ITA s. 216 election for non-residents deriving Canadian rental/timber income is not applicable to this operating software business; (5) no Canadian statutory mechanism exists to elect partnership treatment for a ULC, and CRA may recharacterize artificial structures designed to produce hybrid deductions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canadian dollar (CAD) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. No restrictions on cross-border capital movements, dividend/distribution remittances, or profit repatriation. Remittances are subject to Canadian tax withholding and enhanced AML screening, but there is no commercial convertibility approval requirement. The Bank of Canada does not require licences for FX dealing by corporates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios exist in Canada. All export receipts may be retained in CAD or any freely convertible currency, provided they conform to anti-money laundering / FINTRAC reporting thresholds (e.g., large cash transaction reports, cross-border currency movement reporting).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as the sole central bank; Canada maintains a fully liberalized FX regime with no separate Foreign Exchange Administration. Currency controls are exercised solely through ongoing payment system oversight and monetary policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — Strictly constrained. Domestic digital payment service providers (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) may be engaged as payment facilitators, but they must be registered as money service businesses (MSB) with FINTRAC. A BC ULC may use these to obtain virtual/FX accounts, but only for non-deposit-taking payment activity; the EMI cannot legally venture into Canadian deposit-taking functions unless chartered under the Bank Act. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Standard inbound equity/debt remittances to a BC ULC clear through SWIFT MT103 with a supplementary payment message. Wires should reference: (a) remittance purpose code 'G210' (personal/company investment) or the Bank of Canada-side trial code; (b) beneficiary BC ULC name and account number; (c) the SWIFT BIC of the correspondent. Domestic CAD transfers settle over Payments Canada Lynx (large-value transfer system). For non-CAD capital, settle in USD over US Fedwire/CHIPS, convert at spot to CAD, or hold USD in a cross-border DDA. Verification: source-of-funds disclosure, entity resolution, AML block screen, and UBO declaration for filings under the BC Business Corporations Act.Trade settlements for high-value subscriptions will flow via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (LC) where the supplier demands a documentary undertaking. The ULC's bank will require credit approval for the LC; then the bank issues the LC in favour of the vendor with a fixed USD number. For recurring small-ticket app disbursements, clearing uses automated clearing (Payments Canada ACSS), or international USD zero-balance accounts at a US clearing bank.
