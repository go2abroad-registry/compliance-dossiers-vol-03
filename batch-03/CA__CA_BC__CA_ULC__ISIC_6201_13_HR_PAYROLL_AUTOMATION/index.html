No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the BCBCA. The ULC may be incorporated with zero paid-up capital; shares may be issued without par value at a nominal subscription price set by board resolution. No escrow account or capital deposit is mandated for a private ULC in British Columbia.

Funding & unlock path: Applicable local fire marshal and building official: obtain a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate and an Occupancy Permit for the leased office premises confirming compliance with the BC Building Code. WorkSafeBC clearance: register the firm and obtain a clearance letter confirming that a workplace health and safety program is in place under the Workers Compensation Act, including ergonomic workstation assessments, electrical safety, and emergency evacuation planning. For a purely digital operation, no structural load certification is triggered; however, a structural load review shall be completed if any heavy server or battery room equipment is installed.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada, administered through the federal Business Registration Online portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-taxes/register-your-business.html) for the Business Number (BN), GST/HST account, corporate income tax account, and payroll source-deduction account; BC Provincial Sales Tax is administered separately by the British Columbia Ministry of Finance through eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the ULC under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act (SBC 2002, c.57) using BC Corporate Online / BC Registry; no minimum capital is required and non-resident ownership is permitted. Step 2 - Obtain the federal 9-digit Business Number (BN) through CRA Business Registration Online using the BC incorporation number; a ULC controlled by non-residents may file Form RC1A (Request for a Business Number and Account Registration for Non-Resident Corporations). Step 3 - Open the relevant CRA program accounts: RC001 (Part I corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST registration, mandatory when the small-supplier threshold of CAD 30,000 in taxable supplies made in Canada over four consecutive calendar quarters is exceeded, or voluntary to claim input tax credits), and RP (payroll source deductions to the extent the ULC employs Canadian staff). Step 4 - Register separately with eTaxBC for BC PST only if the ULC makes PST-taxable retail supplies in BC; remotely delivered payroll-SaaS platform services are generally outside the BC PST base.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - note that for Canadian tax purposes the BC ULC is a separate resident corporation taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act; a non-resident-controlled ULC is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation, so it is subject to the 15% federal general corporate rate plus the 12% BC general corporate rate (27% combined) and is ineligible for the 9% federal and 2% BC small business deduction rates; the pass-through character is a legacy US tax classification attribute that is now constrained by US per-se corporate classification rules for BC/AB ULCs.

Available election forms

Canada does not operate a domestic check-the-box classification election; the ULC is a statutory corporation taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act. For US federal tax purposes, Treas. Reg. section 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i), as amended by T.D. 9761 effective for taxable years beginning after December 31, 2016, classifies unlimited liability companies formed under the laws of British Columbia or Alberta as per se corporations, so a Form 8832 election to classify the ULC as a partnership or disregarded entity is unavailable for newly formed or newly classified entities. Any residual hybrid-mismatch relief would be evaluated under the Canada-US Treaty (Article IV(6)-(7) transparent-entity and residency tie-breaker rules) and requires a limitation-on-benefits and functional analysis of the payroll-automation services; no standalone hybrid-mismatch election exists in Canadian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - under Excise Tax Act section 240, a person who is not a small supplier must register for GST/HST; a small supplier is a person whose value of taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) made in Canada in the last four consecutive calendar quarters does not exceed CAD 30,000. BC PST: a vendor must register with eTaxBC before making taxable retail sales in BC; there is no small-supplier exemption threshold for PST, but remote SaaS/ERP payroll software is generally not a PST-taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating applies under the Excise Tax Act, Schedule VI, Part V: section 7 (services) and section 10 (intangible personal property). A BC ULC supplying payroll-SaaS and HR-automation software to non-resident clients will qualify for 0% GST where the recipient is a non-resident person, the service is not a personal service rendered to an individual present in Canada, and the supply is not in connection with real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed. Cross-border payroll data processing and subscription-based software access supplied from Canadian data centres to foreign employers ordinarily meets these criteria and permits the ULC to claim full input tax credits and recover GST paid on Canadian datacenter, software development, and payroll service inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A export reporting / Canada Border Services Agency electronic export declaration) - required only for physical goods leaving Canada; not applicable to pure cross-border software or service exports for which no CBSA filing is made
  • Bill of Lading (or airway waybill / electronic service delivery record) - the functional substitute for services and intangibles is the executed master subscription agreement, statement of work, invoice, and server/session logs demonstrating delivery from Canadian infrastructure to the non-resident recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - CRA does not issue or mandate a formal inward-remittance certificate; the equivalent evidence retained in the export VAT file is the bank SWIFT MT103, wire, or direct-debit settlement record from the foreign client, evidencing the cross-border consideration and the identity of the non-resident payor

Canada does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime for GST/HST. The invoicing obligation under Excise Tax Act section 223 requires the supplier to set out its Business Number, GST registration status, and the total GST/HST charged where the total consideration of a taxable supply exceeds CAD 30. Electronic invoicing in EDI, PDF, or API-passed formats is accepted by the CRA; there is no government API clearance or QR fiscal stamp. BC PST returns and payments are filed through eTaxBC on a monthly or periodic basis depending on declared volume, with prescribed invoice content where PST is chargeable on taxable retail supplies.

CRA may reassess a zero-rating claim within four years of the original assessment; the period extends to ten years where a misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default. A fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claim triggers a penalty under Excise Tax Act section 285 of up to 25% of the tax or refund at issue, plus interest, and retrospective clawback of input tax credits and GST refunds where the non-resident nexus or export documentation is absent; criminal tax-evasion prosecution under Excise Tax Act section 327 may attract fines of 50%-200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment. Canada derives no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure in this dossier is the administrative/contractual benchmark used to evidence timely receipt of exported SaaS consideration and to support the contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation position (Form T106 and underlying records filed with the T2 corporate return and retained for six years).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada generally has no exchange controls, mandatory conversion of foreign receipts, or general restrictions on repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees. Transfers remain subject to corporate-law solvency requirements, applicable tax withholding, transfer-pricing support, sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, beneficial-ownership verification, bank transaction monitoring, and documentation of the commercial purpose. A British Columbia ULC may remit profits or capital to non-residents if its governing documents, directors' duties, solvency requirements, tax obligations, shareholder arrangements, and lender covenants are satisfied. Treaty-reduced withholding rates require applicable residence, beneficial-ownership, and limitation-on-benefits support. A ULC's Canadian tax classification and any foreign fiscally transparent treatment should be confirmed before implementing cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or general deadline for converting export receipts into Canadian dollars. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion. The company should retain contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery evidence, remittance records, and reconciliation support. Bank reporting, sanctions screening, suspicious-transaction reporting, large-cash reporting, electronic-funds-transfer reporting, tax reporting, and Statistics Canada or Canada Border Services Agency requirements may apply depending on the transaction. Foreign-currency balances should be managed under documented treasury, counterparty, liquidity, and hedge-accounting policies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; foreign-exchange, payment, and anti-money-laundering oversight also involves the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC), the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and foreign-exchange execution; low as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account. Canada does not provide a general European-style electronic-money institution passport. A foreign digital EMI may provide services only within the scope of its home-country authorization and Canadian legal exemptions. Canadian payment functions may trigger the Retail Payment Activities Act, FINTRAC money-services-business obligations, provincial consumer and securities rules, or bank-partner requirements. The company should verify whether the provider is registered with FINTRAC where required, registered with the Bank of Canada as a payment service provider where required, able to support Canadian business customers and payroll-related flows, and permitted to safeguard client funds. Customer funds may not have Canadian deposit insurance, and a foreign EMI account may not be accepted for Canadian payroll remittances, tax payments, capital subscriptions, or proof of operating presence. Use regulated banks for core cash, tax, payroll, and reserve accounts, with fintech accounts limited to documented collection, payout, or hedging functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder equity, paid-in capital, or intercompany debt through an account held in the Canadian ULC's legal name at a regulated Canadian financial institution. Use a payment narrative that identifies the legal basis, such as share subscription, capital contribution, or intercompany loan, and include the corporate name, agreement date, and transaction reference. Pre-clear the wire with the bank where the sender, beneficial owner, currency, or corridor is foreign or high risk. Maintain the subscription agreement or loan agreement, board approval, capitalization or debt ledger, beneficial-owner evidence, source-of-funds records, and tax analysis. Reconcile the incoming amount to the general ledger and bank statement. Do not route capital through a customer-collection account or an unrelated group company. A Canadian corporation generally does not require a central-bank approval or foreign-exchange permit for an ordinary capital injection, but sanctions, AML, tax, corporate-law, and beneficial-ownership rules remain applicable.Use domestic EFT, Interac or bank payment rails for Canadian operating payments, and bank wires or SWIFT for international supplier, affiliate, and service payments. SWIFT MT103 or its ISO 20022 successor may be used for a single customer credit transfer; obtain complete originator, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, invoice, and purpose data. Commercial letters of credit may be used for higher-risk goods or counterparties, although they are generally disproportionate for ordinary SaaS services. For recurring software, cloud, payroll, and professional-service invoices, use documented wires, ACH-style credit transfers, or approved direct-debit mandates with dual authorization, beneficiary verification, sanctions screening, and invoice-to-contract matching. For intercompany payments, maintain transfer-pricing support, withholding-tax analysis, treaty documentation, and evidence of services or financing. Canadian banks may request customs, invoice, contract, tax, or source-of-funds evidence before release.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with a regulated bank, supported by documented forecast exposures, counterparty limits, maturity limits, confirmation controls, and board-approved treasury authority.Non-deliverable forwards where the relevant currency or local-market rules make physical delivery impracticable, subject to bank eligibility, jurisdictional legality, net-settlement documentation, and proof of an underlying commercial exposure.Currency swaps or cross-currency swaps for documented financing or recurring multi-currency exposure, subject to ISDA or equivalent documentation, collateral terms, valuation controls, and counterparty-credit limits.Foreign-exchange options or collars for material payroll, subscription, or supplier exposures, subject to suitability, premium approval, accounting treatment, and prohibition on speculative trading.Spot foreign-exchange conversions through regulated banks or authorized dealers for operational settlements and liquidity management.Do not use leveraged retail products, unregulated offshore dealers, speculative derivatives, or third-party netting arrangements without a specific legal, tax, accounting, and counterparty review.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment collection and foreign-exchange execution; low as a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account. Canada does not provide a general European-style electronic-money institution passport. A foreign digital EMI may provide services only within the scope of its home-country authorization and Canadian legal exemptions. Canadian payment functions may trigger the Retail Payment Activities Act, FINTRAC money-services-business obligations, provincial consumer and securities rules, or bank-partner requirements. The company should verify whether the provider is registered with FINTRAC where required, registered with the Bank of Canada as a payment service provider where required, able to support Canadian business customers and payroll-related flows, and permitted to safeguard client funds. Customer funds may not have Canadian deposit insurance, and a foreign EMI account may not be accepted for Canadian payroll remittances, tax payments, capital subscriptions, or proof of operating presence. Use regulated banks for core cash, tax, payroll, and reserve accounts, with fintech accounts limited to documented collection, payout, or hedging functions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation required if the total value of cross-border transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million for services, CAD 5 million for goods, or CAD 1 million for intangibles; additionally, Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) must be filed if the total value exceeds CAD 1 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the concept of 'carrying on business' require real economic activity. A ULC must have substantive operations, employees, and decision-making in Canada to avoid recharacterization as a shell or sham; otherwise, the CRA may reassess and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of the tax evaded (minimum 50%) and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act (s. 238); additional civil penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for corporate tax debts if they fail to remit payroll deductions (source deductions) or GST/HST (s. 227.1); also, if they participate in or consent to evasion, they may face criminal charges and personal fines.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to deny treaty benefits, apply GAAR to recharacterize transactions, impose penalties up to 50% of the tax benefit, and potentially prosecute for tax evasion if intent is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in the sham or failed to ensure the corporation complied with tax laws; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under the Income Tax Act.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations (first offence) and CAD 4 million for subsequent offences; criminal penalties for individuals up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly or negligently fail to comply; they may also face criminal charges and travel bans if the non-compliance is linked to money laundering or terrorist financing.
