Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA-ARC); registration and filing portals: CRA Business Registration Online (BRO) and My Business Account at cra-arc.gc.ca. The British Columbia Ministry of Finance administers the separate Provincial Sales Tax (PST) through the eTaxBC portal.

E-government filing portal

The foreign ULC must obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN), which serves as the income tax account number and the GST/HST program account (RT suffix). Procedure: complete CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) online via BRO, by telephone, fax, or mail; attach certified proof of incorporation from the BC Registrar of Companies, foreign jurisdiction registration particulars, registered office address, director identities, and Canadian representative or agent details if appointed. The BN/RT becomes effective on the date the non-resident first carries on commercial activity in Canada or on an earlier elected date for voluntary GST/HST registration to claim input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under Canadian federal and BC provincial income tax, a BC ULC is a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate tax on a T2 return (federal Part I rate of 15% net of provincial abatement plus BC general corporate rate of 12%, approximately 27% combined). For US shareholders, the ULC is eligible for fiscally transparent treatment by check-the-box election, and under Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Tax Treaty the ULC may be treated as a hybrid entity.

Available election forms

Check-the-box election under US Treasury Regulation Section 301.7701-3 (Form 8832/8858) to classify the BC ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal income tax; election under Article IV(7) of the Canada-US Tax Treaty to apply treaty benefits on a fiscally transparent basis; Income Tax Act Sections 18.4 and 18.5 together with Section 245 (GAAR) govern hybrid mismatch arrangements and prevent duplicate deductions; no domestic Canadian flow-through election exists for a ULC, so the T2 corporate return is mandatory; the Section 216 election remains available for non-residents deriving Canadian real-property rental income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 - worldwide taxable supplies of the non-resident and associated persons in the immediately preceding four consecutive calendar quarters (Excise Tax Act small-supplier threshold, Section 123(1) and Section 240). Registration is mandatory when the ULC carries on a commercial activity in Canada above this threshold; voluntary registration is permitted to recover input tax credits. BC PST registration via eTaxBC is required from the first taxable retail sale in British Columbia, with no small-supplier exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for exported data-labeling and RLHF data-curation services under Schedule VI, Part V, Section 9 of the Excise Tax Act: the service must be supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not registered for GST/HST, must be performed in Canada, and must not relate to real property situated in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of performance. Computer-vision bounding-box annotation and AI training data services transferred to a foreign principal therefore qualify for the 0% export rate. Tangible goods (e.g., shipped hard drives containing annotated datasets) qualify for zero-rating under Excise Tax Act Section 19 when title passes and the goods are exported by or on behalf of the supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13A filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System) with carrier or freight-forwarder confirmation for any tangible goods shipped abroad
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or freight-forwarder certificate evidencing physical export and foreign destination; for pure services, the executed service agreement, work-order, and dataset delivery records serve as the primary export evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration from the non-resident recipient

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory clearance API for GST/HST invoices; electronic invoicing is permitted and the pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework (EBMS/Peppol) is voluntary. GST/HST returns must be filed electronically through CRA My Business Account once taxable supplies exceed prescribed thresholds; non-resident registrants must remit net tax by electronic payment. CRA operates a public GST/HST Registry and validation API for verifying Business Number and RT program account validity.

CRA may reassess beyond the standard 4-year limitation period under Excise Tax Act Section 298 where fraud or misrepresentation is established. Failure to obtain and retain acceptable proof of export within the Section 19 two-year documentary window triggers reassessment at 5% GST on the full consideration, disallowance of input tax credits, arrears interest, and a penalty under Section 285(1) equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 50% of any knowingly false net tax for fraudulent export zero-rating or false substantiation; gross negligence penalties, director liability, and criminal prosecution may also apply for wilful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction. The Canadian dollar is freely convertible, with no current-account or capital-account exchange restrictions. Profit remittances by the BC ULC to non-resident shareholders are legal and unrestricted, and are subject only to standard withholding tax compliance under the Income Tax Act (typically Part XIII on dividends, reduced by applicable tax treaty) plus normal banking remittance documentation. No central bank approval is required for inbound equity injection, treasury concentration, or repatriation of liquidation surplus.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for export receipts. Service-export proceeds from AI data labeling and BPO activities may be retained in CAD or any freely convertible currency in a Canadian-chartered bank account. For tax purposes, revenue must be reported in CAD using the Bank of Canada annual average exchange rate or an acceptable equivalent, and the bank will apply FINTRAC large electronic-funds-transfer reporting on cross-border transfers of CAD 10,000 or more.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with the Department of Finance Canada setting exchange-rate and monetary policy framework; no separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control vetting authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a pay-out-only rail for mass gig-worker and contractor disbursements; Low as a primary treasury clearing bank. Wise, PayPal Business, and comparable non-bank EMIs are lawful in Canada when registered as money services businesses with FINTRAC and licensed or registered as MSBs in British Columbia. They may not accept deposits, may not provide CDIC-insured deposit accounts, and should not hold long-term corporate liquidity. Offshore digital EMIs without Canadian registration cannot provide services into Canada as a principal; they may be used only on a restricted receive-only basis if the ULC itself transmits funds to the EMI's licensed foreign entity and the arrangement is fully documented and AML-compliant.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.0% combined Federal (15%) and British Columbia general corporate income tax (12%). A foreign-controlled ULC is not eligible for the Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) small business deduction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by a Canadian-resident ULC to non-resident shareholders when no treaty relief applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

T2 return must be filed no later than six months after the fiscal year-end. Tax balance is due within two months after fiscal year-end (three months for eligible CCPCs, not relevant for a foreign-controlled ULC). Instalments are due on or before the last day of each quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canada does not mandate OECD-style Local File or Master File filing in ordinary circumstances. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and retained in respect of every non-arm's-length cross-border transaction, and CRA may demand it within a reasonable time after a request. Form T106 filing becomes mandatory where the aggregate value of reportable non-arm's-length transactions with a particular non-resident exceeds CAD 1,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting applies only for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of CAD 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the UAE. However, CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), treaty Limitation-on-Benefits provisions, and OECD BEPS principles to disregard transactions and entities lacking commercial actuality. A BC ULC engaging in AI data-labelling must maintain real presence in Canada: employees, office, decision-making, retained risk, invoicing, contracts, payroll, and accounting substance. Without such substance, CRA may recharacterize arrangements, deny treaty benefits, and impose gross-negligence penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act s.239(1), a conviction for tax evasion can lead to a fine up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment; summary conviction carries up to CAD 100,000 and 2 years. Civil gross-negligence penalties under s.163(2) equal 50% of the understated tax plus interest and arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in false reporting or control accounts can be prosecuted personally. CRA can pierce the corporate veil, issue director-assessment under ITA s.227.1 for source deductions, invoke s.160 transferee liability, and obtain travel bans, restraint orders, and extradition in serious tax fraud cases.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: ITA s.247(2) authorizes CRA to recharacterize or adjust cross-border amounts to reflect arm's-length terms. Section 247(3) imposes a 10% penalty on the net transfer-pricing adjustment where the adjustment exceeds the lesser of CAD 5,000,000 or 10% of gross revenue. A grossly negligent transfer-pricing statement can trigger s.163(2) penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable when transfer-pricing misstatements are combined with failure to remit withholdings or fraudulent conveyance of ULC assets. Directors who approve false invoicing may face criminal fraud charges and civil liability for unpaid corporate tax to the extent the corporate veil is pierced.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may apply GAAR to negate the tax benefit, recompute income, add interest, and issue a 50% gross negligible penalty if the taxpayer recklessly disregarded the law. Treaty withholding rate reductions can be denied, resulting in 25% Part XIII tax plus penalties.

Director personal liability: A director of a hollow ULC may be made personally liable through s.160 where corporate funds are stripped before CRA assessment. Criminal liability under fraud and false-return provisions is possible when directors intentionally use a shell to defeat Revenue Canada.
Part XIII Withholding and Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: ITA s.215(2) and s.227(2) impose penalties of 10% of the unremitted amount plus prescribed daily compound interest. Unremitted Part XIII amounts are held in statutory trust in favour of CRA and may rank ahead of secured lenders.

Director personal liability: Directors have personal statutory liability for unremitted non-resident withholding taxes under s.227.1 after a corporate failure to collect. Directors found to have directed cross-border payments while aware of the withholding obligation can be personally assessed and subjected to garnishment, property liens, and travel restrictions.
AML, Sanctions, and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act sanctions for regulated entities; Criminal Code s.462.31 money-laundering offense is punishable by up to 10 years imprisonment for individuals, with unlimited fines on corporations. FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties can apply for reporting failures.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize, direct, or knowingly facilitate money laundering face personal criminal prosecution and seizure of bank accounts. Corporate veil piercing is common where the ULC is used as a conduit; directors may be subject to asset freezes, travel bans,, extradition requests, and civil shareholder or bankruptcy-restructuring claims.
