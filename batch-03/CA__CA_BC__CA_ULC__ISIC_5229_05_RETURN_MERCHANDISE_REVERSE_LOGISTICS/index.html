Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – GST/HST and Business Number (BN) registration administered through CRA My Business Account portal (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/gst-hst-gst-netfile.html). For British Columbia provincial sales tax, the competent authority is the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Programs Branch, administered via eTaxBC (https://etax.gov.bc.ca).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned BC Unlimited Liability Company (ULC) must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) by filing Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) through the CRA 'Register a business number' online service or by mailing the form; a non-resident ULC applicant will generally use Form RC1WC. A GST/HST account must be activated under the same BN once either the ULC has a permanent establishment in Canada, carries on business in Canada, or ceases to be a small supplier (worldwide taxable supplies exceed C$30,000 in a calendar quarter or in the four consecutive calendar quarters ending with the current quarter). Supporting documents include the BC incorporation/continuance certificate, registered office address, director and officer details, authorized representative appointment if the ULC is operated from outside Canada, and evidence of foreign ownership. Separate BC PST registration is required through eTaxBC before commencing taxable retail sales or services in British Columbia; no minimum capital is required for a ULC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Canadian income tax treats the BC ULC as a resident corporation taxable under Part I of the Income Tax Act on worldwide income, and as taxable under the BC Income Tax Act; pass-through eligibility is available only for U.S. federal tax purposes where the ULC is eligible for check-the-box classification as a disregarded entity or partnership. Territorial_Scope is not applicable; Tax_Exempt_Zone is not applicable.)

Available election forms

No Canadian check-the-box election exists: the ULC is a corporation for Canadian tax purposes. For U.S. tax, the ULC may make a federal check-the-box election on IRS Form 8832 to be disregarded as an entity or to be treated as a partnership, preserving pass-through treatment for U.S. members. Under the Excise Tax Act, the ULC may use ordinary GST/HST elections applicable to closely related registrants, including the section 156 election for deemed zero-rated supplies between registrants that are at least 90% related, and may file voluntary disclosure/amended elections with CRA consent where required. Canadian hybrid mismatch rules in sections 18.4 through 18.6 of the Income Tax Act must be reviewed where the ULC issues hybrid debt or where payments would be deductible in Canada and not included in the U.S. member's income due to check-the-box characterisation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the registrant supplies goods to a non-resident purchaser and exports the goods in accordance with the exporter requirements, generally as soon as reasonably possible after the sale or after delivery to a common carrier for shipment outside Canada. For reverse logistics and returned-goods quality grading, zero-rating of the related services is available only where the supply qualifies under the export-service provisions: the service must be supplied to a non-resident person and must not be in respect of real property situated in Canada; services physically performed in Canada on tangible personal property located in Canada are generally not zero-rated merely because the recipient is non-resident unless the service is supplied in relation to goods that are being exported or falls under a specific zero-rated export service category. A BC ULC providing cross-border return-merchandise processing should document that the returned goods remain owned by the non-resident principal, are imported under the appropriate temporary-import or return-goods customs regimen, and are re-exported or destroyed under customs supervision at the instruction of the foreign principal. Adequate evidence of the foreign supply agreement, commercial invoice, HS classification, export destination, foreign-origin documentation, and payment from non-resident counterparties is essential to sustain a zero-rated treatment and to support claims for input tax credits and refunds of GST/HST paid on Canadian operating expenses.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBSA B13 form or approved electronic export reporting via the Canadian Export Reporting System)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / other contract of carriage showing consignee and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing foreign-currency or cross-border settlement from the non-resident principal
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying returned merchandise, HS tariff classification, origin, value, and processing details
  • Non-resident service contract demonstrating the reverse-logistics service is supplied to and paid by a non-resident principal
  • Customs import records (interim/customs entry documents, Form A8A/B3) and any temporary-import permits or returned-goods exemptions
  • Grading/inspection reports and disposition certificates for returned goods (re-export, destruction, or donation)

Canada has no mandatory real-time electronic invoicing clearance, no national digital stamping regime, and no transactional API fiscal clearance. GST/HST registrants must file periodic GST/HST returns electronically through CRA My Business Account or GST/HST NETFILE, retain all prescribed invoice records, and issue invoices containing the supplier's Business Number, date, consideration, vendor and recipient identification, and tax charged. E-invoicing is optional and may be implemented using Peppol-compliant standards for B2B efficiency. BC PST registrants file separate PST returns through eTaxBC; PST invoices must show the PST amount separately or clearly indicate that PST is included, but there is no real-time clearance.

CRA may reassess GST/HST returns within the normal four-year reassessment period; a false or unsupported export zero-rating is subject to reversal of the zero-rating, reassessment of GST at 5% plus provincial PST exposure, interest, and administrative penalties of up to 25% of the net tax understatement. Knowingly false statements, fraudulent export declarations, or omission of material facts can trigger gross-negligence penalties under section 285 of the Excise Tax Act and customs penalties under the Customs Act, including monetary penalties and seizure/forfeiture of goods. CBSA can apply administrative monetary penalties for inaccurate export declarations, and the unlimited liability of BC ULC shareholders means the corporation's income tax, GST/HST, PST, interest, and penalties may be recoverable directly against the members' personal assets to the extent provided under the BC Business Corporations Act and the applicable income tax collection statutes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction; the Canadian dollar is fully and freely convertible. There are no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, trade financing, profit remittances, dividends, shareholder loans, or liquidation proceeds. A BC ULC can receive foreign currency and conduct cross-border settlements in USD and other currencies without administrative prior approval. Profit remittance is restrained only by Canadian withholding tax under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (25% statutory, reduced to 5% on dividends under the Canada-US tax treaty where the US beneficial owner owns at least 10% of voting shares), and by transfer-pricing rules requiring arm's length treatment of management fees, royalties, and intercompany charges. The ULC's unlimited liability structure may affect treaty eligibility for US shareholders but does not restrict FX conversion or capital movement within Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion timeline, or conversion ratio applies in Canada. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in a USD or other foreign currency account, kept as bank balances, or converted at the open interbank market rate. The only binding obligations are (a) reporting any physical cross-border movement of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more to FINTRAC within applicable timeframes, (b) filing accurate export documentation with the Canada Border Services Agency and Statistics Canada, and (c) computing taxable income in Canadian dollars using Bank of Canada published exchange rates when converting financial results for CRA filings.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC / Banque du Canada), with the Department of Finance Canada maintaining overarching foreign exchange policy, OSFI regulating federally chartered banks, and FINTRAC administering AML, KYC, and sanctions screening obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Airwallex, Wise, and Payoneer are viable as non-depository payment conduits for cross-border refunds, vendor disbursements, and receivables collection, but they are not authorized as Canadian banks under the Bank Act (Schedule III or otherwise) and cannot hold Canadian deposits or provide CDIC-insured accounts. To operate in/from Canada, an offshore EMI must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian compliance agent if required, and avoid any activity that would be construed as deposit-taking. The primary operating and treasury relationship should remain at a Canadian Schedule I bank; EMI accounts may be used as a parallel payment rail for the target ACH and Airwallex deployment, with funds held offshore subject to sanctions and source-of-funds due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the BC ULC should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent/shareholder to the Canadian corporate bank account, using the beneficiary bank's institution number, transit number, and account number. The bank will require: an executed board resolution approving the capital contribution; a share subscription agreement or a formal intercompany loan agreement with terms and interest rate; and a source-of-funds declaration / proof of wealth from the contributing entity. Although Canada imposes no foreign-exchange purpose code, the instruction should clearly state 'Capital contribution / Equity injection' or 'Loan to subsidiary' in field 70. The bank will run sanctions and PEP screening on the sender and intermediate banks; if the amount exceeds the Investment Canada Act financial threshold for a new Canadian business, a post-closing notification may be required, and any acquisition of control of an existing Canadian business may require net-benefit review. The ULC must maintain a BC corporate record verifying the share issuance or loan entry, and the resulting share capital or shareholder debt must be recorded in the statutory accounts.For trade settlements and disbursements, the ULC will use: (a) Payments Canada retail ACH (EFT standard 005) for domestic CAD refunds and invoice payments; (b) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wire outflows, including import payments, freight forwarding fees, and international supplier settlements; and (c) documentary commercial letters of credit (UCP600) for high-value or shipment-backed purchases, issued by a Canadian Schedule I bank and confirmed by the beneficiary's bank where required. For high-frequency cross-border refunds associated with returned merchandise, a fintech EMI such as Airwallex can be used to push local-currency refunds to consumers and to fund overseas distribution agents, provided each payment is matched to an RMA transaction and recorded in a dual-entry ledger. Real-time gross settlement above the Lynx threshold should be executed via the Bank of Canada's high-value payment system; smaller payments can be netted through the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS). All outbound instructions must include full beneficiary details, precise purpose codes in the MT103 (e.g., 'COMM' for trade, 'TRF' for transfer), and counterparty sanctions screening.FX forward outrights (physically settled USD/CAD) executed under an ISDA 1992/2002 Master Agreement with a licensed Canadian Schedule I bank, subject to board-approved treasury policy and credit-limit support.FX swaps (USD/CAD or USD/foreign currency) allowing simultaneous spot and forward legs for liquidity conversion and short-dated balance-sheet hedging.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for hedging non-resident shareholder loans or operational receivables generated in a foreign currency, with mark-to-market collateral under a CSA.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies of jurisdictions with transfer restrictions; these are permissible for a Canadian corporate end-user as an overlay hedge, but the hedged exposure must be documented and the bank must offer the contract offshore or through a licensed derivatives desk.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Airwallex, Wise, and Payoneer are viable as non-depository payment conduits for cross-border refunds, vendor disbursements, and receivables collection, but they are not authorized as Canadian banks under the Bank Act (Schedule III or otherwise) and cannot hold Canadian deposits or provide CDIC-insured accounts. To operate in/from Canada, an offshore EMI must register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian compliance agent if required, and avoid any activity that would be construed as deposit-taking. The primary operating and treasury relationship should remain at a Canadian Schedule I bank; EMI accounts may be used as a parallel payment rail for the target ACH and Airwallex deployment, with funds held offshore subject to sanctions and source-of-funds due diligence.
