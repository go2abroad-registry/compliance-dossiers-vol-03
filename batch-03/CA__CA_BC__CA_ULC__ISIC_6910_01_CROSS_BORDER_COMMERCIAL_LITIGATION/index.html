Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada — national tax authority; primary digital portal: Business Registration Online and My Business Account. British Columbia Ministry of Finance — provincial PST and Employer Health Tax; BC Registrar of Companies for corporate and extra-provincial registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned BC ULC must obtain a CRA 9-digit Business Number (BN) before carrying on commercial activity in Canada; the BN is the Canadian corporate tax identifier. Registration is completed online via Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC460 'Application for Registration of a Non-Resident Corporation'. Register for the RP program account (corporation income tax), the RT account (GST/HST), and the RC account (payroll withholdings) if employees are engaged. The ULC must first be incorporated or extra-provincially registered in BC, provide its certificate of formation and English-language corporate documents, appoint a representative for service of process, and, if operating litigation chambers, open the Law Society of British Columbia designated trust account for client funds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the BC ULC is fiscally transparent for U.S. federal tax purposes only where a check-the-box election is made on IRS Form 8832; absent that election, CRA taxes the ULC as a corporation at the entity level, and Canadian law does not otherwise provide a unilateral flow-through classification.

Available election forms

Available elections include the U.S. check-the-box election (IRS Form 8832) to treat the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership of its U.S. shareholders; CRA does not operate a domestic check-the-box regime, so non-U.S. shareholders remain entity-level taxed in Canada. For cross-border financing, Canada's anti-hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.2 et seq.) apply adjustments for ULC instruments recognized as hybrid arrangements; ULC-specific capital-dividend account and paid-up-capital elections are available in the Canadian corporate tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — CRA small-supplier threshold measured by worldwide taxable commercial revenue over any four consecutive calendar quarters; GST/HST registration becomes mandatory upon exceeding CAD 30,000. BC PST has no equivalent small-supplier exemption, so a PST account is required for taxable services supplied to persons who consume the service in BC; exported legal services supplied to non-residents fall outside BC PST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border commercial litigation and arbitration services supplied by the ULC are eligible for zero-rating under the exported-supply provisions of ETA Schedule VI, Part V where the recipient is a non-resident person: (i) not registered under the GST/HST regime; (ii) not acquiring the service for consumption, use, or supply in Canada; and (iii) receiving services that do not relate to Canadian real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The engagement letter must state that the services are for use wholly outside Canada, and the ULC must retain contemporaneous evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, including foreign tax identification number, certificate of residence, registered address, and bank remittance advice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A) — required only if physical exhibits, case files, or tangible evidence are exported across a border; not determinative of services zero-rating.
  • Bill of Lading or courier air waybill — retained in the rare circumstances where physical documents are shipped; for advisory and court-based services the engagement letter and detailed invoices are the primary export evidence.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/wire advice — issued by the receiving BC bank and retained to prove the foreign-currency consideration was remitted from the non-resident client into the ULC operating account or Law Society of BC trust account.

Canada and British Columbia do not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital duty-stamp mandate. GST/HST invoices must contain the supplier's BN, invoice date, supplier and recipient names, and consideration; Peppol-based e-invoicing is voluntary. Quebec's mandatory B2B e-invoicing framework does not apply to BC registrants. For litigation chambers, Law Society of BC trust ledger and IOLTA-equivalent accounting controls apply as a separate non-tax regulatory mandate.

CRA may disallow zero-rating and reassess uncollected GST/HST where the recipient or the genuine benefit of the service is determined to be in Canada, or where the non-resident substantiation file is incomplete. The normal four-year reassessment period becomes indefinite for misrepresentation, gross negligence, or fraud. Penalties include: ETA s. 285 — a penalty equal to 25% of the evaded tax (minimum CAD 250); ETA s. 280 — conviction for tax evasion carries a fine of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment of up to 5 years; daily compound interest accrues at the CRA prescribed rate from the original remittance due date. Parallel disciplinary consequences under Law Society of BC trust accounting rules apply where billing or trust records are falsified.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member: full current-account and capital-account convertibility, with no exchange controls, no profit-remittance licensing, and no restrictions on dividends, interest, royalty, or capital repatriation to non-resident shareholders. The only hurdles to outward remittance are Part XIII withholding tax and income-treaty procedural requirements, plus FINTRAC/PCMLTFA reporting at C$10,000 thresholds. For a BC ULC, share redemptions may be characterized as return of capital or deemed dividends for tax, but no FX approval is triggered; CAD/USD conversion by a Schedule I bank is unlimited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Canada imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency in Canadian or offshore accounts. Only physical cross-border currency movements of C$10,000 or more must be reported under the PCMLTFA/CBSA regime; electronic receipts are not subject to conversion or surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); foreign exchange and monetary policy administered jointly by the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for AML/CFT oversight. Canada maintains no separate exchange-control authority or foreign-exchange administration license.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Canada has no dedicated electronic-money institution licence; non-resident fintech providers operate as Money Services Businesses registered with FINTRAC, and under the Retail Payment Activities Act (Bank of Canada registration) for consumer payment services. Offshore EMIs cannot lawfully hold IOLTA client trust funds in Canada, because the Law Society of British Columbia mandates that trust money be held in a BC-located bank or credit union approved by the Law Society. EMIs remain suitable only for minor foreign-currency operating floats and outbound disbursement facilitation, subject to the firm's own FINTRAC obligations.

27% (15% federal + 12% British Columbia provincial)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if aggregate value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1 million for services, CAD 5 million for goods, or CAD 1 million for other transactions. Master File required if consolidated revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and cross-border transactions exceed CAD 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, but applies General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles. For ULCs, unlimited liability may be considered a factor in determining substance; however, CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of tax evaded (federal and provincial), plus interest; imprisonment up to 5 years (Income Tax Act s. 239).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for corporate tax debts if they fail to withhold/remit employee source deductions or GST/HST (s. 227.1). For evasion, directors can be jointly and severally liable for penalties if they participated or acquiesced.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of tax, interest, and penalties up to 50% of additional tax (gross negligence penalty under s. 163(2)). GAAR may deny tax benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly allowed the shell to operate without proper tax compliance; courts may pierce the corporate veil in cases of fraud or sham.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for money laundering up to CAD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years (Criminal Code s. 462.31).

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly or negligently fail to implement compliance programs; may face travel bans and asset freezes under FINTRAC enforcement.
