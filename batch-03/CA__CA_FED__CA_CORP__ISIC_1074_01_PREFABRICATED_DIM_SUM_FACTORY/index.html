Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CAD is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions; Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and no exchange controls or residual restrictions from the former Foreign Exchange Control Act (repealed 1951) remain. Profit remittances (dividends, interest, royalties, management fees) face no exchange-control hurdles, but are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate, treaty-reduced (e.g., 5%–15% on dividends under the Canada–US tax convention). Cross-border payments exceeding CAD 10,000 are reportable to FINTRAC, and related-party transactions with non-residents must be reported on Form T106.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio applies in Canada. Exporters of frozen dim sum and dumplings may retain foreign-currency revenues in any bank account in Canada, invoice in CAD or foreign currency, and choose the timing of conversion without government approval. FINTRAC Large Cash Transaction Reporting and cross-border EFT reporting apply at the CAD 10,000 threshold. For income tax purposes, export revenues are converted to CAD using Bank of Canada noonday exchange rates for the booking date, unless a functional-currency election under s. 261 of the Income Tax Act is made.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration; monetary and currency policy is executed by the Bank of Canada as fiscal agent for the Crown, while OSFI supervises federally chartered banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46) and FINTRAC enforces cross-border transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — viable as an ancillary operational rail, not for statutory capital escrow. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) entering Canada must register with FINTRAC as Money Services Businesses or Foreign Money Services Businesses; unregistered EMIs cannot lawfully receive or disburse funds for a Canadian corporate. Legal constraints include pool-account restrictions, inability to hold escrow under bank-grade trust covenants, and domestic bank refusal to credit wires whose origin is an unregulated pooled EMI. Recommended use is limited to low-value vendor payouts and payroll in sub-CAD 10,000 bands, while the primary operating and escrow accounts remain at a Schedule I bank.

Defense evidence: Certified extract of the ISC register, full share-register ledger, certified articles of incorporation, and a notarized source-of-wealth declaration for every UBO holding 25% or more of shares or controlling influence.

Defense evidence: Board resolution authorizing share issuance or subordinated loan, executed subscription agreement, SWIFT MT103 payment advice with purpose code 'EQUI' (equity investment), audited parent financial statements, and Investment Canada Act Form A notification where the investor is a non-Canadian establishing a new Canadian business.
