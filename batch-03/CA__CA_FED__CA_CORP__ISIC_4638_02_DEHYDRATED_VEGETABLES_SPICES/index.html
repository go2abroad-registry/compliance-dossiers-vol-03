Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA); principal portals are Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account, Represent a Client, and the CRA non-resident registration channels. Customs registrations and export filings are administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), principally through the Canada Border Services Agency Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) system and the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS). Provincial and territorial tax authorities may separately administer provincial corporate income tax, retail sales tax, and other subnational taxes.

E-government filing portal

Apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN), generally through Business Registration Online, by telephone, or through an authorized representative; a non-resident corporation may apply using the prescribed non-resident registration process where online access is unavailable. 3. Register the relevant CRA program accounts, including the RC corporate income tax account, RT GST/HST account, RP payroll account if employees are maintained, and other accounts where applicable. 4. Provide the legal name, registered office, mailing address, incorporation jurisdiction and date, business activity, fiscal year-end, directors, authorized representative, non-resident status, and expected taxable supplies. 5. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canadian corporations are generally taxed as separate legal entities and do not have a United States-style check-the-box election. A corporation incorporated in Canada is generally resident in Canada, subject to statutory residence rules, treaty tie-breakers, and factual central-management-and-control principles. Eligible Canadian-controlled private corporations may claim the small business deduction where statutory conditions are satisfied. Corporations may make specific elections under the Income Tax Act and Excise Tax Act, including fiscal-period, capital-cost allowance, foreign-affiliate, GST/HST real-property, closely related-corporation, and joint-venture elections where applicable. Canadian unlimited liability companies may have different foreign classification outcomes, but Canada does not generally elect their classification through a check-the-box regime. Canada's hybrid-mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences for specified deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction arrangements; they are not elective entity-classification rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, excluding supplies of financial services and certain other excluded amounts, measured under the small-supplier rules generally by reference to a single calendar quarter or the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration may be voluntary below the threshold. HST rates are generally 13% in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia, and 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island. Quebec administers QST separately and certain other provinces administer separate provincial sales taxes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada may generally be zero-rated for GST/HST where the supplier ships or causes the goods to be shipped outside Canada, or the purchaser exports the goods under conditions prescribed by the Excise Tax Act and its regulations. The supplier must establish that the goods were exported and retain evidence such as customs records, carrier documentation, bills of lading, delivery records, purchase orders, invoices, and proof of foreign delivery. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated only where the specific statutory conditions for exported services are satisfied; services relating to real property, tangible personal property, or activities performed in Canada may be excluded or subject to special rules. Exporters generally recover eligible GST/HST incurred on inputs through input tax credits, subject to registration, documentation, timing, and attribution requirements. A non-resident corporation carrying on business in Canada, soliciting orders, maintaining inventory, using Canadian facilities, or otherwise creating a Canadian GST/HST nexus may be required to register even if most sales are exported.

  • Canadian customs export declaration or export report filed through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS), where reporting is required; certain goods and destinations may be subject to exemptions or alternative reporting rules
  • Commercial invoice identifying the Canadian exporter, foreign purchaser or consignee, goods description, quantity, value, currency, terms of sale, and destination
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, airway bill, or carrier receipt
  • Proof of export and foreign delivery, including carrier tracking, delivery confirmation, customs exit evidence, warehouse records, or equivalent commercial evidence
  • Purchase order, sales contract, customer correspondence, and accounting records linking the shipment to the zero-rated invoice
  • GST/HST invoice or export invoice showing the applicable zero-rated treatment and the supplier's GST/HST registration number where required
  • Certificate of origin or other origin documentation where required by the destination country's import rules or a preferential trade agreement
  • Inward remittance evidence is not generally a mandatory Canadian GST/HST zero-rating document; bank payment records may nevertheless support the commercial substance and audit trail

Canada has no general federal real-time GST/HST invoice-clearance, mandatory continuous transaction control, or universal government e-invoicing mandate for ordinary business-to-business transactions. Registrants must issue compliant invoices or other documentary evidence when required, retain adequate books and records, and support input tax credit claims with prescribed information, including supplier identity, GST/HST registration number where applicable, tax amount or rate, invoice date, description, and consideration. Electronic invoices and electronic records are generally acceptable if their integrity, accessibility, readability, and audit trail are maintained. GST/HST returns are generally filed electronically by corporations and certain other registrants through CRA systems, subject to filing-method rules and limited exceptions. Export declarations and commercial export reporting are separately handled through CBSA systems, including CERS where applicable.

Canada does not generally impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and does not require an inward remittance certificate as a condition of GST/HST zero-rating. CRA may reassess GST/HST, deny input tax credits or export zero-rating, assess interest, and apply applicable administrative penalties where the supplier cannot substantiate exportation, eligibility, invoicing, or documentary compliance. False statements, gross negligence, deliberate evasion, or participation in fraudulent invoicing may result in significant penalties, prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under the Excise Tax Act and related legislation. CBSA may issue monetary penalties under the Administrative Monetary Penalty System for inaccurate, late, or missing customs reporting and may reassess duties and taxes. Reassessments generally remain subject to statutory limitation periods, with extended or unlimited periods potentially applying in cases of misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful blindness, or fraud. The corporation should preserve export, customs, logistics, customer, payment, and tax records for the applicable statutory retention period, generally at least six years from the end of the relevant tax year, subject to longer requirements where an objection, appeal, investigation, or direction to retain records is pending.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a fully free-floating exchange-rate regime with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. No exchange controls exist under the Currency Act, Bank of Canada Act, or Income Tax Act; profit remittance is unfettered, but dividends paid to non-residents attract Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced typically to 5%-15%, e.g., 5% for a 10%+ corporate shareholder under the Canada-US Treaty). Cross-border electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are FINTRAC-reportable; foreign-currency receipts may be retained indefinitely in authorized foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no export-proceeds surrender, mandatory repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for trade receipts. Exporters of dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper may hold USD and other foreign currency fully offshore or in Canadian-domiciled foreign-currency accounts without conversion obligation. Compliance duties are limited to CRA six-year record retention of export documentation (commercial invoices, bills of lading, customs B3/export declarations) and FINTRAC reporting of receipts in excess of CAD 10,000 per transaction or over a 24-hour period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) as monetary authority; prudential banking supervision by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CFT enforcement and cross-border wire reporting by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility for operational disbursements strictly; not viable for commercial LC origination, Lynx high-value clearing, or trade finance facilities. Global EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) operating in Canada must register as Money Services Businesses with FINTRAC and obtain provincial MSB licenses; they offer efficient MT103-style payouts and multi-currency wallets but lack UCP 600 LC issuance, confirmation, and CAD Lynx (large-value) settlement. Recommended architecture: Schedule I bank as primary settlement and trade-finance rail, EMI as a supplementary low-value disbursement channel capped at CAD 50,000 per transaction.

Payment classification code 'EQUI' (equity-related) should be used per the bank's Trade Services coding; for intercompany shareholder loans, 'INTR' or 'LOAN' with the executed loan agreement bearing arm's-length interest and the relevant Form T1134/T1135 disclosure. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

38% general rate for large corporations; 15% small business rate if taxable capital employed in Canada does not exceed $10 million and taxable income does not exceed $500,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Nil for Canadian residents; 25% statutory rate for non-resident recipients (treaty rates may reduce to 5-15% depending on treaty, e.g., 15% for US, 0% for certain EU countries under conditions)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 250,000 for related party transactions triggering local file requirement; CAD 50 million global revenue for master file

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations do not apply in Canada; instead, General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and transfer pricing rules enforce economic substance through arm's length principles and beneficial ownership requirements

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Income Tax Act, fines up to $25,000 per count, imprisonment up to 2 years, restitution of evaded taxes

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties, interest, and in cases of gross negligence or willful default, criminal liability; potential disqualification from office
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of deductions, penalties up to 50% of tax benefit, possible criminal charges if intent to evade

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors personally liable for taxes, fines, and in severe cases, imprisonment for fraud or evasion
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to $500,000 for corporations, imprisonment up to 5 years; administrative monetary penalties from FINTRAC

Director personal liability: Personal fines, imprisonment, and disqualification; civil liability for damages
