Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); online portals: My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html), GST/HST NETFILE, Business Registration Online (BRO) (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business/register.html)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must determine whether it carries on a business in Canada or makes taxable supplies in Canada. If registration is required, obtain a 9-digit Business Number (BN) by submitting Form RC1 'Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts' or by using CRA's Business Registration Online (BRO) if eligible. The application must include legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, date of incorporation, tax domicile, Canadian contact/agent details, fiscal year-end, and intended trade. A separate GST/HST program account (RT) and Import/Export account (RM) may be created concurrently. Non-resident corporations may also need to complete the non-resident registration worksheet and provide authority for a representative if using an accountant.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not have a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' or hybrid entity classification election under the Income Tax Act. A Canadian Business Corporation is always taxed as a separate taxpayer at the entity level and distributions are subject to the integrated corporate/individual tax system. Available statutory elections relevant to cross-border structures include: (i) the section 216 election allowing non-residents to file Canadian income tax returns on a net rental basis; (ii) the section 219 election for non-resident corporations taxed on certain income; (iii) GST/HST annual filing election for registrants below the prescribed threshold; and (iv) various domestic and treaty-based foreign tax credit and transfer pricing elections. No election is available to treat a Canadian corporation as a pass-through entity for federal tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide taxable supplies for 'small supplier' status; a non-resident supplying taxable goods or services in Canada is required to register for GST/HST unless the small supplier threshold applies, and special rules may impose registration when the non-resident carries on a business in Canada or solicits orders in Canada.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Canada are generally zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act. For sterile examination nitrile gloves (ISIC 3250; NAICS 339113), eligibility requires: (i) the goods must be sold and physically exported to a foreign destination; (ii) the supplier must retain evidence of export within 60 days (or a longer period if due diligence is demonstrated); (iii) the goods must not be acquired for use in Canada before export; and (iv) the supply must be to a non-resident or under a contract with delivery outside Canada. Services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated where the services are not included in exceptions such as Canadian real estate, goods, or performance in Canada. The supplier must keep books and records in Canada or at a designated location.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Canada Customs Coding Form B13A or electronic export declaration EDI if commercial goods exceed prescribed thresholds)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Commercial Invoice showing product description (sterile nitrile gloves), quantity, unit value, HS/NAICS classification, and terms of sale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/TT wire transfer receipt evidencing foreign exchange receipt
  • Delivery confirmation / carrier transport documentation evidencing physical departure from Canada
  • Export permits or certificates as required for medical devices under the Medical Devices Regulations (Health Canada)

Canada does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing clearance system. No API-based tax clearance, digital stamping, or 'fiscal receipt' validation is required for suppliers. Registered persons must issue and retain invoices in a format that can be readily provided to the CRA; electronic invoices (PDF, EDI, XML) are acceptable. Mandatory GST/HST electronic filing applies to registrants whose annual taxable supplies exceed CAD 1.5 million; filing may be done through CRA-certified software or CRA's Web Forms application, with XML programming interfaces for authorized filers.

The CRA can reassess a zero-rating claim if supporting export documentation is deficient. For negligence or misrepresentation, the normal reassessment period is suspended and the CRA may reassess at any time up to 10 years or indefinitely in cases of fraud. A false zero-rated export claim may result in a penalty under subsection 296(5) of the Excise Tax Act equal to the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the net tax understated, plus compound interest computed from the date of filing. If the claim is fraudulent or promotes tax evasion, criminal prosecution under section 327 of the Excise Tax Act or section 380 of the Criminal Code (fraud) may apply, leading to substantial fines and imprisonment. Retrospective clawback includes the full GST/HST amount that would have been collectable, reversal of refunds or credits previously granted, and cancellation of privileges such as zero-rating or registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible and free-floating on both current and capital accounts. No exchange-control authorization or central bank approval is required for equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, dividend or interest remittances, royalty payments, branch remittances, or repatriation of capital. Non-resident profit remittance is constrained only by CRA Part XIII withholding tax (statutory rate 25%, treaty-reduced to as low as 5% for qualifying 10% shareholders under the Canada–US Treaty), the CBCA solvency test, and standard AML documentation. The CAD is CLS-eligible and deeply liquid in spot, forward, and swap markets; convertibility risk is purely market-based, not regulatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency offshore; however, Canadian tax residents whose specified foreign property (including offshore bank accounts) has an aggregate cost amount exceeding CAD 100,000 must file CRA Form T1135. All international electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reported to FINTRAC by financial intermediaries, and physical cross-border movements of currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more require a FINTRAC cross-border currency declaration. Export documentation (commercial invoices, bills of lading, customs entries) must be retained under CRA record-keeping rules to evidence the export transaction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada). Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority; FX supervision is distributed among OSFI (prudential regulation of deposit-taking institutions and FX risk), FINTRAC (cross-border currency and international EFT reporting thresholds), and the Minister of Finance (reserve emergency powers under the Currency Act). The Bank of Canada operates the Lynx wholesale clearing system and monitors FX markets but issues no convertibility permits and imposes no exchange-control licensing regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW as the primary treasury rail; MEDIUM as an auxiliary payment channel. Canada regulates payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act, administered by the Bank of Canada, with mandatory registration applicable from September 2025. Foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may register as foreign Money Service Businesses with FINTRAC, but they cannot gain direct access to the Lynx core clearing system, cannot issue commercial letters of credit or CAD-denominated bankers' acceptances, and are not CDIC-insured deposit takers. EMIs are acceptable only for low-value receivables collection and selected supplier disbursements; capital escrow, LC issuance, FX hedging execution, and clearing continuity must remain with a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity injections and shareholder loans are routed by SWIFT MT103 to the Canadian Schedule I operating account. The payment message should reference: (i) the corporate resolution and share-subscription agreement number; (ii) the 15-digit CRA Business Number (BN) and the bank corporate account number; (iii) the Statistics Canada / Bank of Canada Balance of Payments purpose category for inward direct investment equity or, for shareholder finance, the intra-group lending category; and (iv) the CBCA certificate of incorporation where standing bank instructions require it. The receiving bank performs negative-news and source-of-funds verification, files FINTRAC international EFT reports for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more, and may request an Investment Canada Act notification status letter for any direct investment; no exchange-control permit is required. For capital-escrow structures, funds are held in a segregated, non-interest-bearing account suite and released only upon delivery of a directors' resolution and satisfaction of the bank's LC-issuance conditions.For first-time supplier commitments (nitrile latex from Malaysia, coagulant chemicals from Thailand, packaging inputs), use SWIFT MT700 documentary commercial letters of credit compliant with UCP600/eUCP, requiring presentation of combined transport documents (ocean or multimodal bill of lading, packing list, commercial invoice, certificate of analysis).
