Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None: the BCBCA imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a BC ULC and no escrow account is required; shares may be subscribed for nominal consideration (typically CAD$1).

Funding & unlock path: For a virtual micro-SaaS office: no fire certification or civil-defense clearance is required. For a physical office: obtain from the municipality a business-occupancy and fire-safety inspection certificate (BC Fire Code compliance) before occupation; complete structural/load inspection of the leased premises; register the employer with WorkSafeBC to secure the occupational health and safety clearance.

Paid-in capital rule: None: the BCBCA imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a BC ULC and no escrow account is required; shares may be subscribed for nominal consideration (typically CAD$1).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; primary portal: My Business Account and GST/HST NETFILE at canada.ca; supplementary provincial registrar for British Columbia: BC Ministry of Finance, BC Tax Services (PST account).

E-government filing portal

For a British Columbia unlimited liability corporation, obtain the BC incorporation certificate from the BC Corporate Registry and then apply to the CRA for a Business Number (BN) and the GST/HST program account, normally through the new-registration process in My Business Account or Form RC1. A foreign or non-resident supplier that is not incorporated in Canada may register directly for a CRA BN and GST/HST account under the non-resident registration framework using Form RC1 and must appoint an authorized representative with a Canadian address where required. Because the business uses Paddle or Lemon Squeezy as merchant of record, verify whether the payment platform collects Canadian GST/HST and BC PST on the transactional level; the underlying BC ULC remains separately responsible for its own income tax filing, GST/HST registration obligations, and any PST vendor account for supplies not captured by the merchant of record.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Canada has no domestic check-the-box regime; a BC ULC is a taxable Canadian company for Income Tax Act purposes and therefore is otherwise subject to entity-level Canadian income tax. The Pass_Through_Eligible classification relevant here arises under foreign check-the-box rules, principally U.S. Treasury Regulation §301.7701-3: if the ULC is not a per se corporation for U.S. tax purposes, the shareholders may file IRS Form 8832 to classify the ULC as a partnership, a disregarded entity, or a corporation. Any such election must be assessed against the Canada-U.S. Tax Convention, hybrid mismatch rules, and the potential application of subsection 245(1) or other GAAR provisions. For GST/HST purposes, the ULC is a separate 'person' and may hold its own BN and GST/HST accounts independent of the shareholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For GST/HST, the small-supplier threshold is C$30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies measured over the current calendar quarter and the trailing four calendar quarters; once exceeded, registration is mandatory. Non-resident suppliers of digital goods and services to Canadian recipients must register for GST/HST when their specified taxable supplies to persons in Canada meet the same general economic threshold (C$30,000), unless an exclusion applies. For BC PST, there is no small-supplier monetary exemption for taxable software/SaaS; every supplier making taxable sales into British Columbia, including non-resident suppliers, must obtain a BC PST vendor account.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of services, including SaaS/cloud services, is zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V of the Excise Tax Act when made to a non-resident person, provided the service is not performed for the non-resident while that individual is present in Canada and does not relate to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or another excluded Canadian-situated supply. For digital exports, the supplier must have contemporaneous evidence that the recipient's ordinary residence, business establishment, or other place of residence is outside Canada and that the supply is not made to a person registered for GST/HST in a way that changes the place of supply. Export zero-rating for physical goods applies when the goods are exported and the supplier retains documented proof of export; for a Micro-SaaS business, the supply is normally a pure digital service or intangible personal property and no customs declaration is triggered unless bundled with physical media.

  • Canada Customs Coding Form B13A / Canada Customs Export Declaration for any physical media or hardware exported from Canada
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / courier or postal export record evidencing removal from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice and foreign-currency conversion records demonstrating receipt of offshore payment proceeds

Canada does not operate a continuous transaction control or real-time fiscal clearance system for GST/HST; there is no government-mandated API clearance, digital stamping, or invoice pre-authorization. CRA accepts electronic GST/HST invoices provided they are legible and contain the prescribed particulars, including the supplier's Business Number, the recipient's name, the date of supply, a description of the supply, the GST/HST rate, and the tax amount. Registered suppliers with qualifying turnover are generally required to file GST/HST returns electronically. BC PST invoices must separately state the PST amount for taxable software and may not show it as included unless the tax is expressly absorbed.

CRA may reassess a GST/HST return within four years, and longer in the case of misrepresentation or fraud, to deny zero-rating and claw back input tax credits where the export/non-residence documentation is absent, inconsistent, or unreliable. The penalty regime in section 285 of the Excise Tax Act imposes a minimum penalty of C$250 and up to 50% of the net tax understated or the refund or credit claimed if the false statement or omission was made knowingly or under circumstances amounting to gross negligence. Retrospective interest is chargeable on any GST/HST or PST shortfall. Fraudulent export zero-rating schemes may lead to criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, loss of registration privileges, and joint or director liability where applicable. BC PST penalties separately apply for failure to register, collect, remit, or maintain proper records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. A British Columbia ULC may receive foreign investment, pay foreign suppliers, remit dividends or other permitted distributions, repay shareholder loans, and convert CAD into foreign currencies without a general central-bank approval. Remittances remain subject to corporate-law solvency and authorization requirements, applicable withholding tax, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds checks, sanctions screening, lender covenants, and bank documentation. A ULC does not obtain automatic tax pass-through treatment under Canadian law; its Canadian tax classification and any foreign tax treatment must be separately confirmed. Canadian residents and entities may have foreign-reporting obligations, including specified foreign property reporting where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio generally applies in Canada. Software subscriptions and cloud-software services normally generate service receipts rather than goods-export proceeds subject to a surrender regime. Receipts may remain in CAD or foreign currency accounts and may be converted at the holder's discretion, subject to contractual, tax, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, payment-provider, and bank reporting requirements. Banks may request invoices, contracts, subscription reports, merchant statements, and proof of the commercial purpose of incoming funds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada. Canada has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing exchange controls, mandatory export-receipt surrender, or capital-account conversion quotas. Foreign-exchange reporting, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and prudential obligations may also involve the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, the Canada Border Services Agency, and the Canada Revenue Agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection and treasury operations, but not a substitute for a Canadian operating bank account. Paddle and Lemon Squeezy generally operate as merchant-of-record or payment-platform providers, subject to their own underwriting, restricted-business rules, tax collection processes, reserve policies, settlement currencies, chargeback controls, and payout eligibility. An offshore electronic-money institution may provide collection, virtual accounts, or foreign-currency settlement only where its licence, Canadian solicitation model, correspondent-bank arrangements, sanctions controls, and contractual terms permit the activity. The ULC must maintain accurate beneficial-owner, business-purpose, website, terms-of-service, privacy, refund, customer-location, tax, and source-of-funds information. Platform balances are not necessarily Canadian deposit accounts or CDIC-insured deposits.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined general rate for a non-CCPC ULC (15% federal general + 12% British Columbia general); 11% combined on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income if the ULC is a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) (9% federal + 2% BC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal-period returns are due no later than 6 months after the ULC's taxation year-end; balance due for a non-CCPC ULC is due 2 months after year-end (3 months for a qualifying CCPC associated group business limit; instalment schedule applies where prior-year combined federal/BC tax exceeds CAD 3,000).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same T2 due date (6 months after year-end; no separate extended date)

Deadline: · Frequency:

BC assessment is issued by CRA on the basis of T2 Schedule 403; BC statute-barred period is the same as the federal 4-year reassessment window (3-year normal), i.e., the CRA can reassess within 4 years for non-negligence cases.

Form T106 – Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents / T2 cover page Discloses each related-party cross-border transaction: management fees, royalties, interest, services, cost contributions and purchase price; requires country-by-country identification of the related non-resident and methodology applied. Filing threshold is exceeded when the aggregate FMV of all non-arm's length transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 1,000,000 in the taxation year — for a micro-SaaS this is usually not met; nonetheless, when parent recharges or royalty arrangements cross the threshold, T106 and contemporaneous documentation must be filed before the T2 due date. Schedule 403 provincial tax rate fields BC general rate 12%, small-business rate 2% for CCPCs, BC 'loss restriction rules follow ITA 111; BC no standalone branch tax. Provincial allocation to BC is determined by the ULC's BC permanent establishment assumed 100%; no additional BC return to deliver to BC Ministry of Finance.
