Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account portal, and the GST/HST NETFILE API gateway; provincial counterpart: BC Ministry of Finance for British Columbia PST registration and the BC Corporate Registry.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned BC Unlimited Liability Company (ULC) must: (1) be incorporated or registered extraprovincially under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act and obtain a BC company/incorporation number from the BC Registry; (2) apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using BRO/Form RC1 or, for non-resident applicants, Form RC1J; (3) open the CRA accounts required for the business, notably GST/HST and corporate income tax accounts; and (4) register with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST when making taxable retail sales in BC. The BN is the statutory tax identification number for Canadian federal tax purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (a BC/AB ULC is by default a taxable Canadian corporation subject to entity-level federal and BC income tax, but it qualifies for a statutory pass-through election under Income Tax Act s.39(11) when all shareholders are non-residents of Canada, whereby the ULC is treated as a partnership for Canadian federal and provincial income tax purposes). — A foreign-owned BC Unlimited Liability Company (ULC) must: (1) be incorporated or registered extraprovincially under the British Columbia Business Corporations Act and obtain a BC company/incorporation number from the BC Registry; (2) apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) using BRO/Form RC1 or, for non-resident applicants, Form RC1J; (3) open the CRA accounts required for the business, notably GST/HST and corporate income tax accounts; and (4) register with the BC Ministry of Finance for PST when making taxable retail sales in BC. The BN is the statutory tax identification number for Canadian federal tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (a BC/AB ULC is by default a taxable Canadian corporation subject to entity-level federal and BC income tax, but it qualifies for a statutory pass-through election under Income Tax Act s.39(11) when all shareholders are non-residents of Canada, whereby the ULC is treated as a partnership for Canadian federal and provincial income tax purposes).

Available election forms

Income Tax Act s.39(11) ULC partnership election, available where all shareholders are non-resident persons and the election is filed with the CRA within the prescribed statutory window; no US-style check-the-box regime exists in Canada; hybrid-mismatch and anti-avoidance rules apply under ITA ss.18(18)-(20); inter-provincial permanent-establishment allocation elections under the Income Tax Regulations; ETA small-supplier relief and voluntary GST/HST registration elections; and BC PST foreign-vendor registration elections under the Provincial Sales Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies of goods and services made in Canada in the current calendar quarter and the preceding four calendar quarters (ETA small-supplier threshold, s.148); GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of services or intangible property, including a SaaS SEO/analytics platform, supplied by the Canadian ULC to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the ETA (ss. 5-7) where the supply is not in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada and the recipient is not registered for GST/HST under Division V of Part IX. For digital supplies, the ETA place-of-supply rules attribute the supply to the recipient's jurisdiction, so international B2B customers are generally supplied outside Canada; any portion of the service performed for or enjoyed in Canada (e.g., on-shore management of Canadian-located websites) remains taxable. Canada does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied as prudent treasury policy and to support transfer-pricing documentation.

  • Signed service agreement or MSA evidencing the non-resident recipient
  • Commercial invoice declaring the zero-rated supply and citing ETA Schedule VI Part V authority
  • Evidence of the recipient's non-resident status (foreign corporate registry extract or tax-residence certificate)
  • Bank remittance records evidencing foreign-currency settlement from the overseas client
  • Digital delivery logs and IP geolocation records, including API/server/dashboard logs, demonstrating that the supply is performed outside Canada
  • Customs B13A export declaration and bill of lading, strictly limited to any incidental physical goods accompanying the service

No mandatory national e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime; the CRA operates a voluntary PEPPOL-BIS 3.0 e-invoicing framework. Tax returns are filed electronically via the CRA GST/HST NETFILE and gateway APIs; no digital stamping or per-transaction fiscal authorization is required.

CRA treats zero-rated cross-border digital services as a high-risk audit area. Unsupported zero-rating is clawed back retroactively with prescribed-rate interest within the four-year normal reassessment window (ITA s.152(4) / ETA s.298). Penalties include the gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax (ITA s.163(2)); the ETA s.285 false-statement penalty of up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment; Customs Act s.109 penalties for false export declarations where goods are involved; and transfer-pricing adjustments under ITA s.247 or Part XIII non-resident withholding consequences where income is not repatriated and properly documented within a commercially reasonable period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is generally convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada has no general exchange-control approval requirement for ordinary inbound equity, intercompany funding, dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, or loan principal payments. Banks and payment providers may require evidence of purpose, source of funds, beneficial ownership, and transaction legitimacy under their risk controls and applicable AML obligations. A British Columbia unlimited liability company is a Canadian corporation for Canadian tax and corporate-law purposes; its ULC designation does not itself create Canadian tax pass-through treatment. A non-resident shareholder's distributions may be subject to Canadian withholding tax, commonly 25% under domestic law unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty, with treaty entitlement, beneficial ownership, and procedural requirements to be assessed. Dividends are generally paid from legally available funds and remain subject to corporate-law solvency restrictions. Interest, royalties, service payments, and related-party transactions require separate withholding-tax, transfer-pricing, deductibility, and treaty analysis. Canadian tax filings and any applicable withholding remittances remain due notwithstanding unrestricted currency conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to repatriate export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender of foreign-currency export receipts. Receipts may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the company's discretion. Exporters remain subject to ordinary income-tax, GST/HST, customs, sanctions, AML, contractual, and accounting requirements. A person or entity transporting currency or monetary instruments of CAD 10,000 or more into or out of Canada must comply with applicable CBSA reporting rules; this is a border declaration obligation, not an export-proceeds surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (BoC), Canada's central bank. The BoC conducts monetary policy and oversees payment-system matters; it does not operate a general-purpose foreign-exchange approval or capital-control regime. The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) administers federal anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting requirements for reporting entities. The Canada Border Services Agency administers specified cross-border currency and monetary-instrument reporting at the border.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and disbursement; low as a substitute for a Canadian deposit-taking bank account or unrestricted corporate treasury account. Stripe and Paddle may support eligible Canadian businesses and SaaS payment flows subject to their current country, product, underwriting, and prohibited-business terms. They are payment processors or merchant-of-record providers, not necessarily banks or electronic-money institutions, and balances, settlement currencies, reserves, payout timing, and permitted uses are contractually constrained. An offshore EMI account may be unavailable to a Canadian ULC, may not provide Canadian-dollar clearing or deposit protection, and remains subject to provider onboarding, sanctions screening, beneficial-owner checks, and applicable Canadian regulatory perimeter rules. Confirm provider eligibility, account-holder identity, settlement routing, and safeguarding arrangements before relying on the service.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route funding from an account in the verified investor's or lender's legal name to the ULC's Canadian business account using an international wire through SWIFT or another bank-supported rail. Use accurate remitter and beneficiary legal names, account and bank identifiers, currency, and payment narrative identifying the transaction as paid-in share capital, share subscription proceeds, or a shareholder/intercompany loan, as applicable. Canada does not prescribe a universal foreign-exchange purpose code for ordinary corporate capital transfers; do not invent or misstate a code. Provide the bank with the executed subscription or loan documents, corporate approvals, capitalization records, ultimate beneficial-owner information, source-of-funds evidence, and anticipated use of proceeds. Confirm intermediary-bank charges, FX conversion rate, value date, and any correspondent-bank requirements. Record the transfer consistently in corporate, tax, and accounting records; assess Canadian thin-capitalization, interest deductibility, transfer-pricing, withholding, and treaty consequences for debt funding.For routine supplier payments, use the bank's domestic electronic funds transfer or international SWIFT customer credit transfer service; MT103 is a legacy SWIFT message designation commonly used to describe a customer credit transfer, not a separate settlement network or a guarantee of payment. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (Federal 15% + British Columbia provincial 12% general corporate rate; small business deduction not applicable to ULC unless CCPC status is met, which is atypical for ULC structures)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (Part XIII tax), unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Six months after the corporation's fiscal year-end (e.g., for a December 31 year-end, deadline is June 30 of the following year); balance of tax due is payable within two months of year-end (or three months for eligible CCPCs)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Canadian corporations with gross revenue exceeding CAD 50 million (or assets exceeding CAD 50 million) in the fiscal year are required to prepare and file Form T106 (Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) and maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) if the aggregate value of non-arm's length transactions exceeds CAD 1 million (for goods) or CAD 250,000 (for services, royalties, and interest)

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but the CRA applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to deny tax benefits from transactions lacking economic substance; for ULCs, the CRA may scrutinize whether the entity has real business operations (e.g., employees, office, decision-making) in Canada, especially if it is used as a conduit for US investors; the Canada-US treaty's Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause requires substantive business presence to access treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act, s. 239)

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest; criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties under s. 163(2) of 50% of the understated tax

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts if they fail to withhold and remit source deductions (s. 227.1); for evasion, directors may face criminal charges, travel bans, and seizure of personal assets; CRA can impose a director's liability assessment without proof of intent
Transfer Pricing Penalties (Income Tax Act, s. 247(3))

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment to income (if the adjustment exceeds CAD 5 million or 10% of gross revenue); plus interest on the reassessment; no penalty if the taxpayer has made reasonable efforts and provided documentation

Director personal liability: Directors are not personally liable for TP penalties unless they participated in the decision to set non-arm's length prices; however, they may be liable for gross negligence penalties (s. 163.2) if they knowingly or under circumstances of gross negligence made false statements
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (GAAR, s. 245)

Statutory sanction: GAAR denies the tax benefit, resulting in reassessment of income and penalties; interest accrues from the original due date; CRA may also apply the treaty's LOB clause to deny treaty relief, leading to full 25% withholding on dividends

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the tax benefit denied if they signed the tax return or directed the transaction; they may also face penalties under s. 163.2 for false statements; in extreme cases, criminal charges for conspiracy to evade tax
AML / Anti-Money Laundering (Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million for individuals and CAD 2 million for corporations per violation; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment for willful non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly or negligently failed to implement compliance policies; they may also face criminal charges for money laundering if they direct or participate in illicit transactions
