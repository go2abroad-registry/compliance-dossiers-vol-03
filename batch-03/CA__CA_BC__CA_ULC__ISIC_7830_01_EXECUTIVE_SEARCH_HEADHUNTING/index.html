Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account items. The Canadian dollar floats freely and no exchange-control approval or conversion license is required for inbound equity, shareholder loans, retainer receipts, dividend remittances, or liquidation distributions. Profit remittance from the ULC to non-resident shareholders is subject only to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax (statutory 25%, treaty-reduced under Canada-US Treaty Article X to 5% for a 10% or greater corporate shareholder and 15% otherwise), administered via CRA Forms NR301/NR302 at the paying bank; no FX hurdle applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory surrender window, minimum conversion ratio, or mandatory repatriation for export or cross-border service proceeds (executive search retainers). Foreign currency may be held indefinitely in domiciliary accounts. Administrative reporting applies: single cross-border electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more are reportable to FINTRAC, and physical currency conveyance of CAD 10,000 or more must be declared to CBSA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); FX administration is shared with the Department of Finance Canada, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for transaction monitoring and EFT reporting, and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for cross-border currency declaration. Prudential bank supervision rests with the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI). Canada maintains no dedicated exchange-control office.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rated MEDIUM for low-value operational receipts (the mandated Wise B2B rail) and LOW for capital escrow, reserve concentration, or core clearing. Legal constraints: (1) any offshore EMI accepting CAD/USD for a Canadian entity must hold a FINTRAC money-services business registration; (2) retail payment service providers must comply with the Bank of Canada Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration regime; (3) EMI balances carry no CDIC deposit insurance and offer no credit relationship; (4) an EMI may not be used to segment wire activity or circumvent FINTRAC EFT reporting above CAD 10,000. Recommended architecture: Wise B2B for retainer intakes with daily sweeping into an RBC operational account; escrow and liquidity reserves remain exclusively with a Schedule I bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign-parent equity injection into the BC ULC clears via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire with Canadian correspondent) into the ULC CAD/USD account at a Schedule I bank. Canada has no national FX purpose-code registry; banks accept SWIFT transaction-type codes and free-format narrative, with the recommended purpose reading 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION - ULC SUBSCRIBED SHARE CAPITAL' and a reference to the BC Business Corporations Act Notice of Articles if issuance alters share capital. Receiving-bank verification pack: certified Notice of Articles, board resolution authorizing issuance and signatories, updated shareholder register, UBO declaration, and US tax-pack references (Form 5471/8858) where US counsel directs. Single EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more are FINTRAC-reportable; no central-bank permit or conversion approval is required, and CAD/USD conversion executes at market rate with LVTS same-day settlement for domestic legs.Clearing rails for a headhunting operating model: (1) domestic payables, payroll, and refunds settle via Payments Canada LVTS wire (CAD 100,000+) or standard EFT; (2) cross-border fee splits, subcontractor research fees, and retainer refunds clear via SWIFT MT103 with UETR reference; (3) a standby letter of credit (SBLC) is offered where a client requires performance security on retainer prepayment; a documentary commercial LC is rated not applicable for service-based executive search revenue. Master / Local File threshold: CAD. T106 filing is mandatory for reportable non-arm's length cross-border transactions aggregating more than CAD 50,000 per related person. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation equivalent to OECD Master File and Local File is required by CRA where the ULC's gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million or the aggregate value of its non-arm's length cross-border transactions exceeds CAD 5 million; failure to provide such documentation triggers the 10% penalty under s.247(3).

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations. Economic substance is nevertheless enforced through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s.245 ITA), which recharacterizes transactions lacking a bona fide purpose and may impose a 25% penalty; (ii) the corporate-residency test, requiring central management and control to be exercised in BC; (iii) Canada-US treaty Limitation on Benefits provisions, which require the ULC to maintain real economic activity such as an office, staff, and substantive contracting; and (iv) BC corporate registry rules requiring an active registered office and records office. A substance-less ULC can be administratively dissolved, triggering a deemed year-end and full Part XIII exposure.
