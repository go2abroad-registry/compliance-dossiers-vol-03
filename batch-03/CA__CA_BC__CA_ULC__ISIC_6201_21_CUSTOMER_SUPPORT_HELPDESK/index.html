Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency / Agence du revenu du Canada (CRA/ARC), portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO) at canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. British Columbia's 7% Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance through the OneStop BC Registry.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated BC ULC obtains its statutory tax identifiers by filing CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts), either by mail or online through Business Registration Online. The CRA issues a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with program-account suffixes: 'RT' for GST/HST and 'RC' for corporate income tax (T2). The procedure requires a BC registered office, director and incorporation details (from the BC Corporate Registry), and, for non-resident applicants, evidence of Canadian taxable-supply nexus plus a Canadian contact point or authorized representative (Form RC59). PST registration with BC's Ministry of Finance is handled concurrently through OneStop BC.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-incorporated BC ULC obtains its statutory tax identifiers by filing CRA Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Program Accounts), either by mail or online through Business Registration Online. The CRA issues a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with program-account suffixes: 'RT' for GST/HST and 'RC' for corporate income tax (T2). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under the Canadian Income Tax Act, the BC ULC is a resident corporation subject to entity-level tax and cannot elect domestic pass-through treatment. It is, however, treated as an eligible entity for U.S. federal tax purposes and may file IRS Form 8832 (check-the-box) to be classified as a disregarded entity or partnership. For GST/HST, the ULC may file an election under section 156 of the Excise Tax Act with closely related corporations to treat intercompany supplies as non-taxable, and may voluntarily register for GST/HST to recover input tax credits attributable to zero-rated exports.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in annual worldwide gross revenue from taxable supplies (small-supplier threshold); GST/HST registration is compulsory once the threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration is available to zero-rated exporters seeking input tax credit recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the ULC are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part I of the Excise Tax Act where the supplier ships or delivers the goods to a destination outside Canada, including delivery to a common carrier retained by the supplier. Helpdesk, customer-support, and SaaS cloud services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 where the recipient is a non-resident who is not registered for GST/HST and the service does not relate to real property or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The supplier must retain evidence of non-residency, including the customer's foreign billing address, the executed contract, and foreign-sourced payment records; the zero-rated supplies are reported on the GST/HST return and the related input tax credits are claimed on the same return.

  • CBSA export declaration (B13A or electronic export reporting) for tangible goods
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing shipment to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and signed contract with the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing foreign currency repatriation
  • For SaaS/helpdesk services, non-resident substantiation records including customer billing address, IP/locale metadata, and platform subscription records

Canada imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level API reporting mandate. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA GST/HST NETFILE or My Business Account on monthly, quarterly, or annual cycles; invoices must contain the supplier's BN and the GST charged. A voluntary Pan-Canadian Peppol-based e-invoicing framework is being phased in, and the CRA may require the production of electronic books and records during audit, but there is no pre-clearance obligation.

CRA audits of zero-rated exporters verify export and non-resident documentation; defective evidence results in reversal of the zero-rating, clawback of input tax credits, reassessment at 5% GST, and excise interest. A penalty under Excise Tax Act section 280 applies to false statements or omissions, equal to the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the understated net tax. Knowingly fraudulent claims expose the entity and its directors to criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Excise Tax Act and the Criminal Code. The normal four-year reassessment window is extended to eight years where the return contains misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or willful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD, ISO 4217 code CAD) is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. Canada imposes no exchange control, no capital-movement licensing, no prior approval for inward direct investment, and no profit-repatriation permit. A BC ULC, despite its unlimited liability and possible US check-the-box disregarded-entity election, is a taxable Canadian corporation for Canadian income tax and is required to file a T2 corporate return. Profit and dividend remittances are not blocked but are subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax: statutory 25% on dividends, generally reduced by treaty (for a US beneficial owner, the Canada-US Convention typically reduces the rate to 15% on non-arm's-length? Actually the standard treaty dividend rate is 15%, 5% for 10%-or-more voting shareholders); royalties are subject to statutory 25%, generally treaty-reduced to 10% for industrial/copyright royalties and 0% for certain pure software copyright payments. The absence of exchange-control formalities means any CAD amount can be converted at a Canadian Schedule I bank at the interbank rate minus a dealer spread, without reference to a central-bank approval process.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no mandatory surrender requirement, no statutory repatriation window, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency accounts at Canadian or offshore banks. The only applicable obligations are informational and tax-related: (i) goods exporters must file export declarations with Statistics Canada through the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) for exports valued above the low-value threshold; (ii) pure SaaS/helpdesk services do not require a customs declaration but may be captured by Statistics Canada service-output surveys or the quarterly Survey of International Merchandise Trade/Travel; (iii) any international electronic funds transfer of CAD 10,000 or more must be reported by the Canadian bank to FINTRAC; (iv) foreign exchange gains and losses from export receivables are recognized for corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada), section 39, on a transaction or mark-to-market basis.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); complementary authorities include the Department of Finance Canada for international economic policy and sanctions, OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) for prudential bank supervision, and FINTRAC for anti-money-laundering and international EFT reporting. Canada maintains no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration and no general exchange-control decree under the Currency Act or Bank of Canada Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW to MEDIUM for use as the primary treasury rail; MEDIUM for secondary collection rails. Legal constraints: offshore e-money institutions (EMIs), such as Estonian or UK-licensed payment institutions, may not open settlement accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank unless they register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), now under Bank of Canada oversight with obligations phased from September 2024 to September 2025. An offshore EMI cannot issue payment items clearable through Payments Canada (Lynx or the retail batch system) unless it obtains a Canadian participant arrangement or uses a Canadian MSB sponsor, which is commercially impractical. The helpdesk SaaS may use EMIs (for example Wise Business or Payoneer) as a receivable platform, but settlement must flow in batched MT103 or domestic EFT wires into the ULC's Canadian bank account, with full visibility of the sponsoring settlement bank of the EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for an equity injection from the US parent into the BC ULC: the parent broadcasts a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 at the correspondent level for large-value transfers) from its US depository institution to the ULC concentration account at a Canadian Schedule I bank (for example RBC, TD, or BMO). The remittance information field (field 70) should include the code words 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION', the ULC legal name, the BC incorporation number, and the date of the authorizing board resolution, for instance: 'Subscription for Class A common shares of [ULC name] per resolution dated YYYY-MM-DD.' Canadian banks do not require a statutory purpose code, but a value-date audit trail is mandatory. The transfer must exceed no internal policy limits without explanation; amounts of CAD 10,000 or more require FINTRAC EFT reporting by the receiving bank. On the corporate-law side, BC's Business Corporations Act permits share issuance without minimum paid-up capital and without par value, and there is no mandatory escrow period for foreign direct investment; however, to maintain clean tax characterization, the deposit must be recorded as stated capital in the share ledger, not as a loan. The bank may additionally request a capital verification certificate from a Canadian CPA if the wire exceeds CAD 1 million. For Statistics Canada balance-of-payments purposes, the ULC should be prepared to complete the Canadian Direct Investment Survey if the parent's assets or revenues exceed the reporting threshold.Clearing rails for the helpdesk SaaS operating account: (i) cross-border supplier and contractor payments are executed by SWIFT MT103, with field 70 containing a payment purpose such as 'SAAS SUBSCRIPTION FEE' or 'SOFTWARE MAINTENANCE' and an underlying invoice reference; (ii) Canadian domestic disbursements for wages, rent, and cloud infrastructure use Payments Canada Lynx real-time large-value payment (for single payments above approximately CAD 100,000) or the retail Ace EFT batch for smaller amounts; (iii) USD disbursements to US vendors are cleared through Fedwire or CHIPS using the bank's US correspondent or a US-chartered affiliate, such as City National Bank for RBC clients or BMO Harris Bank for BMO clients; (iv) a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600/URDG 758) is not typical for SaaS licensing but may be required for high-value hardware or data-center procurement; a Standby Letter of Credit (SBLC) is the more common instrument when a Canadian vendor requires a performance or payment guarantee; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW to MEDIUM for use as the primary treasury rail; MEDIUM for secondary collection rails. Legal constraints: offshore e-money institutions (EMIs), such as Estonian or UK-licensed payment institutions, may not open settlement accounts at a Canadian Schedule I bank unless they register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and comply with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), now under Bank of Canada oversight with obligations phased from September 2024 to September 2025. An offshore EMI cannot issue payment items clearable through Payments Canada (Lynx or the retail batch system) unless it obtains a Canadian participant arrangement or uses a Canadian MSB sponsor, which is commercially impractical. The helpdesk SaaS may use EMIs (for example Wise Business or Payoneer) as a receivable platform, but settlement must flow in batched MT103 or domestic EFT wires into the ULC's Canadian bank account, with full visibility of the sponsoring settlement bank of the EMI.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined (15% federal Part I general rate + 12% British Columbia provincial general corporate rate; a Canadian-controlled private corporation may access a reduced federal rate of 9% on active business income up to its small business limit, but a ULC owned by non-residents is ordinarily not a CCPC)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under ITA Part XIII on dividends paid by a Canadian resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, unless an applicable comprehensive tax treaty reduces the rate. Under the Canada-US treaty, the rate is generally 5% for a US corporate beneficial owner owning at least 10% of the voting stock of the Canadian payer, and 15% in other cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The final federal/provincial balance due is 2 months after year-end for a CCPC and 3 months after year-end for a non-CCPC, which is the usual classification of a ULC with non-resident shareholders. Instalments are due on the last day of each quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 in annual aggregate value of reportable non-arm's-length cross-border transactions triggers the CRA Form T106 information return and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. Country-by-Country reporting notification applies when consolidated group revenue reaches CAD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulation equivalent to offshore financial centres. CRA enforces substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITA s.245), statutory anti-hybrid rules, and agency/permanent-establishment jurisprudence. A ULC engaged in helpdesk SaaS must demonstrate actual board decision-making, personnel, and risk management in British Columbia. A US check-the-box flow-through election does not reduce Canadian substance expectations; cross-border transactions between the ULC and foreign affiliates must be documented at arm's length with an ex-ante pricing rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act s.239)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution on indictment carries a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years; summary conviction carries 50% to 100% of the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 2 years. CRA may also assert civil gross-negligence penalties equal to 50% of understated tax, plus interest, asset seizure, and winding-up action.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed or assented to evasion may be jointly and severally liable for corporate source deductions under ITA s.227.1. Courts may pierce the corporate veil, imposing personal civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment where the ULC was used as a mere instrumentality.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may reassess to recharacterize transactions, deny expense deductions lacking business purpose, apply GAAR to eliminate a tax benefit, impose a 50% misrepresentation penalty under ITA s.163(2), and assert Part XIII withholding on deemed distributions. The BC corporate registry may strike the company for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign filings knowing the ULC is a shell are treated as principals in fraud. The corporate veil is disregarded, exposing directors to personal liability for unpaid Canadian taxes, restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment in serious cases.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: CRA will adjust income and charge interest. ITA s.247(3) imposes a transfer-pricing penalty equal to 10% of the net adjustment where documentation is deficient or a gross misstatement exists. T106 late-filing and information penalties are additional.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, approve, or sign mispriced cross-border transactions may be pursued personally as intermeddlers where the ULC is insolvent; criminal conspiracy charges can lead to fines, travel bans, and imprisonment. Courts may pierce the corporate veil when transfer pricing is used to strip assets from Canadian creditors.
AML / Payment Rails Compliance (FinTRAC and Criminal Code)

Statutory sanction: FINTRAC can issue administrative monetary penalties and compliance orders. Money laundering under s.462.31 of the Criminal Code and terrorist-financing offences carry imprisonment up to 10 years; bank accounts are subject to freezing under proceeds-of-crime provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge of laundered funds are personally indictable. Nominated compliance officers can be fined on a personal basis. In serious cases, courts impose travel restrictions, revoke management positions, and decline to recognize the corporate veil for criminal facilitation.
