Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Not permissible as the sole registered office for a federal corporation. The Canada Business Corporations Act, section 19, requires the registered office to be situated at a place in Canada and to be a physical address where process may be served. A virtual mail-forwarding address or postal box cannot satisfy service-of-process requirements. A licensed registered-agent office with a physical street address and on-site acceptance of legal process may be used for the registered office, but for armored vehicle manufacturing under ISIC 3040, the regulator will expect an operational facility, security-controlled premises, and verifiable commercial substance in Canada before issuing controlled goods registration, defense authorization, or government tender eligibility.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies. Shares may be issued without par value for consideration determined by the directors, subject to the Canada Business Corporations Act. Canadian federal law does not require a capital escrow account for incorporation. The corporation must maintain proper stated-capital, share-issuance, securities-register, and accounting records.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building permits, occupancy approval, fire-code inspection and certification, emergency-response and hazardous-material approvals, and any local civil-protection clearance. Obtain workers' compensation registration and maintain documented safety training, preventive maintenance, emergency plans, and inspection records.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies. Shares may be issued without par value for consideration determined by the directors, subject to the Canada Business Corporations Act. Canadian federal law does not require a capital escrow account for incorporation. The corporation must maintain proper stated-capital, share-issuance, securities-register, and accounting records.

Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD $30,000 in taxable supplies (excluding zero-rated exports) made by the entity and all associates over the preceding four calendar quarters; non-resident persons carrying on a Canadian commercial activity must register irrespective of this small-supplier exemption if they are engaged in a commercial activity in Canada and do not meet the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export supply under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when goods are exported by the supplier, or supplied to a non-resident for direct export, and the supplier has documentary proof of export including proof that title and possession passed outside Canada; for all-terrain military tactical vehicles, the exporter must hold a valid export permit and comply with the Export Control List under the Export and Import Permits Act if the goods are controlled, and zero-rating is available for qualifying services provided to non-residents if performed entirely outside Canada or in relation to tangible property situated outside Canada at the time of supply

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A) or CERS electronic export declaration with validated K84 export registration number
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air, rail, or truck) showing the consignee, destination, and export date of the military vehicle
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire record evidencing that export sales proceeds were credited to a Canadian bank account within the prescribed repatriation period

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital stamping for commercial invoices; registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD $1.5M must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI; CRA has an electronic records mandate and may require registered exporters to retain transactional and export documentation in machine-readable format for audit; mandatory CERS (Canadian Export Reporting System) electronic export declarations are required for controlled or regulated exports

CRA may reassess GST/HST and net tax deficiencies within 4 years; the limitation period is waived entirely where misrepresentation, gross negligence, or fraud is established; false or unsupported export zero-rating claims attract civil penalties up to 25% of the net tax deficiency under s. 280 of the Excise Tax Act, arrears interest under s. 280.1, and potential criminal prosecution under s. 327 for knowing misrepresentation; CRA will claw back input tax credits and assessed refunds if the exporter cannot produce the mandatory customs and foreign-exchange documentation within the 180-day repatriation window

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime. The CAD is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Canada has been in continuous compliance with IMF Article VIII since 1952, prohibiting exchange restrictions on current payments and discriminatory currency practices. No exchange-control permits, surrender requirements, or prior central-bank approvals attach to trade settlement, dividends, interest, royalties, or capital flows. Overlay restrictions are sectoral, not monetary: (i) Investment Canada Act mandatory national-security/net-benefit review for non-Canadian capital into defense-related businesses; (ii) CBCA director-residency rules requiring 25% resident-Canadian directors and at least one resident Canadian on boards of fewer than four; (iii) Controlled Goods Regulations requiring registration with the Controlled Goods Directorate for possession/examination of controlled military goods; (iv) EIPA export-permit coverage from Global Affairs Canada for outward movements of military hardware. Profit repatriation: dividends and interest paid to non-residents attract Part XIII withholding tax (25% statutory, treaty-reduced, e.g., 5%–15% under the Canada-US Treaty); branch remittances attract a 25% Part I.14 branch tax (treaty-reduced). These are fiscal burdens, not convertibility impediments; foreign-currency deposit accounts are freely available at Schedule I banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory repatriation deadline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in CAD or foreign-currency accounts at authorized Canadian banks. Ancillary compliance obligations include: (i) FINTRAC electronic funds-transfer reporting for every SWIFT wire of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour period; (ii) export declarations and permit-ledger maintenance under the Customs Act and the Export and Import Permits Act; (iii) Statistics Canada balance-of-payments and CIMT merchandise-trade reporting. For the defense sector, banks independently reconcile inbound export proceeds against the relevant EIPA permit and Controlled Goods Registration; unexplained mismatches or third-country routing trigger Suspicious Transaction Reports. Violations of EIPA or the Defence Production Act create criminal exposure but do not give rise to any currency-surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); ultimate FX policy authority rests with the Minister of Finance via the Exchange Fund Account; FINTRAC administers cross-border funds-transfer reporting under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); OSFI supervises banking soundness and capital adequacy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

RATING: LOW (0.15–0.20 / 1.00). Canada has no EMI charter class; digital payment firms must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA, operative 2024) and with FINTRAC as money services businesses (MSBs). Offshore digital EMIs are functionally non-viable for defense-sector capital flows: Schedule I banks will not route defense receivables through an EMI intermediary due to sanctions and dual-use exposure; EMIs are not Payments Canada members and cannot access Lynx/ACSS; Government Direct Wire settlement requires banking with the Receiver General's designated bank (RBC); and diplomatic LCs require confirmation by a Canadian Schedule I bank. EMIs remain acceptable only for de minimis operational expenses below CAD 10,000 (travel, software, consulting) and must never be used for capital injections, escrow, payroll of controlled-goods personnel, or trade settlements.
