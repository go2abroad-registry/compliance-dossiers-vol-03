No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the BC BCA. Shares may be issued without par value for such consideration as the board determines. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – federal corporate income tax and GST/HST; British Columbia Ministry of Finance – BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) and provincial corporate income tax (administered federally by CRA). Digital portals: CRA My Business Account, CRA Business Registration Online (BRO), and BC eTaxBC.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the BC Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) under the Business Corporations Act (British Columbia) through BC Corporate Online; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and corporate registry number. Step 2: Register the corporation with CRA through Business Registration Online (BRO) to obtain the nine-digit Business Number (BN) and federal program accounts: RT (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) as needed, and RM (import/export) as needed; the BN is the Canadian tax identification number (TIN) for both federal and BC corporate income tax, because BC corporate income tax is collected and administered by CRA. Step 3: Register separately for BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) under the Provincial Sales Tax Act via eTaxBC or the BC Ministry of Finance; the PST account number is separate from the CRA BN. Step 4: For a ULC whose primary activity is overseas PR communications, register for GST/HST voluntarily even if below the CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold in order to claim input tax credits (ITCs) and to zero-rate export services. No minimum share capital is required and non-resident ownership is permitted; a BC registered office and at least one director are required under provincial law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada does not maintain a check-the-box election for domestic tax purposes. A BC ULC is a separate taxable Canadian corporation resident in Canada, subject to Part I federal income tax and BC provincial corporate income tax on worldwide income. The unlimited liability capital structure allows U.S. members to elect pass-through treatment under U.S. check-the-box regulations (Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3) by filing Form 8832; however, Canadian tax law continues to treat the ULC as a corporation. Hybrid mismatch adjustments (Income Tax Act ss.18.2, 18.3, 18.4, 20.03 and Canadian implementation of the OECD Hybrid Mismatch recommendations) may disallow deductions or withhold taxes on certain cross-border payments and should be reviewed when structuring pass-through elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies in any calendar quarter or trailing four quarters; once exceeded, the ULC must register and collect GST/HST. BC PST: no general economic-turnover threshold; any seller making taxable sales of PST-assessable goods or services in British Columbia is required to register, while a non-resident PR firm supplying zero-rated services to overseas clients will not have Canadian PST nexus if no tangible property or enumerated services are supplied in BC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A BC ULC providing international media relations, crisis public relations, and overseas PR communication services may zero-rate its supplies for GST/HST under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, section 7 when the supply is made to a non-resident person (i) who is not registered for GST/HST and is not acquiring the service for consumption, use, or supply in Canada, and (ii) when the service is not performed in connection with real or tangible personal property situated in Canada. The agency should obtain a written declaration of non-residency, a valid OECD tax-residency certificate or equivalent, and contractual terms confirming the overseas-client engagement. Any services that relate to media targeting in Canada, are rendered for a Canadian resident, or are provided to a Canadian GST/HST registrant will be taxable at 5% GST (plus BC PST if separately assessable).

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A / Canada Export Declaration) – required solely where tangible marketing assets, printed media materials, or merchandise are physically exported as part of PR campaign deliverables
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / courier waybill for physical exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT payment or bank confirmation of remittance of fees from overseas clients
  • Executed master services agreement and zero-rated invoices identifying the non-resident client, service scope, and statutory zero-rating basis under ETA Schedule VI Part V s.7
  • Certificate of Residence / OECD tax-residency certificate of the overseas recipient

Canada has no national real-time e-invoicing clearance regime, no mandated interoperable B2B e-invoicing API, and no digital fiscal stamping system. GST/HST registrants are required to file returns electronically through CRA's GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or My Business Account and to remit net tax by the statutory monthly/quarterly deadlines. BC PST returns are filed electronically through eTaxBC. No government pre-approval of invoices is required, but CRA may verify invoices and supporting evidence during audit; registrants must maintain books and records containing all particulars of zero-rated export supplies, including copies of invoices, contracts, and outward remittance evidence.

A claim of export zero-rating is subject to strict documentary scrutiny. If the ULC fails to satisfy the ETA export conditions, CRA may reassess the supplies as taxable at 5% GST (collectible on the gross consideration), deny associated input tax credits, and claw back any GST/HST refunds already credited. In cases of negligence, ETA s.285(1) imposes a penalty of the greater of CAD 100 and 50% of any net tax under-assessed or over-claimed; s.280 and s.281 add daily compound interest for arrears and refund reassessments. Fraudulent claims can be reassessed beyond the normal four-year limitation period and may lead to criminal prosecution under ETA s.238 and Income Tax Act s.327, with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment for up to five years, in addition to provincial administrative penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada provides full current and capital account convertibility and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. Dividends, management fees, royalties and branch profits may be freely repatriated, subject to Part XIII non-resident withholding tax, corporate solvency law, and standard escrow/contractual restrictions. For a BC ULC, no exchange-control approval or conversion permit is required; the practical hurdles are banking KYC, source-of-funds proof, and any intercompany transfer pricing/withholding issues.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose a statutory surrender requirement, forced conversion ratio, or mandatory repatriation timeline for export proceeds or cross-border service revenues such as public relations retainers. Foreign-currency receivables may be retained in Canadian or offshore accounts, but deferred repatriation and passive foreign-currency holdings must be reviewed for CRA foreign accrual property income and transfer pricing exposure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); bank chartering and prudential regulation are administered by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), while anti-money laundering supervision is administered by FINTRAC. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange-control agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditional Medium-Low. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorized to accept deposits or operate as Schedule I banks in Canada and are not a compliant primary treasury rail for a BC ULC. A foreign EMI may be used only as an ancillary payment channel if it is properly licensed in its home jurisdiction and, where applicable, registered with FINTRAC or licensed under Canada's Retail Payment Activities Act. Primary liquidity, client escrow, and capital receipts must be held with a Canadian chartered bank to preserve CDIC-protected settlement and clean corporate audit trails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital may be injected freely as equity subscription or shareholder loan without exchange-control approval. For equity, the ULC must issue shares under the BC Business Corporations Act and may use a share subscription agreement and board resolution; for debt, a formal shareholder loan agreement with market-rate interest and transfer pricing support is required. Wire instructions must use SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the remitting bank to the Canadian bank's correspondent, with the receiving ULC legal name, account number, and Field 70 reference such as 'ULC_SH_CAP' or 'ULC_SH_LOAN'. The receiving Canadian bank should be pre-notified with the board resolution, source-of-funds evidence, organizational chart, and beneficial-ownership declarations. CAD credits are cleared through Canada's Large Value Transfer System; USD credits are cleared through CHIPS/Fedwire via the bank's U.S. correspondent. No statutory waiting period or surrender requirement applies.For payments to vendors, freelancers, photographers, media platforms, and international PR offices, use SWIFT MT103 for every single-beneficiary cross-border disbursement; when an intermediate correspondent consumes cover, use MT202 COVER for USD settlement. Commercial Letters of Credit are permissible but are not necessary for service-based public relations disbursements; a clean SWIFT MT103 with invoices and contracts is the standard rail. Domestic CAD vendor payments should be routed via the Automated Clearing Settlement System or Real-Time Rail, while cross-border CAD/USD payments use the SWIFT network and LVTS/CHIPS.
