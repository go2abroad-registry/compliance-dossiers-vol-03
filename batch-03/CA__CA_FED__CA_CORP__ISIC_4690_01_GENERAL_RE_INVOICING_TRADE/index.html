Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC) – federal GST/HST and corporate income tax administration; official portal: www.canada.ca/revenue-agency; business portals: Business Registration Online (BRO), My Business Account (MyBA), GST/HST NETFILE; customs/export filing administered by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Canadian Automated Export Declaration (CAED).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not resident in Canada that carries on commercial activity in Canada (including a re-invoicing/trading hub) applies for a CRA Business Number (BN) and a GST/HST program account using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or, if no Canadian presence, Form RC1A (Request for a Business Number for Non-Residents), submitted by mail or fax to the Sudbury Tax Centre, accompanied by the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, resolutions designating a Canadian agent/contact, and, where relevant, a power of attorney (Form T1013). Alternatively, Canadian-based entities may register online via the Government of Canada Business Registration Online service. The CRA assigns a 9-digit BN with a program account suffix (e.g., RT for GST/HST, RC for income tax) and issues a confirmation of registration; GST/HST registration is backdated to the date the CAD 30,000 threshold was exceeded. Exporters should also obtain a CBSA exporter business number linked to the BN where goods will be declared under the Customs Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; a federal/provincial Business Corporation is a taxable Canadian corporation (ITA s. 89(1)) taxed at entity level on worldwide income. Relevant elections for cross-border trade include the ETA s. 273 GST/HST grouping election (closely related group members filing as a single registrant; forms GST25/GST26), the ETA small-supplier voluntary registration election, and, for non-residents, the ITA ss. 216 and 219 elective returns. For hybrid mismatch outcomes, Canada's anti-hybrid rules (ITA ss. 18.4 and 18.5, addressing reverse hybrids and deduction/non-inclusion mismatches) operate alongside CRA entity classification determinations; where a foreign transparent entity is interposed, a dedicated CRA technical ruling should be obtained before structuring.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Canadian Dollar (CAD). No exchange control is exercised under the Currency Act or the Bank of Canada Act. Profit remittances, dividends, royalties, and trade payments to non-residents are unrestricted, subject to anti-money laundering (PCMLTFA) record keeping and associated reporting. No prior approval is required for capital inflows or outflows; however, transactions must be reported under the Canadian Payments Act and, for certain cross-border transfers, to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada does not impose mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency or converted freely. Invoices and bank references must record a standard Canadian export declaration (via Statistics Canada / Canadian Border Services Agency) for trade statistics, but no repatriation or liquidation timeline applies. For re-invoicing intermediaries, the tax authority (CRA) will require transfer pricing documentation to support intercompany settlement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (restricted). Canada does not license pure-play electronic money institutions; a fintech wishing to open accounts for a re-invoicing entity must either hold a bank license, an MSB/MT registration with FINTRAC, or operate as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). Foreign EMIs without Canadian presence have limited or no standalone viability; they may rely on white-label sponsorship by a Schedule I bank. Enhanced due diligence and PCMLTFA compliance are mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: inbound equity injections from a parent entity must be transmitted via SWIFT wire using standard purpose code for foreign direct investment (e.g., balance-of-payments code 1000 'Capital contribution' or CFP code). FINTRAC EFT thresholds: report any electronic fund transfer of CAD 10,000 or more. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million and transactions with non-residents exceed CAD 5 million (or if specifically requested by CRA).

Economic substance scope: Canada applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and requires economic substance for treaty benefits; no separate ESR, but CRA may challenge transactions lacking commercial rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s.160) and for negligence; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CRA may deny treaty benefits, reassess income, apply GAAR penalties (25% of tax benefit), and impose interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed for penalties if they knowingly participated in sham arrangements; potential criminal charges for conspiracy.
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 1 million per violation; criminal penalties up to CAD 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to CAD 500,000 and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to comply.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRA may adjust prices, impose penalties up to 10% of the net adjustment (if gross negligence), plus interest; documentation penalties up to CAD 24,000 per failure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they signed false or incomplete returns; potential criminal liability for tax evasion.
