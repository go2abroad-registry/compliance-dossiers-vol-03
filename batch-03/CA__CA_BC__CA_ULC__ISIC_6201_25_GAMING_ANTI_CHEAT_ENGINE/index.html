Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — GST/HST and Income Tax; portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/business-services.html) and BizPortal for BN registration

E-government filing portal

For a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC), the entity is incorporated provincially, obtaining a BC incorporation file number. The foreign owner or the ULC must then obtain a CRA Business Number (BN) using form RC1 or through the CRA's Registering a Business online portal. The BN serves as the combined federal corporate income tax, GST/HST, and payroll account ID. A separate BC PST account is obtained via BC's OneStop portal. Non-resident corporations carrying on business in Canada must register for GST/HST if taxable supplies exceed the small-supplier threshold, and may also be required to apply for income tax recognition in the absence of permanent establishment treaty relief. The ULC's BN is typically formatted as 9 digits plus a two-letter program marker (e.g., 123456789 RT).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation for Canadian federal and BC corporate income tax (a resident Canadian corporation taxable on worldwide income); Pass_Through_Eligible under U.S. check-the-box rules (Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3) if a U.S. parent elects to treat the ULC as disregarded; territorial scope generally extended to non-resident shareholders only through hybrid election mechanics, not by domestic Canadian default. — For a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC), the entity is incorporated provincially, obtaining a BC incorporation file number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation for Canadian federal and BC corporate income tax (a resident Canadian corporation taxable on worldwide income); Pass_Through_Eligible under U.S. check-the-box rules (Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3) if a U.S. parent elects to treat the ULC as disregarded; territorial scope generally extended to non-resident shareholders only through hybrid election mechanics, not by domestic Canadian default.

Available election forms

No Canadian domestic 'check-the-box' election; Canadian tax law imposes entity-level taxation on all corporations incorporated in Canada. For U.S. federal tax, a check-the-box election via Form 8832 can classify the ULC as a disregarded entity or partnership, creating hybrid mismatch treatment. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act s. 18.4–18.7, s. 20.1) may deny deductions for payments giving rise to hybrid mismatches. GST/HST elections include the Quick Method of accounting and election to use the net tax calculation for financial institutions; an election is also available to treat certain foreign-affiliate dividends as deductible.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST: C$30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any single calendar quarter or the trailing four consecutive calendar quarters (small-supplier threshold); BC PST: remote sellers must register when taxable remote sales exceed C$10,000 in a year, and in-province vendors have no small-supplier exemption for taxable PST transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act when the goods are exported by the supplier or delivered to a common carrier for export and are exported within a reasonable time. Certain services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated if the recipient is not registered for GST/HST and the supply is for performance outside Canada (e.g., advisory, data processing, and intellectual-property services). For the anti-cheat engine middleware, a supply of a software licence or digital service to a non-resident end-user or operator will be zero-rated when the intangible property, including the right to use the software, is exploited or used wholly outside Canada, and the recipient is not registered under the GST/HST. The supplier must retain evidence of the export or offshore-service supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form B13A or Canada Customs Coding Form) filed through the Canada Border Services Agency portal for goods exported from Canada
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Canada
  • Contract of Sale, Software Licence Agreement, or Service Agreement with the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement confirming receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • GST/HST return schedules reconciling zero-rated export supplies
  • Commercial invoice and delivery confirmation (e.g., electronic transfer logs, download certificates) for intangible software deliveries

Canada does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. CRA requires electronic filing of GST/HST returns (via GST/HST NETFILE or EDI) for registrants with taxable supplies above C$1.5 million, and mandatory T2 corporate tax return filing electronically for all corporate taxpayers. Invoices must include the supplier's BN, transaction date, supplier/recipient names and details, description, and applicable GST/HST; no government API pre-clearance or invoice stamping is required.

CRA may reassess to deny zero-rating and claw back the unreported GST/HST plus arrears interest where export documentation is insufficient or the supply is found to be for use or enjoyment in Canada. False or misleading export declarations attract penalties under subsection 280(1) of the Excise Tax Act, including not less than C$1,000 and up to 25% of the net tax evaded (or, in certain cases, the greater of C$5,000 and 25% of the tax). More severe criminal tax-evasion penalties under section 327 can reach 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment (up to five years on indictment), with fifteen per cent of the evaded tax payable as a civil penalty where the false statement was made knowingly or with gross negligence. BC PST export exemptions similarly require proof of physical delivery outside BC, and non-compliance may yield 8% per annum interest plus penalties for uncollected PST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is a free-floating and fully convertible currency for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Canada imposes no general foreign exchange controls, no central bank prior approval for outward investment, and no profit remittance restrictions on current earnings. Dividends, interest, royalties, and capital repatriation paid by a British Columbia Unlimited Liability Corporation (ULC) to non-resident shareholders are permissible without exchange-control approval, but remain subject to Part XIII Canadian withholding tax, Ontario/BC corporate tax characterization, arm's-length transfer pricing, and CRS/FATCA reporting. The ULC's pass-through tax status may create unique U.S.-tax and CRA compliance obligations, so treaty-based entitlement must be documented before preserving net-of-tax distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may maintain foreign currency balances, invoice in USD/EUR/JPY, and remit proceeds directly into any resident or non-resident bank account. However, all wire transfers of CAD 10,000 or more across the border are subject to FINTRAC large-value transaction reporting criteria, and Canadian-resident corporations must translate export revenues into CAD for income tax declaration under IFRS/ASPE using the Bank of Canada daily or periodic exchange rate. Trade receivables may remain offshore for treasury management, subject to controlled-foreign-affiliate, FAPI (Foreign Accrual Property Income), and transfer-pricing documentation rules impacting a ULC with non-resident shareholders.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with statutory foreign exchange and AML transaction reporting oversight administered through the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for a BC ULC in the low-risk gaming-security middleware sector, but subject to strict legal constraints.
