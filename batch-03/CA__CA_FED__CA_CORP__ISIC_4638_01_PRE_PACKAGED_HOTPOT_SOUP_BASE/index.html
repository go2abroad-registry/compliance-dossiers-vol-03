Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada (ARC); principal digital portal: CRA My Business Account (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account.html) and Business Registration Online (BRO) for BN and GST/HST account creation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act via Corporations Canada (ISED) and obtain the federal corporation number. Step 2: File Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number and Certain Program Accounts) or register through Business Registration Online (BRO) within 30 days of incorporation; the CRA issues a 9-digit Business Number (BN) that serves as the common tax identifier for all program accounts, including RC (corporate income tax), RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), and RM (import/export). Step 3: For a foreign entity, attach Form RC59 (Business General Enquiries) and, where the entity has no Canadian address, appoint a Canadian authorized agent using Form RC59B; provide certified evidence of foreign incorporation, director identification, and ultimate beneficial ownership attestation. Step 4: The GST/HST program account number (BN-RT0001) constitutes the statutory VAT/TIN identifier and must appear on sales invoices, customs documents, and GST/HST returns. The CRA issues the registration certificate reflecting the registration date, legal name, and program account fields; non-resident exporters without Canadian establishments may voluntarily register under ETA s. 241 to claim input tax credits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box regime; every Business Corporation is taxed as a separate person under Parts I and I.1 of the Income Tax Act. Available elections include: ITA s. 85 (tax-deferred transfer of property to a corporation, Form T2057), ITA s. 216 (non-resident net-basis election for Canadian rental and timber income), ITA s. 256 (associated corporation elections after amalgamation), and ETA s. 156 (election between closely related corporations to treat taxable supplies as made for nil consideration). For hybrid mismatch purposes, Canada implements OECD BEPS Action 2 through ITA ss. 18(1)(f)(IX), 3.5, and 3.6; no elective disclaimers are available, and classification of a federal corporation as flow-through is not permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in total worldwide taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters; a person below this small supplier threshold under ETA s. 148(1) is not required to register, although non-residents carrying on a business in Canada may voluntarily register to recover input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ETA Schedule VI Part I (goods) and Part V (services), a supply of pre-packaged hotpot soup base is zero-rated where: (a) the supplier exports the tangible personal property in the ordinary course of its business; (b) the goods are delivered by a common carrier to a destination outside Canada; or (c) the goods are shipped outside Canada at the request of a non-resident recipient or licensed exporter. The supplier must demonstrate physical removal of the goods from Canada through contemporaneous documentation and must satisfy the ETA s. 222(1) evidence requirements; zero rating also requires that the sale constitute a 'taxable supply' made in Canada by a GST/HST registrant. Services ancillary to offshore distribution qualify only when the services are performed exclusively outside Canada.

  • Canadian Export Declaration filed through CBSA's Canadian Online Export Declarations system (legacy G49/CAED form)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing carrier delivery to a destination outside Canada
  • Commercial Invoice and/or pro forma invoice corresponding to the customs export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Swift MT103 records confirming settlement by the foreign buyer
  • Foreign customs entry or arrival/clearance certificate at the destination jurisdiction (where obtainable)

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or state-run clearance API is currently legislated in Canada. The CRA accepts electronic invoices as valid books and records and has endorsed the Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PCTF) and the PEPPOL V4.2 e-invoicing standard on a voluntary basis; federal procurement transactions require e-invoice exchange via the Pan-Canadian e-Invoicing Service (PCI) or a certified PEPPOL access point. GST/HST returns are transmitted through GST/HST NETFILE, EDI, or other receiver-general prescribed formats, and all invoice and export records must be retained for at least six years, in paper or digital form accessible to the CRA in Canada.

The CRA may reassess a zero-rated export claim within the normal 4-year limitation period under ETA s. 298, and without any time limit where the claim involves misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. Upon denial, GST/HST at 5% (plus any applicable provincial HST) is assessed on the export sale, associated input tax credits are clawed back, and interest accrues under ETA s. 280. False statements or omissions attract a penalty of the greater of CAD 250 or 25% of the tax sought to be evaded, subject to a CAD 100 minimum, under ETA s. 285; separate penalties apply for failure to file under s. 280.1 and for books and records violations under s. 286(1)-(3). Criminal fraudulent procurement of export refunds is prosecuted under Criminal Code s. 380 with imprisonment of up to 14 years, and the CRA's Export Review and Refund Integrity program actively examines exporter invoices, CAED filings, freight patterns, and payment flows to detect circular or artificial export chains.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility; there are no exchange controls, no exchange licensing, and no central bank approval gate for dividend, interest, royalty, or profit remittances to non-residents. The only statutory friction is the Part XIII federal withholding tax of the Income Tax Act on outbound passive payments (generally 25%, reduced by tax treaty, e.g., 15% dividends under the Canada–US treaty and 10% under the Canada–China treaty as applicable). Trade settlement in CAD, USD, EUR, CNY, and exotic currencies is unrestricted, subject only to bank internal counterparty limits and sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Canada imposes no export proceeds surrender duty, no mandatory conversion timeline (no 90-day or 180-day repatriation rule), and no statutory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain USD, EUR, CNY, or other foreign currency proceeds indefinitely in domestic foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts. Triggering compliance obligations: (i) electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more within or outside Canada must be reported by the financial intermediary to FINTRAC under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA); (ii) foreign property with a cost amount over CAD 100,000 must be reported annually by the Canadian exporter on Form T1135; (iii) inter-company export sales to related non-resident distributors must be priced at arm's length under section 247 of the Income Tax Act with contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) — Canada's sole monetary and currency issuance authority under the Currency Act; banking supervision delegated to the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); AML/CTF enforcement by the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC); foreign exchange policy coordination with the Department of Finance Canada. Canada has no separate foreign-exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a supplemental disbursement or supplier-payment rail; Low as a primary treasury custody solution. Foreign digital EMIs (UK FCA-authorised, EU-licensed payment institutions, or Singapore MPI/EMI) are not recognised as authorised deposit-taking institutions in Canada. A non-resident EMI establishing a Canadian nexus must register as a Money Service Business (MSB) with FINTRAC, appoint a Canadian compliance officer, file a FINTRAC annual compliance report, and implement a full AML/CTF program; Quebec-specific money-services business licensing may additionally apply. For the pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot soup base distribution business, the escrowed capital and operational CAD/USD treasury must remain with a Schedule I bank; EMIs may be engaged strictly for low-value cross-border vendor payments and must never hold capital escrow or serve as the designated cash management account.

Verification steps: (i) FINTRAC EFT reporting for every wire equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000; (ii) know-your-customer and know-your-transaction due diligence on the remitter and underlying beneficial owner; (iii) source-of-funds documentation including audited financial statements or board resolution; (iv) update of the CBCA register of individuals with significant control; (v) CRS/FATCA classification certification; and (vi) treasury receipt memo segregating capital escrow from operational settlement balances. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if total value of transactions with non-residents exceeds CAD 5 million (for goods) or CAD 1 million (for services); Master File required if gross revenue exceeds CAD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) but applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles. Transactions must have commercial rationale; shell companies with no real activity risk recharacterization and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Act s.239; fines up to 200% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil penalties of 50% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (s.227.1) if they failed to exercise due diligence; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to 25% of tax avoided, and potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in sham arrangements; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD 2 million for corporations; criminal penalties for money laundering under Criminal Code s.462.31 (imprisonment up to 10 years).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for AML violations if they knowingly failed to implement controls; civil penalties, travel bans, and imprisonment for money laundering.
