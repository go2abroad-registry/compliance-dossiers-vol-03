Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada) — portal: www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html; international and export-registration matters handled by the International and Large Business Directorate; customs export reporting processed through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) via the Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Canadian federal Business Corporation must obtain a CRA Business Number (BN), a 9-digit account identifier used for GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, and import/export accounts. Procedure: (1) incorporate under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) via Corporations Canada online; (2) apply for the BN using Form RC1 (Request for a Business Number) filed by mail, fax, or through Business Registration Online (BRO), ensuring the specific GST/HST account (RT) is requested where taxable supplies are intended to exceed the small-supplier threshold; (3) an RC corporate income tax account is established automatically on incorporation; the CRA then issues a BN Confirmation letter (typically within 2–6 weeks); (4) for foreign principals, appoint a Canadian representative and submit Form T1013 (Authorizing or Cancelling a Representative) to enable CRA online portal access; (5) update the BN profile with Shopify Payments, Stripe, and PayPal where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the CBCA Business Corporation is a resident Canadian corporation subject to federal corporate income tax on worldwide income; no federal tax-exempt zone applies to DTC e-commerce; CCPC status (and the small business deduction) is available only if the corporation is not controlled by one or more non-resident persons and meets the small-business corporate limit tests. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the CBCA Business Corporation is a resident Canadian corporation subject to federal corporate income tax on worldwide income; no federal tax-exempt zone applies to DTC e-commerce; CCPC status (and the small business deduction) is available only if the corporation is not controlled by one or more non-resident persons and meets the small-business corporate limit tests. Pass-through eligibility does not exist for limited-liability corporate shareholders.

Available election forms

Canada has no U.S.-style check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime; corporate status is mandatory and conclusive for tax. Available elections include: (i) GST/HST closely related corporation election under Excise Tax Act (ETA) s. 150 to treat supplies among closely related corporations as taxable and elect for zero-rated nil-consideration treatment; (ii) GST/HST grouping election under ETA s. 156 for consolidated GST/HST reporting; (iii) capital cost allowance elections under Income Tax Act (ITA) ss. 13(3), 13(4), 44(6) and Income Tax Regulations s. 1100(2.2) for tax-depreciation pooling; (iv) ETA s. 221.2 election for self-assessment of tax on financial services (rare for DTC retail); (v) anti-hybrid rules under ITA ss. 18.2–18.4 and s. 258B apply automatically, with no elective override available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in taxable worldwide supplies over any calendar quarter and the trailing four consecutive quarters (small-supplier threshold, ETA s. 148); a Shopify DTC brand must register once the threshold is exceeded, and may voluntarily register before the threshold if it wishes to claim input tax credits on startup costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST/HST applies on (i) tangible personal property exported by or on behalf of the supplier from Canada, provided the goods leave Canada within 60 days of delivery or such extended period acceptable to the CRA (ETA s. 21 and Schedule VI, Part V, s. 1); (ii) services supplied to non-resident persons where the supply is not made in Canada and does not relate to Canadian real property or goods situated in Canada, and where the non-resident is not present in Canada at the time the service is performed (ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 7). Digital products and e-services sold to final foreign consumers through Shopify qualify as zero-rated intangible supplies if the recipient is a non-resident and outside Canada at the time of supply. The merchant bundle must be capable of demonstrating the physical export track of goods and the non-residency of the digital-services recipient. Input tax credits for expenses attributable to zero-rated exports are fully recoverable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (B13A filed through CERS) or CBSA-approved carrier e-report
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Canada to foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, Shopify order invoice, and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank SWIFT/SEPA settlement record, or payment-rail settlement report (PayPal, Stripe, Shopify Payments)
  • Carrier tracking and delivery confirmation (final-mile proof for small-parcel DTC exports)

Canada does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API pre-authorization of invoices at the federal level. Voluntary pan-Canadian e-invoicing interoperability exists through the Peppol framework. Mandates at the filing level: registrants with taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1.5 million annually must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE or participating bank channels. Taxpayers must retain invoices and source records electronically (ETA s. 286, ITA s. 230) for six years; CRA may demand access to e-commerce transaction files, sales reports from Shopify, payment-settlement logs, and webstore back-office data upon audit.

CRA export-verification and GST/HST audit units scrutinize zero-rated claims for goods and services to foreign consumers, requiring transactional substantiation within CRA-prescribed timelines. If zero-rating is denied, the exporter is reassessed for 5% GST (plus provincial HST at the point-of-supply rate up to 15% in participating provinces), with compound daily interest accruing from the original filing date. The normal reassessment period is four years; it extends to seven years where the taxpayer has made a misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness, or wilful default, or has filed a waiver (ITA s. 152(4), ETA s. 298). A gross-negligence penalty of 50% of the resulting tax understatement applies (ETA s. 285, ITA s. 163.2), and false statements or omissions can attract fines of CAD 250 to CAD 50,000 (ETA s. 280). Criminal prosecution for knowingly making false or deceptive statements carries fines up to CAD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to two years (ETA s. 327); fraud in export schemes additionally exposes the taxpayer to VAT-fraud conspiracy charges and full clawback of previously credited input tax refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is freely convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on the purchase or sale of foreign currency, and no approval requirement for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation by federally incorporated corporations. Payments abroad are subject only to standard financial reporting, anti-money-laundering (AML) obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), and Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) reporting thresholds, such as T106 and Form T1134 for foreign affiliates and cross-border transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or prescribed time horizons for export receipts in Canada. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in foreign-denominated bank accounts, convert at commercial rates, or use such proceeds for legitimate business purposes without central bank approval. The only compliance obligations relate to accurate record-keeping, tax reporting of foreign exchange gains and losses, and FINTRAC reporting for transactions exceeding CAD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada; monetary authority and lender of last resort. Canada does not maintain a separate foreign exchange control administration; foreign exchange policy is market-determined and administered by the Minister of Finance in coordination with the Bank of Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for payment facilitation and ancillary treasury services, with legal constraints. Canada does not recognize the EU-style 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) passport. A non-resident fintech or digital bank must either register as a money services business (MSB) with FINTRAC or rely on a Canadian-registered MSB/PSP partner. Regulatory oversight for retail payments is transitioning under the Retail Payment Activities Act, administered by the Bank of Canada. An offshore EMI may act as a payment gateway, but settlement in CAD via Canadian clearing rails generally requires a Canadian-domiciled bank account or partnership with a regulated Canadian bank.

Box 590 Foreign Tax Credits (Credits for taxes paid to foreign jurisdictions). Claim recognized foreign withholding taxes paid on cross-border royalty or interest income to reduce Canadian federal tax. Late filing penalty of 5% of the balance owing plus 1% per complete month (max 12 months). Repeated failure penalty of 10% severity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD $1 million for services with non-resident related parties; CAD $5 million for other related-party transactions (including royalties, interest, and intercompany goods).

Economic substance scope: Canada follows the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under the ITA s.245, disallowing tax benefits from transactions lacking bona fide economic substance. Canada does not operate an ESR registration but mandates substance-over-form, rigorous transfer pricing documentation, and onerous compliance for intercompany services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded, a maximum aggregate fine of CAD $2,000,000, and imprisonment for up to 8 years under ITA s.239 (1).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax liability where the evasion was committed with their knowledge or consent, and may suffer civil restitution, freeze of personal assets, and travel restraints.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under transfer pricing rules (s.247) to treat income as belonging to the Canadian corporation, plus GAAR penalties and 10% misrepresentation penalties under s.163 (2).

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders can be held personally liable for taxes resulting from disallowed avoidance, and may be barred from travel or subject to court-ordered enforcement where evasion is proven.
AML / Non-Compliance with FINTRAC

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties up to CAD $1,000,000 for serious non-compliance; criminal penalties include fines and imprisonment where unlawful activity is knowingly facilitated.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and subjected to prohibition orders if they authorized or acquiesced to the violation of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA).
