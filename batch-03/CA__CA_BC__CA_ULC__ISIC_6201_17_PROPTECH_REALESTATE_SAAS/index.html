Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) / Agence du revenu du Canada; online portal: CRA My Business Account and Business Registration Online (BRO); BC Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is administered separately by the BC Ministry of Finance, Consumer Taxation Branch, through the TaxpayerWeb portal.

E-government filing portal

A BC ULC is incorporated through BC Corporate Online; the BC registrar automatically transmits incorporation data to the CRA, which issues a nine-digit Business Number (BN) with program accounts (RC for corporation income tax, RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RM for import/export). The foreign parent must enrol or activate additional program accounts through BRO or Form RC1. For GST/HST, the ULC may use the standard registration regime or, if classified as a non-resident digital-services supplier, the simplified non-resident GST/HST registration. No minimum capital is required. For Canadian Income Tax Act purposes, the ULC is a corporation under ITA s. 89(1) and is resident in Canada where its central management and control is situated in Canada.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (the BC ULC is eligible for US federal check-the-box classification as a disregarded entity; however, under Canadian domestic law the ULC is a corporation under ITA s. 89(1) and remains subject to entity-level Canadian corporate income tax under Part I of the Income Tax Act; the CRA does not recognize any Canadian flow-through election).

Available election forms

1. US check-the-box: the US parent files IRS Form 8832 to elect the BC ULC as a disregarded entity. 2. Canada-US Tax Treaty Article IV(7) (fifth protocol) hybrid-entity rules determine treaty eligibility and foreign tax credit flows; IRS Form 8833 discloses treaty-based return positions. 3. Canadian anti-hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6, effective 1 July 2022) may deny deductions where the ULC payment is treated inconsistently across jurisdictions, requiring a contemporaneous structural review. 4. GST/HST section 156 election may be filed to zero-rate supplies among closely related GST-registered corporations. 5. No Canadian check-the-box mechanism exists for ULCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident corporations: C$30,000 small-supplier threshold on worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, measured over the current and previous three calendar quarters (ETA s. 148(1)). Non-resident suppliers of digital services or digital goods to Canadian consumers: no small-supplier threshold, mandatory GST/HST registration effective 1 July 2021 (ETA s. 240(2.1)). For B2B SaaS exports by the Canadian ULC to non-resident US business customers, the export supply is zero-rated and does not itself require registration; however, the ULC must register once its worldwide taxable supplies exceed the C$30,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

PropTech SaaS qualifies as a supply of intangible personal property under ETA Schedule VI, Part V, s. 10 and is zero-rated when supplied to a recipient who is (i) a non-resident of Canada, (ii) not registered for GST/HST under Subdivision d of Division V of Part IX, and (iii) the supply does not relate to real property situated in Canada or to tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time of supply. The US property-management customer must not acquire the platform for Canadian-situs property operations. If the SaaS platform is characterized as a service, s. 7 of Part V, Schedule VI provides a parallel zero-rating rule. The ULC must retain evidence of the customer's non-resident status and non-registration; zero-rated exports count toward the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold and must be reported on the GST/HST return with the supporting export documentation.

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or government e-invoice authorisation requirement exists in Canada. The CRA accepts electronic invoices in any format (PDF, EDI, XML, CSV) where data integrity and ETA s. 286 record-retention rules are satisfied. The Pan-Canadian e-invoicing network (PEPPOL) is voluntary and is operated with CRA participation. BC PST compliance is administered through BC TaxpayerWeb with periodic returns; no fiscal clearance API or digital stamping is mandated.

No statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline exists under the ETA or the Income Tax Act; the 180-day figure is a conservative audit-evidence benchmark, but books and records must generally be retained for six years (ETA s. 286(1); ITA s. 230(1)). CRA reassessment windows are four years generally, seven years for misrepresentation attributable to neglect, carelessness or wilful default, and unlimited for fraud (ETA s. 298). If an export zero-rating claim is challenged, the ULC is liable for 5% GST on the disputed revenue, arrears interest, and penalties: 10% of net tax not collected, or 20% where the failure is knowing, under ETA s. 280; a gross-negligence penalty equal to the greater of C$250 and 50% of the tax evaded under ETA s. 285(1); and criminal fines up to C$100,000 plus up to five years imprisonment for tax evasion under ETA s. 327. CRA's International and Large Business Directorate will test W-8BEN-E validity, Canadian resident indicators, and cloud usage traces; any evidence of Canadian consumer or property-management access can also trigger a separate 7% BC PST assessment with a 10% penalty and interest under the BC PST Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since March 25, 1952. There is no exchange-control law, no central bank permission requirement for cross-border equity injections, and no restriction on a BC unlimited liability corporation (ULC) holding foreign-currency bank accounts in Canada or abroad. Capital transfers and profit remittances are unfettered by FX policy, but remain subject to Canadian tax rules: Part XIII non-resident withholding tax at a 25% statutory rate on dividends (treaty-reduced, e.g., 5% under Canada-U.S. treaty where a U.S. corporate shareholder owns at least 25% of the voting stock), Part XIV branch profits tax if the ULC carries on business in Canada as a non-Canadian-controlled entity, and Part XIII withholding on interest, royalties, and management or technical fees. Regulatory screening is imposed solely through PCMLTFA/FINTRAC reporting thresholds, OSFI sanctions enforcement, and FATCA/CRS automatic exchange obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies to export or other current-account receipts in Canada. Exporters and SaaS platforms may retain proceeds in original currencies, maintain domestic foreign-currency bank accounts, or keep funds offshore without central bank consent. Nevertheless, FINTRAC reporting applies: electronic funds transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in a 24-hour window must be reported; cross-border remittances must be supported by underlying commercial documentation and are screened for OFAC/UNSC/Canadian sanctions compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), with prudential regulation of banks by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), enforcement of anti-money-laundering rules by FINTRAC, and oversight of financial market infrastructure, including the Large Value Transfer System (LVTS), by the Bank of Canada under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a supplementary payments rail only. FINTRAC-registered money services businesses and Bank-of-Canada-registered Retail Payment Activity Providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and the Retail Payment Supervision Framework may legally execute CAD/USD transfers, payroll, virtual IBAN disbursements, and currency conversion. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise Payments Canada Corp., Payoneer, and Mercury can serve as secondary disbursement and FX execution channels for the PropTech platform. However, they are not deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot lawfully act as the ULC's primary custodial treasury venue, capital escrow depository, or documentary letter-of-credit issuing bank. A reinforced Schedule I bank relationship is mandatory for capital injections, LC issuance, and treasury concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for all shareholder equity injections, strictly avoiding MT202/MT202COV where possible. CAD inbound wires must be cleared through LVTS with same-day finality; USD wires must be routed through the Canadian bank's U.S. correspondent by Canadian BIC and U.S. ABA number (e.g., RBC uses ROYCUS33PIM in New York). Payment must include the ULC's exact legal name as registered in BC, full bank transit/account details, and an ISO 20022 purpose code such as 'EQUI' for capital contributions or 'COMC' for commercial/remittance-fee flows. The classic correspondent-banking 'travel rule' requires the originating institution to transmit originator and beneficiary identity; for transfers of CAD 10,000 or more in 24 hours, file an Electronic Funds Transfer Report with FINTRAC. The bank's account-opening team must receive the executed subscription agreement, share-certificate register, board resolution authorizing the capital call, and, for non-resident U.S. shareholders, evidence of FATCA/CRS classification. All inbound funds must originate from a bank account in the legal shareholder's name; third-party or cryptocurrency-source funds will be blocked by Canadian AML compliance.Use SWIFT MT103 priority customer transfers for software-development payments, cloud-hosting fees, contractor disbursements, intercompany settlements, and dividend repatriation; include purpose code 'DIVI', 'SUPP', 'TRAD', or 'ROYL' as applicable and attach Part XIII withholding proof, tax clearance, and transfer-pricing documentation. For procured infrastructure or hardware that requires shipping and title documents, issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (documentary LC) through the Canadian issuing bank, with settlement by SWIFT MT700/MT750 and collection by MT798. CAD-denominated vendor payments clear via LVTS; USD-denominated payments clear through Fedwire or CHIPS via the dealer bank's U.S. correspondent window. Recurring low-value SaaS and landlord disbursements should be batched through the primary bank's ACH/EFT file (for domestic Canadian clearing) or through a registered EMI virtual-IBAN system for cross-border micro-payments. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium as a supplementary payments rail only. FINTRAC-registered money services businesses and Bank-of-Canada-registered Retail Payment Activity Providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and the Retail Payment Supervision Framework may legally execute CAD/USD transfers, payroll, virtual IBAN disbursements, and currency conversion. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise Payments Canada Corp., Payoneer, and Mercury can serve as secondary disbursement and FX execution channels for the PropTech platform. However, they are not deposit-taking institutions under the Bank Act and cannot lawfully act as the ULC's primary custodial treasury venue, capital escrow depository, or documentary letter-of-credit issuing bank. A reinforced Schedule I bank relationship is mandatory for capital injections, LC issuance, and treasury concentration.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% combined Canada/BC general corporate rate (15% federal Part I after provincial abatement plus 12% British Columbia provincial corporate income tax); reduced to 11% for Canadian-controlled private corporations on the small-business income basket. A BC ULC is a resident Canadian corporation for Part I tax purposes even where its U.S. shareholder elects check-the-box pass-through treatment in the United States.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII withholding tax on dividends paid by the Canadian ULC to a non-resident shareholder; Canada-US Tax Treaty reduces to 15% generally and 5% where the beneficial ownership is a US corporation holding at least 10% of the voting stock.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A contemporaneous transfer-pricing file (Local File) is required under ITA s.247(4) when aggregate controlled transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents exceed CAD 1,000,000 in a taxation year; a group with consolidated revenue above CAD 50,000,000 should maintain the OECD Master File/Country-by-Country data positions.

Economic substance scope: British Columbia has no Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations. Canadian law applies the general anti-avoidance rule (s.245), source rules, and treaty limitation-on-benefits/beneficial-ownership concepts; a ULC with no employees, office, or operational decision-making has no independent economic substance and will be disregarded for income attribution. As a BC ULC, shareholders face unlimited liability for company obligations, so the entity cannot serve as a pure limited-liability shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under ITA s.239(1) attracts fines of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment up to two years on summary conviction or five years on indictment; the civil gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) adds 50% of the understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for unremitted Part XIII/GST/Payroll amounts under s.227.1; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent returns, allowing CRA to seek civil restitution, freeze personal assets, and pursue travel bans and imprisonment once criminal charges are laid.
